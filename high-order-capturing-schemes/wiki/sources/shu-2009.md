# Summary

A review with no new scheme or test. Merged into the WENO, ENO,
FD, FV, SSP, characteristic, central-WENO, WENO-limited DG and ILW
pages, the smoothness-indicator and weight-condition pages, the new
[negative linear weights](/methods/negative-linear-weights.md) and

critical-point page's closed contradiction.

# Key claims

1. **Procedures** (§2): fifth-order WENO interpolation with linear weights
   $\tfrac1{16}, \tfrac58, \tfrac5{16}$ and its indicators (eqs. 2.1–2.9,
   page image); reconstruction (eqs. 2.11–2.17); derivatives (indicator sum
   from $l = 2$) and integrals (negative weights).
2. **Monotonicity in the worst case** (§2.1): with a step in the target
   interval, every candidate and the big-stencil polynomial are monotone,
   so the WENO combination is too.
3. **Weights** (eqs. 2.7, 2.10): $\omega_k = d_k + O(h^2)$ for fifth order;
   $O(h^4)$ on discontinuous stencils; rational weights chosen over
   exponential ones for cost and performance.
4. **Negative linear weights** (§§2.3.3, 3.5.5): the Shi–Hu–Shu splitting,
   or positive weights at lower order.
5. **FV and FD** (§§3.1–3.4): FD on uniform or smooth meshes only, the
   deeper reason after Merryman; FV two to five times as costly in 2D above
   second order; FD recommended whenever the mesh allows.
6. **Time** (§3.1): WENO5 with forward Euler or RK2 is linearly unstable
   (Wang and Spiteri) and should not be used even for steady states.
7. **Self-similarity** (§§3.5.1, 7): with ε relative to the solution, WENO
   is scale-invariant; mesh-dependent thresholds are discouraged.
8. **Behind shocks** (§3.5.2, Table 3.1, page image): downstream order
   about 2 for systems unless the shock cell uses subcell integration
   (about 4).
9. **Systems, contacts, boundaries, DG** (§§3.5, 5.1): componentwise to
   third order; contacts widen with time; ghost values with characteristic
   decomposition; DG less robust than WENO with strong shocks.
10. **Cost** (§1): 3 to 10 times a second-order scheme, still worthwhile.
11. **Open problems** (§7): implicit WENO, tailored weights, 3D
    unstructured WENO, hybrids, non-polynomial bases, analysis of the
    unmodified schemes.
12. Henrick et al. are cited as the improvement at smooth extrema
    (§2.3.5). The Jiang–Shu critical-point claim is not repeated.
