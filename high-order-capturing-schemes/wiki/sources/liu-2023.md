# Summary

The direct sequel of [Lu et al. (2016)](/sources/lu-2016.md): the static
convection–diffusion ILW is extended to moving Dirichlet boundaries.
Merged into the ILW page (section "Moving boundaries"), the SSP page and
the new [shock-driven cylinder lift-off](/benchmarks/shock-cylinder-lift-off.md)
benchmark.

# Key claims

1. **Setting** (§2, eqs. 2.1–2.7). Third-order FD-WENO for convection,
   fourth-order central differences for diffusion, TVD-RK3, and a
   three-term Taylor expansion about the moving boundary. Newly emerging
   points need no special treatment.
2. **Material derivative** (eq. 2.9). $g'(t) = u_t + \dot a\,u_x$, so every
   ILW relation uses $f'(g) - \dot a$ (eqs. 2.10–2.12).
3. **Blending** (eqs. 2.13–2.14). Lu et al.'s weight with the relative
   speed; it reduces to theirs at $\dot a = 0$.
4. **No zero denominators** (eqs. 2.15–2.18). The quotients are multiplied
   through by the weights, and the two weights are regularized by
   $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ILW}} = 10^{-6}$, so one algorithm covers pure
   convection, pure diffusion and everything between.
5. **Stage values** (eqs. 2.19–2.21). The boundary is frozen at $t^n$; $u_t$
   and $u_{tt}$ follow from the material derivative, and $u_{tx}$ from
   linear extrapolation of interior $u_t$.
6. **Systems** (§§2.2–2.3). Convective and diffusive matrices are
   diagonalized separately. In 2D, expansion along the normal in a rotated
   frame, with the boundary's normal and tangential velocities.
7. **Navier–Stokes** (§3, eqs. 3.1–3.18). An isothermal no-slip wall on
   a rigid body driven by pressure and viscous forces. Wall density from
   the outgoing acoustic characteristic; blends in the sound speed with
   per-component diffusion coefficients.
8. **Tests** (§4). Advection–diffusion with boundaries that reach the
   characteristic speed, viscous Burgers with a sharp layer at the moving
   boundary, 1D linear systems, 1D Navier–Stokes, 2D advection–diffusion on
   translating, shrinking and expanding discs, a shock hitting a free
   cylinder (with trajectory convergence), a free airfoil, and a vortex
   street. Third order; no stability analysis (§5: Neumann conditions
   left for later).
