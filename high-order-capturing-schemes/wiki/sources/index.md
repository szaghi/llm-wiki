# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Baeza, Mulet & Zorío (2016) — High order weighted extrapolation for boundary conditions for finite difference methods on complex domains with Cartesian meshes](/sources/baeza-2016.md) — Ghost-cell values for fifth-order FD-WENO on Cartesian meshes of complex domains by extrapolation along boundary normals with dimensionless, scale-independent weights that reduce to the nearest value across discontinuities, blended with least squares (WLS-GAW) to remove the mild instability of Lagrange extrapolation.
* [Balsara & Kim (2016) — A subluminal relativistic magnetohydrodynamics scheme with ADER-WENO predictor and multidimensional Riemann solver-based corrector](/sources/balsara-2016-b.md) — One-step second- to fourth-order finite-volume relativistic MHD on a Yee mesh that keeps velocities subluminal: one conserved-to-primitive inversion per cell via the 1/24 average–point identity, WENO or PPM reconstruction of the four-velocity, an ADER predictor in those variables, and a multidimensional HLLC/HLLEM Riemann solver for the edge electric fields.
* [Balsara & Nkonga (2017) — Multidimensional Riemann problem with self-similar internal structure – part III – a multidimensional analogue of the HLLI Riemann solver for conservative hyperbolic systems](/sources/balsara-2017-b.md) — A 1D HLLI variant with a Galerkin first-moment constraint (shifted mean flux, new anti-diffusion weights, dissipation within 23.2 % of Roe in the cases shown) and its two-dimensional MuSIC analogue at vertices, which reduces to multidimensional HLL without sub-structure; used in ADER–WENO of orders 2–4 for Euler, MHD and RMHD, where sub-structure cuts the dissipation of a torsional Alfvén wave 6× at second order and 3× at third.
* [Balsara & Shu (2000) — Monotonicity preserving weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes with increasingly high order of accuracy](/sources/balsara-2000.md) — WENO of orders 7–13 (r = 4–7) with tabulated coefficients and indicators, made robust by the Suresh–Huynh bounds (MPWENO); convergence, flat-extremum degradation, artificial-compression tuning by order, and 1D/2D Euler and MHD tests.
* [Balsara & Spicer (1999) — A staggered mesh algorithm using high order Godunov fluxes to ensure solenoidal magnetic fields in magnetohydrodynamic simulations](/sources/balsara-1999.md) — Origin of Balsara–Spicer constrained transport: face-centred magnetic fields updated by discrete Stokes from edge electric fields, each taken as the average of the four adjacent Godunov face fluxes (the flux–electric-field duality), with an optional energy correction for low β and a pressure-switch variant; introduces the MHD rotor, whose printed parameters an erratum corrects.
* [Balsara (1998) — Total Variation Diminishing Scheme for Adiabatic and Isothermal Magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-1998-b.md) — The second-order TVD scheme of the RIEMANN code for adiabatic (seven-wave) and isothermal (six-wave) MHD: characteristic limited slopes (MC-type in genuinely nonlinear, minmod-type in linearly degenerate fields), Yang's steepener in the entropy and Alfvén fields, Colella-type time-centred states, a characteristic-switched artificial viscosity and hyperviscosity, and a spectral Hodge projection whose symbol matches the finite-difference divergence; argues, on split-versus-unsplit comparisons, that divergence cleaning matters.
* [Balsara (2001) — Divergence-free adaptive mesh refinement for magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2001.md) — Second-order divergence-free AMR for MHD on a Yee mesh: the first divergence-free reconstruction of face fields inside a zone, a ratio-2 divergence-free prolongation with bilinear face moments, area-weighted restriction of face fields, and an electric-field correction at coarse–fine edges that transposes Berger–Colella refluxing from faces to edges.
* [Balsara (2004) — Second-order-accurate schemes for magnetohydrodynamics with divergence-free reconstruction](/sources/balsara-2004.md) — Second-order unsplit Runge–Kutta Godunov schemes for MHD on a Yee-staggered mesh: divergence-free reconstruction of the face fields gives a unique zone-centred field, edge electric fields come from four Riemann problems per face without spatial averaging, with TVD, WENO-slope and multidimensional limiters, curvilinear and tetrahedral extensions, and a new MHD vortex test.
* [Balsara (2009b) — Divergence-free reconstruction of magnetic fields and WENO schemes for magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2009-b.md) — Extends divergence-free reconstruction of face-centred magnetic fields to third and fourth order in closed form, fixing free coefficients by minimizing magnetic energy, and builds a Yee-staggered RK-WENO finite-volume MHD scheme that reaches design order to fourth on a magnetized vortex and a 3D torsional Alfvén wave.
* [Balsara (2012) — Self-adjusting, positivity preserving high order schemes for hydrodynamics and magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2012.md) — A reconstruction-level positivity fix for Euler and MHD: a flattener built from the velocity divergence and the neighbourhood's smallest sound speed widens or narrows neighbour-based density and pressure bounds, density is rescaled two-sidedly by Barth's ratio, and pressure one-sidedly by a per-node quadratic (Euler) or cubic (MHD, scaling only the free modes of the divergence-free field); no theorem, three stringent ADER-WENO tests that fail with floors alone.
* [Balsara (2017) — Higher-order accurate space-time schemes for computational astrophysics, Part I: finite volume methods](/sources/balsara-2017-d.md) — Pedagogical review of higher-order Godunov finite-volume methods for Euler, MHD, RHD and RMHD with every formula explicit at third order in 2D: TVD, PPM and modal WENO, SSP-RK and ADER predictors, RKDG with compact WENO and MOOD limiters, PNPM, Balsara's self-adjusting positivity method, HLLI, and the finding that Riemann-solver quality matters less as the order rises.
* [Balsara, Bhoriya & Shu (2026) — An alternative finite difference WENO-like scheme with physical constraint preservation for divergence-preserving hyperbolic systems](/sources/balsara-2026.md) — Extends AFD-WENO to systems with a divergence involution (electrodynamics, MHD, relativistic MHD): cell variables by AFD-WENO, face-averaged normal fields updated FV-style by edge electric fields from a multidimensional LLF/HLL Riemann solver, plus a time-explicit PCP hybridization; orders up to nine.
* [Balsara, Bhoriya, Shu & Kumar (2025) — Efficient alternative finite difference WENO schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/balsara-2025.md) — Makes the alternative finite-difference WENO formulation general-purpose up to ninth order: a derivation of the even-derivative flux corrections, WENO-AO pointwise interpolation formulas, and a boundary-centred WENO interpolation of A∂u that suppresses the corrections at shocks, with any Riemann solver at the cost of classical FD-WENO.
* [Balsara, Garain & Shu (2016) — An efficient class of WENO schemes with adaptive order](/sources/balsara-2016.md) — Legendre-basis reconstructions with smoothness indicators as sums of perfect squares (r = 3–9), and WENO-AO: a large centred high-order polynomial nonlinearly hybridized with the r = 3 central WENO, recursively (AO(7,5,3), AO(9,5,3)), needing no MP step.
* [Balsara, Käppeli, Boscheri & Dumbser (2023) — Curl constraint-preserving reconstruction and the guidance it gives for mimetic scheme design](/sources/balsara-2023.md) — Edge-centred curl-preserving (ECCP) reconstruction for PDEs whose involution keeps a vector field curl-free or curl-constrained: tangential components on edges, a vertex potential upwinded by a multidimensional Riemann solver, closed-form reconstructions from 1D WENO edge moments (2D orders 1–4, 3D orders 1–3), a first-order upwinding argument, von Neumann CFL limits, and tests on a toy curl system and GPR heat conduction.
* [Balsara, Rumpf, Dumbser & Munz (2009) — Efficient, high accuracy ADER-WENO schemes for hydrodynamics and divergence-free magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2009.md) — Structured-mesh finite-volume ADER-WENO schemes to fourth order for Euler and divergence-free MHD: modal Legendre WENO with a dominant central stencil and cheap cross-term stencils, divergence-free reconstruction of face fields, and the continuous-Galerkin ADER-CG predictor, with point-by-point implementation recipes.
* [Barrio Sanchez, Almgren, Bell, Henry de Frahan & Zhang (2024) — A new re-redistribution scheme for weighted state redistribution with adaptive mesh refinement](/sources/barriosanchez-2024.md) — Shows why cut-cell redistribution done level by level breaks conservation when the embedded boundary crosses a coarse–fine interface, and repairs it with a register built from the sparse matrix form of weighted state redistribution, merged with the reflux register and spread over uncovered coarse neighbours; conservation is observed to round-off, but tiny coarse cut cells at the interface still fail.
* [Başkaya, Capriati, Turchi, Magin & Hickel (2024) — Assessment of immersed boundary methods for hypersonic flows with gas–surface interactions](/sources/baskaya-2024.md) — Compares a strictly conservative cut-cell immersed boundary (INCA), two ghost-cell immersed boundaries and a body-fitted reference on reacting hypersonic flows: all agree on adiabatic walls and surface pressure, but on a Mach 8.98 cylinder with a 300 K wall the ghost-cell methods miss the stagnation heat flux (0.167 against 7.19 MW/m²) and the ablation blowing rate, which the authors trace to wall conservation errors rather than reconstruction order.
* [Berger & Colella (1989) — Local Adaptive Mesh Refinement for Shock Hydrodynamics](/sources/berger-1989.md) — The block-structured AMR algorithm for 2D conservation laws: properly nested, grid-aligned rectangular patches refined by an even ratio in space and time, coupled conservatively by averaging down and a flux-register correction of the coarse cells next to fine grids, with bilinear-in-space and linear-in-time ghost interpolation, Richardson-extrapolation error estimation, bisection-and-merge clustering, an unchanged second-order Godunov integrator, a warning about strong shocks crossing grid interfaces, and oblique shock reflection tests with a timing breakdown.
* [Berger & Giuliani (2021) — A state redistribution algorithm for finite volume schemes on cut cell meshes](/sources/berger-2021.md) — Introduces state redistribution: after a provisional full-Δt update, small cut cells are merged into overlapping neighbourhoods whose overlap-weighted averages and limited linear reconstructions are averaged back, which is exactly conservative and linearity-preserving (proved) though not monotone; second order in the volume and about 1.4–1.5 on the boundary, robust down to volume fractions of 3e-7.
* [Berger et al. (2003) — h-box methods for the approximation of hyperbolic conservation laws on irregular grids](/sources/berger-2003.md) — Systematic study of h-box finite-volume methods on grids with cells much smaller than a reference length h: interface fluxes of a small cell come from Riemann problems between states averaged over boxes of length h, so the step follows h; linear-interpolation h-box values with a second-order correction built on h give a proved second-order scheme for 1D advection with one small cell (supraconvergence), observed second order on irregular 1D grids and a 2D nonuniform tensor grid, a transonic-rarefaction defect cured by Lax–Friedrichs fluxes; no cut cells and no embedded boundary.
* [Bernardini, Modesti, Salvadore & Pirozzoli (2021) — STREAmS: a high-fidelity accelerated solver for direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulent flows](/sources/bernardini-2021.md) — Code paper for STREAmS, a CUDA Fortran finite-difference DNS solver for supersonic wall-bounded flows: kinetic-energy-preserving central fluxes of order 2–8 hybridized with characteristic WENO by a Ducros-type sensor, low-storage RK3, a staggered wall, GPU optimizations giving 2.2× on one V100 and 97% weak scaling on 1024 GPUs.
* [Berta, Mignone, Bugli & Mattia (2024) — A 4th-order accurate finite volume method for ideal classical and special relativistic MHD based on pointwise reconstructions](/sources/berta-2024.md) — Fourth-order finite volume with constrained transport for classical and relativistic MHD in PLUTO: averages converted to point values by the Δ/24 Laplacian, MP5 or WENO-Z interpolation of point values with new linear weights, one Riemann problem per face, fourth-order upwind CT, eSSPRK(5,4), and detector-driven order reduction; exactly fourth order on Alfvén waves.
* [Bhole et al. (2023) — Stabilized bi-cubic Hermite–Bézier finite element method for gas–plasma interactions in tokamaks](/sources/bhole-2023.md) — Stabilizes the C1 bi-cubic Hermite–Bézier (× Fourier) Galerkin method of the JOREK MHD code with two terms: a VMS term with a diagonal time scale h_e/λ_max and the residual cut to its advective part (a per-variable streamline diffusion), and a field-aligned artificial viscosity driven by a pressure-gradient sensor times a pressure-rate shock strength; in implicit full-MHD simulations of shattered-pellet injection into a JET-like plasma the viscosity prevents GMRES failure and cuts the solver time to 1.5 ms from 13.6 h to 5 h, while too large a coefficient alters the thermal quench.
* [Borges, Carmona, Costa & Don (2008) — An improved weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/borges-2008.md) — WENO-Z: weights from a global fifth-order indicator τ₅ = |β₀ − β₂|, fourth order at first-order critical points (fifth with q = 2), and the argument that WENO-M and WENO-Z beat WENO-JS at shocks through larger weights on discontinuous stencils, at WENO-JS cost.
* [Brackbill & Barnes (1980) — The Effect of Nonzero ∇ · B on the Numerical Solution of the Magnetohydrodynamic Equations](/sources/brackbill-1980.md) — A short note showing that with a nonzero discrete divergence the conservative MHD momentum equation carries a force along B, whose dot product with B is |B|² ∇·B, which corrupts low-speed flows; it names the Poisson projection in one sentence but proposes and tests the nonconservative (curl B × B) momentum equation with conservative induction and energy instead.
* [Brahmi, Hadjadj, Soni & Chaudhuri (2020) — Analysis of shock-wave diffraction over double concave cylindrical wedges. Part I: Shock dynamics](/sources/brahmi-2020.md) — Viscous simulations (WENO5, RK3, adaptive multiresolution, ray-traced immersed boundary) of a shock over two concave arcs at M_s 1.6–4.5: a five-grid study of the regular-to-Mach transition angle, transition angles rising with M_s and larger on the second arc at M_s 3.5 and 4.5, a single-to-double triple-point sequence, and an empirical time scaling that collapses shock attenuation; no experiment.
* [Brahmi, Hadjadj, Soni & Chaudhuri (2020b) — Analysis of shock-wave diffraction over double cylindrical wedges. Part II: Vorticity generation](/sources/brahmi-2020-b.md) — Part II of the double-concave-arc study (same code, grids and M_s 1.6–4.5): grid-insensitive circulation growing linearly in time with M_s-dependent rate plateaus, a term-by-term vorticity balance in which viscous diffusion outweighs baroclinicity for M_s ≤ 2.5 and the reverse for M_s ≥ 3.5, and up to 90% dynamic-impulse attenuation by the first arc; no experiment.
* [Brio & Wu (1988) — An upwind differencing scheme for the equations of ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/brio-1988.md) — The first Roe-type upwind scheme for 1D ideal MHD: an exact Roe matrix for γ = 2 and an arithmetic-mean Jacobian otherwise, renormalized eigenvectors that stay complete where wave speeds coincide, the coplanar Riemann problem now called the Brio–Wu shock tube, and the proof that ideal MHD is nonconvex, so compound waves exist.
* [Bryson & Gross (1961) — Diffraction of strong shocks by cones, cylinders, and spheres](/sources/bryson-1961.md) — Schlieren experiments in an air shock tube on strong plane shocks passing cones, a circular cylinder and a sphere, with the measured triple-point loci compared with Whitham's shock-dynamics theory extended to blunt bodies; the experimental origin of the shock-reflection-from-a-cylinder benchmark, with regular reflection first and Mach reflection from 40°–50° on the cylinder.
* [Castro, Costa & Don (2011) — High order weighted essentially non-oscillatory WENO-Z schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/castro-2011.md) — Extends WENO-Z to all odd orders with a closed-form global indicator and an optimal-order variant, shows that the power q (not the mapping) restores order at critical points for WENO-Z only, and ranks WENO-JS, WENO-Z, WENO-M by dissipation.
* [Chen, Tóth & Gombosi (2016) — A fifth-order finite difference scheme for hyperbolic equations on block-adaptive curvilinear grids](/sources/chen-2016.md) — Fifth-order alternative-form finite differences with MP5-limited primitive variables, any Riemann solver and an unlimited flux correction built from cell-centre fluxes (three ghost layers), free-stream preserving on curvilinear grids through conservative metrics, on factor-2 block AMR with high-order limited ghost-cell interpolation and no refluxing.
* [Christlieb, Feng, Seal & Tang (2016) — A high-order positivity-preserving single-stage single-step method for the ideal magnetohydrodynamic equations](/sources/christlieb-2016.md) — Single-stage single-step FD-WENO for ideal MHD: Picard-integral (Taylor) time stepping of fluxes, unstaggered vector-potential constrained transport evolved with a Lax–Wendroff Hamilton–Jacobi WENO scheme, and a parameterized positivity flux limiter.
* [Christlieb, Rossmanith & Tang (2014) — Finite difference weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes with constrained transport for ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/christlieb-2014.md) — The original unstaggered constrained-transport FD-WENO scheme for 2D and 3D ideal MHD: Jiang–Wu FD-WENO for the MHD state, Hamilton–Jacobi WENO for the Weyl-gauge vector potential with a gated artificial resistivity in 3D, a fourth-order central curl in every stage of a 10-stage SSP-RK4 at CFL 3, and exact total-energy conservation; fourth order on Alfvén waves and Orszag–Tang stable to t = 30.
* [Cockburn & Shu (1989) — TVB Runge-Kutta Local Projection Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Method for Conservation Laws II: General Framework](/sources/cockburn-1989.md) — Part II of the RKDG series: the 1D scalar framework for any degree K (moments, monotone flux, MUSCL-type mean equation), the local projection limiter on the two face deviations with Shu's TVB correction and a rule for M, rebuilding of the higher moments, TVD Runge–Kutta time stepping, proofs of TVDM/TVBM, TVB and subsequence convergence, entropy variants, linear stability for K = 1, 2, inflow/outflow boundaries, and third-order Burgers and nonconvex tests.
* [Cockburn & Shu (1998) — The Runge–Kutta discontinuous Galerkin method for conservation laws V: multidimensional systems](/sources/cockburn-1998.md) — Extends RKDG to multidimensional nonlinear systems (2D Euler) on rectangles and triangles: P^1/P^2 DG with Lax–Friedrichs fluxes and quadrature-exactness conditions, SSP Runge–Kutta of order k+1 with a limiter after every stage, and a practical TVB minmod slope limiter applied to the linear part in local characteristic variables (M = 50); double Mach reflection, forward step and shock diffraction.
* [Colella & Woodward (1984) — The piecewise parabolic method (PPM) for gas-dynamical simulations](/sources/colella-1984.md) — PPM: fourth-order edge interpolation with monotonized parabolas, Lagrangian and single-step Eulerian Riemann states from characteristic domains of dependence, contact steepening, shock flattening and extra dissipation for slow strong shocks.
* [Colella (1990) — Multidimensional upwind methods for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/colella-1990.md) — Second-order conservative unsplit Godunov schemes in 2D: corner transport upwind for advection (Courant limit max ≤ 1, against the sum form of donor cell), a predictor–corrector for systems with characteristic limiting, upwind tracing and a transverse correction from transverse Riemann fluxes, extensions to quadrilateral grids and gas dynamics, and the 12- or 6-solve 3D variants.
* [Dai & Woodward (1994) — An approximate Riemann solver for ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/dai-1994.md) — A nonlinear Riemann solver for the full seven-wave 1D ideal-MHD system in Lagrangian form, treating every wave as a discontinuity (rarefactions as entropy-decreasing rarefaction shocks) and closing the fan by Newton iteration; it finds a regular solution of the Brio–Wu states without a compound wave and concludes that the MHD Riemann problem is not unique.
* [Dai & Woodward (1998) — On the divergence-free condition and conservation laws in numerical simulations for supersonic magnetohydrodynamic flows](/sources/dai-1998.md) — Face-averaged magnetic field with cell-averaged fluid variables in a Godunov MHD scheme: an exact face update by line- and time-averaged edge values of v×B keeps the net flux through every cell constant while the fluid update stays in conservation form; an operator form for split or unsplit integrators, a critique of projection for supersonic flows, and same-scheme runs with and without the face update that differ little, from which the authors conclude that conservation, not an exactly divergence-free cell-centred field, is what matters.
* [Dedner et al. (2002) — Hyperbolic Divergence Cleaning for the MHD Equations](/sources/dedner-2002.md) — The origin of GLM divergence cleaning for ideal MHD: a scalar coupled to the induction equation through a linear operator gives elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic or mixed cleaning; the mixed form stays conservative except for the damping source, has the cleaning waves ±c_h, a time-step-tied c_h, an exact 2×2 face Riemann solve and an exact damping step with c_p²/c_h = 0.18; the non-conservative EGLM and Galilean-invariant variants lose conservation, and mixed GLM is recommended.
* [Deng, Boivin & Xiao (2019) — A new formulation for two-wave Riemann solver accurate at contact interfaces](/sources/deng-2019-b.md) — Introduces HLL-BVD: the HLL flux keeps its two-wave fan and wave speeds, but the state-difference part of its dissipation is evaluated with the face density pair of smallest jump, chosen by boundary variation diminishing between MUSCL and THINC (tanh) profiles; at second order it resolves contacts more sharply than HLL and, in most tests, HLLC, without the odd–even decoupling, Sedov axis oscillations and kinked Mach stem HLLC shows; robustness is observed, not proved.
* [Derigs et al. (2018) — Ideal GLM-MHD: about the entropy consistent nine-wave magnetic field divergence diminishing ideal MHD equations](/sources/derigs-2018.md) — Derives the Godunov–Powell non-conservative terms as physics, adds the cleaning field through a Galilean-invariant ansatz with its energy in the total energy, proves the resulting nine-wave system entropy-consistent while earlier GLM variants are not, and builds entropy-conservative and entropy-stable fluxes, tested in FLASH against staggered CT.
* [DeVore (1991) — Flux-Corrected Transport Techniques for Multidimensional Compressible Magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/devore-1991.md) — Flux-corrected transport for the MHD induction equation on a staggered collocation (normal fields on cell faces, v×B on edges) that keeps the discrete Gauss law exactly; Zalesak's limiter applied to the edge flux is monotone but too diffusive, so the antidiffusive edge flux is split into the parts driving each field component and each is limited separately, which keeps monotonicity only approximately; tests compare FCT field, FCT vector-potential and Evans–Hawley CT solvers.
* [Ding & Wu (2024) — A new discretely divergence-free positivity-preserving high-order finite volume method for ideal MHD equations](/sources/ding-2024.md) — A provably positivity-preserving high-order FV-WENO method for 2D ideal MHD: a minimum-norm, order-preserving projection makes the interface traces discretely divergence-free, an interface discretization of the Godunov–Powell source term and a point-value Zhang–Shu-type limiter complete it, and geometric quasilinearization proves positivity of the cell averages.
* [Ding, Shu & Zhang (2020) — On the conservation of finite difference WENO schemes in non-rectangular domains using the inverse Lax-Wendroff boundary treatments](/sources/ding-2020.md) — Finite-difference WENO with physical boundaries conserves a total mass that is only second-order accurate; redefining it with Mock–Lax boundary quadrature weights and modifying one inflow flux per grid line in the final Runge–Kutta stage makes ILW schemes conservative to round-off without losing fifth order.
* [Dumbser & Balsara (2016) — A new efficient formulation of the HLLEM Riemann solver for general conservative and non-conservative hyperbolic systems](/sources/dumbser-2016.md) — Generalizes Einfeldt's HLLEM to any conservative or path-conservative hyperbolic system: a linear sub-structure along chosen intermediate fields subtracts an anti-diffusive term with diagonal weight derived from Einfeldt's stability condition (alias HLLI); one eigen-decomposition per face, complete when all intermediate fields are included, exactly well balanced for shallow water; tested in 1D at second order on eight systems.
* [Dumbser & Käser (2007) — Arbitrary high order non-oscillatory finite volume schemes on unstructured meshes for linear hyperbolic systems](/sources/dumbser-2007.md) — ADER finite-volume schemes of arbitrary order on triangles and tetrahedra, built on a reference-element least-squares reconstruction and a WENO that blends whole polynomials of full degree with a dominant central linear weight, so that no negative linear weights arise and one reconstruction per step suffices.
* [Dumbser et al. (2008) — A unified framework for the construction of one-step finite volume and discontinuous Galerkin schemes on unstructured meshes](/sources/dumbser-2008.md) — Defines the PNPM family — data of degree N, a constrained least-squares reconstruction of degree M ≥ N, finite volume for N = 0 and DG for N = M — with a nodal, continuous-in-time local space–time Galerkin predictor solved by Picard iteration and a quadrature-free one-step update; the 1D linear CFL limit depends on N only (Table 1), the intermediate schemes are the most efficient on smooth Euler, and orders up to six are shown on triangles and tetrahedra, with WENO shock tests for MHD, RMHD and elasticity.
* [Dumbser, Zanotti, Hidalgo & Balsara (2013) — ADER-WENO finite volume schemes with space–time adaptive mesh refinement](/sources/dumbser-2013.md) — The first better-than-second-order one-step ADER-WENO finite-volume scheme on cell-by-cell, space–time adaptive Cartesian meshes with time-accurate local time stepping and MPI, using a dimension-by-dimension polynomial WENO and a local space–time DG predictor; tested on Euler and GLM-MHD problems.
* [Dumbser, Zanotti, Loubère & Diot (2014) — A posteriori subcell limiting of the discontinuous Galerkin finite element method for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/dumbser-2014.md) — Extends the a posteriori MOOD paradigm to ADER-DG: an unlimited candidate is tested for positivity and a relaxed polynomial discrete maximum principle, and failed cells are recomputed from the previous time level by third-order ADER-WENO on 2N+1 subcells per direction, which matches the DG time step and keeps subcell resolution up to tenth order.
* [Edelmann et al. (2021) — Well-balanced treatment of gravity in astrophysical fluid dynamics simulations at low Mach numbers](/sources/edelmann-2021.md) — Compares three well-balancing methods (Cargo–LeRoux extended to multi-D, α-β, deviation) with a low-Mach AUSM+-up flux in a second-order implicit finite-volume code over long 2D runs: α-β and deviation preserve hydrostatic states to machine precision and far beat Cargo–LeRoux, only the deviation method balances moving equilibria, and well-balanced runs with low-Mach fluxes can still grow perturbations in stable stratifications.
* [Einfeldt (1988) — On Godunov-type methods for gas dynamics](/sources/einfeldt-1988.md) — HLL for the 1D Euler equations with speeds that need not bound the physical signal speeds but must satisfy a stability condition (the clipped speeds bracket the Roe eigenvalues); a contact anti-diffusion term inside the fan (the later HLLEM) that with Roe-eigenvalue speeds reproduces Roe's flux; two speed algorithms, the Roe eigenvalues (HLLR) and a sqrt(rho)-weighted sound speed (HELL); a min/max entropy modification at sonic rarefactions; no positivity result.
* [Einfeldt et al. (1991) — On Godunov-Type Methods near Low Densities](/sources/einfeldt-1991.md) — Defines positively conservative schemes, proves that Godunov's method and the HLLE scheme are positively conservative by a convex-averaging argument, proves that no Godunov-type scheme built on a linearized Riemann solution is (symmetric double rarefaction, with and without shear), gives the HLLE wave speeds that bound the physical signal speeds and a sharper set with an algebraic positivity proof, and recasts Roe's scheme as HLLEM with Roe-eigenvalue speeds; first-order tests only.
* [Evans & Hawley (1988) — Simulation of magnetohydrodynamic flows: a constrained transport method](/sources/evans-1988.md) — The origin of constrained transport: the induction equation in integral (Stokes) form for the flux through a moving surface, face-averaged field components on a mesh staggered against the hydrodynamic faces, edge electromotive forces computed once and used with opposite signs in the two faces that share them, so the total flux out of every cell keeps its initial value to round-off, in any coordinates, in 3D and on a moving mesh; tested in 2D axisymmetric black-hole accretion against vector-potential codes.
* [Falcovitz, Alfandary & Hanoch (1997) — A two-dimensional conservation laws scheme for compressible flows with moving boundaries](/sources/falcovitz-1997.md) — Moving boundary tracking (MBT): a dimensionally split GRP scheme on a fixed Cartesian grid with conservative, time-changing cut cells whose old and new volumes, time-averaged face apertures and projected boundary areas come from a trapezoid decomposition rule, so that uniform flow moving with the body is exact; first order in boundary cells, small cells merged; origin of the shock-driven cylinder lift-off test.
* [Fisher, Carpenter, Yamaleev & Frankel (2011) — Boundary closures for fourth-order energy stable weighted essentially non-oscillatory finite-difference schemes](/sources/fisher-2011.md) — Boundary closures for the fourth-order energy-stable WENO (ESWENO) scheme that satisfy summation by parts, keep WENO stencil biasing up to the boundary on non-uniform flux points fixed by the norm, and give provable L2 stability with global third (diagonal norm) or fourth (block norm) order.
* [Fu, Hu & Adams (2016) — A family of high-order targeted ENO schemes for compressible-fluid simulations](/sources/fu-2016.md) — TENO: incremental-width candidate stencils, a strongly scale-separating smoothness measure and a 0/1 ENO-like cutoff that keeps stencils at their linear weights, on spectrally optimized background schemes; fifth- and sixth-order members with a single parameter set.
* [Giuliani, Almgren, Bell, Berger, Henry de Frahan & Rangarajan (2022) — A weighted state redistribution algorithm for embedded boundary grids](/sources/giuliani-2022.md) — Extends state redistribution to 3D embedded-boundary grids in AMReX and introduces a weighted variant in which a small cell keeps a share of its own provisional state, so redistribution switches on smoothly; any redistribution matrix with unit column sums is conservative; second order in the volume and about 1.4 on the boundary, run in PeleC and MFIX-Exa at about 2% of the cost.

* [Simon & Mandal (2019) — A simple cure for numerical shock instability in the HLLC Riemann solver](/sources/simon-2019.md) — Writes HLLC as HLL plus an anti-diffusive term and widens the HLL wave speeds only inside the dissipation, with an eigenvalue or pressure sensor (HLLC-SWM): contacts and shear survive, a saw-tooth analysis and a matrix stability analysis of a steady shock fix the coefficient at 3.5, and the carbuncle, odd–even decoupling and kinked Mach stem disappear in first- and second-order tests.
* [Simon & Mandal (2019b) — Strategies to cure numerical shock instability in the HLLEM Riemann solver](/sources/simon-2019-b.md) — Shows by scale and saw-tooth perturbation analysis that HLLEM's anti-diffusion on faces transverse to a strong shock cancels the HLL damping of density and normal velocity, and cures it two ways — widened HLL speeds on all faces near the shock (HLLEM-SWM) or sensor-scaled anti-diffusion on transverse faces (HLLEM-ADC) — while showing that shear-only control (HLLEMS) leaves density undamped; first- and second-order tests only.
* [Sod (1978) — A survey of several finite difference methods for systems of nonlinear hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/sod-1978.md) — Review that runs the 1D Euler methods of 1977 (Godunov, Lax–Wendroff, MacCormack, Rusanov, upwind, Glimm random choice, artificial compression, the Harten–Zwas hybrid, Boris–Book antidiffusion, Hyman's predictor–corrector) on one shock tube, the origin of Sod's problem, and compares contact and shock widths in zones and run times; it prints the initial states but not the diaphragm position or the end time.
* [Suresh & Huynh (1997) — Accurate monotonicity-preserving schemes with Runge–Kutta time stepping](/sources/suresh-1997.md) — MP5: a fifth-order five-point interface value limited by a geometric monotonicity-preserving bound designed for RK3, with intervals enlarged near smooth extrema to keep accuracy and a cheap test that skips limiting in smooth cells.
* [Titarev & Toro (2004) — Finite-volume WENO schemes for three-dimensional conservation laws](/sources/titarev-2004.md) — Extends finite-volume WENO to 3D and to fifth and seventh order by dimension-by-dimension sweeps to two-point Gauss quadratures (with the needed Gauss-point linear weights), and replaces the Rusanov flux by HLLC and the Riemann-solver-free MUSTA flux.
* [Tomida et al. (2026) — Systematic comparison between constrained transport and mixed divergence cleaning methods for astrophysical MHD simulations](/sources/tomida-2026.md) — Same-code (Athena++) comparison of Stone–Gardiner CT and Dedner mixed GLM cleaning: the cleaning is derived under constant, uniform parameters, a time-step-tied cleaning speed makes spurious divergent fields when the step drops, localized fields leak at any resolution, AMR breaks the assumptions, and EGLM's energy term matters at low β; CT is judged more robust.
* [Toro (1997) — Riemann Solvers and Numerical Methods for Fluid Dynamics: A Practical Introduction](/sources/toro-1997.md) — Textbook (1st edition) on Godunov-type methods for the Euler equations: a complete exact Riemann solver with vacuum cases, Godunov's method, and approximate-state, HLL/HLLC, Roe and Osher solvers compared on one set of 1D Riemann problems that later papers reuse as Toro's tests; read in scope (Chs. 4, 6, 9–12, §§17.1–17.2).
* [Toro (2009) — Riemann Solvers and Numerical Methods for Fluid Dynamics: A Practical Introduction](/sources/toro-2009.md) — Textbook (3rd edition), read in scope (Chs. 10, 18–20): the revised HLL/HLLC chapter, with the middle speed derived inside HLLC, two HLLC variants and PVRS-based speeds as default; centred FORCE fluxes extended to multi-D Cartesian and unstructured meshes with monotonicity limits; the generalized Riemann problem GRP_K and its Toro–Titarev solver; and the ADER finite-volume approach built on it.
* [Toro et al. (1994) — Restoration of the contact surface in the HLL-Riemann solver](/sources/toro-1994.md) — The original HLLC paper: a middle wave of speed S_M equal to the star normal velocity, two star states from the integral conservation law across S_L and across S_R with their pressures computed separately, star fluxes for the supersonic cases, three families of wave-speed estimates (isentropic, linearized, hybrid pressure-based) and an iterative revision, all inside the second-order WAF method; Sod, sonic Sod, Woodward–Colella blast waves, a covolume shock tube and a Mach 1.7 wedge reflection.
* [Tsai, Huang, Tsai & Yang (2012) — Unsteady relativistic shock-wave diffraction by cylinders and spheres](/sources/tsai-2012.md) — A special-relativistic kinetic beam scheme (Steger–Warming-form flux-vector splitting of a seven-beam Jüttner approximation) on a body-fitted O-grid, with TVD/ENO flux corrections, Strang splitting and Riemann-invariant wall conditions, run on a planar shock over a cylinder and a sphere at M_s = 1.2, 1.5, 1.7 and, non-relativistically, at M_s = 2.81 against the Bryson–Gross schlieren; all results qualitative.
* [Tóth (2000) — The ∇·B = 0 Constraint in Shock-Capturing Magnetohydrodynamics Codes](/sources/toth-2000.md) — Seven ways of keeping ∇·B = 0 (Powell's eight-wave scheme, three constrained-transport schemes, two new central-difference schemes, projection) added to one base scheme in one code and compared quantitatively: CT schemes are cell-centred finite-volume schemes that conserve a corner divergence, projection is the minimal correction and is consistent, no conservative scheme can keep the discrete Lorentz acceleration exactly orthogonal to B, the eight-wave scheme can converge to wrong jumps, and projection, field-CD and flux-CT are the most accurate.
* [van Leer & Nomura (2005) — Discontinuous Galerkin for diffusion](/sources/vanleer-2005.md) — Proposes the recovery principle for DG diffusion: at each interface, recover the smooth polynomial on the two adjacent cells with the same Legendre moments as the discrete solution and use it in the twice-integrated weak form; Fourier analysis gives fourth order for K = 1 and an eighth-order good eigenvalue for K = 2 (unconfirmed), recovery lies outside the (σ, μ) interior-penalty family, and one steady 1D test confirms order 4 against order 2 for the Symmetric and Baumann schemes.
* [van Leer (1979) — Towards the ultimate conservative difference scheme. V. A second-order sequel to Godunov's method](/sources/vanleer-1979.md) — MUSCL: a second-order extension of Godunov's method for ideal compressible flow, as a Lagrangean step on piecewise-linear slabs with independently stored slopes, an iterated interface Riemann problem with one-step characteristic time-centring, and a least-squares remap, with slope-limiting monotonicity algorithms; tested on Sod's shock tube and a Mach 3 wind tunnel with a step.
* [Wang, Zeng, Deiterding, Yang & Liang (2025) — An improved high-order adaptive mesh refinement framework for shock-turbulence interaction problems based on cell-centered finite difference schemes](/sources/wang-2025.md) — Fifth-order FD-WENO on block-structured AMR (AMReX) with point values stored at cell centres so that refluxing applies, WENO prolongation and restriction in smooth regions, and a troubled-cell switch to conservative second-order interpolation at shocks, which survives shocks crossing or being regridded across levels where pure WENO interpolation fails.
* [Woodward & Colella (1984) — The numerical simulation of two-dimensional fluid flow with strong shocks](/sources/woodward-1984.md) — Review comparing seven shock-capturing schemes (MacCormack with Lapidus viscosity, BBC, ETBFCT, Godunov, MUSCL, and PPM in Lagrange-plus-remap and single-step Eulerian forms) on the interacting blast waves, a Mach 3 wind tunnel with a step and the double Mach reflection, with exact set-ups, an L1 density error and CRAY-1 times; PPM is the most accurate per zone and per CPU second, and only PPM converges at first order on the blast waves.
* [Xie et al. (2021) — Further studies on numerical instabilities of Godunov-type schemes for strong shocks](/sources/xie-2021.md) — Attributes the instability of a steady shock captured with one interior point to too little entropy production inside the numerical shock, supports it with a modified-equation analysis of HLLEM and 1D/1.5D steady-shock experiments, and turns it into an additive entropy-control term, switched by a pressure-ratio sensor, that stabilizes Godunov, Roe, HLLEM and HLLC without dissipating contacts or shear; first and second order only.
* [Yeom & Chang (2010) — Shock wave diffraction about a wedge in a gas-microdroplet mixture](/sources/yeom-2010.md) — An M_s = 1.7 shock over a 25° wedge into air laden with inert microdroplets, solved with an eight-equation two-fluid model by Toro's weighted-average-flux method on an HLL solver (MINBEE limiter, HLL-weighted interface volume fraction, single- or two-phase wave-speed estimates) with Strang-split RK4 sources; the gas-only limit reproduces a single Mach reflection and an interferogram qualitatively.
* [Zachary et al. (1994) — A higher-order Godunov method for multidimensional ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/zachary-1994.md) — A Strang-split second-order Godunov scheme for ideal MHD in a nonconservative form that keeps the ∇·B terms out of the differentiated fluxes: primitive-variable characteristic tracing with a hybrid characteristic/MUSCL slope, a linearized Engquist–Osher flux summed over sonic points, HLLE and Rusanov fallbacks at strong rarefactions and transonic degeneracies, and an optional end-of-step projection that made no noticeable difference in 2D.
* [Zaghi (2026) — ADAM FLUME MHD kernels: the conservative magnetic flux and the EGLM sources](/sources/zaghi-2026.md) — Frozen capture of three source files of the ADAM code's FLUME MHD solver: a conservative Maxwell-stress momentum flux, plain GLM with only a damping source, and EGLM, which adds the momentum source −(∇·B)B so that flux plus source give (∇×B)×B; implicit, hypothesis-grade evidence on the sign of the parallel force.
* [Zanotti, Fambri, Dumbser & Hidalgo (2015) — Space–time adaptive ADER discontinuous Galerkin finite element schemes with a posteriori sub-cell finite volume limiting](/sources/zanotti-2015.md) — Combines ADER-DG with the a posteriori ADER-WENO subcell limiter on cell-by-cell space–time adaptive Cartesian meshes, extending projection and averaging to the subcell data, and shows design order to N = 8 and very low dissipation on Euler and GLM-MHD problems in 2D and 3D.
* [Zhang & Shu (2010) — On positivity-preserving high order discontinuous Galerkin schemes for compressible Euler equations on rectangular meshes](/sources/zhang-2010.md) — A sufficient condition — admissible Gauss–Lobatto point values plus a CFL bound — for positivity of cell averages of any-order FV or DG schemes for Euler, enforced by a conservative, accuracy-preserving linear scaling limiter; extended to rectangular meshes and tested with third-order RKDG.
* [Zhang & Shu (2012) — Positivity-preserving high order finite difference WENO schemes for compressible Euler equations](/sources/zhang-2012.md) — Extends the Zhang–Shu positivity limiter to finite-difference WENO: with a global Lax–Friedrichs splitting the FD scheme is two FV WENO schemes for the split fluxes, whose reconstructed values are limited; includes local splitting, source terms, general equations of state and multi-D.
* [Zhang (2017) — On positivity-preserving high order discontinuous Galerkin schemes for compressible Navier–Stokes equations](/sources/zhang-2017.md) — First high-order schemes for 2D compressible Navier–Stokes that keep density and internal energy positive while conserving energy: a Lax–Friedrichs-type viscous flux whose penalty is chosen from the stress, heat flux and state, weak-positivity theorems for FV and DG on rectangles and triangles, an efficient scaling limiter, and the finding that resolved viscous flows need no extra oscillation limiter.
* [Zhang, Wang, Hao, Huang, Chen & Shi (2017) — Experimental investigation of shock wave diffraction over a single- or double-sphere model](/sources/zhang-2017-c.md) — Shock-tube measurements of the unsteady drag on one 40 mm sphere and on two spheres side by side behind weak planar shocks (M_s 1.10–1.33) from internal accelerometers: a drag peak near half a diameter of shock travel that falls from 14.48 to 4.52 as M_s rises, a negative phase, and double-sphere peaks always above the single-sphere one, largest when the spheres touch; no simulation.
* [Zhu & Qiu (2016) — A new fifth order finite difference WENO scheme for solving hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/zhu-2016.md) — WENO-ZQ: a quartic on the five-point stencil combined with two linear polynomials through a weight-adjusted subtraction, so arbitrary positive linear weights keep fifth order; new τ-based weights; behaves like a van Albada-limited TVD scheme near discontinuities.
