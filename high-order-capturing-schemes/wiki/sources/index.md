# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Baeza, Mulet & Zorío (2016) — High order weighted extrapolation for boundary conditions for finite difference methods on complex domains with Cartesian meshes](/sources/baeza-2016.md) — Ghost-cell values for fifth-order FD-WENO on Cartesian meshes of complex domains by extrapolation along boundary normals with dimensionless, scale-independent weights that reduce to the nearest value across discontinuities, blended with least squares (WLS-GAW) to remove the mild instability of Lagrange extrapolation.
* [Balsara & Kim (2016) — A subluminal relativistic magnetohydrodynamics scheme with ADER-WENO predictor and multidimensional Riemann solver-based corrector](/sources/balsara-2016-b.md) — One-step second- to fourth-order finite-volume relativistic MHD on a Yee mesh that keeps velocities subluminal: one conserved-to-primitive inversion per cell via the 1/24 average–point identity, WENO or PPM reconstruction of the four-velocity, an ADER predictor in those variables, and a multidimensional HLLC/HLLEM Riemann solver for the edge electric fields.
* [Balsara & Shu (2000) — Monotonicity preserving weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes with increasingly high order of accuracy](/sources/balsara-2000.md) — WENO of orders 7–13 (r = 4–7) with tabulated coefficients and indicators, made robust by the Suresh–Huynh bounds (MPWENO); convergence, flat-extremum degradation, artificial-compression tuning by order, and 1D/2D Euler and MHD tests.
* [Balsara (2004) — Second-order-accurate schemes for magnetohydrodynamics with divergence-free reconstruction](/sources/balsara-2004.md) — Second-order unsplit Runge–Kutta Godunov schemes for MHD on a Yee-staggered mesh: divergence-free reconstruction of the face fields gives a unique zone-centred field, edge electric fields come from four Riemann problems per face without spatial averaging, with TVD, WENO-slope and multidimensional limiters, curvilinear and tetrahedral extensions, and a new MHD vortex test.
* [Balsara (2009b) — Divergence-free reconstruction of magnetic fields and WENO schemes for magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2009-b.md) — Extends divergence-free reconstruction of face-centred magnetic fields to third and fourth order in closed form, fixing free coefficients by minimizing magnetic energy, and builds a Yee-staggered RK-WENO finite-volume MHD scheme that reaches design order to fourth on a magnetized vortex and a 3D torsional Alfvén wave.
* [Balsara, Bhoriya & Shu (2026) — An alternative finite difference WENO-like scheme with physical constraint preservation for divergence-preserving hyperbolic systems](/sources/balsara-2026.md) — Extends AFD-WENO to systems with a divergence involution (electrodynamics, MHD, relativistic MHD): cell variables by AFD-WENO, face-averaged normal fields updated FV-style by edge electric fields from a multidimensional LLF/HLL Riemann solver, plus a time-explicit PCP hybridization; orders up to nine.
* [Balsara, Bhoriya, Shu & Kumar (2025) — Efficient alternative finite difference WENO schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/balsara-2025.md) — Makes the alternative finite-difference WENO formulation general-purpose up to ninth order: a derivation of the even-derivative flux corrections, WENO-AO pointwise interpolation formulas, and a boundary-centred WENO interpolation of A∂u that suppresses the corrections at shocks, with any Riemann solver at the cost of classical FD-WENO.
* [Balsara, Garain & Shu (2016) — An efficient class of WENO schemes with adaptive order](/sources/balsara-2016.md) — Legendre-basis reconstructions with smoothness indicators as sums of perfect squares (r = 3–9), and WENO-AO: a large centred high-order polynomial nonlinearly hybridized with the r = 3 central WENO, recursively (AO(7,5,3), AO(9,5,3)), needing no MP step.
* [Balsara, Käppeli, Boscheri & Dumbser (2023) — Curl constraint-preserving reconstruction and the guidance it gives for mimetic scheme design](/sources/balsara-2023.md) — Edge-centred curl-preserving (ECCP) reconstruction for PDEs whose involution keeps a vector field curl-free or curl-constrained: tangential components on edges, a vertex potential upwinded by a multidimensional Riemann solver, closed-form reconstructions from 1D WENO edge moments (2D orders 1–4, 3D orders 1–3), a first-order upwinding argument, von Neumann CFL limits, and tests on a toy curl system and GPR heat conduction.
* [Balsara, Rumpf, Dumbser & Munz (2009) — Efficient, high accuracy ADER-WENO schemes for hydrodynamics and divergence-free magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/balsara-2009.md) — Structured-mesh finite-volume ADER-WENO schemes to fourth order for Euler and divergence-free MHD: modal Legendre WENO with a dominant central stencil and cheap cross-term stencils, divergence-free reconstruction of face fields, and the continuous-Galerkin ADER-CG predictor, with point-by-point implementation recipes.
* [Bernardini, Modesti, Salvadore & Pirozzoli (2021) — STREAmS: a high-fidelity accelerated solver for direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulent flows](/sources/bernardini-2021.md) — Code paper for STREAmS, a CUDA Fortran finite-difference DNS solver for supersonic wall-bounded flows: kinetic-energy-preserving central fluxes of order 2–8 hybridized with characteristic WENO by a Ducros-type sensor, low-storage RK3, a staggered wall, GPU optimizations giving 2.2× on one V100 and 97% weak scaling on 1024 GPUs.
* [Berta, Mignone, Bugli & Mattia (2024) — A 4th-order accurate finite volume method for ideal classical and special relativistic MHD based on pointwise reconstructions](/sources/berta-2024.md) — Fourth-order finite volume with constrained transport for classical and relativistic MHD in PLUTO: averages converted to point values by the Δ/24 Laplacian, MP5 or WENO-Z interpolation of point values with new linear weights, one Riemann problem per face, fourth-order upwind CT, eSSPRK(5,4), and detector-driven order reduction; exactly fourth order on Alfvén waves.
* [Borges, Carmona, Costa & Don (2008) — An improved weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/borges-2008.md) — WENO-Z: weights from a global fifth-order indicator τ₅ = |β₀ − β₂|, fourth order at first-order critical points (fifth with q = 2), and the argument that WENO-M and WENO-Z beat WENO-JS at shocks through larger weights on discontinuous stencils, at WENO-JS cost.
* [Castro, Costa & Don (2011) — High order weighted essentially non-oscillatory WENO-Z schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/castro-2011.md) — Extends WENO-Z to all odd orders with a closed-form global indicator and an optimal-order variant, shows that the power q (not the mapping) restores order at critical points for WENO-Z only, and ranks WENO-JS, WENO-Z, WENO-M by dissipation.
* [Chen, Tóth & Gombosi (2016) — A fifth-order finite difference scheme for hyperbolic equations on block-adaptive curvilinear grids](/sources/chen-2016.md) — Fifth-order alternative-form finite differences with MP5-limited primitive variables, any Riemann solver and an unlimited flux correction built from cell-centre fluxes (three ghost layers), free-stream preserving on curvilinear grids through conservative metrics, on factor-2 block AMR with high-order limited ghost-cell interpolation and no refluxing.
* [Christlieb, Feng, Seal & Tang (2016) — A high-order positivity-preserving single-stage single-step method for the ideal magnetohydrodynamic equations](/sources/christlieb-2016.md) — Single-stage single-step FD-WENO for ideal MHD: Picard-integral (Taylor) time stepping of fluxes, unstaggered vector-potential constrained transport evolved with a Lax–Wendroff Hamilton–Jacobi WENO scheme, and a parameterized positivity flux limiter.
* [Christlieb, Rossmanith & Tang (2014) — Finite difference weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes with constrained transport for ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/christlieb-2014.md) — The original unstaggered constrained-transport FD-WENO scheme for 2D and 3D ideal MHD: Jiang–Wu FD-WENO for the MHD state, Hamilton–Jacobi WENO for the Weyl-gauge vector potential with a gated artificial resistivity in 3D, a fourth-order central curl in every stage of a 10-stage SSP-RK4 at CFL 3, and exact total-energy conservation; fourth order on Alfvén waves and Orszag–Tang stable to t = 30.
* [Colella & Woodward (1984) — The piecewise parabolic method (PPM) for gas-dynamical simulations](/sources/colella-1984.md) — PPM: fourth-order edge interpolation with monotonized parabolas, Lagrangian and single-step Eulerian Riemann states from characteristic domains of dependence, contact steepening, shock flattening and extra dissipation for slow strong shocks.
* [Ding & Wu (2024) — A new discretely divergence-free positivity-preserving high-order finite volume method for ideal MHD equations](/sources/ding-2024.md) — A provably positivity-preserving high-order FV-WENO method for 2D ideal MHD: a minimum-norm, order-preserving projection makes the interface traces discretely divergence-free, an interface discretization of the Godunov–Powell source term and a point-value Zhang–Shu-type limiter complete it, and geometric quasilinearization proves positivity of the cell averages.
* [Ding, Shu & Zhang (2020) — On the conservation of finite difference WENO schemes in non-rectangular domains using the inverse Lax-Wendroff boundary treatments](/sources/ding-2020.md) — Finite-difference WENO with physical boundaries conserves a total mass that is only second-order accurate; redefining it with Mock–Lax boundary quadrature weights and modifying one inflow flux per grid line in the final Runge–Kutta stage makes ILW schemes conservative to round-off without losing fifth order.
* [Dumbser & Käser (2007) — Arbitrary high order non-oscillatory finite volume schemes on unstructured meshes for linear hyperbolic systems](/sources/dumbser-2007.md) — ADER finite-volume schemes of arbitrary order on triangles and tetrahedra, built on a reference-element least-squares reconstruction and a WENO that blends whole polynomials of full degree with a dominant central linear weight, so that no negative linear weights arise and one reconstruction per step suffices.
* [Dumbser, Zanotti, Hidalgo & Balsara (2013) — ADER-WENO finite volume schemes with space–time adaptive mesh refinement](/sources/dumbser-2013.md) — The first better-than-second-order one-step ADER-WENO finite-volume scheme on cell-by-cell, space–time adaptive Cartesian meshes with time-accurate local time stepping and MPI, using a dimension-by-dimension polynomial WENO and a local space–time DG predictor; tested on Euler and GLM-MHD problems.
* [Dumbser, Zanotti, Loubère & Diot (2014) — A posteriori subcell limiting of the discontinuous Galerkin finite element method for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/dumbser-2014.md) — Extends the a posteriori MOOD paradigm to ADER-DG: an unlimited candidate is tested for positivity and a relaxed polynomial discrete maximum principle, and failed cells are recomputed from the previous time level by third-order ADER-WENO on 2N+1 subcells per direction, which matches the DG time step and keeps subcell resolution up to tenth order.
* [Fisher, Carpenter, Yamaleev & Frankel (2011) — Boundary closures for fourth-order energy stable weighted essentially non-oscillatory finite-difference schemes](/sources/fisher-2011.md) — Boundary closures for the fourth-order energy-stable WENO (ESWENO) scheme that satisfy summation by parts, keep WENO stencil biasing up to the boundary on non-uniform flux points fixed by the norm, and give provable L2 stability with global third (diagonal norm) or fourth (block norm) order.
* [Fu, Hu & Adams (2016) — A family of high-order targeted ENO schemes for compressible-fluid simulations](/sources/fu-2016.md) — TENO: incremental-width candidate stencils, a strongly scale-separating smoothness measure and a 0/1 ENO-like cutoff that keeps stencils at their linear weights, on spectrally optimized background schemes; fifth- and sixth-order members with a single parameter set.
* [Gottlieb, Shu & Tadmor (2001) — Strong stability-preserving high-order time discretization methods](/sources/gottlieb-2001.md) — Review and extension of TVD time discretizations under the new name SSP: optimal linear SSP Runge–Kutta methods of any order with CFL coefficient 1, stability for coercive operators, nonlinear and low-storage SSP Runge–Kutta, SSP multistep methods, and the nonexistence of implicit SSP methods above first order.
* [Guo, Xiong & Shi (2014) — A positivity-preserving high order finite volume compact-WENO scheme for compressible Euler equations](/sources/guo-2014.md) — Fifth-order finite-volume compact-reconstruction WENO: three third-order compact candidates under WENO-Z weights give a weight-dependent tridiagonal system per interface; HLLC with Batten wave speeds and the Zhang–Shu limiter make it positivity-preserving.
* [Harten, Engquist, Osher & Chakravarthy (1987) — Uniformly high order accurate essentially non-oscillatory schemes, III](/sources/harten-1987.md) — The arbitrary-order ENO paper: adaptive-stencil reconstruction from cell averages (via primitive function or deconvolution), the abstract reconstruct–evolve–average scheme with Cauchy–Kowalewski solution in the small, characteristic-wise extension to systems, and Euler tests.
* [Henrick, Aslam & Powers (2005) — Mapped weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes: achieving optimal order near critical points](/sources/henrick-2005.md) — Shows that WENO-JS is only third order at critical points with f′ = 0, f‴ ≠ 0 (a missing order condition, hidden by ε), gives necessary and sufficient weight conditions and the rate formula for orders 3–11, and restores fifth order with a weight mapping (WENO-M).
* [Hu, Adams & Shu (2013) — Positivity-preserving method for high-order conservative schemes solving compressible Euler equations](/sources/hu-2013.md) — A cut-off flux limiter that detects interface fluxes which would make density or pressure negative and blends them with a first-order Lax–Friedrichs flux; positivity holds under CFL 1/2, and order is shown preserved a posteriori under a milder time-step condition than the Zhang–Shu limiter needs.
* [Hu, Wang & Adams (2010) — An adaptive central-upwind weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme](/sources/hu-2010.md) — WENO-CU6: four candidate stencils including a downwind one, linear weights giving the sixth-order central scheme, and WENO-Z-type weights with a large constant and a six-point reference indicator τ₆ that switch smoothly between central and upwind.
* [Jiang & Shu (1996) — Efficient implementation of weighted ENO schemes](/sources/jiang-1996.md) — WENO-JS: the finite-difference flux WENO with optimal linear weights and the L²-derivative smoothness indicator, giving fifth order from third-order ENO stencils; error analysis of Liu–Osher–Chan, convergence for smooth solutions, pressure–entropy weights, and a standard test suite.
* [Jiang & Wu (1999) — A high-order WENO finite difference scheme for the equations of ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/jiang-1999.md) — Fifth-order finite-difference WENO for ideal MHD in 1D–3D, with characteristic decomposition on Brio–Wu normalized eigenvectors (seven- and eight-wave), classical RK4 and a Brackbill–Barnes divergence projection, which the Orszag–Tang vortex shows to be necessary for stability.
* [Levy, Puppo & Russo (1999) — Central WENO schemes for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws](/sources/levy-1999.md) — Third- and fourth-order staggered central schemes with a centred WENO reconstruction (convex combination of three parabolas with symmetric constants) and Runge–Kutta natural continuous extensions; no Riemann solver or characteristic decomposition, global smoothness indicators for systems.
* [Li, Shu & Zhang (2016) — Stability analysis of the inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment for high order upwind-biased finite difference schemes](/sources/li-2016.md) — GKS and eigenvalue-spectrum stability analysis of inverse Lax–Wendroff (inflow) and extrapolation (outflow) boundary treatments for upwind-biased schemes of orders 3–13, the linear prototypes of WENO, semi-discrete and with SSP-RK3: full ILW is stable for any cut-cell distance, and the simplified ILW needs a tabulated minimum number of ILW derivatives.
* [Liu, Cheng, Jiang, Lu, Zhang & Zhang (2022) — Numerical simulation of a complex moving rigid body under the impingement of a shock wave in 3D](/sources/liu-2022.md) — Moving-boundary inverse Lax–Wendroff for 3D rigid bodies that translate and rotate under shock loading, for Euler and Navier–Stokes with third-order FD-WENO: ghost values independent of the choice of local frame (Theorem 1), a material derivative for surface-only functions (Theorem 2), third-order accuracy and robust shock–cylinder and shock–sphere runs.
* [Liu, Jiang, Shu & Zhang (2025) — High-order inverse Lax-Wendroff procedure for compressible fluid-structure interaction problems](/sources/liu-2025.md) — Inverse Lax–Wendroff at a moving fluid–solid interface between third-order FD-WENO (Euler, fixed Cartesian grid) and P² DG (linear elasticity, Lagrangian mesh): the normal derivatives of both media are solved together from outgoing characteristics and time-differentiated interface conditions, which removes the added-mass instability of weak coupling; third order, no sub-iteration.
* [Liu, Jiang, Shu, Zhang & Zhang (2023) — A high order moving boundary treatment for convection-diffusion equations](/sources/liu-2023.md) — Inverse Lax–Wendroff for Dirichlet boundaries moving across a Cartesian grid, for convection–diffusion equations, systems and Navier–Stokes with a flow-driven rigid body: material derivatives give the relative speed f′(g) − ȧ, regularized blending weights avoid every zero denominator from pure convection to pure diffusion, and Runge–Kutta stage values use extrapolated mixed derivatives; third order.
* [Liu, Osher & Chan (1994) — Weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes](/sources/liu-1994.md) — The first WENO scheme: a convex combination of all r ENO candidate polynomials with weights from smoothness indicators, cell-average form with TVD Runge–Kutta, one order above the base ENO scheme (r+1).
* [Lu, Fang, Tan, Shu & Zhang (2016) — Inverse Lax–Wendroff procedure for numerical boundary conditions of convection–diffusion equations](/sources/lu-2016.md) — Extends inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment from hyperbolic to convection–diffusion equations, including the incompletely parabolic compressible Navier–Stokes system, by blending a convection-dominated and a diffusion-dominated ILW treatment with a mesh- and viscosity-dependent weight; third order and stable for cut cells at any Reynolds number tested.
* [Lu, Shu, Tan & Zhang (2021) — An inverse Lax-Wendroff procedure for hyperbolic conservation laws with changing wind direction on the boundary](/sources/lu-2021.md) — Inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment that expands the ghost-point solution and flux separately, taking only the first flux derivative from the PDE, so no eigenvalue is ever divided by and boundaries can switch between inflow and outflow; with a multi-resolution WENO extrapolation with constant linear weights; fifth order in 1D and 2D.
* [Martín, Taylor, Wu & Weirs (2006) — A bandwidth-optimized WENO scheme for the effective direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulence](/sources/martin-2006.md) — WENO-SYM: a downwind candidate stencil makes the set symmetric; the optimal weights are order-optimized (WENO-SYMOO, non-dissipative, unstable at contacts) or bandwidth-optimized (WENO-SYMBO); validated on canonical problems, isotropic turbulence and a supersonic boundary layer.
* [McCorquodale & Colella (2011) — A high-order finite-volume method for conservation laws on locally refined grids](/sources/mccorquodale-2011.md) — Fourth-order finite volume in space and time on Berger–Colella AMR hierarchies with subcycling: average–point conversions with h²/24 corrections, a modified Colella–Sekora PPM limiter, classical RK4, conservative least-squares interpolation, refluxing of the RK4 total flux, and coarse–fine ghost values that match the fine Runge–Kutta stages, without which the limiter fires and the order collapses.
* [Mignone, Tzeferacos & Bodo (2010) — High-order conservative finite difference GLM-MHD schemes for cell-centered MHD](/sources/mignone-2010.md) — Third- and fifth-order conservative finite-difference schemes for multi-D ideal MHD with all variables cell-centred and GLM divergence cleaning, using characteristic global Lax–Friedrichs splitting and comparing WENO+3, LimO3, WENO-Z and MP5: fifth order costs 25–48% more than third but needs about a quarter of the points on smooth problems; MP5 ranks slightly ahead of WENO-Z.
* [Owen, Guzik & Gao (2018) — A high-order adaptive algorithm for multispecies gaseous flows on mapped domains](/sources/owen-2018.md) — Fourth-order finite volume on mapped, block-structured AMR grids with RK4 subcycling, extended to thermally perfect, multispecies viscous flow through a fourth-order product rule that lets transport properties be evaluated pointwise at face centres; fourth-order convergence and free-stream preservation under moving refinement.
* [Pirozzoli (2002) — Conservative hybrid compact-WENO schemes for shock-turbulence interaction](/sources/pirozzoli-2002.md) — A conservative fifth-order compact upwind flux (tridiagonal) for smooth regions, switched to WENO5/WENO7 near discontinuities by a density-difference sensor; Fourier and eigenvalue stability analyses; better resolution than WENO and compact-ENO hybrids at lower cost.
* [Qiu & Shu (2005) — Runge–Kutta discontinuous Galerkin method using WENO limiters](/sources/qiu-2005.md) — Limits RKDG by flagging troubled cells with the TVB minmod limiter and rebuilding their polynomial, cell average kept, from finite-volume WENO point values at Gauss or Gauss–Lobatto points; the order and error size of unlimited RKDG are kept even when many good cells are flagged.
* [Rathan & Naga Raju (2017) — An improved non-linear weights for seventh-order weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme](/sources/rathan-2017.md) — Seventh-order WENO (WENO-NS7) with L¹-type local smoothness indicators from generalized undivided differences (fourth-order accurate derivative approximations) and a global indicator, with a power s that keeps seventh order at critical points of order one and two.
* [Reyes, Lee, Graziani & Tzeferacos (2018) — A new class of high-order methods for fluid dynamics simulations using Gaussian process modeling: one-dimensional case](/sources/reyes-2018.md) — Gaussian-process interpolation and reconstruction from cell averages with a squared-exponential kernel, and GP-WENO with RKHS-norm smoothness indicators; order 2R+1 on 2R+1 cells, a tunable length scale, tested on 1D Euler and MHD.
* [Seo & Ryu (2023) — HOW-MHD: a high-order WENO-based magnetohydrodynamic code with a high-order constrained transport algorithm for astrophysical applications](/sources/seo-2023.md) — The HOW-MHD code paper: Jiang–Wu fifth-order FD-WENO with SSPRK(5,4) at its SSP coefficient 1.508 as CFL, and a new fourth- or sixth-order staggered constrained transport built from the upwinded parts of the WENO induction fluxes with a transverse correction, which raises convergence on 3D MHD waves from second to fifth order.
* [Shu & Osher (1988) — Efficient implementation of essentially non-oscillatory shock-capturing schemes](/sources/shu-1988.md) — Introduces TVD (strong-stability-preserving) Runge–Kutta time discretizations as convex combinations of forward-Euler steps, with the optimal second- and third-order methods, and a flux-based finite-difference ENO built on point values.
* [Shu & Osher (1989) — Efficient implementation of essentially non-oscillatory shock-capturing schemes, II](/sources/shu-1989.md) — Simplified flux ENO through the primitive of the flux (Lemma 2.1), the ENO-Roe, ENO-LLF and ENO-RF building blocks, subcell resolution and artificial compression in the flux framework, characteristic and multi-D implementation, and the Shu–Osher shock/entropy-wave test.
* [Shu (1998) — Essentially non-oscillatory and weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/shu-1998.md) — Lecture notes that systematize ENO and WENO: the two approximation problems (reconstruction from averages, conservative derivatives from point values), FV vs FD formulations and costs, characteristic procedures, boundary treatment, provable properties, contact sharpening and TVD time discretizations.
* [Shu (2009) — High order weighted essentially nonoscillatory schemes for convection dominated problems](/sources/shu-2009.md) — SIAM Review survey of WENO to 2008 by a co-author of WENO-JS: interpolation, reconstruction and derivative/integral WENO procedures, negative linear weights, the finite-difference versus finite-volume comparison (FV two to five times costlier in 2D), self-similarity, accuracy downstream of shocks, linear instability with forward Euler and RK2, and open problems.
* [Shu (2020) — Essentially non-oscillatory and weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes](/sources/shu-2020.md) — Acta Numerica survey of ENO and WENO as approximation procedures and the schemes built on them: ENO's sign and TVB properties, classical WENO-JS and its variants, negative linear weights, unequal-stencil and multi-resolution WENO (which Shu advocates), FV, FD and alternative FD schemes, WENO limiters for DG, time stepping, inverse Lax–Wendroff boundaries and bound-preserving limiters.
* [Suresh & Huynh (1997) — Accurate monotonicity-preserving schemes with Runge–Kutta time stepping](/sources/suresh-1997.md) — MP5: a fifth-order five-point interface value limited by a geometric monotonicity-preserving bound designed for RK3, with intervals enlarged near smooth extrema to keep accuracy and a cheap test that skips limiting in smooth cells.
* [Titarev & Toro (2004) — Finite-volume WENO schemes for three-dimensional conservation laws](/sources/titarev-2004.md) — Extends finite-volume WENO to 3D and to fifth and seventh order by dimension-by-dimension sweeps to two-point Gauss quadratures (with the needed Gauss-point linear weights), and replaces the Rusanov flux by HLLC and the Riemann-solver-free MUSTA flux.
* [Wang, Zeng, Deiterding, Yang & Liang (2025) — An improved high-order adaptive mesh refinement framework for shock-turbulence interaction problems based on cell-centered finite difference schemes](/sources/wang-2025.md) — Fifth-order FD-WENO on block-structured AMR (AMReX) with point values stored at cell centres so that refluxing applies, WENO prolongation and restriction in smooth regions, and a troubled-cell switch to conservative second-order interpolation at shocks, which survives shocks crossing or being regridded across levels where pure WENO interpolation fails.
* [Zanotti, Fambri, Dumbser & Hidalgo (2015) — Space–time adaptive ADER discontinuous Galerkin finite element schemes with a posteriori sub-cell finite volume limiting](/sources/zanotti-2015.md) — Combines ADER-DG with the a posteriori ADER-WENO subcell limiter on cell-by-cell space–time adaptive Cartesian meshes, extending projection and averaging to the subcell data, and shows design order to N = 8 and very low dissipation on Euler and GLM-MHD problems in 2D and 3D.
* [Zhang & Shu (2010) — On positivity-preserving high order discontinuous Galerkin schemes for compressible Euler equations on rectangular meshes](/sources/zhang-2010.md) — A sufficient condition — admissible Gauss–Lobatto point values plus a CFL bound — for positivity of cell averages of any-order FV or DG schemes for Euler, enforced by a conservative, accuracy-preserving linear scaling limiter; extended to rectangular meshes and tested with third-order RKDG.
* [Zhang & Shu (2012) — Positivity-preserving high order finite difference WENO schemes for compressible Euler equations](/sources/zhang-2012.md) — Extends the Zhang–Shu positivity limiter to finite-difference WENO: with a global Lax–Friedrichs splitting the FD scheme is two FV WENO schemes for the split fluxes, whose reconstructed values are limited; includes local splitting, source terms, general equations of state and multi-D.
* [Zhu & Qiu (2016) — A new fifth order finite difference WENO scheme for solving hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/zhu-2016.md) — WENO-ZQ: a quartic on the five-point stencil combined with two linear polynomials through a weight-adjusted subtraction, so arbitrary positive linear weights keep fifth order; new τ-based weights; behaves like a van Albada-limited TVD scheme near discontinuities.
