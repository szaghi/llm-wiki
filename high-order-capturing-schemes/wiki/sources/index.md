# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Balsara & Shu (2000) — Monotonicity preserving weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes with increasingly high order of accuracy](/sources/balsara-2000.md) — WENO of orders 7–13 (r = 4–7) with tabulated coefficients and indicators, made robust by the Suresh–Huynh bounds (MPWENO); convergence, flat-extremum degradation, artificial-compression tuning by order, and 1D/2D Euler and MHD tests.
* [Balsara, Garain & Shu (2016) — An efficient class of WENO schemes with adaptive order](/sources/balsara-2016.md) — Legendre-basis reconstructions with smoothness indicators as sums of perfect squares (r = 3–9), and WENO-AO: a large centred high-order polynomial nonlinearly hybridized with the r = 3 central WENO, recursively (AO(7,5,3), AO(9,5,3)), needing no MP step.
* [Borges, Carmona, Costa & Don (2008) — An improved weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/borges-2008.md) — WENO-Z: weights from a global fifth-order indicator τ₅ = |β₀ − β₂|, fourth order at first-order critical points (fifth with q = 2), and the argument that WENO-M and WENO-Z beat WENO-JS at shocks through larger weights on discontinuous stencils, at WENO-JS cost.
* [Castro, Costa & Don (2011) — High order weighted essentially non-oscillatory WENO-Z schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/castro-2011.md) — Extends WENO-Z to all odd orders with a closed-form global indicator and an optimal-order variant, shows that the power q (not the mapping) restores order at critical points for WENO-Z only, and ranks WENO-JS, WENO-Z, WENO-M by dissipation.
* [Colella & Woodward (1984) — The piecewise parabolic method (PPM) for gas-dynamical simulations](/sources/colella-1984.md) — PPM: fourth-order edge interpolation with monotonized parabolas, Lagrangian and single-step Eulerian Riemann states from characteristic domains of dependence, contact steepening, shock flattening and extra dissipation for slow strong shocks.
* [Fu, Hu & Adams (2016) — A family of high-order targeted ENO schemes for compressible-fluid simulations](/sources/fu-2016.md) — TENO: incremental-width candidate stencils, a strongly scale-separating smoothness measure and a 0/1 ENO-like cutoff that keeps stencils at their linear weights, on spectrally optimized background schemes; fifth- and sixth-order members with a single parameter set.
* [Harten, Engquist, Osher & Chakravarthy (1987) — Uniformly high order accurate essentially non-oscillatory schemes, III](/sources/harten-1987.md) — The arbitrary-order ENO paper: adaptive-stencil reconstruction from cell averages (via primitive function or deconvolution), the abstract reconstruct–evolve–average scheme with Cauchy–Kowalewski solution in the small, characteristic-wise extension to systems, and Euler tests.
* [Henrick, Aslam & Powers (2005) — Mapped weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes: achieving optimal order near critical points](/sources/henrick-2005.md) — Shows that WENO-JS is only third order at critical points with f′ = 0, f‴ ≠ 0 (a missing order condition, hidden by ε), gives necessary and sufficient weight conditions and the rate formula for orders 3–11, and restores fifth order with a weight mapping (WENO-M).
* [Hu, Wang & Adams (2010) — An adaptive central-upwind weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme](/sources/hu-2010.md) — WENO-CU6: four candidate stencils including a downwind one, linear weights giving the sixth-order central scheme, and WENO-Z-type weights with a large constant and a six-point reference indicator τ₆ that switch smoothly between central and upwind.
* [Jiang & Shu (1996) — Efficient implementation of weighted ENO schemes](/sources/jiang-1996.md) — WENO-JS: the finite-difference flux WENO with optimal linear weights and the L²-derivative smoothness indicator, giving fifth order from third-order ENO stencils; error analysis of Liu–Osher–Chan, convergence for smooth solutions, pressure–entropy weights, and a standard test suite.
* [Levy, Puppo & Russo (1999) — Central WENO schemes for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws](/sources/levy-1999.md) — Third- and fourth-order staggered central schemes with a centred WENO reconstruction (convex combination of three parabolas with symmetric constants) and Runge–Kutta natural continuous extensions; no Riemann solver or characteristic decomposition, global smoothness indicators for systems.
* [Liu, Osher & Chan (1994) — Weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes](/sources/liu-1994.md) — The first WENO scheme: a convex combination of all r ENO candidate polynomials with weights from smoothness indicators, cell-average form with TVD Runge–Kutta, one order above the base ENO scheme (r+1).
* [Martín, Taylor, Wu & Weirs (2006) — A bandwidth-optimized WENO scheme for the effective direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulence](/sources/martin-2006.md) — WENO-SYM: a downwind candidate stencil makes the set symmetric; the optimal weights are order-optimized (WENO-SYMOO, non-dissipative, unstable at contacts) or bandwidth-optimized (WENO-SYMBO); validated on canonical problems, isotropic turbulence and a supersonic boundary layer.
* [Pirozzoli (2002) — Conservative hybrid compact-WENO schemes for shock-turbulence interaction](/sources/pirozzoli-2002.md) — A conservative fifth-order compact upwind flux (tridiagonal) for smooth regions, switched to WENO5/WENO7 near discontinuities by a density-difference sensor; Fourier and eigenvalue stability analyses; better resolution than WENO and compact-ENO hybrids at lower cost.
* [Rathan & Naga Raju (2017) — An improved non-linear weights for seventh-order weighted essentially non-oscillatory scheme](/sources/rathan-2017.md) — Seventh-order WENO (WENO-NS7) with L¹-type local smoothness indicators from generalized undivided differences (fourth-order accurate derivative approximations) and a global indicator, with a power s that keeps seventh order at critical points of order one and two.
* [Shu & Osher (1988) — Efficient implementation of essentially non-oscillatory shock-capturing schemes](/sources/shu-1988.md) — Introduces TVD (strong-stability-preserving) Runge–Kutta time discretizations as convex combinations of forward-Euler steps, with the optimal second- and third-order methods, and a flux-based finite-difference ENO built on point values.
* [Shu & Osher (1989) — Efficient implementation of essentially non-oscillatory shock-capturing schemes, II](/sources/shu-1989.md) — Simplified flux ENO through the primitive of the flux (Lemma 2.1), the ENO-Roe, ENO-LLF and ENO-RF building blocks, subcell resolution and artificial compression in the flux framework, characteristic and multi-D implementation, and the Shu–Osher shock/entropy-wave test.
* [Shu (1998) — Essentially non-oscillatory and weighted essentially non-oscillatory schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/shu-1998.md) — Lecture notes that systematize ENO and WENO: the two approximation problems (reconstruction from averages, conservative derivatives from point values), FV vs FD formulations and costs, characteristic procedures, boundary treatment, provable properties, contact sharpening and TVD time discretizations.
* [Suresh & Huynh (1997) — Accurate monotonicity-preserving schemes with Runge–Kutta time stepping](/sources/suresh-1997.md) — MP5: a fifth-order five-point interface value limited by a geometric monotonicity-preserving bound designed for RK3, with intervals enlarged near smooth extrema to keep accuracy and a cheap test that skips limiting in smooth cells.
* [Zhu & Qiu (2016) — A new fifth order finite difference WENO scheme for solving hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/zhu-2016.md) — WENO-ZQ: a quartic on the five-point stencil combined with two linear polynomials through a weight-adjusted subtraction, so arbitrary positive linear weights keep fifth order; new τ-based weights; behaves like a van Albada-limited TVD scheme near discontinuities.
