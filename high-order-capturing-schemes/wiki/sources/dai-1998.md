# Summary

Dai and Woodward propose a staggered representation for multidimensional
MHD Godunov schemes: mass, momentum and total energy are cell averages,
and each field component is a face average on the faces normal to it.
Because the induction law for a component contains no derivative along
that component, integrating it over a face and a step gives an exact
update by line- and time-averaged values of $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ on
the face edges. This keeps the net magnetic flux through every cell at
its initial value exactly, while the cell-averaged fluid variables keep
their exact conservative difference form. The face update is an
operator, applied after an unsplit integrator or inside a Strang-split
one. A cell-centred field is kept only as an intermediate, to compute
fluxes and edge values. The demonstration scheme is second-order and
dimensionally split, with linear interpolation and an extension of the
authors' 1994 MHD Riemann solver; tests are oblique fast waves, three
oblique shock tubes, the Orszag–Tang vortex and Mach 15 and Mach 12
shock–cloud problems, plus runs with and without the face update.

# Key claims

1. **Against projection for supersonic flows** (§1). Projection, proposed
   for subsonic MHD by Brackbill and Barnes, needs global information
   (poorly suited to parallel computers); removes a divergence evaluated
   with $O(1)$ truncation errors near discontinuities, so the cleaned field
   is divergence-free "only to the accuracy of a numerical scheme";
   "more seriously", breaks conservation of energy and of the three field
   components, with $O(1)$ errors near discontinuities that "may be easily
   detected" at a strong oblique shock in 2D; and costs a Poisson solve
   per step. In all earlier multidimensional schemes, the paper states,
   either conservation or the divergence-free condition holds only to the
   accuracy of the scheme.
2. **Exact finite-volume form** (§2, eqs. 5–10). With space- and
   time-averaged face fluxes the cell-average update is exact; the
   induction rows of the fluxes are components of
   $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ (the paper's $\Omega$, minus the registry
   electric field).
3. **Face-averaged field** (§2, eqs. 11–14). Each component is averaged
   over the faces normal to it; it "is well defined even if a
   discontinuity surface … coincides with an interface", because the
   normal field is continuous across it.
4. **Discrete divergence** (eqs. 15–16): the net face flux of each cell,
   the face-flux (Stokes) divergence.
5. **Exact face update** (eqs. 17–25). Each face average changes by the
   differences of line- and time-averaged edge values of
   $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ (eqs. 17–19 in 3D, 24–25 in 2D); the signs
   agree with $\partial_t\mathbf B = \nabla\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$
   (reader's check on the page image), and the net flux through each
   cell surface is unchanged (eq. 23). In registry form the 2D update is
   the Yee update of the [constrained-transport page](/methods/constrained-transport.md).
6. **The field must be updated unsplit** (end of §2). The fluid variables
   may be updated split or unsplit, but the face field "should be updated
   in an unsplit manner in order to exactly maintain the divergence-free
   constraint". CT (Evans and Hawley 1988; Stone and Norman 1992) also
   keeps the condition exactly.
7. **Operator form** (§2.1, eqs. 26–27). Unsplit: the face operator
   follows an unsplit integrator "such as those in Colella (1994) and
   Saltzman (1994)". Strang split: the face operator is applied after
   each half of the $xyzzyx$ sequence (eq. 27). Second order if the 1D
   operator is.
8. **Cell-centred field as intermediate** (§2.1, p. 323). Initialized as
   the mean of the two face values; advanced with the fluid variables; it
   "does not exactly satisfy the divergence-free condition" and is used
   only to approximate the edge values.
9. **Riemann states** (p. 324). Left and right states are
   domain-of-dependence averages chosen separately for each wave family;
   "if the left (or right) states for different waves were treated as the
   same, the resulting scheme would be only first-order accurate". The
   solver is the authors' 1994 solver extended to a single-step Eulerian
   form.
10. **Edge values** (eqs. 28–32). In 2D the edge value is
    $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ evaluated at the corner from time-centred
    four-cell arithmetic means of the cell-centred $\mathbf v$ and
    $\mathbf B$ (eqs. 31–32), not from the upwind Godunov fluxes.
11. **Field lines** (§3). The face fields are integrals along
    interfaces, so $A_z$ follows from them "without involving any
    approximation"; its contours are field lines.
12. **Conservation over divergence** (§§3–4). Same-scheme runs with and
    without the face update are "not significantly different, although
    some differences exist in small scales", largest in the shear layer
    behind the cloud, where the run without it has its largest net cell
    flux. The divergence of the cell-centred field is
    interpolation-dependent and $O(1)$ at its maximum in all problems,
    whatever the interpolation (§4). "we believe that $B_{i,j,k}$ do not
    necessarily have to exactly satisfy the divergence-free condition …
    It is the conservation laws which play the crucial role in the
    simulations for supersonic flows."
13. **Staggering** (§4). Only the field is staggered. "Generally, face
    averages and staggered grids are not favored in hyperbolic systems of
    conservation laws", but the missing derivative in each component law
    makes them "particularly useful" for MHD.
14. **Split versus unsplit** (§4, p. 335). In an unsplit scheme the
    time-averaged face fluxes can be used directly for the edge values,
    which "will look more natural". In a split scheme the sweeps occur at
    different instants, and it "is not clear" whether interpolating among
    them keeps second order in time. A multidimensional Riemann solver at
    edges or vertices "will be useful".

# Tests

Uniform grids, $\gamma = 5/3$, Courant numbers "around 0.6", Gaussian
units (§3). The net flux through each cell keeps its initial value. No
error norms, convergence rates or cost data: all findings are visual.

1. **Oblique linear fast wave** (Fig. 1). At 30° to the $x$-axis on a
   periodic $(1/\cos 30°)\times(1/\sin 30°)$ box, $t = 10$, grids $8^2$ to
   $128^2$: visual convergence to the initial profile.
2. **Nonlinear fast wave** (Fig. 2). $128^2$, $t = 1$–5; it steepens
   into a shock.
3. **Three oblique shock tubes** (Figs. 3–5). $1\times1$, 45° (so the
   initial data satisfy the discrete divergence exactly), $256^2$,
   parallel field 5, $t = 0.06$, cuts along $y = x$; initial states shown
   only graphically. Jump conditions are satisfied; there "would be $O(1)$
   truncation errors in the jump conditions if the scheme were not
   conservative for any one of eight conserved quantities". In the third
   tube (two fast and two slow shocks) the parallel field dips to about
   4.99997 (read from the Fig. 5 axis): "the scheme does not have
   mechanisms to maintain the constant" parallel field, a "symptom of
   truncation errors" (p. 333)
   ([grid-oblique MHD shock tubes](/benchmarks/oblique-mhd-shock-tubes.md)).
4. **Orszag–Tang vortex** (Figs. 6–7). $[0,1]^2$,
   $\mathbf v = (-\sin 2\pi y, \sin 2\pi x)$,
   $\mathbf B = (-\sin 2\pi y, \sin 4\pi x)$, plasma beta 10/3 and Mach
   number 1 (Gaussian definitions), $128^2$ to $512^2$, $t = 0.5$;
   attributed to Dahlburg and Picone (1989) and Picone and Dahlburg (1991)
   ([Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md)).
5. **Mach 15 shock on a cloud** (Figs. 8–9). States
   $(\rho, p, v_x, v_y, v_z, B_x, B_y, B_z)$: preshock
   $(1, 1, 0, 0, 0, 2, 2, 2)$, postshock
   $(3.94258, 399.481, 17.3463, -0.040403, -0.040403, 2, 7.89899, 7.89899)$;
   domain $1.5\times1$, cloud of radius 0.15 at $(0.375, 0.5)$ with 10
   times the preshock density; grids up to $768\times512$; field lines at
   $t = 0.055$ ([shock–cloud interaction](/benchmarks/shock-cloud-interaction.md)).
6. **Mach 12 shock on "MHD" letter clouds** (Figs. 10–11). Postshock
   $(3.91109, 253.724, 13.8388, -0.00500, -0.00500, 2, 7.84323, 7.84323)$,
   letters at density 50, $2\times1.5$, $512\times384$; a robustness test.
7. **With versus without the face update** (Figs. 12–19, p. 334). Mach
   15, cloud density 10, shock-frame states preshock
   $(1, 1, -15.2677, 0, 0, 2, 1, 1)$ and postshock
   $(3.94605, 312.063, 0, -0.0229969, -0.0229969, 2, 3.95492, 3.95492)$,
   $384\times256$. Density: "very hard to find differences"; velocities
   differ near the cloud centre and in the tail; $B_x$ is "more clean in
   the tail" with the face update. The net cell flux without it spans
   $-2.84\times10^{-2}$ to $1.74\times10^{-2}$ (Fig. 18); the net flux of
   the cell-centred field with it spans $-4.75\times10^{-3}$ to
   $2.75\times10^{-3}$ (Fig. 19), "1 order lower". No normalization is
   given for these colour bars.
