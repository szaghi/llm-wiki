# Summary

The paper continues Xie et al. (2017) on the carbuncle of low-dissipation
Godunov-type solvers. Its central hypothesis: a captured steady shock
with one interior point becomes unstable because too little entropy is
produced inside the numerical shock structure. The hypothesis rests on
1D and 1.5D steady-shock experiments and on a modified-equation analysis
of [HLLEM](/methods/hllem-riemann-solver.md) in characteristic variables.
Reducing the anti-diffusion on the pressure part of the entropy wave
raises that entropy and stabilizes the shock beyond a threshold that
depends on the Mach number. A linearized analysis shows the same change
reduces the perturbation of the mass flux behind the shock. The paper
generalizes this to an additive **entropy-control** term, switched by a
pressure sensor, for Godunov, Roe, HLLEM and HLLC. All tests are first
or second order; high order is named as future work. Merged into
[numerical shock instability](/definitions/numerical-shock-instability.md),
[its cures](/methods/shock-instability-cures.md) and the benchmarks.

# Key claims

1. **Single-interior-point shocks** (§3.2). For Godunov-type schemes that
   capture steady shocks with one interior point, "the instability is
   inevitable" for some shock positions.
2. **HLLE does not help in 1D** (§3.1.2, Table 1). HLLE and HLLEM have the
   same stability map on the 1D steady shock: unstable for
   $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sh}} = 0$–$0.3$ in the table ("0.1 ∼ 0.3" in the
   text).
3. **Mechanism hypothesis** (§§3.2–3.3, 7). The instability "is due to
   inappropriate entropy production inside the numerical shock
   structure". It is motivated by the entropy overshoot of the viscous
   shock profile (Fig. 4). It is empirical and heuristic, not proved.
4. **HLLEM** (eqs. 13–20). Speeds are Einfeldt's (eq. 10) or Davis's
   (eq. 11), and $\delta_p = c/(c + \vert v_n\vert)$ at the Roe state. With
   Roe-state speeds HLLEM "is identical to the Roe scheme" (§3.3.1).
5. **Entropy-wave viscosity** (eqs. 38–44). In the modified equation, the
   anti-diffusion lowers the entropy-wave viscosity, and HLL
   over-dissipates that wave. Einfeldt's condition caps $\delta_p$ at the
   value already used, so it cannot simply be raised.
6. **HLLEM-ρ and HLLEM-p** (eqs. 45–60). These scale the density part of
   the entropy-wave strength up, or the pressure part down
   ($\varphi_{\mathrm p} < 1$). Either raises the computed entropy of the
   intermediate cell. HLLEM-ρ violates the contact stability condition
   and is discarded.
7. **Thresholds** (Figs. 7–8, 13, read from plots). In 1D,
   $\varphi_{\mathrm p}$ must fall below about 0.73–0.76 for
   $M_s \approx 4$–22. In 1.5D the threshold is 0.33 at $M_s = 6$, about
   0.16–0.34 over the range.
8. **Linearized perturbation analysis** (§4, eqs. 65–76). A smaller
   $\varphi_{\mathrm p}$ lowers the mass-flux perturbation reaching the
   downstream cell in 1D, and the transverse mass flux in 2D. These are
   one-step increments, not a spectrum.
9. **Entropy-control term** (§5, eqs. 77–80). The term is
   $(\varphi_{\mathrm p} - 1)\frac{S_L^- S_R^+}{S_R^+ - S_L^-}\delta_2\frac{p^+ - p^-}{c^2}r^{(2)}$,
   with $\varphi_{\mathrm p}$ the cube of the minimum pressure ratio over
   the face and its neighbours' normal faces. It adds nothing on linearly
   degenerate waves.
10. **Hybrids lose shear** (§6.6). HLLE and Quirk's Roe–HLLE hybrid smear
    Elling's physical carbuncle; the controlled schemes keep it.

**Tests** (§§3, 6):
* the 1D and 1.5D steady shock (50 and $50\times25$ cells), first order;
* Quirk's odd–even test on $800\times20$;
* shock diffraction on $[0,1]^2$, $400\times400$, to $t = 0.15$, at second
  order;
* the double Mach reflection on $960\times240$ to $t = 0.2$, first order
  in space;
* the Mach 20 cylinder on $60\times280$, implicit LU-SGS;
* a flat-plate boundary layer at Mach 0.3, $\mathrm{Re} = 10^5$;
* Elling's physical carbuncle on $100\times40$ at Mach 20.

The spatial order of the Quirk and cylinder runs is not stated. No
reconstruction is named.
