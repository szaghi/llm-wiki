# Summary

A survey with no numerical results. Merged into the ENO, WENO, WENO-AO,
mapped-WENO, central-WENO, WENO-limited DG, AFD, Lax–Wendroff, ILW,
characteristic, polynomial-WENO and positivity pages, the
smoothness-indicator and weight-condition pages, the new negative-weights
page, the critical-point page's closed contradiction, and the high-Mach jet.

# Key claims

1. **ENO** (§2): stencil growth by divided differences; biased ENO
   ($b \approx 2$); the monotone polynomial in a discontinuous cell and the
   TVB property for exact piecewise smooth data; the Fjordholm–Mishra–Tadmor
   sign property, the route to entropy-stable ENO; the shortcomings that
   led to WENO.
2. **WENO-JS** (§3.1, eqs. 3.3–3.5, page images): "roughness indicators";
   if every stencil contains the jump, the combination is still ENO;
   orders 5, 9, 13 reported better than 3, 7, 11; start with WENO-JS5,
   since the benefits of variants "are not universal" (§3.2).
3. **Critical points** (§3.2.1): Jiang and Shu "proved and numerically
   tested" fifth order including at smooth extrema; Henrick et al.'s
   degeneracy appears only for ε of about $10^{-10}$ or smaller, "not
   necessary in practical computation". Recorded under the closed
   contradiction on the critical-point page.
4. **Negative weights** (§3.2.2): interpolation weights always exist and
   are positive (Carlini et al.); the Shi–Hu–Shu treatment; or positive
   weights at lower order.
5. **Unequal stencils** (§3.2.3, eq. 3.6, page image): first in central
   WENO; Zhu–Qiu and multi-resolution WENO; mapped or Z weights
   preferable because the small stencils are second order.
6. **Derivatives** (§3.4.1): the indicator sum starts one order above the
   derivative; including it is "a common mistake".
7. **Schemes** (§4): FV with any Lipschitz monotone flux; componentwise
   reconstruction can oscillate at strong shocks; FD only on smooth meshes;
   the alternative FD form accepts any monotone flux; WENO limiters for
   RKDG (no troubled-cell indicator "is bullet-proof").
8. **Time** (§7): ADER and GRP schemes belong to the Lax–Wendroff class;
   multiderivative integrators; implicit WENO.
9. **Boundaries** (§8): ILW credited to Huang, Shu and Zhang (2008) and Tan
   and Shu (2010), with Goldberg–Tadmor as earlier discussions.
10. **Bound preservation** (§9): WENO3 succeeded and WENO5 failed on Mach
    2000 jets (Ha et al.); higher order is more prone to failure; the
    Zhang–Shu limiter keeps high order.
11. **Applications** (§10): more than 250 papers of 2019 citing Jiang and
    Shu (1996), sampled by field.
