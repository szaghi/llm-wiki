# Summary

A subcycled, block-structured AMR framework for fifth-order FD-WENO-JS
with SSP-RK3. Its two ideas are cell-centred storage of the FD point
values, which puts patch boundaries on flux points, and a hybrid
inter-level interpolation switched by a shock detector. Merged into the
high-order AMR and SSP pages and the Sod, Lax, Shu–Osher, 2D Riemann and
double-Mach benchmarks.

# Key claims

1. **Layout** (§§1, 4.1). Vertex-centred FD-AMR copies coarse values from
   fine ones, and its interface fluxes mismatch. Cell-centred points make
   coarse and fine patches meet at flux points, so Berger–Colella
   refluxing applies. The authors believe this is not previously documented.
2. **Prolongation and restriction** (§4.2, Tables 1–4, Appendix A).
   Sebastian–Shu-type WENO interpolation for prolongation, with
   re-derived coefficients for ratios 2 and 4, and a symmetric six-point
   WENO interpolation for restriction (the paper calls it "central WENO";
   it is not the Levy–Puppo–Russo scheme).
3. **Ghost values in time** (§4.2.3, eq. 15). A quadratic dense output of
   SSP-RK3, $u^n + \chi k_1 + (-\tfrac56k_1 + \tfrac16k_2 + \tfrac23k_3)\chi^2$.
   Evaluating it at the fine stage times mismatches the stages; the
   stage-matched correction follows McCorquodale and Colella, and its
   formulas are not printed.
4. **Refluxing** (§4.3, eqs. 17–19). Stage fluxes are accumulated with the
   Butcher weights.
5. **Failure scenarios** (§5.1). Non-conservative interpolation is
   harmless while a discontinuity stays on the finest level, accumulates
   errors when it crosses an interface, and steepens the shock artificially
   when it is refined or coarsened.
6. **Hybrid** (§5.2, eqs. 21–25). A troubled-cell detector: a Lax entropy
   condition on Roe-averaged speeds, a mapped pressure curvature above
   $10^{-2}$, and a normalized Lax-residual threshold of 0.1 (that
   residual is not defined). Flagged cells use conservative linear
   interpolation and averaging.
7. **Conservation versus order** (§§5.2, 6.1). Without flux correction the
   conservation error converges at fifth order on smooth data (Table 6).
   With it, the error is second order (stated, not tabulated). The paper
   endorses Sebastian and Shu's claim that conservative treatments limit
   FD to second order.
8. **Tests** (§6). About fifth order on smooth 1D problems (Table 5);
   shocks crossing static interfaces, where pure WENO fails at Mach 3;
   Sod, Lax and Shu–Osher; a 2D Riemann problem; double Mach reflection,
   where pure WENO or Lagrange interpolation gives negative density at
   once; and decaying isotropic turbulence at turbulent Mach 1, where WENO
   interpolation blows up at about 3.4 eddy turnover times.
9. **Limitation** (§7). No stability analysis of the hybrid.
