# Summary

ILW obtains normal derivatives at the boundary from the PDE. With
diffusion, the convective and diffusive limits need opposite choices of
which derivative comes from ILW. The paper blends them, first for
scalar equations, then for linear systems and Navier–Stokes in 1D and
2D, with third-order schemes on Cartesian grids. Merged into the new
[inverse Lax–Wendroff](/methods/inverse-lax-wendroff.md) page.

# Key claims

1. **Context** (§1): body-fitted, overlapping, embedded and immersed
   boundary approaches are surveyed. ILW and SILW are stable under the
   periodic CFL condition regardless of cut-cell distance (citing Vilar
   and Shu, and Li et al.).
2. **RK3 boundary matching** (§2): after Carpenter et al.
3. **Scalar case** (§2.2): convection takes $u_x$ from ILW and $u_{xx}$
   by extrapolation; diffusion does the reverse. They are blended with
   $\alpha = f'(g)^2\Delta x^2/(f'(g)^2\Delta x^2 + 9\varepsilon^2)$ (eqs. 2.10–2.11).
4. **Linear systems** (§2.3): characteristic decomposition of the
   convective matrix (inflow and outflow characteristics) for the
   convection-dominated treatment, of the diffusive matrix for the
   diffusion-dominated one, and blending per diffusive field.
5. **Navier–Stokes** (§§2.4–2.5): the system is incompletely parabolic
   and is split into hyperbolic and parabolic parts after Strikwerda and
   Gustafsson–Sundström. Boundary conditions are prioritized $E$,
   $\rho u$, $\rho$. In 2D the treatment is done in normal–tangential
   coordinates at the foot of the normal through each ghost point.
6. **Tests** (§3): $\delta = 10^{-6}$ cut cells; scalar, Burgers, linear
   systems, 1D and 2D Navier–Stokes at $\mathrm{Re} = 1$, 30 and $10^6$,
   mixed regimes, a 45° polygon, and a Mach 3 cylinder with WENO-type
   extrapolation; all third order and stable.
7. **Limits** (§4): only third order and Dirichlet conditions are
   tested. Higher order and Neumann/Robin conditions are left for later.
