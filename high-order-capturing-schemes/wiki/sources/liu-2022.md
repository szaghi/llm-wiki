# Summary

The authors' 2D moving-boundary ILW (inviscid and convection–diffusion)
is extended to 3D rigid bodies driven by the flow. Merged into the ILW
page (new section "Moving boundaries"), the extrapolation and SSP pages,
and the new [shock-driven cylinder lift-off](/benchmarks/shock-cylinder-lift-off.md)
benchmark.

# Key claims

1. **Context** (§1). Original ILW has three drawbacks: heavy algebra,
   trouble at sonic points, and non-conservation. They are answered by
   SILW, Lu et al. (2021) and Ding et al. (2020).
2. **Scheme** (§2, Appendix A). Third-order FD-WENO with characteristic
   Lax–Friedrichs splitting, fourth-order central viscous terms,
   TVD-RK3; the body moves at most one cell per step (eq. 15).
3. **Ghost values** (§3.1, eq. 21). Third-order Taylor expansion along the
   normal through the foot point, in a rotated frame. The zeroth and first
   normal derivatives come from ILW, the second from WENO extrapolation.
4. **Theorem 1** (§3.1, Appendix B). In 3D the rotation to the normal is
   not unique; the Euler ILW ghost values do not depend on the tangent
   vectors. Asserted, not proved, for Navier–Stokes.
5. **Theorem 2** (§3.1.1, eqs. 24–26). A material derivative for
   functions defined only on the moving surface equals $D/Dt$ under
   no-penetration.
6. **Navier–Stokes** (§3.2, eqs. 35–38, page image). Wall density from
   the outgoing acoustic characteristic; first and second derivatives
   blended with weights in the sound speed and the largest diffusion
   coefficient, extrapolation alone for the mass equation.
7. **Stage values** (§§3.1.3, 3.2.3). Boundary velocity at the RK stages
   by Lax–Wendroff; mixed space–time derivatives extrapolated from grid
   values of $u_t$.
8. **Extrapolation** (Appendix C). Three nested least-squares candidates
   on balls of radius $1.1h$, $2.1h$, $3.1h$ with WENO-JS weights whose
   linear weights depend on the grid scale.
9. **Tests** (§5). An isentropic flow around a moving cylinder converges
   at third order ($L^1$ order 3.21 at $h = 1/40$, Table 1).
   Shock–cylinder and shock–sphere interactions at $M_s = 3$ and 6,
   inviscid and viscous, are shown as contour plots only. CFL 0.3.
