# Summary

The paper rewrites the eigenvectors of 1D ideal MHD, in primitive
variables and Gaussian units, in a compact orthonormal form. Two
dimensionless factors $\alpha_f$, $\alpha_s$ with
$\alpha_f^2 + \alpha_s^2 = 1$ make the fast and slow eigenvectors well
formed and complete in every degenerate case except the vacuum limit. At
the triple umbilic ($\vert B_x\vert/\sqrt{4\pi\rho} = c$, $B_\perp = 0$)
the factors are indeterminate; the paper gives their first expansion
there, which depends on the direction of approach, and integrates the fast
and slow wave paths near that point for $\gamma = 5/3$. It shows that a
linearized (Roe-type) flux evaluated at a state within $\epsilon$ of the
triple umbilic changes only by $O(\epsilon)$ with the exact position of
that state, although the individual fast and slow wave strengths change
by $O(1)$; the same argument justifies arbitrary tangential direction
cosines at $B_\perp = 0$. Closed forms of the structure coefficients of
the fast and slow waves recover Brio and Wu's loss of convexity. The paper
is analytical: it has no numerical tests.

# Key claims

1. **System and speeds** (§2, eqs. 1–4). Primitive variables
   $(\rho, v_x, v_y, v_z, B_y, B_z, p)$; Alfvén-velocity components
   $\mathbf B/\sqrt{4\pi\rho}$. The speeds, in increasing order, are
   $v_x \mp c_f$, $v_x \mp c_A$, $v_x \mp c_s$, $v_x$, with $c_f^2$, $c_s^2$
   the roots of
   $$\lambda^4 - \left(c^2 + \frac{\vert\mathbf B\vert^2}{4\pi\rho}\right)\lambda^2 + c^2 c_A^2 = 0$$
   (eq. 3, the speed written $\lambda$ here), so $c_f c_s = c\,c_A$ and
   $c_f^2 + c_s^2 = c^2 + \vert\mathbf B\vert^2/(4\pi\rho)$ (eq. 4). The
   sound speed may be $(\partial p/\partial\rho)_s^{1/2}$ for a general
   equation of state.
2. **Geometry** (Fig. 1). In the plane of the normal and tangential
   Alfvén velocities over $c$, curves of constant $c_f$ are confocal
   ellipses and of constant $c_s$ confocal hyperbolae, with foci at the
   triple points where $c_f = c_s$.
3. **Unnormalized eigenvectors** (eqs. 5–6). The natural fast and slow
   eigenvectors carry the factor $1/(c_{f,s}^2 - c_A^2)$; for the same wave
   the product of left and right eigenvectors is
   $2\lambda^2(c_f^2 - c_s^2)/\vert\lambda^2 - c_A^2\vert$ with $\lambda$
   the wave's speed, singular or indeterminate in several cases.
4. **Normalization** (eq. 7, p. 60).
   $$\alpha_f^2 = \frac{c^2 - c_s^2}{c_f^2 - c_s^2},\qquad \alpha_s^2 = \frac{c_f^2 - c^2}{c_f^2 - c_s^2},$$
   with $\alpha_f^2 + \alpha_s^2 = 1$,
   $\alpha_s^2c_s^2 + \alpha_f^2c_f^2 = c^2$ and
   $\alpha_s\alpha_f = c\,B_\perp/\left(\sqrt{4\pi\rho}\,(c_f^2 - c_s^2)\right)$.
   The right eigenvectors are scaled by $\alpha_{f,s}$ and the left by
   $\alpha_{f,s}/(2c^2)$, the $c^2$ placed in the left ones "to give $r$
   the same dimensions" as the primitive state. On Brio and Wu (p. 60):
   "$\alpha_s$ is defined here identically, but our $\alpha_f$ has an
   additional factor $a/c_f$" ($a$ the paper's sound speed, the registry
   $c$).
5. **Normalized eigenvectors** (eqs. 8–9). With the tangential direction
   cosines $B_y/B_\perp$, $B_z/B_\perp$:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   r_f^\pm &= \left(\alpha_f\rho,\ \pm\alpha_f c_f,\ \mp\alpha_s c_s\,\mathrm{sgn}B_x\tfrac{B_y}{B_\perp},\ \mp\alpha_s c_s\,\mathrm{sgn}B_x\tfrac{B_z}{B_\perp},\ \alpha_s\sqrt{4\pi\rho}\,c\tfrac{B_y}{B_\perp},\ \alpha_s\sqrt{4\pi\rho}\,c\tfrac{B_z}{B_\perp},\ \alpha_f\rho c^2\right)^T,\\
   r_s^\pm &= \left(\alpha_s\rho,\ \pm\alpha_s c_s,\ \pm\alpha_f c_f\,\mathrm{sgn}B_x\tfrac{B_y}{B_\perp},\ \pm\alpha_f c_f\,\mathrm{sgn}B_x\tfrac{B_z}{B_\perp},\ -\alpha_f\sqrt{4\pi\rho}\,c\tfrac{B_y}{B_\perp},\ -\alpha_f\sqrt{4\pi\rho}\,c\tfrac{B_z}{B_\perp},\ \alpha_s\rho c^2\right)^T,
   \end{aligned}$$
   The left
   eigenvectors are $1/(2c^2)$ times the same pattern with first entry 0,
   the field entries divided by $4\pi\rho$ instead of multiplied, and last
   entry $\alpha_{f,s}/\rho$ (eqs. 8–9). At $B_\perp = 0$ the direction
   cosines are indeterminate; following Brio and Wu any values may be
   assigned, e.g. both $1/\sqrt2$, which "preserves the orthonormality".
   Alfvén and entropy eigenvectors: eqs. 17–18.
6. **Footnote 1** (p. 60): an erratum to Zachary and Colella's left slow
   eigenvector, "the last component should be $\tau\alpha_s$" ($\tau$ not
   defined here; presumably the specific volume $1/\rho$, consistent with
   eq. 9), and their normalizing factors "can be simplified
   considerably".
7. **The six degenerate cases** (§2, Fig. 2, eqs. 10–16).
   * I, $B_x \to 0$: $\alpha_f^2 \to c^2/(c^2 + B_\perp^2/4\pi\rho)$,
     $\alpha_s^2 \to (B_\perp^2/4\pi\rho)/(c^2 + B_\perp^2/4\pi\rho)$
     (eq. 10).
   * II, $B_x$ and $B_\perp \to 0$ (hydrodynamic limit): $\alpha_f \to 1$,
     $\alpha_s \to 0$; fast waves acoustic, slow and Alfvén waves merge
     into shear waves (eq. 11).
   * III, $c_A < c - \epsilon$, $B_\perp \to 0$: $c_f \to c$,
     $c_s \to c_A$, $\alpha_f \to 1$, $\alpha_s \to 0$ (eq. 12).
   * IV, $c_A > c + \epsilon$, $B_\perp \to 0$: $c_f \to c_A$,
     $c_s \to c$, $\alpha_f \to 0$, $\alpha_s \to 1$ (eq. 13).
   * V, $c_A \simeq c$, $B_\perp \simeq 0$ (the triple umbilic):
     $c_f \simeq c \simeq c_s$ and $\alpha_{f,s}$ are indeterminate, "but
     since $\alpha_f^2 + \alpha_s^2 = 1$, the eigenvectors cannot be
     singular". Expanding about the point, $\alpha_f$ tends to the sine
     and $\alpha_s$ to the cosine of half the angle of approach (the angle
     whose tangent is $B_\perp/(\vert B_x\vert - c\sqrt{4\pi\rho})$), with
     corrections bounded by $B_\perp/(4c\sqrt{4\pi\rho})$ (eq. 15, bound
     stated without proof). These forms "have not been given previously".
   * VI, vacuum limit ($\vert B_x\vert$ and $B_\perp \gg c\sqrt{4\pi\rho}$): $\alpha_f \to 0$,
     $\alpha_s \to 1$; both slow eigenvectors collapse onto the entropy
     eigenvector, so "the eigenvector system is no longer complete and …
     the MHD equations are ill posed in a way that cannot be removed by
     any renormalization" (eq. 16).

   In cases I–IV the eigenvectors are well formed and independent ("the
   reader can easily verify").
8. **Wave paths near the triple umbilic** (§3, eqs. 19–20, Fig. 3). Near
   the point the fast and slow paths in the plane of Alfvén velocities
   over $c$ have slopes $-\cot$ (fast) and $+\tan$ (slow) of half the approach angle,
   divided by one plus the logarithmic density derivative of the sound
   speed at constant entropy ($(\gamma - 1)/2$ for an ideal gas). For
   $\gamma = 5/3$ the integrated curves are printed in polar form; their
   general shape "is independent of $\gamma$". The topology is
   Schaeffer–Shearer type IV; the Riemann problem without dissipation is
   then well posed "if compressive shocks are the only ones admitted"
   (citing Isaacson et al.). In 3D the diagram rotates about the normal
   axis: fast and slow paths lie in a fixed plane through it, Alfvén paths
   are circles about it.
9. **Insensitivity of the linearized flux** (§4, eq. 21, Fig. 4). The
   Roe-type flux is
   $\hat f = \tfrac12\left(f(u^-) + f(u^+)\right) - \tfrac12\sum_p \vert\lambda^{(p)}\vert\,\ell^{(p)}\cdot(\mathbf W^+ - \mathbf W^-)\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf W}r^{(p)}$
   in primitive eigenvectors. By eqs. 8–9 the fast and slow eigenvectors
   are combinations, with coefficients $\pm\alpha_f$ and $\pm\alpha_s$, of
   an acoustic and a transverse vector that do not depend on the
   linearization state,
   so their combined jump does not either. In the critical case
   $c_s < \vert v_x\vert < c_f$ the individual fast and slow strengths
   change by $O(1)$ as the linearization state moves within $\epsilon$ of
   the triple umbilic, but they are multiplied by speeds that differ by
   at most $\epsilon c$ ($c_f - c_s \le \epsilon c$, stated), so the flux
   changes by $O(\epsilon)$. The same holds near $\mathbf B = 0$ (slow and
   Alfvén speeds both small): the flux does not depend on how the shear
   wave is split between them. "This provides a justification for
   assigning arbitrary values" to the direction cosines in this limit.
   Whether waves with dissipative internal structure near the point are
   captured correctly by a scheme that ignores dissipation is left open.
10. **Structure coefficients** (§5, eqs. 22–24). After Lax,
    $\nabla\lambda^{(p)}\cdot r^{(p)}$ with normalized eigenvectors, in
    primitive variables. For an ideal gas it is
    $$\alpha_f c_f\left[\tfrac{\gamma+1}{2}\alpha_f^2 + \tfrac32\alpha_s^2\right]\ \text{(fast)},\qquad \alpha_s c_s\left[\tfrac{\gamma+1}{2}\alpha_s^2 + \tfrac32\alpha_f^2\right]\ \text{(slow)}$$
    (the paper's general form has one plus the logarithmic density
    derivative of $c$ in place of $\tfrac{\gamma+1}{2}$). With
    $\alpha_{f,s} = 1$ the acoustic result is recovered; the formulas agree
    with "equivalent but more complicated" ones of Shercliff. At
    $B_\perp = 0$ the factor $\alpha_f$ or $\alpha_s$ of the wave vanishes,
    so the coefficient vanishes and the wave is "neither expansive nor
    compressive": "the 'loss of convexity' first observed in" Brio and Wu.
11. **Entropy fix** (§5). Zachary and Colella add dissipation from the
    structure coefficients, after Bell, Colella and Trangenstein. For
    Euler, Roe found extra dissipation needed only at stationary sonic
    points; that this carries over to MHD "appears to be confirmed" by
    Aslan's thesis, "but only extensive testing will decide the issue".
12. **Multi-D** (§6). In conservation form the multi-D eigenvectors are
    not simple extensions of the 1D ones, and $B_x$ constant becomes
    $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$. Powell keeps the 1D eigenstructure in a
    partially conservative form with an eighth wave that advects
    $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B$, used for adaptive-grid comet and solar-wind
    runs.
