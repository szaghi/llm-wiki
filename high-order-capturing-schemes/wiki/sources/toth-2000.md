# Summary

The paper adds seven treatments of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ to the same
shock-capturing base scheme in one code, the Versatile Advection Code
(VAC), and compares them on the same tests with errors measured against
coarsened high-resolution references. The seven are Powell's eight-wave
formulation, three constrained-transport schemes from the literature
(field-CT after Dai and Woodward, flux-CT after Balsara and Spicer,
transport-flux-CT after Ryu, Miniati, Jones and Frank), two new
central-difference schemes (field-CD, flux-CD) and the Brackbill–Barnes
projection. Three theoretical results: every CT scheme is identical to a
cell-centred finite-volume scheme with wider-stencil averaged fluxes, so
the staggered field is unnecessary, and it conserves a cell-corner
divergence; the projection is the minimal $L^2$ correction and keeps the
order of the base scheme; and no conservative scheme can keep the
discrete Lorentz acceleration exactly orthogonal to $\mathbf B$ in every
cell. The eight-wave scheme can converge to wrong jumps, field-CT is
oscillatory, and projection, field-CD and flux-CT are the most accurate.

# Key claims

1. **Three categories** (Abstract, §1.2, Table I). The eight-wave scheme
   keeps $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B$ at truncation-error level; the five CT/CD
   schemes conserve a particular discrete divergence to machine accuracy;
   projection enforces the central divergence to a tolerance. Cost over the
   one-step TVD base scheme: eight-wave 7%, field-CT 4%, flux-CT 5%,
   transport-flux-CT 6%, field-CD 2%, flux-CD 4%, projection about 20%.
2. **No discrete divergence avoids every unphysical effect** (§1.1,
   Appendix A, Fig. 21). "A scheme cannot satisfy both the numerical
   conservation of momentum and the requirement that the discretized
   acceleration due to the Lorentz force should be exactly perpendicular
   to the magnetic field in every grid cell." The proof is a
   counter-example: uniform $\rho$ and $p$, $\mathbf v = 0$, a cell with
   zero central divergence whose neighbours make total $x$-momentum
   non-conserved. The choice of discrete divergence is "always somewhat
   arbitrary". §1.1 names "acceleration of the plasma parallel to the
   field lines" as "the usual example" of such an effect, citing
   Brackbill and Barnes.
3. **Eight-wave system** (§3, eqs. 11–13, read from the text layer). The
   momentum equation is printed with the conservative flux on the left and
   $-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)\mathbf B$ on the right; the energy and
   induction equations carry $-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)\,\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf v$
   and $-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)\mathbf v$. Brackbill and Barnes "already
   [contain] all the source terms". Being non-conservative, the scheme
   can give wrong jump conditions (Lax–Wendroff), and these errors "do not
   decrease with the grid resolution" (§§1.2, 3). The paper does not state
   the component of either force along $\mathbf B$.
4. **Finite-difference CT** (§4.1, eqs. 14–15, page image p. 613). Face
   fields $\bar B_{x;i+1/2,j}$, $\bar B_{y;i,j+1/2}$ are updated by
   differences of the corner electric field $\mathcal E_z$, which keeps the
   face divergence unchanged to round-off.
   * *Field-CT* (eqs. 16–18): $\mathcal E_z = -\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$
     from corner means of eight cell values (four at level $n$, four at
     the provisional level); the cell field is the mean of its two faces.
   * *Flux-CT* (eq. 19): the corner field is the mean of the four
     adjacent upwind face fluxes of the transverse field components (the
     pressure-weighted variant is not used).
   * *Transport-flux-CT* (eqs. 20–22): built from the transport parts of
     the fluxes with factor $\tfrac12$; it reduces exactly to the base
     scheme for grid-aligned 1D problems, field-CT and flux-CT do not.
5. **CT without staggering** (§4.4, eqs. 23–27). Each CT scheme is a
   conservative cell-centred update with averaged fluxes (field-CT: 6
   cells and 2 time levels per flux component; flux-CT: eq. 25,
   denominator 8), "identical with the original staggered schemes in terms
   of the cell centered variables". The conserved quantity is the corner
   divergence (eq. 27), a contour integral over a diagonally rotated
   volume, not the central divergence (eq. 26). With an unsplit base
   scheme the averaged fluxes replace the base fluxes directly. The
   symmetry is "achieved via averaging over a wider stencil".
6. **Central-difference schemes** (§4.5, eqs. 28–31). Field-CD takes the
   cell electric field as the mean of $-\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ at levels
   $n$ and provisional, and updates the cell field by central differences
   of it (eqs. 28–29); flux-CD averages four upwind face fluxes to the
   cell (eq. 31). Both conserve the central divergence exactly, need no
   staggering, and have "no checker-board like instability"; field-CD
   needs no spatial interpolation. Both are second order on uniform grids.
7. **Initial and boundary data** (§4.6). Data must satisfy the scheme's
   own discrete divergence: set $\mathbf B$ from a vector potential
   $\mathbf A$ at cell centres (CD) or corners (CT). Careless flux
   interpolation can put flux through a perfectly conducting wall.
8. **Projection** (§5.1, eqs. 32–34). Solve
   $\nabla^2\Phi + \nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$ and set
   $\mathbf B \leftarrow \mathbf B + \nabla\Phi$ (the paper's potential is
   $-\Phi$). The discrete divergence vanishes exactly if the Laplacian is
   built as the divergence of the gradient with the same difference
   operators. The current is unchanged.
9. **Minimal correction and consistency** (§§5.2–5.3, eqs. 35–44).
   Minimizing $\tfrac12\Vert\mathbf B - \mathbf B^*\Vert^2$ under the
   discrete constraint gives the projection when the difference operators
   are antisymmetric (central differences on uniform Cartesian grids). The
   projected field is at least as close to the provisional one as any
   CT/CD result, so its error is bounded by the worse of the base-scheme
   and CT/CD orders, also for discontinuous solutions. Total energy is
   unaffected; optionally (eq. 40) the thermal energy is held fixed instead.
10. **Poisson solver and boundaries** (§§5.4–5.5). FFT and direct solvers
    cost 20–30% and restrict the boundary conditions; a few CG or BiCGSTAB
    iterations remove the short-wavelength divergence; multigrid "may not
    pay off". Cost about 15% on Cartesian and 30% on general structured
    grids. Ghost-cell rules for the potential are given for periodic,
    shifted-periodic, fixed, open, conducting-wall and antisymmetric
    boundaries. "It is not the projection scheme, but their particular
    Poisson solver (fast Fourier transform) that restricts the possible
    boundary conditions" (said of a remark by Ryu, Miniati, Jones and
    Frank).
11. **Curvilinear and axisymmetric CT/CD** (Appendix B, eqs. B.1–B.9).
12. **Conclusions** (§7, Table IX). All seven keep second order on smooth
    flows; only the eight-wave scheme is non-conservative; the base scheme
    is the least robust; field-CT has spurious oscillations;
    transport-flux-CT is "more complicated, less versatile, and typically
    not more accurate than the flux-CT scheme". "The projection, the new
    field-CD, and the flux-CT schemes are the most accurate for these test
    problems." Accuracy shows "no straightforward relationship" with
    keeping some discrete divergence small (§6.3.2, Table IV).

# Tests

2D ideal MHD; mostly the one-step dimensionally split TVD scheme
(Roe-type flux, MC limiter, no entropy fix, alternating sweeps,
$\nu \approx 0.8$, §6.2). Error (eq. 45): the relative $L^1$ difference
from a coarsened reference, averaged over the non-zero primitive
variables ($\bar\delta_N$ on $N$ cells per direction).

* **Circularly polarized Alfvén wave** (§6.3.1, Table II), at 30° in a
  periodic $1/\cos30°\times1/\sin30°$ box, amplitude 0.1, $\rho = 1$,
  $p = 0.1$, $\gamma = 5/3$, $t = 5$, $N = 8$–64, travelling and standing.
  Travelling $\bar\delta_{64}$: projection 0.012, base 0.012, eight-wave
  0.012, transport-flux-CT 0.014, field-CD 0.025, flux-CD/CT 0.029,
  field-CT 0.044. All about second order.
* **2D rotated shock tube** (§6.3.2, Tables III–V, Figs. 10–13). The
  Dai–Woodward / Ryu–Jones–Frank colliding-flow tube
  ($\rho = 1$, $v_\parallel = \pm10$, $p = 20$ and 1,
  $B_\parallel = B_\perp = 5/\sqrt{4\pi}$, $\gamma = 5/3$) rotated by
  $\theta_{\mathrm{rot}} = \tan^{-1}2 \approx 63.4°$ on $N\times2$ cells
  with shifted-periodic boundaries, minmod, $N = 256$, against a 1D run on
  1024 cells. $\bar\delta_{256}$ (Table III): field-CD 0.0231, flux-CD,
  projection and flux-CT 0.0235, base 0.0238, transport-flux-CT 0.0239,
  field-CT 0.0291, eight-wave 0.0307. The eight-wave normal field is
  wrong by 3–7% between the fast shocks and the error does not converge;
  it comes from the source terms. At 45° the eight-wave normal field is
  wrong by 5–10%. Table IV: the CT schemes keep the corner divergence at
  $10^{-12}$ but not the central one, the CD schemes the reverse;
  projection (20 CG iterations per step) keeps the central one at 0.3
  (max).
* **2.5D rotated shock tube** (§6.3.3, Table VI): the Ryu–Jones /
  Dai–Woodward problem with two fast shocks, two rotational
  discontinuities, two slow shocks and a contact, at 45°,
  $t = 0.2/\sqrt2$. Here the eight-wave scheme is fine and field-CT has
  about 1.5 times the best error.
* **Orszag–Tang** (§6.4, Table VII, Fig. 16), $[0, 2\pi]^2$,
  $\rho = 25/9$, $p = 5/3$, unscaled $\mathbf B$, $\gamma = 5/3$,
  $N = 50, 100, 200$. At $t = 1$, $\bar\delta_{200}$ from 0.0026 (field-CD)
  to 0.0045 (transport-flux-CT), rate about 1.6; at $t = 3.14$ from 0.0300
  (field-CD) to 0.0705 (base, which needed minmod), rate about 1.
  Field-CT looks sharp but has spurious features.
* **Cloud–shock** (§6.5, after Dai–Woodward), unsplit TVD-MUSCL–Hancock,
  $\nu \le 0.6$: all schemes within 5% of $\bar\delta_{50} = 0.2$,
  $\bar\delta_{100} = 0.15$, $\bar\delta_{200} = 0.08$.
* **Rotor** (§6.6, Table VIII, Figs. 19–20). First rotor: speed 2 at
  $r = 0.1$, taper to 0.115, $p = 1$, $B_x = 5/\sqrt{4\pi}$, $\gamma = 1.4$,
  $t = 0.15$. Second rotor, "used to obtain the plots in BS": speed 1,
  $p = 0.5$, $B_x = 2.5/\sqrt{4\pi}$, $\gamma = 5/3$, $t = 0.295$, with the
  energy correction (eq. 40), which removes pressure undershoots at a
  total-energy error below 0.4%. Projection is best in both. Field-CT,
  with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B$ at round-off, distorts the rotating velocity
  field and the eight-wave scheme does not, so the simulations "do not
  support" Balsara and Spicer's explanation of that distortion by the
  divergence error.
