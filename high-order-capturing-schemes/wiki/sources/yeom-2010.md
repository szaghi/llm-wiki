# Summary

A planar shock ($M_s = 1.7$, air at 300 K and 101 kPa) runs over a 25°
wedge into a mixture of gas and inert microdroplets (diameter
0.5–2.5 µm, droplet volume fraction 0.002–2%). Both phases are
compressible: ideal gas, and a stiffened-gas liquid. They are coupled by
an eight-equation two-fluid model with non-conservative
interfacial-pressure terms, drag and heat transfer. The hyperbolic part
is solved by Toro's weighted-average-flux (WAF) method on an HLL Riemann
solver with a TVD limiter; the sources are Strang-split and integrated
with classical RK4, for second order overall. The gas-only limit is
checked against a pure-gas Euler WAF run and an interferogram; the
parameter study concerns the relaxation zones of the
[single Mach reflection](/definitions/mach-reflection.md). The HLL and
WAF content belongs to
[approximate Riemann solvers](/methods/approximate-riemann-solvers.md).

# Key claims

1. **Model** (§2, eqs. 1–12). Eight conserved variables (mass, two
   momenta and total energy of each phase, weighted by its volume
   fraction), plus non-conservative terms in the gradient of the gas
   volume fraction and a drag/heat-transfer source. The phase pressures
   differ by surface tension (eq. 4). The droplets follow a stiffened-gas
   law (water: ratio of specific heats 2.35, stiffening pressure
   $10^9$ Pa, energy constant $-1167\times10^3$ J/kg; Table 2). The
   interfacial pressure is the gas pressure minus a slip-velocity term
   with coefficient 1.2; hyperbolicity for a coefficient $\ge 1$ is cited
   to Paillère et al. (2003), not proved here (eq. 11). The interfacial velocity
   is the mass-weighted mean (eq. 12).
2. **Splitting** (§3.1, eq. 36). Source half step, hyperbolic full step,
   source half step; the sources by classical RK4. Called "second-order
   accurate"; no convergence test is shown.
3. **HLL flux on quadrilaterals** (§3.2, eqs. 37–39). Cell-centred finite
   volume, each face flux
   $\hat f = \dfrac{S_R f(u^-) - S_L f(u^+) + S_L S_R (u^+ - u^-)}{S_R - S_L}$
   in the face-normal direction. The interface gas volume fraction that
   multiplies the non-conservative terms is weighted with the same
   coefficients, $S_R/(S_R - S_L)$ on the left value and
   $-S_L/(S_R - S_L)$ on the right, which suppresses "artificial source
   and sink" (after Saurel & Abgrall 1999 and the authors' 2009 paper).
4. **Wave-speed estimates** (eqs. 40–44). Either the "simple" estimate,
   the minimum and maximum over both phases of $v_n \mp c$ on the two
   sides, or Nguyen et al.'s effective sound speeds of the mixture, which
   combine both phases' densities, sound speeds and volume fractions.
   Nguyen's is called more accurate and the simple one more robust;
   Nguyen's is used except for solid copper particles.
5. **WAF to second order** (eqs. 45–51). The flux is the average over the
   cell of the physical flux of the local Riemann solution at the half
   step (eq. 45). In TVD form it is the central flux
   $\tfrac12\left(f(u^-) + f(u^+)\right)$ minus half the sum, over the two
   HLL waves, of the flux jump across each wave times the sign of its
   Courant number $\nu_k = S_k\Delta t/h$ times a limiter (eq. 46); the
   interface volume fraction is treated the same way (eq. 47). The
   limiter's argument is the ratio of the upwind to the local jump of the
   droplet partial density. MINBEE: 1 for a ratio $\le 0$,
   $1 - (1 - \vert\nu_k\vert)$ times the ratio between 0 and 1, and
   $\vert\nu_k\vert$ for a ratio $\ge 1$ (eq. 51). The 2D extension is
   cited to Toro; whether it is dimensionally split is not said.
6. **Walls and inflow** (§3.3, eqs. 52–56, Fig. 3). Mirror ghost cells:
   volume fraction, density and pressure copied, normal velocity
   reflected; the wall flux comes from the same Riemann solver.
   Transmissive boundaries copy the boundary cell. The wedge is a mesh
   boundary, so the mesh is body-fitted (inferred; the mesh type is not
   named); there is no immersed or cut-cell treatment. The start-up
   expansion of the Rankine–Hugoniot initial shock is cut away after a
   first run, following Toro's book.
7. **Time step** (eqs. 57–59). Courant number 0.4 on the smallest face
   length over the largest phase speed $\vert\mathbf v\vert + c$.
8. **Gas-only limit** (§4.1, Figs. 4–5). With a gas volume fraction of
   $1 - 10^{-8}$ (the model is ill-posed at 0 and 1), the two-fluid
   result matches the Euler WAF solution (density labels 2.98–3.35 kg/m³
   coincide, pre-shock 1.17) and the interferogram "very well", and the
   wall density matches the Euler curve, including the peak near the
   corner. The comparison is visual, with no error norm.
9. **Two-phase findings** (§§4.2, 5, Figs. 6–18). A larger droplet
   volume fraction slows the shock, narrows the relaxation zones and
   strengthens the shock reflected from the mixture's material boundary;
   droplet size in 0.5–2.5 µm "seldom affects" the flow. At $t = 1.8$ ms
   and 0.2% droplets, the wall gas-density jump at the slip-line foot is
   5.78 (6.77/1.17) against 2.71 (3.17/1.17) in pure gas, a factor 2.13.
   Copper particles give the slowest shock and the most smearing.

**Tests** (§4). Inviscid two-fluid model (gas viscosity enters only the
closures), air with $\gamma = 1.4$; 560 × 280 cells, Courant number 0.4;
gas at rest at 300 K and 101 kPa, initial shock 0.04 m right of the
inlet; a gas-only zone 0.7 m high and 0.4 m long ahead of the mixture, and
a wedge 1 m long with half-angle 25°; output at $t = 1.8$ ms. The domain
extents and the wedge apex position are not given in numbers. The
interferogram ($M_s = 1.7$, 25° wedge, air) is reproduced from Toro's
book; the paper runs no experiment, and the two-phase cases have no
experimental reference. The gas-only case is a single Mach reflection on
a straight wedge at low $M_s$, a relative of
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), not a
use of it.
