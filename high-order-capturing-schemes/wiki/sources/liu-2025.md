# Summary

ILW used as a two-sided interface condition between two PDE systems.
Merged into the ILW page (section "Interfaces between two media"), the
characteristic-decomposition, extrapolation and SSP pages, and the
[shock-driven cylinder lift-off](/benchmarks/shock-cylinder-lift-off.md)
benchmark.

# Key claims

1. **Discretization** (§3.1). Third-order FD-WENO in Shu–Osher form for
   the fluid; P² DG with a Lax–Friedrichs flux for the solid; one
   third-order TVD-RK for both, so they exchange data at every stage.
2. **Interface unknowns** (§3.2, eqs. 3.12–3.27, page images). For
   $k = 0, 1, 2$ the normal derivatives of the fluid and solid primitive
   variables solve one local linear system: outgoing characteristics of
   each side, from extrapolated data, plus velocity and traction
   conditions and their time derivatives turned into space derivatives by
   the PDEs. Fluid ghost values follow by Taylor expansion; the solid flux
   uses the $k = 0$ state.
3. **Arbitrary order** (Remark 3.1). Higher derivatives follow similarly;
   only third order is implemented.
4. **Coupled versus weakly coupled** (Remark 3.2). Weak coupling (each
   solver takes the other's value separately) suffers the added-mass
   instability; the coupled system needs no sub-iteration.
5. **2D** (§3.3, Appendix A). A 9×9 system per $k$ in local normal frames
   of each medium; tangential and mixed derivatives extrapolated; time
   derivatives of the normal from the parametrized interface; separate
   ILW passes for fluid ghost points and solid quadrature points.
6. **Stability** (§4, Figs. 5–6). A 1D linear model with a fixed
   interface, analysed through the eigenvalues of the RK3 amplification
   matrix at a Courant number of 0.2. The coupled version is stable for
   all $C_a \in [0, 1)$ at density ratios $10^{-2}$ to $10^2$. The weak one
   is stable for all $C_a$ only below a fluid-to-solid density ratio of
   about 1.67.
7. **Tests** (§5). Manufactured 1D and 2D solutions converge at third
   order (Tables 1–6). An elastic-piston shock tube, a fluid expansion
   driving a piston and a piston–spring system agree with reference
   solutions, while weak coupling fails below the stated solid densities.
   Also a deforming elastic cylinder lifted by a Mach 3 shock, an elastic
   wave hitting a fluid-filled cavity, and a shock on a deforming steel
   panel (Giordano et al.). The interface procedure takes 18–40% of the
   CPU time (Tables 8, 10, 12).
