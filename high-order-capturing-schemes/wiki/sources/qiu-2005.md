# Summary

The first systematic use of finite-volume WENO as a limiter for
Cockburn–Shu RKDG, on rectangular meshes. Troubled cells are detected
by the usual TVB minmod limiter. Their higher moments are rebuilt from
WENO point values at quadrature points, keeping the cell average. Merged
into the new [WENO limiters for DG](/methods/weno-limited-dg.md) page
and into five benchmark pages.

# Key claims

1. **Bottleneck** (§1): robust limiters are "one of the bottlenecks"
   of DG for conservation laws. ENO/WENO is more robust than slope
   limiting, especially at high order.
2. **Framework** (§1): (i) identify troubled cells, (ii) replace their
   polynomials by conservative, equally accurate, less oscillatory
   reconstructions. If (ii) keeps the order, false alarms in (i) cost
   only time.
3. **Reconstruction** (§2): order at least $2K + 1$ from $2K + 1$
   cells. The points are 2-point Gauss ($K = 1$), 4-point Gauss–Lobatto
   ($K = 2$) and 4-point Gauss ($K = 3$). Linear weights are given for
   $K = 2$ at $x_{i+1/2}$ and $x_i \pm \sqrt5 h/10$. The middle Gauss
   point for $K = 2$ has negative linear weights (Remark 2.1). Direct
   moment reconstruction has no linear weights for $K = 3$ (Remark 2.2).
   They use $q = 2$ and $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, and characteristic
   detection for systems.
4. **Accuracy** (Tables 1–3): with $M = 0.01$ the limited RKDG keeps
   the order and error magnitude of the unlimited scheme for 1D/2D
   Burgers and 2D Euler on randomly perturbed meshes.
5. **Shocks** (§3.2): larger $M$ flags fewer cells and resolves better.
   $M = 300$ is too large for the shock–density interaction.
   Troubled-cell percentages for the double Mach reflection are given in
   Table 4 (2–25%).
6. **Outlook** (§4): better detectors, compact Hermite-type
   reconstruction, ENO where linear weights fail to exist, and
   unstructured and 3D meshes.
