# Summary

Brings WENO into the central (Nessyahu–Tadmor) framework, extending
Bianco–Puppo–Russo's central ENO. The CWENO reconstruction blends three
parabolas with weights whose constants are symmetric about the cell
centre, which gives a fourth-order scheme from second-degree polynomials;
time integration of the staggered fluxes uses Runge–Kutta with natural
continuous extensions. Merged into [central WENO](/methods/central-weno.md)
and the system-indicator discussion of
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md).

# Key claims

1. **Central framework** (§2): staggered cell averages; flux quadrature
   at smooth points under a CFL condition; RK with NCE minimizes flux
   evaluations (third- and fourth-order coefficient sets).
2. **CWENO** (§3): three parabolas interpolating three cell averages
   each; convex combination over a five-cell stencil; two sets of
   symmetric constants (cell averages and flux derivative, Table 3.1);
   any symmetric set gives fourth order at the centre; fifth order would
   need a non-convex combination. Jiang–Shu indicator, $q = 2$,
   $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$; scheme nearly insensitive to $\varepsilon$.
3. **Algorithms** (§4): scalar and systems; indicators once per time step
   suffice.
4. **Tests** (§5): third and fourth order reached; "super-convergence" on
   $\sin^4$-type data explained by coarse-grid errors; Burgers weights;
   composite linear advection; Sod, Lax and blast waves with
   component-wise, global ($L^2$-scaled over components) and density
   indicators — global cheaper and better than component-wise, density
   slightly better still.
5. No Riemann solvers, characteristic projections, flux splittings or
   eigen-decompositions are needed.
