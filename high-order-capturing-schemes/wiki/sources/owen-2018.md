# Summary

The authors' Chombo-based fourth-order, free-stream-preserving mapped
finite-volume AMR code is extended to thermally perfect, multispecies,
viscous compressible flow. The new piece is the viscous-flux operator.
Shock capturing (limiter, Riemann solver) is delegated to earlier papers
and not described here. Merged into the new
[fourth-order finite volume](/methods/fourth-order-finite-volume.md) and
[free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md)
pages, and into the AMR, reconstruction, SSP, Sod and vortex pages.

# Key claims

1. **Equations** (§2). Conservative form for $\mathcal J u$ on a static
   mapping with metric matrix $\mathcal J\nabla_{\mathbf x}\mathbf X$;
   Fick and Fourier fluxes with one diffusion coefficient for all species,
   from a Schmidt or Lewis number; NASA (Gordon–McBride) enthalpy fits and
   mixture-averaged transport properties.
2. **Scheme** (§3.1, eq. 10). Fourth-order finite volume with classical
   RK4 (not SSP); inviscid mapped fluxes as in Guzik et al. (2015).
3. **Viscous fluxes** (§3.1–3.2, eqs. 11–17). A fourth-order product rule
   for face averages and a point-to-face-average conversion with the
   tangential second derivatives, in five steps. Transport properties
   are evaluated pointwise at face centres instead of being averaged.
4. **AMR** (§3.3). Block-structured, subcycled (Berger–Colella), refinement
   ratios 2 and 4.
5. **Refinement indicator** (§3.5, eq. 19). Normalized undivided
   differences with a threshold, OR-combined. A criterion consistent with
   fourth order is called still open.
6. **Results.** Couette flow, a species-diffusion bubble and a stationary
   viscous vortex converge at fourth order (Tables 1–3). Free streams are
   preserved to $2.1\times10^{-14}$ under moving refinement on a warped
   grid (§4.6, Table 4). A multispecies shock tube, shock box, Mach
   reflection on a ramp (against Ben-Dor and Glass) and a jet-mixing
   burner are shown qualitatively (§§4.1–4.2, 5). Strong shocks need a
   limiter that the paper does not specify (§5).
