# Summary

Introduces weighted ENO. Instead of the "smoothest" stencil, every cell
uses all $r$ candidate interpolants of the cell-average ENO
reconstruction, combined with weights that are $O(1)$ on smooth stencils
and $O(h^r)$ on discontinuous ones, so the ENO property is kept while
truncation errors cancel. The weights' constants are chosen to cancel
the leading error at the one interface point the upwind flux uses,
giving order $r+1$. Smoother dependence on the data is noted as a
possible route to a convergence proof, and lower communication as an
advantage on parallel machines. Merged into [WENO](/methods/weno.md),
[smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and
[essentially non-oscillatory](/definitions/essentially-non-oscillatory.md).

# Key claims

1. **Formulation** (§2): cell-average scheme with two-point Lipschitz
   monotone fluxes (Engquist–Osher, Godunov, Roe with entropy fix,
   local LF, eqs. 2.4–2.6); third- and fourth-order TVD Runge–Kutta.
2. **Reconstruction** (§3): candidates from the primitive function;
   smoothness indicator $IS_j$ from averaged squares of undivided
   differences; Definition 1 (ENO property of the weights);
   $\alpha_k = C_k/(\varepsilon + IS)^r$ with $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$.
3. **One order of improvement** (§3.4): $C_k$ from the sign of
   $f'(\bar u_j)$ (eq. 3.12) make the error $O(h^{r+1})$ at the chosen
   interface point, hence $L$ is $(r+1)$-th order away from sonic points.
   Explicit $r = 2, 3$ schemes (§§3.5–3.6).
4. **Experiments** (§4): linear advection of $\sin$ and $\sin^4$ (about
   fourth and fifth order for $r = 2, 3$, "surprisingly better than"
   theory; no loss on $\sin^4$, unlike standard ENO), discontinuous
   profiles, Burgers with a shock–rarefaction interaction (errors 0.1 from
   the shock of the same size as the smooth case), non-convex fluxes and
   Buckley–Leverett, Sod and Lax with characteristic reconstruction,
   smooth Euler data (fifth order for $r = 3$).
