# Summary

Brio and Wu build a Roe-type (linearized Riemann solver) upwind scheme
for the 1D ideal-MHD equations, a $7\times7$ system that is not strictly
hyperbolic. For $\gamma = 2$ they find an exact Roe matrix: the Jacobian
at a Roe-averaged state. For general $\gamma$ they find none and use the
Jacobian at an arithmetic-mean state instead. They renormalize the
Jeffrey–Taniuti eigenvectors so that the set stays complete where
$B_x = 0$ or $B_\perp = 0$, and give the wave strengths in closed form.
The test is a coplanar Riemann problem with Sod's hydrodynamic data,
$B_x = 0.75$ and $\gamma = 2$, now the
[Brio–Wu shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md), run with four
schemes. With the complete eigenvectors they prove that ideal MHD is
nonconvex, so a slow intermediate shock with an attached slow rarefaction
(a compound wave) can form. Merged into the
[Brio–Wu shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md),
[local characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md)
and [approximate Riemann solvers](/methods/approximate-riemann-solvers.md),
and the origin of the new [nonconvexity of ideal MHD](/results/mhd-nonconvexity.md)
result page.

# Key claims

1. **Ideal MHD is not strictly hyperbolic** (§§I, II, IV.2). The 1D
   Jacobian is $7\times7$, with eigenvalues, in nondecreasing order,
   $v_x - c_f,\ v_x - c_A,\ v_x - c_s,\ v_x,\ v_x + c_s,\ v_x + c_A,\ v_x + c_f$
   (eq. 31), where $c_A^2 = B_x^2/\rho$ (eq. 32) and
   $c_{f,s}^2 = \tfrac12\left[(c^2 + \vert\mathbf B\vert^2/\rho) \pm \sqrt{(c^2 + \vert\mathbf B\vert^2/\rho)^2 - 4c^2B_x^2/\rho}\right]$
   (eq. 33). Up to five eigenvalues coincide. At $B_x = 0$,
   $c_s = c_A = 0$ and $v_x$ has multiplicity 5. At $B_\perp = 0$,
   $c_f^2 = \max(c^2, c_A^2)$ and $c_s^2 = \min(c^2, c_A^2)$, and
   $v_x \pm c_A$ has multiplicity 2 if $c^2 \ne c_A^2$ and 3 if
   $c^2 = c_A^2$.
2. **Roe matrix at $\gamma = 2$** (§IV.1, eqs. 24–30). The Jacobian at an
   averaged state satisfies
   $f(u_{i+1}) - f(u_i) = A_{i+1/2}(u_{i+1} - u_i)$ (property 9A) when the
   velocities and the total enthalpy are Roe-averaged (weights
   $\sqrt{\rho_i}$, $\sqrt{\rho_{i+1}}$), the density is the geometric mean
   $\sqrt{\rho_i\rho_{i+1}}$, and the tangential field is
   $\sqrt{\rho_i\rho_{i+1}}\,\frac{B_{y,i}/\sqrt{\rho_i} + B_{y,i+1}/\sqrt{\rho_{i+1}}}{\sqrt{\rho_i} + \sqrt{\rho_{i+1}}}$
   (and likewise $B_z$). For the velocities this "is identical to the
   solution for the Euler equations"; applied to Euler, the same
   procedure gives Roe's average uniquely.
3. **General $\gamma$: no Roe matrix found** (§IV.1). The algebraic system
   does not decouple, and "we are not able to solve it analytically"; no
   parameterization was found either. The Jacobian at an arithmetic-mean
   state satisfies Roe's properties 9B and 9C but **not 9A**, so
   stationary discontinuities are no longer exact steady solutions,
   though "they still can be resolved with few grid points". Averaging
   density, velocity, field and **total pressure** keeps the eigenvalues
   real; averaging the energy instead "may lead to a negative averaged
   pressure which is not allowed".
4. **Complete eigenvectors** (§§I, IV.3, eqs. 34–46). The Jeffrey–Taniuti
   eigenvectors (eq. 34) are undefined (0/0) near $B_x = 0$ or
   $B_\perp = 0$, and their matrix becomes singular (determinant
   proportional to $(c_f^2 - c_s^2)^2$). The fast, slow and Alfvén
   eigenvectors are multiplied by $\alpha_f$, $\alpha_s$ and
   $\sqrt\rho/B_\perp$, with
   $\alpha_f = \sqrt{c_f^2 - c_A^2}/\sqrt{c_f^2 - c_s^2}$ (eq. 39), and
   $\alpha_s$ printed as
   $$\alpha_s = \frac{1}{c_A}\frac{\sqrt{c_A^2 - c_s^2}}{\sqrt{c_f^2 - c_s^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{c_f^2 - c^2}}{\sqrt{c_f^2 - c_s^2}}\,\frac{1}{c_f}$$
   (eq. 40; the two forms agree by $c_s c_f = c\,c_A$, eq. 35). As printed
   this $\alpha_s$ has the dimension of an inverse speed, while
   $\alpha_f$ is dimensionless and eq. 46 sets $\alpha_s = 1$; it is the
   Ryu–Jones $\alpha_s$ times $1/c_f$. Whether this is a misprint or a
   deliberate scaling cannot be decided from the paper. The tangential
   direction cosines $B_y/B_\perp$, $B_z/B_\perp$ (eqs. 41–42) are set to
   $1/\sqrt2$ when $B_\perp = 0$ (eq. 45), and $\alpha_f = \alpha_s = 1$
   when also $c^2 = c_A^2$ (eq. 46), with $\mathrm{sgn}(0) := 1$. The fast
   and slow eigenvectors are continuous only if the slow pair (when
   $c^2 > c_A^2$) or the fast pair (when $c^2 < c_A^2$) is multiplied by
   the sign of the tangential field, which the paper calls "crucial" for
   classifying the characteristic fields.
5. **Wave strengths in closed form** (§IV.4, eqs. 47–53). Sum and
   difference variables of the strengths of paired waves split the
   decomposition of $u_{i+1} - u_i$ into two $3\times3$ systems plus one
   scalar equation for the entropy wave.
6. **Ideal MHD is nonconvex** (§VI). With the complete eigenvectors,
   $\nabla_u\lambda^{(p)}\cdot r^{(p)}$ is proportional to $B_y$ near
   $B_y = 0$ ($B_z = 0$), for the fast field if $c^2 < c_A^2$ and for the
   slow field if $c^2 > c_A^2$. Those fields are neither genuinely
   nonlinear nor linearly degenerate. The Jeffrey–Taniuti conclusion that
   the fast and slow fields are genuinely nonlinear (their Theorem E.1)
   "is not correct, since the set (34) is not complete". A small-field
   expansion of the slow shock curve (eqs. 54–55) shows where a
   rarefaction can attach to a shock; details on the
   [result page](/results/mhd-nonconvexity.md).
7. **The compound wave contains an intermediate shock** (§VI). $B_y$
   changes sign across the slow shock of the compound wave. If
   intermediate shocks were inadmissible, then "all numerical schemes
   tested in Section 5 admit nonphysical shocks". The authors cite Wu
   (1987): resistive-MHD runs show that some intermediate shocks,
   including this one, form by steepening of a continuous wave.
8. **Cost** (§VII). The Roe-type scheme costs "about 10 times more
   floating point operations per grid point per time step than the
   Lax–Wendroff scheme", but gives "a reasonable result" with 200 points
   instead of 800, which also cuts the number of steps by 4, so it is
   "more efficient than the Lax–Wendroff scheme with 800 grid points".
   "Nonlinearly stable" is claimed from experience, not proved.
   Nonconvexity "was first discovered by using the upwind scheme" on this
   test.

**Tests** (§V, Figs. 2–9).
* *T1, the coplanar Riemann problem.* Left
  $(\rho, v_x, v_y, p, B_y) = (1, 0, 0, 1, 1)$, right
  $(0.125, 0, 0, 0.1, -1)$, $B_x = 0.75$, $\gamma = 2$; "the initial
  hydrodynamical data used here are identical to those in Sod's shock
  tube problem" ([Sod (1978)](/sources/sod-1978.md)). 800 points,
  $h = 1$, $\Delta t = 0.2$ ("CFL ~ 0.8"), 400 steps, jump in the middle;
  no boundary conditions stated. Waves: fast rarefaction and slow
  compound wave to the left; contact, slow shock and fast rarefaction to
  the right, checked by Riemann invariants and Rankine–Hugoniot
  conditions. Schemes: second-order Roe-type with the $\gamma = 2$ Roe
  matrix and Harten's artificial compression on the linearly degenerate
  field (sharp, non-oscillatory); the same with an arithmetic-mean state
  (constant states agree to 4 significant digits, transitions to 2);
  two-step Lax–Wendroff with Lapidus viscosity (oscillations, contact over
  10–15 points); first-order Lax–Friedrichs (slow shock over about 20
  points; converges to the upwind solution on 20,000 points); FCT
  (oscillations, worse at CFL 0.8). Numbers on the
  [benchmark page](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md).
* *T2.* $B_y = -0.4$ on the right, otherwise as T1 (no figure): the
  upwind and Lax–Friedrichs solutions contain "an almost stationary slow
  intermediate shock where the magnetic field changes its sign";
  Lax–Wendroff could not be made stable "with any value of artificial
  viscosity".
* *T3.* $B_x = 0$ and left pressure 1000, otherwise as T1: a gas-dynamic
  Riemann problem in the total pressure with a Mach 15.5 shock, against
  the exact solution (Fig. 7). Grid and end time are not printed (the
  Fig. 7 abscissa spans 0–200). Harten's artificial compression "does not
  prevent the tangential discontinuity from spreading", and with the
  second-order correction it causes a density undershoot that diffuses in
  time.
