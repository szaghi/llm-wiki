# Summary

A step-by-step 1D ideal-MHD code: Harten's (1983) second-order TVD
scheme on a Roe-type linearization through the Brio–Wu eigensystem, with
interface states from arithmetic averaging. The paper writes out all
seven eigenvalues and the right and left eigenvectors, in the singular
"natural" form and in the renormalized form that stays regular. It also
builds a nonlinear MHD Riemann solver after Dai and Woodward, with exact
rarefactions, and uses the two to check each other on twelve shock tubes.
Merged into the [characteristic-decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md),
[Riemann-solver](/methods/approximate-riemann-solvers.md) and
[TVD](/properties/total-variation-diminishing.md) pages, the
[Brio–Wu](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md) and
[oblique shock-tube](/benchmarks/oblique-mhd-shock-tubes.md) benchmarks,
and the new [Ryu–Jones shock-tube](/benchmarks/ryu-jones-shock-tubes.md)
benchmark.

# Key claims

1. **System** (§2.1, eqs. 2.5–2.9): seven variables, $B_x$ constant, units
   without $4\pi$. Hyperbolic, but eigenvalues can coincide.
2. **Eigenvalues** (eqs. 2.10–2.20, from the page image):
   $v_x \mp c_f$, $v_x \mp c_A$, $v_x \mp c_s$, $v_x$ with
   $c_{f,s}^2 = \tfrac12\left[c^2 + |\mathbf B|^2/\rho \pm \sqrt{(c^2 + |\mathbf B|^2/\rho)^2 - 4c^2B_x^2/\rho}\right]$.
3. **Natural eigenvectors are singular** (eqs. 2.21–2.26) where $B_x = 0$
   or $B_\perp = 0$.
4. **Renormalized eigenvectors** (eqs. 2.27–2.73), after Brio and Wu:
   $\alpha_f = \sqrt{c_f^2 - c_A^2}/\sqrt{c_f^2 - c_s^2}$,
   $\alpha_s = \sqrt{c_f^2 - c^2}/\sqrt{c_f^2 - c_s^2}$, and the tangential
   direction cosines $B_y/B_\perp$, $B_z/B_\perp$, set to $1/\sqrt2$ when
   $B_\perp = 0$ (eq. 2.37); $\alpha_f = \alpha_s = 1$ at the triple point
   (eq. 2.38).
5. **Continuity** (eq. 2.74): some columns are discontinuous; multiplying
   the slow pair (when $c^2 > c_A^2$) or the fast pair (when $c^2 < c_A^2$)
   by the sign of the tangential field makes them continuous.
6. **Planar fields** (eq. 2.79): for $B_z = v_z = 0$ the Alfvén
   eigenvectors are trivial, so 2D-field flows produce no rotational
   discontinuities.
7. **Interface state** (§2.3): the Roe average is not analytic for
   $\gamma \ne 2$, so the primitive variables are averaged arithmetically;
   at $\gamma = 2$ this does "just as well" as the Roe linearization.
8. **TVD scheme** (eqs. 2.87–2.95): Harten's flux with a per-family entropy
   fix, parameter 0.1, 0.2, 0.1, 0.0 for the fast, Alfvén, slow and entropy
   families, Courant number 0.8. Steepening of the Alfvén families
   (eqs. 2.96–2.98) narrows rotational discontinuities from 6–8 to 3–5
   cells; contact steepening adds oscillations and is not used.
9. **Nonlinear Riemann solver** (§3): eight states, Lagrangian jump
   conditions, rarefactions integrated exactly instead of Dai–Woodward's
   "rarefaction shocks", Newton–Raphson on four parameters seeded by the
   linear solution.
10. **Compound waves** (§5, 8192 cells): the Brio–Wu slow compound wave is a
    "2-4" intermediate shock followed by a slow rarefaction, and a fast
    "1-3" compound structure exists for planar fields. An ideal solver that
    allows rotational discontinuities gives a different solution. The
    authors take no side on the reality of intermediate shocks.

**Tests** (§4, $\gamma = 5/3$, $[0, 1]$, 512 cells): twelve shock tubes,
grouped as planar fields, rotating fields, $B_x = 0$, switch-on/off waves
and compound waves; details on the
[Ryu–Jones benchmark](/benchmarks/ryu-jones-shock-tubes.md). Strong shocks
take 2–4 cells, rotational discontinuities 3–5, contacts 6–8, tangential
discontinuities more than 10.
