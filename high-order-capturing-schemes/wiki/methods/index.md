# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [A posteriori subcell finite-volume limiting (MOOD paradigm)](/methods/a-posteriori-subcell-limiting.md) — Run the unlimited high-order scheme, test the candidate solution for positivity and a relaxed discrete maximum principle, and recompute only the failed cells from the previous time level with a robust finite-volume scheme — for DG, a third-order ADER-WENO scheme on 2K+1 subcells per direction, gathered back into the DG polynomial.
* [ADER one-step schemes (arbitrary high order using derivatives)](/methods/ader.md) — Fully discrete one-step finite-volume and DG schemes that evolve the reconstructed polynomial in each cell in time — by Cauchy–Kowalewski Taylor series or by an element-local space–time Galerkin predictor — and update with space–time-averaged interface fluxes, giving equal order in space and time with one reconstruction per step.
* [Alternative finite-difference WENO (AFD-WENO)](/methods/alternative-finite-difference-weno.md) — The second Shu–Osher finite-difference form: WENO-interpolate point values to interfaces, apply any Riemann solver there, and add even-derivative flux corrections so the flux difference is high order; Balsara, Bhoriya and Shu make it general-purpose up to ninth order with a boundary-centred WENO interpolation of the corrections.
* [Approximate Riemann solvers as building blocks of capturing schemes](/methods/approximate-riemann-solvers.md) — The interface fluxes used by FV and alternative-FD WENO schemes in this corpus: Lax–Friedrichs/Rusanov, Roe, HLL and HLLC (with positivity-preserving wave speeds), HLLI/LLFI, the Riemann-solver-free MUSTA flux, and multidimensional HLL/LLF solvers for edge electric fields.
* [Central WENO (staggered, Levy–Puppo–Russo)](/methods/central-weno.md) — A WENO reconstruction with weights symmetric about the cell centre, used inside a staggered Nessyahu–Tadmor-type central scheme with Runge–Kutta natural continuous extensions: no Riemann solver, no characteristic decomposition; fourth order from parabolas.
* [Central-upwind WENO (WENO-CU6)](/methods/central-upwind-weno.md) — Hu–Wang–Adams sixth-order WENO: a downwind candidate stencil completes a symmetric six-point set whose linear weights give the sixth-order central scheme; WENO-Z-type weights with a large constant and a six-point indicator switch smoothly between central and upwind.
* [Compact-reconstruction WENO (nonlinear compact schemes)](/methods/compact-reconstruction-weno.md) — Nonlinear compact schemes in which low-order compact (implicit) candidate stencils are combined with WENO weights, so the interface values come from a weight-dependent tridiagonal system: fifth-order upwind compact in smooth regions, third-order compact at discontinuities, without a shock switch.
* [Conservative finite-difference (flux) formulation](/methods/conservative-finite-difference.md) — Shu–Osher point-value formulation: the numerical flux approximates a function whose sliding average is the physical flux, so ENO/WENO reconstruction of flux point values gives high-order conservative differences, dimension by dimension, on uniform or smoothly mapped grids.
* [Constrained transport for high-order capturing schemes (MHD)](/methods/constrained-transport.md) — Keeping the magnetic field discretely divergence-free alongside a high-order capturing scheme: the unstaggered vector-potential variant (evolve A on the cell mesh, set B as its discrete curl), Yee-staggered variants (face fields updated by edge electric fields from a multidimensional Riemann solver, from upwind CT, or from high-order staggered differences of WENO fluxes), and the related projection and trace-projection treatments.
* [Contact-discontinuity sharpening (subcell resolution, artificial compression, steepening)](/methods/contact-sharpening.md) — Techniques that counter the progressive smearing of linearly degenerate discontinuities in capturing schemes: Harten's subcell resolution, Harten–Yang artificial compression, and PPM contact steepening; applied only to contacts, never to shocks.
* [Cures for numerical shock instability](/methods/shock-instability-cures.md) — Sensor-driven fixes that make low-dissipation Riemann solvers stable at strong shocks while keeping contacts and shear: widening the HLL wave speeds only inside the dissipation (selective wave modification), scaling the anti-diffusion near shocks, adding an entropy-control term on the entropy wave, adding dissipation only to the shear wave, or switching to a dissipative flux near shocks; all tested at first or second order only.
* [Divergence-free reconstruction of face-centred magnetic fields](/methods/divergence-free-reconstruction.md) — Balsara's reconstruction for Yee-staggered MHD: from the normal component of B and its moments on the six faces of a zone, build a polynomial vector field inside the zone that is divergence-free everywhere and matches the faces; closed form at orders 2–4, energy-minimizing where face data leave freedom, and the source of a unique zone-centred field.
* [Edge-centred curl-preserving (ECCP) reconstruction and schemes](/methods/curl-preserving-reconstruction.md) — The curl analogue of constrained transport: a vector field that must stay curl-free (or keep its curl tied to a divergence-free field) lives on mesh edges, a potential lives at vertices and is upwinded by a multidimensional Riemann solver, and closed-form reconstructions built from 1D WENO edge moments keep the curl exactly; orders 1–4 in 2D and 1–3 in 3D.
* [Energy-stable WENO (ESWENO) with summation-by-parts boundary closures](/methods/energy-stable-weno.md) — WENO finite differences recast as a summation-by-parts operator plus a design-order artificial dissipation that makes the nonlinear scheme provably L2-stable for linear hyperbolic systems, with boundary closures on non-uniform flux points that keep WENO stencil biasing up to the boundary.
* [ENO reconstruction and schemes](/methods/eno-reconstruction.md) — Essentially non-oscillatory schemes: an adaptive stencil grown one point at a time toward the smaller divided difference, giving uniform high order up to discontinuities without monotonicity limiters.
* [Explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/explicit-runge-kutta.md) — One-step methods that combine several evaluations of the semi-discrete operator within a step, written in Butcher form; origin in Runge (1895), order by Taylor matching, stability function, embedded pairs, and the methods that change the weights after the stages are known (relaxation, positivity adaptation).
* [Fourth-order finite-volume method with average–point conversions](/methods/fourth-order-finite-volume.md) — The Colella-group fourth-order finite-volume scheme: cell averages are converted to point values (and back) with h²/24 Laplacian corrections, each face flux is one flux evaluation at a face-centre value plus a transverse-Laplacian correction (no Gauss points), limiting uses a modified Colella–Sekora PPM limiter, and classical RK4 advances the scheme; it extends to mapped grids, viscous fluxes and block-structured AMR.
* [Gaussian-process reconstruction and GP-WENO](/methods/gp-weno.md) — Reconstruction as the posterior mean of a Gaussian process conditioned on cell averages: data-independent weights from a squared-exponential kernel, order 2R+1 on 2R+1 cells, a length hyperparameter tuned to the flow, and WENO-type nonlinear weights from GP (RKHS-norm) smoothness indicators.
* [High-order adaptive mesh refinement for capturing schemes](/methods/high-order-amr.md) — Adaptive Cartesian meshes for better-than-second-order schemes: cell-by-cell tree refinement with one-step ADER updates and local time stepping, and block-structured (Berger–Colella) hierarchies with Runge–Kutta subcycling, where ghost values must match the intermediate stages and refluxing, conservative interpolation and high order pull against each other.
* [HLLEM (HLLI) Riemann solver](/methods/hllem-riemann-solver.md) — HLL with a linear sub-structure along chosen intermediate characteristic fields, which subtracts an anti-diffusive term from the HLL flux: Einfeldt's Euler solver, generalized by Dumbser and Balsara to any conservative or path-conservative system with a diagonal anti-diffusion matrix (alias HLLI); it keeps stationary contacts, shear and Alfvén waves, and is shock-unstable unless cured.
* [Hybrid compact-WENO schemes](/methods/hybrid-compact-weno.md) — Pirozzoli's conservative scheme: a fifth-order compact upwind flux (tridiagonal) in smooth regions, switched to WENO5 or WENO7 near discontinuities detected by density differences; spectral-like resolution with shock capturing, cheaper than WENO.
* [Hyperbolic (GLM) divergence cleaning](/methods/hyperbolic-divergence-cleaning.md) — Dedner's generalized-Lagrange-multiplier treatment of ∇·B: an extra scalar couples to the induction equation so that divergence errors propagate away at a cleaning speed and are damped, keeping all variables cell-centred; second-order CTU and high-order finite-difference forms, the non-conservative EGLM and entropy-consistent variants, and the consistency conditions on the cleaning speed and damping that time-step changes and AMR violate.
* [Inverse Lax–Wendroff numerical boundary treatment](/methods/inverse-lax-wendroff.md) — Ghost-point values for wide-stencil high-order finite differences on Cartesian grids cut by an arbitrary boundary: a Taylor expansion from the boundary point whose normal derivatives come from the boundary data through the PDE (inflow) or from extrapolation (outflow), stable under the periodic CFL limit for any cut-cell distance; extended to sign-changing wind, exact conservation, moving bodies and fluid–structure interfaces.
* [Lax–Wendroff (Taylor) single-step time discretization](/methods/taylor-time-discretization.md) — Single-stage, single-step time stepping that expands the solution or the time-averaged flux in a Taylor series and converts time derivatives to space derivatives by the Cauchy–Kowalewski procedure — the alternative to SSP Runge–Kutta for capturing schemes, attractive for AMR and storage.
* [Local characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) — Applying scalar ENO/WENO/MP reconstruction to the characteristic variables of a Jacobian frozen at each interface or cell; needed for robustness at high order, at the cost of most of the floating-point work.
* [Low-Mach-number numerical fluxes](/methods/low-mach-number-fluxes.md) — Why upwind (Godunov-type) fluxes lose accuracy as the Mach number goes to zero — their momentum dissipation scales like 1/Ma against an O(1) physical flux — and the fixes that rescale only the numerical dissipation: Roe flux preconditioning (Miczek et al.) and the Mach-scaled AUSM+-up; both revert to the standard flux for Ma ≥ 1, neither is tested on shocks.
* [Low-storage Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/low-storage-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods arranged to run in two or three solution-sized registers instead of s+1: the Williamson 2N form, the 2N* form that keeps u^n, and the 3S*-type forms with an error-estimate register; which SSP coefficients survive the storage constraint, and the optimized low-storage pairs for compressible CFD.
* [Mapped WENO (WENO-M)](/methods/mapped-weno.md) — Henrick–Aslam–Powers correction of WENO-JS: each nonlinear weight is mapped by a function flat at its linear weight, which restores fifth order at first-order critical points and removes the dependence of the order on ε.
* [Monotonicity-preserving (MP) limiting](/methods/monotonicity-preserving-limiter.md) — Suresh–Huynh bound on a high-order interface value: keep it unless it leaves an interval built from the data, an interval enlarged near smooth extrema so that accuracy is kept there; monotone data stay monotone under RK3. Applied to WENO it gives MPWENO.
* [Negative linear weights in WENO and their treatment](/methods/negative-linear-weights.md) — When accuracy fixes the linear weights, some can be negative or fail to exist (at some target points, for derivatives and integrals, on unstructured meshes); the WENO convex combination then loses its non-oscillatory property. The remedies are the Shi–Hu–Shu splitting into two positive-weight sums, or giving up the big-stencil order for freely chosen positive weights.
* [Piecewise parabolic method (PPM)](/methods/ppm.md) — Colella–Woodward higher-order Godunov method: fourth-order edge interpolation, monotonized parabolas per cell, characteristic domain-of-dependence states for the Riemann problem, contact steepening and shock flattening.
* [Polynomial WENO reconstruction with a dominant central stencil](/methods/polynomial-weno.md) — WENO that blends whole candidate polynomials of the full degree K — one central and several one-sided or sector stencils — with a large linear weight on the central one, so accuracy never depends on optimal linear weights: no negative weights, any mesh, entire polynomials for ADER, at the price of a wider stencil.
* [Positivity-preserving adaptive Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/positivity-preserving-adaptive-runge-kutta.md) — Enforce componentwise positivity (or box bounds) of a Runge–Kutta step after the stages are computed, by solving a linear program for new weights closest in ℓ1 to the original ones subject to the bounds and the order conditions up to a possibly reduced order; conserves every linear invariant, unlike clipping, and pays off mainly for implicit methods.
* [Positivity-preserving flux limiters (high/low-order flux blending)](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md) — Limit each interface flux towards a first-order positivity-preserving flux, with a blending factor chosen so the forward-Euler update stays admissible: the Hu–Adams–Shu cut-off limiter, the parameterized limiter of Christlieb et al. for MHD, and the PCP hybridization of Balsara et al.
* [Reconstruction from cell averages](/methods/finite-volume-reconstruction.md) — Recovering point values (or polynomials) of $k$-th order accuracy from cell averages via interpolation of the primitive function; the building block of finite-volume ENO/WENO/PPM schemes.
* [Relaxation Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/relaxation-runge-kutta.md) — Scale the Runge–Kutta update by a scalar γ found from one scalar equation per step, so that a convex functional (an entropy) changes by exactly the method's own quadrature of its rate: fully discrete entropy conservation or dissipation for any Runge–Kutta method, order kept when the step is assigned to t^n + γΔt (RRK) and lost by one when assigned to t^n + Δt (IDT).
* [SSP linear multistep methods](/methods/ssp-linear-multistep.md) — Explicit linear multistep methods written as convex combinations of forward-Euler steps from past states, with one operator evaluation per step; the fixed-step optimal coefficients, the sharp bound (Ω−p)/(Ω−1) and closed-form optimal formulae for variable steps, greedy CFL-following step selection, and why they are recommended with positivity limiters.
* [Step-size control for Runge–Kutta integration of hyperbolic problems](/methods/runge-kutta-step-size-control.md) — Choosing Δt from an embedded error estimate with a PI/PID controller instead of from a CFL number: the weighted error norm, the controller, step-size-control stability at the linear stability limit where convection-dominated runs live, its cost against CFL control, and the bound-violation and SSP rules that sit beside it.
* [Symmetric and bandwidth-optimized WENO (WENO-SYMOO, WENO-SYMBO)](/methods/bandwidth-optimized-weno.md) — Martín–Taylor–Wu–Weirs modification of WENO-JS for turbulence DNS: an added downwind candidate makes the stencil set symmetric, and the optimal weights are chosen either for maximum order (2r, non-dissipative) or for bandwidth by minimizing a spectral error integral.
* [Targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/teno.md) — Fu–Hu–Adams scheme family: candidate stencils of incrementally increasing width, a strongly scale-separating smoothness measure, and an ENO-like 0/1 cutoff that keeps each stencil at its linear weight or drops it; the background linear scheme is optimized for dispersion and dissipation.
* [TVD / strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) — Explicit Runge–Kutta methods written as convex combinations of forward-Euler steps, so any norm or convex-functional stability of forward Euler (TVD, maximum norm, positivity, entropy) carries over under a scaled time step.
* [Unequal-stencil and adaptive-order WENO (WENO-ZQ, WENO-AO)](/methods/weno-ao.md) — WENO reconstructions that blend one large centred high-order polynomial with small low-order ones through a weight-adjusted subtraction, so arbitrary positive linear weights keep full order: Zhu–Qiu's quartic plus two linear polynomials, and Balsara–Garain–Shu's adaptive-order WENO-AO(5,3), (7,3), (7,5,3), (9,5,3) with an r = 3 central WENO fallback.
* [Weighted (WENO-type) extrapolation for ghost values](/methods/weighted-extrapolation.md) — Ghost-cell values for finite-difference WENO on Cartesian grids cut by complex boundaries, extrapolated along boundary normals with weights that raise the extrapolation degree only where the data are smooth: Baeza et al.'s dimensionless nested weights with a least-squares blend for stability, and the multi-resolution WENO extrapolation with constant linear weights used with inverse Lax–Wendroff.
* [WENO limiters for discontinuous Galerkin schemes](/methods/weno-limited-dg.md) — Limiting RKDG by first flagging troubled cells (TVB minmod) and then replacing their polynomial — cell average kept — by one rebuilt from WENO reconstructions of neighbouring cell averages at Gauss-type points, which keeps the DG order where good cells are wrongly flagged.
* [WENO reconstruction (weighted ENO)](/methods/weno.md) — A convex combination of all r ENO candidate reconstructions with nonlinear weights driven by smoothness indicators: order 2r−1 in smooth regions (r+1 in the original Liu–Osher–Chan form), ENO-like near discontinuities, with a smooth flux and no logical branches.
* [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) — Borges–Carmona–Costa–Don weights built from a global higher-order smoothness indicator τ (|β₀ − β₂| at fifth order): closer to the linear weights in smooth regions, larger weights on discontinuous stencils, no mapping cost; extended to all odd orders by Castro–Costa–Don.
* [Zhang–Shu positivity-preserving scaling limiter](/methods/positivity-preserving-scaling-limiter.md) — A conservative linear rescaling of the high-order polynomial about its cell average, first in density then in pressure, that puts the Gauss–Lobatto point values in the admissible set; with a positive first-order flux and SSP stepping it makes FV, DG and FD WENO schemes positivity-preserving without losing order.
