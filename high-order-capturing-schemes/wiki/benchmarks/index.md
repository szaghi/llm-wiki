# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [Brio–Wu MHD shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md) — The MHD analogue of Sod's problem, (ρ, p, B_y) = (1, 1, 1 | 0.125, 0.1, −1) with B_x = 0.75 and γ = 2: fast rarefactions, a compound wave (intermediate shock attached to a slow rarefaction), a contact and a slow shock; also run rotated on a 2D grid to test divergence control.
* [Circularly polarized (torsional) Alfvén waves](/benchmarks/circularly-polarized-alfven-wave.md) — An exact nonlinear MHD wave, oblique to the mesh: run for a few periods it measures the convergence order of a multi-D MHD scheme and its divergence control; run for many crossings at a small angle to the mesh, its decaying amplitude measures numerical dissipation.
* [Composite-profile linear advection (Jiang–Shu test)](/benchmarks/composite-advection-profile.md) — Linear advection on a periodic domain of a combination of narrow Gaussians, a square wave, a sharp triangle and a half-ellipse: separates smooth-but-sharp peaks from true discontinuities, and exposes extremum clipping and phase error over long times.
* [Cylindrical and spherical explosion problem](/benchmarks/multidimensional-explosion.md) — A Sod-type state jump on a circle or sphere in [-1, 1]^d (inside ρ = p = 1, outside ρ = 0.125, p = 0.1, γ = 1.4), compared with a 1D radial reference solution with geometric sources: tests symmetry preservation for shocks, contacts and rarefactions not aligned with the grid.
* [Double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) — A Mach 10 shock hitting a 30° wedge (posed as a 60° shock on a flat wall): two Mach stems, a wall jet and slip-line roll-up; the standard 2D robustness and resolution test.
* [Double-rarefaction (near-vacuum) Riemann problems](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction.md) — Two rarefactions moving apart from a symmetric Riemann state, leaving vacuum or near vacuum at the centre (the Einfeldt 1-2-3 type and the Linde–Roe variant); the basic test of positivity and of internal-energy errors at low density.
* [Grid-oblique MHD shock tubes](/benchmarks/oblique-mhd-shock-tubes.md) — One-dimensional MHD Riemann problems (Brio–Wu, Ryu–Jones, and others) rotated against a 2D or 3D Cartesian grid, with shifted-periodic boundaries so that the exact 1D solution is the reference: the standard test of how divergence control behaves at discontinuities, where divergence errors show up as oscillations in the normal field.
* [High-Mach-number astrophysical jet (Mach 80 and Mach 2000)](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md) — A supersonic jet injected into ambient gas, with kinetic energy so dominant that the pressure easily turns negative; the Mach 2000 case is the standard stress test of positivity-preserving schemes.
* [Isentropic vortex advection (smooth accuracy test)](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) — A smooth isentropic vortex advected diagonally through a periodic box and back to its start; the standard multi-dimensional convergence test for Euler, with low-density, relativistic and MHD variants.
* [Lax shock tube](/benchmarks/lax-shock-tube.md) — The 1D Riemann problem (0.445, 0.698, 3.528 | 0.5, 0, 0.571), with a strong contact and an intermediate state that must be built up; a hard test for component-wise high-order schemes.
* [Leblanc shock tube (extreme pressure and density ratio)](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md) — A shock tube with jumps of about a billion in pressure and a thousand in density; the shock position and the contact are hard to get right and the low-density side tests positivity.
* [Mach 3 wind tunnel with a forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) — Woodward–Colella Mach 3 inflow over a step (0.2 high at x = 0.6 in a [0,3]×[0,1] tunnel): reflected shocks, a Mach stem and a vortex sheet from it, plus a corner singularity.
* [Magnetic field-loop advection](/benchmarks/field-loop-advection.md) — A weak cylindrical current loop (A_z = A_0(R − r) inside radius R) advected obliquely across a periodic box: a pure test of how a scheme transports a discontinuous magnetic field, measured by the loop's shape, its spurious oscillations and the decay of magnetic energy.
* [MHD blast wave in a low-β medium](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) — An over-pressured region expanding into a strongly magnetized, low-pressure background; strong anisotropic shocks against tiny thermal pressure make it the standard positivity stress test for MHD, also run in relativistic MHD.
* [MHD rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-rotor.md) — A dense, rapidly spinning disc in a magnetized ambient medium launches torsional Alfvén waves; low-pressure regions make it a positivity test, especially in its very-low-β version.
* [Orszag–Tang vortex (2D MHD)](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md) — Smooth periodic MHD data that steepen into interacting shocks and current sheets; the standard 2D test of shock capturing and divergence control in ideal MHD.
* [Ryu–Jones and Dai–Woodward one-dimensional MHD shock tubes](/benchmarks/ryu-jones-shock-tubes.md) — A set of one-dimensional ideal-MHD Riemann problems, several taken from Dai and Woodward and popularized by Ryu and Jones, that together produce every 1D wave structure: fast and slow shocks and rarefactions, rotational and contact discontinuities, switch-on and switch-off waves, tangential discontinuities and compound waves; checked against a nonlinear Riemann solver.
* [Sedov point-blast wave (low-density positivity test)](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) — A point release of energy into a cold gas of near-zero pressure; the strong shock and the near-vacuum interior make it the standard low-density test for positivity-preserving schemes, in a planar 1D and a 2D corner version.
* [Shock diffraction at a backward-facing corner](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) — A Mach 5.09 shock passing a backward step; the strong expansion below and behind the corner drives density and pressure towards zero, so unlimited high-order schemes need ad hoc fixes.
* [Shock-driven cylinder lift-off](/benchmarks/shock-cylinder-lift-off.md) — A Mach 3 (or 6) shock in a [0, 1] × [0, 0.2] channel hits a free cylinder of radius 0.05 resting near the lower wall, lifting and carrying it; the standard test of moving-boundary inverse Lax–Wendroff treatments, in inviscid, viscous, 3D and elastic variants.
* [Shock–cloud interaction (MHD and relativistic MHD)](/benchmarks/shock-cloud-interaction.md) — A strong magnetized shock overrunning a dense stationary cloud; tests resolution of the shocked cloud's fine structure and robustness, in 2D and 3D ideal MHD and in relativistic MHD.
* [Shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) — A small isentropic vortex convected through a stationary Mach 1.1 shock: tests capture of weak acoustic and vortical structure crossing a shock, grid-aligned or oblique.
* [Shu–Osher shock/entropy-wave interaction](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) — A Mach 3 shock running into a sinusoidal density (entropy) field: smooth structure behind a shock, where high-order capturing schemes show their largest advantage over TVD schemes.
* [Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-shock-tube.md) — The 1D Riemann problem (1, 0, 1 | 0.125, 0, 0.1): rarefaction, contact and shock with a monotone density profile; the baseline check of shock, contact and rarefaction resolution.
* [Supersonic flow past a circular cylinder (Cartesian embedded boundary)](/benchmarks/supersonic-cylinder-flow.md) — Mach 3 flow past a unit cylinder computed on a Cartesian grid that does not fit the body, run to a steady bow shock; the standard robustness test of inverse Lax–Wendroff wall treatments for finite-difference WENO.
* [Two-dimensional Riemann problems (four-quadrant configurations)](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) — Four constant states meeting at the origin of [-0.5, 0.5]² (Schulz-Rinne; Lax–Liu; Kurganov–Tadmor configurations, γ = 1.4): tests genuinely two-dimensional wave interaction, and, at very low dissipation, the onset of Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up along slip lines.
* [Woodward–Colella interacting blast waves](/benchmarks/interacting-blast-waves.md) — Two strong blast waves between reflecting walls that collide (pressures 1000, 0.01, 100; t = 0.038): tests robustness, positivity and contact resolution in a confined, strongly interacting flow.
