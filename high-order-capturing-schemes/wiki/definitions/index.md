# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Essentially non-oscillatory (ENO property)](/definitions/essentially-non-oscillatory.md) — A reconstruction or scheme is essentially non-oscillatory if it produces no Gibbs-like O(1) oscillations at discontinuities, while oscillations at the level of the truncation error are allowed.
* [Modified wavenumber (dispersion and dissipation analysis)](/definitions/modified-wavenumber.md) — The Fourier symbol of a discrete first-derivative operator: applied to e^{ikx} it returns i·ξ̃(ξ)/h; Re ξ̃ against ξ = kh measures dispersion and Im ξ̃ dissipation, the standard tool for judging capturing schemes for turbulence.
* [Monotone numerical flux](/definitions/monotone-flux.md) — A two-argument interface flux that is Lipschitz, nondecreasing in its left and nonincreasing in its right argument, and consistent; the first-order building block that high-order capturing schemes evaluate on reconstructed states.
* [Numerical shock instability (carbuncle and related anomalies)](/definitions/numerical-shock-instability.md) — Spurious growth of small perturbations inside a captured strong shock aligned with the grid: the carbuncle ahead of a blunt body, odd–even decoupling along a moving shock, the kinked Mach stem and the one-dimensional steady-shock limit cycle; it strikes low-dissipation complete Riemann solvers and is studied mostly at first and second order (Godunov, MUSCL, first-order fluxes), with PPM and flux-switched WENO the higher-order evidence.
* [Regular and Mach reflection](/definitions/mach-reflection.md) — The two ways a shock reflects from a wall or a symmetry plane: regular reflection, with incident and reflected shocks meeting on the wall, and Mach reflection, where a Mach stem joins the wall to a triple point carrying a slip line; single and double triple points, the transition angle θ_tr, and the Mach stems that collide behind a body.
* [Slowly moving shock anomaly](/definitions/slowly-moving-shock-anomaly.md) — Spurious post-shock oscillations, and a spike in the conserved flux of the shock cell, produced when a captured shock moves slowly relative to the grid; the captured intermediate state is an admissible point of the curved Hugoniot locus, which Navas-Montilla & Murillo argue makes the anomaly appear with any Riemann solver (contested: Osher's solver with O-ordering, see Contradictions) and not shrink under grid refinement.
* [Smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) — The per-stencil measure β_k that drives WENO weights: O(h²) on smooth data, O(1) across a jump; its form (undivided differences vs. L² norms of derivatives) decides the achievable order, including at critical points.
