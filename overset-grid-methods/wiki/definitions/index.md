# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Composite overlapping grid](/definitions/composite-overlapping-grid.md) — Chesshire and Henshaw's formal definition of an overlapping grid: logically rectangular component grids mapped from the unit square or cube, a valid composite grid in which every point is a discretization, interpolation or unused point, the parameters it depends on, and the mapping method of discretizing on it.
* [Domain connectivity and overset grid assembly](/definitions/domain-connectivity.md) — The preprocessing that turns independent component grids into a usable overset grid: the domain connectivity information it produces (hole points, receptors, donors, weights), its phases, the algorithm families from the founding codes to partitioned, GPU and intersection-based assembly, and the measured costs of assembly for static and moving grids.
* [Explicit and implicit interpolation](/definitions/explicit-and-implicit-interpolation.md) — Two kinds of receptor coupling. In explicit interpolation every donor stencil holds discretization points only, so receptors update directly but need more overlap; in implicit interpolation stencils may hold other receptors and the coupled equations are solved as a system. The page gives the costs and separates the term from compact interpolation and from single-system versus Schwarz solves.
* [Exposed points](/definitions/exposed-point.md) — Points of a moving overlapping grid that were inactive (unused or receptors) at the old time level but whose old-time values the new-time stencils need, because an overlying grid has moved away: Henshaw and Schwendeman's definition, detection test and old-time interpolation remedy, and Meakin and Suhs's earlier rule that forbids them by limiting hole motion.
* [Fringe (receptor) points and overlap width](/definitions/fringe-point.md) — Receptor points — the hole-fringe and outer-boundary points that take interpolated values — how many layers of them a discretization needs, the overlap they require (Benek's heuristic, Chesshire's overlap d, the fringe gap of implicit assembly, Yin–Yang buffer), the fringe rules of SUGGAR, TIOGA and PUNDIT, and the fringe-thickness condition of implicit hole cutting.
* [Grid hierarchy and priority](/definitions/grid-hierarchy.md) — The rules that decide which component grid may cut holes in, or supply donors to, which other: Benek's embedding hierarchy of levels, its relaxation in later PEGASUS versions, CMPGRD's priority ordering, SUGGAR's body hierarchy in which siblings cut each other, and the donor-quality criteria that replace any ordering.
* [Orphan points](/definitions/orphan-point.md) — Receptor points that need interpolation but have no valid donor: the definition and its narrower partitioned-assembly form, the causes the sources identify (insufficient overlap, hole-cutting failure, curved-surface mismatch, donor qualification, donor-quality conflicts), the treatments (elimination, averaging, projection, reduced stencils) and the measured counts.
* [Overset (Chimera) grid](/definitions/overset-grid.md) — A covering of the domain by independently generated, mutually overlapping component grids coupled by interpolation at receptor points, with hole points excluded; the hub page tracing the idea from Benek's Chimera and Chesshire and Henshaw's composite grids through moving and adaptive grids, NASA's production tool chain, partitioned and GPU assembly, and Chimera without hole cutting.
* [Patched (zonal) grids](/definitions/patched-grid.md) — Multizone grids whose subdomains meet along common boundaries without overlap: how the founding overset sources define them, contrast them with overset grids (zero-overlap limit, conservation, interface matching, metric discontinuities), and how Wang turns an overlap into a patch to make the overset interface conservative.
* [Point classification and the iblank mask](/definitions/point-classification.md) — The partition of every component grid into discretization, receptor and hole points (with orphans among the receptors), the signed mask that records it, the 0/1 IBLANK of the Chimera codes and how blanking enters implicit solvers, and the vocabularies of the founding sources translated into one.
* [Yin–Yang grid](/definitions/yin-yang-grid.md) — Kageyama and Sato's overset grid for spheres and spherical shells: two congruent latitude–longitude patches, each 90° by 270° plus a buffer, in perpendicular polar frames related by an involution. It is orthogonal and quasi-uniform with no pole problem, reuses one solver and stencil set, rotates vectors by an angle ψ, and overlaps about 6%.
