# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Composite overlapping grid](/definitions/composite-overlapping-grid.md) — Chesshire and Henshaw's formal definition of an overlapping grid: logically rectangular component grids mapped from the unit square or cube, a valid composite grid in which every point is a discretization, interpolation or unused point, the parameters it depends on, and the mapping method of discretizing on it.
* [Domain connectivity and overset grid assembly](/definitions/domain-connectivity.md) — The preprocessing that turns independent component grids into a usable overset grid: the domain connectivity information it produces (hole points, receptors, donors, weights), its phases, the algorithm families of the founding codes, and the measured costs of assembly for static and moving grids.
* [Explicit and implicit interpolation](/definitions/explicit-and-implicit-interpolation.md) — Two kinds of receptor coupling. In explicit interpolation every donor stencil holds discretization points only, so receptors update directly but need more overlap. In implicit interpolation stencils may hold other receptors, so the coupled equations are solved as a sparse system, needing less overlap and fewer points; the page gives the trade-off's costs.
* [Fringe (receptor) points and overlap width](/definitions/fringe-point.md) — Receptor points — the hole-fringe and outer-boundary points that take interpolated values — how many layers of them a discretization needs, the overlap they require (Benek's heuristic, Chesshire's overlap d, Yin–Yang buffer), the node- and cell-centred fringe rules of SUGGAR, and the fringe-thickness condition of implicit hole cutting.
* [Grid hierarchy and priority](/definitions/grid-hierarchy.md) — The rules that decide which component grid may cut holes in, or supply donors to, which other: Benek's embedding hierarchy of levels, its relaxation in later PEGASUS versions, CMPGRD's priority ordering, SUGGAR's body hierarchy in which siblings cut each other, and the donor-quality criteria that replace any ordering.
* [Orphan points](/definitions/orphan-point.md) — Receptor points that need interpolation but have no valid donor: the definition, the causes the founding sources identify (insufficient overlap, hole-cutting failure, curved-surface discretization mismatch, donor qualification near boundaries), the treatments (elimination, averaging, projection, reduced stencils) and the measured counts.
* [Overset (Chimera) grid](/definitions/overset-grid.md) — A covering of the domain by independently generated, mutually overlapping component grids whose solutions are coupled by interpolation at receptor points, with hole points excluded; the hub page tracing the idea from Benek's Chimera through Chesshire and Henshaw's composite grids, Meakin's moving bodies, PEGASUS 5 and SUGGAR.
* [Patched (zonal) grids](/definitions/patched-grid.md) — Multizone grids whose subdomains meet along common boundaries without overlap: how the founding overset sources define them, contrast them with overset grids (zero-overlap limit, conservation, interface matching, metric discontinuities), and how Wang turns an overlap into a patch to make the overset interface conservative.
* [Point classification and the iblank mask](/definitions/point-classification.md) — The partition of every component grid into discretization, receptor and hole points (with orphans among the receptors), the signed mask that records it, the 0/1 IBLANK of the Chimera codes and how blanking enters implicit solvers, and the vocabularies of the founding sources translated into one.
* [Yin–Yang grid](/definitions/yin-yang-grid.md) — Kageyama and Sato's overset grid for spheres and spherical shells: two congruent latitude–longitude patches, each 90° by 270° plus a buffer, in perpendicular polar frames related by an involution. It is orthogonal and quasi-uniform with no pole problem, reuses one solver and stencil set, rotates vectors by an angle ψ, and overlaps about 6%.
