# Summary

Wang, Wu, Gong and Yang simulate the wake of the INSEAN/CNR-INM E779A four-bladed marine propeller
at heavy loading (advance coefficient 0.38). They compare improved delayed detached-eddy simulation
(IDDES, on SST $k$–$\omega$) with large-eddy simulation using the WALE subgrid model, on the same
grid and time step. The solver is OpenFOAM: collocated finite volumes, PISO coupling, second-order
upwind convection, second-order backward time stepping and Rhie–Chow interpolation.

The paper is a propeller-wake turbulence study; the [overset grid](/definitions/overset-grid.md) is
its numerical ingredient. A propeller-fitted component grid rotates inside a stationary cylindrical
background grid, a [moving overset grid](/methods/moving-overset-grid.md) handled by a "dynamic
overset technique" for which the authors cite Carrica et al. (2007) and Liu et al. (2021). The brief description (§III A) defines the [composite
grid](/definitions/composite-overlapping-grid.md) as the set of its component grids, divides the
points of each grid into interior, boundary, interpolation, exterior and unused points ([point
classification](/definitions/point-classification.md)), and names the receptor cells "acceptors"
([fringe points](/definitions/fringe-point.md)) with matching donor cells. The interpolation
coefficients are recomputed at every rotation step, and the [inter-grid
interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md) is trilinear from an 8-point donor cell. The
paper gives no hole-cutting algorithm, donor search, overlap width or interpolation-accuracy
analysis.

The grid has 48 million hexahedral cells with $y^+<1$ and refined tip- and hub-vortex regions over
$2.8D$ downstream. The time step is 1° of rotation; 30 rotations are run and the last 20 averaged.
Thrust and torque coefficients, phase-averaged velocity, vorticity and vortex visualizations agree
qualitatively with experiments. The wake shows stable, transition and unstable regimes; elliptic
instability splits each tip vortex into two interlaced branches, and pairing is driven by mutual
induction, moving the dominant frequency from the blade-passage frequency to its half. On the same
grid, LES resolves more small-scale detail but predicts earlier breakdown than IDDES. All evidence is
numerical and read from figures.

# Key claims

1. The rotating propeller is simulated with a dynamic overset technique, "used widely in problems
   that consider moving geometries". (§III A) **Stated**
2. An overset grid system replaces complex regions with "multiple overlapping, body-conforming,
   curvilinear grids"; component grids can be added "in a mutually independent manner", and "grid
   connectivity needs only be updated locally". (§III A) **Stated**
3. The composite grid $\mathcal G$ consists of component grids $G_k$, $k=1,\dots,n_g$; the points
   of each $G_k$ are interior, boundary, interpolation, exterior or unused. (§III A, Fig. 4)
   **Stated**
4. Interpolation cells are marked as acceptors ($\mathcal R_k$) and donors; Fig. 5 shows the
   active, acceptor and donor cells of the propeller-in-background system ($n_g=2$). (§III A,
   Fig. 5) **Shown** (figure only)
5. New interpolation coefficients are computed each time the propeller grid rotates. (§III A)
   **Stated**
6. Inter-grid transfer is trilinear from the donor cell,
   $u^k_{\mathbf i}=\sum_{\mathbf j\in\mathcal S_{\mathbf i}}\alpha_{\mathbf j}u^{k'}_{\mathbf j}$
   with 8 donor values ($q=2$). (Eq. 10) **Stated** (weights not given)
7. The domain is a cylinder of diameter $10D$, extending $2.5D$ upstream and $15D$ downstream;
   48 million hexahedral cells; $y^+<1$ on all solid surfaces. (§III A) **Stated**
8. The tip-vortex core holds about 130 cells; the smallest cell is about 1.5 % of the chord at
   $0.7R$ (1.3125 mm), the largest about 100 % of it (86 mm). (§III A, Fig. 3) **Stated**
9. Time step 1° of rotation; 30 rotations, the final 20 (7200 steps) used for averages. (§III A,
   §IV) **Stated**
10. Thrust and torque coefficients from IDDES and LES at advance coefficients 0.38, 0.65 and 0.88
    "are in good agreement" with experiment. (§III B, Fig. 6a) **Shown** (qualitative)
11. The phase-averaged axial velocity at $x=0.1D$ (advance coefficient 0.88) agrees with PIV, but
    the computation "tends to overestimate the velocity in the helical sheet and tip vortex regions
    slightly". (§III B, Fig. 6b) **Shown**
12. IDDES vorticity and vortical structures agree with PIV and visualizations, and the elliptic
    instability is reproduced. (§III B, Figs. 7–9) **Shown** (qualitative)
13. The IDDES tip-vortex system shows three regimes: stable, transition and unstable. (§IV,
    Fig. 11a) **Shown**
14. Elliptic instability splits each tip vortex into two interlaced spiral branches, which interact
    with those of adjacent tip vortices and cause breakdown. (§IV, Figs. 10–11; §V) **Shown**
15. Pairing: a downstream tip-vortex cluster pushes the upstream tip vortex into the high-speed
    shear layer at the inner radius; the instability is "triggered mainly by the mutual inductance
    effect between the tip vortices". (§IV, Figs. 15, 20; §V) **Shown**
16. The trailing-edge vortex of the E779A is "not as strong as in the case of Kumar and Mahesh
    (2017)" and is not deformed by the breakdown of its tip vortex. (§IV) **Shown**
17. On the same grid and time step, LES resolves more detail than IDDES but predicts earlier
    tip-vortex diffusion and breakdown, with short stable and transition regimes, and loses the
    near-field coherence. (§IV, Figs. 10–13, 15, 18; §V) **Shown**
18. Vertical velocity fluctuations are "substantially larger" than the streamwise and tangential
    ones for $x/D<1.5$; strong Reynolds stresses mark the IDDES instability beyond $x/D>1.7$. (§IV,
    Fig. 20) **Shown**
19. The shear stress $\overline{u'w'}$ changes sign between tip vortices and "plays an important
    role" in their interaction and destabilization. (§IV, Fig. 21) **Shown**
20. Both IDDES and LES reproduce the $-5/3$ inertial-range slope over a wide range of scales. (§IV,
    Figs. 23–24) **Shown**
21. The mean kinetic energy first rises from probe P1 to P2, then decays; IDDES gives more at
    P1–P3, and the two methods are similar at P4–P6. (§IV, Fig. 22) **Shown**
22. In IDDES the leading frequency is the blade-passage frequency at P1–P2 and shifts toward half
    of it at P3 (pairing); further downstream it drifts toward the shaft frequency (a quarter). In
    LES the shift to half occurs already at P2. (§IV, Figs. 24–26) **Shown**
23. Following Muscari et al. (2013) and Di Mascio et al. (2014), peaks at non-integer multiples of
    the blade-passage frequency indicate tip-vortex pairing or a growing secondary vortex. (§IV)
    **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **After Eq. (2):** "$l_{RANS}$ corresponds to $k^2/(C_\mu\omega)$", which has units m⁴/s³; a
  length is $k^{1/2}/(C_\mu\omega)$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Eqs. (4) and (7):** $C_w=0.15$ (IDDES) and $C_w=0.544$ (WALE) share one symbol; $\Delta$ is
  defined by Eq. (4) and, for Eq. (7), "based on the grid volume V".
- **Eq. (9):** the subtracted trace term is printed
  $\frac13\delta_{ij}(\partial\bar u_k/\partial x_k)^2$, the square of the velocity divergence,
  where the traceless part of the squared velocity gradient needs
  $\frac13\delta_{ij}\,(\partial\bar u_k/\partial x_l)(\partial\bar u_l/\partial x_k)$
  *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **§IV, kinetic-energy spectra:** "In the low-frequency range of the far field, the average value
  of the spectra is greater at P6 than at P5 and is greater at P5 than at P4. Unlike in the
  high-frequency range …, the average value of the spectra is greater at P6 than at P5 and is
  greater at P5 than at P4." The second sentence repeats the first.
- **§II:** "three-dependent conservation of momentum".
- **Citations:** two different works are both cited as "Wang et al., 2021" (Phys. Fluids 33:035146
  and 33:095120), and "Zhang et al., 2021" likewise names two works, so these in-text citations
  cannot be resolved from the label.
- Hunt et al. (1988) is printed "Eddies, stream, and convergence zones in turbulent flows".
