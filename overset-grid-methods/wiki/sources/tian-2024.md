# Summary

"Overlapping meshes" here means a different use of overlap from overset component grids: the vorticity, the velocity field and the potential function are approximated on different meshes that overlap (staggered), so that the velocity is continuous along the interfaces of the primitive mesh and upwind fluxes can be used in the local discontinuous Galerkin (LDG) vorticity equation. Two sufficient conditions give a maximum principle for the vorticity: a divergence-free numerical velocity (which also keeps constant solutions) and positivity of the numerical vorticity, enforced by a time-step restriction and a slope limiter ([DG on overlapping grids](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-on-overset-grids.md), [incompressible flow](/methods/incompressible-flow-on-overset-grids.md)).

# Key claims

1. Vorticity, velocity and potential are solved on different overlapping meshes, making the velocity continuous along the interfaces of the primitive meshes, so that upwind fluxes apply (Abstract). **Stated**
2. Two sufficient conditions give a maximum principle for the vorticity: a divergence-free numerical velocity, which also preserves constant solutions, and positivity of the numerical vorticity (Abstract). **Derived**
3. Suitable time steps make the cell averages of the vorticity positive, after which a slope limiter enforces positivity; constants can then be added to obtain high-order maximum-principle-preserving LDG schemes (Abstract, §3). **Derived**
4. A CFL condition for the maximum-principle-preserving scheme is shown, and computations with and without limiters are reported, e.g. second order for $Q^1$ in Table 4 (Conclusions, §4). **Shown**
