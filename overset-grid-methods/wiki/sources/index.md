# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Abgrall et al. (2026) — Dual formulation finite-volume methods on overlapping meshes for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws](/sources/abgrall-2026.md) — Second-order schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws that evolve the conservative and a primitive (nonconservative) formulation on two overlapping staggered finite-volume meshes, the primitive solution supplying simple fluxes for the conservative one, with a post-processing that ensures nonlinear stability and a conservative coupling of the two sets of variables.
* [Angel et al. (2023) — Efficient upwind finite-difference schemes for wave equations on overset grids](/sources/angel-2023.md) — Adds upwinding to existing finite-difference schemes for wave equations in second-order form as a modular corrector stage acting as a special artificial dissipation, keeping the robustness of upwind schemes on overset grids with thin boundary-fitted grids at about a tenth of the cost of the earlier upwind formulation.
* [Banks et al. (2016) — An added-mass partition algorithm for fluid–structure interactions of compressible fluids and nonlinear solids](/sources/banks-2016.md) — Extends the added-mass partitioned (AMP) fluid–structure algorithm to compressible fluids coupled with nonlinear hyperelastic solids, with interface states from a fluid–solid Riemann problem as impedance-weighted averages, both domains discretized on deforming composite overlapping grids regenerated every step with Ogen in Overture.
* [Benek et al. (1983) — A flexible grid embedding technique with application to the Euler equations](/sources/benek-1983.md) — Founding paper of the Chimera method: independently generated body-fitted grids overset on one another in a level hierarchy, holes cut by level curves of embedded grids, an IBLANK mask in the implicit solver, linear Taylor interpolation at grid boundaries, the PEGASUS and XMERA codes, and 2-D Euler tests on a cylinder and the SKF 1.1 airfoil.
* [Benek et al. (1985) — A 3-D chimera grid embedding technique](/sources/benek-1985.md) — Extends the Chimera grid-embedding scheme to three dimensions: a strict embedding hierarchy, hole cutting by a level surface with a radius-plus-normal test, one fringe layer, trilinear inter-grid interpolation, the PEGSUS connectivity code and the XMER3D implicit Euler solver, shown on transonic wing–body, wing–body–tail and three-ellipsoid configurations.
* [Bergmann et al. (2022a) — Second order ADER scheme for unsteady advection–diffusion on moving overset grids with a compact transmission condition](/sources/bergmann-2022.md) — A space–time finite-volume scheme of ADER type for advection–diffusion on moving Chimera grids, with interpolation polynomials on arbitrarily intersecting space–time cells at block boundaries, a mesh-free predictor and finite-volume corrector, and a space–time local Lax–Friedrichs speed, second order in space and time.
* [Bergmann et al. (2022b) — ADER scheme for incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on overset grids with a compact transmission condition](/sources/bergmann-2022-b.md) — Extends a second-order ADER-type space–time finite-volume scheme on moving overset grids to the two-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, with a compact hybrid stencil that couples the grids through fluxes rather than fringe interpolation, verified on cavity, cylinder and sedimentation problems.
* [Carlino & Boscheri (2024) — Arbitrary-Lagrangian-Eulerian finite volume IMEX schemes for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on evolving Chimera meshes](/sources/carlino-2024.md) — A second-order finite-volume semi-implicit IMEX Runge–Kutta scheme with an incremental projection for incompressible flow on evolving Chimera meshes, the mesh motion encoded in the numerical flux, a time step limited only by the relative fluid velocity, and compact fringe-to-field transmission by extrapolation on a minimal stencil.
* [Carson et al. (2025) — High-order accurate implicit–explicit time-stepping schemes for wave equations on overset grids](/sources/carson-2025.md) — Single-step, three-level implicit modified-equation schemes of second and fourth order for wave equations in second-order form on overset grids, with upwind dissipation for stability, and a spatially partitioned implicit–explicit scheme that integrates component grids with small cells implicitly and the others explicitly.
* [Carter et al. (2026) — A fourth-order accurate single-step Lax–Wendroff-type scheme for Maxwell's equations on overlapping grids](/sources/carter-2026.md) — A single-step fourth-order Lax–Wendroff-type scheme for Maxwell's equations on overlapping grids, from a Taylor expansion in time with five-point finite differences, stable up to CFL one on periodic grids and robust on overlapping grids, exactly divergence preserving on Cartesian grids, with fourth-order convergence on periodic and perfect-conductor problems.
* [Chan (2009) — Overset grid technology development at NASA Ames Research Center](/sources/chan-2009.md) — Historical review, without equations or new results, of the NASA Ames structured overset tool chain from 1983 to 2008: the Chimera scheme and iblank, hyperbolic and collar grid generation, off-body Cartesian grids, hole cutting from PEGASUS to object X-rays, zipper-grid force integration, Chimera Grid Tools, the OVERFLOW solvers and their applications.
* [Chandar & Sitaraman (2022) — A flux correction approach for the pressure equation in incompressible flows on overset meshes](/sources/chandar-2022.md) — Derives the discrete global mass-conservation condition of the pressure equation of a pressure-correction finite-volume solver on overset meshes and imposes it implicitly at one flux-balance cell, recovering machine-level conservation, second order in 1-D and lower errors in 2-D for inverse-distance, polynomial and RBF interpolation.
* [Chesshire & Henshaw (1990) — Composite overlapping meshes for the solution of partial differential equations](/sources/chesshire-1990.md) — Foundational description of composite overlapping grids and the CMPGRD generator: point classification with a signed donor-grid flag, an ordering-based composite-grid algorithm with donor search by Newton inversion, explicit and implicit Lagrange interpolation, a 1-D theory of the required interpolation width versus overlap, IMEX time stepping, and compressible Navier–Stokes verification.
* [Delcour et al. (2022) — Investigating the influence of compressibility on the second-mode flutter of a cantilevered cylinder in axial flow](/sources/delcour-2022.md) — Validates a partitioned fluid–structure interaction framework with a Chimera mesh for a flexible cantilevered cylinder in confined axial flow against water experiments, then shows that air compressibility at inlet Mach numbers 0.66 and 0.72 accelerates second-mode flutter and changes its mode shape.
* [Devlin et al. (2023) — Direct Flux via Virtual Faces (DFVF-overset): interpolation-free, conservative overset grids](/sources/devlin-2023.md) — A conservative overset scheme that generalizes the finite-volume method to overlapping cells: cell volumes are shared through a normalized top-hat weight function, fluxes pass between overlapping cells through virtual faces of defined area, and no interpolation or assembly is needed; implemented for OpenFOAM and tested on diffusion, cavity and volume-of-fluid cases.
* [Ding et al. (2025) — Robust multi-resolution weighted nonlinear interpolations for inter-grid boundaries of overset grids](/sources/ding-2025.md) — Arbitrary-location nonlinear interpolations for the inter-grid boundaries of curvilinear structured overset grids, extending multi-resolution WENO to unequal-size nested sub-stencils so that accuracy degrades progressively to first order with several discontinuities in one stencil, with location-independent weights and a freestream-preserving overset discretization.
* [Ding et al. (2026) — A novel overset grid strategy and high-order inter-grid boundaries treatment for cell-centered finite difference method](/sources/ding-2026.md) — An automated structured overset grid assembly for cell-centred finite differences — hybrid approximate/direct hole cutting, implicit hole-boundary optimization with quality comparison, interpolation-cell cancellation for large stencils and island removal without wall distance — and an optimized high-order inter-grid interpolation minimizing spectral errors.
* [Hedayat et al. (2022) — A parallel dynamic overset grid framework for immersed boundary methods](/sources/hedayat-2022.md) — Extends a curvilinear immersed-boundary incompressible solver with fixed overset grids to moving overset grids with a fully parallel in-core assembly: explicit hole cutting by ray tracing, oriented-bounding-box query search, walking donor search, a PETSc interpolation matrix, non-inertial frames per moving grid and a flux correction for mass conservation.
* [Henshaw & Schwendeman (2006) — Moving overlapping grids with adaptive mesh refinement for high-speed reactive and non-reactive flow](/sources/henshaw-2006.md) — Combines moving composite overlapping grids with block-structured adaptive mesh refinement on every component grid for the reactive Euler equations with rigid bodies, introducing exposed points, an incremental Ogen connectivity update, a predictor–corrector moving-grid algorithm and second-order slip-wall conditions, verified against exact and self-convergence solutions.
* [Henshaw (1994) — A fourth-order accurate method for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on overlapping grids](/sources/henshaw-1994.md) — Fourth-order finite-difference method of lines for incompressible Navier–Stokes on curvilinear overlapping grids: velocity–pressure form with the divergence-free boundary condition, ghost-point numerical boundary conditions, quartic Lagrange interpolation with two interpolation lines, divergence damping, and twilight-zone and exact-solution convergence tests in 2-D and 3-D (code CGINS).
* [Henshaw (2006) — A high-order accurate parallel solver for Maxwell's equations on overlapping grids](/sources/henshaw-2006-b.md) — Fourth-order time-domain Maxwell solver on overlapping grids in second-order (vector wave) form: modified-equation time stepping, symmetric high-order curvilinear Laplacians that conserve a discrete energy, centred compatibility boundary and dielectric-interface conditions, interpolation of width p+1 with high-order dissipation against weak instability, and a parallel implementation.
* [Horne & Mahesh (2021) — A hardware accelerated unstructured overset method to simulate turbulent fluid flow](/sources/horne-2021.md) — Ports a massively parallel unstructured overset method to GPUs: hole cutting and donor search reduced to overlap queries answered by a linear bounding volume hierarchy plus the GJK test, and an incompressible solver with a pressure-rate term that lets overset values be injected into the pressure equation.
* [Hu et al. (2027) — Physics-based reconstruction of flow variables for solution transfer in overset grid methods](/sources/hu-2027.md) — A physics-consistent lattice Boltzmann solution reconstruction for overset grids: virtual control volumes and interfaces are built along overset boundaries and a lattice Boltzmann flux solver reconstructs the conservative variables as the transferred solution, reducing accumulated transfer errors and smoothing the solution across overlaps against conventional interpolation.
* [Kageyama & Sato (2004) — "Yin-Yang grid": an overset grid in spherical geometry](/sources/kageyama-2004.md) — Introduces the Yin–Yang grid, two identical low-latitude latitude–longitude patches in perpendicular spherical coordinates that overlap to cover the sphere without pole singularity or grid convergence, with the Cartesian involution and vector-rotation formulas between the two components and minimum-overlap variants.
* [Kochi & Ramakrishna (2022) — A discontinuous Galerkin overset scheme using WENO reconstruction and subcells for two-dimensional problems](/sources/kochi-2022.md) — Couples discontinuous Galerkin solutions on two-dimensional overset quadrilateral grids by reconstructing the higher modes of each artificial-boundary cell with a compact subcell WENO procedure fed by donor-polynomial values in a ghost cell, giving order N+1 on smooth problems and acting as a limiter when shocks cross the overset boundary.
* [Kochi & Ramakrishna (2023) — Shock capturing using discontinuous Galerkin method and overset grids](/sources/kochi-2023.md) — Confines shock capturing to a refined structured overset grid aligned with the shock, built from the troubled-cell data of a coarse discontinuous Galerkin solution, and reruns the solver with the high-resolution flux and limiter only on that grid, giving sharp grid-aligned shocks for ramps, reflections, a cylinder bow shock and a Mach reflection.
* [Kopriva et al. (2022) — On the theoretical foundation of overset grid methods for hyperbolic problems: well-posedness and conservation](/sources/kopriva-2022.md) — Proves with the energy method when the continuous overset initial-boundary value problem for linear symmetric hyperbolic systems is well posed, equal to the single-domain problem and conservative: characteristic coupling in one dimension, and weighted penalty coupling at the overlap boundaries and inside the overlap in one and two dimensions.
* [Kopriva et al. (2025) — Energy bounds for discontinuous Galerkin spectral element approximations of well-posed overset grid problems for hyperbolic systems](/sources/kopriva-2025.md) — Shows that a DG spectral element approximation of the well-posed overset problem for hyperbolic systems is energy bounded only for fixed order, mesh and time: without dissipation the overlap coupling admits positive eigenvalues; upwind fluxes stabilize it in one dimension, and a new penalty applying dissipation at points in the overlap adds more.
* [Kumar et al. (2026) — GPU optimized integration of immersed boundary method and overset meshes](/sources/kumar-2026.md) — A GPU-accelerated overset immersed-boundary framework for incompressible flows with several moving bodies, combining Cartesian immersed boundaries with overset local refinement, with a lightweight sliding-window reallocation strategy and minimal interface storage to follow arbitrary body motion, applied to bio-inspired and drone-propeller aerodynamics.
* [Lee & Baeder (2003) — Implicit hole cutting - a new approach to overset grid connectivity](/sources/lee-2003.md) — Three-dimensional paper of implicit hole cutting, extending the 2-D original: every point of every grid is tested against all overlapping grids and the smallest-volume cell wins, so holes appear as a by-product of cell selection, with stencil-walk donor search, Hermite cubic interpolation and an argument that hole points need no blanking.
* [Li & Wang (2022) — A multiple instance solver framework based on dynamic overset grid method for flow field simulation of array configurations](/sources/li-2022.md) — Proposes the Multiple Instance-Solvers Framework, which runs one instance of a single-solver code per moving component on its own MPI communicator, coupled by dynamic overset grid assembly with implicit hole cutting, exact-inverse-map donor search and a stricter traversal of overlapping process pairs, demonstrated on airfoils, turbine arrays and rotors.
* [Luo & Fan (2025) — The Yin–Yang Magnetic Flux Eruption (Yin–Yang MFE) code: a global corona and solar wind MHD model](/sources/luo-2025.md) — Describes a global single-fluid MHD code for the solar corona and solar wind on the Yin–Yang grid, with electron heat conduction, radiative cooling, empirical heating and the Boris correction, boundary updates in the ghost zones of the two overlapping domains, MPI parallelism, standard tests and a solar-minimum wind solution.
* [Meakin & Suhs (1989) — Unsteady aerodynamic simulation of multiple bodies in relative motion](/sources/meakin-1989.md) — Moving-body Chimera paper: a thin-layer Navier–Stokes solver with time metrics, coupled each step to an unsteady PEGASUS (the domain connectivity function) whose search is restricted to bands around the previous hole boundaries, cutting assembly cost about threefold under a one-cell hole-motion constraint; store and Shuttle SRB separation demonstrations.
* [Noack (2005) — SUGGAR: a general capability for moving body overset grid assembly](/sources/noack-2005.md) — Describes SUGGAR, a stand-alone overset assembler for structured, unstructured and polyhedral grids and for node- or cell-centred solvers, with binary-tree hole cutting, a body hierarchy, cell-centred fringe and donor-weight rules, polyhedral neighbour-walk donor search and moving-body coupling through named pipes.
* [Oh et al. (2024) — Exact and efficient wall distance pre-classification overset grid assembly](/sources/oh-2024.md) — An overset grid assembly for unstructured grids that classifies points by an exact wall-distance criterion before the donor search, computing distances stepwise from vertex-based bounds to element-based exact values, with voxelization, ray tracing and load balancing, faster than post-classification assembly in all tests.
* [Picard et al. (2021) — A Finite Volume Chimera Method for Fast Transient Dynamics in Compressible Flow Problems](/sources/picard-2021.md) — Cell-centred second-order finite-volume Chimera method for fast transient compressible two-component flows: a fixed patch superposed on an unmodified substrate without hole cutting, two ghost-cell layers, intersection-volume-weighted limited linear interpolation, a corner-radius limiter, and tests of accuracy, conservation and patch/substrate cell ratio.
* [Rogers et al. (2003) — PEGASUS 5: an automated preprocessor for overset-grid computational fluid dynamics](/sources/rogers-2003.md) — Describes PEGASUS 5, a rewrite of the PEGASUS connectivity code that automates overset assembly with boundary-condition-driven input, Cartesian hole-map hole cutting, exhaustive tree-and-stencil-jumping donor search, a cell-difference donor criterion, overlap optimization, surface projection and MPI parallelism, cutting the Boeing 777 connectivity work from 32 to 3 days.
* [Roget & Sitaraman (2014) — Robust and efficient overset grid assembly for partitioned unstructured meshes](/sources/roget-2014.md) — Fully automated parallel overset grid assembly on solver-partitioned unstructured meshes using only local data: automatic hole cutting from Cartesian wall approximations, resolution-based field/receptor selection, a line-walk donor search made robust by an exact inverse map, an alternating-digital-tree alternative and adaptive load re-balancing, implemented in PUNDIT.
* [Sharma et al. (2021) — Overset meshes for incompressible flows: On preserving accuracy of underlying discretizations](/sources/sharma-2021.md) — Studies how overset information exchange affects incompressible-flow accuracy: verifies when linear fringe interpolation suffices and when higher order is needed as the overlap shrinks with the mesh, adds a polynomial-augmented RBF point-cloud interpolation, and compares a single coupled linear system with additive-Schwarz coupling.
* [Sherer & Scott (2005) — High-order compact finite-difference methods on general overset grids](/sources/sherer-2005.md) — Extends a sixth-order compact finite-difference solver with high-order Padé filters to general overset grids with holes, analyses explicit, compact, wavenumber-optimized and B-spline inter-grid interpolation by Fourier error measures, and recommends explicit non-optimized Lagrange interpolation of high order.
* [Teschner & Mundt (2022) — Fully conservative overset mesh to overcome the blunt body metric singularity](/sources/teschner-2022.md) — Removes the axis metric singularity of a curvilinear finite-difference blunt-body solver by a small hexahedral overset mesh around the symmetry line whose calculation points coincide with those of the body mesh, so that values are copied rather than interpolated, tested on a supersonic hemisphere cylinder in two and three dimensions.
* [Tian et al. (2024) — Maximum-principle-preserving high-order discontinuous Galerkin methods for incompressible Euler equations on overlapping meshes](/sources/tian-2024.md) — A local discontinuous Galerkin method for the two-dimensional incompressible Euler equations in vorticity–stream-function form that solves vorticity, velocity and potential on different overlapping meshes, so that the velocity is continuous across primitive-mesh interfaces and upwind fluxes apply, with conditions and limiters for a maximum principle of the vorticity.
* [Wang & Stern (2022) — Volume-of-fluid based two-phase flow methods on structured multiblock and overset grids](/sources/wang-2022.md) — Extends geometric volume-of-fluid interface capturing to static and dynamic overset grids by reconstructing the volume fraction of fringe cells from interpolated distance function and normal, and replaces a level-set ghost fluid method by a volume-of-fluid one, verified on advection and two-phase tests and applied to ship hydrodynamics.
* [Wang (1995) — A fully conservative interface algorithm for overlapped grids](/sources/wang-1995.md) — Turns two overlapping grids into two patched zones by using the minor grid's overlapped boundary as a cut-cell interface in the major grid, with Riemann fluxes on the smallest faces, giving a globally conservative Chimera scheme that needs no inter-grid interpolation.
* [Wang et al. (2021) — Numerical simulation of the wake instabilities of a propeller](/sources/wang-2021.md) — IDDES and WALE-LES study of the wake instability of the E779A marine propeller at heavy loading in OpenFOAM, the rotating propeller grid coupled to a stationary background by a dynamic overset grid with trilinear interpolation; identifies tip-vortex instability regimes, elliptic instability and pairing by mutual induction.
* [Wang et al. (2025) — A novel overset grid assembly strategy based on immersed boundary method for fluid–structure interaction problems](/sources/wang-2025.md) — Replaces hole cutting and iterative information exchange in overset fluid–structure computations by two diffuse-interface immersed-boundary body-force terms in the background momentum equations: one imposes the no-slip condition of the body implicitly, the other carries the overset information, with pressure interpolation against immersed-boundary pressure oscillations.
* [Wang et al. (2026) — A high-fidelity framework for point-particle direct numerical simulation on multi-block overset curvilinear grids](/sources/wang-2026.md) — Point-particle DNS of particle-laden flow on multi-block overset curvilinear grids with high-order accuracy, using overset techniques for particle storage and redistribution — inter-block mapping in overlapping zones, a velocity-based heterogeneous zonal search-locate algorithm and an overset-aware message-passing strategy — validated against newly derived analytical force models.
* [Xiao et al. (2023) — Enhancement on parallel unstructured overset grid method for complex aerospace engineering applications](/sources/xiao-2023.md) — A parallel implicit-hole-cutting overset assembly for unstructured grids in moving-body aerospace flows, accelerated by a bookkeeping alternating digital tree for donor search, a parallel advancing-front wall-distance computation and a lean message-passing strategy, embedded in a URANS solver for a helicopter, store separation and component deployment.
* [Zhao et al. (2022) — An overset boundary condition-enforced immersed boundary method for incompressible flows with large moving boundary domains](/sources/zhao-2022.md) — Attaches a locally refined Cartesian overset mesh, translating rigidly with the body, to a boundary-condition-enforced immersed boundary method with a lattice Boltzmann flux solver, coupled by two acceptor layers and single-donor piecewise-linear reconstruction, cutting cell counts and CPU time by 25–94 % on moving-body tests.
* [Zhou et al. (2024) — Solving for hydroelastic ship response using a high-order finite-difference method on overlapping grids](/sources/zhou-2024.md) — Extends the potential-flow seakeeping solver OceanWave3D-seakeeping, which uses high-order finite differences on overlapping curvilinear body-fitted grids, to hydroelastic responses of flexible ships through generalized modes of an Euler–Bernoulli beam, validated on three cases, and derives both forms of the hydrostatic stiffness of flexible modes.
