# Summary

Wang addresses the lack of global conservation of the interpolation-coupled
[overset (Chimera) grid](/definitions/overset-grid.md) method, whose zones exchange data by
non-conservative [inter-grid interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md). For two
overlapping zones, a major grid $G_{k'}$ and a minor grid $G_k$ with overlap $\Omega_{kk'}$, the
paper writes conservation as two conditions: the rate of change of the conserved quantities in
$\Omega_{kk'}$ must be the same on both grids (Eq. 8), and the fluxes through the two overlapped
boundaries must balance (Eq. 10). Enforcing both at once on a general overlap is called
"extremely difficult, if not impossible".

The contribution, the [conservative overset interface](/methods/conservative-overset-interface.md),
avoids the overlap. The overlapped boundary of the minor grid, $\Gamma_{k|k'}$, becomes a new
internal boundary in the major grid. The pair then becomes two
[patched zones](/definitions/patched-grid.md), and conservation reduces to flux conservation on
their common boundary (Eq. 11). Zonal Interface Generation (ZIG) intersects $\Gamma_{k|k'}$ with
the faces of $G_{k'}$. It produces cut faces and "smallest faces" and sorts the major-grid cells
into cut, normal and hole cells. This is a [hole cutting](/methods/hole-cutting.md) whose hole
boundary is exactly $\Gamma_{k|k'}$. Each smallest face gets a Roe flux from constant (first-order)
or least-squares linear (second-order) reconstructions on both sides, so no interpolation between
zones is needed. Cut cells are updated with area-weighted face fluxes plus the smallest-face
fluxes (Eq. 14).

The scheme runs in the cell-centred finite-volume code CFD-FASTRAN and is compared with the
"original Chimera" in CFL3D on four two-dimensional Euler tests: a transonic channel bump, a
slow moving shock, a three-element airfoil (a [multi-element airfoil](/benchmarks/multi-element-airfoil.md)
case) and shock diffraction by a cylinder. The conservative scheme gives a mass imbalance of
$6\times10^{-6}\,\%$ against $0.38\,\%$, converges to machine zero where the original Chimera
stalls, and carries the slow moving shock through the interface, where the original Chimera
dissipates it.

# Key claims

1. Zonal interfaces are artificial boundaries. Unless they are treated conservatively, a
   multizonal scheme is "zonally conservative" but "nonconservative globally". (§1, p. 97)
   **Stated**
2. Patched grids are fully conservative and need no inter-zone interpolation for cell-centred
   finite volumes, but they need an exact interface match. Overlapped grids allow arbitrary
   interfaces and relative motion, but they are not fully conservative and need two-way
   interpolation. (§1, items a–b) **Stated**
3. On overset grids the convergence rate of a steady problem can depend on the overlap region, and
   in some cases convergence was not reached (citing [9]). (§1) **Stated**
4. Moon & Liou [10] enforced only condition (8), in two dimensions. Their constraint "was not
   sufficient to guarantee conservation", and its three-dimensional extension "is almost
   impossible to implement". (§1, §3) **Stated**
5. Let $G_{k'}$ and $G_k$ overlap in $\Omega_{kk'}$, and let both be discretized conservatively
   (Eqs. 6–7). Then Eqs. (6)–(9) are necessary and sufficient for global conservation. Hence
   uniqueness in the overlap,
   $\sum_{V_c\subset\Omega_{kk'}}\lvert V_c\rvert\,\partial_t\mathbf q^{k}_c=\sum_{V_c\subset\Omega_{kk'}}\lvert V_c\rvert\,\partial_t\mathbf q^{k'}_c$
   (Eq. 8), together with the overlap flux balance
   $\sum_{V_c\subset\Omega_{kk'}}\lvert V_c\rvert\,\partial_t\mathbf q_c=-\sum_{f\in\Gamma_{k|k'}\cup\Gamma_{k'|k}}\mathbf F_fS_f$
   (Eq. 10, with $\mathbf n_f$ outward from $\Omega_{kk'}$), is necessary and sufficient. (§2,
   Eqs. 8–10) **Derived**
6. For patched zones, the necessary and sufficient condition is conservation of the total flux
   across the common boundary,
   $\sum_{f\in\Gamma_{k'|k}}\mathbf F_fS_f=-\sum_{f\in\Gamma_{k|k'}}\mathbf F_fS_f$ (Eq. 11).
   (§2) **Derived**
7. Using $\Gamma_{k|k'}$ as a patch boundary between $G_{k'}\setminus\Omega_{kk'}$ and $G_k$
   makes the scheme conservative, gives a unique solution in $\Omega_{kk'}$, and makes interpolation
   between zones unnecessary. (§3, Fig. 2, items 1–3) **Derived**
8. Conserving mass, momentum and energy flux on each smallest face "in the local sense"
   guarantees global conservation. (§3.1) **Stated**
9. Cell-wise constant reconstruction at the patch boundary gives a first-order interface. Cell-wise
   linear (least-squares gradient) reconstruction gives a second-order interface. (§3.2)
   **Stated**
10. With the area weights $W_f$ of Eq. (14), the flux balance of every cut cell "can be cast in the
    same form no matter what shapes they happen to be in". (§3.3, Fig. 4) **Stated**
11. Freestream conservation served as the debugging criterion. It requires correct area vectors
    for the smallest and cut faces, and it held in every test case. (§5, p. 100) **Shown**
    (asserted; no numbers given)
12. Near a discontinuity, the conservation errors of a nonconservative scheme can exceed a
    first-order error in a smooth region, even though they are formally of truncation-error
    order. They "can potentially result in shock waves with incorrect jump conditions or wrong
    locations even if the numerical scheme itself is of high-order in accuracy". (§5, p. 101)
    **Stated**
13. CFL3D and CFD-FASTRAN share the spatial discretization (cell-centred finite volume, MUSCL with
    minmod, Roe). They differ only in time integration (diagonalized implicit against fully
    implicit with point-Jacobi), a difference that "should disappear at steady state". (§5)
    **Stated**
14. Transonic bump: the inflow/outflow mass imbalance is $4\times10^{-6}\,\%$ on a single zone
    (CFD-FASTRAN), $6\times10^{-6}\,\%$ with the conservative interface and $0.38\,\%$ with the
    original Chimera (CFL3D) on the same two-zone grid, "about five orders larger". (Table I,
    §5.1) **Shown**
15. Transonic bump: the Mach and pressure contours and the lower-wall $C_p$ of both overset methods
    agree "fairly well" with the single-zone fine-grid reference. (Figs. 7–8) **Shown**
16. The second-order (linear) interface gives smoother transitions between the overlapped zones
    than the first-order (constant) interface. (Fig. 9) **Shown**
17. The conservative scheme converges to machine zero, while the original Chimera stalls after
    several orders of residual reduction. The "nonconservative interface treatment may have
    contributed" to the stall. (§5.1, Fig. 10) **Shown** (the cause: **Stated**)
18. Slow moving shock (shock speed $0.015$): the original Chimera develops instabilities that
    dissipate the shock until it disappears, while the conservative scheme passes it through the
    interface. (§5.2, Fig. 13) **Shown**
19. Part-Enander and Sjögreen [16] had shown that a conservative interface lets a moving shock pass
    an overlapped interface while a nonconservative one fails. (§5.2) **Stated**
20. Three-element airfoil (inviscid, three zones): $C_p$ agrees "generally fairly good" with the
    experiment [17]. The main-element residual stalls after 1500 iterations in a limit-cycle-like
    oscillation, attributed to leading-edge separation in an inviscid model of a physically
    turbulent flow. (§5.3, Figs. 16–17) **Shown** (the attribution: **Stated**)
21. Shock diffraction by a cylinder: shock waves and slip lines pass freely through and along the
    overlapped interface, in "very good" qualitative agreement with an experimental shadowgraph.
    (§5.4, Figs. 19–20) **Shown**
22. In two dimensions ZIG costs about one flow-solver step, and it runs once for fixed grids. In
    three dimensions it is expected to cost much more. The per-iteration smallest-face fluxes cost
    under $5\,\%$ of the total CPU time. (§6) **Shown** (2-D) / **Stated** (3-D)
23. The interface is as accurate as the interior when the interface reconstruction has the
    interior's order, so the "loss in accuracy due to grid overlapping is minimized". (§6 b)
    **Stated**
24. The nonconservative Chimera "can capture a strong steady shock wave probably because it is
    zonally conservative". (§6 c) **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (9)** prints the global balance
  $\sum_{\Delta V\in G}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}\Delta V=\sum_{f\in\Gamma_G}F_f\,dS_f$
  without the minus sign that Eqs. (4)–(7) carry. Eq. (10) as printed follows from (6)–(8) only
  with $-\sum_{f\in\Gamma_G}F_f\,dS_f$ on the right of (9) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Eq. (16)** prints the cell interval as "$\xi_{i-1/2}\le\xi\le\xi_{i-1/2}$". The upper bound is
  $\xi_{i+1/2}$, the point at which Eq. (18) evaluates (16).
- **Eq. (25)** is introduced as "Linearizing $F_f^{n+1}$ with respect to $F_f^n$ up to second
  order", but the expansion holds only first-derivative terms in the primitive variables.
- **Eq. (29)** prints "$\theta+1<0_{\max}$" where the text defines $\theta_{\max}$.
- **Table I** prints $6\times10^{-6}\,\%$ for the overlapped CFD-FASTRAN imbalance. From the
  printed rates, $\lvert269.47414-269.47412\rvert/269.47414=7.4\times10^{-6}\,\%$ *(compiler check,
  not in the sources)*. The other two rows agree with their rates.
- **Ref. [10]** (Moon & Liou) prints "AIAA 10th Computational Fluid Dynamics Conference, 1989;
  AIAA-89-1980". Ref. [8] dates the 9th conference to 1989 and ref. [4] the 10th to 1991, so the
  number or the year of ref. [10] is wrong.
