# Summary

OceanWave3D-seakeeping solves the linearized potential-flow problem with high-order finite differences on overlapping curvilinear body-fitted grids. Zhou et al. add generalized modes for the flexural response of a ship girder modelled as an Euler–Bernoulli beam (zero forward speed), validate against experiments and reference numerical results on three cases, and derive both disputed forms of the linearized hydrostatic stiffness of flexible modes (Newman 1994; Malenica & Bigot 2020), showing the buoyancy terms equivalent for pure elastic motions. Overlapping grids are the discretization, not the subject ([high-order discretizations](/methods/high-order-overset-discretization.md), [fluid–structure interaction](/methods/fluid-structure-interaction-on-overset-grids.md)).

# Key claims

1. OceanWave3D-seakeeping solves linearized potential flow with high-order finite differences on overlapping curvilinear body-fitted grids; generalized modes capture flexural responses (Abstract). **Stated**
2. The implementation is validated against experiments and WAMIT reference solutions for three ship-like cases (Abstract, Conclusions). **Shown**
3. Both forms of the linearized hydrostatic stiffness of flexible modes are derived, including gravitational terms; the buoyancy terms are equivalent for pure elastic motions, and the gravitational terms for divergence-free elastic modes; the remaining differences are insignificant for a typical ship (Abstract, Conclusions). **Derived**
4. Shear effects are neglected (Euler–Bernoulli beam); a Timoshenko beam and non-zero forward speed are future work (Conclusions). **Stated**
