# Summary

The volume-of-fluid function is a discontinuous step function; trilinear [inter-grid interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md) from a coarse donor gives fully wet [fringe cells](/definitions/fringe-point.md) values below one. Wang & Stern instead interpolate the smooth distance function and the interface normal to each fringe cell, rebuild the interface there as a plane, and compute the volume fraction geometrically by sub-cell integration. A volume-of-fluid ghost fluid method with interface-free ghost-pressure coefficients handles gravity and surface tension. The method, in CFDShip-Iowa V5.5 with SUGGAR assembly ([SUGGAR](/software/suggar.md)), is verified on three advection tests and two-phase cases and applied to a hydrofoil, a planing surface and a double-step planing hull on [moving overset grids](/methods/moving-overset-grid.md); it is the base of [multiphase flow on overlapping grids](/methods/multiphase-flow-on-overset-grids.md).

# Key claims

1. The volume fraction cannot be interpolated through overset boundaries with the trilinear interpolation used for continuous variables (§1, Fig. 1). **Stated** (illustrated)
2. The distance function, built in a four-cell band, is extended to the whole domain by solving the reinitialization equation with an anchored interface, because donor cells outside the band can feed fringe cells (§2.2). **Stated**
3. In fringe cells the distance function and the normal are interpolated from donors; the interface is the plane $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x=\alpha$ with $\alpha$ from the interpolated distance (§2.2, Eqs. (5)–(6)). **Derived**
4. The fringe-cell volume fraction is the water volume of $l\times m\times n$ sub-cells on the water side of the plane over the cell volume (§2.2, Eqs. (7)–(9)). **Derived**
5. The ghost-pressure coefficients use only cell densities; equal densities give no correction, and in hydrostatic conditions the total-pressure form recovers the one-fluid gradient (§2.3, Eqs. (19)–(26)). **Derived**
6. Advection tests (Zalesak slotted disk, single vortex, LeVeque deformation) on a background grid with one overlapping block: the overset grid raises mass and position errors by at most 0.8 percentage points against a single block (§3.1–3.3, Tables 1, 3, 5). **Shown**
7. Grid convergence of the relative position error: average order above 1.24 (slotted disk) and 2.15 (single vortex) (Tables 2, 4). **Shown**
8. Hydrostatic tank on curvilinear grids: the body-force method produces spurious currents, the volume-of-fluid ghost fluid method does not (§3.4). **Shown**
9. Rising 2 mm air bubble crossing an overlapping block: terminal velocity 0.291 mm/ms against 0.287 mm/ms from the Tomiyama correlation, shapes close to the single-block result (§3.6). **Shown**
10. Applications: hydrofoil force error 20.8 % (V4.5) to 7.8 % (V5.5); planing-surface drag −0.28 % against experiment; double-step hull averaged error 17.6 % to 6.5 % with 2-DOF dynamic overset grids (§4.1–4.3, Tables 6–8). **Shown**
11. A wall blanking distance with $30<y^+<200$ is needed for the interface functions at no-slip walls (§4). **Stated**
