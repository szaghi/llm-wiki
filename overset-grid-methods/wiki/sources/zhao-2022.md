# Summary

Diffused-interface [immersed-boundary](/methods/immersed-boundary-on-overset-grids.md) methods need a fine uniform mesh wherever the body may go. Zhao et al. attach a locally refined Cartesian overset mesh to the body; it only translates with the body's centre-of-mass velocity over a coarse background, while rotation and deformation are carried by the Lagrangian boundary points ([moving overset grids](/methods/moving-overset-grid.md)). Weakly compressible ALE equations are solved on both meshes by a lattice Boltzmann flux solver; the boundary-condition-enforced velocity correction acts on the overset mesh only. Two layers of acceptor cells on each mesh take values from the single nearest donor by a gradient-based piecewise-linear reconstruction ([inter-grid interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md)). Tests: an oscillating [cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md), particle sedimentation, a spinning ball on an unknown trajectory and a self-propelled flapping foil ([fluid–structure interaction](/methods/fluid-structure-interaction-on-overset-grids.md)).

# Key claims

1. The overset mesh is attached to the body and only translates; local rotation and deformation are represented by the immersed-boundary points, so the overset mesh is never rotated, deformed or re-meshed (§II). **Stated**
2. Two layers of acceptor cells on each mesh, at the outer boundary of the overset mesh and around the body in the background (cells inside inactive), exchange data (§II C). **Stated**
3. Each acceptor takes its value from the single nearest donor by $\tilde W=W_{\mathrm{donor}}+\mathbf d\cdot\nabla W_{\mathrm{donor}}$, reusing the Green–Gauss gradient; the paper argues second order by a Taylor expansion, against "first-order" multi-donor inverse-distance interpolation (§II C, Eqs. (45)–(49)). **Derived** (no convergence study)
4. Transversely oscillating cylinder at $Re=40$: piecewise-linear transfer gives smooth forces, inverse-distance transfer high-frequency noise; 31.2 % fewer cells and 25.6 % less CPU time than the single-mesh method (§III A). **Shown**
5. Particle sedimentation (against Wan & Turek): 68.9 % fewer cells, 61.1 % less CPU time; spinning ball: 93.7 % and 92.1 %; self-propelled flapping foil: 84.5 % and 81.7 % (§III B–D). **Shown**
6. All timings are serial on one Intel Xeon E5-2690 v3 core (§III). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (49) bounds the donor–acceptor distance by $0.5h$; in 2-D the Euclidean distance to the nearest cell centre can reach $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}h$, which leaves the $O(h^2)$ conclusion unchanged *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eqs. (16)–(17) add terms of different dimensions under the inner square root ($4u^2c^2$ and $6c^2$) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (20) has a stray minus sign; Eq. (24) mixes the summation indices; Table V prints 81.7 % for $1-37.2/204.3=81.8$ %; §III C calls a five-level refinement "three-level"; the time level changes from $n+1$ (Eq. (36)) to $n$ (Eqs. (38), (42)) without comment.
