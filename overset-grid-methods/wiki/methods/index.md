# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Adaptive mesh refinement on overlapping grids](/methods/amr-on-overset-grids.md) — Block-structured adaptive mesh refinement on composite overlapping grids: Henshaw and Schwendeman build refinement patches on every component grid as restrictions of its mapping, so they move with moving base grids; the page gives the hierarchy, regridding, error estimator and time stepping, the equal-resolution and cost evidence, and the earlier proposals the sources recall.
* [Conservative overset interface](/methods/conservative-overset-interface.md) — Why interpolation-coupled overset grids are not globally conservative, the conditions for conservation over an overlap, Wang's interface that cuts the minor grid's boundary into the major grid as a patched interface carrying Riemann fluxes (mass imbalance 6e-6 % against 0.38 %), Picard et al.'s intersection-weighted transfer with conservation defects up to 0.635 %, and when conservation matters.
* [Donor search](/methods/donor-search.md) — Donor search finds, for each receptor, the enclosing cell of another grid and the receptor's local coordinates in it; the page synthesizes pruning by hierarchy and bounding boxes, start cells from trees and auxiliary grids, stencil walking and jumping, the partition-robust line walk, ADT and GPU bounding-volume searches, incremental search for moving grids, high-order offsets, and measured costs.
* [Fluid–structure interaction on overlapping grids](/methods/fluid-structure-interaction-on-overset-grids.md) — Partitioned fluid–structure coupling on moving and deforming overlapping grids: Henshaw and Schwendeman's rigid bodies driven by compressible flow with a predictor–corrector Newton–Euler integrator, and Banks et al.'s added-mass partitioned scheme whose impedance-weighted interface projection stays stable for light nonlinear solids where the traditional partitioned scheme fails.
* [High-order discretizations on overlapping grids](/methods/high-order-overset-discretization.md) — How fourth- to sixth-order finite-difference schemes are run on overset grids: interpolation of matching order and width, compact Padé schemes and filters with holes and two-point fringes, Fourier error analysis of interpolation formulas, symmetric operators, and high-order dissipation against the weak instability that interpolation causes.
* [Hole cutting](/methods/hole-cutting.md) — Hole cutting removes from an overset grid the points that lie inside bodies or are made redundant by a finer overlapping grid; the page compares the surface-based tests of the Chimera papers, Cartesian hole maps, analytic shapes and object X-rays, tree, auxiliary-grid and GPU collision-detection cutters with flood fills, priority-driven classification, cut-cell clipping, and Chimera without holes.
* [Implicit hole cutting](/methods/implicit-hole-cutting.md) — Implicit hole cutting (Lee and Baeder 2003) replaces cutting by body surfaces with cell selection: every point is tested against all overlapping grids and the smallest enclosing cell wins; the page gives the rule, its algorithm and cost, its unstructured partitioned form in PUNDIT and TIOGA, the shrinking fringe gap it causes, and its relation to CMPGRD and PEGASUS 5.
* [Incompressible flow on overlapping grids](/methods/incompressible-flow-on-overset-grids.md) — Incompressible Navier–Stokes on overlapping grids: Henshaw's fourth-order velocity–pressure method with div u = 0 on the boundary, quartic interpolation and divergence damping; second-order finite-volume projection and projection–artificial-compressibility schemes; single-system versus Schwarz coupling of the grids; and the interpolation order the elliptic pressure needs.
* [Inter-grid interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md) — How overset grids exchange data: each receptor takes a weighted sum of donor values. Covers linear Taylor, trilinear, tensor-product Lagrange, Hermite, compact, intersection-volume, radial-basis-function and unstructured cell weights, their order and width with measured effects, cell-size ratio, geometry consistency, monotonicity at shocks, parallel evaluation and cost.
* [Maxwell's equations on overlapping grids](/methods/maxwell-equations-on-overset-grids.md) — Henshaw's fourth-order time-domain Maxwell solver on composite overlapping grids: the second-order (vector wave equation) form, modified-equation time stepping, symmetric curvilinear operators, compatibility boundary and dielectric interface conditions with their stability lemmas, Lagrange interpolation of width p+1, dissipation against interpolation-induced weak instability, and measured fourth-order convergence.
* [Moving overset grids](/methods/moving-overset-grid.md) — Moving overset grids let component grids follow rigid, rotating or deforming bodies, with domain connectivity recomputed every time step and coupled to the flow solver and body dynamics: Meakin and Suhs's unsteady Chimera cycle and hole-motion limit, mapped equations with grid velocity, exposed points, Ogen's incremental update, SUGGAR's body-frame trees, parallel and GPU per-step assembly, and moving-body demonstrations.
* [Overlap optimization](/methods/overlap-optimization.md) — Overlap optimization reduces the overlap of an assembled overset grid to what interpolation needs, keeping the better-suited points active and interpolating the rest; the page covers the CMPGRD deletion and conversion steps, PEGASUS 5's finest-points-kept algorithm and cell-difference parameter, the size criteria of implicit and partitioned assembly, cutting volumes, and the grid-size ratios the sources tolerate.
* [Parallel and GPU overset grid assembly](/methods/parallel-overset-grid-assembly.md) — How overset grid assembly is distributed: PEGASUS 5's coarse-grained master–worker MPI over grids and grid pairs, Roget and Sitaraman's assembly on the solver's own partitions with local data, query routing and adaptive load re-balancing (PUNDIT, HART-II scaling to 8192 cores), and Horne and Mahesh's GPU cutting and donor pairing by bounding volume hierarchies and GJK.
* [Shock capturing on overlapping grids](/methods/shock-capturing-on-overset-grids.md) — How shocks, detonations and material interfaces are captured on overset grids: what happens when a discontinuity crosses an interpolation boundary, the Godunov-type schemes run on each component grid, Picard et al.'s limited intersection-weighted transfer and corner-radius limiter, the patch-to-substrate cell-size ratio (artifacts above 4), slip-wall conditions, and the measured accuracy for discontinuous flows.
* [Twilight-zone flow (manufactured solutions)](/methods/twilight-zone-flow.md) — Verification by forcing: add to the equations, boundary and interface conditions the residual of a chosen smooth function so that it becomes the exact solution, then measure convergence rates through boundaries and inter-grid interpolation; covers incompressible, compressible, Maxwell, fluid–structure and finite-volume proxy tests on overlapping grids, and unforced exact solutions used the same way.
