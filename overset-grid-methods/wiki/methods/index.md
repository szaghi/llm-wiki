# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Conservative overset interface](/methods/conservative-overset-interface.md) — Why interpolation-coupled overset grids are not globally conservative, the conditions for conservation over an overlap, and Wang's interface: the minor grid's boundary is cut into the major grid as a patched interface carrying Riemann fluxes, giving a mass imbalance of 6e-6 % against 0.38 % and carrying moving shocks through it.
* [Donor search](/methods/donor-search.md) — Donor search finds, for each receptor of an overset grid, the enclosing cell of another grid and the receptor's local coordinates in it; the page synthesizes pruning by hierarchy and bounding boxes, start-cell trees, stencil walking and jumping with Newton inversion, inside tests, the polyhedral neighbour walk, and measured costs.
* [Hole cutting](/methods/hole-cutting.md) — Hole cutting removes from an overset grid the points that lie inside bodies or are made redundant by a finer overlapping grid; the page compares the surface-based tests of the Chimera papers, the Cartesian hole map with line of sight, tree-based approximations, priority-driven classification and cut-cell clipping.
* [Implicit hole cutting](/methods/implicit-hole-cutting.md) — Implicit hole cutting (Lee and Baeder 2003) replaces cutting by body surfaces with cell selection: every point of every grid is tested against all overlapping grids, the smallest enclosing cell wins, and holes appear as a by-product; the page gives the selection rule, its algorithmic parts, cost and relation to CMPGRD and PEGASUS 5.
* [Incompressible flow on overlapping grids](/methods/incompressible-flow-on-overset-grids.md) — Henshaw's fourth-order method of lines for incompressible Navier–Stokes on curvilinear composite overlapping grids: velocity–pressure form with a pressure Poisson equation and the boundary condition div u = 0, ghost-point numerical boundary conditions, quartic interpolation, divergence damping, and fourth-order convergence on overlapping 2-D and 3-D grids.
* [Inter-grid interpolation](/methods/inter-grid-interpolation.md) — How overset grids exchange data: each receptor takes a weighted sum of donor-stencil values. Covers the formulas of the founding sources (linear Taylor, trilinear, tensor-product Lagrange to quartic, Hermite cubic, cell-centred unstructured weights, Yin–Yang symmetric stencils), their order and width, geometry consistency, monotonicity at shocks, and cost.
* [Moving overset grids](/methods/moving-overset-grid.md) — Moving overset grids let component grids follow bodies in relative motion, with domain connectivity recomputed every time step and coupled to the flow solver and body dynamics; the page covers Meakin and Suhs's unsteady Chimera cycle, time metrics and hole-motion limit, and SUGGAR's body-frame trees, hierarchical transforms and process coupling.
* [Overlap optimization](/methods/overlap-optimization.md) — Overlap optimization reduces the overlap of an assembled overset grid to what interpolation needs, keeping the better-suited points active and interpolating the rest; the page covers the CMPGRD deletion and conversion steps, PEGASUS 5's finest-points-kept algorithm with its cell-difference parameter, and the size criteria of implicit hole cutting.
* [Twilight-zone flow (manufactured solutions)](/methods/twilight-zone-flow.md) — Verification by forcing: add to the equations the residual of a chosen smooth function so that it becomes the exact solution, then measure convergence rates through boundaries and inter-grid interpolation; covers Henshaw's trigonometric incompressible solutions and Chesshire and Henshaw's compressible Navier–Stokes and Poisson tests.
