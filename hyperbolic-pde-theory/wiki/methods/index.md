# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [First-order linear PDEs: general solution along characteristics](/methods/first-order-linear-pde-characteristics.md) — The general solution of b1 u_x + b2 u_y = 0 is an arbitrary function of an invariant constant along the characteristics dx/b1 = dy/b2; a zeroth-order term is absorbed by a particular solution factor, a forcing by a particular integral, and data on a non-characteristic curve then fix the arbitrary function where its characteristics reach.
* [General solution of constant-coefficient second-order PDEs](/methods/constant-coefficient-second-order-general-solution.md) — For a11 u_xx + 2 a12 u_xy + a22 u_yy = 0 with constant coefficients, u = F(x + beta1 y) + G(x + beta2 y) where the beta are the roots of a22 beta^2 + 2 a12 beta + a11 = 0, or F + x G in the repeated-root parabolic case; in characteristic coordinates the equation reduces to u_xi_eta = 0, or u_eta_eta = 0 when parabolic.
* [Green's functions for linear ODEs](/methods/greens-functions-for-linear-odes.md) — The solution of an nth-order linear ODE L y = psi with homogeneous boundary conditions is the integral of G(x, x') psi(x') where L G = delta(x - x'), G obeys the boundary conditions in x, its first n-2 derivatives are continuous at x = x' and the (n-1)th jumps by 1/a_n(x'); G depends on the boundary conditions, and one-point conditions give a causal kernel.
* [Green's functions for linear PDEs](/methods/greens-functions-for-pdes.md) — Solve L u = psi by the response G to a unit point source: with homogeneous boundary conditions u is the integral of G times the source; Green's second theorem gives a representation formula carrying boundary data, which a Dirichlet Green's function (zero on the boundary) or a Neumann one (constant normal derivative, by the compatibility condition) reduces to known data; images build G for planes, spheres and discs.
* [Integral transform methods](/methods/integral-transform-methods.md) — Transform a linear PDE (or ODE) in one variable, Laplace in time for initial-value problems, Fourier in space on infinite ranges, to remove that variable's derivatives, solve the resulting ODE (or algebraic equation) with the data built in, and invert, using convolution to obtain Green's-function integrals such as the Gaussian heat-kernel solution.
* [Separation of variables](/methods/separation-of-variables.md) — Seek product solutions of a linear PDE so that it splits into ODEs coupled only through separation constants, choose the constants and the form of each factor from the boundary conditions, and superpose over the allowed constants, a sum for discrete values (Fourier, Bessel, Legendre expansions) or an integral for a continuum; in polar coordinates single-valuedness and regularity select the special functions.
* [Series solutions of linear ODEs (Frobenius' method)](/methods/frobenius-series-solutions.md) — About an ordinary or regular singular point of y'' + a1 y' + a0 y = 0, substitute a power series times (x - x0)^nu: the lowest power gives the indicial equation for nu, the rest a recurrence for the coefficients; Fuchs' theorem guarantees at least one such solution, a second follows from the Wronskian and carries a logarithm when the indicial roots coincide or differ by an integer, and the regular solution is the one kept on the axis or at the origin when separated PDEs produce Bessel and Legendre equations.
* [Solution methods for first-order ODEs](/methods/first-order-ode-solution-methods.md) — Closed-form methods for first-order ODEs of the kind the characteristic equations of first-order PDEs produce: separation of variables, exact equations and integrating factors (in particular exp of the integral of the coefficient for linear equations), homogeneous and isobaric substitutions, Bernoulli and Riccati equations, and for higher degree the factorisation and Clairaut forms with their singular solutions.
* [Variation of parameters](/methods/variation-of-parameters.md) — A particular integral of an nth-order linear ODE with known complementary function y_c = sum c_j y_j is sum k_j(x) y_j with the k_j' fixed by n linear equations whose determinant is the Wronskian: the first n-1 combinations of derivatives vanish and the last equals psi/a_n.
