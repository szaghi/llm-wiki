# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Characteristic curves](/definitions/characteristic-curve.md) — Curves along which data on a curve cannot determine the derivatives of the solution: for a first-order linear PDE the curves dy/dx = b2/b1 along which the solution's invariant is constant, for a second-order PDE the real roots of the characteristic ODE; they carry information, and data on a characteristic, or on a curve crossing one twice, leave the solution undetermined or overdetermined.
* [Classification of second-order linear PDEs](/definitions/second-order-pde-classification.md) — A second-order linear PDE in two variables is hyperbolic, parabolic or elliptic as the discriminant of its principal part is positive, zero or negative; the type can change across the plane, a change of variables preserves it, and it fixes the number of real characteristic families.
* [Diffusion equation](/definitions/diffusion-equation.md) — The model parabolic equation, kappa times the laplacian of u equals u_t, derived from a gradient flux law and the divergence theorem over an arbitrary volume; it admits no travelling-wave general solution, but has the error-function similarity solution in x over the square root of t, decaying separated modes, and the Gaussian heat-kernel solution of the initial-value problem.
* [Dirichlet, Neumann and Cauchy boundary conditions](/definitions/boundary-condition-types.md) — Dirichlet data prescribe the solution, Neumann data its normal derivative, Cauchy data both, on an open or a closed boundary (which may lie partly at infinity); a condition is homogeneous when it survives scaling of the solution, and prescribing both Dirichlet and Neumann data for Poisson's equation overdetermines it.
* [Fundamental solution](/definitions/fundamental-solution.md) — A solution of the operator equation with a unit point source and no boundary conditions beyond behaviour at infinity: -1/(4 pi |x-x'|) for the Laplacian in three dimensions, (1/2 pi) ln|x-x'| plus a constant in two, and the Gaussian heat kernel for the one-dimensional diffusion equation; Green's functions are built from it.
* [Homogeneous equations and homogeneous problems](/definitions/homogeneous-linear-problem.md) — A linear equation is homogeneous when every multiple of a solution is a solution, a problem when its boundary conditions share the property; the general solution of an inhomogeneous linear problem is any particular solution plus the general solution of the homogeneous problem, and inhomogeneity can be moved between the boundary data and the equation.
* [Laplace and Poisson equations](/definitions/laplace-and-poisson-equations.md) — The model elliptic equations, laplacian of u equals zero or a given source: steady states of diffusion and potentials of gravity, electrostatics and irrotational flow; closed-boundary Dirichlet or Neumann data fix the solution (up to a constant for Neumann), the two-dimensional general solution is F(x+iy)+G(x-iy), and separated, Green's-function and image solutions are available.
* [Wave equation](/definitions/wave-equation.md) — The model hyperbolic equation, laplacian of u equals u_tt over c squared: derived for the small transverse vibrations of a string, its one-dimensional general solution is two counter-propagating waves of fixed shape along the characteristics x - ct and x + ct, in three dimensions plane waves along any unit vector, and in bounded domains separated normal modes.
* [Wronskian](/definitions/wronskian.md) — The determinant of n functions and their first n-1 derivatives; a non-zero Wronskian on an interval proves linear independence (the converse fails for general functions), and it is the determinant that variation of parameters and the two-point ODE Green's function divide by.
