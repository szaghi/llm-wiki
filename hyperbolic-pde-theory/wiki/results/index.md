# Results

Theorems, proved properties, impossibility results.

* [Boundary data by equation type](/results/boundary-data-by-equation-type.md) — Stated without proof: a hyperbolic equation takes Cauchy data on an open boundary, a parabolic one Dirichlet or Neumann data on an open boundary, an elliptic one Dirichlet or Neumann data on a closed boundary (possibly at infinity), these pairings giving a unique (sometimes up to a constant) and well-defined solution.
* [d'Alembert's formula](/results/dalembert-formula.md) — The solution of the one-dimensional wave equation on the whole line with initial displacement u0 and velocity u1 is the mean of u0 at x - ct and x + ct plus 1/(2c) times the integral of u1 over [x - ct, x + ct]: half of each initial disturbance travels each way along the characteristics.
* [Structure of linear ODE solutions](/results/linear-ode-solution-structure.md) — The general solution of an nth-order linear ODE is the complementary function, a superposition of n linearly independent homogeneous solutions with n arbitrary constants, plus any particular integral; with constant coefficients the homogeneous solutions come from the roots of the auxiliary polynomial (x^k e^{nu x} for a repeated root), and Euler, Legendre and canonical-form substitutions reduce some variable-coefficient cases.
* [Uniqueness theorem for Poisson's equation](/results/uniqueness-theorem-poisson.md) — A real C2 solution of the laplacian of u equals psi in a region, with either u or its normal derivative prescribed on the whole boundary (possibly including a sphere at infinity), is unique, up to an additive constant in the Neumann case; the proof applies Green's first theorem to the difference of two solutions (an energy argument), which also gives uniqueness for the laplacian of u minus k^2 u = 0.
