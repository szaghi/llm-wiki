# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Brackbill & Saltzman (1982) — Adaptive zoning for singular problems in two dimensions](/sources/brackbill-1982.md) — Variational mesh generation minimizing a weighted sum of smoothness, orthogonality and weighted-volume functionals; a solution-dependent weight adapts the mesh and reduces the error of singular problems several-fold.
* [Deng, Mao, Tu, Liu & Zhang (2011) — Geometric conservation law and applications to high-order finite difference schemes with stationary grids](/sources/deng-2011.md) — The conservative metric method: conservative metric forms with outer operators equal to the flux operator are proved sufficient for the surface conservation law of high-order finite differences, applicable to central but not to upwind schemes.
* [Deng, Min, Mao, Liu, Tu & Zhang (2013) — Further studies on Geometric Conservation Law and applications to high-order finite difference schemes with stationary grids](/sources/deng-2013.md) — The symmetrical conservative metric method: metrics in the symmetrical (coordinate-invariant) conservative form with a consistent Jacobian, equal to finite-volume face vectors and cell volume at second order, improving high-order results on irregular grids.
* [Gordon & Hall (1973) — Construction of curvilinear co-ordinate systems and applications to mesh generation](/sources/gordon-1973.md) — Mesh generation as the construction of univalent maps of the unit square onto the domain by transfinite (blending-function) interpolation: Boolean sums of univariate projectors that match the boundary everywhere, with the Coons patch and isoparametric maps as special cases.
* [Huang, Ren & Russell (1994) — Moving mesh partial differential equations (MMPDES) based on the equidistribution principle](/sources/huang-1994.md) — Continuous moving mesh equations derived from the equidistribution principle, analysed for stability and mesh crossing; versions relaxing toward equidistribution are robust and crossing-free.
* [Johnen, Remacle & Geuzaine (2013) — Geometrical validity of curvilinear finite elements](/sources/johnen-2013.md) — Provable bounds on the Jacobian determinant of curved polynomial elements from its Bézier expansion with adaptive subdivision, giving a sharp validity test and a distortion measure for all element types.
* [Knupp (2001) — Algebraic mesh quality metrics](/sources/knupp-2001.md) — A mathematical theory of mesh quality metrics built on element Jacobians referenced to an ideal element: factorization into orientation, volume and shape, algebraic shape metrics, and the condition number as distance to degeneracy, equivalent to the mean ratio.
* [Kopriva (2006) — Metric identities and the discontinuous spectral element method on curvilinear meshes](/sources/kopriva-2006.md) — Discrete metric identities for discontinuous spectral elements on curved quadrilaterals and hexahedra: the cross-product form satisfies them in 2-D for isoparametric geometry but not in 3-D, while interpolated conservative and invariant curl forms always do.
* [Nonomura, Iizuka & Fujii (2010) — Freestream and vortex preservation properties of high-order WENO and WCNS on curvilinear grids](/sources/nonomura-2010.md) — Comparison of WENO and weighted compact nonlinear schemes on curvilinear grids: finite-difference WENO in generalized coordinates cannot preserve the free stream, while WCNS with conservative metrics differenced like the fluxes preserves it and resolves vortices better.
* [Steger & Chaussee (1980) — Generation of body-fitted coordinates using hyperbolic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1980.md) — Hyperbolic grid generation: orthogonality plus a prescribed arc-length increment or cell area marched outward from a body by a noniterative implicit scheme, giving fast, smooth exterior grids with spacing control near the body.
* [Steger & Sorenson (1979) — Automatic mesh-point clustering near a boundary in grid generation with elliptic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1979.md) — Control functions of the elliptic generator that decay away from the inner boundary and are solved for iteratively so that the grid has a prescribed first spacing and intersects the boundary at a prescribed angle.
* [Tang & Tang (2003) — Adaptive mesh methods for one- and two-dimensional hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/tang-2003.md) — A moving mesh algorithm for hyperbolic conservation laws that alternates solution evolution by any finite-volume scheme with iterative mesh redistribution and a conservative interpolation, which conserves mass and is TVD in one dimension.
* [Thomas & Lombard (1979) — Geometric conservation law and its application to flow computations on moving grids](/sources/thomas-1979.md) — Formulates the geometric conservation law for the volume element under time-dependent mappings and solves it with the flow scheme's own difference operators, so the Jacobian is consistent with the scheme and uniform flow is preserved on moving grids.
* [Thompson, Thames & Mastin (1974) — Automatic numerical generation of body-fitted curvilinear coordinate system for field containing any number of arbitrarily shaped two-dimensional bodies](/sources/thompson-1974.md) — Elliptic generation of body-fitted coordinates for multiply connected regions with any number of bodies: coordinates as solutions of Laplace or Poisson equations with Dirichlet data, inverted onto a rectangular computational domain, with control functions for line spacing.
* [Thompson, Warsi & Mastin (1982) — Boundary-fitted coordinate systems for numerical solution of partial differential equations — A review](/sources/thompson-1982.md) — Comprehensive review of boundary-fitted coordinate generation (conformal, orthogonal, algebraic, elliptic, hyperbolic), line control, adaptive and time-dependent systems, grid-induced truncation error and geometrically conservative forms.
* [Toulorge, Geuzaine, Remacle & Lambrechts (2013) — Robust untangling of curvilinear meshes](/sources/toulorge-2013.md) — Untangling of high-order curvilinear meshes by unconstrained optimization with moving log-barriers on the element distortion, applied locally around invalid elements, with a metric-eigenvalue term that controls the explicit stable time step.
* [Visbal & Gaitonde (2002) — On the use of higher-order finite-difference schemes on curvilinear and deforming meshes](/sources/visbal-2002.md) — Sixth-order compact schemes with high-order filters on stretched, curvilinear and deforming meshes: filtering for nonsmooth meshes, conservative metric evaluation for 3-D free-stream preservation, and the time derivative of the Jacobian from the geometric conservation law on moving meshes.
* [Winslow (1966) — Numerical solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation in a nonuniform triangle mesh](/sources/winslow-1966.md) — Finite-volume (Ritz-equivalent) solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation on a topologically regular triangle mesh by over-relaxation, with an appendix that builds the mesh by solving inverted Laplace equations — the origin of elliptic grid generation.
* [Xie, Sevilla, Hassan & Morgan (2013) — The generation of arbitrary order curved meshes for 3D finite element analysis](/sources/xie-2013.md) — A posteriori curving of straight-sided meshes by a high-order linear elasticity analogy with boundary displacements onto the NURBS geometry as loads, for isotropic and anisotropic 3-D meshes, assessed by the scaled Jacobian.
* [Zhu & Hu (2019) — Free-stream preserving linear-upwind and WENO schemes on curvilinear grids](/sources/zhu-2019.md) — Free-stream preservation for linear-upwind, WENO and hybrid finite-difference schemes on stationary curvilinear grids by splitting the flux into a central part with symmetrical conservative metrics and a dissipative part whose neighbouring-point differences share one set of metrics.
