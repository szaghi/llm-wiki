# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Brackbill & Saltzman (1982) — Adaptive zoning for singular problems in two dimensions](/sources/brackbill-1982.md) — Variational mesh generation minimizing a weighted sum of smoothness, orthogonality and weighted-volume functionals; a solution-dependent weight adapts the mesh and reduces the error of singular problems several-fold.
* [Gordon & Hall (1973) — Construction of curvilinear co-ordinate systems and applications to mesh generation](/sources/gordon-1973.md) — Mesh generation as the construction of univalent maps of the unit square onto the domain by transfinite (blending-function) interpolation: Boolean sums of univariate projectors that match the boundary everywhere, with the Coons patch and isoparametric maps as special cases.
* [Huang, Ren & Russell (1994) — Moving mesh partial differential equations (MMPDES) based on the equidistribution principle](/sources/huang-1994.md) — Continuous moving mesh equations derived from the equidistribution principle, analysed for stability and mesh crossing; versions relaxing toward equidistribution are robust and crossing-free.
* [Knupp (2001) — Algebraic mesh quality metrics](/sources/knupp-2001.md) — A mathematical theory of mesh quality metrics built on element Jacobians referenced to an ideal element: factorization into orientation, volume and shape, algebraic shape metrics, and the condition number as distance to degeneracy, equivalent to the mean ratio.
* [Steger & Chaussee (1980) — Generation of body-fitted coordinates using hyperbolic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1980.md) — Hyperbolic grid generation: orthogonality plus a prescribed arc-length increment or cell area marched outward from a body by a noniterative implicit scheme, giving fast, smooth exterior grids with spacing control near the body.
* [Steger & Sorenson (1979) — Automatic mesh-point clustering near a boundary in grid generation with elliptic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1979.md) — Control functions of the elliptic generator that decay away from the inner boundary and are solved for iteratively so that the grid has a prescribed first spacing and intersects the boundary at a prescribed angle.
* [Thomas & Lombard (1979) — Geometric conservation law and its application to flow computations on moving grids](/sources/thomas-1979.md) — Formulates the geometric conservation law for the volume element under time-dependent mappings and solves it with the flow scheme's own difference operators, so the Jacobian is consistent with the scheme and uniform flow is preserved on moving grids.
* [Thompson, Thames & Mastin (1974) — Automatic numerical generation of body-fitted curvilinear coordinate system for field containing any number of arbitrarily shaped two-dimensional bodies](/sources/thompson-1974.md) — Elliptic generation of body-fitted coordinates for multiply connected regions with any number of bodies: coordinates as solutions of Laplace or Poisson equations with Dirichlet data, inverted onto a rectangular computational domain, with control functions for line spacing.
* [Thompson, Warsi & Mastin (1982) — Boundary-fitted coordinate systems for numerical solution of partial differential equations — A review](/sources/thompson-1982.md) — Comprehensive review of boundary-fitted coordinate generation (conformal, orthogonal, algebraic, elliptic, hyperbolic), line control, adaptive and time-dependent systems, grid-induced truncation error and geometrically conservative forms.
* [Winslow (1966) — Numerical solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation in a nonuniform triangle mesh](/sources/winslow-1966.md) — Finite-volume (Ritz-equivalent) solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation on a topologically regular triangle mesh by over-relaxation, with an appendix that builds the mesh by solving inverted Laplace equations — the origin of elliptic grid generation.
