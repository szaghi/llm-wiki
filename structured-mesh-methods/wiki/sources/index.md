# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Assonitis, Paciorri, Ciallella, Ricchiuto & Bonfiglioli (2022) — Extrapolated shock fitting for two-dimensional flows on structured grids](/sources/assonitis-2022.md) — Structured extrapolated shock fitting: the fitted shock blanks the cells it crosses and exchanges data with the surrounding flow by Taylor extrapolation, decoupling shock and grid topology and recovering second-order convergence.
* [Bach, Rueda-Ramírez, Kopriva & Gassner (2025) — Mimetic metrics for the DGSEM](/sources/bach-2025.md) — Divergence-free metric terms for discontinuous Galerkin spectral elements from projections that commute with the discrete de Rham complex, preserving the free stream by construction with smaller rounding errors than the curl form.
* [Barrera, Kolev, Mittal & Tomov (2023) — High-order mesh morphing for boundary and interface fitting to implicit geometries](/sources/barrera-2023.md) — Morphing high-order meshes so that selected faces fit the zero isocontour of a discrete level-set function, by adding an adaptive fitting penalty to target-matrix optimization and representing the level set on an adaptive background mesh.
* [Brackbill & Saltzman (1982) — Adaptive zoning for singular problems in two dimensions](/sources/brackbill-1982.md) — Variational mesh generation minimizing a weighted sum of smoothness, orthogonality and weighted-volume functionals; a solution-dependent weight adapts the mesh and reduces the error of singular problems several-fold.
* [Camier, Dobrev, Knupp, Kolev, Mittal, Rieben & Tomov (2023) — Accelerating high-order mesh optimization using finite element partial assembly on GPUs](/sources/camier-2023.md) — GPU-oriented high-order target-matrix optimization recast as finite-element operations with partial assembly, sum factorization and matrix-free Newton–MINRES solves, plus a reproducible mesh-optimization benchmark.
* [Chen, Li, Wan, He, Gong, Pang & Liu (2022) — MGNet: a novel differential mesh generation method based on unsupervised neural networks](/sources/chen-2022.md) — Unsupervised, physics-informed neural network for structured mesh generation: the network represents the computational-to-physical map, the loss holds the generator's PDE residual and boundary terms, and trained networks predict meshes of any size.
* [Chen, Liu & Artola-Zúñiga (2025) — Linear-stream preserving finite difference method on curvilinear grids](/sources/chen-2025.md) — Linear-stream preservation, a requirement stronger than free-stream preservation, met by a discrete-equivalence finite-difference framework with gradient-based interpolation that reduces errors on non-smooth curvilinear grids.
* [Chen, Liu, Zhang, Liu, Wang, Deng & Pang (2023) — Developing a novel structured mesh generation method based on deep neural networks](/sources/chen-2023.md) — MeshNet: a physics-informed neural network for structured meshes trained on a Poisson generator's residual, boundary terms and coarse-mesh data, predicting meshes of any density without retraining.
* [Cicchino, Del Rey Fernández, Nadarajah, Chan & Carpenter (2022) — Provably stable flux reconstruction high-order methods on curvilinear elements](/sources/cicchino-2022.md) — Provably stable energy-stable flux reconstruction on curvilinear elements: conservative and non-conservative DG forms differ even with exact metrics, so split forms and metric-dependent correction functions on the full split form are required.
* [Deng, Mao, Tu, Liu & Zhang (2011) — Geometric conservation law and applications to high-order finite difference schemes with stationary grids](/sources/deng-2011.md) — The conservative metric method: conservative metric forms with outer operators equal to the flux operator are proved sufficient for the surface conservation law of high-order finite differences, applicable to central but not to upwind schemes.
* [Deng, Min, Mao, Liu, Tu & Zhang (2013) — Further studies on Geometric Conservation Law and applications to high-order finite difference schemes with stationary grids](/sources/deng-2013.md) — The symmetrical conservative metric method: metrics in the symmetrical (coordinate-invariant) conservative form with a consistent Jacobian, equal to finite-volume face vectors and cell volume at second order, improving high-order results on irregular grids.
* [Dobrev, Knupp, Kolev, Mittal & Tomov (2019) — The target-matrix optimization paradigm for high-order meshes](/sources/dobrev-2019.md) — Extension of target-matrix optimization to high-order isoparametric meshes: targets at every quadrature point, a variational objective, shape, size and alignment metrics with barrier categories, and a safeguarded Newton solver, implemented in MFEM.
* [Dobrev, Knupp, Kolev, Mittal & Tomov (2022) — hr-Adaptivity for nonconforming high-order meshes with the target matrix optimization paradigm](/sources/dobrev-2022.md) — hr-adaptivity combining target-matrix node optimization with nonconforming isotropic and anisotropic refinement chosen by TMOP quality estimators, meeting size and aspect-ratio targets with fewer degrees of freedom.
* [Dobrev, Knupp, Kolev, Mittal, Rieben & Tomov (2020) — Simulation-driven optimization of high-order meshes in ALE hydrodynamics](/sources/dobrev-2020.md) — High-order target-matrix optimization driven by discrete simulation fields for multi-material ALE shock hydrodynamics: adaptive targets, normalized multi-metric objectives, space-dependent limiting of node motion and TMOP-based ALE triggers.
* [Dzanic, Mittal, Kim, Yang, Petrides, Keith & Anderson (2024) — DynAMO: Multi-agent reinforcement learning for dynamic anticipatory mesh optimization with applications to hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/dzanic-2024.md) — Multi-agent reinforcement learning of local refinement policies that anticipate future solution states, as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process, for discontinuous Galerkin solutions of hyperbolic problems.
* [El Aouad, Larcher & Hachem (2022) — Anisotropic adaptive body-fitted meshes for CFD](/sources/elaouad-2022.md) — Body-fitted anisotropic meshes from an immersed level-set description: metric-based anisotropic adaptation to the level-set gradients followed by node relocation onto the interface and edge swapping of cut elements.
* [Gordon & Hall (1973) — Construction of curvilinear co-ordinate systems and applications to mesh generation](/sources/gordon-1973.md) — Mesh generation as the construction of univalent maps of the unit square onto the domain by transfinite (blending-function) interpolation: Boolean sums of univariate projectors that match the boundary everywhere, with the Coons patch and isoparametric maps as special cases.
* [Green, Kirilov, Turner, Marcon, Eichstädt, Laughton, Cantwell, Sherwin, Peiró & Moxey (2024) — NekMesh: An open-source high-order mesh generation framework](/sources/green-2024.md) — Open-source high-order mesh generation framework: a CAD-independent module pipeline for curving from CAD or linear meshes, isoparametric boundary-layer splitting and variational optimization and untangling.
* [Han, Xu & Liu (2022) — Improvement to a general methodology for free-stream preservation on curvilinear grids](/sources/han-2022.md) — Free-stream preservation for linear upwind and WENO finite differences by solving the full transformed equations with the source term discretized like the fluxes, implemented by freezing metrics and Jacobian within each stencil during flux splitting.
* [Hu & Chen (2026) — A review of high-order curvilinear mesh generation methods](/sources/hu-2026.md) — Review of high-order curvilinear mesh generation: validity, quality and fidelity measures, optimization by node relocation and topology change, direct and indirect generation methods, and isoparametric boundary-layer splitting.
* [Huang, Ren & Russell (1994) — Moving mesh partial differential equations (MMPDES) based on the equidistribution principle](/sources/huang-1994.md) — Continuous moving mesh equations derived from the equidistribution principle, analysed for stability and mesh crossing; versions relaxing toward equidistribution are robust and crossing-free.
* [Johnen, Remacle & Geuzaine (2013) — Geometrical validity of curvilinear finite elements](/sources/johnen-2013.md) — Provable bounds on the Jacobian determinant of curved polynomial elements from its Bézier expansion with adaptive subdivision, giving a sharp validity test and a distortion measure for all element types.
* [Kim, Chae & You (2025) — Automatic mesh generation for optimal CFD of a blade passage using deep reinforcement learning](/sources/kim-2025.md) — Deep multi-agent reinforcement learning of structured-mesh parameters for turbomachinery blade passages, giving grid-converged single simulations at a desired cost for new configurations.
* [Kirilov, Zhou, Peiró & Moxey (2026) — High-order curvilinear mesh generation from third-party meshes](/sources/kirilov-2026.md) — End-to-end high-order meshing of meshes from other software: reconstruction of mesh–CAD connectivity, vertex projection, curving, node-sliding optimization and boundary-layer splitting, even with non-watertight CAD.
* [Knupp (2001) — Algebraic mesh quality metrics](/sources/knupp-2001.md) — A mathematical theory of mesh quality metrics built on element Jacobians referenced to an ideal element: factorization into orientation, volume and shape, algebraic shape metrics, and the condition number as distance to degeneracy, equivalent to the mean ratio.
* [Kopriva (2006) — Metric identities and the discontinuous spectral element method on curvilinear meshes](/sources/kopriva-2006.md) — Discrete metric identities for discontinuous spectral elements on curved quadrilaterals and hexahedra: the cross-product form satisfies them in 2-D for isoparametric geometry but not in 3-D, while interpolated conservative and invariant curl forms always do.
* [Liao & Jin (2025) — On geometric conservation for Runge-Kutta methods with dynamically deforming grid in high-order cell-centered finite difference method](/sources/liao-2025.md) — Discrete geometric conservation for explicit and implicit Runge–Kutta methods on deforming grids in high-order cell-centred finite differences, by applying the solver's own time-difference combinations to the face time metrics.
* [Ma, Luo, Li, Qiu, Ni & Chen (2024) — High-order adaptive multi-resolution method on curvilinear grids](/sources/ma-2024.md) — Geometric-conservation-preserving high-order adaptive multi-resolution method on curvilinear grids: metric errors removed from the WENO-ZQ dissipation and metric-consistent prediction and projection operators.
* [Nikl, Kuchařík & Weber (2022) — High-order curvilinear finite element magneto-hydrodynamics I: A conservative Lagrangian scheme](/sources/nikl-2022.md) — Conservative high-order Lagrangian resistive MHD on curvilinear finite elements, with exactly divergence-free magnetic field and conservation of mass, momentum, magnetic flux and energy, on meshes that resist tangling.
* [Nonomura, Iizuka & Fujii (2010) — Freestream and vortex preservation properties of high-order WENO and WCNS on curvilinear grids](/sources/nonomura-2010.md) — Comparison of WENO and weighted compact nonlinear schemes on curvilinear grids: finite-difference WENO in generalized coordinates cannot preserve the free stream, while WCNS with conservative metrics differenced like the fluxes preserves it and resolves vortices better.
* [Peng & Deiterding (2023) — A three-dimensional solver for simulating detonation on curvilinear adaptive meshes](/sources/peng-2023.md) — Block-structured adaptive finite-volume detonation solver on 3-D structured curvilinear meshes, with grid-aligned Riemann solvers, volume-weighted restriction and prolongation and conservative coarse–fine flux correction on mapped meshes.
* [Rochery & Loseille (2023) — Fast high-order mesh correction for metric-based cavity remeshing and a posteriori curving of P2 tetrahedral meshes](/sources/rochery-2023.md) — Correction of curved meshes by linear programs that maximize the smallest Bézier control coefficient of the Jacobian per control point, with Riemannian edge-length curving and a cavity operator for P2 meshes.
* [Steger & Chaussee (1980) — Generation of body-fitted coordinates using hyperbolic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1980.md) — Hyperbolic grid generation: orthogonality plus a prescribed arc-length increment or cell area marched outward from a body by a noniterative implicit scheme, giving fast, smooth exterior grids with spacing control near the body.
* [Steger & Sorenson (1979) — Automatic mesh-point clustering near a boundary in grid generation with elliptic partial differential equations](/sources/steger-1979.md) — Control functions of the elliptic generator that decay away from the inner boundary and are solved for iteratively so that the grid has a prescribed first spacing and intersects the boundary at a prescribed angle.
* [Su, Cai, Pan & Hu (2023) — Reformulated dissipation for the free-stream preserving of the conservative finite difference schemes on curvilinear grids](/sources/su-2023-b.md) — Free-stream preservation for high-order upwind conservative finite differences by subtracting a reference cell-face state from every stencil value, which reformulates the dissipation and frees the metrics from matching the flux discretization.
* [Su, Cai, Qu & Pan (2023) — A sufficient condition for free-stream preserving WENO schemes on curvilinear grids](/sources/su-2023.md) — A sufficient condition for free-stream preserving WENO: when the dissipation is written as sub-stencil reconstructions, it vanishes for uniform flow if the metrics are identical under those reconstructions; free-stream preserving metrics for WENO5 and WENO7 follow.
* [Tang & Tang (2003) — Adaptive mesh methods for one- and two-dimensional hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/tang-2003.md) — A moving mesh algorithm for hyperbolic conservation laws that alternates solution evolution by any finite-volume scheme with iterative mesh redistribution and a conservative interpolation, which conserves mass and is TVD in one dimension.
* [Thomas & Lombard (1979) — Geometric conservation law and its application to flow computations on moving grids](/sources/thomas-1979.md) — Formulates the geometric conservation law for the volume element under time-dependent mappings and solves it with the flow scheme's own difference operators, so the Jacobian is consistent with the scheme and uniform flow is preserved on moving grids.
* [Thompson, Thames & Mastin (1974) — Automatic numerical generation of body-fitted curvilinear coordinate system for field containing any number of arbitrarily shaped two-dimensional bodies](/sources/thompson-1974.md) — Elliptic generation of body-fitted coordinates for multiply connected regions with any number of bodies: coordinates as solutions of Laplace or Poisson equations with Dirichlet data, inverted onto a rectangular computational domain, with control functions for line spacing.
* [Thompson, Warsi & Mastin (1982) — Boundary-fitted coordinate systems for numerical solution of partial differential equations — A review](/sources/thompson-1982.md) — Comprehensive review of boundary-fitted coordinate generation (conformal, orthogonal, algebraic, elliptic, hyperbolic), line control, adaptive and time-dependent systems, grid-induced truncation error and geometrically conservative forms.
* [Toulorge, Geuzaine, Remacle & Lambrechts (2013) — Robust untangling of curvilinear meshes](/sources/toulorge-2013.md) — Untangling of high-order curvilinear meshes by unconstrained optimization with moving log-barriers on the element distortion, applied locally around invalid elements, with a metric-eigenvalue term that controls the explicit stable time step.
* [Visbal & Gaitonde (2002) — On the use of higher-order finite-difference schemes on curvilinear and deforming meshes](/sources/visbal-2002.md) — Sixth-order compact schemes with high-order filters on stretched, curvilinear and deforming meshes: filtering for nonsmooth meshes, conservative metric evaluation for 3-D free-stream preservation, and the time derivative of the Jacobian from the geometric conservation law on moving meshes.
* [Wang, Duan, Ma & Zhang (2024) — An adaptive moving mesh finite difference scheme for tokamak magneto-hydrodynamic simulations](/sources/wang-2024.md) — Adaptive moving poloidal mesh for a finite-difference tokamak MHD code: curvilinear-coordinate MHD on a uniform computational mesh with a variational, monitor-driven mesh equation evolved together with the physics.
* [Wang, Zhang, Li & Li (2025) — MeshAFN: An efficient attention-based Fourier network for self-supervised mesh generation](/sources/wang-2025.md) — Self-supervised structured mesh generation with an attention-based Fourier network and a loss combining Navier–Lamé and Laplace equations.
* [Winslow (1966) — Numerical solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation in a nonuniform triangle mesh](/sources/winslow-1966.md) — Finite-volume (Ritz-equivalent) solution of the quasilinear Poisson equation on a topologically regular triangle mesh by over-relaxation, with an appendix that builds the mesh by solving inverted Laplace equations — the origin of elliptic grid generation.
* [Xie, Sevilla, Hassan & Morgan (2013) — The generation of arbitrary order curved meshes for 3D finite element analysis](/sources/xie-2013.md) — A posteriori curving of straight-sided meshes by a high-order linear elasticity analogy with boundary displacements onto the NURBS geometry as loads, for isotropic and anisotropic 3-D meshes, assessed by the scaled Jacobian.
* [Yu, Jiang & Zhang (2020) — Free-stream preserving finite difference schemes for ideal magnetohydrodynamics on curvilinear meshes](/sources/yu-2020.md) — Free-stream preserving finite-difference WENO for ideal MHD on stationary and moving curvilinear meshes: the alternative (solution-interpolating) WENO formulation for the conservation laws and a metric-consistent Hamilton–Jacobi solver for the magnetic potential of constrained transport.
* [Zhu & Hu (2019) — Free-stream preserving linear-upwind and WENO schemes on curvilinear grids](/sources/zhu-2019.md) — Free-stream preservation for linear-upwind, WENO and hybrid finite-difference schemes on stationary curvilinear grids by splitting the flux into a central part with symmetrical conservative metrics and a dissipative part whose neighbouring-point differences share one set of metrics.
