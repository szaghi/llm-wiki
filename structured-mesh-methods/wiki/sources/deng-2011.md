# Summary

Deng et al. separate the geometric conservation law into volume and surface laws and give the conditions under which high-order finite differences satisfy the surface law exactly ([conservative metric evaluation](/methods/conservative-metric-evaluation.md)).

# Version read

Version of record, J. Comput. Phys. 230, 1100–1115 (2011). §§1–3 and §5 read; §4 tests in outline.

# Key claims

1. Conservative metric forms with the outer operator equal to the flux operator in each direction suffice for the discrete surface conservation law, also at boundary closures (§2). **Derived**
2. The method applies to central schemes, including WCNS, but not to upwind or dissipative schemes (§3, §5). **Derived**
3. Taking the inner operator equal to the outer one avoids extra errors (§2.2). **Shown**
