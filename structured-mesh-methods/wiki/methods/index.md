# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Anisotropic mesh adaptation](/methods/anisotropic-mesh-adaptation.md) — Adapting the size, shape and orientation of elements to a metric tensor built from solution or geometry derivatives (Hessians, recovered gradients, edge-based error estimates), so that elements stretch along features; combined with node relocation and edge swapping it can turn an immersed level-set description into a body-fitted anisotropic mesh.
* [Conservative metric evaluation](/methods/conservative-metric-evaluation.md) — Computing the metric terms of a curvilinear discretization in a conservative (curl) form with the same discrete operators as the flux divergence, so that the discrete metric identities hold: the Thomas–Lombard form, the conditions of the conservative metric method (outer operator equal to the flux operator, inner equal to outer), the symmetrical (coordinate-invariant) form, the curl forms for spectral elements, and why upwind and WENO fluxes break it.
* [Elliptic grid generation](/methods/elliptic-grid-generation.md) — Curvilinear coordinates taken as solutions of Laplace or Poisson equations in the physical domain with Dirichlet data on all boundaries, inverted into a quasilinear elliptic system for the physical coordinates on the computational square (Winslow); extended to multiply connected regions with control functions for line spacing (Thompson–Thames–Mastin) and automatic boundary spacing and orthogonality (Steger–Sorenson).
* [High-order mesh curving](/methods/high-order-mesh-curving.md) — A posteriori generation of curved high-order meshes: a straight-sided mesh is generated first, its boundary entities are curved onto the CAD geometry, and the interior is deformed — by a linear elasticity analogy with the boundary displacements as loads, or by optimization of node positions with log-barriers on the element distortion that untangles invalid elements locally.
* [Hyperbolic grid generation](/methods/hyperbolic-grid-generation.md) — Body-fitted grids marched outward from a boundary by solving a hyperbolic system made of an orthogonality condition plus a prescribed arc-length increment or cell volume; fast (about one relaxation sweep of an elliptic generator) with good spacing control near the body, for problems whose outer boundary is free.
* [Moving mesh partial differential equations](/methods/moving-mesh-pde.md) — Continuous equations for the node motion of a mesh with a fixed number of points, derived from the equidistribution principle and solved together with the physical PDE; versions with a relaxation term that pulls the mesh back toward equidistribution are stable and provably free of mesh crossing, unlike the plain time-differentiated principle.
* [Neural-network mesh generation](/methods/neural-network-mesh-generation.md) — Structured meshes generated by neural networks that represent the map from computational to physical coordinates; unsupervised, physics-informed training puts the residual of an elliptic or hyperbolic generator and the boundary conditions into the loss, so no mesh dataset is needed and trained networks predict meshes of any size quickly.
* [Shock fitting on structured grids](/methods/shock-fitting.md) — Treating shocks as discontinuities with Rankine–Hugoniot jumps instead of capturing them: boundary and floating shock fitting tie the grid topology to the shock topology, while extrapolated shock fitting blanks the cells the shock crosses and exchanges data by Taylor extrapolation, recovering design-order convergence on unmodified structured grids.
* [Target-matrix optimization (TMOP)](/methods/target-matrix-optimization.md) — Mesh optimization by node movement that minimizes an integral of a quality metric of T = AW⁻¹, the Jacobian from target to physical element, with targets defined at every quadrature point of high-order elements; shape, size, alignment and combined metrics, barrier categories, Newton solution with safeguards, space-dependent limiting, simulation-driven targets and ALE triggers, and hr-adaptivity with nonconforming refinement.
* [Transfinite interpolation](/methods/transfinite-interpolation.md) — Algebraic grid generation by Boolean sums of univariate interpolation projectors with blending functions: the mapping of the unit square (cube) matches the boundary curves (and optionally interior curves and derivatives) at a non-denumerable set of points; the bilinear case is the Coons patch, isoparametric mappings are special cases, and validity (univalence) is not guaranteed.
* [Variational grid generation](/methods/variational-grid-generation.md) — Grids obtained by minimizing a weighted combination of integral measures of the mapping — smoothness (whose minimizer is Winslow's generator), orthogonality and weighted cell volume — whose Euler–Lagrange equations are solved by relaxation; a solution-dependent weight concentrates cells and reduces the error on singular problems.
