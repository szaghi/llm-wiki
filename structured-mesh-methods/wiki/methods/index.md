# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Elliptic grid generation](/methods/elliptic-grid-generation.md) — Curvilinear coordinates taken as solutions of Laplace or Poisson equations in the physical domain with Dirichlet data on all boundaries, inverted into a quasilinear elliptic system for the physical coordinates on the computational square (Winslow); extended to multiply connected regions with control functions for line spacing (Thompson–Thames–Mastin) and automatic boundary spacing and orthogonality (Steger–Sorenson).
* [Hyperbolic grid generation](/methods/hyperbolic-grid-generation.md) — Body-fitted grids marched outward from a boundary by solving a hyperbolic system made of an orthogonality condition plus a prescribed arc-length increment or cell volume; fast (about one relaxation sweep of an elliptic generator) with good spacing control near the body, for problems whose outer boundary is free.
* [Moving mesh partial differential equations](/methods/moving-mesh-pde.md) — Continuous equations for the node motion of a mesh with a fixed number of points, derived from the equidistribution principle and solved together with the physical PDE; versions with a relaxation term that pulls the mesh back toward equidistribution are stable and provably free of mesh crossing, unlike the plain time-differentiated principle.
* [Transfinite interpolation](/methods/transfinite-interpolation.md) — Algebraic grid generation by Boolean sums of univariate interpolation projectors with blending functions: the mapping of the unit square (cube) matches the boundary curves (and optionally interior curves and derivatives) at a non-denumerable set of points; the bilinear case is the Coons patch, isoparametric mappings are special cases, and validity (univalence) is not guaranteed.
* [Variational grid generation](/methods/variational-grid-generation.md) — Grids obtained by minimizing a weighted combination of integral measures of the mapping — smoothness (whose minimizer is Winslow's generator), orthogonality and weighted cell volume — whose Euler–Lagrange equations are solved by relaxation; a solution-dependent weight concentrates cells and reduces the error on singular problems.
