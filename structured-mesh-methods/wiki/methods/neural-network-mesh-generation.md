# Neural-network mesh generation

Differential generators ([elliptic](/methods/elliptic-grid-generation.md),
[hyperbolic](/methods/hyperbolic-grid-generation.md)) give smooth,
near-orthogonal grids but need large nonlinear solves, and early neural
approaches trained feedforward networks on datasets of meshes.
[Chen et al. (2022)](/sources/chen-2022.md) instead let a network represent
the map $\mathbf x(\boldsymbol\xi)$ and train it **unsupervised**: the loss is
the residual of the generator's governing equation at sampled points, with
derivatives from automatic differentiation, plus weighted boundary terms
from regression fits of the boundary curves; the penalty weights are
adapted during training against gradient pathologies, and Adam is followed
by L-BFGS-B. Once trained, the network predicts meshes of any number of
cells by a forward evaluation. On several 2-D geometries and both elliptic
and hyperbolic equations the meshes match or beat algebraic
([transfinite interpolation](/methods/transfinite-interpolation.md)) and
differential methods in quality, and the authors propose them as initial
meshes for adaptation and optimization.
