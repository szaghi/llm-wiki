# Properties

Constraints and invariants a method can hold.

* [Accuracy at critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md) — A nonlinear reconstruction keeps its design order at a critical point, where the first m derivatives vanish, only if its weights still converge fast enough to the linear weights there; WENO-JS and WENO-Z lose orders, and mapped, ZN and related weight designs restore them up to a scheme-dependent critical-point order.
* [Bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md) — A scheme is bound-preserving when its discrete solution stays in the convex admissible set G of the PDE; the page states the definition, the convexity requirement, the cell-average route of Zhang and Shu, the flux- and convex-limiting routes, and the geometric-quasilinearization proofs.
* [Divergence-free magnetic field](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) — The magnetic field of MHD obeys div B = 0; discretizations enforce it exactly, locally, through divergence cleaning or not at all, and Wu and Shu show that bound preservation of high-order MHD schemes requires a discrete divergence-free condition, recovered by a Godunov-symmetrization source term.
* [Entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md) — A scheme is entropy stable when its discrete total entropy obeys the entropy inequality of the PDE; the page gives the flux- and scheme-level conditions, entropy-stable DG from EC volume and ED surface fluxes, which limiting steps keep the property, the KPP evidence, and the minimum entropy principle as a convex bound.
* [Free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md) — A discretization on curvilinear grids is free-stream preserving when a uniform state stays an exact discrete steady solution, which requires discrete metric identities; the page records the DGSEM metric treatment, the subcell metric identities of Hennemann et al. and their reformulation as Pazner's conservation condition, and the untested free-stream claim made for interpolation-based TENO.
* [Kinetic energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) — A semi-discretization is kinetic-energy-preserving when its convective terms neither create nor destroy discrete kinetic energy, which Coppola and Veldman reduce to the skew-symmetry of C − ½diag(d_mass); the page gives that criterion, its local form, the finite-volume face-flux condition, the second-order barrier for product fluxes, and the DG two-point fluxes with the property.
* [Scale and affine invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md) — A reconstruction or limiter is scale-invariant when it commutes with multiplication of the data by a constant, and affine-invariant when it also commutes with adding a constant; classical WENO weights have neither property because ε is absolute, and the sources reach it by normalised variables, relative ε, or globally normalised damping.
* [Stationary contact preservation](/properties/stationary-contact-preservation.md) — A scheme preserves stationary contacts when an isolated discontinuity of a linearly degenerate field with zero speed stays exactly on the mesh; Balsara et al. obtain it in high-order FD-WENO through HLLI anti-diffusion, while Lax–Friedrichs-type FD-WENO and central-upwind schemes do not have it.
* [Symmetry preservation](/properties/symmetry-preservation.md) — Symmetry preservation has two meanings in the chunk-1 sources: keeping the mirror symmetry of a symmetric flow (Rayleigh–Taylor, 2D Riemann problems) in the presence of round-off, which low-dissipation schemes lose unless implemented consistently, and preserving the skew-symmetry of the continuous convective operator, which yields discrete conservation of kinetic energy; this page states both and the evidence on each.
* [Well-balancing](/properties/well-balancing.md) — A scheme for a balance law is well-balanced if it preserves a steady state such as the lake at rest exactly; in this bundle it follows from affine-invariant WENO weights without the WENO linearization technique (shallow water) and from stationary-contact-preserving fluctuations (layered and debris-flow models).
