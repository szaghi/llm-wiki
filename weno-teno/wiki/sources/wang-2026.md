# Summary

Wang et al. (2026) address high-order
[adaptive mesh refinement](/methods/adaptive-mesh-refinement.md) for
finite-difference WENO shock capturing. Earlier high-order finite-difference AMR
(Shen, Qiu & Christlieb; Sebastian & Shu in a multidomain setting) is node-based:
coarse and fine points coincide at coarse–fine interfaces, inter-level transfers are
point-wise interpolations, and the scheme is not conservative. The authors use a
cell-centred (staggered) layout, with flux points at cell faces and solution points
at cell centres, so that patch boundaries are flux points and Berger–Colella
refluxing applies to a finite-difference scheme advanced by SSP-RK3 with
subcycling ([local conservation](/results/local-conservation-flux-form.md)).

Two closures are built on this layout. AMR-Berger&Colella uses limited
piecewise-linear prolongation and averaging restriction and is globally
conservative (Appendix B). AMR-WENO uses a fifth-order WENO interpolation for
prolongation (refinement ratios 2 and 4) and a sixth-order central WENO
interpolation on six fine points for restriction; it is not conservative, and
Appendix C shows that non-conservative transfers violate the discrete
[Rankine–Hugoniot](/definitions/rankine-hugoniot-condition.md) balance and make
shocks drift. A Bloch-wave analysis of a periodic coarse–fine–coarse macro-cell
extends the [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
to composite AMR operators: AMR-WENO stays closest to the uniform fifth-order
operator near the grid cutoff, and the AMR closures add
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) at high wavenumbers.

Because non-conservative interpolation breaks down when a shock crosses a level
boundary or is refined or coarsened abruptly, the authors propose AMR-HybridWENO:
WENO interpolation in smooth regions and second-order linear interpolation at cells
flagged by a [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md)
(a Roe-averaged Lax wave-speed criterion combined with a mapped pressure
curvature). Tests: manufactured-solution convergence, an Euler–Burgers coupling
case ([Burgers tests](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md)), slowly moving
Mach-2 and Mach-3 shocks ([steady shock problems](/benchmarks/steady-shock-problems.md)),
[Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), a
[2D Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and decaying
[compressible isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/compressible-isotropic-turbulence.md)
at $M_{t,0}=1.0$, where pure AMR-WENO fails during coarsening and HybridWENO
survives. The code is built on AMReX. The paper does not name
[AMROC](/software/amroc.md); it cites Deiterding's thesis for the refluxing
(ref. [10]) and three AMR papers coauthored by Deiterding (refs. [53]–[55], the
last, Ziegler et al., the origin of the troubled-cell indicator).

# Key claims

Registry notation: solution points $x_i$ and faces $x_{i+1/2}$, velocity $v$
(source $u$), Lax–Friedrichs speed $a$ (source $\alpha$), reduced wavenumber
$\vartheta$ and modified wavenumber $\tilde\vartheta$ (source $\kappa$, $\kappa^*$),
CFL number $\nu$ (source $\sigma$). Page-local: $\Delta x_c$, $\Delta x_f$ coarse and
fine spacings, $r_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the refinement ratio (source $\xi_i$, $r$),
$\theta_{\mathrm B}$ the Bloch phase (source $\theta$), $p_{\mathrm{FD}}$ the
finite-difference order (source $p$), $\theta^{\mathrm p}_i$ the mapped pressure
curvature and $\alpha_{\mathrm{Liu}}$, $\theta_{\mathrm{map}}$ the indicator
thresholds.

1. In the cell-centred layout patch interfaces coincide with flux points, so refluxing enforces local conservation at coarse–fine interfaces; node-based finite-difference AMR has no natural location to reconcile fluxes (§3.1, §3.4). **Stated**
2. With Berger–Colella prolongation and restriction plus refluxing, the composite-grid totals change only through physical boundary fluxes (Eq. (B.7), Appendix B, static two-level 1D hierarchy, point values read as cell averages). **Derived**
3. Non-conservative transfers produce a composite mass jump (Eq. (C.3)) of $O(\Delta x)$ at discontinuities and $O(\Delta x^{p_{\mathrm{FD}}})$ in smooth regions, independent of the time step and accumulating under repeated restrictions; refluxing alone cannot repair it (Appendix C). **Derived**
4. The resulting violation of the discrete Rankine–Hugoniot balance perturbs the shock speed by $O(\Delta x)$, so slowly moving shocks drift in long runs (Appendix C). **Derived**
5. With Berger–Colella transfers the local truncation error at fine points whose stencil crosses the interface falls to $O(\Delta x_f)$ (Eq. (D.13)), and the global $L_1$ truncation error is $O(\Delta x^2)$ whatever $p_{\mathrm{FD}}$ (Eq. (D.21)). **Derived**
6. With WENO prolongation of order $p_{\mathrm{FD}}$ the local truncation error near interfaces drops at most to $O(\Delta x_f^{p_{\mathrm{FD}}-1})$ (Eq. (D.17)), the global $L_1$ error stays $O(\Delta x^{p_{\mathrm{FD}}})$ because the number of interface points is bounded (Eq. (D.25)), and with an order-$q$ Runge–Kutta method the error converges as $\min(p_{\mathrm{FD}},q)$ (Eq. (D.26)). **Derived**
7. Fifth-order WENO prolongation coefficients, linear weights and smoothness-indicator coefficients for refinement ratios 2 and 4 are tabulated, e.g. linear weights $(15/64,21/32,7/64)$ for ratio 2 (Tables 1–2, §3.2.1); the other fine positions follow by reflection about $x_i$. **Derived**
8. Restriction uses a sixth-order central WENO interpolation on six fine points (four 3-point substencils, linear weights $1/32,15/32,15/32,1/32$, Table 3), applied to conserved variables without upwind bias; near patch boundaries two fine ghost cells are filled from coarse data (§3.2.2, Fig. 2, Appendix A). **Derived**
9. The central WENO restriction "maintains still a minimum third-order accuracy even near discontinuities" (Appendix A). **Stated**
10. Fine-ghost-cell time interpolation for SSP-RK3: $u(t^n+\eta\Delta t_c)=u^n+k_1\eta+\big(-\tfrac56k_1+\tfrac16k_2+\tfrac23k_3\big)\eta^2+O(\Delta t_c^3)$ (Eq. (8), after McCorquodale & Colella), with alignment to the RK stages for third-order temporal accuracy (§3.3). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: at $\eta=1$ the formula returns $u^n+\tfrac16k_1+\tfrac16k_2+\tfrac23k_3$, the SSP-RK3 update with $\Delta t$ absorbed in the $k$'s.)*
11. Bloch analysis (macro-cell of length 1, $r_{\mathrm{ref}}=2$, 8 fine and 40 coarse cells, 44 unknowns; block-Toeplitz operator, symbol $\mathbf A(\theta_{\mathrm B})$, extended-zone unfolding, branch chosen by maximal projection on a reference plane wave, Eqs. (26)–(37)): the cell-centred closures deviate less in dispersion from the uniform operator than the node-based closure, most visibly for $\vartheta\in[1.5,2.5]$ (§4.4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown** (linear weights)
12. All AMR closures match the uniform dissipation at low wavenumbers and are more dissipative ($\tilde\vartheta_i$ more negative) from $\vartheta\approx2$, interpreted as damping of interface-generated parasitic modes; AMR-WENO deviates least near the cutoff (§4.4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
13. The global spectra of the cell-centred Berger–Colella and AMR-WENO operators lie in the closed left half-plane for 100, 200 and 1000 coarse cells (Eq. (39), Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. With one global time step set by the fine-level CFL number $\nu=a\Delta t/\Delta x_f$, all AMR eigenvalues lie inside the SSP-RK3 stability region at $\nu=1.43$, "aligned with the uniform scheme" (§4.4.2, Eqs. (40)–(42), Fig. 6). **Shown** (linear weights)
15. Three scenarios for non-conservative interpolation at discontinuities: (I) the discontinuity stays in the finest level and errors are confined to the coarse level; (II) it crosses a coarse–fine interface and conservation errors accumulate, curable by flux correction in the cell-centred framework; (III) it is refined or coarsened abruptly, the WENO restriction picks one-sided stencils, steepens the shock and corrupts coarse values that are then advanced (§5.1, Fig. 9). **Stated**
16. "No single interpolation scheme can simultaneously maintain high-order accuracy in smooth regions and guarantee conservation at discontinuities" (§5.1). **Stated**
17. WENO interpolation is "significantly more robust than the Lagrange interpolation of comparable order" (§5.3); in double Mach reflection both pure WENO and Lagrange interpolation fail at once with negative density (§6.4.2). **Stated** / **Shown**
18. Threshold insensitivity: in the Euler–Burgers case $L_1(\rho)=0.001590$ and $L_\infty(\rho)=0.003035$ for all $\alpha_{\mathrm{Liu}}\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{map}}\in\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$, with the maximum troubled-cell ratio between 5.6338% and 0% (Table 4); in the Mach-3 moving shock the shock-position error 0.001791 and overshoot 0.001609 are unchanged (Table 5). Recommended $\alpha_{\mathrm{Liu}}=0.1$, $\theta_{\mathrm{map}}=10^{-2}$ (§5.3–5.4). **Shown**
19. Manufactured-solution convergence on a two-level grid: $L_1$ orders approach 5 ($\rho$: 4.35, 4.86, 5.40, Table 6(a)); unsteady Euler with dynamic refinement and $\Delta t=0.2\Delta x^2$ gives $\rho$ orders 4.65, 4.83, 4.92 (Table 6(b)). **Shown**
20. Without flux correction the conservation error of the smooth problem converges at fifth order, consistent with the truncation error of the WENO interpolation (Table 7); with flux correction "the coarse-fine flux mismatch dominates and limits the global conservation error convergence to second order" (§6.1). **Shown** (fifth order) / **Stated** (second order, no table)
21. Euler–Burgers coupling with seven coarse–fine interfaces and periodic boundaries: WENO and HybridWENO interpolation keep the shocks in place with flux correction; WENO without flux correction shows significant artifacts (§6.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
22. Mach-3 slowly moving shock: WENO interpolation fails with or without flux correction, HybridWENO keeps a sharp shock to $t=30$ (§6.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
23. Sod with regridding every 5 steps and Lax every 10: AMR-WENO oscillates and overshoots the shock speed, AMR-HybridWENO matches the uniform reference; with regridding every 2 steps both coincide (§6.3.1–6.3.2, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
24. Shu–Osher (base 128 cells, two levels): HybridWENO and WENO interpolation are less dissipative in smooth regions than Berger–Colella transfers (§6.3.3, Fig. 15). **Shown**
25. Decaying isotropic turbulence at $M_{t,0}=1.0$ on a two-level grid with a $64^3$ base: WENO interpolation resolves most but stops at $t\approx3.4\tau$ while shocklet regions are coarsened, Berger–Colella is the most dissipative, HybridWENO is robust and approaches WENO interpolation in dissipation (§6.4.3, Figs. 21–22). **Shown**
26. On a uniform $64^3$ grid at $M_{t,0}=0.6$, $\mathrm{Re}_{\lambda,0}=100$, the single-level scheme agrees with the filtered $256^3$ reference and with Johnsen et al., slightly more dissipative in dilatational kinetic energy (§6.4.3, Fig. 19). **Shown**
