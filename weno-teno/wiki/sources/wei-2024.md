# Summary

The paper proposes a limiter for Runge–Kutta
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) methods. In a troubled
cell, the DG polynomial is not replaced by the output of a low-order limiter but by a
convex combination of the two. A jump indicator, presented as a modification of the
KXRCF [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md), sums the
absolute interface jumps of the DG solution at the boundary quadrature points of a
cell and normalizes them by $N_dh^{\alpha_h}C_\beta$. A cell is troubled when the
indicator exceeds 1, and for the Euler equations only the density is tested. In a
troubled cell the new polynomial is $\omega^{\mathrm h}_iP^N_{\mathrm{DG}}+(1-\omega^{\mathrm h}_i)P^1_{\mathrm{lim}}$.
The weight decreases linearly from 1 at indicator value 1 to 0 at the largest
indicator in the mesh, so the indicator both detects and blends. Conservation is
inherited from the low-order limiter.

Three low-order limiters supply $P^1_{\mathrm{lim}}$: the Cockburn–Shu minmod
TVD/TVB limiter, applied in [characteristic variables](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md);
the Tu–Aliabadi slope limiter; and the Luo–Baum–Löhner
[Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) limiter. [WENO-type limiters](/methods/weno-limiter-dg.md)
are surveyed as alternatives. Time stepping is [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)
and the flux is mostly [HLLC](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md). In two tests the
[Zhang–Shu limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) is added.

Nothing is proved. On [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) and the
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md), the errors equal those of
unlimited DG to the printed digits. On
[Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the hybrid limiter flags far fewer
cells than the TVD limiter (Sod, $N=4$: 1.72 % against 26.54 % on average). On the
[Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), the
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md), the
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) and the
[Sedov blast](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), on rectangles and triangles, it gives
sharper slip lines and better resolution below the Mach stem. The authors claim low
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) and a local data
structure. However, the weight uses a maximum over all cells.

# Key claims

Registry notation. Page-local: $\eta^{\mathrm J}_i$ the jump indicator,
$\omega^{\mathrm h}_i$ the hybrid weight, $\alpha_h$ and $C_\beta$ the normalization
exponent and constant, $\theta_{\mathrm{mm}}$ the minmod parameter, $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}$
the TVB constant, $N$ the DG degree (see the Notation map).

1. $P^N_{\mathrm{hyd}}=\omega^{\mathrm h}_iP^N_{\mathrm{DG}}+(1-\omega^{\mathrm h}_i)P^1_{\mathrm{lim}}$ with $0\le\omega^{\mathrm h}_i\le1$ is a convex combination of two polynomials with the same cell average, so it "does not violate the conservation scheme if the low-order limiter is mass conservation" (§3.2, Eqs. 10–11). **Derived**
2. The jump indicator $\eta^{\mathrm J}_i=\sum_{j=1}^{N_d}\lvert u^{\mathrm{int}}_{h,j}-u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h,j}\rvert/(N_dh^{\alpha_h}C_\beta)$ (Eqs. 7, 9), with $N_d$ the boundary points (2 in 1D, the flux quadrature points in multi-D), both flags troubled cells ($\eta^{\mathrm J}_i>1$) and gives the weight $\omega^{\mathrm h}_i=(\max_l\eta^{\mathrm J}_l-\eta^{\mathrm J}_i)/(\max_l\eta^{\mathrm J}_l-1)$ for $\eta^{\mathrm J}_i>1$, $\omega^{\mathrm h}_i=1$ otherwise (§3.1, Eq. 11). **Stated** (definition)
3. A larger $C_\beta$ flags fewer cells; "setting $C_\beta=1$ is effective enough" (§3.1). **Stated**
4. For the Euler equations only the density is tested; entropy or enthalpy "have obtained qualitatively similar comparison results" (Remark 3.1). **Stated** (not shown)
5. The indicator "offers advantages in the implementation" over the Fu–Shu indicator (§3.1). **Stated** (no comparison)
6. The TVB limiter (12)–(14) uses $\theta_{\mathrm{mm}}=1$, "the mildest limiter possible"; $\theta_{\mathrm{mm}}=1/2$ "is restored to the classic MUSCL limiter" (§3.2.1). **Stated**
7. For systems the low-order limiting is characteristic-wise with Roe-averaged $L$, $R$, "more effective and robust than the component-wise fashion" (§3.2.1). **Stated**
8. Any low-order limiter can supply $P^1_{\mathrm{lim}}$, WENO-type or even the cell average; with $P^0$ "more sophisticated transition functions need to be designed" (Remark 3.2). **Stated**
9. Burgers ($u_0=1/2+\sin x$, $T=0.5$, 40–320 cells, $C_\beta=1/4$): errors with the hybrid limiter equal the unlimited ones to the printed digits for $N=1,\dots,4$, with finest-pair $L^2$ orders 1.96, 2.93, 3.93, 4.93 (Table 1). **Shown**
10. Isentropic vortex ($T=0.2$, $32^2$–$256^2$, $C_\beta=0.5$): limited and unlimited errors coincide, finest-pair $L^2$ orders 1.99, 3.00, 4.00, 4.89 (Table 5). **Shown**
11. Sod (100 cells): average percentage of troubled cells 6.95, 3.21, 2.63, 1.72 (hybrid) against 32.44, 29.66, 26.93, 26.54 (TVD) for $N=1,\dots,4$; maxima 11, 7, 6, 7 against 61, 54, 50, 50 (Table 2). **Shown**
12. Lax: averages 11.99, 8.54, 7.07, 6.74 against 34.39, 31.98, 29.85, 29.31; maxima 16, 11, 10, 9 against 64, 61, 51, 54 (Table 3). **Shown**
13. Shu–Osher (200 cells): averages 9.31, 6.93, 2.58, 2.33 against 31.18, "3118", 30.96, 30.85; maxima 11.00, 11.00, 5.00, 4.50 against 60.00, 60.00, 59.50, 60.00 (Table 4). **Shown**
14. In the Jiang–Shu combined-wave advection test (200 cells, $T=4$) the hybrid limiter resolves the extrema better than the TVD limiter, more so at higher $N$ (Example 4.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
15. Burgers with a shock ($T=1.5$, 80 cells): both limiters are oscillation-free, and the hybrid one has smaller errors, especially near the shock (Example 4.2, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
16. Shu–Osher: the hybrid limiter captures the high-frequency waves better than the TVD limiter, and higher degrees do better (Example 4.5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
17. Woodward–Colella blast waves (400 cells, $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}=50$): the hybrid limiter resolves better than the minmod TVB limiter (Example 4.6, Fig. 8). **Shown**
18. Forward-facing step (rectangles, $h=1/160$, $T=4$): the slip line is better resolved than with the TVB limiter (Example 4.8, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
19. Double Mach reflection (rectangles, $h=1/200$, $T=0.2$): the flow below the Mach stem is better resolved, increasingly so with the degree (Example 4.9, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
20. Mach 5.09 shock diffraction ($N=2$, $h=1/20$, Lax–Friedrichs flux, $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}=50$, with the Zhang–Shu limiter, $T=2.3$): the slip line is better captured than with the TVB limiter (Example 4.10, Figs. 13–15). **Shown**
21. On triangles, hybridizing the Tu–Aliabadi slope limiter (primitive variables, no characteristic decomposition; cheaper but more dissipative than the TVD limiter) "significantly improved the resolution" of the double Mach reflection ($h=1/240$, $N=1,2$) (Example 4.12, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
22. Hybridizing the HWENO limiter of Luo et al. on 87930 triangles of size 0.01 "significantly improves the resolution of the original scheme" on the double Mach reflection (Example 4.12, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
23. "Regardless of which $P^1_{lim}$ limiter is used in combination, it can improve the resolution of the original limiter", and minmod-based results "are comparable to accuracy-preserving WENO limiters in the references" (Remark 3.2). **Stated** (the first part is supported by Examples 4.12–4.13; no WENO-limiter comparison is shown)
24. The choice of $C_\beta$ "is not particularly sensitive for the standard test cases presented in our paper" (§4.2). **Stated** (no sensitivity study)
25. The hybrid limiter "maintains the local data structure and can, therefore, preserve the parallel efficiency of the DG method" (abstract, §5). **Stated** (no timings; the weight (11) uses $\max_l\eta^{\mathrm J}_l$ over all cells, a global reduction at every stage as printed)
26. HLLC "may lead to some instability due to insufficient numerical dissipation, especially in high-dimensional problems", while the Lax–Friedrichs flux "may introduce more dissipation which could smear shocks" (§2). **Stated** (cited)
27. At the corner of the forward-facing step DG does not blow up, unlike finite differences, but produces a spurious entropy layer on the downstream wall unless the nearly-steady corner fix is used (Example 4.8). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* For a smooth solution the DG interface jumps
are $O(h^{N+1})$, so with $\alpha_h=1$ the indicator is $O(h^N)$ in smooth cells and
$O(h^{-1})$ at a discontinuity. The weight of a cell therefore depends on the
strongest jump anywhere in the mesh: the cell with the largest jump is fully limited,
and every other troubled cell keeps part of the unlimited polynomial.

# Printed slips

- Table 4: the TVD average for $N=2$ is printed "3118", without a decimal point, between the entries 31.18 and 30.96 of the same row.
- Eq. (22), Lax problem: the intervals are printed $[-0.5,0)$ and $[0,0.5]$, but the axis of Fig. 6 runs over $-5\le x\le5$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* the right-state sound speed is $\sqrt{1.4\cdot0.571/0.5}=1.264$, so in $T=1.3$ the right-moving shock travels more than $1.64$ and would leave $[0,0.5]$. The Sod problem is posed on $[-5,5]$ (Eq. 19).
- After Eq. (11): $\tau_M=\max_i[u_h|_{\partial\Delta_i}]$ and $\tau_m=N_dh^\alpha C_\beta$ are defined but not used in (11). *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* the stated identity $\tau_M/\tau_m=\max_i\eta_i$ holds only if $h$ and $N_d$ are the same in every cell, which fails on non-uniform triangular meshes ($h$ is the inscribed radius of each cell).
- §3.1 says $C_\beta=1$ is "effective enough", but the tests use $C_\beta=1/4$ (1D), $5$ (rectangles) and $0.5$ (vortex); only the triangle tests use 1.
- Eqs. (12)–(14): detection uses the minmod without $\theta$, the limited slope (14) uses $\theta(\cdot)$; the two agree only for $\theta=1$, the value used.
- Symbol reuse: $K$ is the number of degrees of freedom per cell (§2) and the number of cells (Eqs. 20–21); $L$ is both the spatial operator (Eq. 4) and the left-eigenvector matrix.
- Fig. 23's caption repeats its sentence; Example 4.12 reads "zoom-in results in 18" (Fig. missing) and takes the triangle-mesh DMR conditions from [63], the Shu–Tan inverse Lax–Wendroff chapter; Fig. 1 labels the axis with $\eta_h$ for $\eta_i$.
- No CFL number is printed anywhere.
