# Summary

The paper treats the accuracy near the boundary of the second-order
cell-centred unstructured finite-volume method for the Euler equations, with
[gradients reconstructed](/methods/unstructured-gradient-reconstruction.md) by
inverse-distance weighted least squares (WLSQ) on face-neighbour stencils and
face states extrapolated linearly from the cell gradient. Boundary cells have
too few neighbours, and the boundary-face value that enters the flux (a
zeroth-order extrapolation modified by the boundary condition) is not linearly
exact. Four conventional treatments are reviewed: the vertex-neighbour stencil
(WLSQ-EC), a mirrored ghost cell with the symmetric or curvature-corrected
symmetry technique (WLSQ-GC), and Haselbacher's constrained least squares by
elimination (WLSQ-EA) or weighting (WLSQ-WA).

The contribution, **WLSQ-GCC**, gives both the boundary cell and its mirrored
ghost cell WLSQ gradients from stencils that include each other, extrapolates
an inner and an outer boundary-face state from the two sides, imposes the
boundary condition as relations between these two states (for a slip wall:
equal density, pressure and tangential velocities, opposite normal velocity),
and finds the unknown ghost values by a secant iteration on the residuals.
Cells with several boundary faces get one ghost per face and a coupled system.

On a linear function, the WLSQ-GCC boundary-cell gradient is exact to machine
precision on quadrilateral, regular and perturbed triangular grids, like
WLSQ-EC, while WLSQ-WA and WLSQ-GC have $O(1)$ relative errors. On $x^2e^y$
only GCC and EC keep the design convergence of the boundary gradients. In flow
tests (a supersonic vortex between arcs, the inviscid
[flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md) at
$M=0.38$, a Mach 0.5 bump channel and a 3D ellipsoid at $M=0.5$) only GCC
reaches second order near the boundary, with less spurious entropy and
symmetric contours. The extra cost is 4.4–6.8% of the total CPU time, with at
most two secant iterations per boundary cell observed. No shocked or viscous
case is computed.

# Key claims

Registry notation and the symbols of the
[gradient-reconstruction](/methods/unstructured-gradient-reconstruction.md)
page: $u$ is the reconstructed (primitive) variable (the source's $\psi$),
$w_{0i}=\lvert\Delta r_{0i}\rvert^{-n}$ the least-squares weights (the source's
$\omega_{0i}$), $\theta_i$ the slope-limiter coefficient (the source's
$\phi_i$), $C_0$ the boundary cell, $C_G$ its ghost cell and $B$ the boundary
face.

1. The conventional treatments WLSQ-EA, WLSQ-WA and WLSQ-GC (with the symmetric or the curvature-corrected technique) are not linearly exact in boundary cells; only WLSQ-EC and WLSQ-GCC give machine-zero gradient errors for $u=30x+100y+15$ on quadrilateral, regular triangular and perturbed triangular grids (§6.1, Tables 1–3). **Shown**
2. The WLSQ-GC gradient error for the linear function reaches an $L_\infty$ relative error of 1.483 ("148%") on the regular triangular grid and 0.5 on the quadrilateral grid (Tables 1–2). **Shown**
3. The WLSQ-WA errors grow with the inverse-distance exponent $n$ (WA(2) worse than WA(1)), because the boundary-face value in the stencil is not linearly exact (§6.1). **Shown**
4. For $u=x^2e^y$ on $11\times11$ to $81\times81$ grids, only WLSQ-GCC and WLSQ-EC keep the design order of the boundary gradients on the three grid types, GCC with the smaller error (§6.2, Figs. 8–10). **Shown**
5. WLSQ-GCC obtains the boundary-cell gradients, the ghost-cell values and the boundary-face states together, with the boundary condition satisfied by the reconstructed face states (§5.1, Eqs. (23)–(31)). **Derived** (construction)
6. The boundary condition enters only through the relations between the inner and outer face states; for a slip wall $\rho^R_B=\rho^L_B$, $p^R_B=p^L_B$, $V^R_B=-V^L_B$, $U^R_B=U^L_B$, $W^R_B=W^L_B$ in the local normal, streamline and binormal frame (Eqs. (27)–(28)). **Stated**
7. The residuals are solved by a secant method started from the mirrored ghost value, with at most 10 iterations and tolerance $10^{-10}$ (§5.1, Fig. 5). **Stated**
8. Supersonic vortex on five triangular grids: only WLSQ-GCC is second order near the boundary in the $L_\infty$ relative errors of density and pressure, the others first order; GCC is second order in $L_2$ over the whole field (§7.1, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
9. The conventional methods have an error maximum in the corner cell where an outlet or inlet meets the wall, which has two boundary faces of different types and a reduced stencil (§7.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
10. Cost model: $T_0=T_{in}+T_{bd}+T_{iter}$ with $T_{iter}=2\sum_i(m_i-1)T^*+\sum_i\mathrm{sign}(m_i)\,3T^*$, $T^*$ the cost of one least-squares gradient and $m_i$ the iterations of boundary cell $i$: the first iteration costs $3T^*$, each further one $2T^*$ (§7.1, Eqs. (40)–(41)). **Derived** (but see Printed slips)
11. Supersonic vortex, grid (d): 3840 cells of which 208 boundary cells (5.4%); $\max m_i\le2$; total CPU time (700 steps) of GCC 1.068 times that of WLSQ-EC, single-step $T_0$ up to 1.163 times (Table 4; laptop, 1.8 GHz). **Shown**
12. Cylinder ($M=0.38$, $160\times40$ quadrilaterals, 160 wall cells, 3000 steps): total time of GCC 1.044 times that of WLSQ-EC, $\max m_i\le2$ (Table 5). **Shown**
13. On the cylinder only WLSQ-GCC and the curvature-corrected WLSQ-GC give symmetric downstream Mach contours; the others show zigzag lines, and GC a "non-physical protrusion" on the leeward side (§7.2, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
14. GCC produces less spurious entropy around the cylinder, which the authors read as "lower overall numerical dissipation" (§7.2, Fig. 19). **Shown** (the reading **Stated**)
15. The windward–leeward pressure difference $(p_{A_u}-p_{A_d})/p_\infty$ on the cylinder is at most 0.0041 with GCC, the best symmetry (Eq. (42), Fig. 22). **Shown**
16. WLSQ-WA with $n=4$ crashed on the cylinder through ill-conditioning; constraint weights $\theta_{Bi}\le2$ are used (§4.2.2, §7.2). **Shown**
17. Bump channel ($M=0.5$): orders of the $L_2$ entropy error GCC 2.117 and 2.035, GC 1.810 and 1.801, EC, EA and WA about 1.38–1.44 (§7.3, Table 6). **Shown**
18. Bump: the maximum wall entropy error with GCC is "only 0.0001" (Fig. 27). **Shown**
19. 3D ellipsoid ($M=0.5$, hexahedra): orders of the trailing-edge stagnation-pressure error GCC 1.817 and 2.151, the others 0.77–1.10 (§7.4, Table 7). **Shown**
20. Weak boundary conditions converge better and are more accurate than strong ones; the weak-Riemann approach implies a ghost cell (§1, citing Mengaldo et al.). **Stated**
21. Boundary-face values of second-order finite volumes are usually zeroth-order extrapolations; the paper uses them for every one-sided reconstruction and first-order gradient extrapolation for the conventional ghost-cell methods (§1). **Stated**
22. The elimination approach should ideally use one constraint only and does not apply to corner cells, so EA is left out of §6 (§4.2.1, §6). **Stated**
23. WLSQ-GCC can be combined with conventional slope limiters for shock capturing and extended to viscous boundary conditions by changing the constraint equations (§5.2, §8). **Stated** (not tested)

# Printed slips

- Eqs. (6) and (9): "$\frac{\partial\psi_0}{\partial y}y_{01}$" and "$\frac{\partial\psi_0}{\partial y}y_{0i}$" lack the $\Delta$ ($\Delta y_{01}$, $\Delta y_{0i}$).
- Eq. (7) multiplies both sides of each row by $\omega_{0i}$, which makes the functional $\sum\omega_{0i}^2(\cdot)^2$, while Eq. (9) writes $\sum\omega_{0i}(\cdot)^2$ and Eq. (12) carries $\omega_{0i}$ to the first power.
- Eq. (24), last row: "$\omega_{G3}\Delta z_{GN}$" for $\omega_{GN}\Delta z_{GN}$.
- Fig. 5: the final step, "Calculate the fluxes of the boundary cell $C_0$", is numbered (1) after step (5).
- Eq. (41): for $m_i=0$ the first sum contributes $2(0-1)T^*=-2T^*$, against the text "When $m_i=0$ … $T_{iter}=0$"; the first sum needs $\max(m_i-1,0)$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §7.4 says that "the medium grid size is 1.4 times the fine grid size", but the cell counts give $(120000/61440)^{1/3}=1.25$; the printed medium–fine orders of Table 7 follow from 1.25 (GCC: $\ln(4.5481/2.8145)/\ln1.25=2.151$; with 1.4 it would be 1.426) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Table 5: the normalized $T_0$ of WLSQ-GCC with $m_i=2$ is printed 1.109, but $27.745/25.345=1.095$; the normalized $T_{all}$ of WLSQ-GC is printed 0.997, but $125.42/127.85=0.981$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (34) divides by the vector $\nabla\psi_{exact}$; the tables give per-component errors.
- Eq. (2) uses $U$ for the normal velocity, Eq. (28) for the streamline-tangential component.
- Ref. [11] prints the arXiv identifier "1702.045818v1".
- Typos: "archiving" (§5), "catergorized" (§4), "surprising shock-capturing oscillations" (§2, for suppressing); in the reference list "Tsoutsains" [3], "Jamseson" and "Rung-Kutta" [26], "aaurate" [19], "grirds" [16].
