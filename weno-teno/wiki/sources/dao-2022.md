# Summary

The paper builds an explicit continuous Galerkin method with Lagrange elements
$\mathbb P^N$, $N=1,2,3$, for the ideal MHD equations. It is stabilized by a residual-based
[artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md) (RV). Plain Galerkin is equivalent to
central differencing: it has no [numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md)
and oscillates at shocks. Galerkin-least-squares stabilization is implicit and hard to make
high order in time, so the authors instead add an elliptic regularization whose coefficients
are nodal finite element functions. The paper sits next to the
[WENO-stabilized continuous finite elements](/methods/weno-stabilized-continuous-galerkin.md)
of other sources, though it does not cite them.

There are two contributions. The first is a new viscous regularization: the resistive-MHD
viscous flux plus a mass-diffusion term $\nu_h\nabla\rho$, on Guermond and Popov's argument for
the Euler equations. The second is a normalization of the PDE residual that stays strictly
positive for mixed-sign fields such as the momentum and $B$. The viscosity is the minimum of a
first-order Lax–Friedrichs-like value $C_{\max}h_ia_i$ (the
[Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) in 1D) and a high-order value
$C_Rh_i^2\widetilde R_i$. Here $\widetilde R_i$ is the largest normalized residual over the
components, so the residual also acts as a
[shock indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md). Time stepping is classical RK4.
The [divergence of $B$](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) is controlled by
Brackbill–Barnes projection, a pseudo-time variant or GLM cleaning.

The evidence is numerical only. On an MHD version of the
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) and on a smooth wave, RV errors stay
close to the Galerkin errors: about fourth order for $\mathbb P^3$, but only second order for
$\mathbb P^2$. The other tests are the [Brio–Wu shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md),
the 2D [Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md) up to $t=1$, a comparison of
the cleaning techniques, a 3D Orszag–Tang and the [MHD rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md).
[Bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md) is explicitly out of scope. The paper
points to [convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md) as the route to invariant-domain
preservation.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $N$ is the polynomial degree of the continuous Lagrange space (the
source's $k$), $u=(\rho,\rho v,E,B)$, $c$ the sound speed and $a_i$ the nodal maximum wave
speed (the source's $\lambda_{\max,i}$). The page-local symbols are the nodal mesh function
$h_i$ (already scaled by $1/N$), the viscosity coefficients $\mu_h,\nu_h,\kappa_h$ and
$\eta_{\mathrm{res}}$, the normalization $n(w)$ with constant $C_l$, and the normalized residual
$\widetilde R_i$.

1. The continuous finite element approximation of the flux terms is equivalent to central differencing and oscillates at shocks and contacts (§4, after Eq. 4.2). **Stated**
2. GLS-type stabilizations are implicit by construction, hard to make high order in time, and need time-dependent test functions. Dropping the GLS terms from residual-based shock capturing leaves the residual-based artificial viscosity method (§1). **Stated**
3. The new viscous regularization of ideal MHD is the resistive-MHD viscous flux (viscous stress, heat conduction $\kappa_h\nabla T$, resistivity $\eta_{\mathrm{res}}$) plus a mass-diffusion term $\nu_h\nabla\rho$ (Eq. 4.4), motivated by Guermond–Popov for Euler. With $B\equiv0$ and no mass term it is the Navier–Stokes flux (§4.1). **Derived** (heuristic; positivity, entropy and Galilean invariance are not analysed and are left to future work, §6)
4. The first-order viscosity $\mu^L_i=C_{\max}h_ia_i$ with $C_{\max}=0.5$ gives the first-order Lax–Friedrichs scheme in 1D; a "typical range" is "reportedly" $C_{\max}\in[0.15,0.5]$, citing their ref. [27] (§4.2). **Stated** ("it can [be] shown"; not shown)
5. A positivity-preserving first-order scheme would need a sharper maximum-wave-speed estimate than the largest eigenvalue used here (§4.2). **Stated**
6. The finite element residual is $O(h^{N+1})$ in smooth regions and has jumps of size $O(h^{-1})$ at discontinuities, so it serves as a shock indicator (§4.3). **Stated**
7. The new normalization $n(w)_i=\bar S(w)\big(1-C_l\frac{\max_{\mathcal I(i)}w-\min_{\mathcal I(i)}w}{\max_\Omega w-\min_\Omega w}\big)$, with $\bar S(w)=\lVert w-\mathrm{mean}(w)\rVert_{L^\infty}$ and $C_l<1$, lies in $[(1-C_l)\bar S,\bar S]$ and is strictly positive. The earlier normalization (global factor 1) can vanish for mixed-sign $w$ where the local jump equals the global spread, and then grow "uncontrollably" (§4.3, Eqs. 4.6–4.7). **Derived**
8. In smooth regions $n(w)$ reduces to $\bar S(w)$, the classical normalization of the authors' residual viscosity (§4.3). **Derived**
9. A second-order BDF approximation of $\partial_tq$ in the residual is "sufficient in getting 4th order accurate scheme" (§4.3). **Stated** (consistent with the $\mathbb P^3$ tables)
10. For $\mathbb P^1$ and $\mathbb P^3$ the residual projection can be mass-lumped "without destroying robustness", because $m_i=\int\varphi_i>0$ (§4.3). **Stated**
11. The viscosity is $\mu_i=\min(\mu^L_i,\mu^H_i)$ with $\mu^H_i=C_Rh_i^2\widetilde R_i$, $C_R=1$ (a "typical range" "reportedly" $[0.1,1]$, citing ref. [27]), and $\nu_h=\kappa_h=\eta_{\mathrm{res}}=\mu_h$ (Eq. 4.10, §5). **Stated** (design)
12. All tests use $C_{\max}=0.5$, $C_R=1$, $C_l=0.4$, classical RK4 and CFL 0.3. $C_l$ is "not sensitive": any $C_l\in(0,1)$ gives satisfactory results apart from the smoothness at sharp shocks (§5). **Stated** (no sensitivity study shown)
13. Smooth MHD vortex ($\hat t=0.05$): RV errors stay close to the Galerkin errors at every resolution. $\mathbb P^1$ rates are 2.02–2.06 and $\mathbb P^3$ $L^1$ rates 3.71–4.04, but $\mathbb P^2$ rates fall to 2.00. The paper calls this "suboptimal" and attributes it to a known continuous-Galerkin issue (§5.1.1, Table 1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
14. Smooth wave ($\hat t=0.1$): $\mathbb P^3$ RV $L^1$ rates are 4.19, 4.24 and 4.03 (Galerkin 4.19, 4.24, 4.04), and $\mathbb P^2$ rates approach 2. RV "does not destroy high-order accuracy of the Galerkin smooth solutions" (§5.1.2, Table 2). **Shown**
15. Brio–Wu at equal degrees of freedom (600 and 1200): $\mathbb P^3$ is sharper than $\mathbb P^1$, against an Athena reference on 10000 points (§5.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
16. 2D Orszag–Tang to $t=1.0$ with $\mathbb P^1$ and $\mathbb P^3$ on $450\times450$ nodes, all parameters unchanged: every shock is "captured locally and accurately". $\mathbb P^3$ reproduces a twisting central density upsurge seen with a third-order DG scheme (§5.3, Figs. 3–4). **Shown** (visual only)
17. Without divergence cleaning on a fine enough mesh, Orszag–Tang breaks down near $t\approx0.7$ because of the divergence error (§5.3). **Stated** (Fig. 5 shows only a $40\times40$ $\mathbb P^1$ run)
18. On the $40\times40$ $\mathbb P^1$ Orszag–Tang, projection, pseudo-time stepping (10 iterations) and GLM cleaning all keep $\int\lvert\nabla\cdot B\rvert$ small. Pseudo-time stepping is the most accurate and the most expensive, and projection costs more than GLM because it solves a Poisson problem per step (§5.3, Fig. 5). **Shown** (accuracy) / **Stated** (cost; no timings)
19. Up to $t=0.5$, divergence cleaning makes no noticeable difference to Orszag–Tang, which agrees with Balsara (§5.3). **Stated**
20. $t=3.14$ in Tóth's normalization corresponds approximately to $t=0.5$ here (§5.3). **Stated**
21. Guillet et al. report that, with DG, not all slope limiters and Powell-term discretizations survive beyond $t=0.5$, with blow-ups at $0.75\le t\le0.85$ (§5.3). **Stated** (cited)
22. 3D Orszag–Tang with $\mathbb P^3$ on $120^3$ nodes, projection cleaning, $t=0.5$: the $z=0$ cut reproduces the 2D solution and agrees with Helzel et al. (§5.4, Fig. 6). **Shown** (visual)
23. MHD rotor with $\mathbb P^3$ on $300\times300$ nodes, $t=0.15$: no negative pressure (the minimum printed is $3.86\times10^{-2}$), and the circular Mach contours are captured (§5.5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
24. The method does not provide positivity or invariant-domain preservation; this is left beyond scope (§1, §6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §3.1: "$a:=\gamma p/\rho$ be the speed of sound" lacks a square root *(compiler check, not in the sources: with $B=0$ the printed fast speed gives $c_f^2=a^2$, and $\lambda_{1,8}=v\cdot e\mp c_f$ needs $c_f$ to be a velocity, so $a=\sqrt{\gamma p/\rho}$)*.
- §5.1.1: the vortex prints $p_0=0$ with $\delta p=-\mu^2(1+r^2)e^{1-r^2}/(8\pi^2)<0$, so the printed initial pressure is negative everywhere *(compiler check, not in the sources: about $-1.00$ at $r=0$ for $\mu=5.389489439$)*. This cannot be the state that was run.
- §4.5: the slip condition replaces the boundary momentum by "$\mathbf m^{n+1}_h(\mathbf N_j)-\mathbf m^{n+1}_h(\mathbf N_j)\cdot\mathbf n$", a vector minus a scalar; the normal projection needs $(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n$.
- Table 1: the second blocks of the $\mathbb P^2$ and $\mathbb P^3$ panels are headed "$u$", while the $\mathbb P^1$ panel and the caption say $\mathbf B$; the caption is the paper's own statement of what is tabulated.
- Fig. 3(c) caption: "Density at t = 0.5", but the panel and its printed range belong to $t=1.0$.
- §5.4: the initial state lists $\mathbf B_0+\delta\mathbf B$, but no $\delta\mathbf B$ is defined in 3D; $p$ is reused for the perturbation amplitude 0.2.
- §5.5: the rotor velocity $u_0$ appears in the formula, but its value is never given.
- §4.1: the bulk viscosity $\lambda_h$ appears in the stress, but its value in the tests is not stated.
- Minor: "Alfén" (§3.1); "It can shown" (§4.2). References: [14] prints "C.C. Wu" twice; [23] "Pasqueti"; [30] has a doubled "https://doi.org/" prefix; the URL of [48] is malformed, but its DOI field is complete.
