# Summary

The paper builds an explicit, fifth-order, positivity- and boundedness-preserving
finite-difference method for compressible gas–liquid flows governed by the
[five-equation model](/definitions/five-equation-model.md) of Allaire, Clerc and Kokh,
with each phase closed by the
[stiffened-gas equation of state](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md). The
admissible set $G$ asks for non-negative partial densities, a volume fraction in
$[0,1]$ and $\rho c^2>0$. Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 prove that $G$ is convex when the ideal-gas
phase has the smaller ratio of specific heats. The first-order HLLC scheme
([HLL-family solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md); Einfeldt speeds, Batten contact
speed) is then proved admissible under CFL $\le0.5$ with a Hu–Adams–Shu directional split.

The high-order scheme, WCNS-IS, is a
[weighted compact nonlinear scheme](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md). It
interpolates characteristic-projected primitive variables from nodes to midpoints with
an incremental-stencil WENO interpolation (two 2-point and two 3-point substencils,
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) of Jiang–Shu type, and the
reference indicator $\tau_5$ of [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md)). Weights have the
[WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) form. The midpoint HLLC fluxes are differenced by Deng's
explicit hybrid cell-midpoint/cell-node scheme, written in the
[conservative flux-difference form](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md).
Two limiter stages give [bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md):
an interpolation limiter of [Zhang–Shu type](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) and
a [flux limiter](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md) that blends the
high-order flux with the first-order HLLC flux. Both extend to
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) by convexity.

Tests: fifth-order advection of a volume fraction, interface advection, a gas–liquid
[shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), a multi-material
[Sedov blast](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), [shock–water-column](/benchmarks/shock-water-column.md)
interactions at Mach 2.4 (against experiment) and Mach 10 (where the unlimited scheme fails),
and a Mach 100 water jet modelled on the [astrophysical jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md).
The fifth-order proof excludes [critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md).

# Key claims

Symbols (see the Notation map): $\phi_k$ volume fraction of phase $k$, $\phi_k\rho_k$
partial density, $p_{\infty,k}$ stiffening pressure (registry §10); $\bar\gamma$, $\bar p_\infty$ the
mixture parameters and $s^-$, $s^+$, $s_*$ the HLLC speeds (page-local).

1. If $p_{\infty,2}=0$, $\gamma_1\ge\gamma_2$ and $0\le\phi_1\le1$, then $\rho e-\bar p_\infty$ is concave in the conserved vector $u$; if $\gamma_2>\gamma_1$ it cannot be concave (§2, Lemma 1, Eqs. 13–14). **Proved**
2. The admissible set $G$ (Eq. 12) is convex (Theorem 1, from Lemma 1 and Jensen's inequality). **Proved**
3. The assumption $\gamma_{\mathrm{liquid}}>\gamma_{\mathrm{gas}}$ is "very mild": water has $\gamma$ of 4–7 in the literature (a "rare but justified" 1.932 in [5]), ethanol 2.1, mercury 8.2, against 1.4 (air) and 1.67 (monatomic gas) (§2, p. 6). **Stated**
4. With Einfeldt speeds (Eq. 26) and the Batten contact speed (Eq. 27), every HLLC star state has non-negative partial densities, keeps the volume fraction ($\phi_{1,*}=\phi_1^-$) and has $(\rho e)_*>\bar p_\infty$. The bound needed, $s^-<v_1^--(p^-+\bar p_\infty^-)/\sqrt{2\rho^-(\rho^-e^--\bar p_\infty^-)}$ (Eq. 57), follows from $s^-\le v_1^--c^-$ (§3.3, Eqs. 46–57). **Proved**
5. The first-order HLLC scheme that uses the contact speed as the interface advection velocity of $\phi_1$ (Eqs. 40–43) is positivity- and boundedness-preserving in 2D under CFL $\le0.5$, with the directional split $\sigma_x+\sigma_y=1$ of Eq. 59 (§3.3, Eqs. 58–70). **Proved**
6. With $\psi_{\mathrm H}=256/175$ the explicit hybrid scheme is eighth order for exact midpoint fluxes, with leading error $-\frac1{40320}\partial_x^9f\,\Delta x^8$ (§4.1, Eqs. 77–79). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: (78) with $\psi_{\mathrm H}=256/175$ cancels the $\Delta x^6$ term and gives the printed $1/40320$)*
7. Any central explicit or compact finite-difference scheme can be rewritten in flux-difference form [44]; the implied interface flux of the hybrid scheme is Eq. 80 (§4.1). **Derived** (form cited to [44])
8. The four substencil interpolants with $d_0=15/32$, $d_1=5/32$, $d_2=5/16$, $d_3=1/16$ combine into the fifth-order 5-point interpolant $\frac1{128}(3u_{i-2}-20u_{i-1}+90u_i+60u_{i+1}-5u_{i+2})$ (§4.3, Eqs. 88–90). **Derived**
9. WCNS-IS is fifth order for a scalar law with $f'>0$ away from critical points. The sufficient condition is $\omega_k-d_k=O(\Delta x^4)$, which holds because $\tau_5=(u_i''')^2\Delta x^6+O(\Delta x^8)$ gives $\tau_5/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^4)$ (Appendix B, Eqs. B.7, B.14, B.20–B.21). **Derived** (Taylor expansion; the last step defers to Borges et al. [4] and Wang et al. [48])
10. The IS interpolation is as robust as the IS reconstruction, because both can select a 2-point stencil when discontinuities are close together (§4.3, p. 20, citing [48]). **Stated**
11. Explicit FD WENO/WCNS are "four times cheaper in 2D and nine times cheaper in 3D" than finite-volume WENO, because they avoid multi-dimensional reconstruction (§4.3, citing [36]). **Stated**
12. FD WENO "can only be used with flux-vector splitting methods … when high-order of accuracy is desired": Riemann solvers in FD WENO make it "degenerate to only second order", whereas WCNS keeps high order with a Riemann solver (§4.3, p. 18). **Stated** *(compiler note, not in the sources: the claim does not address the [alternative formulation](/methods/alternative-weno.md), which uses a two-point Riemann flux at fifth order in FD, e.g. [Zheng et al. 2021](/sources/zheng-2021.md))*
13. Primitive-variable interpolation (characteristic fields projected from primitive variables) is needed to avoid spurious oscillations at material interfaces; conservative-variable reconstruction produces them (Appendix A, citing [23], [8], [49]). **Stated**
14. The interpolation limiter (Algorithms 1–3) gives midpoint states with $\phi_k\rho_k\ge\epsilon_{\phi\rho}$, $\epsilon_\phi\le\phi_k\le1-\epsilon_\phi$ and $\rho\tilde c^2\ge\epsilon_{\tilde c}$, by successive convex combinations with the node state $u_i$ (§5.1). **Derived** (constructive, uses the convexity of Theorem 1)
15. The flux limiter (Algorithms 4–6) blends the high-order flux with the first-order HLLC flux, with $\theta=\min(\theta^+,\theta^-)$ per interface, so that the four half-cell states are admissible; the forward-Euler update is their convex combination and is admissible (§5.2, Eqs. 102–107). **Derived**
16. The limiting is discretely conservative for every conservative equation of the model, that is all but the $\phi_1$ equation (§5, §5.2, §7). **Stated** (follows from blending in flux form)
17. Extension to SSP Runge–Kutta is "trivial", since its stages are convex combinations of forward-Euler steps; the CFL limit stays 0.5 (§5.3). **Stated**
18. Tolerances: $10^{-10}$ (partial densities), $10^{-10}$ (volume fraction), $10^{-8}$ ($\rho\tilde c^2$); hard-switch tolerances $10^{-11}$, $10^{-11}$, $10^{-9}$. They must lie below the minima of the initial fields (§5.1, §5.2). **Stated**
19. 2D volume-fraction advection: PP-WCNS-IS $L_2$ errors fall from $3.136\times10^{-5}$ ($8^2$) to $1.714\times10^{-13}$ ($256^2$), with orders 7.69, 4.97, 4.85, 4.96, 4.97; HLLC orders 0.98–1.00 (Table 4). **Shown**
20. 1D interface advection: PP-WCNS-IS gives much thinner interfaces than HLLC and no spurious oscillations; relative velocity and pressure errors are $O(10^{-12})$ and $O(10^{-10})$ (Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
21. Gas/liquid shock tube: PP-WCNS-IS resolves interface and shock with few points where HLLC merges them, with slightly larger under- and overshoots at the expansion fan (Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
22. Multi-material Sedov: both schemes are free of spurious oscillations; PP-WCNS-IS gives much sharper shocks (Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
23. Mach 2.4 shock on a water cylinder, $3072\times2048$: incident, reflected and transmitted shocks, expansion waves and the two counter-rotating vortices match the experiment of Sembian et al. (Fig. 12). **Shown** (qualitative)
24. Mach 10 shock on a water cylinder, $1152\times768$: WCNS-IS without limiters fails (imaginary sound speed) at the incident shock, in low-pressure regions inside the column and in the vortices; PP-WCNS-IS runs, keeps $\phi_1$ within the thresholds and resolves baroclinic roll-up that HLLC smears (§6.6, Figs. 14–16). **Shown**
25. Mach 100 water jet, $1024\times512$: neither scheme fails; HLLC smears the jet-front interface and shows artifacts at the bow shock (§6.7, Figs. 17–19). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- The value of $\epsilon$ in the nonlinear weights (Eq. 93) is never given.
- §6.4: "Figures 9(b) and 10 show the density, velocity, pressure and volume fraction profiles", but density is Fig. 9(a).
- Fig. 12 caption says "shadowgraph images from [38]"; §6.5 says "grey schlieren images from the experiment [38]".
- §4.1 calls the hybrid scheme the "sixth order accurate explicit scheme", while with the adopted $\psi_{\mathrm H}=256/175$ it is eighth order (Eq. 79).
- The letter $G$ is both the admissible set (Eqs. 10, 12) and the flux vector $\mathbf G$.
- Ref. [27] prints its authors twice ("T. Linde, P. Roe, T. Linde, and P. Roe"); ref. [21] has no volume.
- Typos: "conincide", "presure", "buff body" (§6.5), "extraploation" (§6.6), "artificts", "schilren" (§6.7), "Comparision" (Fig. 12), "Taylor-sereis" (App. B), "positively preserving" (§5.2); §3.3 is titled "Proof of positivity- and bounded-preserving preservation".
