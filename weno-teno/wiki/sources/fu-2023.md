# Summary

A review, by the originator of the method, of the
[targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md) shock-capturing family, with
compressible turbulence as the motivating application: the excess
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) of
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) damps broadband turbulence.
Nothing is proved; all numerical evidence is reproduced from earlier papers,
mostly the author's.

The finite-difference framework (§2) uses $K-2$ candidate stencils of
incremental width $r_k=\{3,3,3,4,\dots\}$, whose three smallest are the WENO5
stencils, a strong scale-separation measure
$\gamma_k=\big(C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)^q$ with $C=1$, $q=6$ built on
Jiang–Shu [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md),
normalization $\chi_k$, a sharp cut-off $\delta_k=0$ if $\chi_k<C_T$, and final
weights $\omega_k=d_k\delta_k/\sum_ld_l\delta_l$. A stencil is either used with
its linear weight or discarded. The linear weights are set for maximum order or
by spectral optimization through the
[approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md).
Systems are handled by [characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md)
and global [Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md), with
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stepping.

The variants reviewed (§3) are very-high-order TENO up to the 10-point TENO10,
TENO-N without convex combination, TENO with MP/TVD limiters,
[hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) linear–TENO schemes driven by a TENO
discontinuity indicator, [TENO-THINC](/methods/teno-thinc.md) and BVD-TENO-THINC
([THINC](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md),
[BVD](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md)), finite-volume TENO with
a switched [HLL-family](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) flux that avoids the
[carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md), compact TENO
([WCNS](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md) with TENO weighting),
[symmetry preservation](/properties/symmetry-preservation.md), unstructured
finite-volume TENO, [AFTENO](/methods/afteno.md), Hermite TENO
([Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md)) and WENO3-NN. §4 presents
[adaptive TENO (TENO-A)](/methods/adaptive-teno.md), whose cut-off $C_T$ is set
locally by a low-order discontinuity-strength measure, as an implicit LES
model. §§5–6 survey applications (two-phase flow, detonation, MHD,
wall-bounded and hypersonic turbulence) and open-source codes. The review
positions TENO against [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md),
[WENO-Z+](/methods/weno-z-plus.md), [mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md) and
[CWENO](/methods/cweno.md)-type unequal-stencil schemes.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's $w_k$ is $\omega_k$,
its $\tau_K$ is kept as on the [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) page (subscript =
full-stencil size), its $\varepsilon$ is $\epsilon$, its reduced wavenumber
$\xi$ is $\vartheta$.

1. A linear scheme for the constant-speed linear scalar law is at most first-order accurate or oscillatory (Godunov's theorem), which motivates nonlinear schemes (§1). **Stated**
2. For broadband turbulence the excess dissipation of WENO can underpredict "approximately the upper 3/4 of the resolvable wavenumbers" (§1). **Stated**
3. Incremental-width stencils give odd- and even-order schemes in one framework and keep WENO5-JS robustness at very high order, because the three smallest candidate stencils are always those of WENO5-JS (§2.1, Eq. 5, Fig. 1). **Stated**
4. Starting from 2-point stencils is possible but adds the dissipation of a second-order candidate and needs extra techniques, "which renders the motivation questionable" (§2.1). **Stated**
5. In TENO a candidate stencil is used with its linear weight or discarded ($\delta_k\in\{0,1\}$). This "ENO-like stencil selection" separates TENO from WENO, where all stencils contribute with smoothness-based weights, and from ENO, which picks the smoothest stencil and cannot recover full-stencil optimal accuracy; TENO "tends to rule out the nonsmoothest stencil crossed by genuine discontinuities" (§2.3, Eqs. 8–10). **Stated**
6. The finding of Acker et al. that raising the weights of less-smooth stencils matters more than keeping the order at critical points "is consistent with the commitment of TENO scheme" (§2.3). **Stated**
7. For the first derivative on uniform Cartesian meshes the TENO linear combination always exists by the Kang–Li criterion, and it extends to nonuniform Cartesian meshes because every candidate stencil contains the evaluation point (§2.5). **Stated**
8. TENO keeps the exact dispersion and dissipation of its background linear scheme up to intermediate wavenumbers, and $C_T$ sets the separation wavenumber (Fig. 2: five-point TENO with $C_T=10^{-5},10^{-6},10^{-7}$ against WENO-JS and WENO-Z; Fig. 3: TENO8 and the spectrally optimized TENO8-opt) (§2.5). **Shown** (reproduced figures)
9. The order condition on the global indicator is $\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^s)$ for some $s>0$, "much weaker" than the $\tau_K=O(\Delta x^K)$ typical of WENO-Z; a higher-order $\tau_K$ improves scale separation and robustness (§3.1, Eq. 17, citing Fu, Hu and Adams 2016). **Stated** (contradicted by Ye et al. 2022; see the Contradictions of [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md))
10. With $\tau_K=\lvert\beta_K-\tfrac16(\beta_1+\beta_2+4\beta_0)\rvert=O(\Delta x^6)$ ($\beta_K$ the indicator of the full stencil), TENO reaches its design order for Gaussian-pulse advection, with no degeneration at a second-order critical point or for Burgers, whereas WENO-JS and WENO-Z degenerate at critical points and need $\epsilon$ tuning (§3.1, Eq. 18). **Stated** (numerics in cited work)
11. The simplified measure $\gamma_k=1/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^q$ with $q=7$ is validated "for up to 10-point 8th-order TENO scheme"; with a positivity-preserving limiter it handles near-vacuum problems (astrophysical jet, Fig. 5, $448\times224$ and $800\times400$) (§3.1, Eq. 19). **Shown** (reproduced)
12. Hierarchical voting (Eq. 20) cuts the number of admissible $\delta_k$ distributions "from 15 to 9 for the six-point reconstruction" (§3.2, Fig. 6). **Stated** (see Printed slips)
13. TENO-N, which replaces the convex combination by a predefined optimal linear scheme on the largest stencil not crossed by a discontinuity, avoids negative or non-existent linear weights by construction, easing unstructured-mesh and semi-Lagrangian constructions (§3.2). **Stated**
14. TENO with additional nonlinear limiters (MP or TVD applied to the non-smooth candidates) keeps the order and spectral properties in smooth regions and improves monotonicity for extremely high-Mach flows (§3.3). **Stated**
15. Hybrid TENO, using the linear scheme without characteristic decomposition where the TENO indicator flags smoothness ($C_T=5\times10^{-4}$), improves efficiency "by a factor of four" while keeping shock capturing and low dissipation (§3.4, Eqs. 21–24, Fig. 7: 2D Riemann configuration 12). **Shown** (reproduced)
16. Hybrid schemes perform better with low-dissipation TENO than with WENO as the nonlinear component, regardless of the sensor (§3.4, citing a survey). **Stated**
17. TENO-THINC resolves contacts and shocks "much sharper" than standard WENO and TENO and improves the shear-layer instabilities of the double Mach reflection (§3.5, Fig. 8, $512\times128$). **Shown** (reproduced)
18. Finite-volume Riemann solvers are over-dissipative for smooth flow; switching between a low-dissipation flux (central or HLLC) when both full stencils are smooth and a dissipative one (HLL) otherwise improves wave resolution (Titarev–Toro, 1000 points, Fig. 9) and avoids the carbuncle (§3.6, Eq. 30). **Shown** (reproduced)
19. BVD-TENO5-THINC performs better than BVD-WENOZ-THINC (§3.6). **Stated**
20. Targeted compact nonlinear schemes (TCNS) clearly beat WCNS-Z; TCNS6 is better than seventh- and ninth-order WCNS-Z on the Shu–Osher problem, and TCNS is insensitive to the definition of $\tau_K$ (§3.7). **Stated**
21. Symmetry breaking is not caused by low dissipation: Fleischmann et al. attribute it to floating-point inconsistencies that grow exponentially and restore symmetry to machine precision with TENO5, against the earlier observation that TENO5 breaks Rayleigh–Taylor symmetry on a coarse grid where WENO5-JS keeps it (§3.8). **Stated**
22. Unstructured finite-volume TENO up to sixth order in 3D uses one large central-biased stencil plus small directional stencils: the large stencil alone if it is smooth, otherwise the smooth small stencils with **equal weights** (§3.9, Figs. 10–11, double Mach reflection on 151,850 / 369,794 / 752,708 triangles). **Shown** (reproduced)
23. High-order undivided differences cannot separate high-wavenumber turbulent fluctuations from genuine discontinuities, low-order ones can; this motivates the TENO-A discontinuity measure (§4.1, Eqs. 31–32, Figs. 12–13). **Stated**
24. TENO-A (5 to 8 points) resolves "similar or even more small-scale flow structures than the corresponding WENO schemes of same accuracy order with half resolution in each coordinate direction", recovers Kolmogorov scaling for infinite-$\mathrm{Re}$ incompressible turbulence, beats the dynamic Smagorinsky model and ALDM for the Taylor–Green dissipation rate on $64^3$, and matches filtered DNS for decaying compressible isotropic turbulence on $64^3$ (§4.2, Figs. 14–17). **Shown** (reproduced)
25. The steady-state residual of WENO stalls at truncation-error level because of slight post-shock oscillations; TENO behaves the same way, "an open problem" (§4.2). **Stated**
26. TENO6-A costs only 17% more than WENO5-JS on GPU (47% on CPU) and gives the best agreement with DNS for transitional shock–boundary-layer interaction (§5.4). **Stated**
27. With standard TENO6 the main features of shock–boundary-layer interaction in a transitional duct are captured on a mesh with 60% fewer points; for a shock train $C_T=10^{-5}$ is adopted and results are insensitive to $C_T$ (§5.4). **Stated**
28. For MHD with unstaggered constrained transport the cost of TENO5-A is similar to WENO5-JS/WENO5-Z in 1D, and even TENO8-A adds "negligible" cost over WENO5-JS (§5.3). **Stated**
29. For two-phase flow (finite volume, primitive-variable reconstruction), TENO with $C_T<10^{-3}$ captures cavitation onset better than WENO-Z (§5.1). **Stated**
30. TENO5 beats WENO5-JS and WENO-M for the 2D self-sustained detonation cellular structure on coarse meshes, and WENO5-JS gives incorrect cellular structures (§5.2, Fig. 18). **Shown** (reproduced)

# Printed slips

- §3.4: "a novel hybrid strategy is proposed in [121, 143,178,179]"; [178] (Roe) and [179] (Rusanov) are not hybrid-strategy papers.
- §3.6: "the Roe [178] solver, the Rusanov solver [193]"; [193] is Nishikawa & Kitamura (rotated-hybrid Riemann solvers), Rusanov is [179].
- §1: "Godunov theorem [22,23]"; [22] is Gottlieb & Shu on the Gibbs phenomenon.
- §3.2, Eq. (20): as printed, the first voting rule ranges over $k\in\{4,\dots,K-3\}$, which is empty for $K=6$; the only remaining rule, $\delta_1\delta_2=1\Rightarrow\delta_0=1$, excludes 2 of the $2^4=16$ flag distributions of the four candidate stencils and leaves 14, not the stated 9. The "15" before voting excludes the all-smooth case while Fig. 6 (8 panels plus the all-smooth case "not shown") counts it in the 9. *(compiler check, not in the sources: enumeration of the 16 distributions of $(\delta_0,\dots,\delta_3)$ under the printed Eq. (20).)*
- "K-point TENO is Kth order" (§2, §3.1) against "10-point 8th-order TENO scheme" for TENO10 (§3.1, §4.2, §7); the review does not explain the lower order.
- Fig. 8 caption: double Mach reflection "at the simulation time t = 2.0". The figure is adapted from Takagi et al. (2022), whose text gives $t=0.2$ (§4.6) while its Fig. 10 caption carries the same "t = 2.0"; Ye et al. (2022, §4.8) also run the problem to $t=0.2$.
- Eq. (25) has unbalanced parentheses, "$h_i(x)=f_++f_-(\tanh(\beta(X_i-d_i))$", and the conservation constraint $f_i=\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}h(x)\,dx$ lacks the factor $1/\Delta x$.
- Eq. (12) preamble: "$Df(x)|_j=\frac1{\Delta x}\sum_ld_lu_{j+l}$" mixes $f$ and $u$.
- Ref. [232] "Gas pase detonations" (phase); ref. [6] "heatingin" (missing space).
