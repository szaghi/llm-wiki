# Summary

The paper builds a first-order finite-volume scheme for the 1D isentropic
(barotropic) Euler equations with gravity and friction. The scheme has
[asymptotic preservation](/properties/asymptotic-preservation.md) under a
multiscale relaxation scaling and [well-balancing](/properties/well-balancing.md)
for hydrostatic states. After scaling, the squared Mach number is
$O(\varepsilon^{2\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}})$, and the squared Froude number and the scaled
friction coefficient are $O(\varepsilon^{1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}})$, with
$\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}\in[0,1]$. As $\varepsilon\to0$ the density obeys a porous-medium
equation when $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$ (hyperbolic-to-parabolic relaxation) and a
transport equation $\partial_t\rho+\partial_x(\rho\partial_x\phi)=0$ when
$\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}<1$ (hyperbolic-to-hyperbolic relaxation). The authors say that
standard IMEX Runge–Kutta schemes cannot switch between these limits.

Following Boscarino, Pareschi and Russo, the time step is implicit only in the
momentum, in the mass flux and in the friction term. Eliminating the new momentum
gives an explicit "reformulated" scheme. Its mass update carries both hyperbolic
and second-derivative terms, with coefficients depending on $\varepsilon$ and
$\Delta t$ through $c_1=\varepsilon^{1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}/(\varepsilon^{1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}+\Delta t)$.

The space discretization has three parts. A Rusanov-type
[Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) acts on hydrostatically
reconstructed interface states (the "P-reconstruction", in pressure). The gravity
source is upwinded to the interfaces. The parabolic mass terms are discretized
centrally, with the potential term on the arithmetic-mean interface density, so
that the discrete equilibrium also balances the mass update. A truncation
$[\cdot]_+$ keeps the reconstructed interface densities nonnegative, a weak form
of [bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md). The source term is
upwinded, so the momentum update is not in pure flux form
([local conservation](/results/local-conservation-flux-form.md)). Theorem 4.3
states consistency, well-balancing for a discrete hydrostatic state, and the
asymptotic-preserving property for both limits, with short Taylor and limit
proofs.

The tests are 1D and first order: a barotropic
[Sod tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) under gravity, isothermal and
isentropic hydrostatic equilibria with small perturbations and long runs, and a
mesh-sensitivity test in which a non-AP scheme blows up on the coarse grid.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's velocity $u$, momentum $q$, pressure law
$P(\rho)$, numerical flux $\mathcal F$ and CFL number $\lambda_{\rm CFL}$ become
$v$, $\rho v$, $p(\rho)$, $h$ and $\nu$; the scaling exponent $\beta$ becomes
$\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}$; $\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}_{i+1/2}=\tfrac12(\rho_i+\rho_{i+1})$ (see
the Notation map).

1. In the scaled system (2.5)–(2.6), with $M^2=O(\varepsilon^{2\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}})$ and $\mathrm{Fr}^2=\bar\mu=O(\varepsilon^{1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}})$ (2.4), the pressure gradient is $O(\varepsilon^{-2\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}})$ and gravity and friction are $O(\varepsilon^{-(1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}})})$ (§1, §2). **Derived**
2. For $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$, $\varepsilon\to0$ gives the equilibrium $\rho^{(0)}v^{(0)}=\rho^{(0)}\partial_x\phi-\partial_xp(\rho^{(0)})$ (2.8) and the porous-medium equation $\partial_t\rho^{(0)}+\partial_x(\rho^{(0)}\partial_x\phi)=\partial_{xx}p(\rho^{(0)})$ (2.9) (§2). **Derived** (formal asymptotics)
3. For $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}\in[0,1)$ the limit is the transport equation $\partial_t\rho^{(0)}+\partial_x(\rho^{(0)}\partial_x\phi)=0$ (2.10) (§2). **Derived**
4. The hydrostatic states are $v_e=0$ with $\rho_e=C\exp(\varepsilon^{\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}-1}\phi)$ for $\gamma=1$ (2.13) and $\rho_e=\big(\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\varepsilon^{\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}-1}\phi+C\big)^{1/(\gamma-1)}$ for $\gamma>1$ (2.14) (§2.1). **Derived**
5. "Standard Implicit-Explicit Runge-Kutta schemes are incapable of switching between these asymptotic regimes" (Abstract; §1 cites unified AP IMEX schemes of Boscarino–Pareschi–Russo and Albi–Dimarco–Pareschi). **Stated**
6. The semi-implicit step (3.1)–(3.2), implicit only in the new momentum, can be rewritten as the explicit scheme (3.3)–(3.4) (§3). **Derived**. *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: eliminating $(\rho v)^{n+1}$ from (3.1)–(3.2) reproduces the coefficients of (3.3)–(3.4), e.g. $c_1\Delta t$ for $\partial_x(\rho v)^n$ and $\varepsilon^{1-\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}\Delta t^2/(\varepsilon^{1+\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}+\Delta t)$ for $\partial_{xx}p(\rho^n)$ in the mass update.
7. In the first-derivative part of (3.3)–(3.4), the eigenvalues tend to those of the Euler system as $\Delta t\to0$ at fixed $\varepsilon$ and stay bounded as $\varepsilon\to0$ at fixed $\Delta t$. The parabolic stability restriction "does not degenerate in the stiff limit" (Remark 3.1, citing Boscarino, Pareschi and Russo). **Stated**
8. Theorem 3.2: the reformulated semi-discrete scheme is consistent with (2.5)–(2.6) away from vacuum and asymptotically consistent with both limits. As $\varepsilon\to0$ it gives $\rho^{n+1}_{(0)}=\rho^n_{(0)}-\Delta t\,\partial_x(\rho^n_{(0)}\partial_x\phi)+\Delta t\,\partial_{xx}p(\rho^n_{(0)})$ (3.5) for $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$, and (3.6), the same without the last term, for $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}<1$ (§3). **Proved** (short limit argument)
9. The usual choice $h(u_i,u_{i+1})$ with a pointwise source $S_i=S(u_i)$ "produces large errors near non-constant steady states", so it is not well-balanced (§4). **Stated**
10. The E-reconstruction (4.13), in the specific enthalpy $\mathsf h(\rho)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}$, and the P-reconstruction (4.14), in the pressure, give the interface states $\rho^\pm_{i+1/2}$ with $\phi_{i+1/2}=\min(\phi_i,\phi_{i+1})$. The truncation $[\cdot]_+=\max(0,\cdot)$ keeps the reconstructed density nonnegative. $p$ is invertible also for $\gamma=1$, $\mathsf h$ only for $\gamma>1$ (§4.1). **Derived**
11. Remark 4.2: a balance built on the E-reconstruction "can lead to the loss of the AP property for an isentropic Euler system", because it gives a wrong diffusion coefficient in the parabolic limit (citing Natalini, Ribot and Twarogowska). Only the P-reconstruction is used afterwards (§4.2). **Stated**
12. The discrete equilibrium (4.16), $\varepsilon^{1-\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}p(\rho_{i+1})-\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}_{i+1/2}\phi_{i+1}=\varepsilon^{1-\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}}p(\rho_i)-\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}_{i+1/2}\phi_i$, is balanced in the mass update by the central parabolic flux plus the averaged potential flux $\big[\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}_{i+1/2}(\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)-\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}_{i-1/2}(\phi_i-\phi_{i-1})\big]/\Delta x^2$ (§4.2). **Derived**
13. Theorem 4.3(i): the fully discrete scheme (4.17)–(4.18) is consistent with (2.5)–(2.6) away from vacuum. The proof uses $u^{n,-}_{i+1/2}=u^n_i+O(\Delta x)$, $u^{n,+}_{i+1/2}=u^n_{i+1}+O(\Delta x)$ (4.20) and source consistency in the sense of Perthame and Simeoni (4.22)–(4.25). **Proved** (sketch)
14. Theorem 4.3(ii): the scheme is well-balanced for the discrete hydrostatic state (4.26). There $u^{n,-}_{i+1/2}=u^{n,+}_{i+1/2}$, so the flux differences and the upwinded sources cancel (4.27), and the central parabolic and potential terms cancel by (4.16) (§4). **Proved** (sketch)
15. Theorem 4.3(iii): the scheme is asymptotic preserving independently of $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}$. As $\varepsilon\to0$, $c_1\to0$ and the mass update becomes the explicit discretization (4.29) of the porous-medium equation ($\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$) or (4.30) of the transport equation ($\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}<1$) (§4). **Proved** (limit argument)
16. The time step is $\Delta t=\nu\min\big(\Delta x^2/\varepsilon^{1-\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}},\ \Delta x/\lvert\partial_x\phi\rvert\big)$ (5.1), and $h$ is "a Rusanov-type approximate Riemann solver" (§5). **Stated**
17. The CFL restriction "becomes less and less severe as $\varepsilon\to0$" for $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}\in[0,1)$, and the scheme is "stable under a parabolic CFL condition" (§1, §6). No stability proof is given; the claim rests on Boscarino, Pareschi and Russo. **Stated**
18. Sod tube under gravity $\phi=x$, $\varepsilon=1$: the coarse solution ($N_x=100$) "is in good agreement" with the fine one ($N_x=1000$) at $\nu=0.45$, with a right-moving shock and an expansion (§5.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
19. For $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $N_x=100$, $\nu=0.45$: with $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$ the density is "in perfect agreement" with a standard first-order scheme for the porous-medium equation (2.9); with $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=0.1$ it is in "good agreement" with a first-order upwind scheme for (2.10) (§5.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
20. Isothermal hydrostatic equilibrium ($\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$, $\rho_e=\exp\phi$, $T=2$): the $L^1$ density errors for $\phi=x$ are $2.6815\times10^{-6}$ ($N_x=100$) and $2.7018\times10^{-8}$ ($N_x=1000$) at $\varepsilon=1$, and $1.1705\times10^{-6}$ and $1.1390\times10^{-8}$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. For $\phi=\sin(2\pi x)$ they are about $2.2\times10^{-4}$ and $2.1\times10^{-6}$ for all $\varepsilon$ (Table 1). **Shown**. *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: the errors fall by a factor of about 100 per tenfold refinement, so they are $O(\Delta x^2)$ truncation errors, not round-off.
21. Isentropic hydrostatic equilibrium ($\gamma=1.4$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ms}}=1$, $T=2$): for $\phi=x$ the density errors are $3.0399\times10^{-7}$ and $3.0717\times10^{-9}$ at $\varepsilon=1$, and $1.8445\times10^{-8}$ and $1.6062\times10^{-10}$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$. For $\sin(2\pi x)$ they are about $3.6\times10^{-5}$ and $3.6\times10^{-7}$ (Table 2). **Shown**
22. Perturbations $\zeta_{\mathrm p}=10^{-3},10^{-5}$ of the isothermal state with $\phi=x$, $T=0.25$: at $\varepsilon=1$ the well-balanced scheme resolves the perturbation and the non-well-balanced one (the same AP time step without equilibrium reconstruction) does not. At $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ the non-well-balanced scheme gives similar results, "explained by the fact that" the porous-medium equation and the Euler system share the stationary state and that scheme is AP (§5.2.1, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
23. Long isothermal run ($T=100$, $\zeta_{\mathrm p}=10^{-3}$): for $\varepsilon=1$ the well-balanced momentum converges and the non-well-balanced one drifts from the steady state (Fig. 5). For $\varepsilon=0.1$ and $0.01$ the non-well-balanced scheme oscillates, which the authors attribute to the loss of accuracy of IMEX schemes for intermediate $\varepsilon$ (Figs. 6–7). At $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ the two schemes are comparable (Fig. 8). **Shown**
24. Mesh sensitivity on a step datum from Chalons et al., $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, periodic: the AP scheme gives "almost identical results" for $\Delta x=0.01$, $0.001$ and $0.0001$ in the parabolic regime. A non-AP scheme blows up on $\Delta x=0.01$ (Fig. 11b, plotted at $T=0.000084$) and agrees on the finer meshes. In the hyperbolic regime it does not blow up but oscillates at the discontinuities on the coarse mesh (§5.3, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
25. The scheme is better than its non-well-balanced and non-AP counterparts in both the stiff and the non-stiff regimes (§5, §6). **Shown** (qualitatively, by figure comparison only)

# Printed slips

- Gravity sign: (2.2) prints the source as $-\frac{1}{\bar\mu}\rho u-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^2}\rho\partial_x\phi$, while (2.6) prints $-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1+\beta}}(\rho u-\rho\partial_x\phi)$, that is $+\rho\partial_x\phi$. Everything after (2.6), including (2.13)–(2.14), (5.3) and (5.5), uses the (2.6) sign.
- (2.14) has unbalanced parentheses: "$\rho_e(x)=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\phi(x)+C)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}$".
- (4.6) defines $S^\mp$ with denominator $\Delta x$ only; (4.18) and (4.23) carry $1/(\varepsilon^{2\beta}\Delta x)$.
- (4.7) and (4.18) write the friction term as $c_1(0,-q_i^n)^T$ and "$-c_1q_i^n$", without the factor $1/\varepsilon^{1+\beta}$ of (4.3).
- (4.17)–(4.18) are in incremental form ($\rho^{n+1}_i=\rho^n_i-\dots$) but keep the coefficients $c_1,c_2$ of the per-$\Delta t$ form (4.5). *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: by (3.3) the coefficient of $\partial_xq$ is $c_1\Delta t$ and that of $\partial_{xx}(q^2/\rho)$ is $c_2\Delta t=c_1\Delta t^2$, so a factor $\Delta t$ is missing on the right-hand side. (4.28) uses $c_1$ for the flux and $c_1\Delta t$ for $\partial_{xx}(q^2/\rho)$, still one factor $\Delta t$ short, but its last two terms carry the coefficients of (3.3). The limits (4.29)–(4.30) are unaffected.
- (4.22)–(4.23) group the sources as $S^{n,-}_{i+1/2}+S^{n,+}_{i+1/2}$, while (4.6)–(4.7) use $S^-_{i+1/2}+S^+_{i-1/2}$; the regrouping is not explained.
- (4.24), second line: "$-\varepsilon^{1+\beta}P(\rho^n_{i+1})$" should be $-\varepsilon^{1-\beta}P(\rho^n_{i+1})$, as in the first line and (4.14).
- §5.2.2 says the well-balanced scheme resolves the solution "much better than the non well-balanced scheme for both high and low values of $\varepsilon$", and the next sentence says the non-well-balanced scheme "improves" in the stiff regime (Fig. 10).
- The Fig. 9 caption is garbled, and the Fig. 12 caption repeats "for different mesh sizes".
- §5.3 calls the step datum (5.6) "a centered arch function".
- Table 2: the $\varepsilon=0.1$ panel header prints "N φ" in swapped order.
- §5.2.1 gives no final time for the perturbation test; $T=0.25$ appears only in the figure titles. $\gamma$ is not printed for the Sod test, nor $\phi$ and, in the hyperbolic regime, $\beta$ for the mesh-sensitivity test.
