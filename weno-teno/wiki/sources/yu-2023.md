# Summary

The paper treats the 1D Euler equations of an ideal gas with a singular
source at $x=0$, $\partial_tu+\partial_xf(u)=\delta(x)S$. The source
generalizes the condensation source of Cheng et al.:
$S=\mathrm{diag}(k_1,k_2,k_3)f(u^-)$ when both one-sided velocities are
positive, $\mathrm{diag}(k_1,k_2,k_3)f(u^+)$ when both are negative, and zero
otherwise. The source creates a stationary wave on the $t$-axis with the jump
relation $f(u^-)+S=f(u^+)$, a zero-speed
[Rankine–Hugoniot](/definitions/rankine-hugoniot-condition.md) relation
modified by the source. The analysis rests on the authors' companion
preprint on the Riemann problem: a monotonicity criterion selecting admissible
equilibria, admissible Mach sets, stationary-wave curves, and seven solution
structures, two non-choked and five choked (a sonic state next to the
stationary wave).

On this basis the paper makes three contributions. It proves that
[operator splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md) (convection step, then
exact source step) is not [well-balanced](/properties/well-balancing.md) for
any $\Delta t>0$, even with exact sub-solvers and an upwind source step
(Theorem 3.1). It writes every unsplit scheme in conservation form with two
one-sided fluxes at the source interface, whose difference approximates the
time-integrated source, and proves that a source-aware consistency condition
on this pair suffices for well-balancing (Theorem 3.2). And it shows that the
Kröner–Thanh (K-T) nozzle flux is well-balanced but becomes unavailable when
the one-sided Mach numbers leave their admissible sets. The proposed remedy is
a solution-structure based approximate Riemann solver. It predicts the
structure from $(u_L,u_R,k)$ (Algorithm 1), computes the states at
$x/t=0^\mp$ (Table 2, a bisection for Type 1), reproduces a single stationary
wave exactly (Lemma 4.1) and gives a well-balanced scheme (Theorem 4.3).

The tests use third-order RKDG ([discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md))
with a characteristic-wise TVD limiter, local
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) fluxes away from the source
and third-order [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). There is one
stationary wave and seven Riemann problems of Types 1–7. The new scheme
matches the exact solutions; splitting leaves errors that do not shrink under
refinement; K-T needs ad hoc corrections in choked cases and oscillates or
converges to wrong solutions there. No convergence orders or timings are
reported.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the conserved state $U$ is $u$, the flux $F$ is $f$, the
velocity $u$ is $v$, the mesh size $h$ is $\Delta x$, the cell index $j$ is
$i$, the one-sided states $U_\mp=U(0^\mp,t)$ are $u^\mp$, the one-sided
interface fluxes $\hat F_{j+1/2,\mp}$ are $\hat f_{i+1/2,\mp}$, with the source
interface at $x_{i_0+1/2}=0$.

1. The splitting method $u^{n+1}=\mathcal S^{(\Delta t)}\mathcal C^{(\Delta t)}(u^n)$ (Eq. (3.6)) is not well-balanced for any $\Delta t>0$, even when the convection and source sub-problems are solved exactly (§3.1, Theorem 3.1). **Proved**
2. Consequently no consistent and stable PDE scheme can make the splitting method well-balanced (§3.1). **Derived**
3. The upwind source step (3.9) leaves every stationary initial datum (3.4) unchanged, so the failure of splitting comes from the convection step (§3.1). **Stated** ("It can be shown")
4. Placing the source at a cell interface, $x_{i_0+1/2}=0$ (Eq. (3.3)), avoids the large numerical dissipation incurred when the origin lies inside a cell (§3). **Stated**
5. Every unsplit discretization can be written in the conservation form (3.18) with two one-sided fluxes at the source interface, $\hat f^n_{i_0+1/2,+}-\hat f^n_{i_0+1/2,-}\approx\int_{t^n}^{t^{n+1}}S\,dt$ (Eqs. (3.10)–(3.21)). **Derived**
6. With classical consistency $\hat f_\mp(u,u)=f(u)$ (3.22) at regular interfaces and $\hat f_-(u^-,u^+)=f(u^-)$, $\hat f_+(u^-,u^+)=f(u^+)$ for every admissible stationary pair (3.23) at the source interface, the scheme (3.18) is well-balanced in the sense of Definition 3.1 (Theorem 3.2). **Proved**
7. The K-T flux (4.1), transposed by the authors from the nozzle and shallow-water setting of Kröner & Thanh, satisfies (3.23), so the K-T scheme is well-balanced for any $\Delta x$ under the CFL condition (Theorem 4.1). **Proved** (via Theorem 3.2)
8. The K-T fluxes are unavailable whenever a one-sided Mach number leaves its admissible set, $M^{h,n}_-\notin\Gamma^-$ or $M^{h,n}_+\notin\Gamma^+$ (Theorem 4.2). **Stated** (no proof printed)
9. K-T selects the stationary-wave branch from one side only, so the two fluxes at the source can use different branches; with a discontinuous stationary-wave curve this makes the flux error uncontrollable (§4.1). **Stated**
10. The approximate solver ignores Types 4 and 6 (limits of Types 3/2 and 5/1, Remarks 2.1–2.2) and predicts one of Types 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 (§4.2.1, Algorithm 1). **Stated** (design choice)
11. If $M_L\in\Gamma^-$ and $u_R$ lies on the stationary-wave curve of $u_L$, the solver returns $\tilde u^-=u_L$, $\tilde u^+=u_R$ (Lemma 4.1). **Proved**
12. The fluxes $\hat f_{i_0+1/2,-}=f(\tilde u^-)$, $\hat f_{i_0+1/2,+}=f(\tilde u^+)$ (4.7), with consistent fluxes elsewhere, give a well-balanced scheme (Theorem 4.3). **Proved** (Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 3.2)
13. In the new scheme both source-interface fluxes use the same stationary-wave branch, the one of the predicted structure (§4.2.2). **Stated** (by construction)
14. Because the stationary wave does not move and its influence travels by convection, the standard bound $\sup\lvert\lambda_l\rvert\Delta t/\Delta x\le1/2$ suffices (§5). **Stated**
15. Test 1 (single stationary wave): splitting produces spurious perturbations along all three characteristic families and overshoots near the origin, which do not decrease from $\Delta x=0.05$ to $0.00625$ (Fig. 2); K-T and the new scheme preserve the equilibrium (Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
16. Tests 2 (Type 1) and 3 (Type 2), non-choked: all three schemes agree with the exact solution; splitting overshoots near the stationary wave and K-T oscillates slightly at the downstream contact in Test 2; the new scheme has neither defect (Fig. 5). **Shown**
17. Test 4 (Type 3, choked, $k>0$): the K-T fluxes violate the conditions of Theorem 4.2, K-T runs only with ad hoc corrections and still oscillates, and the new scheme resolves each wave better with less dissipation (Fig. 6, left). **Shown**
18. Test 5 (Type 4, choked, sonic right state): K-T oscillates strongly, which the authors suspect comes from branch switching as the state right of the origin oscillates around sonic; the new scheme agrees well although its solver omits Type 4 (Fig. 6, right). **Shown** (the cause is **Stated** as a suspicion)
19. Tests 6 and 7 (Types 5 and 6, choked, $k<0$): K-T gives incorrect results; splitting is more dissipative and oscillates in the constant region right of the origin in Test 7; the new scheme agrees well (Fig. 7). **Shown**
20. Test 8 (Type 7, $k=0$, rarefactions touching the origin on both sides): all three schemes approximate the solution and the new scheme resolves the two rarefactions best (Fig. 8). **Shown**
21. The Godunov scheme with the exact Riemann solution is well-balanced for conservation laws with singular sources, but the complete Riemann solution with a source is generally unavailable (§1). **Stated** (cited)
22. The solver extends "directly", through reconstruction, to moving singular sources and to several dimensions (§6). **Stated** (not shown)

# Printed slips

- Property 2.1 against Table 1: (i) calls the stationary-wave curve a singleton iff "$k>0$ & $M_1^*<M_-<M_2^*$ or $k<0$ & $M_-\ge M_3^{**}$", and (iii) empty otherwise. But Table 1 gives the admissible left sets $(0,M_1^*]\cup[M_2^*,\infty)$ for $k>0$ and $(0,1]\cup[1,M_3^{**})$ for $k<0$. The printed singleton ranges are exactly the complements of these sets, so (i) and (iii) contradict Table 1 (pp. 6–7).
- p. 9: the combined 3-wave curve is printed as the shock for $p\le p_0$ and the rarefaction for $p>p_0$, while Eqs. (2.8)–(2.9) define the 3-shock for $p\ge p_0$ and the 3-rarefaction for $p\le p_0$.
- After Eq. (2.9): the rarefaction relations are written with the shock functions $f^{1-S}$, $f^{3-S}$ instead of $f^{1-R}$, $f^{3-R}$.
- Fig. 1 caption: "Left: associate Riemann problem (2.1). Right: Riemann problem (2.11)" — (2.11) is the associate problem and (2.1) the one with the source; the text calls the figure "Figure2.3".
- p. 10: "$\mathcal S(U_L,U_1)\oplus\mathcal C(U_1,U_2)$ means that $U_L$ and $U_1$ are connected by a shock and a shock wave".
- Definition 2.1 uses an undefined $m$ and reuses $k$ as an index while $k$ is the source parameter (2.3).
- Eq. (3.10) evaluates the source at $x^-_{j_0-1/2}$ for $x^-_{j_0+1/2}$ (repeated after (3.13)); Eq. (3.11), second line, has $x^+_{j-1/2}$ for $x^+_{j+1/2}$.
- Eq. (3.11) defines $\hat F$ as an approximation of $\int_{t^n}^{t^{n+1}}F\,dt$, yet (3.13), (3.16) and (3.18) multiply $\hat F$ by $\Delta t/h$; (3.10) likewise multiplies the time-integrated source by $\Delta t$. The normalization is inconsistent; the well-balancing proofs use only flux equalities.
- Proof 3.2 calls (3.22)–(3.23) "the monotonicity condition"; they are the consistency conditions. Definition 3.1 is an incomplete sentence.
- §4.2.2 after Theorem 4.3 names $\hat F^n_{j_0+1/2,-}$ twice, the second for $\hat F^n_{j_0+1/2,+}$.
- §5: "As shown in Figure5, the splitting scheme is not well-balanced" refers to Fig. 2 (Fig. 5 shows Tests 2–3).
- Algorithm 1: if the condition of line 1 holds but neither inner test (lines 2, 4) passes, no branch returns, while the text says "Otherwise, we check whether $U_L$ and $U_R$ satisfy the condition of Type1".
- Eq. (2.7), case $k=0$: the ranges are printed in $M_-$ although the backward curve is parametrized by $M_+$.
- Table 2 is cited as "Table4.2.1"; typos "souce", "basesd", "preperty", "sturctures", "sover-based", "stantionary", "empolyed"; "Abstract" printed twice; AMS code "35L867".
