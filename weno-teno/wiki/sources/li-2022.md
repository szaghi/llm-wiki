# Summary

The paper starts from the fourth-order
[MDCD scheme](/methods/mdcd-scheme.md) of Sun et al. (2011). It is a
[conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md) flux on
the six-point stencil $\{i-2,\dots,i+3\}$ with two free parameters. $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$
controls the dispersion and $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$ the dissipation, and the modified
wavenumber separates exactly into a real part fixed by $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ and an
imaginary part $c_{\mathrm{diss}}g(\cos\vartheta)$ (the
[approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md) and
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) pages). The contributions
are four. (i) A [scale sensor](/definitions/scale-sensor.md), the effective scaled
wavenumber $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$, built from ratios of Taylor-term magnitudes and exact
for a sine wave. (ii) The MDAD scheme, in which $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$ becomes a
piecewise square-root function of $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ at each interface, between 0.001
and 0.012, designed from the Hu–Adams dispersion–dissipation ratio $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}=9$.
(iii) MDAD-WENO, which writes the MDAD flux as a combination of four three-point candidate
fluxes, with [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md)-type nonlinear weights and
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md). (iv) MDAD-HY, a
[hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) of MDAD and MDAD-WENO switched by a new shock detector.
The detector is Ren et al.'s (2003) detector with second differences added to the left and
right variation measures.

For systems, smooth interfaces use component-wise global
[Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md). Flagged interfaces use
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) and a second
detection on the split characteristic fluxes. The tests are a multi-wave advection, the
[Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), the
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md), a viscous
[shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) on a curvilinear mesh,
the [shock–mixing-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-mixing-layer-interaction.md) and the
inviscid and $\mathrm{Re}=1600$ [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md).
The comparators are [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md), MDCD-HY and Fu's TENO-HY
([TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md)). MDAD-HY gives the lowest errors at matched cost; the
measure is $1/(\text{error}\times\text{CPU time})$. The curvilinear remark covers only
Fourier validity: [free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md) is not
addressed. [Mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md) appears only in the introduction.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the grid index $i$ (the source's $j$), the reduced wavenumber
$\vartheta$ (the source's $k$) and the modified wavenumber
$\tilde\vartheta=\tilde\vartheta_r+\mathrm i\tilde\vartheta_i$, dissipative for
$\tilde\vartheta_i\le0$. The page-local symbols are $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ and
$c_{\mathrm{diss}}$ (MDCD parameters), $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}$ (dispersion–dissipation ratio),
$\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ (scale sensor), $\mathsf D_p=f^{(p)}\Delta x^p$ (Taylor-term
magnitude), $\beta_6$ (six-point indicator), $\mathfrak s_i$ and $\mathfrak s_c$ (detector measure and
threshold), $\mathsf a$, $\mathsf b$ and $\xi$ (detector variations and constant) and $\delta^{\mathrm H}_{i+1/2}\in\{0,1\}$ (hybrid switch), as on the [MDCD](/methods/mdcd-scheme.md), [hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) and [scale-sensor](/definitions/scale-sensor.md) pages. See the Notation map.

1. The fourth-order MDCD scheme (Eq. 6; flux Eq. 7) has $\tilde\vartheta_r=c_{\mathrm{disp}}\sin3\vartheta-(4c_{\mathrm{disp}}+\tfrac16)\sin2\vartheta+(5c_{\mathrm{disp}}+\tfrac43)\sin\vartheta$ and $\tilde\vartheta_i=c_{\mathrm{diss}}(\cos3\vartheta-6\cos2\vartheta+15\cos\vartheta-10)$, so dispersion and dissipation are decoupled (§2.1, Eq. 11). **Derived**
2. $\tilde\vartheta_i=c_{\mathrm{diss}}g(\cos\vartheta)$ with $g(x)=4(x-1)^3\le0$ on $[-1,1]$. For positive advection speed, $c_{\mathrm{diss}}\ge0$ gives stability and dissipation that grows with $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$, and $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0$ is the dissipation-free central scheme (Eqs. 14–15). **Proved** (frozen-coefficient linear scheme)
3. The optimized $c_{\mathrm{disp}}=0.0463783$ (Sun et al. 2011, weight $e^{-8\vartheta}$ in the integrated error, Eq. 12) gives the well-resolved wavenumber $\vartheta^*_c=1.297$ (criterion $\lvert\tilde\vartheta_r-\vartheta\rvert<0.005$). For C6 and UW5 it is 0.976, a "32.9 % improvement" (§2.1, Figs. 1–2). **Shown**
4. UW5 and C6 have exactly the same dispersion (§2.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
5. Imposing $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}(\pi)\le9$ gives the minimum fixed $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.012$. MDCD(0.035) has dissipation similar to UW5, and MDCD(0.012) is recommended (§2.1, Eqs. 16–17, Fig. 3). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\lvert\partial_\vartheta\tilde\vartheta_r-1\rvert_{\vartheta=\pi}=16c_{\mathrm{disp}}+8/3$ and $\lvert g(-1)\rvert=32$, so $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}=9$ needs $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.01184$; 0.012 gives $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}(\pi)=8.88$)*
6. For a pure sine wave, $\delta=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta x}\sqrt{\lvert f'/f'''\rvert}=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta x}\sqrt{\lvert f''/f''''\rvert}$ equals the number of points per wavelength. The pairing of orders $p=q+2$ is "the simplest" one that gives exact scaling (§2.2, Eqs. 20–25). **Proved** (for a sine)
7. The sensor $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}=\sqrt{(\lvert\mathsf D_3\rvert+\lvert\mathsf D_4\rvert)/(\lvert\mathsf D_1\rvert+\lvert\mathsf D_2\rvert+\epsilon_{\mathrm s})}$ combines both ratios, which avoids the singularity at critical and inflection points (Eq. 29). **Derived**
8. Evaluating $\mathsf D_p$ at the highest order possible on the six-point stencil is "not the best". Optimized six-point differences are used (Eq. 30), and the optimization procedure is not given (§2.2). **Stated**
9. Adding $\lvert\mathsf D_5\rvert$ (Eq. 32) "cannot give the ideal result", because $\mathsf D_5$ is unique on six points and leaves no room for optimization (§2.2). **Stated**
10. The regularization is amplitude-dependent, $\epsilon_{\mathrm s}=0.005e^{-500\max(f_{\max}-f_{\min}-0.001,0)}$ (Eq. 33). With $f_{\max}-f_{\min}\le0.001$, $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}<1$ at all wavenumbers; above 0.01 the sensor is essentially unaffected. $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ is accurate for $\vartheta<1.5$ and underestimates above, but it is monotone in $\vartheta$ with maximum 2.45 (§2.2–2.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. In static tests on 192 points (sine, two sines with a jump, growing amplitude, chirp), the sensor gets the sine wavenumbers right, passes smoothly through critical and inflection points, and reads a discontinuity as high-frequency content (§2.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. At $\Lambda_{\mathrm{dd}}=9$, Eq. 36 has two branches of $c_{\mathrm{diss}}(\vartheta)$ that meet at $\vartheta=1$. For $\vartheta\le1$ (more than six points per wavelength) the authors set $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.001$. For $\vartheta>1$ the branch is approximated by $\min\big(0.001+0.011\sqrt{(\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}-1)/(2.45-1)},\,0.012\big)$ (§2.3, Eqs. 36–38, Fig. 6). **Derived** (design choice)
13. The constraint $0\le c_{\mathrm{diss}}\le\min(c_{\mathrm{disp}},1/9-c_{\mathrm{disp}}/3)$ "guarantees" non-negative linear weights $d_k$ of the four candidates, so that the MDAD flux is a convex combination (§2.3, §3.1, Table 1). **Derived**
14. MDAD has the same maximum dissipation as MDCD(0.012) but much less for $\vartheta\in(0.6,1.0)$. The range where $\lvert\tilde\vartheta_i\rvert\le0.001$ is $\vartheta<1.02$, "35 % larger" than for MDCD ("1.348 times" in the §3.4 remark) (§2.3, Fig. 7). **Shown** (sine-wave calibration of a solution-dependent scheme)
15. The new detector (Eq. 60, $\xi=10^{-2}$, $\mathfrak s_c=0.4$) keeps $\delta^{\mathrm H}=1$ on sine waves up to $\vartheta=2.06$. Fu's TENO-based detector is correct only for $\vartheta<0.64$, and Harten's and Ren's detectors fail "at very low wavenumbers" (§3.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
16. On a jump plus two sines, every detector flags the jump, but only the new one separates the jump from both waves. Harten's and Ren's misjudge critical points of the low- and medium-wavenumber waves, and Fu's fails on the medium one (§3.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. By ADR, MDAD-HY recovers the MDAD dissipation and the MDCD dispersion up to $\vartheta=2.06$; beyond that WENO switches on and dissipation rises. TENO-HY and MDCD-HY show irregular dissipation at low wavenumbers because their detectors misjudge, and WENO-Z is the most dissipative (§3.3, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
18. On a smooth curvilinear grid, the 1D stencil applied to the transformed flux $J(\xi_xf+\xi_yg)$ has the same modified wavenumber (Eq. 69 = Eq. 11). So dispersion optimization, dissipation control and the sensor carry over to computational coordinates, assuming smooth grids, periodicity and a Fourier component (§3.4 remark, Eqs. 62–69). **Derived**
19. Linear advection of $u_0=\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^m\sin(2\pi kx)$, $m=5,10,15,20$: WENO-Z and TENO-HY reach fifth order, MDCD-HY and MDAD-HY fourth order on fine grids, and MDAD-HY has the lowest $L_2$ error on relatively coarse grids (§4.1, Fig. 14). **Shown**
20. Shu–Osher problem on 200 points: MDAD-HY resolves the post-shock crests and troughs best, and only its detector separates the entropy-wave fluctuations from the shocklets over time (§4.2, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
21. Robustness needs case tuning. The blast waves need $\mathfrak s_c=0.5$ (MDAD-HY) and $C_T=10^{-5}$ (TENO-HY). Sedov needs $\mathfrak s_c=0.4$ (MDCD-HY) and $0.8$ (MDAD-HY), and TENO-HY and the optimized TENO6 fail on Sedov "even with $C_T=0.1$" (§4.2). **Shown**
22. Double Mach reflection ($800\times200$) and Rayleigh–Taylor ($128\times512$): MDAD-HY gives the finest slip-line and shear-layer structures, and its detector flags only vortex edges, not vortex interiors. TENO-HY's detection here is "obviously better" than in Fu's own paper, which the authors attribute to normalizing the detector input (Eq. 61) (§4.3.1–4.3.2, Figs. 19–22). **Shown**
23. Shock–vortex interaction on a $58\times58$ curvilinear mesh: MDAD-HY is the only scheme whose centreline density $L_1$ error is below $10^{-3}$ ($0.9768\times10^{-3}$, 1.644 s). WENO-Z gives $2.464\times10^{-3}$ (3.013 s), MDCD-HY $1.624\times10^{-3}$ (1.656 s) and TENO-HY $1.920\times10^{-3}$ (1.776 s) (§4.3.3, Table 2). **Shown**
24. At matched error ($L_1<10^{-3}$), the efficiency $1/(\text{error}\times\text{time})$ of MDAD-HY (622.7) is 1.5, 2.2 and 5.8 times that of MDCD-HY ($70^2$), TENO-HY ($80^2$) and WENO-Z ($88^2$). No hardware is reported (§4.3.3, Eq. 70, Table 3). **Shown**
25. Shock–mixing-layer interaction ($1600\times320$, $\mathrm{Re}=500$, $t=120$): the detector captures the oblique shock and the vortex-induced shocklets without flagging the acoustic waves (§4.3.4, Fig. 26). **Shown**
26. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex ($128^3$, $t=10$): C6 and MDCD(0.001) blow up at $t=4$–$5$, while MDAD runs without tuning. UW5 and MDCD(0.012) run but are more dissipative. MDAD best preserves kinetic energy and follows the $-5/3$ slope at $t=10$ (§4.4.1, Figs. 27–28). **Shown**
27. Viscous Taylor–Green vortex, $\mathrm{Re}=1600$: C6 diverges, and MDAD matches the DNS kinetic energy before $t=9$ and the peak dissipation rate better than WENO-CU6-M1. On $256^3$ the enstrophy at $t=9$ is 9.866 for MDAD (4.1 % error), against 8.581 for UW5 and 9.214 for MDCD (DNS 10.284). MDAD costs about 19.3 % more CPU time (§4.4.2, Fig. 29, Table 4). **Shown**
28. At matched enstrophy error (about 16.6 %), MDAD needs $168^3$ points, MDCD $208^3$ and UW5 $256^3$. The efficiency ratios are 3.7 against UW5 and 1.8 against MDCD (§4.4.2, Table 5, Fig. 30). **Shown**
29. The approach can be carried over to finite-volume schemes (§3.4). **Stated**
