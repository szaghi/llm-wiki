# Summary

[Subcell limiting](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-limiting.md) for
[DGSEM](/methods/dgsem.md) blends, at each Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto subcell
interface, the algebraic subcell flux of the high-order scheme with that of a
sparse low-order scheme on the subcells. The low-order scheme is
positivity-preserving and entropy stable, and the blending follows
[Pazner (2021)](/sources/pazner-2021.md),
[Rueda-Ramírez et al. (2022)](/sources/ruedaramirez-2022.md) and monolithic
[convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md). The existing entropy fixes act
at the nodes: a local minimum principle on the specific entropy, limited to
compressible flow and costly in accuracy, or Tadmor's condition on each
subcell flux.

Lin & Chan enforce instead only the **semi-discrete cell entropy inequality**,
one scalar inequality per element and per dimension
([entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md)). The high- and
low-order schemes share the same local
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) interface flux, so their
surface contributions coincide. The cell inequality then becomes one linear
inequality in the limiting factors of the volume fluxes. Maximizing the sum of
the factors subject to that inequality and to the upper bounds $l^{\mathrm C}$
from any convex-constraint limiter ([bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md))
is a continuous-knapsack linear program. A greedy algorithm solves it exactly
at cost $O(m\log m)$, $m=N(N+1)$. Theorems prove solvability, local
conservation, cell entropy stability, constraint preservation and optimality.
A shock-capturing variant tightens the bound with a fraction
$\beta_{\mathrm e}\epsilon_K$ of the low-order entropy dissipation.

With [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stepping, the limiter
removes the entropy-violating solutions of a modified
[Sod](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) tube and of the
[KPP problem](/benchmarks/kpp-rotating-wave.md). On the
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) it gives rates between
about $N+\tfrac12$ and $N+1$, against at most 1 and 2 for the minimum entropy
principle and its relaxed form. The [Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md)
and Mach 2000 [jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md) runs are robust.
In the jet, small $\beta_{\mathrm e}$ produces a
[carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md), which the authors attribute
to too little shock dissipation, not to a loss of entropy stability. High order
is shown only numerically, and the smoothness factor is a
[troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): degree $N$, nodal mass matrix $M$,
SBP operators $Q$, entropy $\eta$ with flux $F_\eta$, entropy variables $w$
(source $\mathsf v$), potential $\psi$, wave speed $a$ (source
$\lambda_{\max}$). Page-local: subcell limiting factors $l_i$ (1 = high order,
i.e. $l=1-\alpha$ in the registry's blending convention), smoothness factor
$\epsilon_K$ (source $\epsilon$), blending fraction $\beta_{\mathrm e}$
(source $\beta$).

1. The subcell limiting strategies of Pazner, Rueda-Ramírez et al. and monolithic convex limiting for DGSEM do not provably satisfy a semi-discrete cell entropy inequality. The paper extends subcell and monolithic convex limiting so that they do, by computing the limiting factors from an optimization problem (Abstract, §1). **Stated**
2. The local minimum principle on specific entropy is limited to compressible flow and "may reduce the accuracy of the solution to at most second order for smooth solutions". Tadmor's entropy condition on subcell algebraic fluxes "also does not preserve high-order accuracy for DGSEM" (the latter by private communication, footnote 1) (§1). **Stated**
3. The low-order scheme (13), a local Lax–Friedrichs-type finite-volume scheme on the subcells built from a sparse operator $Q^{\mathrm L}_{1D}$, satisfies a semi-discrete entropy inequality (citing Lin et al. 2023, Thm. 6.1; semi-discrete only, footnote 3). With SSP Runge–Kutta under a time-step condition it is also positivity-preserving (§2.3.2). **Stated**
4. Both schemes are locally conservative and share the same interface flux, so the boundary algebraic fluxes coincide: $\bar f^{\mathrm H}_0=\bar f^{\mathrm L}_0=-h^{\mathrm{LF}}(u_1,u_1^+)$ and $\bar f^{\mathrm H}_{N+1}=\bar f^{\mathrm L}_{N+1}=-h^{\mathrm{LF}}(u_{N+1},u_{N+1}^+)$ (Eq. 17). The surface entropy contribution therefore does not depend on the limiting factors (Eq. 22). **Derived**
5. The cell entropy inequality reduces to one linear inequality in the volume limiting factors, $\sum_{i=1}^N(w_i-w_{i+1})^T\bar f_i(l_i)\le P^{\mathrm{vol}}$, with $P^{\mathrm{vol}}=\psi(u_{N+1})-\psi(u_1)$ and $\bar f_i$ linear in $l_i$ (Eqs. 20, 23–24). **Derived**
6. The limiting factors solve the linear program (25): maximize $\sum_il_i$ subject to the entropy inequality and $0\le l_i\le l^{\mathrm C}_i$, where $l^{\mathrm C}$ comes from any convex-constraint limiter (§3.1.1). **Derived**
7. Theorem 3.1: LP (25) is solvable, and its solution is locally conservative, satisfies the semi-discrete cell entropy inequality (20) and preserves the convex constraints of $l^{\mathrm C}$. Solvability follows from the entropy stability of the low-order scheme: $l=0$ is feasible (Eq. 26). **Proved**
8. LP (28), $\max\sum_ix_i$ subject to $a^Tx\le b$, $0\le x\le U$ (page-local symbols), is a continuous knapsack problem, and the greedy Algorithm 1 gives its optimal solution (Theorem 3.2, proof by contradiction, §3.1.2). **Proved**
9. In 2D the subcell-limited DGSEM is written with a difference operator $\Delta_{1D}$ of size $(N+1)\times(N+2)$ split into volume and surface parts (Eqs. 29–37). The $(N+2)(N+1)$ subcell fluxes per dimension are well defined by dimension-by-dimension conservation, citing Pazner (Prop. 8) (§3.2.1). **Stated**
10. Enforcing the inequality dimension by dimension (Eq. 40) reduces the unknowns per dimension from $(N+2)(N+1)$ to $N(N+1)$. The resulting LP (46) is again a knapsack problem (§3.2.2). **Derived**
11. Theorem 3.3: LP (46) is solvable, and its solution is locally conservative, satisfies the semi-discrete entropy inequality (39) and preserves the convex constraints (§3.2.2). **Proved**
12. Shock-capturing variant: the entropy bound is replaced by $(1-\beta_{\mathrm e}\epsilon_K)\big[\mathbf 1^TB_k\psi_k-w^T\Delta^{\mathrm{vol}}_k\bar f^{\mathrm L}_k\big]$ (Eq. 49, LP 51), with $\epsilon_K$ the modal smoothness factor of Eq. (50) and $\beta_{\mathrm e}\in[0,1]$ user-defined; $\beta_{\mathrm e}=0$ recovers (46b) (§3.3). **Derived**
13. Algorithm 2 sorts the coefficients decreasingly and accumulates the dot product, at cost $O(m\log m)$, $m=N(N+1)$. Coefficients below $\epsilon_0=10^{-14}$ are skipped; for non-positive coefficients the optimum is the upper bound (§3.4). **Stated**
14. The SSP time integrator and the CFL condition (52) are needed only for positivity, not for the cell entropy inequality (footnote 7). **Stated**
15. The Davis wave-speed estimate (54) is not always an upper bound of the maximum wave speed, so with it the low-order scheme is not provably positivity-preserving or entropy stable. No issues were observed (footnote 8). **Stated**
16. Modified Sod tube ($K=50,100$, $N=3$, $T=0.2$): DGSEM violates the entropy condition with a jump at the sonic rarefaction; ESDG and DGSEM with the cell-entropy limiter give a smooth rarefaction (§4.1.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
17. 2D KPP ($N=3$, $64^2$ and $128^2$ elements): DGSEM with Hennemann et al.'s blending converges to a non-entropic solution; with the cell-entropy limiter the solution is entropic (§4.1.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
18. 2D isentropic vortex ($l^{\mathrm C}=1$): the cell-entropy-limited DGSEM shows "an asymptotic convergence rate between $O(h^{N+1/2})$ and $O(h^{N+1})$" (Table 1; last printed rates 1.84, 2.74, 4.28, 4.36 for $N=1,\dots,4$) (§4.2). **Shown** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: recomputed from the printed errors, the last rates are 1.84, 2.47, 4.28, 4.36; the $N=2$ value lies below $N+\tfrac12$, $N=3$ above $N+1$ and $N=4$ below $N+\tfrac12$, so not all rates lie in the stated band.)*
19. The minimum entropy principle limiter gives at most first order (Table 2; last rates 0.86, 0.93, 0.90, 0.95) and its relaxed form at most second order (Table 3; last rates 1.52, 1.21, 1.49, 1.31) for every $N$. The subcell entropy fix reduces to second order on a smooth sine wave (private communication, footnote 9) (§4.2). **Shown** (Tables 2–3) and **Stated** (fix)
20. High-order accuracy of the cell-entropy limiter is not proved; only numerical evidence is given (§4.2). **Stated**
21. Kelvin–Helmholtz ($N=3$, $80\times80$, $T=10$): DGSEM with relaxed positivity (62) and the cell entropy inequality is robust and resolves fine-scale features. Applied to ESDG it gives visibly different structures, so the choice of the high-order scheme matters (§4.3, Figs. 4–5). **Shown**
22. Astrophysical jet (Mach about 2000, $N=3$, $150\times150$, $T=0.001$): robust with TVD-like density bounds (67)–(68), relaxed internal-energy positivity and the cell entropy inequality (Fig. 6). **Shown**
23. With $\beta_{\mathrm e}=1,0.1,0.01,0$ finer features appear as $\beta_{\mathrm e}$ decreases, but carbuncles appear at 0.01 and 0, and also with the minimum entropy principle (Fig. A.8c). Hence "the carbuncle effect is not due only to a loss of entropy stability, but rather a lack of sufficient dissipation near shocks", and the solution is highly sensitive to $\beta_{\mathrm e}$ (§4.4, Fig. 7). **Shown** (the attribution is the authors', from this one test)
24. The elementwise Zhang–Shu-limited ESDG shows no carbuncle, while the subcell-limited ESDG with the cell entropy inequality does; the authors partly explain this by the Zhang–Shu limiter being more dissipative (§4.4, Fig. A.8). **Shown**
25. The limiting also applies to ESDG through an alternative definition of the high-order residual (footnote 4); curved meshes are treated in Lin et al. (2023), App. A. **Stated**
