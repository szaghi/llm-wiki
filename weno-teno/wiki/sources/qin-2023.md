# Summary

High-order finite-difference schemes on multi-block grids lack
[shock-capturing boundary closures](/methods/shock-capturing-boundary-closure.md):
at a block boundary or interface the stencil is truncated, and
non-conservative interface interpolation produces oscillations, phase lag,
wrong jumps, stalled slow shocks or convergence to a false solution. The
authors start from their own globally conservative staggered
[WCNS](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md) framework. In it the first
two and last two solution points are moved to irrational "conservative
solution points" and combined with sixth-order interior and fourth-order
boundary flux-difference formulas, which gives a discrete global conservation
identity $\sum_j\mathsf w_jf'_j\Delta x=\hat f_{N_x+1/2}-\hat f_{1/2}$ with
explicit weights.

The interpolation step is a fifth-order
[multi-resolution](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) WCNS (WCNS-MR) on nested
sub-stencils of 1, 3 and 5 points, with the simplified nonlinear combination of
Wang et al. (2021) and WENO-Z-type weights. There are two contributions.
(i) A free scale factor $\lambda_{\mathrm s}$ in the derivative-sum
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md), which weights the
$n$-th derivative by $\lambda_{\mathrm s}^{n-1}$; $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$ is
recommended because it suppresses oscillations and lets slow shocks cross
interfaces without reducing the order. (ii) The same multi-resolution recipe
is applied near the boundary on one-sided nested stencils built on the
conservative points and the boundary flux point, with coefficients tabulated
to 17 digits.

The tests are fifth-order convergence on linear advection,
[Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) and 1D Euler; a Burgers shock
through a periodic boundary; slow shocks across two-block interfaces with
spacing jumps (Burgers and 2D Euler); the
[Buckley–Leverett](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md),
[Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) problems across interfaces; a
four-block [shock–bubble interaction](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md);
supersonic flow over a 4% bump on three blocks; and a transonic NACA0012 whose
shocks are cut by block interfaces. The interface overhead in CPU time is
negligible. Stability of the nonlinear closures is shown only numerically.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $N_x$ is the number of solution points of a block (the
source's $N$), $x_j$ the solution points and $x_{j+1/2}$ the flux points,
$\hat f_{j+1/2}$ the numerical flux, $d_k$ the linear weights (the source's
$\gamma_k$), $P_k$ the Lagrange interpolant on sub-stencil $k$ (the source's
$\phi_k$), and $\lambda_{\mathrm s}$ the new scale factor (the source's
$\lambda$). The sub-stencils are numbered $k=1,2,3$ from the smallest, as in the
source, not from the most upwind.

1. No existing high-order finite-difference method has both the global conservation property and nonlinear shock-capturing boundary closures (§1). **Stated**
2. With the conservative solution points $x_{1,2}=a+\frac{12204\mp\sqrt{53326826}}{13420}\Delta x$ (Eq. (2)) and their mirror images, and the flux-difference formulas (3) with the Table 1 coefficients (sixth order inside, fourth order at $j=1,2,N_x-1,N_x$), the scheme satisfies $\sum_{j=1}^{N_x}\mathsf w_jf'_j\Delta x=\hat f_{N_x+1/2}-\hat f_{1/2}$ with $\mathsf w_j=1$ for $3\le j\le N_x-2$ and $\mathsf w_{1}=\mathsf w_{N_x}=1-\frac{11237\sqrt{53326826}}{639921912}$, $\mathsf w_{2}=\mathsf w_{N_x-1}=1+\frac{11237\sqrt{53326826}}{639921912}$ (§2, Eqs. (3)–(5)). **Stated** (proved in Deng & Chen 2018, recalled; numerically $x_1-a=0.36524\,\Delta x$, $x_2-a=1.45354\,\Delta x$, $\mathsf w_1=0.87177$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*)
3. Global fifth order needs a fifth-order interior and boundary closures of at least fourth order (Gustafsson), so it suffices that the interpolation step be fifth order everywhere (§2). **Stated**
4. The simplified nonlinear combination $u_{j+1/2}=\omega_1u^{(1)}+\omega_2u^{(2)}+\omega_3\big(\tfrac{1}{d_3}u^{(3)}-\sum_{k=1}^2\tfrac{d_k}{d_3}u^{(k)}\big)$ (Eq. (10)), with $u^{(k)}=P_k(x_{j+1/2})$, is more efficient than the combination of Zhang, Wang & Zhang (2020) (§2.1, citing Wang et al. 2021). **Stated**
5. The weights are $\omega_k\propto d_k\big(1+\tau/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)$ with $d=(1,10,100)/111$, $\epsilon=10^{-10}$ and $\tau=\big((\lvert\beta_3-\beta_2\rvert+\lvert\beta_3-\beta_1\rvert)/2\big)^2$ (Eqs. (11)–(12)). The new indicators are $\beta_k=\sum_n\big(\lambda_{\mathrm s}^{n-1}\Delta x^nP_k^{(n)}(x_j)\big)^2$, $n=1,2$ for $k=2$ and $n=1,\dots,4$ for $k=3$; $\beta_1=\min(\delta_0^2,\delta_1^2)$ from one-sided divided differences (Eqs. (13)–(22)). $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$ "causes failure in shock-transmission at interface when encountering slowly propagating shocks"; $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$ is adopted (§2.1). **Stated** (the choice is empirical)
6. Near the boundary ($j=1,2,N_x-1,N_x$) the interior formulas are applied unchanged on one-sided nested sub-stencils, the left ones using the boundary value $u_{1/2}$ as a node; only the coefficients change (Tables 6–9). For $j=3,4,N_x-3,N_x-2$ the interior stencils contain conservative points and take modified coefficients (Tables 2–5) (§2.1–§2.2, Fig. 2). **Derived**
7. Linear advection with time-dependent inflow, $t=1$: $L_2$ errors from $6.92\times10^{-6}$ ($N_x=20$) to $6.40\times10^{-12}$ ($N_x=320$), order 5.01, identical for $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$ and $2$ in every printed digit (§3.1, Table 10). **Shown**
8. Smooth Burgers at $t=0.3$: order 5.16 ($L_2$) and 4.97 ($L_\infty$) at $N_x=640$; with $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$ the coarse-grid errors are larger ($6.92\times10^{-5}$ against $4.37\times10^{-5}$ in $L_2$ at $N_x=40$) and the two coincide from $N_x=160$ (Table 11). **Shown**
9. The Burgers shock passes through the periodic boundary, where the closures are active, with correct profile and location at $t=4$, $7.9$, $8.1$ ($N_x=200$) (§3.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. A slow Burgers shock (speed 0.01) crosses a two-block interface with a 2:1 spacing jump ($160$ and $80$ points) with "invisible oscillation and phase lag" at $t=40$, $80$ (§3.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. Buckley–Leverett on two blocks converges to the single-domain reference ($N_x=4000$), with a smooth transition just after the shock crosses the interface (§3.3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. 1D Euler density wave, $t=1$: $L_2$ error $6.38\times10^{-12}$ ($\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$) and $6.39\times10^{-12}$ ($\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$) at $N_x=320$, order 5.01, with super-convergent coarse-grid orders 6.40–7.17 (Table 12). **Shown**
13. On the Lax problem (single domain, $t=0.14$), $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$ suppresses the oscillations of $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$. WENO-JS and multi-resolution WENO (characteristic-wise) are less oscillatory than WCNS-MR with $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$, which the authors attribute to the wide explicit difference stencil (3) of WCNS (§3.4.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. Sod and Lax shocks cross an interface at $x=0.75$ with a spacing jump (160 points on $[0,0.75]$, 40 on $(0.75,1]$), with shock locations that agree with the exact solutions (Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
15. Shu–Osher on two blocks (250 and 500 points, interface $x=5$) gives a smooth transition at $t=1.0$, $1.8$ against a single-domain reference with 6000 points; with $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$ "the shock fails to cross the interface" (§3.4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown** ($\lambda_{\mathrm s}=2$); **Stated** ($\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$, no figure)
16. Interface coupling costs negligible CPU time: at 10000 points, Lax 283.4 s (1 zone) against 284.5 s (2 zones), Sod 189.1 s against 191.4 s, Shu–Osher 376.1 s against 375.4 s, serial on an AMD Ryzen7-5800H (§3.4.4, Table 13). **Shown**
17. A 2D slow shock (speed 0.015) crosses the interface $y=1$ between two $40\times40$ blocks without visible oscillations and at the correct location ($t=9$, $11$, $30$); the closures are called a "conservative, high-order, yet easy-implemented remedy" for the slow-shock interface problem (§3.5.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
18. Shock–bubble interaction on four blocks of different spacings: contacts and small scales well resolved; the contours are not exactly symmetric because the spacing differs between blocks (§3.5.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
19. Supersonic $M=1.4$ flow over a 4% circular-arc bump on three blocks (interfaces $x=\pm0.5$), which avoids the metric singularities of a single block at the bump's edges: sharp shocks unhindered by the interfaces, no spurious vortex, wall pressure along $y=0.4$ matching the single domain and the literature (§3.5.3, Figs. 12–14). **Shown**
20. Transonic NACA0012 ($M=0.8$, $\alpha=1.25^\circ$, $240\times100$, three blocks) with interfaces cutting both shocks: $C_p$ coincides with the single-domain result and is "comparable to" a reference solution; such a partition "is surely to be avoided" with traditional closures (§3.5.4, Figs. 15–17). **Shown**
21. The method applies to orders other than five; the generalization is left to future work (§2, §4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (40), 2D slow shock: the states are labelled $(\rho,u,v,E)=(1.4,0,4.2,8.8)$ and $(5.38064,0,4.25971,27.2576)$. Read with $v=4.2$ and $\gamma=1.4$, the left pressure is $0.4(8.8-1.4\cdot4.2^2/2)=-1.42$. Read as $(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)$, the states have $v=3$, $p=1.0$ and $v=0.7917$, $p=10.229$, and the Rankine–Hugoniot speeds from mass and energy are $0.015000$ and $0.014997$, the stated $c=0.015$ (the momentum equation gives $0.01473$). The second and third entries are therefore the momenta $\rho v_1$, $\rho v_2$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (41), shock–bubble interaction: the inflow $(\rho,v_1,v_2,p)=(3.81,2.85,0,10)$ against $(1,0,0,1)$ is not a Rankine–Hugoniot shock state for $\gamma=1.4$ (§3.4): the mass and momentum jumps give shock speeds $3.86$ and $3.68$, and a single shock with the printed $\rho$ and $p$ would carry $v_1\approx2.58$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. The paper does not comment on it.
- §3.4 and §3.5 call $E=p/(\gamma-1)+\rho u^2/2$ the "total energy per unit mass"; the formula is per unit volume.
- Eq. (22): $\delta_0=\frac{u^{II}_2-u^{II}_1}{x^{II}_2-x^{II}_1}=\frac{u_j-u_{j-1}}{x_j-x_{j-1}}\Delta x$ (and likewise $\delta_1$): the factor $\Delta x$ appears only in the last member.
- Table 10 prints identical $\lambda=1$ and $\lambda=2$ columns in every entry, whereas Tables 11–12 differ on coarse grids; the paper does not comment.
- Ref. [24] prints the author as "Bertil G." (given name as surname; the text names Gustafsson); ref. [8] prints "Fernáandez DCDR".
- Typos: "Rather then using equal-size stencils" (§2.1), "the devised scheme can achieved" (§4), "we focus on the these schemes" (§1).
