# Summary

High-order entropy-stable [discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) (ESDG)
discretizations of the compressible Euler and Navier–Stokes equations satisfy a semi-discrete
entropy inequality only while density and internal energy stay positive, and nothing in them
enforces that. The authors build a [positivity-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md)
limiter for nodal ESDG on quadrilaterals and triangles, fully explicit, with explicit viscous
terms.

The construction has three parts. First, a sparse low-order
[SBP operator](/operators/summation-by-parts-operator.md) $Q^{\mathrm L}_d$ with the SBP and
conservation properties of the high-order one: Pazner's piecewise-linear operator on
tensor-product elements, and on simplices a new construction from an adjacency graph of the
nodes, whose skew part comes from a graph-Laplacian solve for a potential vector. Second, a
low-order scheme: the skew-symmetric DG form with $Q^{\mathrm L}_d$, central fluxes of the
total (inviscid minus viscous) flux, and a graph viscosity whose coefficient is Zhang's
Navier–Stokes positivity coefficient $a^{\mathrm{NS}}$
([positivity-preserving Navier–Stokes flux](/methods/positivity-preserving-navier-stokes-flux.md)).
Written as a convex combination of bar states it is positive under forward Euler and
$\Delta t\le m_i/(2\lambda_i)$ (Theorem 5.2), and with the coefficient enlarged by a maximum
wave-speed bound it is also semi-discretely [entropy stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md)
(Theorem 6.1), by reading it as a subcell finite-volume scheme with
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)-type fluxes
([subcell limiting](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-limiting.md)). Third, an elementwise blending
of low- and high-order updates in the manner of the
[Zhang–Shu limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md), one parameter per element, the
largest that keeps every nodal value above relaxed bounds $\zeta_{\mathrm r}\rho(u^{\mathrm L}_i)$
and $\zeta_{\mathrm r}\rho e(u^{\mathrm L}_i)$; it has a closed form, and because one parameter
blends two entropy-stable schemes, conservation and the semi-discrete entropy inequality are
kept (Theorem 6.2). Appendix B gives a [convex-limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md) (subcell)
variant and explains why it loses the cell entropy inequality when positivity is the only
constraint.

The tests are the [Leblanc shock tube](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md), a 1D viscous shock
profile at $M_0=3$ and 20, the [Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), a strong
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md), the
[Sedov blast wave](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and the Daru–Tenaud
[viscous shock tube](/benchmarks/viscous-shock-tube.md). The limited scheme is robust in all of
them and converges at high order where limiting is inactive or active only on coarse meshes;
without monotonicity constraints it oscillates near shocks.

# Key claims

Registry notation and the page-local symbols of the
[SBP](/operators/summation-by-parts-operator.md) and
[convex-limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md) pages: $Q_d$ and $Q^{\mathrm L}_d$ the high- and
low-order SBP matrices in direction $d$, $\mathcal R^\top B_\partial N_d\mathcal R$ the boundary
term (the source's $\mathbf E^\top\mathbf B_d\mathbf E$), $m_i$ the lumped mass, $\hat d_{ij}$ the
graph viscosity, $\breve u$ the bar states, $a^{\mathrm{NS}}$ Zhang's coefficient (the source's
$\beta$), $\mathsf l_K\in[0,1]$ the elementwise blending parameter ($1$ = high order, so the
registry blending coefficient is $\alpha=1-\mathsf l_K$), $\zeta_{\mathrm r}$ the bound relaxation,
$\nabla_dw$ the entropy-variable gradients, $N_{\mathrm{el}}$ the number of elements.

1. The admissible set $G=\{\rho>0,\ \rho e>0\}$ of the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations is convex, as an intersection of superlevel sets of concave functions (Eq. (3), citing Zhang 2017). **Stated**
2. A diagonal-norm SBP operator is determined by its skew-symmetric part, $Q_d=\tfrac12(Q_d-Q_d^\top)+\tfrac12\mathcal R^\top B_\partial N_d\mathcal R$ (Eqs. (9)–(10)); a sparse low-order SBP operator on simplices is built by prescribing the sparsity of the skew part from an adjacency graph (Eq. (11)) and solving the graph-Laplacian system $\mathsf L\psi^{\mathrm{SBP}}=\tfrac12\mathcal R^\top B_\partial N_d\mathcal R\,\mathbf 1$, $(\psi^{\mathrm{SBP}})^\top\mathbf 1=0$ (§3.2). **Derived**
3. Sparsifying the SBP operator keeps the accuracy of the low-order scheme from degrading as $N$ grows (§5, citing Pazner); Table 1a and Fig. 3 show nearly identical low-order Leblanc solutions for $N=2$ and $N=5$ at 600 degrees of freedom. **Shown**
4. Lemma 5.1: for admissible $u_L,u_R$, the bar states (23) built with $a^{\mathrm{NS}}_{L,R}=\max\{a^{\mathrm{NS}}(u_L,\cdot),a^{\mathrm{NS}}(u_R,\cdot)\}$ (24) are admissible (§5, via (25) and Zhang's condition). **Proved**
5. Theorem 5.2: on a periodic domain the low-order scheme (16) is conservative, $\mathbf 1^\top M u^{n+1}=\mathbf 1^\top Mu^n$, and for $u^n_i\in G$ and $\Delta t\le m_i/(2\lambda_i)$, $\lambda_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal I(i)}\hat d_{ij}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal B(i)}\sum_d(\lambda_d)_i$ (Eq. (27)), the forward-Euler update is in $G$ (Eq. (28)). **Proved**
6. The positivity time step (27) scales as $O(h/N^2)$ and has been observed to be about 2–3 times smaller than the largest stable high-order DG step (Remark 1, citing Zhang & Shu); no backtracking heuristic is used. **Stated**
7. With explicit viscous terms the step is also limited by a parabolic CFL condition: (27) implies positivity, not stability (Remark 2). The hyperbolic step scales as $O(h)$ and the parabolic as $O(h^2\mathrm{Re})$, so for $h=O(1/\mathrm{Re})$ the parabolic restriction is "not overly restrictive" (§5). **Stated**
8. Theorem 6.1: with $\hat d_{ij}=\max\{a(u_i,u_j,n_{ij}/\lVert n_{ij}\rVert),a^{\mathrm{NS}}(u_i,\cdot),a^{\mathrm{NS}}(u_j,\cdot)\}\lVert n_{ij}\rVert$ (Eq. (45)), $a$ a maximum wave-speed bound, the low-order scheme (16) also satisfies a semi-discrete entropy balance per element (Eq. (46)); the proof reads (16) as a subcell finite-volume scheme with LLF-type fluxes (47)–(48) and uses their entropy stability (53). **Proved**
9. For the Euler equations $a^{\mathrm{NS}}$ never exceeds the maximum wave speed, so (45) reduces to the wave-speed estimate itself (§6.2). **Stated**
10. Theorem 6.2: on a periodic domain the elementwise-limited semi-discrete scheme is conservative and satisfies $\frac{d}{dt}\int_D\eta(u)\le-\sum_{d,d'}(\nabla_dw)^\top M\mathsf K_{dd'}\nabla_{d'}w$ (Eq. (54)), because the limited solution (55) is a convex combination, with one parameter per element, of two conservative entropy-stable solutions. **Proved**
11. Theorem 6.2 is semi-discrete only: to the authors' knowledge no fully discrete entropy inequality can be proved for the high-order method with forward Euler or Runge–Kutta stepping; space–time DG or relaxation Runge–Kutta are pointed to (§6.2). **Stated**
12. The blending parameter has a closed form: $\mathsf l^\rho$ from a linear constraint (Eq. (40)), $\mathsf l^{\rho e}$ as the smallest non-negative root of a quadratic (Eq. (41)), $\mathsf l_K=\min(\mathsf l^\rho,\mathsf l^{\rho e})$ (Eq. (42)), citing Guermond, Popov & Tomas (2019) for the proof. **Stated**
13. The relaxed bounds $\rho^{\min}_i=\zeta_{\mathrm r}\rho(u^{\mathrm L}_i)$, $\rho e^{\min}_i=\zeta_{\mathrm r}\rho e(u^{\mathrm L}_i)$, $\zeta_{\mathrm r}\in(0,1]$ (43), are stronger than the minimal bounds $\epsilon_0>0$ (44) and avoid issues such as "the time-step size converging to zero" without compromising accuracy (§6.1). **Shown** (asserted from experiments; no table compares (43) with (44))
14. [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) methods preserve positivity because they are convex combinations of forward-Euler steps (§7). **Stated**
15. Leblanc shock tube ($N=2,5$, $N_{\mathrm{el}}=50$–$800$, CFL 0.5, $T=2/3$): all strategies are "first order accurate, which is optimal for this test case"; limited $N=5$ errors at $N_{\mathrm{el}}=800$ are $6.333\times10^{-4}$ ($\zeta_{\mathrm r}=0.1$) and $5.889\times10^{-4}$ ($\zeta_{\mathrm r}=0.5$), against $2.868\times10^{-2}$ for the low-order scheme; the observed low-order rates are 0.35–0.69 (Table 1). **Shown**
16. Viscous shock profile at $M_0=3$: the low-order scheme is first order in $L^1$ (rates 0.84–0.97 for $N=2,3,4$, Table 2); limiting is active only at $N_{\mathrm{el}}=10$, and the limited solutions converge at "$O(h^N)$ to $O(h^{N+1/2})$" (Tables 3–4; $N=5$, $N_{\mathrm{el}}=160$: $6.652\times10^{-7}$, rate 6.13). **Shown**
17. Viscous shock profile at $M_0=20$, $\mu=0.001$: unlimited ESDG fails on coarse meshes; the limited solutions converge at rates between $O(h^N)$ and $O(h^{N+1/2})$ while limiting is active, and at $N_{\mathrm{el}}=1600$ are "virtually identical" to unlimited ESDG (Table 5). **Shown**
18. Isentropic vortex with strength 8.5 in the source's form (printed maximum and minimum density 1.0 and $2.145\times10^{-3}$, see Printed slips): unlimited ESDG fails; the limited solutions on quadrilaterals (CFL 0.9) and triangles (CFL 0.5) reach asymptotic rates "between $O(h^{N+1/2})$ and $O(h^{N+1})$" (Tables 6–9; quadrilaterals $N=4$, $N_{\mathrm{el}}=32$: $1.915\times10^{-4}$). **Shown**
19. Limiting for positivity only, without monotonicity or a minimum entropy principle, leaves spurious oscillations near shocks (Shu–Osher, Sedov, double Mach reflection); $\zeta_{\mathrm r}=0.5$ or the shock-capturing blending reduces them (§8.2–8.4, Figs. 5–11). **Shown**
20. Double Mach reflection on $875\times250$ quadrilaterals, $N=3$: robust, resolves fine vortical structures, and "suggests that the proposed limiting strategy does not introduce excessive numerical dissipation" (§8.4, Fig. 9). **Shown** (qualitative)
21. Daru–Tenaud at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$, $\zeta_{\mathrm r}=0.1$, no shock capturing: all runs robust; $N=2,3$ oscillate more near the shock than $N=1$ at equal 720000 degrees of freedom; the higher-order solutions resemble the fine-grid reference of Guermond et al. (2022, ref. [72]), but the limited scheme does not converge uniformly to it ($N=3$, $K_{1D}=200$ differs near $x\in[0.45,0.65]$ at the bottom wall) (§8.5, Fig. 12). **Shown**
22. In the Daru–Tenaud test, adding shock capturing changes the shock structure in $[0.45,0.64]\times[0.04,0.18]$ and the boundary layer in $[0.45,0.64]\times[0,0.04]$ substantially, and the results "appear to be incorrect" against Daru & Tenaud (2009) and Guermond et al. (2022); $\zeta_{\mathrm r}$ has little effect at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$, more at $\mathrm{Re}=10000$ (§8.5, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
23. The Davis wave-speed estimate (58) is not a guaranteed upper bound: Guermond & Popov show a case where the Rusanov estimate underestimates the maximum wave speed, which could compromise the positivity and entropy stability of the low-order scheme; no such issue was observed here (footnote 3). **Stated**
24. The slip-wall condition from the exact Riemann solution of van der Vegt & van der Ven is not provably positivity preserving but greatly improves robustness near walls (§8). **Stated**
25. Convex (subcell) limiting with positivity as the only constraint cannot be shown to preserve a semi-discrete cell entropy inequality, whereas elementwise limiting with one parameter per element does (App. B.1). **Stated**
26. When the high- and low-order schemes share the same LLF interface flux and interface matrices, convex limiting localizes to element interiors and needs no exchange of limiting parameters between neighbours (App. B.2, Eqs. (80)–(81)). **Derived**
27. The sparse low-order scheme shows a staircase-like effect near element boundaries, also seen in other bar-state discretizations (personal communication with Guermond, Popov and Maier); the authors speculate that it comes from the smaller diagonal mass entries near element boundaries (footnote 4). **Stated**
28. 2D extruded viscous shock profile (App. C): the limited solutions with $\zeta_{\mathrm r}=0.1$ converge asymptotically at rates between $O(h^{N+1/2})$ and $O(h^{N+1})$ on quadrilaterals and triangles (Tables 10–11; $N=4$, $N_{\mathrm{el}}=80$: $3.231\times10^{-6}$ and $2.807\times10^{-6}$). **Shown**
