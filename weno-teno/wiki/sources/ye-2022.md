# Summary

The paper builds [AFTENO](/methods/afteno.md), which joins the stencil
selection of [targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md) (Fu, Hu & Adams
2016) to the [alternative formulation of finite-difference WENO](/methods/alternative-weno.md)
(AFWENO, Jiang, Shu & Zhang 2013). In the alternative formulation, the
[conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
flux is not reconstructed from split point fluxes. It is a monotone two-point
flux $h(u^-_{i+1/2},u^+_{i+1/2})$ evaluated on *interpolated* states, plus
even-derivative corrections $a_{2\ell}\Delta x^{2\ell}\partial_x^{2\ell}f$
that restore high order. AFTENO replaces the WENO interpolation of the states
by a TENO interpolation: incremental candidate stencils of widths
$3,3,3,4,4,\dots$, the scale-separated measure
$\gamma_k=\big(C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)^q$ built on
[Jiang–Shu smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md), the
cut-off $\delta_k$ with threshold $C_T$, and $\omega_k=\delta_kd_k/\sum_l\delta_ld_l$.
The fifth-order global indicator is the [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) $\tau_5$,
the sixth-order one that of [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md).

The claimed advantages over TENO are freedom to interpolate conserved,
primitive or characteristic variables, any monotone flux
([Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md), Steger–Warming,
[HLLC](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md)) and better
[free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md), which is
never tested. Over AFWENO, the claims are robustness to several discontinuities
in the global stencil and even as well as odd orders. AFWENO is said to be
equivalent to a [hybrid WCNS](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md)
under specific parameters. All evidence is numerical and at fifth order:
accuracy tests on advection and Euler density waves, then Sod,
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), a Titarev–Toro-type
shock–entropy wave, [Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and the
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at $\mathrm{Re}=1600$.
AFTENO5 is reported as the least dissipative of AFWENO-JS, AFWENO-Z and TENO5,
at about 1.5 times the cost of AFWENO and 3 times that of TENO5. Time stepping
is RK4 for accuracy tests and a low-storage Runge–Kutta otherwise (see
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)); no CFL number is stated.
The baseline comparator is [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (index $i$, design order $K$, candidate interpolants
$u^{(k)}$; see the notation map).

1. AFTENO interpolates conserved variables, primitive variables or the physical flux with the TENO algorithm and combines the result with any monotone flux; classical TENO can only reconstruct a smooth flux-splitting function (Abstract, §1, §5). **Stated**
2. Unlike AFWENO, which has odd orders only, AFTENO reaches arbitrary odd and even orders because the TENO candidate stencils grow incrementally (Abstract, §2.4, §3.4, §5). **Stated**; coefficients are given up to $K=8$ (Tables 1–2) and derivative-correction coefficients up to order 10 (Tables 3–6); only fifth order is tested.
3. With two discontinuities in the global stencil, all candidate stencils of seventh-order WENO are non-smooth, while seventh-order TENO still contains a smooth three-point stencil (§2.1, Figs. 1–2). **Stated** (schematic)
4. The candidate stencil widths of $K$th-order TENO are $\{3,3,3,4,\dots,(K+2)/2\}$ for even $K$ and $\{3,3,3,4,\dots,(K+1)/2\}$ for odd $K$, $k=0,\dots,K-3$ (Eq. 1). **Stated** (background, Fu et al. 2016)
5. The seventh-order AFTENO interpolants $u^{(0)},\dots,u^{(4)}$ (Eq. 14) combine with $d_k=(35/64,35/128,7/128,7/64,1/64)$ (Eq. 17) into the seven-point global interpolant (Eq. 15). **Derived**
6. The derivative corrections contribute little to oscillations because their coefficients carry at least $\Delta x^2$, so low-order central differences of the physical flux at grid points, without characteristic projection or nonlinear weighting, suffice (§3.2). **Stated** (after Jiang et al. 2013)
7. A purely central approximation of the derivative corrections can be unstable in some problems; an upwind (flux-split) approximation as in Liu (2017) fixes this but "increases the computational cost significantly" (§3.2, Eqs. 19–23). **Stated**
8. For fifth-order AFTENO, $\tau_K=\lvert\beta_2-\beta_1\rvert$ (the WENO-Z $\tau_5$ in relabelled stencils), $\tau_K=O(\Delta x^5)$ and $\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^3)$, $k=0,1,2$ (Eqs. 24–27). **Derived** (Taylor expansion)
9. For sixth-order AFTENO, $\tau_K=\beta_6-\tfrac16(\beta_1+\beta_2+4\beta_0)=O(\Delta x^6)$ (WENO-CU6 type) and $\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^4)$, $k=0,\dots,3$ (Eqs. 28–32). **Derived**
10. $K$th-order accuracy requires $\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^{K-2})$; since $\beta_k=O(\Delta x^2)$, $\tau_K=O(\Delta x^K)$; a sixth-order $\tau_K$ (Eq. 39) is "enough to achieve very good performance" (§3.5, Eqs. 36–39, citing Fu, Hu and Adams 2017). **Stated/Derived** (contradicted by Fu 2023, Eq. 17; and for $K\ge7$ the sixth-order $\tau_K$ of Eq. 39 violates the paper's own Eq. 38; see the Contradictions of [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md))
11. The derivative-correction coefficients are $a_2=-1/24$, $a_4=7/5760$, $a_6=-31/967680$, $a_8=127/154828800$, $a_{10}=-73/3503554560$ (Appendix, Eqs. A.15–A.17). **Derived**
12. Linear advection $\partial_tu+\partial_xu=0$, $u_0=\sin(\pi x)$, $t=2$: AFTENO5-LF reaches fifth order, with $L_1$ error $6.95\times10^{-12}$ at $N_x=640$ against $6.31\times10^{-11}$ (AFWENO5-LF), $9.72\times10^{-12}$ (TENO5) and $7.94\times10^{-11}$ (WENO5-JS) (Tables 7–8). **Shown**
13. 1D Euler density wave: all schemes show orders near 5 on coarse meshes and 6.1–7.0 on the three finest; AFTENO5 $L_1$ at $N_x=640$ is $7.86\times10^{-14}$ against $1.08\times10^{-13}$ (AFWENO5) (Tables 9–10). **Shown**
14. 2D Euler density wave: AFTENO5 has the smallest $L_1$ errors ($5.60\times10^{-11}$ at $320^2$ against $2.90\times10^{-10}$ for AFWENO5-JS and $2.71\times10^{-10}$ for TENO5); its observed order drops to 4.70 on the finest mesh and its $L_\infty$ error is larger than AFWENO5-JS on every mesh ($3.22\times10^{-10}$ against $1.48\times10^{-10}$ at $320^2$) (Tables 11–12, §4.1). **Shown**
15. Cost (2D density wave, 500 steps, fifth order, global LF): at $160^2$ AFTENO takes 269.85 s total and 235.92 s in the flux, AFWENO 176.86 s / 143.24 s (+52.6 % / +64.7 %), TENO 90.94 s / 60.51 s (+196.7 % / +289.9 %); at $320^2$ AFTENO 2119.94 / 1876.65 s, AFWENO 1348.72 / 1107.37 s (+57.2 % / +69.5 %), TENO 731.51 / 486.99 s (+189.8 % / +285.4 %) (Table 13, §4.2). **Shown**
16. The extra cost over AFWENO (about 60 %) comes from scale separation and the ENO-like selection; the interpolation-based flux with high-order derivatives takes "nearly tripling time" over TENO, a cost that buys better free-stream preservation (§4.2). **Shown** (timings) / **Stated** (free-stream preservation is not tested)
17. Sod problem ($N_x=200$, $t=0.2$): all schemes agree with the exact solution; AFWENO-Z captures discontinuities better than AFWENO-JS, and AFTENO best (§4.3, Fig. 3). **Shown**
18. Shu–Osher problem ($N_x=200$, $t=1.8$): AFTENO and TENO keep the density-wave amplitude much better than the AFWENO schemes (§4.4, Fig. 4). **Shown**
19. Shock–entropy wave ($N_x=1500$, $t=5$): the exponent $q$ of $\gamma_k$ has a large effect on the entropy-wave amplitude, $C_T$ a smaller one; with $q=2$ AFTENO is closest to the reference, with $q=6$ its amplitude falls below that of AFWENO-Z (§4.5, Fig. 5). **Shown**
20. Blast waves ($t=0.038$): AFTENO is closer to the reference than AFWENO-JS and AFWENO-Z in density and pressure (§4.6, Fig. 6). **Shown**
21. Rayleigh–Taylor ($\Delta x=1/480$, $t=1.95$, HLLC): AFTENO5 gives the richest small-scale structures, comparable to fifth-order WENO-JS at $\Delta x=1/1920$ or ninth-order WENO-JS at $1/960$ (Shi, Zhang & Shu 2003); a smaller $C_T$ ($10^{-6}\to10^{-8}$) resolves more small scales (§4.7, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
22. Double Mach reflection ($\Delta x=1/256$, $t=0.2$): AFWENO-JS is the most dissipative and AFTENO the least, "very close" to fifth-order WENO-JS at $1/960$ and ninth-order WENO-JS at $1/480$ [34] (§4.8, Fig. 9). **Shown**
23. Taylor–Green vortex ($M=0.1$, $\mathrm{Re}=1600$, $256^3$, $t\le20$): the kinetic energy is nearly scheme-independent; for the dissipation rate all TENO-type schemes beat the WENO-type ones, which underestimate the peak (more so with a dissipative flux), AFTENO5-HLLC is best, and HLLC outperforms Steger–Warming in both AF schemes (§4.9, Figs. 10–12). **Shown**
24. A smaller $C_T$ gives higher wavenumber resolution; typical $C_T\approx10^{-5}$–$10^{-7}$ (§2.3, §4.7). **Stated** (background) and **Shown** (Fig. 8)
