# Summary

The paper extends the [MDCD](/methods/mdcd-scheme.md) line of six-point, fourth-order linear
finite-difference fluxes with a dispersion parameter $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ and a dissipation
parameter $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$ (Sun et al. 2011; the MDAD scheme of
[Li et al. (2022a)](/sources/li-2022.md)). The authors name two defects of MDAD. Its adaptive
dissipation is designed for the semi-discrete scheme and ignores the time integrator. Its
[scale sensor](/definitions/scale-sensor.md) $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ underestimates the local
reduced wavenumber at medium and large $\vartheta$ and so adds too little dissipation there.

The new scheme, ADADF, is designed on the fully discrete scheme with three-stage third-order
Runge–Kutta. The amplification factor $\mathcal G$ gives a total dissipation error $|\mathcal G|$ and
a total dispersion error $\delta_\nu=\arg\mathcal G+\vartheta\nu$, a fully discrete form
of the [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md). On it
the authors (i) minimize a fully discrete integrated phase error, which gives a fitted
$c_{\mathrm{disp}}(\nu)$; (ii) re-optimize the six-point derivative stencils of the sensor so
that its relative error stays within 0.6 % up to $\vartheta=2.08$; (iii) impose a fully discrete
dispersion–dissipation ratio $\le10$, whose iso-surface gives a "critical-adaptive"
$c_{\mathrm{diss}}$; and (iv) make $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ adaptive by setting
$\delta_\nu=0$ at the sensed wavenumber. Both parameters are fitted by cubics for
$\nu=0.1,0.3,0.5$.

For shocks, ADADF is blended with [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md), whose linear weights are the
MDCD ones, through Ren et al.'s continuous indicator ([hybrid WENO](/methods/hybrid-weno.md));
the result is ADADF-HY, applied characteristic-wise with Roe averages and global Lax–Friedrichs
splitting. Spectral curves show about 65.58 % resolving efficiency for every $\nu$ tested, while
MDCD and MDAD lose resolution as $\nu$ grows. The tests (multi-wave advection, Shu–Osher, 2D
Riemann configurations 3, 6, 12, 15, Kelvin–Helmholtz, Rayleigh–Taylor, double Mach reflection,
inviscid Taylor–Green vortex) compare WENO-Z, MDCD-HY, MDAD-HY and ADADF-HY by figures only: no
error norms, convergence study or timings. Only the fourth-order member of the claimed
"arbitrarily high-order" family is built.

# Key claims

Registry notation: grid index $i$ (the source's $j$), reduced wavenumber $\vartheta$ (the
source's $\kappa$), modified wavenumber $\tilde\vartheta=\tilde\vartheta_r+\mathrm i\tilde\vartheta_i$,
CFL number $\nu$ (the source's $N_c$), MDCD parameters $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$, $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$,
scaled derivatives $\mathsf D_p=f^{(p)}\Delta x^p$; page-local $\mathcal G$ (RK3 amplification
factor), $\delta_\nu$, $\mathsf r$ (fully discrete ratio), $\vartheta_{\mathrm{sc}}$,
$\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}}$ (sensors), $\mathfrak s$ (Ren indicator) and
$\mathfrak w$ (blending weight).

1. The ADADF framework constructs "arbitrarily high-order" spatio-temporal optimized schemes whose dispersion and dissipation are set by two free parameters; only the fourth-order six-point member is constructed and tested (Abstract, §1, §3.3). **Stated**
2. With three-stage third-order RK the fully discrete solution is $u_0\mathcal G$, $\mathcal G=1+K+K^2/2+K^3/6$, $K=-\mathrm i\tilde\vartheta\nu$; the total dissipation error is $|\mathcal G|$ and the total dispersion error $\delta_\nu=\arg\mathcal G+\vartheta\nu$ (§3.1, Eqs. (16)–(18)). **Derived**
3. Fully discrete, $\delta_\nu$ and $|\mathcal G|$ each depend on both $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ and $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$, unlike $\tilde\vartheta_r$ and $\tilde\vartheta_i$; the cross-effects are "insignificant" for $c_{\mathrm{disp}}<0.1$, $c_{\mathrm{diss}}<0.012$ and are ignored, a visible deviation appearing only at $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.033$ (§3.1, Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
4. The fully discrete integrated dispersion error $\mathsf J=e^{-\mu_{\mathrm w}\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{\mu_{\mathrm w}(\pi-\vartheta)}(\arg\mathcal G+\nu\vartheta)^2d\vartheta$, $\mu_{\mathrm w}=8$ (Eq. (26)), gives $c_{\mathrm{disp}}=0.0464$ for $\nu\le0.25$ and $c_{\mathrm{disp}}=-0.0426\nu^3+0.0004\nu^2+0.0077\nu+0.0448$ for $\nu>0.25$ (Eq. (27)). **Derived** (numerical optimization and fit)
5. The MDAD sensor $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}=\sqrt{(|\mathsf D_3|+|\mathsf D_4|)/(|\mathsf D_1|+|\mathsf D_2|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm s})}$ (Eq. (11)) loses accuracy rapidly at medium and large $\vartheta$, underestimates it, and so gives too little dissipation through Eq. (15) (§3.2, Fig. 4a). **Shown**
6. Adding a fifth-derivative term (sensor $\vartheta_{\mathrm{sc}}$, Eqs. (29)–(30)) produces strong oscillations of $\vartheta_{\mathrm{sc}}(\vartheta)$, caused by the inconsistency of its three component ratios (Eq. (31)); the first, $\sqrt{|\mathsf D_3|/(|\mathsf D_1|+10^{-3})}$, is the most accurate and is exact for $\vartheta<1.33$ (§3.2, Figs. 4b, 5). **Shown**
7. Re-optimizing the free coefficients of the first- and third-derivative stencils (Eq. (32)) to a 0.6 % relative-error tolerance, after Lele's resolving efficiency, gives $\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}}$ (Eqs. (33)–(34)); its largest reliable wavenumber exceeds 2.08 for amplitudes 5, 0.5 and 0.05, "66.21 % resolving efficiency", against "around 41.06 %" for $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ (§3.2, Fig. 6). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $2.08/\pi=0.662$; and by Taylor moments about $x_{i+1/2}$ the optimized $\mathsf D_1$ stencil of Eq. (34) is consistent with $f'\Delta x$ only to leading error $-\tfrac{29}{600}f'''\Delta x^3$, $\mathsf D_2$ with $f''\Delta x^2$ to error $\tfrac{2287}{7500}f^{(4)}\Delta x^4$, $\mathsf D_3$ with $f'''\Delta x^3$ to error $-\tfrac1{40}f^{(5)}\Delta x^5$; the fourth-derivative stencil is that of Eq. (12).)*
8. On three waveform tests (Eqs. (35)–(37)) $\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}}$ approximates the exact $\vartheta$ better than $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ESW}}$ at high wavenumber, with slight oscillations (§3.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
9. The sensor extends to non-uniform meshes by a Jacobian transformation or fully integrated metrics; this is deferred and only uniform meshes are treated (§3.2). **Stated**
10. The fully discrete dispersion–dissipation condition is $\mathsf r=|\delta_\nu|/\big||\mathcal G|-1\big|\le10$ (Eq. (38)): the larger the total dispersion error, the more dissipation is required (§3.3). **Stated** (design criterion)
11. On the critical surface $\mathsf r=10$, with $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ from Eq. (27), $c_{\mathrm{diss}}$ increases steadily with $\nu$ at large $\vartheta$ and first rises then falls with $\nu$ at small $\vartheta$ (§3.3, Fig. 8; Appendix A, Fig. 26). **Shown**
12. Cubic fits of $c_{\mathrm{diss}}(\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}})$ for $\nu=0.1,0.3,0.5$, with floor 0.001 below 1.711, 1.727 and 1.928 respectively and cap 0.012 above $\pi$ (Eqs. (39)–(41), (A.6)–(A.8)). **Derived** (fit)
13. Adaptive dispersion: $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ is chosen so that $\delta_\nu=\arg\mathcal G+\vartheta\nu=0$ at the sensed wavenumber (Eq. (43)), generalizing the semi-discrete condition $\tilde\vartheta_r=\vartheta$ of Li, Ren & Su (arXiv 2021; Eq. (42)); $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ increases with $\nu$ (§3.3, Fig. 9; Appendix B, Fig. 27). **Derived**
14. Cubic fits of $c_{\mathrm{disp}}(\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}})$ for $\nu=0.1,0.3,0.5$, constant below 0.3 and above the sensor limit 2.08 (Eqs. (44)–(46), (B.3)–(B.5)). **Derived** (fit)
15. The semi-discrete and fully discrete adaptive $c_{\mathrm{disp}}$ differ negligibly at small $\nu$ and significantly at large $\nu$, measured by their relative difference (Eq. (47), Fig. 10). **Shown**
16. Because adaptive dispersion removes much of the dispersion error for $\vartheta\le2.08$, the floor $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.001$ is extended up to $\vartheta^{\mathrm{OPT}}_{\mathrm{sc}}<2.08$ for all three $\nu$ (Eqs. (48)–(50)); the effect on the spectral properties is said to be small and ignored (§3.3). **Stated**
17. ADADF keeps about 65.58 % resolving efficiency for every $\nu$, while the total dispersion errors of MDCD and MDAD grow rapidly with $\nu$; its dissipation is much lower than MDCD's at low $\vartheta$ and grows at large $\vartheta$ (§3.4, Fig. 11). **Shown**
18. ADADF-HY: $\hat f_{i+1/2}=\mathfrak w\,\hat f^{\mathrm{ADADF}}_{i+1/2}+(1-\mathfrak w)\hat f^{\mathrm{WENO\text{-}CU6}}_{i+1/2}$ with $\mathfrak w=\min(1,\mathfrak s_{i+1/2}/\mathfrak s_c)$, $\mathfrak s_c=0.4$, $\mathfrak s_{i+1/2}=\min(\mathfrak s_i,\mathfrak s_{i+1})$ and Ren et al.'s $\mathfrak s_i=(|2\Delta f_{i+1/2}\Delta f_{i-1/2}|+\epsilon_{\mathfrak s})/((\Delta f_{i+1/2})^2+(\Delta f_{i-1/2})^2+\epsilon_{\mathfrak s})$, $\epsilon_{\mathfrak s}=10^{-3}$; WENO-CU6 uses $\alpha_k=d_k\big(C+\tau_6/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)$ with $C=100$, $q=1$, $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ and the MDCD linear weights of Table 1; where $\mathfrak s\ge\mathfrak s_c$ the WENO-CU6 flux is not computed (§3.5–3.6, Eqs. (51)–(57), (60)). **Stated**
19. Multi-wave linear advection ($m=20$ waves, 150 points, $\nu=0.5$, $t=1$): only ADADF-HY reproduces phase and amplitude; with $m=10$ all schemes do (§4.1, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
20. [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) (200 points, $t=1.8$, reference WENO-Z on 3000 points): ADADF-HY resolves crests and troughs best, including the high-wavenumber fluctuation near $x=5.72$ (§4.2, Fig. 15). **Shown**
21. 2D Riemann configurations 3 and 6: ADADF-HY resolves more small-scale structures and vortices; in configurations 12 and 15 all schemes capture the discontinuities (§4.3, Figs. 16–19). **Shown**
22. Kelvin–Helmholtz ($512^2$, $\nu=0.5$, $t=1$): ADADF-HY captures the most small-scale detail (§4.4, Fig. 20). **Shown**
23. Rayleigh–Taylor ($120\times480$, $\nu=0.5$, $t=1.95$): ADADF-HY shows more vortices but a slightly asymmetric density field, which the authors attribute to low dissipation not damping round-off and truncation disturbances; WENO-Z, the most dissipative, stays symmetric (§4.5, Fig. 21). **Shown** (asymmetry) / **Stated** (explanation)
24. Double Mach reflection ($800\times200$, $t=0.2$): all schemes capture the wall jet and Mach stems; ADADF-HY resolves more structures along the contact (§4.6, Figs. 22–23). **Shown**
25. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex ($128^3$, $t=10$): "the ADADF scheme" preserves kinetic energy best, the kinetic energy decays as $t^{-1.2}$, "which agrees well with" Lesieur & Ossia (2000, ref. [40]), the spectrum follows the $-5/3$ slope best and the $Q=3$ iso-surfaces show more fine structure (§4.7, Figs. 24–25). **Shown**
26. ADADF provides "physically consistent dissipation and dispersion" for implicit LES of turbulence (§4.7, §5). **Stated**
27. No timing, order-of-accuracy test or quantitative error norm is reported anywhere in the paper. **Stated** (absence)
