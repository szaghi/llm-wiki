# Summary

The paper builds a positivity-preserving finite-volume [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md)
scheme for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations; for $\mathrm{Re}=\infty$ it is a
positivity-preserving scheme for Euler. It combines two earlier works. The first is the
[hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) HWENO of Zhao, Chen & Qiu (2020), which evolves the
cell average $\bar u_i$ and the first moment $\bar u^{(1)}_i$. That scheme uses a linear
Hermite reconstruction in smooth cells and the nonlinear HWENO reconstruction, with a
limiter on the first moment, only in cells flagged by a
[troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md). The second is
Zhang's positivity framework for Navier–Stokes. It applies a
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)-type flux to the total (advective minus
diffusive) flux, with a coefficient that depends on the viscous stress and heat flux,
and adds the [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md).

The paper's contributions are four. Two theorems (1D and 2D) give sufficient conditions
for the forward-Euler cell averages to stay in $G=\{\rho>0,\ \rho e>0\}$: the
reconstructed traces and a Gauss–Lobatto decomposition residual must lie in $G$, under
a CFL bound proportional to the boundary weight $\varpi=1/(N_{\mathrm{GL}}(N_{\mathrm{GL}}-1))$.
The decomposition is the classical one, not the
[optimal cell-average decomposition](/methods/optimal-cell-average-decomposition.md). A
density-then-internal-energy scaling limiter enforces the conditions from point values
alone. An appendix reconstructs the derivatives needed by the viscous flux, with
nonlinear Jiang–Shu-type [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md).
A time step that is halved and restarted whenever a stage leaves $G$ completes the
[bound-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md) construction with
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Fifth order is observed in 1D and
fourth in 2D. The scheme is robust on the [Lax tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
the [double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md), the
[Leblanc tube](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md), the 1D and 2D
[Sedov blast waves](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) and a Mach 5.09 shock diffraction,
where the plain hybrid HWENO blows up. Hybridization saves 59–90% of the CPU time. The
first-moment limiter is read as [artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's $\mathbf U$ is $u$, its cell
average $\overline{\mathbf U}_i$ is $\bar u_i$, its first moment $\overline{\mathbf V}_i$
is $\bar u^{(1)}_i$, its Lax–Friedrichs-type coefficient $\beta_{i+1/2}$ is
$a_{i+1/2}$ (a generalized speed that includes viscous terms), its Gauss–Lobatto
weights $\hat\omega_\alpha$ are $\mathsf w_\alpha$, with the endpoint weight
$\hat\omega=\varpi$, and its number of Gauss–Lobatto points $N$ is $N_{\mathrm{GL}}$.

1. HWENO schemes have more compact stencils and higher resolution than WENO at the same order, but are less robust and costlier because of the extra derivative or moment equation (§1). **Stated**
2. For linear schemes for scalar diffusion (conventional finite volume, most DG), bound preservation can be enforced as for Euler only up to second order (§1). **Stated** (cited)
3. The hybrid HWENO of Zhao, Chen & Qiu is more robust than the original HWENO but still unstable on many low-density, low-pressure problems; it blows up on Examples 4.3–4.7 for Euler from loss of positivity (§1, §4, p. 12). **Shown** (asserted; no blow-up figure)
4. Unlike the positivity-preserving HWENO of Cai, Zhang & Qiu, which reconstructs from function and derivative averages with two stencil sets, the present scheme reconstructs from zeroth and first moments with one stencil set, and hybridization lowers the cost (§1). **Stated**
5. The 1D semi-discrete scheme evolves $\bar u_i$ and $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ (Eq. (3.2)); the interior flux integral uses four-point Gauss–Lobatto quadrature with weights $\tfrac1{12},\tfrac5{12},\tfrac5{12},\tfrac1{12}$ (Eq. (3.6)). **Derived**
6. With $\rho>0$, $p>0\Leftrightarrow e>0$ for the ideal gas, so positivity of internal energy is enforced; the equivalence fails for other equations of state such as JWL (§3.1, p. 4). **Stated**
7. $\rho e(u)$ is concave in $u$ for $\rho>0$, so $G$ is convex (Eqs. (3.7)–(3.8)). **Stated** ("straightforward to check")
8. **Theorem 1:** for the forward-Euler scheme (3.3) with reconstruction polynomials of degree $k$, $\bar u_i^{n+1}\in G$ if $u^+_{i-1/2},u^-_{i+1/2}\in G$ and $\big(\bar u_i^n-\varpi u^+_{i-1/2}-\varpi u^-_{i+1/2}\big)/(1-2\varpi)\in G$ for all $i$, under $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\max_ia_{i+1/2}\le\varpi=\frac1{N_{\mathrm{GL}}(N_{\mathrm{GL}}-1)}$, $N_{\mathrm{GL}}=\lceil(k+3)/2\rceil$ (Eqs. (3.11)–(3.12)). The update is rewritten as a convex combination and Zhang's lemma $u\in G\Rightarrow u\pm a^{-1}f(u,\nabla u)\in G$ is applied. **Proved**
9. **Theorem 2 (2D):** the same holds for (3.22) when the mean-value state (3.27) and all edge Gauss-point traces lie in $G$, under $\Delta t\big(\frac1{\Delta x}+\frac1{\Delta y}\big)\max_{i,j}\{a_{i+1/2},a_{j+1/2}\}<\varpi$ (Eqs. (3.29)–(3.30)). **Proved** (sketched, "following the same line")
10. The density-then-internal-energy scaling limiter (Eqs. (3.14)–(3.15); (3.31)–(3.32) in 2D) enforces the hypotheses of the theorems from the cell average and point values alone, without the polynomials, and is conservative and high-order accurate (Remarks 3.2, 3.4). **Derived** (accuracy cited)
11. Since SSP-RK3 is a convex combination of forward-Euler steps and $G$ is convex, the theorems "still hold for (2.6) if the CFL is one third of (3.12) and (3.30)" (§3.3). **Stated**. For the same integrator [Wong et al. (2021)](/sources/wong-2021.md) (§5.3) state that the CFL upper limit stays that of forward Euler. *(compiler check, not in the sources: each stage of (2.6) as printed is a convex combination, with non-negative coefficients, of earlier stages and a forward-Euler step of size $\Delta t$, so the convexity argument needs only the forward-Euler bound; the factor one third is sufficient but not required.)*
12. The bounds (3.12)/(3.30) give $\Delta t=O(\Delta x)$, since the coefficient is $O(1)$ for smooth solutions; they do not imply the linear-stability limit $\Delta t=O(\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta x^2)$, so both are needed, and both are sufficient, not necessary (§3.3). **Derived**
13. Time step: $\Delta t=\min\{\frac{a_t}{a}\Delta x,\ b_t\,\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta x^2\}$ with $a_t=1/12$, $b_t=0.001$ for Navier–Stokes, $\Delta t=\frac{\Delta x}{12a}$ for Euler; if a stage cell average leaves $G$, the step is halved and restarted from the first stage, which terminates by Theorems 1–2 (§3.3, Eq. (3.33)). **Derived**
14. Accuracy (Example 4.1, $\mathrm{Re}=100$): 1D $L^1$ order 5.87 at 160 cells and 5.06 at 640 cells ($L^1$ error $2.14\times10^{-10}$); 2D $L^1$ orders 4.01–4.09 from $80^2$ to $320^2$ ($2.70\times10^{-10}$ at $320^2$), read as fifth order in 1D and fourth in 2D, "consistent with the designed order" (Table 4.1). **Shown**
15. The non-hybrid HWENO on zeroth and first moments, without the first-moment modification and without the positivity limiter, is non-oscillatory for the Lax problem at $\mathrm{Re}=100$ but oscillates and loses stability at larger $\mathrm{Re}$ and for Euler (Fig. 4.1, $\mathrm{Re}=100,1000,10^4,\infty$): it "itself is unstable" (Example 4.2). **Shown**
16. On the Lax problem the positivity limiter is never triggered in the hybrid scheme (ratio 0.00%), which the paper credits to the first-moment limiter: "the hybrid HWENO scheme without any PP limiter is already very stable" (Example 4.2, Table 4.2). **Shown**
17. Troubled-cell fractions: Lax 2.43% ($\mathrm{Re}=1000$) and 2.68% ($\infty$); double rarefaction 11.30% / 19.57%; 1D Sedov 11.92% / 14.50%; Leblanc 0.97% / 1.35%; 2D Sedov 13.82% / 14.62%; shock diffraction 4.39% / 5.07% (Examples 4.2–4.7). **Shown**
18. CPU saving of the hybrid over the non-hybrid positivity-preserving HWENO (Table 4.2), at $\mathrm{Re}=\infty$: Lax 69.98%, double rarefaction 59.05%, 1D Sedov 68.86%, Leblanc 88.59%, 2D Sedov 62.64%, shock diffraction 63.76%; at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$: 71.20%, 67.34%, 69.02%, 89.64%, 63.64%, 64.45%. **Shown** (hardware not stated; see Printed slips)
19. The positivity-limiter trigger ratios are identical for the hybrid and non-hybrid schemes at both $\mathrm{Re}$: Lax 0.00%, double rarefaction 10.00%, 1D Sedov 3.00%, Leblanc 0.25%, 2D Sedov 1.09%, shock diffraction 0.23% (Table 4.2). **Shown**
20. The first-moment limiter "simply induces artificial viscosity": with fewer troubled cells at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ than at $\infty$, the $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ discontinuities are slightly sharper, here and in other tests (Example 4.2, §5). **Shown** (qualitative)
21. A small velocity dent in the Lax problem (Fig. 4.2) is a numerical artifact also seen in the finite-volume WENO of Liu, Osher & Chan (Example 4.2). **Shown**
22. The scheme is "much more robust" than the hybrid HWENO for Euler, more efficient than conventional HWENO, and better resolved at high $\mathrm{Re}$ "due to less artificial viscosity" (§5). **Shown**
23. In 2D the derivative reconstruction combines eight incomplete Hermite cubics into a third-order approximation of $\partial_xu$, $\partial_yu$ at each point; exactness on $\{1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2,x^3,y^3\}$ needs $\sum_nd^{(k)}_{xn}=1$, two more constraints for $x^2y$, $xy^2$ leave 5 free parameters per group, fixed by minimising $\sum_n(d^{(k)}_{xn})^2$ (Appendix A.2). **Derived**
24. Future work: unstructured meshes and a positivity-preserving finite-difference WENO for compressible Navier–Stokes (§5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (3.7): "$\rho e(\mathbf U)=(\gamma-1)(E-\frac12\frac{m^2}{\rho})$" is the pressure; the 2D Eq. (3.25) prints $\rho e=E-\frac12\frac{m^2+n^2}{\rho}$ without $(\gamma-1)$. Harmless for the sign condition.
- §3.1, p. 6: "$t^k_\varepsilon=\frac{\rho e(\hat{\mathbf q}^k)-\varepsilon}{\rho e(\overline{\mathbf P}_i)-\rho e(\hat{\mathbf q}^k)}$"; the numerator should be $\rho e(\overline{\mathbf P}_i)-\varepsilon$, as in (3.15) and the 2D version on p. 10. As printed the factor is negative whenever it is triggered.
- p. 6 "$\tilde{\mathbf U}^\pm_{i\mp1/2}=\tilde{\mathbf P}_i(x_{j\mp1/2})$" and p. 11 "$\hat{\mathcal H}^+_{j-1/2,\beta}$": index $j$ for $i$. Eq. (3.24): "$\mathbf U^+_{\beta,j+1/2}-\mathbf U^-_{\beta,j-1/2}$", second index should be $j+\frac12$.
- p. 10, $\hat{\mathbf q}^9_{ij}$: "$\frac{\lambda_1}{\mu}$", "$\frac{\lambda_2}{\mu}$" with an undefined $\mu$; (3.26) has $\mu_1$, $\mu_2$.
- CFL non-strict "$\le\hat\omega$" in (3.12), strict "$<\hat\omega$" in (3.30).
- Eq. (4.1), Lax problem: both states printed on "$x\in[0,5)$" and "$x\in[0,5]$"; the figures show $[-5,5]$.
- Example 4.2 text: the hybrid saves "about 77.68% and 75.08%" for $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ and $\infty$ "as shown in Table 4.2", which gives 71.20% and 69.98%.
- §4 states quintic reconstruction in 1D; Example 4.1 says "the reconstruction polynomial has degree four in one dimension". Example 4.1 gives no final time.
- Fig. 4.3 caption: "Re = ∞ (right) and 1000 (left)", the reverse of Figs. 4.2 and 4.4–4.7.
- Appendix A.2: the introduction uses HWENO "only when $G_\ell$ is in the interior of a troubled cell", Step A.4 "only when $G_\ell$ is located on the cell boundary of a troubled cell"; the 1D rule agrees with Step A.4.
- Step A.2, p. 21: the second formula is labelled "$(u_x)^+_{i+1/2}=p_0'(x_{i+1/2})$", the left-biased state $(u_x)^-_{i+1/2}$ by the convention of Step A.1.
- Step A.3, p. 21: the coefficients "$\frac1{72}$" and "$\big(\frac{99\sqrt5}{360}-\frac1{72}\big)$" lack the factor $1/\Delta x$ that a derivative needs.
- Typos: "Double rarefraction problem" (Table 4.2), "Navies-Stokes" (p. 11), "Gauss-Labotto" (Remarks 3.1, 3.3), "HWNEO" (p. 12), "Weighted Essential Non-oscillatory" (§1).
