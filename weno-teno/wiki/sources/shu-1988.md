# Summary

Shu & Osher (1988) simplify the ENO and TVD schemes of the time in two independent ways,
both aimed at several space dimensions and at problems with forcing terms.

**Time discretization (§II).** The semi-discrete system $\frac{d}{dt}u=\mathcal L(u)$ is
advanced by explicit Runge–Kutta methods rewritten as
$u^{(i)}=\sum_{k<i}\big[\alpha_{ik}u^{(k)}+\beta_{ik}\Delta t\,\mathcal L(u^{(k)})\big]$ with
$\alpha_{ik}\ge0$, $\sum_k\alpha_{ik}=1$. When every $\beta_{ik}\ge0$ each stage is a convex
combination of forward-Euler steps, so the scheme is TVD (or TVB) under the forward-Euler
restriction times $\min\alpha_{ik}/\lvert\beta_{ik}\rvert$ (Prop. 2.1). Negative $\beta_{ik}$
are handled by an auxiliary operator that is TVD for the time-reversed problem. The
three-stage third-order scheme (2.18) with CFL coefficient 1 is the
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stepper of most of this bundle; the paper calls
these methods "TVD Runge–Kutta". Second-, fourth- and a six-stage "fifth-order" scheme are
printed too.

**Flux-based ENO (§III).** Instead of reconstructing from cell averages, the
[conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
takes its flux from point values. With the global
[Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) $f^\pm=\frac12(f\pm au)$, each split
flux is interpolated by a degree-$2m$ polynomial on $2m+1$ points chosen by the
[ENO](/methods/eno-reconstruction.md) divided-difference rule, starting upwind; the interface
flux is the interpolant at $x_{i+1/2}$ plus Taylor corrections with $-\frac1{24}$ and
$\frac7{5760}$, the coefficients later reused by [alternative WENO](/methods/alternative-weno.md).
The paper adds a TVB cap, an abandoned non-conservative variant (which, in the paper's words, "gives the wrong shock location" on Burgers' equation, p. 20;
see the [Lax–Wendroff theorem](/results/lax-wendroff-theorem.md)),
[flux-splitting](/methods/flux-vector-splitting.md) building blocks other than Lax–Friedrichs, and a
[characteristic](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) version for systems.

§IV tests third- and fourth-order schemes on 1D and 2D
[Burgers problems](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md), a rotated square advected to $t=16$,
[nonconvex Riemann problems](/benchmarks/nonconvex-scalar-riemann-problem.md) and
[Buckley–Leverett](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md). The paper also recalls that
[TVD](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md) schemes degenerate to first order at extrema
([critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)) and describes ENO by the
[ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) of high order up to discontinuities.

# Key claims

Registry notation: grid index $i$ (source $j$), semi-discrete operator $\mathcal L$ (source $L$;
the source's $\mathcal L$ is the exact spatial operator), maximum wave speed $a$ of the splitting
(source $\alpha$), eigenvectors $R$, $L$ (source $r^{(i)}$, $\ell^{(i)}$). Page-local:
$\alpha_{ik}$, $\beta_{ik}$ the Shu–Osher-form coefficients (double subscripts, distinct from the
weights $\alpha_k$ and indicators $\beta_k$), $\tilde{\mathcal L}$ the operator TVD for the
time-reversed problem (source $\tilde L$), $\Delta t_{\mathrm{FE}}$ the forward-Euler TVD limit
(source $\lambda\le\lambda_0$), "CFL coefficient" the factor of $\Delta t_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (source
"CFL#"), $p^\pm_i$ the ENO interpolants of $f^\pm$, $a_{2k}$ the Taylor-correction constants.

1. A Lax–Wendroff-type time discretization of ENO/TVD schemes is complicated to program in several dimensions with forcing terms, it is hard to prove TVD/TVB for it, and only second-order-in-time TVD/TVB schemes of this type exist (§I(1), pp. 4–5). **Stated**
2. Linear stability analysis assumes a fixed stencil and "does not apply to ENO schemes at all", which motivates a nonlinear (TVD) analysis of the time discretization (§I(1), p. 5). **Stated**
3. Any explicit Runge–Kutta method (2.10) can be rewritten, for any $\alpha_{ik}\ge0$ with $\sum_k\alpha_{ik}=1$, as $u^{(i)}=\sum_{k=0}^{i-1}\big[\alpha_{ik}u^{(k)}+\beta_{ik}\Delta t\,\mathcal L(u^{(k)})\big]$ (2.12) with $\beta_{ik}=c_{ik}-\sum_{l=k+1}^{i-1}c_{lk}\alpha_{il}$ (§II, p. 9). **Derived**
4. Prop. 2.1: scheme (2.12) is TVD under $\Delta t\le\Delta t_{\mathrm{FE}}\min_{i,k}\alpha_{ik}/\lvert\beta_{ik}\rvert$ (2.13), provided $\mathcal L$ is replaced by $\tilde{\mathcal L}$ wherever $\beta_{ik}<0$; (2.12) is then a convex combination of TVD (or TVB) operators (p. 10). **Proved** (one-line argument)
5. Remark 2.1: the total variation may be replaced by any convex functional $G\ge0$ on the grid-function space; then $G(u^n)\le G(u^0)$ and the formal order is unchanged (p. 10). **Stated**
6. The optimal second-order scheme (2.15) is Heun's (modified Euler) method, CFL coefficient 1, no $\tilde{\mathcal L}$ (p. 11). **Derived** (Taylor analysis omitted)
7. "Extensive searching" gives the third-order scheme (2.18), $u^{(1)}=u^n+\Delta t\mathcal L(u^n)$, $u^{(2)}=\frac34u^n+\frac14u^{(1)}+\frac14\Delta t\mathcal L(u^{(1)})$, $u^{n+1}=\frac13u^n+\frac23u^{(2)}+\frac23\Delta t\mathcal L(u^{(2)})$, CFL coefficient 1, no $\tilde{\mathcal L}$; each stage needs only the last operator evaluation, which saves storage; the authors could not identify it with a classical third-order method, and the classical third-order methods of Lambert give negative $\beta_{ik}$ and small CFL coefficients (p. 12). **Derived**
8. At fourth order the accuracy conditions are 7 equations in 16 unknowns; searching "seems to indicate" that negative $\beta_{ik}$ cannot be avoided (p. 12). **Stated**
9. Classical RK4 in form (2.12) is (2.20), CFL coefficient $\frac23$, needing $\tilde{\mathcal L}(u^{(0)})$ and $\tilde{\mathcal L}(u^{(1)})$; a variant (2.21) of its third stage raises the coefficient to $\frac{137}{200}$ (p. 13). **Derived**
10. A second fourth-order scheme (2.22) has CFL coefficient 0.87 and still needs $\tilde{\mathcal L}(u^{(0)})$, $\tilde{\mathcal L}(u^{(1)})$ (p. 13). **Derived**
11. The six-stage method of Lambert (p. 143), rewritten as (2.23) and labelled "fifth order", has CFL coefficient $\frac7{30}$ and needs $\tilde{\mathcal L}$ at $u^{(0)},u^{(1)},u^{(3)}$ (p. 14). **Stated** (see Printed slips)
12. Taylor expansion gives constants $a_{2k}$ such that $\hat f_{i+1/2}=f_{i+1/2}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}a_{2k}\Delta x^{2k}(\partial_x^{2k}f)_{i+1/2}+O(\Delta x^{2m+1})$ (3.5) makes the conservative scheme of order $2m$, "for example $a_2=-\frac1{24}$, $a_4=\frac7{5760}$" (p. 15). **Derived**
13. Flux-based ENO (3.6)–(3.15): $\hat f_{i+1/2}=\hat f^+_{i+1/2}+\hat f^-_{i+1/2}$, each the value at $x_{i+1/2}$ of a degree-$2m$ interpolant $p^\pm_i$ of the point values $f^\pm(u_l)$ plus the corrections $\sum a_{2k}\Delta x^{2k}\partial_x^{2k}p^\pm_i$; the stencil starts at $x_i$ for $f^+$ and $x_{i+1}$ for $f^-$ and grows by one point at a time toward the smaller divided difference (ties go left); order $2m$ follows when the interpolants are accurate to $O(\Delta x^{2m+1})$ (pp. 15–17). **Derived**
14. Remark 3.1(a): the first-order member of the family is the Lax–Friedrichs scheme $\hat f_{i+1/2}=f^+_i+f^-_{i+1}$ (3.4) (p. 17). **Stated**
15. Remark 3.1(b): the second-order member is "similar to the usual minmod second order TVD scheme", minmod being replaced by "choosing the value closer to zero", and "is still a TVD method" (p. 18). **Stated** (derivation omitted)
16. Remark 3.1(c): for $\partial_tu+c\,\partial_xu=0$ the schemes coincide with the cell-average ENO schemes of [Harten et al. (1987a)](/sources/harten-1987.md) with primitive-function reconstruction, apart from the stencil choice, which there uses divided differences of cell averages of $u$ and here of $f^\pm(u)$ (p. 18). **Stated** (derivation omitted)
17. No rigorous TVB theory exists for these ENO schemes; capping the divided differences (3.16) makes the flux the second-order TVD flux plus $O(\Delta x^2)$ (3.17), hence TVB; numerically the cap makes "no essential difference", and the authors "strongly believe" the uncapped schemes are TVB "for most practical problems" (pp. 18–19). **Derived** (with the cap) / **Stated** (without)
18. A non-conservative variant (3.18) is, with the cap, "essentially conservative" (3.19): it keeps the conclusion of the Lax–Wendroff theorem, the entropy condition and the convergence properties of first-order monotone schemes (p. 20). **Stated**
19. Without the cap, the variant (3.18) puts the shock of a Burgers Riemann problem ($u_L=\frac12$, $u_R=-1$, fifth-order versions) in the wrong place; with the cap the location is right but the shock is severely smeared, so the variant was abandoned (p. 20). **Shown** (no figure or table printed)
20. Any monotone flux or E-flux $h$ can replace the Lax–Friedrichs building block through the flux increments (3.20)–(3.21); accuracy needs $h$ smooth to the order of the scheme; Godunov, Engquist–Osher and entropy-fixed Roe fluxes are not smooth at sonic points, and ENO schemes built on them "will lose accuracy at sonic points", whereas Lax–Friedrichs is $C^\infty$ (pp. 20–22). **Stated**
21. Higher-order ENO with the Lax–Friedrichs building block is only "slightly inferior" to upwind building blocks, a much smaller gap than at first order (p. 21). **Stated**
22. In $d$ dimensions the 1D procedure is applied to each $\partial_{x_d}f^{(d)}$ and the Runge–Kutta CFL coefficients shrink by $1/d$ (p. 22); in Ex. 3 the 1D CFL numbers in 2D "led to instability–overflow" (p. 27). **Stated** / **Shown**
23. Ex. 1 (1D Burgers, smooth, $t=0.3$) shows "full order of accuracy in both $L_1$ and $L_\infty$": printed orders at $\Delta x=1/40$ are 2.89 ($L_\infty$) and 3.13 ($L_1$) for the third-order scheme, 4.35 and 4.76 for the fourth-order one (p. 24, Table 1). **Shown**
24. After the shock forms there are no oscillations, and errors at distance $\ge0.1$ from the shock have the size of the smooth case (Table 2, Figs. 1–12). **Shown**
25. Ex. 2 (2D Burgers, dimension by dimension, CFL 0.4 and 0.3) gives "essentially the same results as in the 1-dim Example 1" (Tables 3–4). **Shown**
26. Ex. 3 (rotated square, $t=16$): the fourth-order scheme resolves the discontinuities better than the third-order one, with "negligible" over- and undershoots (Figs. 13–20). **Shown**
27. Ex. 4: the schemes converge to the entropy solutions of the quartic flux $f(u)=\frac14(u^2-1)(u^2-4)$ and of Buckley–Leverett; the Lax–Friedrichs building block smears more than Engquist–Osher (Figs. 21–38). **Shown** (entropy convergence not proved)

Setting: TV, TVD and TVB are defined by (1.5)–(1.7); order is meant in the sense of local
truncation error (p. 3). Tested schemes are named "$p$-$q$-LF-ENO" ($p$th order in time, $q$th in
space, Lax–Friedrichs building block) and "3-3-EO-ENO"; time steppers (2.18) and (2.20); CFL 0.8
(third order) and 0.6 (fourth order) in 1D, halved in 2D. For systems the splitting constant
becomes a constant matrix $R(\bar u)\,\mathrm{diag}(\bar\lambda_l)\,L(\bar u)$ frozen at one state
with $\bar\lambda_l\ge\lvert\lambda_l(u)\rvert$ "throughout the region", and the characteristic
projection uses the eigenvectors of $A(u_i)$ at the node; Euler computations are "work currently
under way" (pp. 22–23).

# Printed slips

- **(2.23), the "fifth-order" scheme** (p. 14): in Butcher form the printed coefficients give
  $b^\top Ac=\frac8{45}\ne\frac16$, so the scheme as printed satisfies the order conditions only
  through order 2; its CFL coefficient $\frac7{30}=\min\alpha_{ik}/\lvert\beta_{ik}\rvert$
  (stage 5, $\frac{87/200}{261/140}$) is reproduced. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:
  conversion of the transcribed Shu–Osher form to Butcher weights
  $b=(\frac7{90},0,\frac{16}{45},\frac2{15},\frac{16}{45},\frac7{90})$ and abscissae
  $c=(0,\frac12,\frac14,\frac12,\frac34,1)$.)* Lambert's original was not consulted.
- **(2.21)** (p. 13) prints the last term of the stage defining $u^{(3)}$ as
  "$+\Delta t\,L(u^{(3)})$", which makes the stage implicit; with $\mathcal L(u^{(2)})$ in its place
  and the other stages of (2.20), the scheme is classical RK4 and the minimum ratio is exactly
  $\frac{137}{200}$, the printed CFL coefficient. *(Compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- **Stage-count index** (§II, pp. 8–14): (2.10) runs $i=1,\dots,m$ with $u^{(m)}=u^{n+1}$, but the
  cases are headed "Second order case, $m=1$" (final stage $u^{(2)}$), "$m=2$" ($u^{(3)}$),
  "$m=3$" ($u^{(4)}$) and "Fifth order case, $m=5$" ($u^{(6)}$).
- **Table 1**, 4-4-LF-ENO, $L_\infty$, $\Delta x=1/40$: printed "5.4-5" with order 4.35.
  *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\log_2(1.1\times10^{-4}/5.4\times10^{-5})=1.03$, while
  $5.4\times10^{-6}$ gives 4.35.)* All other orders of Tables 1 and 3 agree with the printed errors
  to mantissa rounding.
- **Systems paragraph** (pp. 22–23): eigenvalues and eigenvectors are indexed $1,\dots,d$, while
  (1.1) uses $d$ for the space dimension and $m$ for the system size.
- p. 19: "$f^-[u(x_j),(x_{j+1})]$" (a $u$ is missing).
- Ex. 2 (pp. 25–26): the near-shock error lists of the fourth-order scheme at $t=2/\pi$ and both
  lists at $t=1.1$ are digit-for-digit those of the 1D Ex. 1; the paper does not comment.
- Odd orders: §III constructs only even orders $2m$, while §IV runs "third order in space" and p. 20
  "fifth order versions"; the construction of an odd-order member is not printed.
- Typos: "Range-Kutta" (p. 22), "assoicate" (p. 20), "extremacy" (p. 3), equation label "((4.4))"
  (p. 27); Ex. 1 cites "(2.19)–(2.20)" for the third- and fourth-order steppers.
