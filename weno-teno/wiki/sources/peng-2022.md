# Summary

The paper extends [AMROC](/software/amroc.md), a
[block-structured AMR](/methods/adaptive-mesh-refinement.md) framework with Clawpack-type
finite-volume solvers for thermally perfect, reacting
[multispecies gas mixtures](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md), to structured
curvilinear ("mapped") meshes. The aim is unsteady shock-induced combustion in front of a
blunt body without the cost of the [level-set/ghost-fluid](/methods/level-set-method.md)
embedded boundaries that AMROC otherwise uses. It is refinement by patches (h-adaptation),
in contrast to the [moving-mesh redistribution](/methods/adaptive-moving-mesh.md) the paper
cites for the same physics.

The discretization is a second-order cell-centred finite-volume scheme in physical space,
written with a capacity function (ratio of physical to computational cell area). Fluxes are
grid-aligned by rotating the momentum into the face-normal frame: AUSM (Liou–Steffen) or a
hybrid Roe/[HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) solver with multidimensional entropy
correction and mass-fraction positivity correction. Reconstruction is
[MUSCL–Hancock](/methods/muscl-hancock.md) with the minmod
[limiter](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md), with slopes divided by physical
centroid-to-face distances. Chemistry (detailed H$_2$ mechanisms) enters by Strang splitting
and a semi-implicit fourth-order generalized Runge–Kutta integrator (GRK4A). The AMR changes are an
area-weighted conservative restriction and a bilinear prolongation whose factors come from a
Newton solve of the bilinear map of physical cell centres. Refinement is flagged by scaled
gradients and by a Richardson estimate of the truncation error of the species mass fractions.
The paper does not discuss [free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md).

Verification uses [manufactured solutions](/definitions/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md)
on stretched, skewed and distorted meshes with three static levels, a multispecies
[shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), a multispecies shock box (a
[two-dimensional Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) in a closed
box) and a 2D cellular H$_2$–O$_2$–Ar detonation. Validation uses Lehr's ballistic
[shock-induced combustion](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md) experiments at Mach 4.18,
4.48 and 5.04. The oscillation frequencies at the stagnation point match experiment within a
few per cent with the right mechanism, and AMR saves a factor 1.74 in CPU time at the finest
resolution.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): conserved state $u=(\rho Y_1,\dots,\rho
Y_{N_{sp}},\rho v,E)$, capacity function $\mathsf k_{ij}$, limiter $\psi$, MUSCL parameter
$\kappa_{\mathrm M}$, interface states $u^\mp_{i+1/2}$.

1. AMROC-Clawpack runs block-structured AMR on a mapped curvilinear mesh with its data layout unchanged; the mapping is applied to the refined computational coordinates of every level (§§2.5, 5). **Stated**
2. The finite-volume update is done in physical space with a capacity function $\mathsf k_{ij}$, the ratio of physical to computational cell area (Eq. (12)), rather than by transforming the equations to computational space (§2.2). **Derived**
3. Fluxes are grid-aligned by rotating the velocity with a matrix $T_S$ built from the face normal $(n_x,n_y)$ (Eqs. (13)–(14)); AUSM and a hybrid Roe/HLL scheme with multidimensional entropy correction and mass-fraction positivity correction are available (§2.3). **Stated**
4. On the mapped mesh the MUSCL slopes divide differences by physical centroid-to-face distances, $\delta_{i,j}=(u_{i+1,j}-u_{i,j})/(L^-_{i+1,j}+L^+_{i,j})$ (Eq. (18)), and the reconstructions (Eqs. (19)–(20)) weight the slopes by $L^\pm_{i,j}$; minmod limiter and $\kappa_{\mathrm M}=0$ (§2.4). **Derived** (see Printed slips)
5. MUSCL–Hancock gives a scheme second-order accurate in space and time (§2.4). **Stated**
6. Restriction on the mapped mesh is the area-weighted mean (Eq. (29)); it is conservative even when refined cells are not aligned with their coarse cell, but "may cause accuracy losses when the total area of refined cells are not equal to the area of their coarse grid cells" (§2.5.2). **Stated**
7. Prolongation is bilinear interpolation (Eq. (30)) with factors $f_1,f_2$ found by solving the bilinear map of physical cell centres (Eq. (31)) by Newton–Raphson, with gradient descent as fallback; on a Cartesian mesh with refinement factor 2, $f_1=f_2=0.75$ (§2.5.2, Fig. 2). **Stated**
8. Cells are flagged (a) where undivided differences of a scalar in four directions exceed a threshold (Eq. (21)), and (b) by a Richardson estimate of the local truncation error (Eqs. (22)–(27)), applied to the species mass fractions with a scaling of 1% of the maximum Chapman–Jouguet mass fraction (§2.5.1). **Derived**
9. AMR parameters for all cases unless stated: regridding every coarser-level step, buffer width 2 cells, clustering efficiency 0.7 (§2.5). **Stated**
10. Manufactured solutions on stretched, skewed and distorted meshes with three static levels: the $L_2$ errors of $\rho$, $Y_{\mathrm O_2}$, $\rho v_1$ and $E$ decrease over base resolutions 12, 24, 48, 96, and the paper concludes that "the present solver can achieve second order of accuracy … on a hierarchically refined mapped mesh" (§3.1, Fig. 5). **Shown** The paper tabulates no observed order; the second-order conclusion rests on the plotted slopes.
11. The multispecies shock tube (H$_2$:O$_2$:Ar = 2:1:7) agrees with published results on several meshes (§3.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
12. The multispecies shock box at 2 ms agrees with Owen et al. in pressure and Mach number; the reflected shock is captured by the finest level of the distorted mesh (§3.3, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
13. 2D cellular detonation (H$_2$:O$_2$:Ar 2:1:7, 298 K, 10 kPa, 9 species and 34 reactions): detonation speed 1645.24 m/s (Cartesian) and 1641.82 m/s (stretched mesh) against the Chapman–Jouguet 1629.34 m/s, at up to 56.2 points per induction length (0.878 mm) (§3.4). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
14. The computed detonation cell size, 8–10 mm, is close to the 11.4 mm of the Gavrikov correlation but much smaller than the extrapolated experimental 40 mm, a gap attributed (after earlier work) to wall heat loss in experiments (§3.4, Fig. 11). **Shown** (explanation **Stated**)
15. Mesh study, Lehr Mach 4.18 with AUSM: without AMR (14 μm) the reaction front oscillates at a wrong 40 kHz and the bow-shock oscillation is missed; with 2 or 3 levels the frequency is 160 kHz, with 4 levels 155 kHz, against 148 kHz in experiment; three levels are chosen (§4.1, Figs. 13–15). **Shown**
16. CPU time from 0 to 0.2 ms at Mach 4.18, uniform against adaptive: 240.0 against 308.6 cpu·h at 7 μm, 1286.7 against 1193.5 at 3.5 μm, 9590.6 against 5521.6 at 1.75 μm; AMR pays off only at high resolution because the domain is already tight (§4.1, Table 3). **Shown**
17. Refinement-criterion study (Mach 4.18, Roe/HLL, 3 levels): criteria 1, 2, 3 cost 974.2, 1622.8 and 2424.0 cpu·h; criterion 2 is chosen; the density gradient decides whether contacts are refined (§4.2, Table 4, Fig. 18). **Shown**
18. Oscillation frequencies (kHz) at Mach 4.18 / 4.48: experiment 148 / 425; Jachimowski with AUSM 160 / 425 and with Roe/HLL 165 / 430; UCSD-2016 with AUSM 145 / 395 and with Roe/HLL 155 / 395 (§4.3, Table 5). **Shown**
19. At Mach 4.48 the UCSD mechanism produces a mode coupling a high- and a low-frequency oscillation with both fluxes, while Jachimowski gives a stable high-frequency oscillation; AUSM gives more regular oscillations than Roe/HLL at Mach 4.18 (§4.3, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
20. Superdetonative Mach 5.04 (Roe/HLL, Jachimowski): 1155 kHz against 1040 kHz in experiment; finer meshes make the simulation unstable (the reaction front couples to the bow shock and triggers a detonation), attributed to the missing physical viscosity and to errors of the chemistry (§4.3, Fig. 21). **Shown** (causes **Stated**)
21. The bow-shock stand-off on the AMR mapped mesh is "nearly the same" as in Lehr's schlieren images; the Mach 4.48 reaction-front offset is attributed to the 12° viewing angle of the experimental image (§4.1, Fig. 16). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (2): the axisymmetric source $S_{\rm axis}$ is printed $(0,\dots,0,p,0,0)^T$, which puts $p$ in the $\rho u$ row. With the switch $\varpi_a=(1-\varpi)+\varpi y$ of Eq. (3), $y$ is the radial coordinate, and $\partial_y(yp)=y\,\partial_yp+p$ puts the geometric source in the $\rho v$ row. *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- Eqs. (19)–(20): both face values weight the slope $\delta_{i-1}$ by $L^-_{i,j}$ and $\delta_i$ by $L^+_{i,j}$. For linear data along a grid line ($\delta_{i-1}=\delta_i=b$, minmod inactive, $\kappa_{\mathrm M}=0$), Eq. (19) gives the left-face value $u_{i,j}-\tfrac12b(L^-_{i,j}+L^+_{i,j})$, where the exact value is $u_{i,j}-bL^-_{i,j}$; the two agree only when $L^-_{i,j}=L^+_{i,j}$. Either the extrapolation distances are misprinted or the reconstruction is not exact for linear data on stretched meshes; the paper does not say which. *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- §2.5: "the block-structured adaptive mesh refinement technique (SAMR) proposed by Berger and Colella [16]"; [16] is Berger & Oliger, Berger & Colella is [17].
- §2.3: the rotation of the velocities to the face frame is credited to [3], which is Lehr's experiments; [30] (Toro) is cited for $T_S$ in the next sentence.
- Ref. [31] (Deiterding 2011, ESAIM Proc.) is never cited in the text.
- §3.4: the base mesh $2000\times128$ on $1000\times32$ mm has $\Delta x=0.5$ mm and $\Delta y=0.25$ mm; five levels (factor 2, levels counted from the base as in §3.2) give finest cells of $0.03125\times0.015625$ mm, so the printed 56.2 points per induction length hold only in $y$; along the propagation direction there are $0.878/0.03125\approx28.1$. *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- Eq. (7) is called a "mass generation rate" but lacks the molar-mass factor $W_i$; as printed it is a molar rate. *(compiler check, not in the sources: units of the printed right-hand side, mol m$^{-3}$ s$^{-1}$.)*
- Table 4 caption: "shock-induced steady combustion" for the unsteady Mach 4.18 case.
- The Conclusion speaks of "numerical pressure oscillation frequencies"; §4.1 and Table 5 give frequencies of the H$_2$O mass fraction at the stagnation point.
- §2.5.1 defines $S_{Y_i}$ as 1% of the maximum Chapman–Jouguet mass fraction, while Tables 1–2 list explicit per-species values; the paper does not say whether these are the 1% values.
- Ref. [34] prints the authors "Press, W.H., William, H., Teukolsky, S.A., Vetterling, W.T., Saul, A. and Flannery, B.P.".
