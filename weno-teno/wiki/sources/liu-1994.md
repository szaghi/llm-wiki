# Summary

Liu et al. (1994) introduce **weighted ENO (WENO)** schemes as a modification of the
cell-average [ENO reconstruction](/methods/eno-reconstruction.md) of Harten & Osher and
[Harten et al. (1987a)](/sources/harten-1987.md). Only the reconstruction changes: instead of the
single smoothest of the $r$ candidate stencils, the reconstruction in each cell is a convex
combination of all $r$ candidate polynomials of degree $r-1$, obtained from the primitive
function. The weights are $\alpha_k=C_k/(\epsilon+\beta_k)^r$ with $\epsilon=10^{-5}$, where the
[smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) $\beta_k$ is a sum of averaged squared
undivided differences of the cell averages. The authors define the
[ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) of a set of weights asymptotically: normalised
weight $O(1)$ on smooth stencils and at most $O(\Delta x^r)$ on discontinuous ones, which their
weights satisfy.

The constants $C_k$ are chosen, from the sign of $f'(\bar u_i)$, so that the leading truncation
error cancels at the upwind end point of each cell. The semi-discrete finite-volume operator,
built with a two-point [monotone flux](/definitions/monotone-scheme.md) (Engquist–Osher,
Godunov, or [Roe](/methods/roe-riemann-solver.md) with an entropy fix that falls back to
[local Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)), is then of order $r+1$ away from sonic
points, "one order of improvement" over ENO; it is advanced by the TVD
[Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) schemes of [Shu & Osher (1988)](/sources/shu-1988.md).
For $r=3$ the normalised constants coincide with the linear weights that
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) later derives from a fifth-order target, although
they are derived here from a different requirement.

Tests (linear advection of smooth and discontinuous data, [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md),
[nonconvex Riemann problems](/benchmarks/nonconvex-scalar-riemann-problem.md),
[Buckley–Leverett](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md), the
[Sod and Lax tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and smooth Euler data) show about
fourth order for $r=2$ and fifth order for $r=3$, one order above the analysis, and no
accuracy loss on $\sin^4(\pi x)$ data, where standard ENO "experienced an (easily fixed) loss of
accuracy" (see [critical-point accuracy](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)). The acknowledgement credits
C.-W. Shu with the squared form of the indicator; the authors first used absolute values.

# Key claims

Registry notation: cell index $i$ (source $j$), $\Delta x$, $\Delta t$ (source $h$, $\tau$),
smoothness indicator $\beta_k$ of the $k$th candidate stencil of cell $i$ (source $IS_{j+k}$,
$k=0$ the leftmost), unnormalised and normalised weights $\alpha_k$, $\omega_k$ (source
$\alpha^j_k$, $\alpha^j_k/\sum_l\alpha^j_l$), weight exponent $q$ (here $q=r$), two-point flux
$h(u^-,u^+)$ (source $\tilde h$), Lax–Friedrichs coefficient $a$ (source $\beta$). Page-local:
$C_k$ the unnormalised constants of the weights (source $C^j_k$; their normalisation plays the
role of $d_k$), $R_i(x)$ the reconstruction in cell $i$, $W$ the primitive function, $x^*_i$ the
end point where the higher accuracy is enforced, $\mathcal L_i$ the spatial operator.

1. WENO differs from cell-average ENO only in the reconstruction: a convex combination of all $r$ candidate polynomials replaces the smoothest one, with weights that make discontinuous stencils contribute "essentially" nothing (Abstract, §1, §3.3). **Stated** (design)
2. The convex combination cancels truncation errors of the candidates and "improves the order of accuracy by one" (Abstract, §1, §3.4). **Derived**
3. WENO's "smoother dependence on data" may lessen ENO's oscillatory behaviour near convergence and "may help in obtaining a convergence proof" (§1). **Stated**
4. Spatial operator $\mathcal L_i(\bar u)=-\frac1{\Delta x}\big[h\big(R_i(x_{i+1/2}),R_{i+1}(x_{i+1/2})\big)-h\big(R_{i-1}(x_{i-1/2}),R_i(x_{i-1/2})\big)\big]$ with $h$ a two-point Lipschitz monotone flux (2.7); if $u=R_i+O(\Delta x^r)$ in $I_i$ and $O(\Delta x^{r+1})$ at one chosen end point, then $\frac{d}{dt}\bar u_i=\mathcal L_i(\bar u)+O(\Delta x^{r+1})$ away from sonic points and $O(\Delta x^r)$ near them (2.8)–(2.10). **Derived**
5. Because of (2.10), time integration needs the $(r+1)$th-order TVD Runge–Kutta schemes of Shu & Osher (1988); third- and fourth-order versions are printed (§2, p. 202). **Stated**
6. The reconstruction is conservative, $\frac1{\Delta x}\int_{I_i}R_i\,dx=\bar u_i$, whatever the weights, because every candidate interpolates $W$ at both ends of $I_i$ (3.2), (3.6)–(3.7). **Proved** (two-line argument)
7. $R_i(x)=u(x,t)+O(\Delta x^r)$ in smooth regions for any positive weights (§3.3). **Stated**
8. Definition 1: $R_i$ has the "ENO property" if $\omega_k=O(1)$ when stencil $k$ lies in a smooth region (3.8a) and $\omega_k\le O(\Delta x^r)$ when it lies in a discontinuous region (3.8b) (§3.3, p. 203). **Definition**
9. $\beta_k\approx O(1)$ if $u$ is discontinuous on the stencil and $\beta_k\approx O(\Delta x^2)$ if it is continuous (§3.2, p. 203). **Stated**
10. With $\alpha_k=C_k/(\epsilon+\beta_k)^r$ and $\epsilon=10^{-5}$, the normalised weight of a discontinuous stencil is $\le\max(O(\epsilon^r),O(\Delta x^{2r}))$, so the weights (3.9) satisfy the ENO property "($O(\epsilon^r)\le O(10^{-10})$)", assuming at least one smooth stencil (p. 203). **Derived**
11. $\epsilon$ is added because $\beta_k$ "could be zero and $1/x$ is too sensitive as $x$ is near zero" (p. 203). **Stated**
12. For $u\in C^{r+1}$ on the big stencil: $\lvert\beta_k\rvert\le O(\Delta x^2)$, $\lvert\beta_k-\beta_0\rvert\le O(\Delta x^2)$, the error coefficients are $O(\Delta x^r)$, and divided differences of $W$ on neighbouring stencils differ by $O(\Delta x)$ (3.11b). **Stated** (used in the derivation)
13. With $C_k$ chosen by (3.12), the leading error term at the chosen point vanishes: the reconstruction error is $O(\Delta x^{r+1})$ at $x^*_i$ and $O(\Delta x^r)$ elsewhere in $I_i$; the $C_k$ do not depend on $\Delta x$ (3.12)–(3.14b). **Derived**
14. Remark 1: at least one positive and one negative error coefficient at $x^*_i$ ($\eta_p\ge1$, $\eta_n\ge1$) are needed for (3.13) (p. 204). **Stated**
15. Choosing $x^*_i=x_{i+1/2}$ if $f'(\bar u_i)>0$ and $x^*_i=x_{i-1/2}$ if $f'(\bar u_i)<0$ (either if zero) makes $\mathcal L_i(\bar u)$ accurate to $O(\Delta x^{r+1})$ in smooth regions away from sonic points, and $O(\Delta x^r)$ in "a bounded, in fact small, number of cells" near them (§3.4, (3.15)). **Derived**
16. Remark 2: for $r=2,3$ WENO is "a little more expensive" than ENO of the same order on sequential computers and "much less expensive" on parallel computers, because ENO needs more data transport (p. 205). **Stated**
17. $r=2$: claimed third order ("we achieved fourth-order accuracy"); $C=(\frac12,1)$ if $f'(\bar u_i)>0$, $(1,\frac12)$ if $f'(\bar u_i)\le0$ (§3.5, (3.16)–(3.17b)). **Derived**
18. $r=3$: claimed fourth order ("we achieve fifth-order accuracy"); $C=(\frac1{12},\frac12,\frac14)$ if $f'(\bar u_i)>0$, $(\frac14,\frac12,\frac1{12})$ if $f'(\bar u_i)\le0$ (§3.6, (3.18)–(3.19b)). **Derived** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: normalised by their sum $\frac56$, the $f'>0$ constants are $(\frac1{10},\frac6{10},\frac3{10})$ for the stencils from left to right, the linear weights $d_k$ of WENO-JS; for $r=2$ the normalised constants are $(\frac13,\frac23)$.)*
19. Linear advection, $u_0=\sin(\pi x)$, $t=1$, $\Delta t/\Delta x=0.8$ (Table I): $r=2$ reaches $L_1$ orders 3.81, 4.22 and $L_\infty$ 3.45, 4.70 (80–320 cells); $r=3$ reaches $L_1$ 4.56, 5.82, 4.82 and $L_\infty$ 3.71, 5.80, 6.73 (80–640 cells), with $L_1$ error $6.07\times10^{-10}$ at 640 cells. **Shown**
20. Linear advection, $u_0=\sin^4(\pi x)$ (Table II): $L_1$ orders 2.52, 3.65, 4.11 ($r=2$) and 4.26, 4.93, 5.86 ($r=3$), 80–640 cells. **Shown**
21. For both smooth data the observed orders are "about fourth- (for $r=2$) and fifth- (for $r=3$)", "surprisingly better than third- and fourth-order, the theoretical results" (p. 207). **Shown**
22. Standard ENO loses accuracy on the $\sin^4$ data ("easily fixed", refs. [6, 7]); "No such degeneracy was found with our present methods" (p. 207). **Shown** (WENO) / **Stated** (ENO, cited)
23. A square wave, a semi-ellipse and a Gaussian advected to $t=0.5$ with $r=3$ show "good resolution", but "Linear discontinuities are smeared a bit"; the authors expect to fix this with Harten's subcell resolution or Yang's artificial compression (Figs. 1–3, p. 208). **Shown** (qualitative)
24. Burgers, $u_0=\frac12+\sin(\pi x)$, $r=3$: about fifth order before the shock ($t=0.15$, Table III: $L_1$ 4.13, 4.79; $L_\infty$ 3.41, 6.21); at $t=0.55$ the errors at distance $\ge0.1$ from the shock (Table IV: $L_1$ 4.89, 6.70; $L_\infty$ 4.32, 5.72) have the size of the smooth case, showing that "the error propagation of the schemes is still very local" (p. 208). **Shown**
25. For the quartic flux $f(u)=\frac14(u^2-1)(u^2-4)$ and Buckley–Leverett, the $r=3$ scheme converges "with good resolution" to the entropy solutions, compared with Lax–Friedrichs on a very fine grid (Example 3, Figs. 6–8). **Shown** (qualitative)
26. Euler Sod and Lax problems, $r=3$, characteristic reconstruction with the Roe flux and entropy fix built on Roe's average (Example 4, Figs. 9–10). **Shown** (qualitative)
27. Euler, smooth periodic data $\rho=\rho v=E=2+\sin(\pi x)$ at $t=1$: "fifth- ($r=3$) order accuracy in all three components"; density $L_1$ orders 4.03, 6.12, 5.90, error $2.92\times10^{-9}$ at 640 cells (Table V, Example 5). **Shown**
28. The squared differences in $\beta_k$ (Shu's suggestion) and the absolute differences originally used "work well"; one of them gives a "smoother ($C^\infty$ vs. Lipschitz) numerical flux which may be helpful for steady state convergence or convergence proof" (Acknowledgment; see Printed slips on which one the sentence names). **Stated**

Setting: the indicator is
$\beta=\sum_{l=1}^{r-1}\big(\sum_{k=1}^{l}(\Delta^{r-l}\bar u_{i-r+k})^2\big)/l$ with undivided
forward differences $\Delta$, "the summation of all averages of square values of the same order
differences" (p. 203); for $r=3$, $\beta=\frac12\big((\Delta\bar u_{i-2})^2+(\Delta\bar u_{i-1})^2\big)+(\Delta^2\bar u_{i-2})^2$
on the leftmost stencil. Every difference order has equal weight; it is not the integral form of
[Jiang & Shu (1996)](/sources/jiang-1996.md). The constants $C_k$ follow from (3.12) by
cancelling the bracket of (3.11c) at $x^*_i$; one polynomial $R_i$ per cell is evaluated at both
of its ends. Monotone fluxes: Engquist–Osher (2.4), Godunov (2.5), and the scalar "Roe with
entropy fix" (2.6a), upwind when $f'$ keeps one sign between the two states and
$h^{\mathrm{LF}}$ with $a=\max\lvert f'\rvert$ over that interval otherwise (2.6b).

# Printed slips

  on $y'=\lambda y$ it gives
  $R(z)=1+\frac{145}{108}z+\frac{43}{54}z^2+\frac7{24}z^3+\frac1{12}z^4$, which is not first-order
  consistent; with $\frac12\mathcal L$ in the first stage it gives $\sum_{k\le4}z^k/k!$.
  *(Compiler check, not in the sources: stage-by-stage substitution of $\mathcal L(u)=zu$.)* The
  printed third-order scheme gives $1+z+\frac{z^2}2+\frac{z^3}6$.

  although $\mathcal L_i$ is defined with $1/\Delta x$ (2.7b).

  the second as $x^*_{j-1}=x_{j-1/2}$.

- **Table II**, $r=3$, $L_\infty$: errors 6.87D-03, 3.93D-04, 3.25D-05, 6.63D-07 with orders 3.91,
