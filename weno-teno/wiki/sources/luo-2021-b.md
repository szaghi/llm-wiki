# Summary

The note modifies the fifth-order [WENO-Z+](/methods/weno-z-plus.md) scheme
of Acker, Borges & Costa (2016), which adds to the [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)
unnormalized weight a term $\mu z_k^{-1}$, with
$z_k=(\tau_5+\epsilon)/(\beta_k+\epsilon)$, so that the less-smooth
candidate stencil gets more weight; Acker et al. chose $\mu=\Delta x^{2/3}$.
Luo & Wu compute the [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
of WENO-Z+ numerically (Pirozzoli's method) and find negative dissipation in a
narrow band around $\vartheta\approx1.1834$, which decreasing $\mu$ removes
only slowly while degrading resolution elsewhere. They propose WENO-Z+M: the
extra term is weakened to $z_k^{-1/2}$, and the constant $\mu$ is
replaced by $\mu \zeta$, with $\zeta$ the ratio of the WENO-Z weight of the
least-smooth stencil to its linear weight. $\mu$ is then pushed to the
largest value for which the [interacting blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md)
still run, $\mu=579$. Like [mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md), the
scheme moves the weights of the [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md)
family toward or beyond the linear ones.

The conceptual point is that classical weights give the less-smooth stencil
less than its linear weight, which adds positive nonlinear dissipation, while
WENO-Z+-type weights give it more, which offsets part of the linear
dissipation, so the scheme can resolve better than the linear upwind scheme
UD5 in a wavenumber band. Tests use finite differences with global
[Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md),
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) and
[SSP RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md): broadband advection and sound waves,
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), Titarev–Toro,
[Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), a 2D smooth accuracy test,
[2D Riemann configuration 3](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), the
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) and a shock/shear
layer interaction. The ninth-order WENO-Z9 fails on two of them. The
comparators include the incremental-stencil WENO-IS-I of Zhu & Hu, a
relative of the [hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) and incremental designs. No
theorem is proved.

# Key claims

Registry notation; the source's $\xi_k$, $\eta_k=1/\xi_k$, $\lambda$, $z$, $q$ and
$p$ are written $z_k$, $z_k^{-1}$, $\mu$, $\zeta$, $q_\eta$ and $q$, as on the
[WENO-Z+](/methods/weno-z-plus.md) page (see the notation map); $\omega_D$, $d_D$ are the weights of the stencil with the
largest $\beta_k$.

1. In WENO-Z the weight of the less-smooth candidate stencil is always below its linear weight; increasing it moves the weights toward the linear ones (§1). **Stated**
2. Acker et al. proved that WENO-Z+ keeps the ENO property as long as $\mu=O(1)$ (§2.3). **Stated** (cited)
3. Acker et al. chose $\mu=\Delta x^{2/3}$ as a compromise: with $\Delta x^{1/2}$ the Titarev–Toro smooth waves were magnified, with $\Delta x$ seriously attenuated (§2.3). **Stated** (cited)
4. For WENO-Z+ with relatively large $\mu$ ($\Delta x^0$, $\Delta x^{1/2}$, $\Delta x^{2/3}$, $\Delta x$ in Fig. 1), the ADR shows negative dissipation ($\tilde\vartheta_i>0$) in a band; near $\vartheta\approx1.1834$ the dissipation grows much more slowly with decreasing $\mu$ than elsewhere, so reducing $\mu$ "sacrifices the spectral properties in vain" (§3.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
5. Replacing $z_k^{-1}$ by $z_k^{-1/q_\eta}$ (Eq. (7)) eliminates negative dissipation once $q_\eta$ reaches 2; larger $q_\eta$ damages resolution; $q_\eta=2$ is adopted (§3.2). **Shown** (ADR, partly displayed)
6. With the weakened term, increasing $\mu$ ($1,25,600$ in Fig. 2) monotonically improves dispersion and dissipation without negative dissipation (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
7. The adaptive factor $\zeta=(1+z_{\min}^2)/\sum_kd_k(1+z_k^2)$ of Eq. (8) satisfies $\zeta\le1$, $\zeta=1+O(\Delta x^6)$ in smooth regions and $\zeta=O(\Delta x^4)+\Theta(\epsilon^2)$ at a discontinuity (§3.2). **Stated** (cited from Borges et al. and Acker et al.)
8. WENO-Z+M runs the interacting blast waves with $\mu=579$ and fails with $\mu=580$; $\mu=579$ is fixed for all tests (§3.2, Fig. 13). **Shown** (one test, $N_x=401$)
9. The ADR of WENO-Z+M is significantly better than those of WENO-Z and WENO-Z+ (§3.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. Previous shock-capturing schemes, including previous WENO schemes, always have spectral properties inferior to the corresponding linear schemes (§4). **Stated**
11. The ADR curves of WENO-Z+ cross those of UD5 at $\vartheta\approx1.3418$, better below and worse above; WENO-Z+M is better than UD5 over a much broader band (§4, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
12. On Fourier modes, at $\vartheta=1.25$ WENO-Z+ gives the less-smooth stencil more than its linear weight at every interface; at $\vartheta=1.45$ its weights almost equal those of WENO-Z (below linear) except at two points; WENO-Z always gives less than linear (§4, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
13. Mechanism: the nonlinear dissipation is positive when $\omega_D<d_D$ and negative when $\omega_D>d_D$; an excessive $\omega_D$ makes the total dissipation negative (WENO-Z+ near $\vartheta\approx1.1834$) (§4). **Derived** (heuristic, no analysis)
14. At $\vartheta=1.1834$ WENO-Z+M raises $\omega_D$ less than WENO-Z+ (no negative dissipation); at $\vartheta=1.45$ WENO-Z+M has $\omega_D>d_D$ at all interfaces, WENO-Z+ mostly $\omega_D<d_D$ (§4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
15. Broadband advection, $N_x=25$, $t=30$: WENO-Z+M resolves better than UD5, WENO-IS-I and WENO-Z9; WENO-Z+ does not visibly improve over UD5 (§5.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
16. Broadband sound waves, $N_x=129$: WENO-Z+M resolves significantly better than UD5, WENO-IS-I and WENO-Z9 (§5.2.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
17. Shu–Osher (201 points): WENO-Z+ improves the high-frequency waves over WENO-Z but not the discontinuity; WENO-Z+M improves both, better than WENO-IS-I and WENO-Z9 on the waves and better than WENO-IS-I at the discontinuity (§5.2.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
18. Titarev–Toro (1001 points): WENO-Z+ resolves the high-frequency waves somewhat better than WENO-Z+M but falsely amplifies the smooth waves ahead of and behind the shock (negative dissipation); WENO-Z+M does not, and is much better than WENO-Z9; WENO-IS-I has milder negative dissipation (§5.2.3, Fig. 11). **Shown**
19. Lax problem (101 points): WENO-Z+M improves shock and contact markedly over WENO-Z and WENO-Z+, is less oscillatory than WENO-IS-I with lower dissipation; WENO-Z9 is less dissipative but more oscillatory (§5.2.4, Fig. 12). **Shown**
20. Interacting blast waves (401 points): WENO-Z9 fails (WENO-Z7 shown instead); WENO-Z+M is better than WENO-IS-I and WENO-Z7 (§5.2.5, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. 2D smooth density wave ($L_1$/$L_2$ of $\rho$, $t=0.2$): at $641^2$, WENO-Z gives $L_1=0.7896\times10^{-13}$, equal to UD5, WENO-Z+ $0.1549\times10^{-12}$, WENO-Z+M $0.3913\times10^{-13}$, about half of UD5; the observed $L_1$ orders of WENO-Z+M are 5.61, 5.46, 5.31, 5.16, 5.08 (Table 1, §5.3.1). **Shown**
22. "WENO-Z+M scheme achieves significantly higher accuracy than UD5 when there are no critical points", attributed to $\omega_D>d_D$ giving smaller dispersion and dissipation errors (§5.3.1). **Shown** (one test) / interpretation **Stated**
23. 2D Riemann configuration 3 ($401^2$, $t=0.8$): WENO-Z+ is close to WENO-Z; WENO-Z+M has better multiscale resolution than WENO-IS-I and WENO-Z9, and is more symmetric (§5.3.2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
24. Double Mach reflection ($961\times241$, $t=0.2$): WENO-Z9 needs $\nu=0.1$, so all schemes use it; WENO-Z+M gives the best roll-up resolution (§5.3.3, Fig. 15). **Shown**
25. Forward-facing step ($481\times161$, $t=4$, $\nu=0.5$): WENO-Z7 and WENO-Z9 both fail; WENO-Z+M is markedly better than WENO-IS-I, especially in the shear layer (§5.3.4, Fig. 16). **Shown**
26. Shock/shear layer interaction (Navier–Stokes, $251\times51$, $t=120$, sixth-order central viscous terms): WENO-Z+M has the best resolution (§5.4, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
27. A well-designed nonlinear mechanism can improve resolution beyond the underlying linear scheme, against the view that nonlinear shock-capturing mechanisms always degrade spectral properties; this is an alternative to optimizing linear coefficients (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §2.1, condition (2): "$(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)$ approach the ideal weights $(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(1/10,6/10,3/10)$", while everywhere else $k=0,1,2$.
- §3.2: "Since generally $\beta_k>>\tau$ … $\eta_k=\beta_k/\tau$" writes $\tau$ for $\tau_5$ and drops the $\varepsilon$ of Eq. (6).
- Eq. (6) defines $\xi_k=(\tau_5+\varepsilon)/(\beta_k+\varepsilon)$ and calls it "the original term of the WENO-Z weights", whose form in Eq. (4) has no $\varepsilon$ in the numerator.
- §3.2: "$z=O(\Delta x^4)+\Theta(\varepsilon^2)$" mixes $O$ and $\Theta$ notation.
- Fig. 2 displays $\lambda=600$, although §3.2 reports that $\lambda=580$ already fails; the displayed WENO-Z+M curve is not the adopted $\lambda=579$.
- Fig. 4 caption "Fig. 4 partial enlarged view of Fig. 3." (self-reference); the Fig. 7 legend reads "WENO-Z+M1"; §4 "red circus" (circles); §5.4 "2501 × 501grid points".
- Eq. (12): the initial velocity is printed $u(x,0)=u_0+\frac2{\gamma-1}(c(x,0)/c_0)$, presumably $u_0+\frac2{\gamma-1}(c(x,0)-c_0)$. *(compiler check, not in the sources: for $\gamma=1.4$ and $c\approx c_0$ the printed form gives $u\approx u_0+5$, not a perturbation of $u_0$.)*
- §5.2.1 reuses $\varepsilon$ for the perturbation amplitude ($0.001$) after it denoted the regularization ($10^{-40}$).
- Ref. [14] prints the authors as "F. Acker, R.B. de, R. Borges, B. Costa", splitting R.B. de R. Borges into two names; ref. [6] prints no pages or article number.
