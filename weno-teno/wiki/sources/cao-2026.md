# Summary

Cao et al. (2026) propose [COS(DG)](/methods/cos-dg.md), a convex
oscillation-suppressing post-processing for Runge–Kutta
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) schemes. After the
final stage of each time step (not after every stage) the polynomial $u_h|_{I_i}$
is replaced by the convex combination
$(1-\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i)\bar u_i+\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_iu_h|_{I_i}$: every
non-mean mode is damped by one scalar
$\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i=\exp(-a_i\Delta t\,\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i/\Delta x_i)\in[0,1]$.
The indicator $\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i$ compares the cell polynomial, extrapolated
onto each neighbour, with the neighbour's polynomial, normalised by the
neighbour's fluctuation about the cell mean. It is derivative-free, uses only the
immediate neighbours and is invariant under $u\mapsto\sigma u+\mu$ on the stencil
([local scale invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md)); the factor
$a_i\Delta t/\Delta x_i$ makes the result independent of a rescaling of the wave
speeds at fixed CFL number. For systems the $L^2$ distance is replaced by an
entropy-induced distance (a Jensen-gap form or a frozen-Hessian form of a convex
entropy), which avoids [characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md).

The operator preserves cell averages, is $L^2$-nonexpansive, solves a linear
damping ODE exactly (which relates it to the modal filter of
[OEDG](/methods/oedg.md)), and does not increase any convex entropy cell by cell,
so an [entropy-stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md) base DG stays entropy
stable. It perturbs an $(N+1)$-th order accurate state by $O(\Delta x^{N+1})$ in
$L^2$. Its form matches the [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md),
and a combined [bound-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md) and
oscillation-suppressing factor is proposed. A local variant applies COS only at
interfaces flagged by a Lax-type eigenvalue criterion
([troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md)).

Numerics (Python, $\mathbb P^1$–$\mathbb P^3$, [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)
for discontinuous tests) cover [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md),
the [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md),
[Jiang–Shu advection](/benchmarks/jiang-shu-composite-advection.md),
a strong [single shock](/benchmarks/steady-shock-problems.md),
[Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and a multi-scale Lax problem,
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and
[Mach 80 and Mach 2000 jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md), against
OEDG, OFDG and seven KXRCF-flagged limiters (TVB, two moment limiters, [MP and MMP](/methods/monotonicity-preserving-limiter.md),
[WENO and SWENO](/methods/weno-limiter-dg.md)). The COS step takes 15.6–18.8% of
the run time against 35.3–40.6% for the OE step of OEDG.

# Key claims

Registry notation: DG degree $N$ (source $k$), cell $I_i$ (source $K_i$), width
$\Delta x_i$ (source $h_i$), local maximum wave speed $a_i$ (source $\beta_i$),
convex entropy $\eta$ (source $\eta$, Thm 2.4). Page-local: $\eta_{\mathrm d}$ the convex entropy inducing the distance (source $g$), $\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i$ the COS
factor (source $\lambda_i$), $\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i=\sigma^{\mathrm{OS},-}_i+\sigma^{\mathrm{OS},+}_i$
the indicator (source $\sigma_i$), $\mathsf N^\pm_i$ its normaliser (source
$\Theta^\pm_i$), $C_{\mathrm{OS}}$ its constant (source $C_k$), $\epsilon_{\mathrm{OS}}$
its threshold, $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}$ the COS operator,
$\mathsf k_i$ and $\mathsf X_i$ the sensor weight and oscillation indicator of
§5, $\tau_{\mathrm v}$ the tolerance of Definition 5.1.

1. COS is applied once per time step, after the final RK stage, unlike the OE procedure of OEDG and most limiters, which act at every stage (§1, §2.1, Remark 3). **Stated**
2. $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u_h)|_{I_i}=(1-\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i)\bar u_i+\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_iu_h|_{I_i}$ with $\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i=\exp(-a_i\Delta t\,\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i/\Delta x_i)$, $a_i=\max_{i-1\le l\le i+1}\lvert f'(\bar u_l)\rvert$; $\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i$ depends only on the polynomials of $I_{i-1},I_i,I_{i+1}$ (Eqs. (5)–(6), Property 2.1). **Stated** (definition)
3. The indicator (Eqs. (7)–(9)), $\sigma^{\mathrm{OS},\pm}_i=C_{\mathrm{OS}}\lVert u_{i\pm1}-u_i\rVert^2_{L^2(I_{i\pm1})}/\mathsf N^\pm_i$ if $\mathsf N^\pm_i=\lVert u_{i\pm1}-\bar u_i\rVert^2_{L^2(I_{i\pm1})}\ge\epsilon_{\mathrm{OS}}$ and $0$ otherwise ($u_i$ extrapolated onto $I_{i\pm1}$), is $O(\Delta x^{2N})$ in smooth regions, generically $O(\Delta x^{2N-2})$ near smooth extrema and $O(1)$ across jumps (§2.1, §5.1). **Stated** (the rates are asserted; §4 proves the $L^2$ bound of claim 15, not these rates)
4. Parameters of all computations: $C_{\mathrm{OS}}=0.31$, $0.005$, $0.00036$ for $\mathbb P^1$, $\mathbb P^2$, $\mathbb P^3$; $\epsilon_{\mathrm{OS}}=10^{-8}\max\{\bar u_i^2,1\}$ (scalar) or $10^{-8}\max\{\eta_{\mathrm d}(\bar u_i),1\}$ (systems) (§2.1, §2.3). **Stated**
5. Local scale invariance: $\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i(\sigma u_h+\mu)=\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i(u_h)$, so the COS solution transforms as $\sigma u+\mu$; OEDG is only globally scale-invariant (Property 2.3, Fig. 2). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the switch $\mathsf N^\pm_i\ge\epsilon_{\mathrm{OS}}$ uses an absolute threshold, which the map $u\mapsto\sigma u+\mu$ does not leave invariant; the identity holds wherever the switch is not active.)*
6. Evolution invariance: for $\partial_tu+\partial_x(cf(u))=0$ at fixed CFL number, $a\Delta t$ is unchanged, so COS gives identical results at corresponding physical times (Property 2.5; the notion is credited to OEDG). **Derived**
7. COS preserves cell averages, hence local conservation (Property 2.4). **Proved**
8. $\lVert\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u_h)\rVert_{L^2(\Omega)}\le\lVert u_h\rVert_{L^2(\Omega)}$ (Thm 2.2, Eq. (12)), from $\lVert\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u_h)\rVert^2_{L^2(I_i)}=(\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i)^2\lVert u_h\rVert^2_{L^2(I_i)}+\Delta x_i(1-(\theta^{\mathrm{OS}}_i)^2)\bar u_i^2$ and Jensen's inequality. **Proved**
9. $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}$ is the solution at pseudo-time $\Delta t$ of the linear damping problem (13), in which the fluctuation $u-\bar u_i$ decays at the frozen rate $a_i\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i/\Delta x_i$ (Thm 2.3). **Proved**
10. The OE operator of OEDG is a modal filter with mode-dependent exponents $\sum_{m=0}^{l}\sigma^m_i$ for mode $l$; COS damps all non-mean modes uniformly; the two also differ in derivative-free versus jump-based indicators, once-per-step versus per-stage application, local versus global scale invariance, and nodal-or-modal versus modal-only implementation (Remark 3). **Derived**
11. A component-wise COS for systems does not guarantee entropy stability, and a scalar indicator taken as the maximum over components "often leads to excessive dissipation and loss of accuracy" (§2.3). **Stated**
12. For scalar $\eta_{\mathrm d}(u)=u^2$ both entropy distances (20) and (21) reduce to the $L^2$ norm (Example 2.1). **Derived**
13. On a compact convex subset of $G$ where the eigenvalues of $\eta_{\mathrm d}''$ lie in $[c_{\min},c_{\max}]$, $c_{\min}>0$, both distances are equivalent to the $L^2$ norm with these constants (Lemma 2.1). **Proved**
14. For any convex entropy $\eta$, not necessarily the $\eta_{\mathrm d}$ inducing the distance, $\int_{I_i}\eta(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u_h))\,dx\le\int_{I_i}\eta(u_h)\,dx$ (Thm 2.4). **Proved**
15. If $u_h$ is $(N+1)$-th order accurate, $N\ge d/2$, the exact solution stays in the interior of $G$ and $\eta_{\mathrm d}''\succ0$ on $G$, then $\lVert\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u_h)-u_h\rVert_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C\Delta x^{N+1}$ for $\Delta x\le\Delta x^*$ and $a_{\max}\Delta t/\Delta x\le\nu$ (Thm 4.1, multi-D systems, through a compact convex covering of the solution states, Lemma 4.4; Thm 2.1 is the 1D scalar case). **Proved** (a single application of the operator; error accumulation over time steps is not analysed)
16. Weighted envelope: $\mathsf k_i\,\mathsf X_i[\mathcal F_{\mathrm{OS}}(u)\vert u]\le1/(eC_{\mathrm{OS}})$ for every input (Thm 5.1, 1D scalar; Thm 5.2, multi-D systems), from $\max_{x\ge0}xe^{-C_{\mathrm{OS}}x}=1/(eC_{\mathrm{OS}})$; the design rule $C_{\mathrm{OS}}\ge1/(e\tau_{\mathrm v})$ guarantees a tolerance $\tau_{\mathrm v}\in(0,1)$ (Corollary 5.1). **Proved** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\tau_{\mathrm v}<1$ requires $C_{\mathrm{OS}}>1/e\approx0.368$. All three values used in §6 (claim 4) lie below it; for them the envelope $1/(eC_{\mathrm{OS}})$ is about 1.19, 73.6 and 1022, outside the range the corollary covers.)*
17. The Lax-type criterion (23), on the jumps of the extremal eigenvalues of the flux Jacobian between neighbouring cell averages with $\delta_{\mathrm L}=0.05$, sufficed "across all DG orders and Euler equations with various Mach numbers"; its cost is $O(1)$ per interface (§2.4). **Stated**
18. The Jensen distance needs the extrapolated polynomials to stay admissible (a Zhang–Shu limiter may be required before COS); the frozen-Hessian distance is defined for all states (§2.3, Example 2.2). **Stated**
19. The two distances give "similar results throughout our test cases"; only the frozen-Hessian one is shown (§6). **Stated**
20. Smooth Burgers ($t=0.6$): optimal order, e.g. $\mathbb P^3$ at 1280 cells, $L^1$ error 1.37e-11 with rate 3.96 (Table 1). **Shown**
21. Isentropic vortex ($t=0.01$): $\mathbb P^1$ rates rise from 1.84 to 2.00, $\mathbb P^2$ rates are about 2.8–2.95 ($L^1$ rate 2.89 at $N_x=1280$), $\mathbb P^3$ rates about 3.98–4.01 (Table 2). **Shown**
22. Single shock with pressure ratio $10^5$, Lax, Woodward–Colella and Shu–Osher: third-order COS(DG) gives the sharpest non-oscillatory profiles among the ten methods compared (Figs. 1, 6, 8, 10, 11). **Shown**
23. Multi-scale Lax problem: OEDG produces spurious undershoots in the small-scale region, COS(DG) does not (Fig. 9, Example 6.8). **Shown**
24. CPU share (single core, Python, Woodward–Colella and Shu–Osher with 400 cells): OE 35.3–40.6%, COS 15.6–18.8%, local COS 6.3–10.4% (Table 3). **Shown**
25. The Mach 80 and Mach 2000 jets run with fourth-order global COS(DG) plus the Zhang–Shu positivity-preserving limiter, applied at the RK stages (Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
26. Local COS improves the resolution of the shear layers in double Mach reflection (Fig. 16). **Shown**
27. The combined factor $\min\{\exp(-a_i\Delta t\,\sigma^{\mathrm{OS}}_i/\Delta x_i),\theta_i\}$, with $\theta$ the Zhang–Shu factor, unifies bound preservation and oscillation suppression (Remark 2); it is proposed, not used in §6. **Stated**
28. OFDG may smear excessively or keep oscillating when scales or wave speeds vary widely (citing OEDG), and its damping terms become stiff at strong discontinuities; bound-preserving limiters alone "may reduce, but still exhibit, spurious oscillations" (§1, Remark 2). **Stated**
