# Summary

The paper designs a sixth-order finite-volume [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) scheme
for hyperbolic conservation laws in 1D and 2D on uniform Cartesian meshes. It evolves the
cell average $\bar u_i$ and the first moment
$\bar u^{(1)}_i=\frac1{\Delta x}\int_{I_i}u\frac{x-x_i}{\Delta x}dx$, which come from testing the
equation with $1$ and $(x-x_i)/\Delta x$, as in a $\mathbb P^1$
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) weak form. Earlier HWENO schemes
evolve derivative averages. Only point values at the four Gauss–Lobatto points of each cell are
reconstructed: the two boundary values feed an HLLC flux
([HLL-family solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md)) and all four feed the volume integral.

The reconstruction is a [multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) hierarchy in
the style of [WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md), on the compact 3-cell (1D) or $3\times3$ (2D)
stencil. It nests polynomials of degree 0, 2, 3 and 5, with unnormalized linear weights
$1,10,100,1000$ and [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type nonlinear weights with a squared global
indicator $\tau_4$. The degree-0 [smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md)
is built from an auxiliary WENO-Z combination of linear polynomials. The new ingredient is a
moment modification: in cells flagged by a modified KXRCF
[troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md), the first moment is replaced
by that of a quartic built from neighbouring data, and the reconstruction is repeated. A Taylor
argument gives sixth order in smooth regions, without
[critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md). It also argues that the modification
keeps the nonlinear weights near the linear ones at shocks.

The evidence is numerical. Sixth order is observed on
[Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) and smooth Euler tests, and fifth order when
every cell is modified. The Lax [shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), the
[Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), a
[Sedov blast](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), the
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and the
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) are compared by figures only. The
unmodified scheme blows up on the double Mach reflection. Time stepping is
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) with CFL 0.6.

# Key claims

Local symbols (see the Notation map; those of the [multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md)
and [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) pages): $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ the first moment, $P_l$ the
central polynomials of degree 0, 2, 3, 5, $\tilde P_l$ their nested ("equivalent") decomposition,
$d_{l,4}$ the linear weights, $\aleph_i$ the KXRCF quantity.

1. The scheme is sixth order in smooth regions, and "arbitrarily high order HWENO schemes can be designed following the same lines" (§2, p. 5; Remark 1, Eq. 2.29). **Derived** (Taylor expansion, not a theorem)
2. Only the values at the Gauss–Lobatto points ($x_{i\mp1/2}$, $x_i\mp\frac{\sqrt5}{10}\Delta x$ in 1D; boundary and interior tensor points in 2D) are reconstructed, whereas [14] reconstructed function and first-derivative values at the cell boundaries (Abstract; §1, p. 4; §4). **Stated**
3. The stencil is as small as that of general HWENO and more compact than WENO of the same order: 3 cells in 1D and $3\times3$ in 2D for sixth order (Abstract; §4). **Stated**
4. The linear weights "can be any positive numbers as long as their sum equals one" without changing the smooth-region accuracy. They do change shock resolution: larger weights on higher-degree polynomials give steeper shocks but a less stable scheme (Abstract; Remark 1, p. 12). **Stated** (the accuracy part follows from Eq. 2.29)
5. The CFL number is 0.6 in 1D and 2D (Abstract; §3, p. 21; §4). **Shown** (used in every shock test; no stability analysis)
6. Without modification, a discontinuity in $I_i$ makes $\bar u^{(1)}_i=O(1)$, so $\beta_1=\beta_2=O(\Delta x^2)$, $\beta_3=\beta_4=O(1)$ and $\tau_4=O(1)$. Then $\alpha_{l,4}=d_{l,4}(1+O(\Delta x^{-2}))$ for $l=1,2$ and $\alpha_{l,4}=d_{l,4}(1+O(1))$ for $l=3,4$, so the low layers dominate and the discontinuity is smeared (Remark 1, Eq. 2.30). **Derived**
7. After modification, $\bar u^{(1)}_i=O(\Delta x)$, $\beta_l=u'^2\Delta x^2+O(\Delta x^4)$, $\tau_4=O(\Delta x^8)$ and $\alpha_{l,4}=d_{l,4}(1+O(\Delta x^6))$. Each layer's nonlinear weight stays near its linear weight, and resolution near discontinuities "can be increased significantly" (Remark 1, p. 12). **Derived** (heuristic: the modified moment is built from neighbouring averages that straddle the discontinuity)
8. The modification also improves stability, by reducing the first moments near discontinuities (§1, p. 5; Remark 1). **Shown** (double Mach reflection, Example 3.9)
9. The KXRCF indicator needs no extra reconstruction, because all its values are computed in Step 1; the indicators of Zhao et al. [25,26] do (Remark 2, p. 13; Remark 3, pp. 20–21). **Stated**
10. The modified KXRCF quantity, with exponent $(l+1)/3$ in place of KXRCF's $(k+1)/2$ and $l=5$, behaves as $\aleph_i=O(h^{2(l+1)/3})\to0$ in smooth regions and as $1/O(h^{(l+1)/3})\to\infty$ at discontinuities, with threshold 1 (Eqs. 2.25, 2.32–2.34). **Derived** (from the estimates of [13])
11. The modified scheme HWENO6-M5-I reaches sixth order: $L^1$ order 5.96 at 320 cells (error 3.95E-13) for 1D Burgers, 6.36 at $320^2$ (6.83E-12) for 2D Burgers, 6.03 at 320 (1.69E-12) for 1D Euler and 6.49 at $320^2$ (4.67E-11) for 2D Euler (Tables 3.1–3.4). **Shown**
12. HWENO6-M5-NI, which modifies every cell, is fifth order "as expected", because the quartic modification lowers the accuracy: finest-grid $L^1$ orders 4.97, 4.88, 5.05 and 5.10 (Tables 3.1–3.4; p. 25). **Shown**
13. On the smooth tests the modification "has not been enacted" in HWENO6-M5-I, i.e. no cell is flagged (p. 25). **Stated** (inferred from the orders; no count of flagged cells)
14. On Burgers at $T=1.5/\pi$ (with a shock), HWENO6 and HWENO6-M5-I both match the exact solution with "not much difference" (Figs. 3.1–3.2, p. 25). **Shown**
15. On the six shock tests (Lax, shock–density wave, blast waves, Sedov, double Mach, forward step), HWENO6-M5-I gives "much better resolutions and sharper shock transitions" than HWENO6 (p. 31). **Shown** (figures only, no error norms)
16. The unmodified HWENO6 blows up on the double Mach reflection (Example 3.9, p. 31). **Shown** (stated as observed; no figure)
17. Against multi-resolution WENO, the main advantages are compact stencils and smaller errors on the same meshes at a fixed order (§4, p. 34). **Stated** (no MR-WENO comparison in the paper)
18. The original HWENO of Qiu and Shu [19,20] is "not stable enough" for severe problems such as the double Mach reflection and the forward step, largely because the first-derivative values become very large near discontinuities (§1, p. 3). **Stated** (cited)
19. The predecessor MR-HWENO [14] improved resolution but not stability, "since the first two layers in the hierarchy do not contain the information of the first order derivative"; here the coupling between function and moment is "stronger" (§1, p. 4). **Stated**
20. The framework "would be particularly efficient and simple on unstructured meshes" (ongoing work) (§4). **Stated**
21. The HLLC flux, with a PVRS-type pressure estimate and pressure-dependent wave-speed coefficients (Eqs. 2.5–2.7), is chosen "to better adapt to our high order scheme" (§1, p. 5). **Stated**

The moment modification (2.27) reads
$\bar u^{(1)}_i=-\frac5{76}\bar u_{i-1}+\frac5{76}\bar u_{i+1}-\frac{11}{38}\bar u^{(1)}_{i-1}-\frac{11}{38}\bar u^{(1)}_{i+1}$
*(compiler check, not in the sources: the first moment of the quartic matching three averages and the
two neighbouring first moments has exactly these coefficients)*. The nested decomposition (2.12),
$\tilde P_2=\frac{11}{10}P_2-\frac1{10}P_1$,
$\tilde P_3=\frac{111}{100}P_3-\frac{11}{100}P_2$,
$\tilde P_4=\frac{1111}{1000}P_4-\frac{111}{1000}P_3$, follows from the
unnormalized weights $10^{l-1}$ *(compiler check)*.

# Printed slips

- Figs. 3.1 and 3.2, for the scalar Burgers equation, are captioned "Left: density; right: density zoomed in".
- In Examples 3.1–3.2 the Burgers unknown is called $\mu$, which is the velocity in the Euler examples; the plots label it $u$.
- $sf(\cdot)$ in Eqs. (2.31) and (2.59) is never defined.
- Eq. (2.51): $\sum_{k\ne l}\lvert\beta_{1k}-\beta_{1l}\rvert/6$ has 12 terms over ordered pairs; the divisor 6 matches unordered pairs, which the text does not say.
- §3 prints the linear weights as $\gamma_{1,4}=1$, $\gamma_{2,4}=10$, …, without the bar of the unnormalized $\bar\gamma$ of Eq. (2.11).
- $\gamma$ is not printed for the smooth Euler tests (Examples 3.3–3.4) or for the Lax, Shu–Osher, blast-wave and Sedov tests; only the double Mach reflection states $\gamma=1.4$.
- $\omega$ is both the Gauss–Lobatto weight (Eq. 2.8) and the nonlinear weight (Eqs. 2.17, 2.21). $\gamma$ is both the linear weight and the ratio of specific heats, and $p$ is the pressure, the hierarchy polynomials $p_l$ and the polynomial degree in Eq. (2.32).
