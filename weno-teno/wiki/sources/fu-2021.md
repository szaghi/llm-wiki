# Summary

The paper proposes TENO-AA, a family of very-high-order
[targeted ENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) reconstructions with adaptive
accuracy order and adaptive dissipation control (see
[adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md)). Standard TENO builds a $K$-point
scheme from $K-2$ upwind-biased candidates of increasing width. TENO-AA keeps
the three 3-point stencils $S_0,S_1,S_2$ of TENO5 and adds a nested family of
symmetric large stencils $S_3$ (6 points), $S_4$ (8 points), $S_5$
(10 points). Groups $(S_0,S_1,S_2,S_k)$ are tested from the largest $k$ down;
the first large stencil that survives the cut-off test (measure
$\gamma_k=(\beta_k+\epsilon)^{-q}$ with $q=7$, normalization $\chi_k$,
threshold $C_T$) supplies the *whole* flux. If every large stencil is rejected,
the scheme falls back to cut-off-and-renormalize weights on the three small
stencils. The idea parallels the unequal-stencil [WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md)
family.

The large-stencil linear schemes are optimized in spectral space: the
dispersive part minimizes a weighted error in the modified wavenumber, the
dissipative part satisfies the [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md),
and the order is relaxed to 4, 6 and 8 on the 6-, 8- and 10-point stencils.
$C_T$ is switched between about $10^{-7}$ near discontinuities and $10^{-14}$
in smooth regions by a first-difference [shock sensor](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md).
Explicit TENO8-AA (sixth order) and TENO10-AA (eighth order) are printed,
with the [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) of the
large stencils as exact rational quadratic forms.

The discretization is a [conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
with [characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md),
[Rusanov or local Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)
and [SSP RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Validation is numerical, against
[WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md) mainly: Gaussian advection, Sod, Lax,
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), Titarev–Toro,
[blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) (which loses
[symmetry](/properties/symmetry-preservation.md)),
[Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md), and extreme
cases, including [Mach 80 and 2000 jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md)
and 3D Noh, run with the Hu–Adams–Shu positivity-preserving flux limiter
(compare [convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md)). The
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) and [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)
families appear as background. No theorem is proved.

# Key claims

Registry notation (final weights $\omega_k$; the source's $C_T$ exponent
$\beta$ is written $b$, its constants $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ are $b_1$, $b_2$,
its sensor $m$ is $\mu_d$ and its map $g$ is $\varphi$, as on the
[adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) page; see the notation map).

1. TENO-AA allows "constructing arbitrarily high-order TENO schemes in a unified paradigm": the large candidates nest and the reconstruction degrades recursively to lower order where smoothness fails (Abstract; §3.1, Fig. 1(b); §3.3, Remark 3.2). **Stated** (by construction; no general-$K$ formulas)
2. Upwind-biased large candidate stencils are "particularly not recommended": their spectral properties "typically do not satisfy the approximate dispersion-relation even with sophisticated optimization methods"; symmetric stencils allow dispersion and dissipation to be optimized separately once the order requirement is relaxed (§3.1). **Stated**
3. Scale separation uses $\gamma_k=1/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^q$ with $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ and $q=7$ ("rather than 2" as in WENO-JS) "to achieve sufficient scale separation" (§3.2, Eq. (11)). **Stated**
4. For a smooth solution the largest candidate is always selected, so the "desired accuracy order can be achieved" (Remark 3.2). **Stated**; confirmed numerically in claim 15
5. The three small stencils serve both as the reference against which a large stencil's smoothness is judged and as the nonlinear adaptation for robust shock capturing (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
6. The nonlinear cut-off weights on $S_0,S_1,S_2$ are invoked only when all large stencils are rejected; otherwise the flux is a single linear large-stencil flux (§3.3 flowchart; Conclusions). **Stated** (definition of the algorithm)
7. Standard TENO is "still excessively dissipative" for high-wavenumber physical fluctuations, because its smoothness indicators, built from high-order undivided differences, respond to such fluctuations as to genuine discontinuities (§3.4). **Stated**
8. Adaptive cut-off: $C_T=10^{-\lfloor b\rfloor}$, $b=b_1-b_2\big(1-\varphi(\mu_d)\big)$, $\varphi(\mu_d)=(1-\mu_d)^4(1+4\mu_d)$, $b_1=14$, $b_2=6.4$, sensor threshold $C_r=0.265$; $C_T\to10^{-7}$ for $\mu_d\approx1$ (discontinuity) and $C_T\approx10^{-14}$ for $\mu_d\approx0$ (smooth) (§3.4, Eqs. (17)–(19)). **Stated** (parameters not justified by analysis)
9. The 10-point linear scheme on $S_5$ is constrained to eighth order, the 8-point one on $S_4$ to sixth and the 6-point one on $S_3$ to fourth (§4.1, Eqs. (25)–(27)). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: applied to cell averages of $x^m$, the printed coefficients reproduce the primitive exactly, to $3\times10^{-11}$ or better, up to degree 7, 5 and 3 respectively, and fail at the next degree.)*
10. The small-stencil linear weights are taken from Arshed & Hoffmann: $d_0=0.5065006634$, $d_1=0.3699651429$, $d_2=0.1235341937$, not the fifth-order weights $(0.6,0.3,0.1)$ (§4.1). **Stated**
11. TENO8-AA is TENO10-AA without the 10-point stencil, because the framework is recursive (§4.3). **Stated**
12. Wide stencils increase arithmetic intensity and halo communication, but on GPU systems this is not the parallel bottleneck, citing other TENO codes and the HTR solver (96.6 % weak-scaling efficiency on 512 GPUs of Lassen) (§4.4). **Stated** (no timings of TENO-AA)
13. All built-in parameters are fixed across cases, "not tuned case-by-case" (§5; Abstract). **Stated**
14. Discretization: conservative finite differences, dimension by dimension, Roe-averaged characteristic decomposition, Rusanov splitting for conventional gas dynamics and local Lax–Friedrichs for extreme cases, SSP RK3 with $\nu=0.4$ by default (§5, Eq. (30)). **Stated**
15. Gaussian-pulse advection: TENO8-AA and TENO10-AA reach their design orders in $L_\infty$, with slopes matching the sixth- and eighth-order lines, for $\Delta x$ from about $0.02$ to $0.003$ (§5.1, Fig. 2). **Shown** (no error table)
16. Shu–Osher ($N_x=200$) and Titarev–Toro ($N_x=1000$, $\nu=0.1$): TENO-AA resolves the high-wavenumber density waves better than WENO-CU6 in amplitude and phase; WENO-CU6 produces density and velocity oscillations near shocklets, TENO-AA does not (§5.4, Figs. 4–5). **Shown**
17. Blast waves ($N_x=400$): TENO-AA gives a better density peak near $x\approx0.78$ than WENO-CU6 and is free of its pressure oscillations and velocity overshoot near $x\approx0.86$ (§5.5, Fig. 6). **Shown**
18. Double Mach reflection: at $512\times128$, TENO8-AA and TENO10-AA resolve "much more small-scale vortical structures than WENO-CU6 with a resolution of $1024\times256$", "the best among those from other low-dissipation schemes at similar resolutions" (§5.5.1, Figs. 7–8). **Shown** (visual comparison)
19. Rayleigh–Taylor ($64\times256$, $t=1.95$): finer structures than WENO-CU6 but loss of symmetry, attributed to round-off that the low dissipation no longer hides; Fleischmann et al. are cited for a cure by careful floating-point implementation (§5.5.2, Fig. 9). **Shown** (asymmetry) / **Stated** (cause)
20. Kelvin–Helmholtz at $512\times512$: more small-scale structure than WENO-CU6 (§5.6, Fig. 10). **Shown**
21. Extreme cases (local Lax–Friedrichs plus the positivity-preserving flux limiter): double rarefaction, Le Blanc, Sedov and the Mach 80 and Mach 2000 jets run stably, with "much richer small-scale structures" on the jets than fifth- and seventh-order WENO and a DG scheme (§5.7, Figs. 11–14). **Shown**
22. 3D Noh ($64^3$, Rusanov splitting): spherical shock shape kept, no wall heating, smaller density overshoot at the origin than WENO-CU6-M1 at the same resolution, and a more accurate post-shock density with TENO10-AA than TENO8-AA (§5.8, Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. Positivity and ENO-property preservation in extreme simulations are achieved "with the assistant of a positivity-preserving flux limiter"; the reconstruction alone is not claimed positivity-preserving (Abstract; §5.7). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (11) runs over "$k=0,\dots,p-1$" with $p$ "the total candidate stencil number", while the flowchart (§3.3) uses $S_p$ as the largest candidate and TENO10-AA has $S_0,\dots,S_5$; the range and the stencil $S_p$ cannot both hold.
- Eqs. (17)–(18) switch from index $i$ to $j$, and $\Delta f_{j\pm1/2}$ is never defined.
- $\beta$ denotes both the smoothness indicator and the exponent of $C_T$ (Eq. (19)); $m$ both the sensor (Eq. (17)) and the half-width of the linear schemes (Eqs. (21)–(22)).
- The weight $\nu$ of the error integral (Eq. (23)) and the dissipation level used for Eqs. (25)–(27) are not printed, so the optimization cannot be reproduced, only its coefficients used.
- Eq. (29): a stray comma in the line for $\hat f_{3,i+1/2}$, "$+0.6253788187359414f_{i+1},$".
- §5.2 "Conventional gas dynamics" is an empty heading; double Mach reflection and Rayleigh–Taylor are numbered §5.5.1–5.5.2, as subsections of "Interacting blast waves".
- Eq. (44), Sedov: the corner-cell condition reads "if $x\le\Delta x$ and $x\le\Delta y$" (the second presumably $y\le\Delta y$); $\gamma$ is not printed for this case.
- §5.8, 3D Noh: the initial condition is printed "$(\rho,u,v,p)=(1,-x/r,-y/r,10^{-6})$", without the third velocity component.
- Rusanov is cited as [56] in §5 but as "[33]" (Jiang–Shu) in §5.8.
- The order of the five-point fallback is not stated; the text calls it "the five-point reconstruction". *(compiler check, not in the sources: with the printed $d_k$, the combination of the three TENO5 candidate fluxes reproduces cell averages of polynomials exactly only up to degree 2, so the fallback linear scheme is third order.)*
- Reference [73] prints only "Woodward, P." although the text says Woodward and Colella; [71] prints the first author as "von, N. J." (von Neumann); [66] as "Huynhb, H."; [30] is "(In press)" with an arXiv number and no year.
