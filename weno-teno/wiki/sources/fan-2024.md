# Summary

The paper carries the oscillation-eliminating (OE) step of
[OEDG](/methods/oedg.md) (Peng et al. 2024) over to moment-based
finite-volume [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) (HWENO). The scheme is
called OE-HWENO and is worked out at sixth order in 1D and 2D. HWENO evolves the
cell average $\bar u_i$ and the first moment $\bar u^{(1)}_i$. The authors locate
the source of HWENO oscillations in the uncontrolled first moments near
discontinuities. Earlier moment limiters cap a sixth-order HWENO at fifth order,
and the damping terms of the oscillation-free OF-HWENO (Zhao & Qiu) are stiff,
carry empirical parameters, and need exponential Runge–Kutta.

After every [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stage the OE step
solves a linear damping ODE in pseudo-time $\hat t$ exactly. The cell averages
are unchanged and each first moment is multiplied by
$\exp(-a\Delta t\,\hat\sigma_i/\Delta x)$. The dimensionless coefficient
$\hat\sigma_i$ is built from the value and first-derivative jumps of a *linear*
sixth-order Hermite reconstruction and divided by the global range
$\max_i\lvert\bar u_i-u_\Omega\rvert$. The step is shown to keep the design
order under a CFL condition, to commute with affine maps of the data
([scale and affine invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md)), and to make
the scheme evolution invariant. A generic stencil-local dimensionless
transformation $\mathcal D$ is proposed to make the HWENO reconstruction itself
affine-invariant. For [bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md),
the 2D [optimal cell-average decomposition](/methods/optimal-cell-average-decomposition.md)
gives the BP CFL $\varpi_\star=2-\sqrt{14}/2\approx0.1292$ in the isotropic
case, against $1/12$ for the classic Zhang–Shu decomposition.

The numerical evidence: sixth-order or higher accuracy (Burgers, Euler density
wave); unit and data rescaling tests (LWR traffic flow, Lax at
$\lambda\in\{10^{-7},1,10^7\}$, a 2D Riemann problem at $\lambda=100$) in which
OF-HWENO changes and OE-HWENO does not; and extreme tests with the
positivity-preserving limiter (Sedov, Leblanc, double rarefaction, double Mach
reflection, a Mach 2000 jet). The approach is a moment filter, not
[artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md). It is set against
moment limiting, not against viscous regularization.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): cell width $\Delta x$ (source $h_x$),
global wave speed $a$ (source $\alpha$), first moment $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ (source
$\bar v_i$), affine map $u\mapsto\sigma u+\mu$ (source $A_{\lambda,c}$),
OCAD anisotropy $\varkappa$ and boundary weight $\varpi_\star$ (source $\theta$,
$\overline\omega_\star$). The polynomial degree $k$ is page-local (sixth order:
$k=5$).

1. HWENO moment-limiting techniques keep at most fifth-order accuracy for originally sixth-order HWENO schemes. The OE procedure keeps the original order (§1, §2.2.2 before Thm. 2). **Stated** for the earlier works; for OE see claim 6.
2. The damping terms of OF-HWENO contain empirical, problem-dependent parameters. They become highly stiff for strong discontinuities and large-scale problems, so (modified) exponential Runge–Kutta time stepping is required (§1, Remark 1, §3). **Stated**
3. 1D scalar OE step: the exact solution of the damping ODE (2.14) is $\bar u^\sigma_i=\bar u_i$, $\bar u^{(1),\sigma}_i=\bar u^{(1)}_i\exp\big(-a\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\hat\sigma_i(u_h^*)\big)$. The final formula does not need the linear Hermite reconstruction $\Pi_h$ itself (Thm. 1, Eq. 2.16). **Proved**
4. At sixth order the jumps of $u_h^*$ and $\partial_xu_h^*$ at $x_{i+1/2}$ are explicit linear combinations of $\bar u_{i-1},\dots,\bar u_{i+2}$ and $\bar u^{(1)}_{i-1},\dots,\bar u^{(1)}_{i+2}$ (Eq. 2.17, from the interface values on p. 12). **Proved** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the coefficients of Eq. 2.17 are the differences of the printed interface values of $p_0^{(i+1)}$ and $p_0^{(i)}$, and the value $p_0^{(i)}(x^-_{i+1/2})$ reproduces constant data and linear data, for which $\bar u^{(1)}_i=u'\Delta x/12$.)*
5. The OE step preserves cell averages, so it keeps local conservation. It is non-intrusive and costs one multiplication per moment (Remarks 2–3). **Derived**
6. Let $a\Delta t/\Delta x\le C_{\mathrm{cfl}}<1$, $u\in C^{k+1}$, periodic boundaries, and $(k+1)$th-order accurate input moments. Then the output of the OE operator $F_{\mathrm{OE}}$ is also $(k+1)$th-order accurate (Thm. 2, Eq. 2.21; 2D: Thm. 9). **Proved** (the proof of Thm. 9 is omitted as similar). The proof assumes $\max_i\lvert\bar u_i-u_\Omega\rvert\ge C>0$.
7. $F_{\mathrm{OE}}$ commutes with every affine map $u\mapsto\sigma u+\mu$, $\sigma\ne0$, because $\hat\sigma_i(\sigma u_h^*+\mu)=\hat\sigma_i(u_h^*)$ (Def. 1, Thm. 4). The source calls this "scale invariance"; in the bundle's terms it is affine invariance. **Proved**
8. The standard HWENO operator $\mathcal H$ is not affine-invariant. The dimensionless operator $\mathcal H_{\mathcal D}=\mathcal D_u^{-1}\mathcal H\mathcal D$ is affine-invariant if the regularization $\epsilon_{\mathcal D}=10^{-15}$ in $\mathcal D$ is treated as machine zero. The authors say $\mathcal D$ can make any nonlinear reconstruction (WENO, HWENO) affine-invariant. The theorem is proved for HWENO only (Defs. 2–3, Thm. 5). **Proved** (for HWENO)
9. If $f$ and $\hat f$ are homogeneous, the OE-HWENO solution operator with $\mathcal H_{\mathcal D}$ is homogeneous: $E_n(\sigma U^0)=\sigma E_n(U^0)$ (Thm. 6). **Proved** (short argument)
10. Evolution invariance: for $\partial_tu+\lambda\partial_xf(u)=0$ on a fixed mesh at the same CFL number, $E^\lambda_n=E_n$ for all $\lambda>0$, because the OE factor depends on $a\Delta t$, which is invariant (Thm. 7). **Proved** (short argument)
11. The approximate dispersion relation of the sixth-order HWENO scheme with and without OE is "nearly identical", so the OE step does not affect the spectral properties for smooth solutions (§2.2.6, Fig. 2.1). **Shown**
12. 2D OE step: $\bar u^\sigma=\bar u$, and both first moments are multiplied by $\exp\big(-a^{(1)}\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\hat\sigma_{ij}-a^{(2)}\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\tilde\sigma_{ij}\big)$. The jumps are given by constant matrices $A^{(m)},B^{(m)},C^{(m)}$ (App. C) (Thm. 8, Eqs. 2.45–2.46). **Proved**
13. For systems the coefficient is the maximum over components, $\hat\sigma_i=\max_\ell\hat\sigma_i(u_h^{*,\ell})$, and the same exponential factor applies to all components (Eq. 2.29, Thm. 3; 2D Eq. 2.50, Thm. 10). **Stated** (proof omitted as similar)
14. The classic cell-average decomposition is already optimal in 1D, so the bound-preserving analysis is done in 2D only (§2.4). **Stated**
15. With the classic decomposition the 2D scheme is bound-preserving under $\Delta t\big(a^{(1)}/\Delta x+a^{(2)}/\Delta y\big)\le\mathsf w_1^{\mathrm{GL}}=1/12$ at sixth order (Eq. 2.52, following Zhang & Shu). **Stated**
16. OCAD bound-preserving theorem: if the Gauss-point face values lie in $G$ (2.55) and the interior value $\Gamma_{ij}\in G$ (2.56), then $\bar u^{n+1}_{ij}\in G$ under $\Delta t\big(a^{(1)}/\Delta x+a^{(2)}/\Delta y\big)\le\varpi_\star(\varkappa)$ (2.57). The proof writes the update as a convex combination of four 1D three-point first-order [Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) updates (Thm. 11). **Proved**
17. At $\varkappa=0$ ($a^{(1)}\Delta y=a^{(2)}\Delta x$), $\varpi_\star=2-\frac{\sqrt{14}}2\approx0.1292$, "notably milder" than $1/12\approx0.0833$ (p. 31). **Derived** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with the arccos argument $(1476\varkappa^2-244)/(78\varkappa^2+46)^{3/2}$ the printed formula gives $\varpi_\star(0)=0.1291713=2-\sqrt{14}/2$, and $\varpi_\star(\pm1)=1/12$, the 1D Gauss–Lobatto value; see Printed slips.)*
18. This is the first use of the 2D optimal cell-average decomposition to analyse the bound preservation of HWENO. The analysis also applies to standard HWENO without OE (Remark 5). **Stated**
19. Burgers accuracy (1D and 2D, $N_x=30$ to $180$, $T=0.5/\pi$, $\Delta t\propto\Delta x^2$): $\ell^\infty$ orders about 6.1–6.6 in 1D and 5.4–6.4 in 2D (Table 3.1). **Shown**
20. Euler density-wave accuracy ($T=2$): orders about 6.5–7.0, above the design order six. The authors attribute this to the dominance of the high-order damping error, as observed for OEDG, OF-HWENO and OFDG (Table 3.2). **Shown** (the attribution is **Stated**)
21. LWR traffic flow in three equivalent unit systems: the OE-HWENO results agree. OF-HWENO smears in one system and oscillates and shifts the discontinuity in another (Ex. 3, Fig. 3.1). **Shown**
22. Lax problem with data scaled by $\lambda\in\{10^{-7},1,10^7\}$: the OE-HWENO solution scales with the data. OF-HWENO smears at $\lambda=10^7$ and oscillates at $\lambda=10^{-7}$ (Ex. 4, Fig. 3.2). **Shown**
23. On the 1D Sedov problem OF-HWENO depends on its parameter $\omega_d\in\{2.5,3.5,5,10\}$; $\omega_d=10$ works best but still oscillates. Standard HWENO with the positivity-preserving limiter oscillates. OE-HWENO with the limiter is essentially non-oscillatory (Ex. 6, Figs. 3.4–3.5). **Shown**
24. 2D Riemann problem at $\lambda=100$: OF-HWENO "excessively smears", while OE-HWENO matches its $\lambda=1$ result. At $\lambda=1$ OF-HWENO shows a few oscillations near $(0.75,0.65)$ (Ex. 9, Fig. 3.8). **Shown**
25. Mach 2000 jet: standard HWENO with the positivity-preserving limiter shows obvious non-physical oscillations. OE-HWENO resolves the bow shock and the shear layer (Ex. 12, Fig. 3.11). **Shown**
26. OE-HWENO keeps the HWENO features: compact stencils, arbitrary linear weights and high resolution (§1, §4). **Stated**
