# Summary

The paper raises the second-order finite-volume path-conservative
[central-upwind](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) (PCCU) scheme of Castro Díaz,
Kurganov and Morales de Luna (2019) for non-conservative systems
$\partial_tu+\partial_xf(u)+\mathcal C(u)\partial_xu=0$ to fifth order inside the
[alternative WENO (A-WENO)](/methods/alternative-weno.md) finite-difference
framework of Jiang, Shu and Zhang, a [conservative finite-difference
scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md) on point values whose
flux is a finite-volume numerical flux plus high-order derivative corrections. The
device is a quasi-conservative form $\partial_tu+\partial_xK(u)=0$ with the global
flux $K=f+\int^x\mathcal C(u)\partial_\xi u\,d\xi$: the PCCU flux of $K$ enters the
A-WENO flux, and the second- and fourth-derivative corrections of $K$ are split into
finite differences of $f$ and of $\mathcal C(u)\partial_xu$.

The interface states come from fifth-order [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) interpolation
of point values with Jiang–Shu [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md),
in [local characteristic variables](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) of a
Roe-type matrix; the [path-conservative](/methods/path-conservative-scheme.md)
interface terms use the straight-line path. The cell integral of the
non-conservative product, which a finite-difference scheme cannot take from a
reconstructed polynomial, is computed by a new five-point quadrature on the
interfaces, quarter points and centre of the cell, the quarter-point values coming
from WENO-Z interpolation with new linear weights. Two dimensions are handled
dimension by dimension. The relation to the
[fluctuation-form finite-difference WENO](/methods/fluctuation-form-fd-weno.md)
family and the [Lax–Wendroff](/results/lax-wendroff-theorem.md) setting of
non-conservative weak solutions are context.

The application is the two-layer shallow water system written in equilibrium
variables, for which the scheme is claimed [well-balanced](/properties/well-balancing.md)
for the lake at rest; this is asserted, not proved. Nine examples (accuracy, lake-at-rest
perturbations, interface-propagation Riemann problems, internal dam breaks, circular
internal dam breaks; cf. the multiphase [Riemann problems](/benchmarks/gas-liquid-riemann-problems.md)
of gas dynamics) compare the fifth-order scheme with the second-order PCCU scheme,
including one comparison at equal CPU time. The claim that flux splitting is "quite
diffusive" places the scheme in the [numerical-dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md)
debate. Time stepping is [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's $U$, $F$, $B$, cell index $j$,
WENO exponent $p$ and $\varepsilon$ are $u$, $f$, $-\mathcal C$, $i$, $q$ and
$\epsilon$; its one-sided speeds $a^\pm$, linear weights $d_k$, indicators
$\beta_k$, weights $\alpha_k$, $\omega_k$ and global indicator $\tau_5$ agree with
the registry.

1. The second-order PCCU schemes are robust, but resolving internal waves of two-layer shallow water needs very fine meshes; the paper extends them to fifth order in the A-WENO framework (§1, §4). **Stated**
2. Original finite-difference WENO relies on flux splitting, "which is quite diffusive"; A-WENO uses standard finite-volume fluxes without splitting (§1). **Stated**
3. The built-in anti-diffusion term of the central-upwind flux is included here; it "was switched off" in the original PCCU paper, which built on an earlier central-upwind scheme (§2, after Eq. 2.6). **Stated**
4. The A-WENO scheme (3.1)–(3.3) is fifth order once the interface values used in the finite-volume flux are fifth-order accurate (§3). **Stated** (from Jiang, Shu & Zhang 2013)
5. Writing $\partial_tu+\partial_xK(u)=0$ and substituting the PCCU flux of $K$ into the A-WENO flux gives the fifth-order scheme (4.1), with $K_{xx}=f_{xx}+(\mathcal C\partial_xu)_x$ and $K_{xxxx}=f_{xxxx}+(\mathcal C\partial_xu)_{xxx}$ evaluated by explicit finite differences (§4). **Derived**
6. The right-hand side of (4.1) is fifth order provided the cell integral is computed by a fifth-order quadrature; the second-order formula cannot be used because A-WENO reconstructs no uniformly fifth-order polynomial in the cell (§4). **Stated**
7. A new quadrature (4.4) for $\int_{I_i}\sigma\,\partial_x\phi\,dx$ on the five points $x_{i\pm1/2}$, $x_{i\pm1/4}$, $x_i$, built on quartic interpolants (4.3), is fifth-order accurate: "it is easy to show" (§4, Remark 4.1). **Stated** (no proof printed). *(compiler check, not in the sources: (4.4) is exactly the integral of the product of the printed interpolant of $\sigma$ and the derivative of that of $\phi$.)*
8. WENO-Z interpolation at $x_{i-1/4}$ uses the same three parabolas, indicators and $\tau_5=\lvert\beta_2-\beta_0\rvert$, with linear weights $d_0=15/64$, $d_1=21/32$, $d_2=7/64$ (§4). **Stated**. *(compiler check, not in the sources: these weights reproduce the five-point Lagrange interpolant at $x_{i-1/4}$ exactly.)*
9. The two-dimensional scheme (5.2) is obtained dimension by dimension from global fluxes in $x$ and $y$ (§5). **Derived**
10. The two-layer system (6.1) rewritten in equilibrium variables $(h_1,q_1,w,q_2)$, $w=h_2+Z$, gives (6.3); the two forms are equivalent for smooth solutions, and the second-order PCCU schemes applied to them are equivalent, "along the lines of" the original paper (§6.1). **Stated**
11. The A-WENO PCCU schemes for (6.3) and (6.6) are "capable of exactly preserving" the lake at rest (6.2) (§6.1, abstract, conclusions). **Stated** (no proof, no round-off test; Example 2 shows oscillation-free perturbations)
12. The local characteristic decomposition uses the Roe-averaged matrix $A(\hat u_{i+1/2})+\mathcal C(\hat u_{i+1/2})$, with eigenvectors computed numerically and applied to the six values $i-2,\dots,i+3$ (Remarks 6.2–6.3). **Stated**
13. Example 1 (smooth data, $t=0.1$): $L^1$ self-convergence rates of the fifth-order scheme are 7.69, 3.55, 5.01, 5.37 for $h_1+w$ and 4.74, 5.44, 5.72, 6.99 for $h_1$ (final error $9.28\times10^{-9}$ at $\Delta x=\pi/800$); those of the second-order scheme are 2.71–3.22; "the achieved convergence rates even exceed the formal orders for both schemes" (Table 7.1). **Shown** (CFL $\frac1{10}\Delta x^{2/3}$, SSP-RK3)
14. At long time ($t=40$) the fifth-order scheme clearly beats the second-order one on the interface (Fig. 7.2). **Shown**
15. For a $10^{-5}$ perturbation of the lake at rest over smooth and discontinuous bottoms neither well-balanced scheme oscillates, and the fifth-order one resolves much better (Example 2, Fig. 7.3). **Shown**
16. In interface-propagation Riemann problems the intermediate state ($h_1\approx0.475$) and the velocity plateau are "much better resolved" by the fifth-order scheme, which justifies high order "even in examples with Riemann initial data" (Example 3, Fig. 7.4). **Shown**
17. The fifth-order scheme resolves sharply the discontinuous steady interface (hydraulic jump) of the internal dam break, which the second-order one smears (Example 4, Fig. 7.6). **Shown**
18. At approximately equal CPU time, the fifth-order scheme on $\Delta x=1/200$ resolves the 2D interface propagation better than the second-order scheme on $1/450$ (Example 6, Fig. 7.10); no timings or hardware are printed. **Shown**
19. Compared with the earlier central-upwind results of Kurganov & Petrova, the intermediate state is sharper and the solution "substantially less oscillatory", attributed to the characteristic decomposition (Example 7). **Shown** (visual)
20. In the circular internal dam break over a non-flat bottom the fifth-order scheme develops "small water surface oscillations" but much higher resolution (Example 9, Figs. 7.13–7.14). **Shown**
21. Default time stepping is SSP-RK3 at CFL 0.5; the second-order scheme uses the generalized minmod with $\theta=1$ (§7). **Stated**
22. Extension to moving-water steady states by flux globalization is left to future work (§8). **Stated**
