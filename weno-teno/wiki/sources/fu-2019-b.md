# Summary

Fu (2019b) builds a [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) from the
[TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) weighting, the
[TENO-based discontinuity indicator](/methods/teno-discontinuity-indicator.md), and a
[hybrid scheme](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) driven by it. The motive is cost: characteristic
decomposition and nonlinear weights are the expensive parts of a high-order shock-capturing scheme
for systems (§1, §5). The paper first constructs a six-point TENO6 of fourth order, with
spectrally optimized linear weights after Fu et al. (2017) and $C_T=10^{-6}$ (§2). It then recasts the
[monotonicity-preserving limiter](/methods/monotonicity-preserving-limiter.md) and a TVB minmod as
binary flags (§3). The new indicator applies the simplified measure $\gamma_k=(\beta_k+\epsilon)^{-6}$
to four equal three-point stencils spanning the flux stencil and flags a cell if some normalized
$\chi_k$ is not above $C_T=5\times10^{-4}$, which the author presents as a wavenumber cut-off and hence
case-independent (§4). Unflagged cells take the fifth-order upwind flux with global Lax–Friedrichs
dissipation, component-wise and without
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md); flagged cells take TENO6 in
characteristic variables (§5). On linear advection, Burgers, the
[Sod and Lax tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), two
[2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and a periodic implosion
([explosion and implosion problems](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md)) the TENO flag
marks fewer cells than the MP flag (except on Burgers) and depends less on its parameters than the
TVB flag; the hybrid is 3.2–3.84 times faster than TENO6 on configuration 3 (§6, Table 1).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the Notation map). TENO6 candidates use the TENO numbering ($S_0$ central),
the indicator stencils the registry's (most upwind first), as the paper's Figs. 1 and 3 do.

1. TENO6 has four candidates of incremental width, the three WENO5 stencils and $\{i,\dots,i+3\}$, with the candidate fluxes $\tfrac16(-f_{i-1}+5f_i+2f_{i+1})$, $\tfrac16(2f_i+5f_{i+1}-f_{i+2})$, $\tfrac16(2f_{i-2}-7f_{i-1}+11f_i)$, $\tfrac1{12}(3f_i+13f_{i+1}-5f_{i+2}+f_{i+3})$ and Jiang–Shu indicators, the four-point one printed as a $\tfrac1{240}$ quadratic form (§2.2.1–2.2.2, Eqs. (2.8)–(2.10), Fig. 1). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the $\tfrac1{240}$ form equals the Jiang–Shu integral of the cubic exactly.)*
2. Scale separation $\gamma_k=(C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q$ with $\epsilon=10^{-40}$, $C=1$, $q=6$ (Eq. (2.11)); $\tau_6=\lvert\beta_6-\tfrac16(\beta_1+\beta_2+4\beta_0)\rvert=O(\Delta x^6)$ credited to Fu et al. (2017) (Eq. (2.12)), with $\beta_6$ printed in full, prefactor $\tfrac1{120960}$ (Eq. (2.13)). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\beta_6$ is the exact Jiang–Shu integral of the quintic; $\tau_6$ vanishes through $\Delta x^5$ on smooth data.)*
3. Cut-off $\delta_k=0$ if $\chi_k<C_T$, $\omega_k=d_k\delta_k/\sum_ld_l\delta_l$, $C_T=10^{-6}$ "according to spectral analysis and numerical experiment" (§2.2.3, Eqs. (2.14)–(2.17)). **Stated**
4. The linear weights make "the resulting six-point optimal linear scheme ... fourth-order accurate" with an approximate dispersion relation satisfied following Fu et al. (2017): $d=(0.4294317718960898,\ 0.1727270875843552,\ 0.0855682281039113,\ 0.312272912415645)$ (§2.2.3). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the combined flux is exact to fourth order, not fifth; $d_1\ne0.3$, so it is not the TENO6-opt of Fu et al. (2016); the weights are those of TENO6-A in Fu et al. (2019d) and give the [MDCD](/methods/mdcd-scheme.md) flux with $c_{\mathrm{disp}}=0.05455$, $c_{\mathrm{diss}}=0.0025$.)*
5. The MP limiter becomes a flag: $\mathrm{Flag}=1$ iff the MP clip of the value of TENO6's linear scheme differs from it, with $\xi_{\mathrm{MP}}=2$, large-curvature constant 4 and the four-argument limited curvature for both bounds, after Suresh & Huynh and Qiu & Shu (§3.1, Eqs. (3.1)–(3.11)). **Stated** (definition)
6. The TVB flag tests the third-order value on $\{i-1,i,i+1\}$ with a TVB-corrected minmod of threshold $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}\Delta x^2$; small $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}$ behaves as minmod, large $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}$ misses troubled cells and gives Gibbs oscillations, after Qiu & Shu (§3.2, Eqs. (3.12)–(3.16)). **Stated**
7. "The TENO weighting strategy can explicitly distinguish smooth scales from nonsmooth scales in spectral space"; $C_T$ "determines the cut-off wavenumber" and "takes effects in wavespace rendering its high generality", against thresholds "typically defined in physical space" (Abstract, §1, §4, Fig. 2). **Stated** (the evidence, Fig. 2, is the approximate dispersion relation of five-point TENO reproduced from Fu et al. 2016, not an analysis of the indicator)
8. Indicator: four equal three-point stencils $\{i-2,i-1,i\}$, $\{i-1,i,i+1\}$, $\{i,i+1,i+2\}$, $\{i+1,i+2,i+3\}$, the last with $\beta_3=\tfrac13[u_{i+1}(22u_{i+1}-73u_{i+2}+29u_{i+3})+u_{i+2}(61u_{i+2}-49u_{i+3})+10u_{i+3}^2]$ "rather than" a four-point stencil, to cut cost; no candidate flux computed (§4.1, Eq. (4.1), Fig. 3). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: Eq. (4.1) is exactly the Jiang–Shu integral over $I_i$ of the quadratic of $\{i+1,i+2,i+3\}$ extrapolated to the target cell.)*
9. $\gamma_k=1/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^q$, $q=6$, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ (1D) and $10^{-3}$ (multi-D), $\chi_k=\gamma_k/\sum_l\gamma_l$, $\mathrm{Flag}=0$ iff $\chi_k>C_T$ for all $k$, $C_T=5\times10^{-4}$; the full TENO measure "is also applicable but with more complexity"; a cell is troubled "if there exists at least one candidate stencil" judged non-smooth (§4.2, Eqs. (4.2)–(4.4)). **Stated** (definition)
10. Hybrid: in unflagged cells "the fifth-order Lax-Friedrichs scheme without characteristic decomposition" (Eq. (5.1)), $a$ the maximum characteristic speed over the whole domain; in flagged cells TENO6 in characteristic variables with Roe-averaged left eigenvectors and Rusanov splitting per field (§5, Eq. (5.2), Algorithm 1), dimension by dimension. **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with $f_{i+2}$ corrected to $f_{i-2}$, Eq. (5.1) is the fifth-order upwind flux applied to $f^\pm=\tfrac12(f\pm au)$.)*
11. The indicator reads density and total energy in 1D, density alone in 2D, in physical space; a shock oblique to the grid "may be only detected as a discontinuity in one dimension", and $\mathrm{Flag}=\max(\mathrm{Flag}_x,\mathrm{Flag}_y)$ is named as a remedy "if deemed necessary", but "the current implementation", with separate flags, "can generate oscillation-free solutions" (§6). **Stated**
12. Setup: finite differences after Jiang & Shu, SSP-RK3 at CFL 0.4, Rusanov splitting for TENO6 unless stated, all parameters "fixed as the recommended data without tuning case by case" (§6, Eq. (6.1)). **Stated**
13. Linear advection ($200$ points, $t=8$): the TENO flag and TVB with $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}=10$ "perform the best" (§6.1, Fig. 4). **Shown** (visual)
14. Burgers ($u_0=0.5+\sin\pi x$, 100 and 200 cells, $t=1.5/\pi$): the MP flag is best, the TENO flag marks two more cells at 100 points, the TVB flag oscillates for $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}=10,100,1000$ (§6.2, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
15. Sod and Lax (200, 400, 800 points): the TENO flag identifies shock, contact and expansion, the solutions converge, the flagged cells decrease with refinement, and the MP flag marks "much more cells" (§6.3, Figs. 7–9). **Shown**
16. Shu–Osher (200, 400, 800 points; reference WENO5-JS on 2000): the TENO flag separates smooth waves from shocklets, its troubled fraction decreases with resolution; the MP flag fires on constant data and its $10^{-10}$-tolerance variant (Eq. (6.8)) "does not identify the shocklets and sharp features as accurate as the proposed" indicator; the TENO flag is insensitive to $\epsilon=10^{-3},10^{-4},10^{-5}$ and $C_T=5\times10^{-3},5\times10^{-4},5\times10^{-5}$ at 400 points; the TVB flag at 400 points "fails to distinguish the physical fluctuations from the shock discontinuities" (§6.4, Figs. 10–15). **Shown**
17. Blast waves (400 points, Roe with entropy fix): hybrid TENO6 "much less dissipative than the hybrid WENO5 scheme"; 80–90 % of the cells take the linear flux (§6.5, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
18. 2D Riemann configurations 12 ($t=0.25$) and 3 ($t=0.3$) on $512\times512$: more than 98 % of the cells linear; the MP flag marks more; the TVB flag depends strongly on $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}$ (§6.6, Figs. 18–23). **Shown**
19. Table 1 (configuration 3): TENO6 69.75, 593.7, 5752.1 s against the hybrid's 21.15, 186, 1499.5 s on $256^2$, $512^2$, $1024^2$, speed-ups 3.3, 3.2, 3.84, "much higher than" Hybrid-cF and Hybrid-MR of Don et al. (2016); "the same desktop workstation", unspecified (§6.6). **Shown** *(Compiler check: the printed ratios follow from the printed times.)*
20. Double Mach reflection ($800\times200$, $t=0.2$): more than 90 % of the cells linear (0.91–0.98, read from Fig. 25); small scales finer than nine-point WENO-Z and comparable to eleven-point WENO-Z of Castro et al. (2011, Fig. 9 there) at the same resolution (§6.7, Figs. 24–25). **Shown** / across papers
21. Implosion ($1024\times1024$, $t=0.75$, periodic $[0,0.6]^2$): more than 97 % of the cells linear, better than the MP and TVB flags; TVB best at $M_{\mathrm{TVB}}=1000$ (§6.8, Figs. 26–29). **Shown**
22. Classical indicators (minmod-TVB, Harten's subcell resolution, average total variation) are "mostly case-sensitive due to the fact that only low-order information is examined and problem-dependent parameters are typically involved" (§1). **Stated**
23. The proposed algorithm "delivers better performance with a fixed set of parameters for all considered benchmarks" (Abstract, §7). **Stated** ($\epsilon$ differs between 1D and multi-D, and the TENO6 splitting changes on the blast waves)

# Printed slips

- **Lax left velocity** (§6.3, Eq. (6.5)): printed $(\rho,v,p)=(0.445,0.689,3.528)$; the standard data, and Fu et al. (2019d) Eq. (42), have $0.698$ (as Fu et al. (2016) print 0.689, see the [Sod and Lax shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md)).
- **Eq. (5.1):** the central part is printed $(f_{i+3}-8f_{i+2}+37f_{i+1}+37f_i-8f_{i-1}+f_{i+2})$, with $f_{i+2}$ twice and no $f_{i-2}$; it must end $+f_{i-2}$ *(compiler check, not in the sources: the fifth-order upwind flux with global Lax–Friedrichs splitting gives $\tfrac1{60}(1,-8,37,37,-8,1)$ on $f_{i-2},\dots,f_{i+3}$; the dissipative part prints correctly)*.
- **Eq. (2.6):** the sum over the candidates is printed $\sum_{k=0}^{r_k-1}$; the candidates run $k=0,\dots,K-3$, as the text's "$K-2$ candidate stencil fluxes" says.
- **Fig. 3 against §4.1:** §4.1 says that the indicators of $S_0$, $S_1$, $S_2$ "are identical to that of Eq. (2.10)", but Fig. 3 labels $\{i-2,i-1,i\}$, $\{i-1,i,i+1\}$, $\{i,i+1,i+2\}$ as $S_0$, $S_1$, $S_2$, which are Eq. (2.10)'s $\beta_{2,3}$, $\beta_{0,3}$, $\beta_{1,3}$; the set is the same, the labels are permuted. Eq. (4.2) keeps the subscript $\beta_{k,r_k}$ although every width is 3.
- **§3.1, after Eq. (3.5):** "CFL $\le1/(1+\text{ff})$", a glyph misprint for $1/(1+\alpha)$; with the recommended $\alpha=2$ the bound is $1/3$, below the CFL 0.4 of all runs (harmless: the MP limiter only flags).
- **§3.1:** "the interface $x_{1+1/2}$" for $x_{i+1/2}$; **§1:** "minimod-based TVB limiter".
- **§2.2.3:** the printed $d_k$ sum to $1+1.3\times10^{-15}$ (rounding of the printed digits).
- **§6.4:** no final time is printed for the Shu–Osher problem (the flag histories end near $t\approx1.8$).
- **§6.5:** "hybrid WENO5" is used without definition.
- **§6.8:** the implosion is attributed to Liska & Wendroff [46] and the Shadowfax code paper [47], but the printed low state occupies the square $\lvert x-0.3\rvert<0.15$ **and** $\lvert y-0.3\rvert<0.15$ of the periodic box $[0,0.6]^2$, not the Liska–Wendroff diamond *(compiler check: area 0.09 against 0.045; a different problem)*.
- **Abstract, §7:** "a fixed set of parameters for all considered benchmarks" against $\epsilon=10^{-4}$ (1D) and $10^{-3}$ (multi-D) (§4.2) and Roe against Rusanov splitting by case (§6, §6.5).
- **Fig. 2 caption:** "five-point stencil schemes"; the figure, reproduced from Fu et al. (2016), is the spectral evidence offered for a six-point indicator with a different measure (a gap between evidence and claim, not an error).
