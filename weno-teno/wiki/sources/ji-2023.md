# Summary

The sequel to [Ji et al. (2022)](/sources/ji-2022.md). It builds cell-centred
finite-volume [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) reconstructions of fourth to seventh
order (TENO4-D to TENO7-D) on triangular, quadrilateral, tetrahedral and mixed
meshes in the open-source code [UCNS3D](/software/ucns3d.md). The candidates are
arranged as in [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md). One large central-biased stencil
$S_{\mathrm L}$ carries a polynomial $P_{\mathrm L}$ of the target order, and $n_s$
small directional stencils carry low-order polynomials $P_k$. All are
least-squares fits of cell averages in a reference space, on stencils of $2N_k$
cells.

The named contribution is the "dual ENO-like stencil selection". The measure
$\gamma_k=(\beta_k+\epsilon)^{-6}$, with $\epsilon=10^{-12}$, is built on a
reference-space [smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md). It is
normalized over all $n_s+1$ candidates and cut off at $C_{T,1}=10^{-7}$. If the
large stencil survives, the reconstruction is $P_{\mathrm L}$ exactly. Otherwise the
measure is renormalized among the small stencils and cut off at the larger
$C_{T,2}=10^{-5}$, and the reconstruction is the mean of the surviving $P_k$. The
small $C_{T,1}$ is meant to keep the linear scheme up to higher wavenumbers, and the
larger $C_{T,2}$ to give strong adaptation at discontinuities. The 2022 paper had
the same two-step structure with one $C_T=10^{-6}$.

Fluxes are [HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) in the rotated face frame,
chosen over HLLC and Roe against the
[carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md). Time stepping is
[SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) at CFL 0.4, with fixed parameters
throughout. The evidence is numerical:

* 2D linear advection, where TENO-D reproduces the linear schemes digit for digit;
* [solid-body rotation](/benchmarks/solid-body-rotation.md) and two 2D
  [explosions](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md);
* the [Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) and the
  [double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md);
* [shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) and
  Schardin's [shock–wedge diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md);
* the [triple-point problem](/benchmarks/triple-point-problem.md) and a 3D spherical
  explosion.

TENO6-D and TENO7-D are less dissipative than CWENO6 and CWENO7 at about the same
cost. No theorem is proved.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the page-local symbols of [Ji et al. (2022)](/sources/ji-2022.md):
large stencil $S_{\mathrm L}$ and polynomial $P_{\mathrm L}$, small stencils $S_k$
and polynomials $P_k$ for $k=0,\dots,n_s-1$, polynomial degree $r$ (order $r+1$),
space dimension $n_d$, and $N_k$ the number of polynomial coefficients.

1. On unstructured triangular and tetrahedral meshes the optimal linear weights of classical WENO "may vary significantly for different quadrature points and different mesh topologies, and even become negative", which can cause instability and needs special treatment (§1, §3.1.2). **Stated**
2. TENO-D uses one large central-biased stencil with the target-order polynomial and $n_s$ small directional stencils with low-order polynomials. The large stencil is built by Tsoutsanis's Naive Cell Based algorithm, the small ones by the "Type3" algorithm (§3.2.1). **Stated**
3. For TENO4-D on a 3D mixed mesh the large stencil is fourth order and there are six third-order small directional stencils, $n_s=6$ (Fig. 1 caption). **Stated**
4. The stencil size is $2N_k$ with $N_k=\frac1{n_d!}\prod_{m=1}^{n_d}(r+m)$, "a balanced performance between numerical stability and computational cost" (§3.1.1, Eq. (16)). **Stated**
5. The least-squares system is solved by the pseudo-inverse $(\mathcal A^\top\mathcal A)^{-1}\mathcal A^\top$, which depends only on the geometry and is precomputed once by Householder QR (§3.1.1, Eqs. (20)–(22)). **Stated**
6. The reconstruction is carried out in a reference space $(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$ for better conditioning and less scaling effect; non-simplex elements are first split into triangles or tetrahedra (§3.1.1, Eq. (17)). **Stated**
7. Stage 1: $\gamma_k=(\beta_k+\epsilon)^{-6}$ with $\epsilon=10^{-12}$ for all $n_s+1$ candidates, normalized to $\chi_{k,1}$, with $\delta_{k,1}=0$ if $\chi_{k,1}<C_{T,1}=10^{-7}$. If the large stencil survives, the reconstruction is $P_{\mathrm L}$ (§3.2.2, Eqs. (29)–(32)). **Stated** (definition)
8. Stage 2, if the large stencil is cut: $\chi_{k,2}=\gamma_k/\sum_{l<n_s}\gamma_l$, cut-off $C_{T,2}=10^{-5}$, and the reconstruction $\sum_{k<n_s}\omega_kP_k$ with $\omega_k=d_k\delta_{k,2}/\sum_ld_l\delta_{l,2}$ and $d_k=1/n_s$ (§3.2.2, Eqs. (33)–(36)). **Stated** (definition). With equal $d_k$ this is the mean of the surviving small polynomials, and at least one survives, since the largest $\chi_{k,2}\ge1/n_s>C_{T,2}$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
9. When the large stencil is judged smooth, "the desirable high-order accuracy is restored exactly without any compromise", whereas CWENO recovers the target reconstruction only asymptotically (§3.2.2, §3.3). **Stated** (immediate from the definition)
10. $C_{T,2}>C_{T,1}$ gives "a stronger separation of the discontinuities", so that "sufficient numerical dissipation is generated for capturing discontinuities stably and sharply" (§3.2.2, §3.3, §5). **Stated**
11. The $C_T$ parameters "function in the spectral space" and are "rather generally applicable", giving good results without case-by-case tuning, as in Cartesian TENO. The CWENO linear weights are "rather ad hoc" (§3.3). **Stated**
12. The framework "allows for arbitrarily high-order unstructured TENO reconstructions with good numerical robustness" (§1, §3.3, §5). **Stated** (shown only for orders 4–7)
13. Relative to the 2022 version, the "novel dual ENO-like stencil selection" is the new element: the smaller stage-1 cut-off lets the linear scheme resolve smooth flows of higher wavenumber (§3.3). **Stated**
14. Linear advection $\partial_tu+\partial_xu+\partial_yu=0$ with $u_0=\sin(2\pi x)\sin(2\pi y)$, periodic, $t=1$, on triangular meshes of 374 to 58,330 elements. The TENO-D errors equal the linear schemes' at every resolution. The finest-pair ($h=1/80\to1/160$) $L_\infty$/$L_2$ orders are 3.61/3.83 (fourth order), 4.93/5.01 (fifth), 5.97/5.90 (sixth) and 6.44/6.53 (seventh), with finest $L_\infty$ errors $5.60\times10^{-7}$, $3.33\times10^{-8}$, $3.72\times10^{-10}$, $2.86\times10^{-11}$. The text says "the expected convergence orders are obtained without degeneration" (Tables 1–4). **Shown**
15. Solid-body rotation (14,860 triangles, $h\approx1/80$, one revolution): all TENO-D keep the shapes well, and "No significant improvement can be noticed by increasing the reconstruction accuracy order" (§4.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
16. 2D explosions EP01 and EP02 (18,464 triangles, $h\approx1/50$, $t=0.2$): discontinuities are captured without obvious oscillations, in agreement with the reference and with CWENO of equal order (§4.3, Figs. 3–6). **Shown**
17. Shu–Osher (200 cells, "triangular mesh with $h\approx1/20$", $t=1.8$, reference WENO5-JS on 2000 cells): TENO6-D and TENO7-D "perform much better than the CWENO schemes of same accuracy order" on the high-wavenumber density waves, and are similar to fourth- and fifth-order [WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md) at $h=1/40$ in a figure of another paper (§4.4, Figs. 7–8). **Shown** (the WENO-AO comparison is figure to figure)
18. Shu–Osher cost ($h\approx1/20$): CWENO6 173.12 s, CWENO7 211.01 s, TENO6-D 164.26 s, TENO7-D 196.57 s, "approximately the same". Hardware, compiler and parallel setup are not stated (§4.4, Table 6). **Shown**
19. Double Mach reflection on 151,850, 369,794 and 752,708 triangles ($h\approx1/125$, $1/200$, $1/280$), $t=0.2$: higher-order TENO-D resolves more small scales in the blow-up region while keeping the shocks sharp, and at equal order shows "much lower numerical dissipation" than CWENO (§4.5, Figs. 9–14). **Shown**
20. TENO-D at $h\approx1/200$ is "significantly better" than fifth-order WENO-AO at $h\approx1/400$, in a figure of another paper (§4.5). **Shown** (figure to figure)
21. DMR cost ($h\approx1/125$): CWENO6 2517.38 s, CWENO7 3050.80 s, TENO6-D 2694.45 s, TENO7-D 3126.84 s, "approximately the same" (§4.5, Table 7). **Shown**. The TENO-D/CWENO ratios are 0.949 and 0.932 in Table 6 but 1.070 and 1.025 in Table 7 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
22. Shock–vortex interaction (Mach 1.1 shock, 45,790 triangles, "$h\approx1/150$", $t=0.6$): all schemes give similar solutions, with no artificial instabilities (§4.6, Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. Shock–wedge (Schardin) problem on 403,660 triangles: TENO7-D at $t=2.1$ agrees well with the shadowgraph in Van Dyke's album, and all orders give similar shock patterns and counter-rotating vortices at $t=4.0$ with no visible oscillations (§4.7, Figs. 16–17). **Shown** (qualitative)
24. Single-material triple point on 210,000 and 840,000 quadrilaterals ($h\approx1/100$, $1/200$), $t=5.0$: higher-order TENO-D is less dissipative and resolves the interfacial instability better, and is better than CWENO of equal order, "particularly in the vicinity of the tips of the mushroom structures" (§4.8, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
25. 3D explosion on uniform tetrahedra ($h\approx1/30$, $t=0.25$; the sentence is printed in §4.8): no obvious artefacts, and the TENO6-D and TENO7-D density and pressure profiles agree with the analytical solution and with CWENO (§4.9, Fig. 21). **Shown**
26. HLL is chosen over HLLC and Roe for robustness: contact-resolving solvers "may suffer from stubborn Carbuncle phenomena" near strong shocks, and the dissipation is controlled through the low-dissipation reconstruction instead (§2.2). **Stated**
27. CWENO keeps the "smooth weighting" of WENO, so "the nonlinear adaptation will be activated in the low-wavenumber ranges generating excessive numerical dissipation", which is undesirable for multiscale simulations (§3.1.2). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (43): the vortex temperature perturbation is printed $\tilde T=-\frac{(\gamma-1)\varepsilon^2e^{2a(1-\gamma^2)}}{4a\gamma}$. The exponent contains $\gamma^2$ where the velocity terms have $\tau^2$, $e^{a(1-\tau^2)}$. A radial profile needs $e^{2a(1-\tau^2)}$.
- Eqs. (16) and (18): $N_k=\frac1{n_d!}\prod_{m=1}^{n_d}(r+m)$ counts all coefficients of a degree-$r$ polynomial including the constant ($r=1$, $n_d=2$ gives 3), yet (18) sums over $l=0,\dots,N_k$, i.e. $N_k+1$ terms, and (19)–(20) solve for $l=1,\dots,N_k$. The indices are off by one *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- The sentence "The final simulation time is t = 0.25. A uniform tetrahedron mesh with edge length h ≈ 1/30 is adopted." closes §4.8 (the triple point, run to $t=5.0$ on quadrilaterals) but describes the 3D explosion of §4.9. The 3D domain is never stated.
- The quoted mesh sizes follow two definitions. For 45,790 triangles on $[0,2]\times[0,1]$, "$h\approx1/150$" equals $\sqrt{A}$ of the mean element area $A$, while the equilateral edge $\sqrt{4A/\sqrt3}$ is $\approx1/100$. For solid-body rotation (14,860 on the unit square, "$h\approx1/80$") the equilateral edge gives $1/80$ and $\sqrt A$ gives $1/122$. §§4.2, 4.3 and 4.5 follow the edge, §§4.6 and 4.7 follow $\sqrt A$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §4.3 places the explosion domain, "a unit circle of radius R = 1", at "[1, 1]", while the profile axes of Figs. 4 and 6 are symmetric about 0.
- §4.4 speaks of "200 uniformly distributed mesh cells" for a 1D problem, while the captions speak of a triangular mesh with $h\approx1/20$, without the number of triangles.
- Typos: "HLL Riemman Solver" (§2.2), "Schearldin's problem" (§4.7), "lager $C_T$" (§3.3), "large central-based stencil" (§3.3, elsewhere "central-biased"). The CWENO weights are written $w_k$ in Eq. (23) but $\omega_k$ in Eq. (25).
