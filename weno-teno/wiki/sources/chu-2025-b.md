# Summary

Chu et al. (2025b) start from the second-order
[path-conservative](/methods/path-conservative-scheme.md) central-upwind (PCCU)
schemes of Castro Díaz, Kurganov & Morales de Luna, made
[well balanced](/properties/well-balancing.md) by
[flux globalization](/methods/flux-globalization.md) in
[Kurganov et al. (2023)](/sources/kurganov-2023.md). These schemes are robust but
dissipative. The authors replace the scalar central-upwind diffusion by the
[LCD central-upwind flux](/methods/lcd-central-upwind-scheme.md) of
[Chertock et al. (2023)](/sources/chertock-2023.md), which had been used only for
conservation laws. The LCD-CU flux is applied to the quasi-conservative form
$\partial_tu+\partial_xK=0$, $K=f-\mathcal G$, so the
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) uses the
eigensystem of $A+\mathcal C$ rather than the flux Jacobian. The authors argue that
this amounts to a very specific discretization of the source and nonconservative
terms, hard to design without flux globalization.

§2 restates a modified LCD-CU scheme: generalized-minmod reconstruction of
conservative rather than characteristic variables ($\theta_{\mathrm{mm}}=1.3$), and a
new three-branch desingularization of the diagonal weights. §3 builds the 1D LCD-PCCU
scheme. It reconstructs equilibrium variables and uses modified states
$\check u^\pm$ in the diffusion term, which gives a short well-balancing proof
(Remark 3.1). §4 extends the scheme dimension by dimension to 2D, where it is well
balanced only for steady states with $x$-independent $K$ and $y$-independent
$K^{(2)}$.

Tests (SSP-RK3, CFL 0.45) cover the γ-based nonconservative
[multicomponent Euler equations](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md) with
[stiffened gases](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md): 1D gas and
water–air and 2D helium and R22 [shock–bubble](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md)
problems, and an [underwater explosion](/benchmarks/underwater-explosion.md). They also
cover the thermal rotating [shallow-water equations](/definitions/shallow-water-equations.md):
a constant-pressure equilibrium and its perturbation, a
[dam break](/benchmarks/dam-break-problem.md) over a bump, a perturbed 2D steady state,
a radial dam break and equatorial β-plane anomalies. LCD-PCCU has the smaller $L^1$
error in every table and is more accurate than PCCU at equal CPU time (Example 10).

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the registry form $\partial_tu+\partial_xf+\mathcal C(u)\partial_xu=S$
of the [path-conservative](/methods/path-conservative-scheme.md) page: the source
writes $U_t+F_x=BU_x+S$, so its $B$ is $-\mathcal C$, and its matrix
$\mathcal A=\partial F/\partial U-B$ is $A+\mathcal C$. The source's $W$, $K$, $L$ are
$\mathcal G$, $K$, $K^{(2)}$ (§10, flux globalization); its $E$ and $\breve U^\pm$ are
$\mathsf E$ and $\check u^\pm$; its $P^{LCD},M^{LCD},Q^{LCD}$ are
$\mathcal P,\mathcal M,\mathcal Q$; its $\Gamma$, $\Pi$, $\pi_\infty$ are $\mathsf G$,
$\mathsf A$, $p_\infty$. Page-local: $\theta_{\mathrm{mm}}$ the generalized-minmod
parameter, $\varepsilon_0$ the desingularization constant, $\lambda^\pm_l$ the
one-sided per-field speeds.

1. LCD-CU fluxes, introduced for conservation laws, extend to nonconservative balance laws when applied to the flux-globalized form $\partial_tu+\partial_xK=0$, $K=f-\mathcal G$, $\mathcal G=\int_{\hat x}^x[-\mathcal C\,\partial_\xi u+S]\,d\xi$ (Eqs. (1.2)–(1.3)); otherwise "its proper extension to the hyperbolic systems of balance laws is not straightforward" (§1). **Stated**
2. The approach is "equivalent to a very specific discretization of the source and nonconservative product terms, which could be hard to develop without the flux globalization approach" (§1). **Stated**
3. The LCD-CU flux is $\hat f_{i+1/2}=R\mathcal PL\,f(u^-)+R\mathcal ML\,f(u^+)+R\mathcal QL\,(u^+-u^-)$ at $x_{i+1/2}$ (Eq. (2.2)), and with $\mathcal P+\mathcal M=I$ (Eq. (2.9)) it is identical to the flux of Chertock et al. (Remark 2.1). **Stated**
4. The field-wise weights are $(\lambda^+_l,-\lambda^-_l,\lambda^+_l\lambda^-_l)/(\lambda^+_l-\lambda^-_l)$ when $\lambda^+_l-\lambda^-_l>\varepsilon_0$, fall back to the scalar central-upwind weights $(a^+,-a^-,a^+a^-)/(a^+-a^-)$ when only $a^+-a^->\varepsilon_0$, and to $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,0)$ otherwise, $\varepsilon_0=10^{-18}$ (Eq. (2.8)); the authors find this desingularization "more robust" than the earlier one (Remark 2.3). **Stated** (no evidence shown)
5. Reconstructing conservative variables instead of local characteristic ones causes no substantial spurious oscillations in any example and makes the scheme more efficient than the original LCD-CU scheme (Remark 2.2). **Shown** (qualitatively; no timing for this point)
6. In LCD-PCCU the characteristic decomposition and the one-sided speeds use the eigensystem of $A+\mathcal C$ evaluated at an average state $\hat u_{i+1/2}$ (§3). **Derived**
7. The values $u^\pm_{i+1/2}$ recovered from the reconstructed equilibrium variables cannot enter the diffusion term, because $u^+\ne u^-$ is possible at discrete steady states; modified states $\check u^\pm$ with $\check u^+=\check u^-$ whenever $\mathsf E^+=\mathsf E^-$, and $\check u^\pm=u^\pm+\mathcal O(\Delta x^2)$ for smooth data, are used instead (§3). **Derived**
8. The 1D semi-discrete LCD-PCCU scheme is well balanced: if $\mathsf E_i\equiv\hat{\mathsf E}$, then $K^\pm_{i+1/2}=\hat K$ for all $i$ (Eq. (3.4)), and with $\mathcal P+\mathcal M=I$ and $\check u^+=\check u^-$ the numerical flux equals $\hat K$, so the right-hand side vanishes (Remark 3.1). **Proved** (short proof, after Kurganov et al.)
9. The 2D scheme preserves exactly the discrete steady states with $K$ independent of $x$ and $K^{(2)}$ independent of $y$, not genuinely 2D ones; "to the best of our knowledge, no existing numerical method is capable of exactly preserving general 2-D steady states, even the smooth ones" (Remark 4.1). **Stated** (proof omitted)
10. For the γ-based multifluid system the conservative form in $\rho\mathsf G$, $\rho\mathsf A$ may give significant spurious velocity and pressure oscillations at material interfaces, so the nonconservative advection of $\mathsf G$, $\mathsf A$ is solved (Remark 5.2). **Stated**
11. On the nonconservative multifluid system LCD-PCCU satisfies Abgrall's principle: velocity and pressure stay constant near isolated contacts, "as one can easily prove" (Remark 5.2). **Stated** (no proof; see [pressure-equilibrium preservation](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md))
12. Example 1 (1D gas shock–bubble), $L^1$ errors in $\rho$ at $\Delta x=1/50,1/100,1/200$: PCCU 1.16e0, 6.65e-1, 3.39e-1 (rates 0.803, 0.972); LCD-PCCU 8.53e-1, 4.21e-1, 1.96e-1 (rates 1.019, 1.103) (Table 5.1). **Shown**
13. Example 2 (1D water–air shock–bubble), $L^1$ errors in $\rho$ at $\Delta x=1/10,1/20,1/40$: PCCU 4.06e-1, 2.57e-1, 1.53e-1 (0.660, 0.748); LCD-PCCU 1.93e-1, 1.05e-1, 5.70e-2 (0.878, 0.881), with a sharper contact near $x\approx3$ (Table 5.2). **Shown**
14. Examples 3–4 (shock–helium and shock–R22 bubbles, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/500$, $t=3$): LCD-PCCU resolves the bubble interface and its instabilities better at all times, in agreement with the Haas–Sturtevant experiment and earlier computations (Figs. 5.4–5.5). **Shown** (qualitative)
15. Example 5 (cylindrical underwater explosion): PCCU on $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/160$ is close to LCD-PCCU on $1/80$ at $t=0.02$ (Figs. 5.6–5.7). **Shown** (qualitative)
16. Example 6 (constant-pressure equilibrium of the 1D Ripa system): both schemes preserve the steady state exactly, "the obtained errors for both h and b are zeros"; for its perturbation the $L^1$ errors in $h$ at $t=1.2$, $\Delta x=1/20,1/40,1/80$, are PCCU 8.16e-2, 4.72e-2, 2.49e-2 (0.790, 0.923) and LCD-PCCU 1.25e-2, 8.55e-3, 5.05e-3 (0.548, 0.760), the latter "much more accurate" and "substantially less oscillatory" (§5.2.1, Table 5.3). **Shown**
17. Example 7 (dam break over a non-flat bottom), $L^1$ errors in $h+Z$ at $t=0.2$, $\Delta x=1/100,1/200,1/400$: PCCU 5.44e-2, 3.08e-2, 1.48e-2 (0.821, 1.057); LCD-PCCU 2.99e-2, 1.65e-2, 7.50e-3 (0.858, 1.137), the middle entry corrected from the printed 1.65e-3 (Table 5.4; see Printed slips). **Shown**
18. Examples 8–9 (2D perturbed steady state, radial dam break, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/50$): LCD-PCCU resolves $h$ and $b$ more sharply (Figs. 5.12–5.15). **Shown** (qualitative)
19. Example 10 (β-plane anomalies, $t=120$): at equal CPU time, LCD-PCCU on $\Delta x=\Delta y=2/15$ against PCCU on $1/9$, LCD-PCCU is still more accurate; no absolute CPU times or hardware are given (Fig. 5.18). **Shown** (qualitative)
20. Appendices A and B give $\hat A+\hat{\mathcal C}$, $R$ and $L$ in closed form for the 1D and 2D multifluid and thermal rotating shallow-water systems; the eigenvalues are $\hat v-\hat c,\hat v,\dots,\hat v+\hat c$ with $\hat c=\sqrt{\hat\gamma(\hat p+\hat p_\infty)/\hat\rho}$, and $\hat v\mp\sqrt{\hat b\hat h}$, $\hat v$ for shallow water. **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: symbolically, the printed $R$ of A.1, A.2, B.1, B.2 diagonalize the printed matrices with these eigenvalues, and the printed $R^{-1}$ of B.1 and B.2 are exact inverses; for A.1 and A.2 see Printed slips.)*
