# Summary

The paper builds a non-dissipative, second-order central discretization of the
Kapila [five-equation model](/definitions/five-equation-model.md) with
[stiffened-gas](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md) phases,
combined with the authors' conservative diffuse-interface (phase-field)
regularization. That regularization adds a volume-fraction flux
$a_k=\Gamma_{\mathrm I}\big(\epsilon_{\mathrm I}\nabla\phi_k-\phi_k(1-\phi_k)n_k\big)$
and consistent corrections to mass, momentum and energy. The target is a KEEP
(kinetic energy- and entropy-preserving) scheme that stays stable in
under-resolved inviscid turbulence of two-phase flows, where single-phase
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) schemes
diverge. No [numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) and
no subgrid model are used.

Analytically, phase entropy is conserved only when the interface is in its
tanh equilibrium profile, so the regularization is irreversible.
Discretely, the [conservative flux-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
scheme is written component by component in
[split convective forms](/methods/split-convective-forms.md) (divergence,
quadratic, cubic, and new quartic and quintic splits). Theorems 3.1–3.2 give
the global and local kinetic-energy conditions. A mass-summation condition, an
interface-equilibrium condition (uniform velocity and pressure stay uniform,
the multiphase analogue of [stationary contact preservation](/properties/stationary-contact-preservation.md))
and a pressure-work condition then fix the scheme once the mass and sharpening
fluxes are chosen. The resulting internal-energy flux, $\hat I=\{\!\{\rho e\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}$
(QS), is compared with Kennedy–Gruber/Kuya (CS) and Chandrashekar's
[entropy-conservative-type](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md) harmonic-mean
form (CS-H).

In the inviscid [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) ($64^3$)
and in isotropic turbulence ($32^3$), all three forms are stable for a single
phase, but with a second fluid only QS conserves kinetic energy and
approximately entropy, and CS and CS-H diverge. "Entropy preserving" means
observed approximate conservation of physical phase entropy, not
[entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md) in Tadmor's sense.
Time stepping is classical RK4, not [SSP](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). A
$256^3$ droplet-laden decaying turbulence reproduces the incompressible trends
of Dodd & Ferrante (2016).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's grid index $m$ is $i$,
its two-point averages $\overline{(\cdot)}^{(m\pm1/2)}$ are $\{\!\{\cdot\}\!\}$,
its velocity $u_i$ is $v$, its phase index $l$ is $k$ ($\phi_k$, $\rho_k$), its
stiffening pressure $\pi_l$ is $p_{\infty,k}$, its regularization scales
$\Gamma$, $\epsilon$ are $\Gamma_{\mathrm I}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm I}$, its
specific kinetic energy $k$ is $\tfrac12\lvert v\rvert^2$.

1. For compressible flows discrete kinetic-energy conservation is not sufficient for stability, unlike incompressible flows, and entropy constraints are also needed; for compressible two-phase flows entropy stability is not sufficient either: volume-fraction boundedness and the interface-equilibrium condition are also required (§1). **Stated**
2. Existing single-phase KEEP schemes do not give stable simulations of compressible two-phase flows (§1, citing Jain & Moin 2020). **Stated**, then **Shown** (§4.1.2)
3. The conservative diffuse-interface method with the five-equation model conserves the mass of each phase, momentum and total energy, keeps the volume fraction bounded and TVD, maintains mechanical equilibrium at the interface and reduces to single-phase Navier–Stokes when the phases coincide (§1, §2.3). **Stated** (properties of Jain et al. 2020)
4. The Kapila et al. (2001) five-equation model is preferred over Allaire et al. (2002) for strong compression or expansion; for weakly compressible flows both gave identical results (§2). **Stated** (not shown)
5. Phase entropy obeys Eqs. (19)–(20): $\rho_k\phi_k\,Ds_k/Dt$ equals terms proportional to the regularization flux $a_k$, so $Ds_k/Dt=0$ only for the equilibrium (tanh) interface; in that limit the total phase entropies and the mixture entropy are conserved (§2.2, Eq. (21)). **Derived**
6. Interface regularization is irreversible: the entropy change is small and positive, $\Delta S_k/S_{k,0}$ of order $10^{-7}$ (fluid 1) and $10^{-8}$ (fluid 2) for $\epsilon_{\mathrm I}=0.75\Delta x$ and $1.25\Delta x$, negligible for $\epsilon_{\mathrm I}=\Delta x$ (App. A, Fig. 12). **Shown**
7. **Theorem 3.1:** global kinetic energy is conserved (without pressure work, viscosity, surface tension, gravity and time error) if $\hat M_{i+1/2}=\hat C_{i+1/2}\{\!\{v\}\!\}_{i+1/2}$ and $\hat R_{i+1/2}=\hat F_{i+1/2}\{\!\{v\}\!\}_{i+1/2}$ (§3.2, Eqs. (43)–(44)). **Proved** (telescoping argument)
8. **Theorem 3.2:** local kinetic-energy preservation also requires $\hat K_{i+1/2}=\hat C_{i+1/2}\,\tfrac12v_i\cdot v_{i+1}$ and $\hat T_{i+1/2}=\hat F_{i+1/2}\,\tfrac12v_i\cdot v_{i+1}$ (§3.2, Eqs. (50)–(51)). **Proved**
9. Mass consistency: $\sum_k\hat Q_k=\hat C$, $\sum_k\hat B_k=\hat F$, and the split form of the mass flux $\hat C$ must equal that of the volume-fraction flux $\hat\Phi$ (§3.2, Eqs. (55)–(57)). **Derived**
10. **Lemma 3.1 (6.1):** the interface-equilibrium condition of Abgrall (1996), uniform $v_0$, $p_0$ stay uniform, holds if the internal-energy flux $\hat I$ has the same split form as $\hat C$ and $\hat\Phi$, and the enthalpy regularization flux $\hat H$ the same as $\hat F$ (§3.3; proof App. B, Eqs. (69)–(75)). **Proved** for the stiffened-gas law; extension to cubic equations of state **Stated**
11. Pressure consistency: $\hat P_{i+1/2}\{\!\{v\}\!\}_{i+1/2}+p_i\{\!\{v\}\!\}_{i+1/2}=\hat W_{i+1/2}$, so discrete pressure work plus pressure dilatation equal the conservative pressure flux (§3.3, after Kok 2009 and Kuya et al. 2018). **Derived**
12. Conditions 1–5 fully determine the scheme once $\hat C$ and $\hat F$ are chosen; for a single phase, conditions 1, 3 and 5 suffice once $\hat C$ is chosen (§3.4). **Stated**
13. The proposed scheme (Eqs. (58)–(59)): $\hat C=\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}$, $\hat M=\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}$, $\hat K=\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}\tfrac12v_i\cdot v_{i+1}$, $\hat I=\{\!\{\rho e\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}$, $\hat P=\{\!\{p\}\!\}$, $\hat W=\tfrac12(v_{i+1}p_i+v_ip_{i+1})$, with the sharpening term in quartic split, $\hat R$ quintic and $\hat H$ quartic (§3.5). **Derived**
14. The scheme conserves kinetic energy exactly and entropy approximately "in the absence of pressure work, viscosity, thermal diffusion effects, and time-discretization errors" (abstract, §6). **Shown** (numerically, §4)
15. For a single phase the KEEP scheme is not unique: the QS, CS ($\hat I=\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}\{\!\{e\}\!\}\{\!\{v\}\!\}$) and CS-H (harmonic mean of $e$) forms all conserve kinetic energy and entropy and are stable in the inviscid Taylor–Green vortex at $M_0=0.05$ and $0.2$ (§3.5, §4.1.1, Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
16. In the two-phase Taylor–Green vortex (density ratio 10, $M_0=0.05$, $0.2$) only QS is stable; CS and CS-H violate the interface-equilibrium condition, cause "spurious mixing of internal energy at the interface" and diverge (§4.1.2, Figs. 4–5). **Shown**
17. A cubic-split $\hat H$ instead of quartic would violate the consistency conditions and "would not be stable" (§4.1.2). **Stated** (not shown)
18. The scheme is stable for density ratio 1000 at $M_0=0.05$, $0.2$, $0.4$ up to $M_0t=100$; the kinetic energy departs from its initial value at larger $M_0$ only through reversible pressure work (§4.1.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
19. Two-phase inviscid isotropic turbulence ($32^3$, density ratio 10, turbulent Mach number 0.07): only QS is stable; CS and CS-H diverge (§4.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
20. A quadratic-split mass flux is needed: the divergence form $\hat C=\{\!\{\rho v\}\!\}$, even with consistent $\hat M$, $\hat K$, diverges in the two-phase Taylor–Green vortex, attributed to aliasing (App. C, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. Inconsistent $\hat M$ (Eq. (78)), $\hat K$ (Eq. (79)) or $\hat T$ (Eq. (80)) each make the two-phase Taylor–Green vortex diverge (App. D, Fig. 14). **Shown**
22. Droplet-laden decaying isotropic turbulence ($256^3$, 1000 droplets, $\mathrm{Re}_\lambda\approx75$, turbulent Mach 0.07, $\mathrm{We}_{\mathrm{rms}}=1$): droplets enhance the early kinetic-energy decay, more so for denser droplets, "in very good agreement" with Dodd & Ferrante (2016); the crossover against single phase near $t\approx10$ (density ratio 1) and $t\approx18$ (ratio 10) is explained by surface-energy release as droplets coalesce (§5, Figs. 9–10, Eqs. (67)–(68)). **Shown** (qualitative)
23. The consistency conditions are general and apply to other interface-capturing methods (VOF, level set) and to multicomponent flows (§1, §6). **Stated**
24. Setup: RK4 with $\Delta t=\mathrm{CFL}\min(\Delta x/c_1,\Delta x/c_2)$, CFL ≈ 0.65, $\Gamma_{\mathrm I}=\lvert v\rvert_{\max}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm I}=\Delta x$ (§4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- p. 18, §3.5: "QS and CS stands for cubic-split and quadratic-split forms, respectively", inverted against Eqs. (61)–(62) and §3.1: QS is the quadratic split, CS the cubic split.
- pp. 21, 24: "only the QC form proposed in this work"; it is QS.
- p. 4: "$e=\sum_{l=1}^2\rho_le_l$" is dimensionally inconsistent; App. B (p. 30) uses the consistent $\rho e=\sum_l\rho_le_l\phi_l$.
- p. 7: "$\partial p_l/\partial\rho_l|_{s_l}=c_l$"; Eqs. (17)–(18) carry $c_l^2$.
- p. 7, after Eq. (19): "$l'=1-l$ denotes another phase"; for $l\in\{1,2\}$ the other phase is $3-l$.
- p. 13, Eqs. (48)–(49): $\hat M_j$, $\hat R_j$ drop the index $i$ of (43)–(44).
- p. 30, App. B: "$h_l=(p-\beta_l)/\alpha_l+p$"; the right side is $\rho_lh_l$. *(compiler check, not in the sources: with $\rho_le_l=(p-\beta_l)/\alpha_l$, $\alpha_l=\gamma_l-1$, $\beta_l=-\gamma_l\pi_l$, one gets $\rho_le_l+p=\gamma_l(p+\pi_l)/(\gamma_l-1)=\rho_lh_l$ by Eq. (9).)* Also "$p_i|^{k+1}_m=p_0$" (pp. 30–31) carries a stray index.
- p. 32, App. D: "This form of $\hat M_{ij}$ in Eq. (80)" and "This form of $\hat K_j$ in Eq. (80)"; they are Eqs. (78) and (79).
- The App. B lemma is numbered "Lemma 6.1" while restating Lemma 3.1.
- p. 22: the scheme "is verified for different initial Mach numbers and densities of the fluids in Figure 7", but Fig. 7 fixes the density ratio at 1000 and varies only the Mach number.
