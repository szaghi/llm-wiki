# Summary

Cai et al. (2026) (Cai, Cheng, Wu & Qiu) address the cost of
[advection-based remapping](/methods/conservative-remapping.md) in rezoning
[moving-mesh](/methods/adaptive-moving-mesh.md) finite-volume schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws. Each
physical step is an evolution on the fixed mesh, a rezoning and a remap, the remap being a pseudo-time
transport $\partial_{\tau_{\mathrm p}}u=0$ on a mesh moving from the old to the new grid. Being an ALE
discretization, the remap inherits the need for a regular mesh velocity; solution-driven rezoning gives
bounded but discontinuous mesh velocities, so current practice, e.g. [Gu et al. (2023)](/sources/gu-2023.md),
takes $N_\ell=O(\Delta x^{-1})$ pseudo-time levels per physical step.

The authors define [transport polynomial exactness](/properties/transport-polynomial-exactness.md)
TPE($k$): the fully discrete solution of linear advection with a degree-$k$ polynomial datum equals the
projection of the exact translated solution at every discrete time. TPE(0) is
[free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md), which the classical geometric
conservation law (GCL) enforces. They show that the GCL is not enough for $k\ge1$: the reconstruction
also needs the higher geometric moments $\int x^sy^r$, and exact moments are inconsistent with the
numerically evolved cell integrals. Their fix, evolved geometric moments, advances every moment with
$s+r\le2$ by its own Reynolds-transport equation, discretized exactly like the conserved variables (same
Gauss–Lobatto edge rule, same [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)). With a 2-exact reconstruction, a
[hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) of the optimal quadratic and the third-order WENO of Zhu & Qiu switched by
the KXRCF [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md), with
[affine-invariant](/methods/affine-invariant-weno.md) indicators, they prove the remap, the evolution and
the whole scheme TPE(2) for any mesh motion and step, and the evolved moments equal to the exact ones
under SSP-RK3, which acts as Simpson's rule. Only the CFL limit then bounds the pseudo-time step, so
$N_\ell=O(1)$.

Numerics (C++): polynomial advection on random meshes is exact to round-off; on a smooth Euler
[density wave](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md) on random meshes the scheme converges at third order
with $N_\ell=2$ where the GCL-only variant loses order and the exact-moment variant does not converge; the
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[2D Riemann](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) and
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) problems run on ALE or extreme random meshes.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's $U$ is $u$, the advection velocity $a$ is $\mathsf v$, the pseudo-time
$\tau$ is $\tau_{\mathrm p}$ with final value $T_{\mathrm r}$ (the source's $\tau^{\rm final}$), the mesh
velocity $w$ is $v_{\mathrm g}$, the moments $M_{s,r}$ and evolved moments $\widetilde M_{s,r}$ are
$\mathfrak M_{s,r}$ and $\widetilde{\mathfrak M}_{s,r}$, and the cell integrals $V$ are $\mathfrak V$ (all
page-local, as on [transport polynomial exactness](/properties/transport-polynomial-exactness.md)); the
polynomial space $\mathbb P^m_k$ is $(\mathbb P^k)^m$; $N_\ell$ and $C_{\mathrm{CFL}}$ are kept.

1. An advection-based remap is an ALE discretization of $\partial_{\tau_{\mathrm p}}u=0$ and inherits the ALE need for mesh-velocity regularity; with bounded but discontinuous mesh velocity, conventional strategies need $N_\ell=O(\Delta x^{-1})$ pseudo-time levels, with $\Delta t=O(\Delta x)$ the cost of an effective step $O(\Delta x^2)$ (§1.2). **Stated**
2. Intersection-based and swept (flux-based) remaps need no mesh-velocity smoothness but require costly geometric operations on general cells (§1.2). **Stated**
3. Definition: a scheme is TPE($k$) if, when the law reduces to $\partial_tu+\mathsf v\cdot\nabla u=0$ with $u_0\in(\mathbb P^k)^m$, the fully discrete solution is the projection of the exact solution at all discrete times; TPE(0) is free-stream preservation, "precisely the property enforced by the classical GCL" (§1.3, Def. 2.1). **Stated** (definition)
4. The classical GCL constrains only the volume and does not give TPE($k$), $k\ge1$: mismatch between evolved $\mathfrak V$ and exact $\mathfrak M_{s,r}$ "degrades accuracy and can preclude convergence" (§3.1.1, §5.1–5.2). **Shown**
5. Evolved geometric moments: $\frac{d\mathfrak M_{s,r,i,j}}{d\tau_{\mathrm p}}=\int_{\partial I_{i,j}}x^sy^r(v_{\mathrm g}\cdot n)\,dS$, discretized with the three-point Gauss–Lobatto edge rule of the conserved integrals; $\widetilde{\mathfrak M}_{0,0}$ recovers the classical GCL (Eqs. (3.7)–(3.10)). **Stated** (construction)
6. Lemma 3.3: with a 2-exact reconstruction and cell averages generated by one quadratic through the evolved moments, the semi-discrete operator of $\mathfrak V$ is $\sum_{s+r\le2}c_{s,r}$ times that of $\widetilde{\mathfrak M}_{s,r}$. **Proved**
7. Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 4.4: the relation $\mathfrak V=\sum c_{s,r}\widetilde{\mathfrak M}_{s,r}$ is preserved by forward Euler and hence by SSP-RK3, so the remap is TPE(2) for any mesh motion, mesh quality and $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}$. **Proved**
8. Remark 4.5: the remap is TPE($k$) with a $k$-exact reconstruction and any SSP time discretization. **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: Theorem 4.3 gives exactness with respect to the evolved moments; it equals the projection of Def. 2.1 because Theorem 4.7 makes the evolved moments exact for $s+r\le2$. For $s+r=3$ the edge integrand has degree $s+r+1=4$ in the edge parameter and, by Lemma 4.8, $d\mathfrak M_{s,r}/d\tau_{\mathrm p}$ has degree 4 in $\tau_{\mathrm p}$, beyond the cubics that the three-point Gauss–Lobatto rule and Simpson's rule integrate exactly; so with the paper's quadrature and SSP-RK3 the evolved moments of order 3 are not exact in general, and TPE(3) in the sense of Def. 2.1 needs richer rules.)*
9. Proposition 4.1: the fixed-mesh evolution (Gauss–Lobatto edge rule, 2-exact reconstruction, SSP-RK3) is TPE(2) for any $\Delta t$: the spatial operator is exact on quadratics and $\mathcal L^3u^n=0$, so $1+z+z^2/2+z^3/6$ equals $e^{\Delta t\mathcal L}$ there; Remark 4.2: TPE(3) with a 3-exact reconstruction. **Proved**
10. Theorem 4.6: the whole rezoning moving-mesh scheme is TPE(2) for any $\Delta t$ and $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}$. **Proved**
11. Lemma 4.8: the exact moment of a quadrilateral whose vertices move linearly in $\tau_{\mathrm p}$ is a polynomial of degree at most $s+r+2$ in $\tau_{\mathrm p}$. **Proved**
12. Lemma 4.9: for $s+r\le2$ the edge rule gives $d\mathfrak M_{s,r}/d\tau_{\mathrm p}$ exactly. **Proved**
13. Lemma 4.10: for $u'=f(\tau_{\mathrm p})$, SSP-RK3 with stage times $\tau^n_{\mathrm p}$, $\tau^n_{\mathrm p}+\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}$, $\tau^n_{\mathrm p}+\tfrac12\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}$ is Simpson's rule. **Proved**
14. Theorem 4.7: under SSP-RK3, $\widetilde{\mathfrak M}^{\ell+1}_{s,r,i,j}=\mathfrak M_{s,r,i,j}(\tau^{\ell+1}_{\mathrm p})$ for $s+r\le2$, a "fourth-order superconvergence"; no geometric refreshing or source-term correction, which would break conservation, is needed (§4.4). **Proved**
15. Remark 4.11: Ketcheson's ten-stage fourth-order SSP method lacks this superconvergence and reaches the same geometric exactness at higher cost. **Stated**
16. Pseudo-time step: $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}\le C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\min_{i,j}\mathfrak M_{0,0,i,j}/\max_k a^{\max}_{i,j,k}|l^k_{i,j}|$ gives $N_\ell\ge\max_{i,j}\max_P|\delta x_P|\max_k|l^k_{i,j}|/(C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\mathfrak M_{0,0,i,j})$, which is $O(1)$ for bounded physical mesh velocity and $\Delta t=O(\Delta x)$ (Eqs. (4.14)–(4.15)). **Derived**
17. Remark 4.12: the updates depend only on $v_{\mathrm g}\Delta\tau_{\mathrm p}$, so the result is independent of $T_{\mathrm r}$, taken $=\Delta t$. **Derived**
18. Remark 4.13: $C_{\mathrm{CFL}}\le\tfrac14$ keeps the evolved volume positive. **Stated**
19. For quadratic data the KXRCF indicator "automatically" flags no cell, so the hybrid reconstruction is 2-exact (§3.4). **Stated**
20. Polynomial advection (40×40, random rezoning, $T=0.1$, 20 instances): maximum $L^1$ errors $7.13\times10^{-15}$, $6.30\times10^{-15}$, $4.93\times10^{-15}$ (quadratic, linear, constant) for the TPE(2) scheme; GCL-only $1.88\times10^{-4}$, $1.06\times10^{-4}$, $5.00\times10^{-15}$; exact-moment schemes $10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}$ (Table 1). **Shown**
21. Euler density wave on random meshes ($80^2$–$320^2$): TPE(2) $L^1$ orders 2.93–2.98 with $N_\ell=2.0$; GCL-only $L^\infty$ orders fall to 1.06, −0.00, −0.55; the exact-moment scheme with $N_\ell=O(1)$ does not converge, and with Gu et al.'s strategy recovers third order with $N_\ell$ rising from 4.7 to 19.7 (Table 2). **Shown**
22. On $320^2$ the total CPU times are 5795.1 s (TPE(2)), 6211.8 s (GCL-only), 16490.4 s and 67168.3 s (exact moments, $N_\ell=O(1)$ and Gu et al.'s strategy), the remapping times 3942.6, 4365.8, 10975.8 and 64781.5 s (Xeon Gold 6348, C++); the exact-moment scheme with $N_\ell=O(1)$ is about three times slower because the geometric mismatch makes KXRCF flag smooth cells (Table 3). **Shown**
23. With Gu et al.'s strategy $N_\ell\approx20$, "nearly a tenfold increase", and the remap is "an order of magnitude slower" (§5.2); the conclusion speaks of "roughly a tenfold increase in remapping time" (§6). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
24. ALE rezoning (Lagrangian step plus the mesh optimization of Cheng, Zhao & Qiu) with evolved moments keeps the resolution gain over a fixed mesh on Woodward–Colella and Shu–Osher (400×10) (Figs. 4–5). **Shown** (visual)
25. 2D Riemann problem on extreme random meshes: the exact-moment scheme needs $N_\ell=6$ for what TPE(2) gives at $N_\ell=2$, and TPE(2) hardly changes with $N_\ell$ (Fig. 6). **Shown** (visual)
26. Double Mach reflection on 1920×480 extreme random meshes runs without mesh-induced artifacts (Fig. 7). **Shown** (visual)
27. The approach needs no reference-to-physical mappings or Jacobian evaluations, and "challenges the prevailing assumption that high-order accuracy in ALE methods necessitates mesh-velocity smoothness" (§1.4, §6). **Stated**

The theory covers convex quadrilaterals with straight edges and vertices moving linearly in pseudo-time;
the reconstruction's stencils, linear weights and $\epsilon$ are not printed.
