# Summary

The paper proposes third-, fourth- and fifth-order finite-volume
[multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) schemes "with
adaptive linear weights" (ALW-MR-WENO-3, -4, -5) for 2D conservation laws on
triangular meshes. It modifies the triangular MR-WENO of Zhu & Shu, which uses
a hierarchy of nested central stencils $T_1\subset T_2\subset T_3\subset T_4$.
Each new scheme keeps only **two** unequal-sized central stencils: the one-cell
stencil $T_1$, with the constant $q_1=\bar u_0$, and one big stencil ($T_2$,
$T_3$ or $T_4$, at least 6, 10 or 15 cells). On the big stencil a polynomial of
degree 2, 3 or 4 is fitted by constrained least squares. The two polynomials
are recombined in [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md) form,
$p_{\ell_2}=q_{\ell_2}/d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}-(d_{1,\ell_2}/d_{\ell_2,\ell_2})p_1$,
with [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type weights and
$\tau_{\ell_2}=\lvert\beta_{\ell_2}-\beta_1\rvert^2$.

The novelty is an a posteriori adjustment of the two linear weights in each
cell. The unnormalized high-order weight starts at $\hat d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}=10^8$
(with $\hat d_{1,\ell_2}=1$). If the tentative reconstruction at any boundary
Gauss point deviates from $\bar u_0$ by more than a threshold, the weight is
divided by 100 and the nonlinear weights are recomputed. The test, "Condition
I", is presented as an improvement of the Krivodonova et al.
[troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md). The schemes
use a [Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) flux,
characteristic-wise reconstruction and third-order
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) at CFL 0.6.

The evidence is numerical only. It comprises accuracy tables for 2D
[Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) and the 2D
[density wave](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md), quasi-1D
[Lax and Sod](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) and
[blast-wave](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md) problems on thin
strips, Schardin's [shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), the
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) and transonic and
supersonic [NACA 0012](/benchmarks/naca0012-airfoil.md) flows. The ALW schemes
match MR-WENO's accuracy and need 0.60–0.96 of its CPU time, with the largest
saving at fifth order. No accuracy, ENO or positivity property is proved.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): linear weights $d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}$,
$\hat d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}$ (source $\gamma$, $\hat\gamma$), unnormalized
nonlinear weights $\alpha_{\ell_1,\ell_2}$ (source $\bar\omega$),
regularization $\epsilon$, Gauss points per edge $n_{\mathrm G}$ (source
$rr$), circumradius $r_\circ$ (source $r$ in Condition I), wave speed $a$.

1. ALW-MR-WENO schemes of orders 3, 4 and 5 on triangles use only two unequal-sized central stencils: a one-cell stencil and one big stencil "having enough cells for designing an arbitrarily high-order WENO spatial approximation" (Abstract, §1). **Stated**
2. Priority claim: "it is the first time that any high-order WENO schemes with the application of only two unequal-sized stencils are obtained on triangular meshes" (Abstract). **Stated** (the paper itself cites CWENO schemes on unstructured and triangular meshes, among them Semplice, Coco & Russo's, without comparing with them)
3. The big stencils need at least six cells (quadratic), ten (cubic) and fifteen (quartic). $T_2$, $T_3$ and $T_4$ list 8, 12 and 20 cells (Fig. 2.1). If $T_3$ has at most nine cells, or $T_4$ at most fourteen, the edge neighbours of its cells are added (§1, Steps 1.1–1.3). **Stated**
4. The big-stencil polynomials are constrained least-squares fits: exact cell average on $\triangle_0$, least-squares match of the other averages, in a monomial basis scaled by $\lvert\triangle_0\rvert$ (Eqs. 2.5–2.7, following Hu & Shu). **Stated**
5. The CWENO-form polynomials $p_{\ell_2}$ (Eqs. 2.8–2.10) are built "with similar ideas for classical CWENO schemes". **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: if $\omega_{\ell_1,\ell_2}=d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}$, then $Q=d_{1,\ell_2}p_1+d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}p_{\ell_2}=q_{\ell_2}$ for any admissible linear weights, which is why the weights are free.)*
6. Initial linear weights: $\hat d_{1,\ell_2}=1$, $\hat d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}=10^8$, normalized $d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}=\hat d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}/(\hat d_{1,\ell_2}+\hat d_{\ell_2,\ell_2})$. They are justified by the "balance between the essentially non-oscillatory transitions in discontinuous areas and high-order accuracy in smooth areas" (§2, after Eq. 2.10). **Stated**
7. The nonlinear weights are WENO-Z-type, $\alpha_{\ell_1,\ell_2}=d_{\ell_1,\ell_2}\big(1+\tau_{\ell_2}/(\epsilon+\beta_{\ell_1})\big)$ with exponent 1, $\tau_{\ell_2}=\lvert\beta_{\ell_2}-\beta_1\rvert^2$ (Eq. 2.12, citing Borges et al.) and $\epsilon=10^{-10}$ (Eq. 2.13). **Stated**
8. Adaptive linear weights. Condition I is $\lvert Q(x_G,y_G)-\bar u_0\rvert>\max(\Phi_1,\Phi_2)$ at the boundary Gauss points, with $\Phi_1=r_\circ\min(\lvert\bar u_0\rvert,\dots,\lvert\bar u_3\rvert)$ and $\Phi_2=\min_{j=1,2,3}\lvert\bar u_0-\bar u_j\rvert$. It is "an improvement of original troubled cell indicator". While it holds and $\hat d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}>10^{n_{\mathrm G}}$, the weight is divided by 100 (Eq. 2.15) and the weights are recomputed (Steps 4.3–4.4). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: from $10^8$ the sequence is $10^8,10^6,10^4,10^2$ for $n_{\mathrm G}=2$ or 3, so at most three reductions occur and $d_{\ell_2,\ell_2}\ge100/101$ always.)*
9. The schemes keep the design order in smooth regions and "reduce to the first-order accuracy so as to keep essentially non-oscillatory properties around strong discontinuities" (Abstract, §1). **Stated** (orders **Shown** in Tables 3.2–3.3; the ENO behaviour only in figures)
10. 2D Burgers at $t=0.5/\pi$, meshes of 58 to 14848 cells (Table 3.2): on the finest mesh ALW-MR-WENO-3 reaches $L^1$ order 2.84 and $L^\infty$ order 2.75, ALW-MR-WENO-4 reaches 4.41 and 3.95, and ALW-MR-WENO-5 reaches 4.88 and 4.82. On the two finest meshes the errors are essentially those of MR-WENO. **Shown** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: along $x+y$ the equation is $\partial_tw+2w\partial_sw=0$, which breaks at $t=1/\pi$, so $t=0.5/\pi$ is smooth.)*
11. 2D Euler density wave at $t=2$ (Table 3.3): finest-mesh $L^1$ orders 3.03, 4.32 and 5.10 for orders 3, 4 and 5, with errors equal to MR-WENO's to the printed digits. **Shown**
12. CPU time (Table 3.1, Cases 3.3–3.11), as ALW/MR-WENO ratios: 0.63–0.87 at third order, 0.80–0.96 at fourth and 0.60–0.77 at fifth. No hardware, compiler or parallel setup is stated. **Shown**
13. The largest times are those of the double Mach reflection: 660330.33 s for MR-WENO-4 against 611079.93 s for ALW-MR-WENO-4, and 713307.99 s against 451954.05 s at fifth order (Table 3.1). **Shown**
14. In the Lax, Sod, Shu–Osher and blast-wave problems the ALW schemes match MR-WENO of the same order (Figs. 3.4–3.7); for Sod they "can capture the contact discontinuities precisely" (Case 3.4). **Shown** (qualitative)
15. In Schardin's problem, the double Mach reflection, the forward step and NACA 0012, ALW and MR-WENO "can provide good resolution" and agree. For the forward step the resolution, "especially for the vortex sheet roll-up", improves with order (Figs. 3.9–3.26). **Shown** (qualitative)
16. The main benefits are "efficiency, compactness, and simplicity in large scale engineering applications on unstructured meshes" (Abstract, §4). **Stated**
17. Classical Hu–Shu triangular WENO needs nine three-cell stencils (third order) or six six-cell stencils (fourth order), with "very sophisticated" linear weights; the two-stencil design "greatly simplifies the spatial reconstructions" (§1, §4). **Stated**
18. Characteristic-wise reconstruction is used for the Euler equations, at CFL 0.6 (§3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Step 4.4.2: "If Condition I is satisfied, $\gamma_{\ell_2,\ell_2}$ is decreased as", but Eq. (2.15) decreases the unnormalized $\hat\gamma_{\ell_2,\ell_2}$ and then renormalizes.
- Eq. (2.11) defines $\beta_\ell$ only for $\ell=2,3,4$. $\beta_1$, which enters (2.12) and (2.13), is deferred to Zhu & Shu; applied to the constant $q_1$, (2.11) would give zero.
- The letter $r$ is the derivative order in (2.11) and the minimum circumradius in $\Phi_1$ (Step 4.3). Step 1.1 writes $T_1=\{\Delta_0\}$ where the rest of the paper has $\triangle_0$.
- Case 3.11: "the single NACA0012 airfoil configuration is shown in Case 3.11" refers to itself; the configuration and mesh are in Case 3.10.
- Case 3.4 (Sod) prints $(\rho,u,\nu,p)=(1,0,0,2.5)$ and $(0.125,0,0,0.25)$, whereas Case 3.3 lists the pressure in the fourth slot. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: 2.5 and 0.25 equal $E=p/(\gamma-1)$ for the Sod pressures $p=1$, $0.1$ with $\gamma=1.4$.)* The paper prints no $\gamma$.
- Table 3.3, ALW-MR-WENO-4, $L^\infty$ at 232 cells: printed order 3.12, while $\log_2(1.09\times10^{-1}/1.27\times10^{-2})=3.10$, at most 3.11 within the rounding of the printed errors. *(Compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- Ref. [47] (Zhu & Shu, J. Comput. Phys. 392) is printed with year 2018; J. Comput. Phys. 392 is a 2019 volume. The bibliography records the work by volume and page.
