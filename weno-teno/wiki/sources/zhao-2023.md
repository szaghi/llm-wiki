# Summary

Zhao, Ji, Shyy & Xu argue that a physically consistent explicit scheme for compressible flow
must be *compact*: its numerical domain of dependence should match the physical one. That
means a CFL number of order one and only nearest-neighbour cells. A compact scheme of order
three or more must then evolve more than cell averages. Here the extra unknowns are
cell-averaged gradients, updated by Gauss–Green from interface values at $t^{n+1}$. The
authors call the construction of the time-accurate interface solution and flux that closes
this update "direct modeling". They hold the Riemann solver to be an inadequate evolution
model for high-order compact schemes.

The scheme is a compact [gas-kinetic scheme](/methods/gas-kinetic-scheme.md) (GKS), whose
interface flux and its time derivative come from the integral solution of the BGK equation.
Two ingredients are new. (i) Left and right interface states at $t^{n+1}$, each a blend of
the equilibrium value and an independently evolved non-equilibrium value with weight
$e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\mathrm c,0}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm c,0}=5\lvert(p^--p^+)/(p^-+p^+)\rvert\Delta t$.
(ii) A nonlinear limiter on the two-stage fourth-order (S2O4) time stepping. It multiplies
the third- and fourth-order time-derivative terms on each cell side by a weight
$\omega^{\mathrm t}_l\in[0,1]$, built from the spatial
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and a WENO-Z-type reference
value. The weight is $1+O(\Delta x^3)$ in smooth flow and $O(\Delta x^4)$ at shocks, where the
update falls back to the second-order GKS step. The spatial reconstruction is a
[WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md)-type combination with [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type weights:
eighth order on a 1D compact stencil, fourth order on triangles.

The tests are 1D [density-wave advection](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md) (eighth
order), [Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md) in 1D and
on triangles, the [Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), long-time
entropy-wave advection against DG-$\mathbb P^5$, the
[Mach 3 forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md), inviscid and viscous
hypersonic [flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md), the
[viscous shock tube](/benchmarks/viscous-shock-tube.md) at $\mathrm{Re}=200$ and a lid-driven
cavity. Nearly all runs use CFL 0.8 with no troubled-cell detection; the previous compact GKS
is run at 0.1–0.4 in the comparisons.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $u$ conserved variables, $\bar u_i$ cell averages, $\Delta x$ cell size,
$u^\mp$ the left and right interface states, $\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal L_t$ the spatial
operator and its time derivative, $d_k$, $\beta_k$, $\omega_k$, $\epsilon$ as in WENO.
Page-local: $\tau^{\mathrm Z}$ the WENO-Z-type reference value, $\omega^{\mathrm t}_l$ the
temporal weight of cell side $l$, $\tau_{\mathrm c}$ the BGK collision time, $r_\tau$ and $r_\beta$ the
orders of $\tau^{\mathrm Z}$ and of the polynomials behind the smoothness indicators.

1. A physically consistent scheme must be compact: it uses only the nearest neighbouring cells under a CFL number of order one, so that numerical and physical domains of dependence agree (Abstract; §2, p. 3). **Stated**
2. For a compact scheme of order three or more, updating cell averages alone (Eq. (1)) is not enough; additional subcell variables must be evolved, here cell-averaged gradients updated by Gauss–Green from interface values at $t^{n+1}$ (Eq. (2)) (§2, p. 3). **Stated**
3. Using the Riemann solver directly as the evolution model is "not adequate in its dynamics" for high-order compact schemes; the authors attribute the poor robustness and small CFL numbers of existing compact schemes to this (Abstract; §2, p. 7). **Stated**
4. Inside a time step the interface flux can be discontinuous in time, for example when a shock crosses the interface (Fig. 1); the S2O4 expansion (Eqs. (3)–(4)) assumes a time-continuous flux and can then fail (§2, pp. 6–7; §3.3, p. 13). **Stated**
5. The S2O4 update (Eqs. (4), (23)–(24)) is rederived from a cubic $P^3(t)$ matched to $\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal L_t$ at $t=0$ and $t=\Delta t/2$ (Eqs. (18)–(22)); the intermediate state (24) is third-order accurate and the full update fourth order with local error $O(\Delta t^5)$ (§3.3, pp. 12–13). **Derived**
6. The limiter multiplies only the terms $-\frac{\Delta t^2}{3}\tilde{\mathcal L}_t(u^n)+\frac{\Delta t^2}{3}\tilde{\mathcal L}_t(u^{n+1/2})$ by $\omega^{\mathrm t}_l\in[0,1]$; the second-order part $\Delta t\mathcal L+\frac{\Delta t^2}{2}\mathcal L_t$ is not limited, because "the one-step second-order evolution model will not have the mechanism described in Fig. (1)" (Eqs. (25)–(26); §3.3, pp. 13–15). **Stated**
7. The weight is $\omega^{\mathrm t}_l=\min\{\alpha^-_2,\alpha^+_2\}$ with $\alpha_2=2\tilde\alpha_2/(\tilde\alpha_1+\tilde\alpha_2)$, $\tilde\alpha_1=1+\big(\tau^{\mathrm Z}/(\beta_{\min}+\epsilon)\big)^2$, $\tilde\alpha_2=1+\big(\tau^{\mathrm Z}/(\beta_{\max}+\epsilon)\big)^2$ on each side, $\epsilon=10^{-5}$ (Eq. (27)). In smooth regions $\tilde\alpha_i=C_0\big(1+O(\Delta x^{2(r_\tau-2)+r_\beta})\big)$, with $r_\tau\ge3$ the order of $\tau^{\mathrm Z}$ and $r_\beta\ge1$ the polynomial order behind $\beta_{\min}$, $\beta_{\max}$; hence $\omega^{\mathrm t}_l=1+O(\Delta x^3)$, the added truncation error is $O(\Delta x^3\Delta t^2)$, and the limited S2O4 stays fourth order (§3.3, p. 14). **Derived**
8. Near a shock with $O(1)$ jumps, $\omega^{\mathrm t}_l=O(\Delta x^4)$ and the update reverts to the lower-order one (§3.3, p. 15). **Stated** (no derivation)
9. With characteristic-wise reconstruction in 2D, four weights arise per interface. The weight used is the minimum over the first and fourth characteristic fields, because "the shock wave can only correspond to the first or fourth characteristic variables" (§3.3, p. 15). **Stated**
10. For a stationary shock located at a cell interface the flux is continuous in time and the scheme works without the temporal limiter (§3.3, p. 15). **Stated**
11. Evolving separate left and right interface states (Eq. (17)), instead of a single equilibrium value, "greatly improve[s] the reliability of the updated slope" and makes the scheme insensitive to the WENO parameters (§2, pp. 6–7; §3.4, p. 15). **Stated**
12. The reconstruction (Eq. (28)) is insensitive to its constants; $C=5$ and $C_k=1/n$ ($d_0=C/(1+C)$, $d_k=C_k/(1+C)$) work well. Setting the large-stencil indicator to $\beta_0=\max\{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_n\}$ instead of computing it is "efficient and robust" (§3.4, pp. 15–16). **Stated**
13. 1D accuracy of the eighth-order compact GKS with $\Delta t=0.5\Delta x^2$, $\Delta x=1/10$ to $1/80$: $L_1$ errors $2.7627\times10^{-4}$, $3.5968\times10^{-6}$, $1.2672\times10^{-8}$, $5.9689\times10^{-11}$ (orders 6.26, 8.15, 7.73); $L_\infty$ errors $5.6985\times10^{-4}$, $8.1259\times10^{-6}$, $6.3630\times10^{-8}$, $1.5194\times10^{-10}$ (orders 6.13, 7.00, 8.71) (Table 1, §4.1). **Shown**
14. 1D blast wave, 400 cells, $t=0.038$: the new scheme runs at CFL 0.8. The text says the previous eighth-order compact GKS "can only use CFL number 0.2", while the Fig. 5 legend shows it at 0.1 (§4.2). **Shown**
15. Shu–Osher, 200 cells, $t=1.8$: "a large CFL number can be used"; the new scheme is shown at CFL 0.4 and 0.8, the previous one at 0.4 (§4.3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
16. Entropy-wave advection to $t=400$ ($b=2.0$ and $1.5$, $\Delta x=1$): the nonlinear and linear eighth-order compact GKS at CFL 0.4 and 0.8 are compared with the sixth-order DG-$\mathbb P^5$ of Cheng et al. ($\Delta x=6$, CFL $1/30$); the scheme "works very well even for the linear wave propagation" (§4.4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
17. The fourth-order compact GKS on a triangular mesh ($h=1/400$, CFL 0.8) is stable for the 2D blast-wave interactions (§4.5, Fig. 8). **Shown**
18. Mach 3 forward step on triangles ($h=1/120$, $t=4$): no corner fix is used and the slip-line instability is captured (§4.6, Fig. 9). **Shown**
19. Inviscid cylinder flow at Mach 8 and 20 on triangles, CFL 0.8: the Mach 20 case starts from $t=0$ "without additional treatment in the initial phase"; the results agree with non-compact high-order GKS on structured meshes (§4.7, Figs. 10–11). **Shown** (agreement asserted, no comparison figure)
20. Viscous cylinder flow at $M=5$, $\mathrm{Re}=1.835\times10^5$, on high-aspect-ratio triangles at CFL 0.35 (§4.7, Fig. 12). **Shown**
21. Viscous shock tube at $\mathrm{Re}=200$, $h=1/400$: the fourth-order compact GKS resolves the λ-shock and vortices. Its lower-wall density is closer to the non-compact high-order GKS reference ($h=1/720$) than third-order CPR-GKS on $h=1/500$, which needs troubled-cell detection (§4.8, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
22. Lid-driven cavity at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ on $33\times33\times2$ triangles (stretching 1.2, minimum $h\approx0.0052$): the centreline velocities agree with reference solutions (§4.9, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
23. All tests use the same nonlinear reconstruction, with no troubled-cell detection and no problem-specific limiter; CFL 0.8 unless stated (§4, p. 18). **Shown**
24. The compact GKS reaches fourth order in space and time on structured and unstructured meshes at CFL about 0.8, against 0.1 for a DG method of the same order (§2, pp. 7–8). **Stated** (the DG value is asserted, not demonstrated)
25. "For the first time" a limiting process is applied to the high-order time derivatives of the flux, generalizing S2O4 to the discontinuous case (§5, p. 28). **Stated** (priority claim)
26. Multi-stage [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) methods are designed for time-independent fluxes and do not apply directly to fluxes with time derivatives; the SSP property "can be hardly extended" to such evolution models (§2, p. 7). **Stated** (cited)

# Printed slips

- Eq. (20): the printed sums $\sum_{k=1}^3b_k\frac{(\Delta t/2)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}$ and $\sum_{k=2}^3b_k\frac{(\Delta t/2)^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}$ are $P^3_t(\Delta t/2)$ and $P^3_{tt}(\Delta t/2)$, not the stated conditions $P^3(\Delta t/2)=\mathcal L^{n+1/2}$ and $P^3_t(\Delta t/2)=\mathcal L^{n+1/2}_t$. *(compiler check, not in the sources)*: with $P^3=b_0+b_1t+b_2t^2/2+b_3t^3/6$, the stated conditions give exactly the printed $b_2$, $b_3$ of Eq. (21) with $\Delta t$ powers, and $\int_0^{\Delta t}P^3\,dt=\Delta t\,b_0+\frac{\Delta t^2}{6}b_1+\frac{\Delta t^2}{3}\mathcal L^{n+1/2}_t$, consistent with Eqs. (4) and (22).
- Eq. (21) prints $\frac4{t^2}$ and $\frac{24}{t^3}$ for $\frac4{\Delta t^2}$ and $\frac{24}{\Delta t^3}$.
- Text after Eq. (25): "$\tilde{\mathcal L}_{j,t}(W^{n/2})$" for $\tilde{\mathcal L}_{j,t}(W^{n+1/2})$; Eq. (25) writes $W^n$ without the cell subscript.
- Text after Eq. (14): "$W^{n+1/2}_{j+1/2}$ is the evolved solution at the cell interface", while Eq. (14) uses $W^{n+1}_{j\pm1/2}$.
- Text after Eq. (17): "the contributions from $W^l_0$, $W^r$, and $W^e$" for $W^r_0$; the weight is written "$e^{-\Delta t/\tau}$" while Eq. (17) uses $\tau_0$.
- §4.2: the previous scheme "can only use CFL number 0.2", but the Fig. 5 legend reads "CFL=0.1".
- §4.7: "The mesh and pressure distributions are presented in Fig. 10", but Fig. 10 shows only the mesh; Fig. 11 holds pressure and Mach number for both Mach 8 and Mach 20, under the caption "Inviscid cylinder flow at mach 20".
- §4.8: "bounded in a unit square cavity", while the domain is $[0,1]\times[0,0.5]$ with a symmetry boundary on top.
- Eq. (12) writes $f_0(x_0-u(t-t_0),\dots)$ with $t_0$, while the integral starts at $0$.
- Eq. (2) writes $\Omega_j(\nabla W)_j$ with $\Omega_j$ standing for the volume $\lvert\Omega_j\rvert$.
- Ref. [20] prints the author name "Duraismay".
