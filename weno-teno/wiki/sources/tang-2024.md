# Summary

The paper proposes TENO-AZ, a fifth-order finite-difference
[targeted ENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) scheme with the simplest possible
adaptive cut-off. A new global
[smoothness](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) factor
$\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}=\lvert\beta_0+\beta_2-2\beta_1\rvert$ replaces the WENO-Z
$\tau_5$ in the scale-separated measure
$\gamma_k=\big(1+\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)^6$, and the cut-off
is $C_T=\min(\lambda_d,\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}})$ with $\lambda_d=10^{-5}$. The
motivation is twofold. The [adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) variants
TENO-A (Fu, Hu & Adams 2018) and TENO-LAD (Peng et al. 2021) carry many free
parameters, floor functions and extra stencil operations. And all TENO schemes,
adaptive ones included, lose order at
[critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md) of order
$n_{\mathrm{cp}}\ge2$. In smooth regions $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}\to0$, so
$C_T\to0$, every stencil is kept and the scheme is the linear fifth-order
upwind scheme. Near discontinuities $C_T$ is capped at $10^{-5}$.

The evidence is entirely numerical. On $f(x)=e^x-\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{cp}}}x^k/k!$
with $\epsilon=\Delta x^{2n_{\mathrm{cp}}+3}$, TENO-Z, TENO-A and TENO-LAD keep
the design order at $n_{\mathrm{cp}}=1$ but drop towards orders 2 and 3 at
$n_{\mathrm{cp}}=2,3$. TENO-AZ keeps the linear-scheme errors in all three
cases. An [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
analysis places TENO-AZ between TENO-Z and TENO-A/LAD. The Euler tests use
global [Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) and
third-order [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md):
[Sod](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[123](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md),
[2D Riemann](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[implosion](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md),
[shock–mixing layer](/benchmarks/shock-mixing-layer-interaction.md) and
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md). TENO-AZ costs about
as much as TENO-Z (0.95–1.03 in 1D), TENO-A 1.06–1.44 and TENO-LAD 1.10–1.38
times TENO-Z. On the shock–mixing layer at $1000\times200$, TENO-AZ is the only
scheme that runs at CFL 0.3, 0.5 and 0.8. The author attributes this to the
continuity of the new cut-off. Since $\chi_k$ is dimensionless while
$\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}$ scales as $f^2$, scaling the data by $\sigma$ changes the
cut-off from $\min(10^{-5},\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}})$ to
$\min(10^{-5},\sigma^2\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}})$, so the scheme is not
[scale-invariant](/properties/scale-invariance.md); the paper does not discuss
scaling *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's candidate fluxes $q_k$ are
$\hat f^{(k)}_{i+1/2}$, its index $j$ is $i$, the new global factor $\tau$ is
written $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}$ (page-local), its reduced wavenumber $k$ is
$\vartheta$. TENO-A's $m$, $\eta_j$, $g(m)$, $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, $\beta$,
$\xi$ are $\mu_d$, $\sigma_i$, $\varphi(\mu_d)$, $b_1$, $b_2$, $b$,
$\xi_{\mathrm A}$ as on [adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md).

1. TENO-AZ is TENO-Z (Eqs. (9)–(12)) with $\tau_5$ replaced by $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}=\lvert\beta_0+\beta_2-2\beta_1\rvert$ (Eq. (19)), $C=1$, $q=6$, and the cut-off $C_T=\min(\lambda_d,\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}})$, $\lambda_d=10^{-5}$ (Eq. (20)) (§3). **Stated** (definition)
2. In smooth regions $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}$ tends to zero, which forces $C_T=0$, and the scheme "acts as a standard fifth-order upwind scheme". In non-smooth regions $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}>0$ "limits the maximum attainable value of $C_T$ to $10^{-5}$ or less" (§3). **Stated** (no asymptotic order of $\tau_{\mathrm{AZ}}$ is derived)
3. Using the WENO-Z $\tau_5$ as the correction term in $C_T$ "will produce numerical oscillations in the computation of many problems, leading to computational instability" (§3). **Stated** (no result shown)
4. Replacing only $C_T$ of TENO-Z by Eq. (20) while keeping $\tau_5$ also gives optimal order at higher-order critical points but no efficiency gain, because $\tau_5$ needs absolute values (§3). **Stated**
5. ADR: TENO-AZ has better dispersion and lower dissipation than TENO-Z but is worse than TENO-A and TENO-LAD; near $\vartheta=1.5$ (dispersion) and $\vartheta=2.0$ (dissipation) its curves "show some bias" (§3, Fig. 1). **Shown**
6. At an $n_{\mathrm{cp}}=1$ critical point the four TENO schemes give identical errors, with orders 6.00, 5.58, 5.41, 5.32, 5.26 for $\Delta x=0.001/2^l$, $l=0,\dots,5$ (Table 1). **Shown**
7. At $n_{\mathrm{cp}}=2$ TENO-Z, TENO-A and TENO-LAD give orders 3.00, 2.58, 2.41, 2.32, 2.26 ("reduces to the 2nd-order accuracy at the 2nd-order critical point"); TENO-AZ keeps 6.00–5.26 with the errors of Table 1 (§4.1, Table 2). **Shown**
8. At $n_{\mathrm{cp}}=3$ TENO-Z, TENO-A and TENO-LAD give orders 4.01, 3.59, 3.41, 3.32, 3.26; TENO-AZ again 6.00–5.26 (Table 3). **Shown**
9. The critical-point tests use $\epsilon=\Delta x^{2n_{\mathrm{cp}}+3}$, after the observation (Vevek et al.; Don & Borges) that $\epsilon$ must be of higher order than $\beta_k$ (§4.1). **Stated** (choice)
10. TENO-AZ reaches the optimal order "regardless of the value of $q$"; other values were tested but are not shown (§4.1). **Stated**
11. Linear advection Case 1 (step, $\Delta x=0.01$, $t=2$): TENO-A is closest to the exact solution, TENO-AZ second, and TENO-LAD shows a non-physical oscillation near $x=0$ (§4.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
12. The efficiency index $\mathrm{ACE}=\mathrm{LE}\cdot\mathrm{LT}$, $\mathrm{LE}=-\log_{10}(\text{error})$, $\mathrm{LT}=-\log_{10}(\text{CPU time})$ (Eq. (26)), ranks Case 1 "TENO-AZ>TENO-A>TENO-LAD>TENO-Z" (§4.2, Table 4). **Shown** (only the first place holds at every grid; see Printed slips)
13. Linear advection Case 2 (composite profile, $\Delta x=0.01$, $t=4$): TENO-AZ is better than TENO-Z and TENO-LAD at the square wave; TENO-A shows significant unphysical oscillations where the left Gaussian group meets the square wave (§4.2, Figs. 4–5). **Shown**
14. 1D Euler, 200 cells: TENO-A shows pronounced unphysical oscillations in Sod and the blast wave and, in the 123 problem, "produces a trend directly opposite to that of the trusted reference solution in the region around the point (0, 0)". TENO-AZ is comparable to TENO-LAD and better than TENO-A and TENO-Z, most visibly in the 123 problem (§4.3, Figs. 6–9). **Shown**
15. TENO-A and TENO-LAD fail the interacting blast waves at CFL above 0.2, so CFL 0.2 is used there (§4.3). **Shown** (asserted outcome)
16. 1D CPU time (Intel i7-9700, mean of 1000 runs), ratio to TENO-Z: TENO-AZ 1.00 (Sod), 0.95 (Shu–Osher), 0.96 (123), 1.03 (blast); TENO-A 1.19, 1.15, 1.06, 1.30; TENO-LAD 1.33, 1.35, 1.38, 1.10. TENO-LAD is slower than TENO-A in most 1D cases (§4.3, Table 5). **Shown**
17. 2D CPU time (mean of 100 runs to $t=\nu\Delta x$), ratio to TENO-Z over seven problems: TENO-AZ 0.98–1.01, TENO-A 1.33–1.44, TENO-LAD 1.11–1.18 (§4.4.8, Table 6). **Shown**
18. Isentropic vortex ($\Delta x=0.2$, $t=100$): the TENO-AZ density along $y=5$ is closer to the exact solution than the others (§4.4.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
19. 2D Riemann problem ($512^2$, $t=0.8$): TENO-AZ generates more refined Kelvin–Helmholtz microstructures (§4.4.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
20. Implosion ($400^2$, $t=2.5$): TENO-AZ builds finer and longer diagonal jets; TENO-Z and TENO-LAD show much noise (§4.4.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
21. Double Mach reflection ($960\times240$, $t=0.2$): TENO-AZ gives finer vortices than TENO-Z and TENO-LAD; TENO-A gives more vortices but significantly more noise (§4.4.4). **Shown**
22. Rayleigh–Taylor ($256\times1024$, $t=1.95$): TENO-AZ "preserves the richest vortex structure while maintaining symmetry" (§4.4.5, Fig. 14). **Shown**
23. Shock–mixing layer at $1000\times200$: TENO-Z and TENO-LAD fail at CFL 0.3, 0.5 and 0.8, TENO-A runs at 0.3 and fails at 0.5 and 0.8, TENO-AZ runs at all three (§4.4.6, Fig. 16). **Shown**
24. The cut-off functions of TENO-Z, TENO-A and TENO-LAD "have jump breaks which can lead to instability", while the new adaptive cut-off "has continuity and thus superior numerical stability" (§4.4.6). **Stated** (the cause is not tested; the flag $\delta_k$ stays a 0/1 switch in all four schemes)
25. Forward-facing step ($720\times240$, $t=4$, CFL 0.45 because TENO-Z and TENO-A "may fail if the CFL number is too large"): TENO-AZ captures finer vortices near $y=0.7$ (§4.4.7, Fig. 17). **Shown**
26. TENO-LAD saves about 20% of the CPU time of TENO-A in 2D Euler problems, as reported by Peng et al. (§3). **Stated** (cited)
27. The adaptive process "ensures that the truncation parameter $C_T$ converges to zero at arbitrary order critical points, thereby achieving optimal order accuracy at these points" (§5). **Stated** (shown only for $n_{\mathrm{cp}}\le3$)

# Printed slips

- §1 calls the new cut-off "the maximum of a very small constant $\lambda_d$ and a chosen global smoothness factor"; Eq. (20), §3 and the abstract use the minimum.
- §2, after Eq. (13): "typical values $C_T=1$ and $q=6$" for the parameters $C$ and $q$; §3 writes "choosing $C=1$ and $q=6$".
- Eq. (15), TENO-A: $C_T=10^{\lfloor b\rfloor}$ with $b=b_1-b_2(1-\varphi(\mu_d))$ (checked in the text). With $b_1=10$, $b_2=5$ and $\varphi\in[0,1]$, $b\in[5,10]$ and $C_T\ge10^5$, while $\chi_k\le1$; every $\delta_k$ would vanish and Eq. (10) would divide by zero. The exponent must be $-\lfloor b\rfloor$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §2 calls $q_k$ "the $r-1$ order interpolated value" and, two lines later, "third order fluxes" (correct for $r=3$).
- §3: "since $q$ takes the value of 4" is unexplained; $q=6$ everywhere else. §4.1 reuses $q$ for the grid level ($\Delta x=0.001/2^q$) and writes "critical points of all circles" for "orders".
- Eq. (22): "$f^{(k)}(0)=0$ for $k\le n_{\mathrm{cp}}$" fails at $k=0$, where $f(0)=1$; it holds for $1\le k\le n_{\mathrm{cp}}$.
- Eq. (25): the fourth piece prints $F(x,\alpha,a-\delta)+F(x,\alpha,a+\delta)+4F(x,\alpha,z)$, mixing $a$ and $z$, and gives no value of $a$.
- Fig. 4 caption reads "linear advection Case 1" while the text uses Fig. 4 for Case 2; the Fig. 13 caption reads "implosion problem" while §4.4.4 uses Fig. 13 for the double Mach reflection.
- §4.3, after Eq. (27): "$p$ represents the total energy, and $E$ represents the total energy per unit mass, defined by $E=\frac p{\gamma-1}+\frac12\rho u^2$"; $p$ is the pressure and this $E$ is per unit volume.
- Table 5, interacting blast waves: TENO-A ratio printed 1.30, while $2.0913/1.5552=1.34$; the other ratios of Tables 5 and 6 agree with the printed times to two decimals, the TENO-AZ blast-wave ratio $1.6097/1.5552=1.035$ being printed 1.03 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Table 4 against the text's ranking "TENO-AZ>TENO-A>TENO-LAD>TENO-Z": TENO-Z has an ACE equal to or above TENO-A and TENO-LAD at 200–500 cells (e.g. 500 cells: 1.95, 1.81, 1.78), and equals TENO-LAD at 100 cells (3.07); only the first place of TENO-AZ holds at every grid.
- The introduction says TENO-Z, TENO-A and TENO-LAD "fail at $CFL\ge0.3$" on $1000\times200$ or finer meshes, while §4.4.6 says TENO-A runs at CFL 0.3.
- Eq. (33) prints "$r=\bar x^2+\bar y$" (checked visually) for $r^2=\bar x^2+\bar y^2$.
- Eq. (36), double Mach reflection: the states printed as $(\rho,u,v,p)=(8,57.1597,-33.0012,563.544)$ and $(1.4,0,0,2.5)$ are the conserved variables $(\rho,\rho v_1,\rho v_2,E)$. The pre-shock $E=1/0.4=2.5$ for $p=1$. The post-shock $\rho v_1=8\cdot8.25\cos30^\circ=57.16$, $\rho v_2=-33.0$ and $E=116.5/0.4+\tfrac12\cdot8\cdot8.25^2=563.5$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Rayleigh–Taylor: Eq. (32) has no gravity source term although the problem is gravity driven.
- §4.4 announces "the computational performance of various WENO schemes"; the schemes compared are TENO schemes.
