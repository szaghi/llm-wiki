# Summary

The paper builds **HWENO-R**, a fifth-order finite-difference
[Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) scheme for the 1D and 2D compressible
Euler equations. As in earlier finite-difference HWENO schemes (Liu & Qiu 2015;
Zhao, Zhang & Qiu 2020; Zhang & Zhao 2023, HWENO-L), it evolves the conservation
law together with equations for the spatial derivatives $u_x$ (and $u_y$), and
reconstructs global [Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) split fluxes
of both systems in the [conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
form, from point values of $f^\pm$ and of the derivative flux
$h^\pm=\tfrac12\big(f'(u)u_x\pm a\,u_x\big)$.

The change from HWENO-L is that the derivative at the target point $x_i$ is dropped
from the flux reconstruction: the big stencil $\{f_{i-1},f_i,f_{i+1},h_{i-1},h_{i+1}\}$
gives a quartic (fifth order) instead of HWENO-L's sixth-order quintic, and the two
small stencils are linear polynomials of point values. Nonlinear weights follow the
WENO-ZQ form ([WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md) family), with Jiang–Shu
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and arbitrary positive
linear weights. The derivative flux is reconstructed linearly; the derivative
variables pass through an HWENO limiter only where they enter the
[SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stage combinations; the main fluxes are
reconstructed in [characteristic variables](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md);
2D mixed-derivative fluxes are fourth-order linear.

The authors trace HWENO-L's failures on strong shocks to its unlimited target-point
derivative, which exceeds 4000 after one step of the 1D [Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md)
test against less than 20 for HWENO-R. HWENO-R runs the 1D and 2D Sedov,
[double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md) and
[Leblanc](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md) problems without positivity limiters;
the Mach 2000 [jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md) and a Mach 10
[shock reflection–diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) need the
parametrized [positivity-preserving flux limiter](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md)
of Xiong, Qiu & Xu, implemented in 2D in the Appendix, rather than the
[Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md)
([bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md)). Further tests:
[Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md), a near-vacuum
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[forward step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) and
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md), where the compact
stencil gives lower [numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md).
HWENO-R is more accurate than WENO-ZQ at equal CPU time and slightly cheaper than
HWENO-L. No theorem is proved.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's derivative variable $v=u_x$
is written $u_x$ (registry $v$ is the velocity); its flux linear weights $\gamma_k$
are $d_k$, the limiter's linear weights $d_k$ are $\hat d_k$; its $\alpha$ (splitting
speed) is $a$; its $\tilde\omega_k$ are $\alpha_k$.

1. HWENO-R omits the target-point derivative $h^+_i$ from the flux reconstruction; the big stencil is $\{f^+_{i-1},f^+_i,f^+_{i+1},h^+_{i-1},h^+_{i+1}\}$ (§2.1 Step I, Fig. 2.1, Remark 2.1). **Stated**
2. The scheme is fifth order in 1D and 2D, and the order of the flux reconstruction equals that of the scheme ("optimal"), unlike earlier finite-difference HWENO schemes (§1, Remark 2.1). **Shown** (Tables 3.1–3.3)
3. HWENO-L uses a sixth-order reconstruction yet achieves only fifth order, because the HWENO derivative limiter (2.7) is only fifth order accurate (Remark 2.1, §2.2). **Stated**
4. With $\epsilon\ll\beta_k$, Taylor expansion gives $\tau/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^4)$, hence $\omega_k=d_k+O(\Delta x^4)$, enough for fifth order (§2.1 Step III). **Derived** (expansion not shown)
5. The linear weights of the flux reconstruction and of the limiter can be any positive numbers summing to one (§2.1 Step IV, §2.2). **Stated**
6. The derivative flux $\hat h^+_{i+1/2}$ is the linear value $q_0'(x_{i+1/2})$; "numerically we find that the linear reconstruction ... works well without spurious oscillations" (Eq. 2.5). **Shown**
7. The derivatives are limited by (2.7) only in the values entering the Runge–Kutta stages; the spatial operator uses the unlimited derivatives (Eq. 2.6, §2.2). **Stated**
8. In 2D the mixed-derivative fluxes use the fourth-order linear formula $\frac1{12}(-1,7,7,-1)$ (Eq. 2.11), instead of the third-order split reconstruction of earlier schemes, making the whole scheme fifth order (§2.3). **Stated**
9. Recommended weights: $d_0=0.99$, $d_1=d_2=0.005$ in (2.5) and $\hat d_0=0.9$, $\hat d_1=\hat d_2=0.05$ in (2.7); ratios $d_0/d_1\in[100,300]$ and $\hat d_0/\hat d_1\in[10,30]$ give similar non-oscillatory, high-resolution results (§3, Ex. 3.4, Table 3.4, Figs. 3.5–3.7). **Shown**
10. Burgers (meshes $\ge160$): CPU ratio HWENO-R/WENO-ZQ $\approx1.195$ (1D) and $1.453$ (2D) with error ratios $\approx1/5.263$ and $1/5.291$; HWENO-L/WENO-ZQ $\approx1.452$ and $1.798$ with error ratio $\approx1/6.623$ (Ex. 3.1, Table 3.1). **Shown**
11. 1D Euler (meshes $\ge80$): HWENO-R/WENO-ZQ CPU $\approx1.301$, error $\approx1/5.780$; HWENO-L/WENO-ZQ CPU $\approx1.546$, error $\approx1/5.988$; 320-cell $L^1$ errors $1.51\times10^{-11}$ (both HWENO) against $9.01\times10^{-11}$ (WENO-ZQ) (Ex. 3.2, Table 3.2). **Shown**
12. Isentropic vortex with strength $\mathcal E=10.0828$ (exact $\rho_{\min}=7.8\times10^{-15}$, $p_{\min}=1.7\times10^{-20}$): all three schemes reach fifth order with or without positivity limiters; on meshes $\ge320^2$ the HWENO-R/WENO-ZQ CPU ratio is 1.823 without and 1.609 with limiter, error ratio $\approx1/5.524$ (Ex. 3.3, Table 3.3). **Shown**
13. "At the same CPU cost, the HWENO schemes are more accurate than the WENO-ZQ scheme as the product of the CPU time ratio and the error ratio is less than 1" (Ex. 3.1–3.3). **Shown**
14. Coarse-mesh orders far from five (e.g. 6.99, 7.65, 7.11) are attributed to errors not yet in the asymptotic form $Ch^5$ (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
15. On the Woodward–Colella blast wave both HWENO schemes resolve better than WENO-ZQ, HWENO-R slightly better than HWENO-L (Ex. 3.4, Fig. 3.4). **Shown**
16. HWENO-L cannot run the 1D Sedov problem even with the positivity-preserving flux limiter; after one step its density derivative exceeds 4000, against less than 20 for HWENO-R; positivity limiters "can keep the positivity ... but cannot control numerical oscillations" (Ex. 3.5, Fig. 3.9). **Shown**
17. HWENO-R runs the 1D Sedov, double rarefaction, Leblanc (pressure ratio $10^9$) and 2D Sedov problems without positivity limiters; "other FD HWENO schemes ... cannot simulate" Leblanc and 2D Sedov "directly" (Ex. 3.5–3.7, 3.10). **Shown** for HWENO-R; **Stated** for the other schemes beyond HWENO-L
18. The Mach 2000 jet and the Mach 10 shock reflection–diffraction need the positivity-preserving flux limiter for both HWENO-R and WENO-ZQ; it activates in 0.0146% and 0.0012% of the cells (Remark 2.3, Ex. 3.11–3.12, Remark 3.2). **Shown**
19. Forward step (960×320): CPU 29093.92 s HWENO-R, 31945.12 s HWENO-L, 20542.55 s WENO-ZQ (ratios 1.416 and 1.555); jet 2292.73 s (ratio 1.577); diffraction 12902.85 s (ratio $\approx1.496$); Fortran 95 on an Intel Xeon Gold 6130 (Ex. 3.9, 3.11, 3.12). **Shown**
20. Rayleigh–Taylor: HWENO-R on 120×480 is comparable to WENO-ZQ on 240×960, i.e. lower dissipation from compact stencils (Ex. 3.13, Fig. 3.17). **Shown**
21. The 2D parametrized positivity-preserving flux limiter (Appendix, Eqs. A.4–A.10, Algorithm A.1) is sufficient only, so it may activate without negative density or pressure; that it keeps high order "relies on numerical verification" (Appendix). **Stated**
22. A fifth-order finite-difference WENO uses five points, a same-order HWENO only three (§1). **Stated**
23. Boundary treatment of the derivatives: $u_x$ follows $u$ for periodic, reflective and outflow boundaries, and is set to 0 at Dirichlet or inflow boundaries (Remark 2.4). **Stated**
24. The parametrized flux limiter is preferred to polynomial scaling because it "allows larger time steps in practice and is more convenient for FD schemes" (§1, Remark 2.3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §2.1 Step I, p. 4: $q_0(x_{i+1/2})$ is printed with "$-\Delta x\big(\frac3{40}h^+_{i-1}-\frac7{40}h^+_{i+1}\big)$"; the five Hermite conditions give $-\Delta x\big(\frac3{40}h^+_{i-1}+\frac7{40}h^+_{i+1}\big)$. Data of $f=x$ must return $\Delta x/2$; the printed formula gives $0.85\,\Delta x$. The printed $q_0'(x_{i+1/2})$ and $\beta_0$ agree with the quartic. *(compiler check, not in the sources: symbolic solution of the five conditions and evaluation of the Jiang–Shu indicator of the quartic.)*
- §2.2, p. 6: the printed $\hat\beta_0$ of the derivative limiter is not the Jiang–Shu indicator (2.4) of the stated point-value quartic $p_0$. For $u=x^3$ ($u_{i\pm1}=\pm1$, $u_i=0$, $(u_x)_{i\pm1}=3$, $\Delta x=1$), (2.4) gives $3129/80=39.1125$ and the printed formula $39.06$; the two agree on quadratic data. With $A=u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}$, $B=\Delta x\big((u_x)_{i-1}+(u_x)_{i+1}\big)$, $C=u_{i-1}-2u_i+u_{i+1}$, $D=\Delta x\big((u_x)_{i-1}-(u_x)_{i+1}\big)$, the indicator of $p_0$ is $(78309A^2+138138AB+66549B^2+4265648C^2+4221520CD+1051628D^2)/26880$. Either the printed coefficients are wrong or $p_0$ is defined differently in HWENO-L; the printed $\hat\beta_0$ should not be transcribed as exact. *(compiler check, not in the sources: symbolic interpolation and integration.)*
- pp. 5–6: the weight normalizations sum "$\tilde\omega_1+\tilde\omega_2+\tilde\omega_3$" and "$\varpi_1+\varpi_2+\varpi_3$" over $k=0,1,2$.
- §2.3: "$w_0(x,y)=w_y(x,y,0)$" should read $u_y(x,y,0)$.
- Appendix (A.2): "$Q(U,W)=G'(U)W$"; by (2.9) it is $F'(U)W$.
- Appendix Step II: the box $S_\rho=[0,\Lambda^\rho_L]\times[0,\Lambda^\rho_R]\times[0,\Lambda^\rho_L]\times[0,\Lambda^\rho_D]$; its last two factors should be $[0,\Lambda^\rho_D]\times[0,\Lambda^\rho_U]$.
- Algorithm A.1, line 15: "If $f(k)=1$" should be "If $I_s(k)=1$".
- Ex. 3.12: the domain is printed "a union of $[0,1]\times[0,1]$ and $[-1,1]\times[1,3]$"; the wall $\{1\}\times[-1,0]$ and the plotted axes require $[1,3]\times[-1,1]$.
- Table 3.4 caption "HWENO-R:(Gk,Dk)" against the text "HWENO-R:(Dk,Gk)".
- Grant number printed "2022YFA1004500" (p. 1) and "2022Y-FA1004500" (p. 23).
