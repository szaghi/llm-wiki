# Summary

The HLLEM solver of Einfeldt et al. adds to HLLE anti-diffusion terms for the
two linearly degenerate fields (entropy and shear waves). It resolves contacts
and shear layers like Roe, but it shares the
[carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md) and odd–even decoupling of
Roe and HLLC at strong grid-aligned shocks
([HLL-family solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md)). The paper diagnoses
the instability with two tools. A Quirk-type saw-tooth linear analysis of a
uniform flow shows that the transverse HLLEM flux leaves density and
shear-velocity perturbations with amplification factor exactly 1. A
Dumbser–Moschetta–Gressier matrix analysis of an isolated Mach 8 shock finds
eigenvalues with positive real part for HLLEM but not for HLLE. Hybrid
experiments show that both the entropy-wave and the shear-wave anti-diffusion
of the transverse flux must be controlled.

The cure, RHLLEM, multiplies both anti-diffusion terms by a smooth switch
$f_{\mathrm s}=\tfrac12(1-\cos\pih_{\mathrm p})$, where $h_{\mathrm p}$ is the minimum
pressure ratio over the four transverse interfaces of the two adjacent cells.
It has no tunable parameter and adds no CFL restriction. RHLLEM-LD then writes
the flux as a central part plus a [dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md)
term and takes the density inside that term from a
[BVD](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md) choice among
[THINC](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md) and second-order
[MUSCL](/methods/muscl-hancock.md) interface values that minimizes the density
jump. Time stepping is second-order [TVD Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md).

Twelve 1D and 2D tests compare HLLE, HLLEM, HLLC, Park & Kwon's HLLE+ and
RHLLEM-LD: an isolated contact, [Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
the Titarev–Toro variant of [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), an
interface-only advection, [Richtmyer–Meshkov](/benchmarks/richtmyer-meshkov-instability.md)
and [Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md), a
stationary inclined shock, Elling's test, [Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md),
Quirk's moving shock, the [forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md)
and [shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md). RHLLEM-LD gives the
sharpest contacts and shows no shock instability, yet keeps Elling's physical
carbuncle, which HLLE suppresses. The contact tests concern moving contacts;
[stationary contact preservation](/properties/stationary-contact-preservation.md)
is the property HLLEM inherits from Roe. The "hybrid" here is a directional
hybrid of Riemann solvers, not a [hybrid central–WENO scheme](/methods/hybrid-weno.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's $S_L$, $S_R$ are $s^-$,
$s^+$, its velocity components $u$, $v$ are $v_1$, $v_2$, its sound speed $a$
is $c$, its states $(\cdot)_L$, $(\cdot)_R$ are $(\cdot)^-$, $(\cdot)^+$, its
switching function $f$ and sensor $h$ are $f_{\mathrm s}$, $h_{\mathrm p}$, its
$\lambda=\sigma_ya_0$ is $\nu$, its Roe averages $\tilde{(\cdot)}$ are $\hat{(\cdot)}$, and its
$R_k$, $\alpha_k$, $\delta_k$ ($k=2,3$) are $\hat r_k$, $\mathfrak w_k$, $\delta_{\mathrm a}$, as on the
[HLL-family solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) page.

1. Compared with Roe, HLLEM is more efficient, lets the dissipation on nonlinear waves be adjusted through $s^\pm$, and is free of the sonic-point glitch and expansion shocks (§1, §2.3). **Stated**
2. HLLEM is equivalent to the Roe solver for appropriate anti-diffusion parameters and wave speeds (§2.3). **Stated**
3. For a supersonic mean flow HLLE and HLLEM coincide (upwind flux); the streamwise saw-tooth amplification matrix (3.3) has eigenvalues $1-2\sigma_xv_{1,0}$ (double) and $1-2\sigma_x(v_{1,0}\pm c_0)$, so it is stable for $\sigma_x<1/(v_{1,0}+c_0)$, $\sigma_x=\Delta t/\Delta x$ (§3.1.1, Eqs. (3.3)–(3.4)). **Derived**
4. Subsonic mean flow, streamwise: HLLE eigenvalues $1-2\sigma_xc_0$ (double), $1-2\sigma_x(c_0\pm v_{1,0})$; HLLEM $1-2\sigma_xv_{1,0}$ (double), $1-2\sigma_x(c_0\pm v_{1,0})$; both stable for $\sigma_x<1/(c_0+v_{1,0})$ (Eqs. (3.5)–(3.8)). **Derived**
5. Transverse saw-tooth perturbations: the HLLE amplification matrix is $(1-2\sigma_yc_0)I$, stable for $\sigma_y<1/c_0$ (Eq. (3.11)). **Derived**
6. Transverse, HLLEM: $\delta\rho^{n+1}=\delta\rho^n-(2\sigma_y/c_0)\delta p^n$, $\delta v_1^{n+1}=\delta v_1^n$, $\delta v_2^{n+1}=(1-2\sigma_yc_0)\delta v_2^n$, $\delta p^{n+1}=(1-2\sigma_yc_0)\delta p^n$; the spectral radius is 1 for every $\sigma_y$, because density and shear-velocity perturbations are not damped (Eq. (3.12)). **Derived**. *(compiler check, not in the sources: for a saw-tooth perturbation the linearized HLL flux difference is $\frac{2(s^++s^-)A-4s^-s^+}{s^+-s^-}\,\delta$; with $s^\mp=v_{1,0}\mp c_0$ (streamwise) its eigenvalues give the factors of claims 3–4, the HLLEM coefficient $\delta_{\mathrm a}=c_0/(c_0+v_{1,0})$ turning $1-2\sigma_xc_0$ into $1-2\sigma_xv_{1,0}$. Transversally ($s^\mp=\mp c_0$, $\delta_{\mathrm a}=1$) HLLE damps every component by $1-2\sigma_yc_0$, while HLLEM removes the damping of the entropy combination $\delta\rho-\delta p/c_0^2$ and of the shear velocity $\delta v_1$, which reproduces (3.12); scaling that anti-diffusion by $f_{\mathrm s}$ reproduces (4.5).)*
7. "The non-attenuation of density and shear velocity perturbations in the transverse flux is directly responsible for the shock instability" (§3.1.2). **Stated** (inference from a linear analysis about a smooth uniform flow, supported by the numerics of §3.2)
8. Matrix stability analysis of an isolated steady Mach 8 shock ($[0,1]^2$, $30\times30$ cells, noise $10^{-8}$): HLLE attenuates random noise, HLLEM does not, by the criterion $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathsf S)\le0$ (Figs. 3.1–3.2, Eq. (3.16)). **Shown**
9. The matrix analysis shows "unconditional instability" of HLLEM, since it is semi-discrete and independent of the CFL number (§3.2). **Stated**
10. Directional hybrids (HLLEM in $x$ with HLLEC, HLLES or HLLE in $y$) show that the instability depends on both the shear-wave and the entropy-wave anti-diffusion of the transverse flux (Figs. 3.3–3.4); on the stationary inclined shock, HLLEM-EV and HLLEM-SV, which switch only one term, stay unstable (Fig. 5.7, §5.7). **Shown**
11. A shock-stable scheme must pass both criteria; the two analyses differ in mean state (smooth flow against a Rankine–Hugoniot shock), perturbation (saw-tooth against random) and locality (§3.3). **Stated**
12. RHLLEM (4.1): $\hat f=\dfrac{s^+f(u^-)-s^-f(u^+)}{s^+-s^-}+\dfrac{s^-s^+}{s^+-s^-}\big(u^+-u^--f_{\mathrm s}\delta_{\mathrm a}\mathfrak w_2\hat r_2-f_{\mathrm s}\delta_{\mathrm a}\mathfrak w_3\hat r_3\big)$ with $h_{\mathrm p}=\min$ of $\min(p^-/p^+,p^+/p^-)$ over the four transverse interfaces and $f_{\mathrm s}=\tfrac12(1-\cos\pih_{\mathrm p})$ or $h_{\mathrm p}^3$ (Eqs. (4.1)–(4.4)); $f_{\mathrm s}=1$ is HLLEM, $0$ is HLLE; both switches give shock-stable solutions, but $h_{\mathrm p}^3$ is more dissipative and smears physical structures, so the cosine form is used throughout (§4.1). **Shown** (Fig. 5.8g)
13. RHLLEM transverse amplification factors are $1-2\nu(1-f_{\mathrm s})$ for $\delta\rho$, $\delta v_1$ and $1-2\nu$ for $\delta v_2$, $\delta p$, $\nu=\sigma_yc_0$; stability requires $0<\nu<\min\big(1,1/(1-f_{\mathrm s})\big)$, i.e. $0<\nu<1$: the switch adds no CFL restriction (Eqs. (4.5)–(4.6)). **Derived**
14. Matrix stability of RHLLEM on the same shock: $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathsf S)=-0.6963$ (Fig. 4.2). **Shown**
15. Unlike HLLE+ of Park & Kwon (empirical $\beta^*$) and the abrupt HLLC-to-HLL switch of Kim et al., RHLLEM has no empirical parameter and switches smoothly, which "does not affect the convergence of the scheme" and suits unsteady shock problems (§4.1). **Stated**
16. RHLLEM-LD: the density in the dissipation term $D_{1/2}$ of $\hat f=\tfrac12(f^-+f^+)+D_{1/2}$ is chosen among the THINC and MUSCL left and right values to minimize $\lvert\rho^+-\rho^-\rvert$ over all four combinations (Eqs. (4.7)–(4.9), five-step algorithm, §4.2). **Stated** (algorithm)
17. The BVD density does not change robustness at strong shocks: HLLEM-LD stays unstable like HLLEM, RHLLEM-LD stable like RHLLEM (Fig. 5.7g–h, §5.7). **Shown**
18. Isolated contact (100 cells, CFL 0.9, $t=2$): RHLLEM-LD is sharper than HLLE and HLLEM (Fig. 5.1). **Shown**
19. Woodward–Colella blast waves (500 cells, CFL 0.9, $t=0.038$): RHLLEM-LD has "obviously higher resolution for all waves" (Fig. 5.2). **Shown**
20. Titarev–Toro (1000 cells, CFL 0.9, $t=5$): HLLE is highly damped; RHLLEM-LD is more accurate on the high-frequency wave (Fig. 5.3). **Shown**
21. Interface-only advection ($100\times100$, $t=0.3$), Richtmyer–Meshkov ($640\times160$, $t=9$) and Kelvin–Helmholtz ($1000\times1000$, $t=2$): RHLLEM-LD gives the sharpest interfaces; in the Kelvin–Helmholtz run it alone keeps the symmetry and yields more filamentation (Figs. 5.4–5.6). **Shown**
22. Elling test: HLLC and HLLEM reproduce the physical carbuncle but oscillate along the shock; HLLE is stable but misses it; HLLE+ fails with $\beta^*=0.01$ and is "slightly better but still undesirable" with $0.1$; RHLLEM-LD gives an oscillation-free shock and the physical carbuncle; with the switch $h_{\mathrm p}^3$ it suppresses the oscillation but misses the carbuncle (Fig. 5.8). **Shown**
23. Sedov ($960\times960$), Quirk's moving Mach 8 shock ($1500\times20$, $t=100$), forward-facing step ($720\times240$, $t=4$) and Mach 5.09 shock diffraction ($400\times400$, $t=0.15$): HLLC and HLLEM ("especially the latter") show shock instabilities, RHLLEM-LD does not (Figs. 5.9–5.13). **Shown**
24. Increasing shear viscosity alone cannot always remove the carbuncle; rotated solvers are inefficient, with two flux evaluations per interface (§1). **Stated**
