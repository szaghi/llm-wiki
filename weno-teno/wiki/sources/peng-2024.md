# Summary

The paper proposes the [oscillation-eliminating discontinuous Galerkin (OEDG)](/methods/oedg.md)
method, a modification of the oscillation-free DG (OFDG) method of Lu, Liu and Shu.
OFDG adds damping terms to the semi-discrete [DG](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md)
equations. Near discontinuities these terms are very stiff, so small explicit time
steps or exponential Runge–Kutta integrators are needed. OEDG splits the evolution
instead: after each Runge–Kutta stage of the unmodified DG scheme, an
oscillation-eliminating (OE) step solves a separate damping ODE over one full time
step. Its coefficients are frozen at the stage solution, so the ODE is linear and
diagonal in a Legendre modal basis and is solved exactly. Each mode of degree $l\ge1$
is multiplied by $\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{a_i\Delta t}{\Delta x_i}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\sigma_i^m\big)$,
and the cell average is untouched. The damping is therefore a modal filter, which links
it to spectral viscosity and [artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md).
Unlike [troubled-cell indicators](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) and limiters,
it is applied in every cell.

The new damping coefficients are normalized by the global quantity
$\lVert u_h-\mathrm{avg}(u_h)\rVert_{L^\infty(\Omega)}$ and multiplied by a local wave
speed $a_i$. This makes the OE step [scale invariant](/properties/scale-invariance.md)
($\mathcal F_{\Delta t}(\lambda u_h+\mu)=\lambda\mathcal F_{\Delta t}u_h+\mu$) and the
scheme evolution invariant. The step acts on conservative variables, with no
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md).

For linear advection with the upwind flux on 1D and 2D Cartesian meshes, the paper
proves the fully discrete estimate $\mathcal O(\Delta x^{N+1}+\Delta t^{r_{\mathrm{RK}}})$
in $L^2$. Numerical tests show optimal orders for advection, Burgers and Euler, and
exact scale and evolution invariance where OFDG smears or oscillates. Further tests,
with the [local Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) and
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md), are the
[Lax problem](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast wave](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) and a
[Mach 2000 jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md).

# Key claims

The source writes $k$ for the DG degree, $\tau$ for the time step, $h_j$ for the cell
width, $\beta_j$ for the wave-speed estimate and $j$ for the cell index. Below these are
$N$, $\Delta t$, $\Delta x_i$, $a_i$ and $i$; modes are indexed by $l$ and the RK order
is $r_{\mathrm{RK}}$.

1. OFDG makes the semi-discrete system (1.2) highly stiff for discontinuous problems, so explicit time steps become very restrictive and (modified) exponential Runge–Kutta methods are needed (§1). **Stated**
2. OEDG alternates the standard semi-discrete DG scheme $\frac{d}{dt}U=\mathcal L(U)$ with a quasi-linear damping ODE (1.3); the OE step is applied after every Runge–Kutta stage ((2.3)–(2.4), (3.4)–(3.7)). **Derived**
3. With coefficients frozen at the stage solution and an orthogonal basis, the damping ODE is linear and diagonal. Its exact solution multiplies the modal coefficient of degree $l\ge1$ by $\exp\!\big(-\frac{a_i\Delta t}{\Delta x_i}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\sigma_i^m\big)$ and leaves the cell average unchanged ((2.9)–(2.12)). **Derived**
4. The apparently first-order splitting does not reduce the order, because the damping term is of high order for smooth solutions (§1.1). **Stated** (backed by Thm 4.1 for linear advection)
5. The OE step preserves cell averages, and hence local conservation (Remark 2.2). **Derived**
6. The OE step does not increase the $L^2$ norm in any cell, and OEDG is stable under the usual CFL condition even when the damping is very stiff near strong shocks (Remark 2.3, §5). **Derived** (norm), **Shown** (CFL)
7. The damping operator acts as a modal filter, which links oscillation-free damping to spectral viscosity (Remark 2.3, §1.2). **Derived**
8. Scale invariance: $\sigma_i^m(\lambda u_h+\mu)=\sigma_i^m(u_h)$ for all $\lambda\ne0$ and $\mu\in\mathbb R$, hence $\mathcal F_{\Delta t}(\lambda u_h+\mu)=\lambda\mathcal F_{\Delta t}(u_h)+\mu$ (Thm 2.6). **Proved** (immediate from the definition)
9. Homogeneity: if $f$ and the numerical flux are homogeneous, then $\mathcal E_n(\lambda u_h^0)=\lambda\mathcal E_n(u_h^0)$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ (Thm 2.7). **Proved**
10. Evolution invariance: for $\partial_tu+\lambda\partial_xf(u)=0$, $\lambda>0$, on a fixed mesh at the same CFL number ($\Delta t_\lambda=\Delta t/\lambda$), $\mathcal E_n^\lambda=\mathcal E_n$. This requires $a_i$ to scale with $\lambda$ (Thm 2.10, (2.15)–(2.16)). **Proved**
11. The OFDG coefficient (2.13) is not scale invariant, $\hat\sigma_i^m(\lambda u_h)=\lvert\lambda\rvert\hat\sigma_i^m(u_h)$, and it carries no wave speed (Remark 2.8, §1.1). **Derived**
12. The damping rate times $\Delta t$ is the argument of an exponential, so it must be dimensionless. Scale invariance makes $\sigma_i^m$ dimensionless, and the factor $a_i$ makes $a_i\Delta t/\Delta x_i$ dimensionless (Remark 2.1, §1.2). **Derived**
13. OEDG needs no characteristic decomposition for systems. Without scale-invariant coefficients the result would depend on the arbitrary scaling of the eigenvectors, and a characteristic decomposition can destroy scale invariance (Remark 2.12; Ex. 5.7, Fig. 4). **Shown**
14. Thm 4.1: assume linear advection with the upwind flux on quasi-uniform 1D meshes or 2D Cartesian meshes, explicit Runge–Kutta, $\lVert u_h^{n,\ell}-\mathrm{avg}(u_h^{n,\ell})\rVert_{L^\infty}\ge C>0$, $N>d/2$, $r_{\mathrm{RK}}>d/2+1$, an RKDG scheme stable in the sense of Assumption 4.5, a smooth solution and $\lVert u_\sigma^0-u(\cdot,0)\rVert\le C\Delta x^{N+1}$. Then $\max_{n\le n^\star}\lVert u_\sigma^n-u(\cdot,t^n)\rVert\le C(\Delta x^{N+1}+\Delta t^{r_{\mathrm{RK}}})$ (4.2). **Proved**
15. The authors suggest this may be "the first generic fully-discrete error estimates for nonlinear DG schemes with an automatic oscillation control mechanism" (abstract, §1.3, §6). **Stated**
16. The restrictions $N>d/2$ and $r_{\mathrm{RK}}>d/2+1$ are technical: they validate the a priori assumption (4.20) in Lemma 4.10. Optimal convergence is observed with lower $N$ and $r_{\mathrm{RK}}$ in multi-D (Remark 4.4). **Stated / Shown**
17. RKDG stability (Prop. 4.6, collected from the literature): for $r_{\mathrm{RK}}$-stage, order-$r_{\mathrm{RK}}$ Runge–Kutta on linear problems, Assumption 4.5 holds with $\kappa_t=1+1/r_{\mathrm{RK}}$ if $r_{\mathrm{RK}}\equiv1\pmod4$; $\kappa_t=1+1/(r_{\mathrm{RK}}+1)$ if $r_{\mathrm{RK}}\equiv2\pmod4$; $\kappa_t=1$, $C_s=0$ if $r_{\mathrm{RK}}\equiv3\pmod4$; and $\kappa_t=1$, $C_s=0$ after combining 2 or 3 steps if $r_{\mathrm{RK}}\equiv0\pmod4$. **Stated** (cited)
18. The OE perturbation is bounded: if $\lVert e^{n,\ell+1}\rVert_{L^\infty}\le\Delta x$, then $\lVert u_\sigma^{n,\ell+1}-u_h^{n,\ell+1}\rVert\le C\Delta t\big(\lVert e^{n,\ell+1}\rVert+\Delta x^{N+1}\big)$ (Lemma 4.14). **Proved**
19. Ex. 5.1 (1D advection of $\sin^2(2\pi x)$): optimal order $N+1$ in $L^1$, $L^2$ and $L^\infty$, approached from above; on coarse meshes the damping dominates the error (for $N=2$ the $L^1$ rates are 3.91, 3.64, 3.39, 3.21). Cell averages and downwind point values superconverge at order $N+2$ (final rates 2.99, 4.02, 5.00 for $N=1,2,3$). This is not proved for fully discrete OEDG (§5.1, Table 1). **Shown**
20. On coarse meshes OEDG errors exceed plain RKDG errors: at $N_x=128$, $N=2$, $L^2$ errors are $1.08\times10^{-5}$ against $2.25\times10^{-6}$; at $N_x=2048$, $6.23\times10^{-10}$ against $5.48\times10^{-10}$ (Tables 1–2). **Shown**
21. Optimal $N+1$ orders also hold for Burgers (Table 3, up to $N_x=8192$) and 1D Euler (Table 4) with smooth solutions, beyond the linear theory (§5.2, §5.4). **Shown**
22. Ex. 5.2: OEDG reproduces $u_h^\lambda/\lambda=u_h^1$ exactly for $\lambda\in\{1,0.01,100\}$, and the same solution when the advection speed is scaled by $0.01$ or $100$ at the scaled final time. OFDG oscillates for small amplitude or high speed and smears for large amplitude or low speed (Fig. 1). **Shown**
23. Lighthill–Whitham–Richards traffic flow (Ex. 5.4): OEDG gives the same results in three systems of units, while OFDG smears in one and oscillates in another (Fig. 2). **Shown**
24. Lax and Shu–Osher problems scaled by $\lambda\in\{1,0.01,100\}$: OEDG is exactly scale invariant and free of oscillations. OFDG smears at $\lambda=100$, oscillates at $\lambda=0.01$ and undershoots near the Lax shock even at $\lambda=1$ (Ex. 5.6, 5.8; Figs. 3, 5). **Shown**
25. Woodward–Colella blast wave: OFDG without characteristic decomposition smears strongly, while OEDG works with or without it and resolves the solution better (Ex. 5.7, Fig. 4). **Shown**
26. 2D Euler Riemann problems at $\lambda=100$: the OFDG damping grows "to $\lambda^2$ times its original magnitudes" and smooths out features, while OEDG matches its $\lambda=1$ result (Ex. 5.15, Figs. 10–11). **Shown** (the $\lambda^2$ factor is **Stated**; see Printed slips)
27. Steady states: the mean residual reaches machine-precision level for the shock reflection problem (from about $t=9$) and for supersonic flow past two plates (Ex. 5.13–5.14, Figs. 8b, 9b). **Shown**
28. Robustness: OEDG computes double Mach reflection ($\mathbb P^3$, $\Delta x=1/240$), shock–vortex interaction and the Mach 2000 jet ($320\times160$, third order) without nonphysical oscillations. No positivity-preserving limiter is mentioned (Ex. 5.16–5.18). **Shown**
29. A locally scale-invariant coefficient (2.14) is proposed, but "it is difficult to determine a unified mesh-independent constant" for it, which is left to future work (Remark 2.9). **Stated**
30. The new damping coefficient, used inside OFDG, would give OFDG the same scale and evolution invariance (Remark 2.11, §1.1). **Stated** (not shown)

# Printed slips

- Remark 2.3, displayed identity (p. 7): the $L^2$ norm in a cell is written as $\sum_{i=0}^k\lvert u_j^{(i)}\rvert\,\lVert\phi_j^{(i)}\rVert^2_{L^2(I_j)}$; the modal coefficients should be squared.
- §2.2: "Let $\{\phi_j^{(i)}(x)\}_{i=1}^k$ be a local orthogonal basis of $\mathbb P^k(I_j)$": the index should start at $i=0$, since $\phi^{(0)}=1$ is used.
- Ex. 5.15 against Remark 2.8: the text says the OFDG damping grows "to $\lambda^2$ times its original magnitudes" at $\lambda=100$, while Remark 2.8 gives $\hat\sigma(\lambda u)=\lvert\lambda\rvert\hat\sigma(u)$, a factor $\lambda$.
- Table 4 (1D Euler): the first rows for $k=1$ and $k=2$ ($N_x=256$) carry rates 2.78 and 4.02 with no coarser row printed; Table 5 (2D advection) has the same defect in its first $k=3$ row (rates 6.11, 6.00, 5.79).
- (3.12): $1/\lvert h_{y,ij}\rvert$ and $1/\lvert h_{x,ij}\rvert$ inside the face averages do not match the notation $h_{y,j}$, $h_{x,i}$ defined just above.
- (4.4): "$P^\star\eta^-_{j+1/2}=0$" is printed; the Gauss–Radau condition is $\eta^-_{j+1/2}=0$.
- Typos: "ones has" (Thm 2.6), "C along" for "C alone" (§4), "ithe component" (§3.1), mixed dashes in "Lighthill—Whitham-–Richards".
