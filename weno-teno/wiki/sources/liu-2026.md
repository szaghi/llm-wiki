# Summary

Liu et al. (2026) propose the neural entropy-stable conservative flux form neural
network (NESCFN), a data-driven model that learns an unknown hyperbolic
conservation law and a matching convex entropy from discrete solution
trajectories. It extends the authors' KT-ESCFN, in which a network replaced the
physical flux inside a fixed Kurganov–Tadmor
[central scheme](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) with a known entropy. NESCFN
drops the prescribed scheme and follows Tadmor's construction of
[entropy-stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md) fluxes: an
[entropy-conservative flux](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md) plus a
dissipation $\tfrac12\mathcal D_{i+1/2}[\![w]\!]_{i+1/2}$ with
$\mathcal D\succeq0$, specialised to a Rusanov-type
$\mathcal D=a\,(\eta'')^{-1}$ ([Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)
in entropy variables). Three networks are trained: a fully connected network for
the physical flux, an input convex neural network (ICNN) for the entropy, convex by
construction, and a network mapping the flux Jacobian to a wave speed bounded by
$\Delta x/\Delta t$. The entropy-conservative flux is replaced by the average of
the neural flux at minmod-limited Kurganov–Tadmor interface states; the time
stepper is a two-stage TVD Runge–Kutta method. Training combines a recurrent
trajectory loss with penalties driving $\eta''$ towards the identity and
$\eta''f'$ towards symmetry, the condition under which a strictly convex $\eta$ is
an entropy. The method sits next to the
[learned WENO weights](/methods/learned-weno-weights.md) as another use of
networks inside a conservative finite-volume update, here for the flux and entropy
rather than for the reconstruction.

Tests on 1D [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md), 1D
[shallow water](/definitions/shallow-water-equations.md) with
[dam-break](/benchmarks/dam-break-problem.md) data, 1D Euler with
[Sod-type](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and
[Shu–Osher-type](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) families, and 2D Burgers, with
training data from PyClaw and Gaussian noise of relative level up to 1, show
predictions beyond the training window, shock formation in Burgers learned from
pre-shock data only, small conservation errors and a non-positive discrete entropy
remainder over each family of 101 out-of-distribution initial conditions. NESCFN is
less noise-robust than KT-ESCFN on Burgers. No convergence or stability theorem is
proved for the learned scheme.

# Key claims

Registry notation: cells $I_i$ and averages $\bar u_i$ (source $I_j$, $u_j$),
entropy $\eta$ and flux $F_\eta$ (source $G$), entropy variables $w=\eta'(u)^\top$
(source $v$), entropy-conservative flux $f^{\mathrm{ec}}$ (source $f^*$, $g^*$),
interface states $u^\mp_{i+1/2}$ (agree), maximum wave speed $a$ (source
$\lambda^{\max}$). Page-local: $\mathcal D_{i+1/2}$ the dissipation matrix (source
$D$), $f_\mu$ the neural physical flux (source $\mathcal F^\mu$), $\eta_\theta$ the ICNN entropy,
$a_w$ the learned wave speed (as in the source), $\mathcal J$ the entropy remainder.

1. NESCFN learns both a numerical flux and a corresponding entropy directly from trajectories, without a predefined numerical discretization (Abstract, §1, Remark 3.2). **Stated**
2. The structure-preserving networks CFN and RoeNet give no formal entropy-stability guarantee; RoeNet "claims that entropy is not violated" without analysis or empirical validation (§1). **Stated**
3. ESCFN embeds a neural flux in the second-order Kurganov–Tadmor scheme, which implicitly assumes a class of entropy pairs and a pre-specified scheme (§1, §2.2.3). **Stated**
4. Theorem 2.3 (adapted from Godlewski–Raviart): a strictly convex $\eta$ is an entropy if and only if the matrices $\eta''(u)f_d'(u)$ are symmetric; a strictly convex entropy implies symmetrizability and hence hyperbolicity (§2.1.2, sketch given). **Derived** (recalled result)
5. The entropy-stable flux $\hat f_{i+1/2}=f^{\mathrm{ec}}_{i+1/2}-\tfrac12\mathcal D_{i+1/2}[\![w]\!]_{i+1/2}$ (Eq. (2.14), Tadmor, Thm 2.6) is only first order in space, because $[\![w]\!]=O(\Delta x)$, even with a high-order entropy-conservative part; higher order needs a reconstruction of $w$ (§2.2.2). **Stated**
6. With $\mathcal D_{i+1/2}=\tilde R(aI)\tilde R^\top$ and $u'(w)=\tilde R\tilde R^\top$ (scaled eigenvectors, existence cited to Barth), the Rusanov-type flux is $\hat f_{i+1/2}=f^{\mathrm{ec}}_{i+1/2}-\tfrac12a_{i+1/2}\big(\eta''(u_{i+1/2})\big)^{-1}[\![\eta'(u)]\!]_{i+1/2}$ (Eqs. (3.1)–(3.2)). **Derived**
7. The NESCFN flux (Eq. (3.5)) is $h_{i+1/2}=\tfrac12\big(f_\mu(u^+_{i+1/2})+f_\mu(u^-_{i+1/2})\big)-\tfrac12\max\big(a_w(u^+),a_w(u^-)\big)\big(\eta_\theta''(\{\!\{u\}\!\}_{i+1/2})\big)^{-1}\big(\eta_\theta'(u^+_{i+1/2})-\eta_\theta'(u^-_{i+1/2})\big)$, $\{\!\{u\}\!\}_{i+1/2}=\tfrac12(u^-+u^+)$, the averaged neural flux standing in for $f^{\mathrm{ec}}$ (Eq. (3.4)). It is said to be entropy stable for the learned law $\partial_tu+\partial_xf_\mu(u)=0$ with respect to $\eta_\theta$ "thanks to the structural guarantees of convex input networks and Theorem 2.6" (§3.1). **Stated** (no proof) *(Compiler check, not in the sources: Thm 2.6 assumes that the central part satisfies Tadmor's condition $[\![w]\!]\cdot f^{\mathrm{ec}}=[\![\psi]\!]$, and an arithmetic average of the flux does not in general. For Burgers with $\eta=u^2/2$, so $w=u$ and $\psi=u^3/6$, the states $u^-=0$, $u^+=1$ give $[\![\psi]\!]=1/6$ but $[\![w]\!]\cdot\tfrac12(f(u^-)+f(u^+))=1/4$. The training does not enforce the condition. The paper's evidence for entropy stability is the numerical test of claims 15, 17, 19 and 21.)*
8. If training enforces the symmetric structure of Theorem 2.3, the learned law is hyperbolic (§3.1). **Stated** (the training penalises asymmetry; the second training stage is switched off for three of the four problems, Table 4.1)
9. The wave-speed surrogate $a_w=\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}\big[1-\tanh\lvert\phi_w(f_\mu'(u))\rvert\big]$ is positive, bounded by $\Delta x/\Delta t$ and enforces the CFL condition (§3.1). **Stated**
10. Roe-type dissipation $\tilde R\lvert\Lambda\rvert\tilde R^\top$ would be less diffusive but is left to future work, because $\tilde R$ is hard to compute stably in a learnable setting (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
11. The Hessian is inverted by a Cholesky solve of $\eta_\theta''+C_1C_2^{\mathrm{epoch}}I$, $C_1=10$, $C_2=0.3$ (Eq. (3.6), §4.4.3). **Stated**
12. TVD-RK2 is chosen for its small memory footprint on GPUs (§3.2, Alg. 3.1). **Stated**
13. Training data are realistic: no oracle access to future solution profiles and no curation for smoothness (§4.1). **Stated**
14. 1D Burgers: training data only on $[0,0.1]$, before the shock forms, yet NESCFN captures the shock formation at $t=1$ and predicts up to $t=3$ (§5.2.1, Fig. 5.1). **Shown**
15. 1D Burgers: the shock structure survives in long-time predictions for noise level $\le0.5$, against $\le1.0$ for KT-ESCFN, which the authors attribute to learning the entropy; the entropy remainder $\mathcal J$ is non-positive for all 101 test initial conditions at all noise levels (§5.2.1, Figs. 5.2–5.3). **Shown**
16. Shallow water: the shock structure is captured even at noise level 1, because the training data contain discontinuities (§5.2.2, Fig. 5.4). **Shown**
17. Shallow water: conservation holds "up O($10^{-5}$)" and $\mathcal J\le0$ over the test family (§5.2.2, Figs. 5.5–5.6). **Shown**
18. Euler: accuracy comparable to ESCFN even with heavy noise; at high noise the learned flux adds diffusion, approaching in the worst case a Lax–Friedrichs flux with constant wave speed (§5.2.3, Fig. 5.7). **Shown**
19. Euler: conservation to numerical tolerance and $\mathcal J\le0$ over the Sod-type and Shu–Osher-type families (Figs. 5.8–5.9). **Shown**
20. 2D Burgers: good accuracy at noise level 1 with only 5 trajectories of 20 steps (a GPU memory limit), but more numerical diffusion than expected, attributed to the difficulty of learning the local maximum wave speed (§5.2.4, Fig. 5.10). **Shown**
21. 2D Burgers: $\mathcal J\le0$ over three out-of-distribution families (Fig. 5.11). **Shown**
22. None of the hyperparameters of Table 4.1 was fine-tuned (§4.4.3). **Stated**
23. Convergence and stability analysis, and a DG extension, are left to future work (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (3.5): the second argument of the max is printed $a_w(u^-_{j-1/2})$, while the first is $a_w(u^+_{j+1/2})$ and the jump uses $u^\pm_{j+1/2}$; the local speed (2.15) of KT-ESCFN takes both states at $x_{j+1/2}$.
- Eq. (3.5) evaluates the Hessian at $\bar u_{j+1/2}$, while (3.6) regularises $\eta_\theta''(u_{j+1/2})$.
- §4.4.3(a): "SiLU(x) = Sigmoid(x) · ReLU(x)", chosen for "its differentiability, which is essential for computing the Jacobian". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the printed product vanishes for $x<0$ and equals $x\,\mathrm{sigmoid}(x)$ for $x>0$; its one-sided derivatives at $0$ are $0$ and $1/2$, so the printed function is not differentiable at $0$. The product $x\,\mathrm{sigmoid}(x)$, which agrees with it for $x>0$, is smooth.)*
- Alg. 3.1 (TVD-RK2): the second stage is printed $z^l=\tfrac12z^{l-1}+\tfrac12z^{(1)}$, without $+\tfrac12\Delta t\,G(z^{(1)})$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: as printed the step is $z^l=z^{l-1}+\tfrac{\Delta t}2G(z^{l-1})$, a forward-Euler half step.)*
- Eq. (2.6) calls $u^i_j(t)=\int_{I_j}u^i\,dx$ a cell average without the factor $1/\Delta x$; the slope of §2.2.3, $(u^i_x)_j=\psi(r)(u^i_{j+1}-u^i_j)$, lacks the division by $\Delta x$ that (2.16) assumes when it multiplies it by $\Delta x/2$.
- §2.2.3: the local speed is $a^{NN}_{j+1/2}$ in the flux and $\alpha^{NN}_{j+1/2}$ in (2.15).
- Refs. [21] and [22] are the same work (Kurganov & Tadmor, J. Comput. Phys. 160:241–282, 2000), cited as [22] in §1 and [21] in §2.2.3.
- §4.4.3: "each network has both input and output dimensions equal to the number of state variables", which the scalar entropy and wave-speed networks contradict; §4.4.1 places "the maximum wave speed in (3.7)", but (3.7) is the learned conservation law.
- §5.1.3: the nominal training pressure is printed $\hat p=10.32333$ and velocity $\hat u_l=2.62936$, while the Shu–Osher-type family (5.10) uses $p_l=10.33333$ and $u_l=2.629$ at $\kappa=0$; $N_{\rm train}$ is used for the number of trajectories, and the energy $E=p_0/(\gamma-1)+\dots$ uses an undefined $p_0$.
- Thm 2.6: "[[v]] denotes the jump of the entropy v" ($v$ is the entropy variable); $\hat G$ prints $\bar v_{j+1/2}D[\![v]\!]$ without the transpose.
- §5.2.1: Fig. 5.3 is said to show "the non-positivity of the learned neural entropy $\eta_\theta$"; the plotted quantity is the remainder $\mathcal J$.
- Typos: "breifly" (§4.4), "pertubed" (§5.2.2), "to recovers" (§1), "arbitraryorder" (ref. [23]).
