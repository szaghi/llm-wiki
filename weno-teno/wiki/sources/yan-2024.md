# Summary

Yan et al. (2024) build [discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) schemes
for the Kapila form of the [five-equation model](/definitions/five-equation-model.md), in 1D and
2D on uniform Cartesian meshes. The schemes are high order everywhere,
[bound preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md), and keep the Abgrall equilibrium: uniform
velocity and pressure across an isolated material interface, a case of
[pressure-equilibrium preservation](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md). The
volume-fraction equation is rewritten as conservative advection with a non-conservative source,
$\partial_t\phi_1+\nabla\cdot(\phi_1v)=\chi_1\phi_1\nabla\cdot v$, $\chi_1=K/\phi_1+1$, and the
whole system takes an [HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) flux. The source integral is split
into a cell-interior part and the interface contributions. Inside the cell, Gauss quadrature is
applied to $\partial_xv$ of a Gauss–Lobatto re-interpolation of $v$, which makes the quadrature
equal the velocity jump exactly. At each interface the velocity jump is shared between the two
cells with weights $-a^-/(a^+-a^-)$ and $a^+/(a^+-a^-)$ built from the HLL wave speeds. The
averaging discretization of earlier bound-preserving DG schemes is formally second order. The
new one stays high order and makes the updates of $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ identical in form, which
is what proves $\phi_1\le1$.

An [OEDG](/methods/oedg.md) damping step follows every Runge–Kutta stage. The authors prove it
*linearity-invariant*: it keeps every linear identity of the state, hence the Abgrall condition.
Bounds on the cell averages are proved for $\phi_1\in[0,1]$ by convex splitting and for the
partial densities and internal energy through the
[geometric quasilinearization](/methods/geometric-quasilinearization.md) of the admissible set,
under explicit CFL conditions. A two-step [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md),
also linearity-invariant, enforces the point-value hypotheses. The "symmetry" the paper relies on is
the identical form of the $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$ updates, an algebraic property distinct from the
spatial [symmetry preservation](/properties/symmetry-preservation.md) of other sources.
Accuracy tests give optimal order $N+1$ for $\mathbb P^2$ and $\mathbb P^3$, against about
second order or less for the averaging variant "BP-OEDG-AVE". Thirteen benchmarks follow:
[moving interface](/benchmarks/material-interface-advection.md),
[double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md), an air–air shock–interface
interaction (filed with the [two-phase Riemann problems](/benchmarks/gas-liquid-riemann-problems.md)),
[Leblanc](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md), two-phase [Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), a [high-Mach jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md),
[Richtmyer–Meshkov](/benchmarks/richtmyer-meshkov-instability.md), helium and R22
[shock–bubble interactions](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md) with
[Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) roll-up, and the
[triple point](/benchmarks/triple-point-problem.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the Notation map). Page-local: $\phi_k$, $\rho_k$ the volume fraction and
density of phase $k=1,2$; $K$ the Kapila coefficient; $\chi_1=K/\phi_1+1$,
$\chi_2=-K/\phi_2+1$, so $\chi_1\phi_1+\chi_2\phi_2=1$; $\mathcal A_1$, $\mathcal A_2$ the CFL
constants of the interior source and of the interface terms; $\varpi$ the endpoint weight of the
Gauss–Lobatto cell-average decomposition; $\theta$ the limiter factors.

1. The Kapila model with Wood's sound speed is hyperbolic, with eigenvalues $\{v-c,v,v,v,v+c\}$ in 1D, provided $p>0$, $\phi_k\rho_k\ge0$, $\phi_k\in[0,1]$ (§1, Eq. 1.4). **Stated** (cited)
2. The averaging discretization of the non-conservative source used in earlier bound-preserving DG schemes (e.g. Eq. 2.16) is formally second order; Zhang & Cheng (2023) state themselves that it may locally reduce DG accuracy to second order (Remarks 2.2, 3.2). **Stated** (and **Shown** in Tables 2, 3, 5, 6)
3. The new source discretization (2.8) (2D: 3.6) combines interior Gauss quadrature with an HLL-weighted split of the interface velocity jump, derived by splitting the integral into the interior and $\varepsilon$-neighbourhoods of the interfaces (§2.1.2, §3.1.2, Eqs. 2.11–2.15, 3.9–3.15). **Derived**
4. Differentiating the $(N+1)$-point Gauss–Lobatto interpolant of $v$ makes $\Delta x\sum_\nu\mathsf w_\nu(\partial_xv)^{[\nu]}_j=v^-_{j+1/2}-v^+_{j-1/2}$ hold exactly (2.10) (2D: 3.8), although $v=\rho v/\rho$ is rational in each cell (Remarks 2.1, 3.1). **Derived**
5. The OE operator $\mathcal F_{\Delta t}$ is linearity-invariant: if $\Lambda u_h\equiv c_\Lambda$ then $\Lambda\mathcal F_{\Delta t}u_h\equiv c_\Lambda$, for any $\Lambda\in\mathbb R^{q\times5}$, $c_\Lambda\in\mathbb R^{q}$ (Thm. 2.3). **Proved**
6. The OE step conserves cell averages, is $L^2$-bounded and is evolution- and scale-invariant, $\mathcal F_{\Delta t}(\sigma u_h+\mu)=\sigma\mathcal F_{\Delta t}u_h+\mu$ for $\sigma\ne0$ (Remark 2.4, Eq. 2.22). **Stated** (cited to Peng, Sun & Wu)
7. The semi-discrete DG scheme (2.9) with forward Euler preserves the Abgrall condition (2.23): uniform $v_0$, $p_0$ stay uniform (Lemma 2.5). **Proved**
8. The fully discrete OEDG scheme (2.17)–(2.18) with an SSP Runge–Kutta method preserves the Abgrall condition (Thm. 2.6; in 2D Thm. 3.4, proof omitted as similar). **Proved** (1D)
9. In 2D only the "special" Abgrall condition (3.19), a constant velocity vector, is preserved; with a constant normal and a jumping tangential velocity the scheme does not preserve it in general, even for a grid-aligned contact (Remark 3.5). **Stated**
10. If $\phi_1\in[0,1]$ at the Gauss and Gauss–Lobatto points of every cell, then $\bar\phi_1^{n+1}\in[0,1]$ under $\Delta t/\Delta x\le1/(\mathcal A_1+\mathcal A_2)$ (Thm. 2.7, Eqs. 2.31–2.32). **Proved**
11. The upper bound $\phi_1\le1$ follows because the scheme for $\phi_2=1-\phi_1$ produced by the new discretization has exactly the form of the scheme for $\phi_1$, using $\phi_1+\phi_2=1$, $\chi_1\phi_1+\chi_2\phi_2=1$ and (2.10) (proof of Thm. 2.7, Eq. 2.35; Remark 2.9). **Proved**
12. $\mathcal A_1$ is bounded by $C\lVert v_h\rVert_\infty\max\{\lVert\chi_1\rVert_\infty,\lVert\chi_2\rVert_\infty\}$ through inverse inequalities, so (2.31) is "a reasonable CFL-type condition" and the 2D condition (3.22) "standard and not overly restrictive" (Remarks 2.8, 3.7). **Derived**; the ratio $r_1=\mathcal A_1/\mathcal A_2\ll1$ is **Shown** in Figs. 9 and 21
13. The admissible set $G$ (2.29), with the nonlinear constraint $\rho e(u)>0$, equals $G_*$ (2.36), in which every constraint is linear: $u\cdot n_1\ge0$, $u\cdot n_2\ge0$, $u\cdot n_*>0$ for all $v_*\in\mathbb R$, $0\le\phi_1\le1$, with $n_*=(v_*^2/2,v_*^2/2,-v_*,1,0)^\top$ (Lemma 2.10; 2D Lemma 3.8). **Proved** (via Wu & Shu's GQL theorem)
14. For $u\in G_*$: $f(u)\cdot n>\sigma^-u\cdot n$ and $-f(u)\cdot n>-\sigma^+u\cdot n$ for $n=n_1,n_2,n_*$, with $\sigma^-=\min\{v-c,0\}$, $\sigma^+=\max\{v+c,0\}$ (Lemma 2.11; 2D Lemma 3.9). **Proved**
15. 1D bound preservation: point values in $G_*$ at all Gauss and Gauss–Lobatto points give $\bar u^{n+1}_j\in G_*$ under $\Delta t/\Delta x\le\min\{1/(\mathcal A_1+\mathcal A_2),\ \varpi/\max_j(a^+_{j+1/2}-a^-_{j+1/2})\}$ (Thm. 2.12, Eq. 2.37; proof in App. A). **Proved**
16. 2D bound preservation: under $\Delta t\le\min\{\varpi/(\mu_x+\mu_y),\ 1/(\mathcal A_1+\mathcal A_2)\}$ (3.29), with the tensor-product Gauss/Gauss–Lobatto point set (3.28), $\bar u^{n+1}_{i,j}\in G$ (Thms. 3.6, 3.10). **Proved**
17. The limiter (2.39)–(2.40) (2D: 3.33–3.34) keeps cell averages, enforces the point-value hypotheses, keeps high-order accuracy, and is linearity-invariant (Thm. 2.13, proof omitted), so it preserves the Abgrall condition (Thm. 2.14). **Proved** (Thm. 2.14); accuracy **Stated** (cited)
18. The optimal cell-average decompositions of Cui, Ding & Wu would improve the 2D bound-preserving CFL condition (Remark 3.11). **Stated**
19. All tests use CFL number $1/(2N+1)$ for $\mathbb P^N$, an $(N+1)$th-order explicit Runge–Kutta method for accuracy tests and SSP-RK3 for discontinuous problems (§4). **Stated**
20. 1D accuracy, constant velocity, $t=1$, up to 1280 cells: $\mathbb P^2$ reaches $l^1$ order 3.24 at the finest level ($l^1$ error $4.21\times10^{-10}$), $\mathbb P^3$ order 4.75 ($2.81\times10^{-13}$) (Table 1). **Shown**
21. 1D accuracy with a non-vanishing source ($v=1+0.4\cos\pi x$, $t=0.05$): $\mathbb P^2$-AVE drops to $l^1$ orders 1.75, 1.69, 1.84 while $\mathbb P^2$-BP-OEDG keeps 3.77, 3.39, 3.25; $\mathbb P^3$-AVE gives 1.94, 1.96, 1.95 ($l^\infty$ down to 0.94), $\mathbb P^3$-BP-OEDG 4.50, 4.51, 4.40 (Tables 2–3). **Shown**
22. 2D accuracy with non-vanishing $\nabla\cdot v$ ($t=0.005$): $\mathbb P^3$-AVE $l^1$ orders 0.91, $-0.08$, 1.42 against 3.56, 4.12, 4.07 for $\mathbb P^3$-BP-OEDG; $\mathbb P^2$-AVE 1.88, 1.96 against 3.35, 3.42 (Tables 5–6). **Shown**
23. Moving interface: the velocity and pressure errors of $\mathbb P^2$/$\mathbb P^3$ BP-OEDG on 200 cells at $t=1$ stay within $\pm1.5\times10^{-12}$ (Ex. 4.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
24. Without the limiter, the double-rarefaction (Ex. 4.3) and jet (Ex. 4.9) runs can stop on negative density or pressure (§4). **Stated**
25. Leblanc: BP-OEDG resolves the solution better than BP-OEDG-AVE on 400 cells at $t=6$ (Ex. 4.5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
26. The bound-preserving CFL bounds are not restrictive: $r_1\ll1$, and the ratio $r_2$ of the two bounds is close to 1 in 1D and close to or above 1 in 2D (Figs. 9, 21). **Shown**
27. The OEDG method is said to reveal "for the first time" the close relationship between OE damping, modal filtering and moment limiting; optimal order $N+1$ for advection with the upwind flux is proved by Peng, Sun & Wu (§1). **Stated** (cited)
28. The damping term of the oscillation-free DG of Lu, Liu & Shu is stiff near strong discontinuities and needs restricted explicit steps or exponential Runge–Kutta methods; OEDG avoids this (§1). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* The last inequality of the proof of Lemma 2.11,
$\big(v-\frac{p}{\rho\sqrt{2e}}\big)u\cdot n_*>\sigma^-u\cdot n_*$, needs $\frac{p}{\rho\sqrt{2e}}<c$
for the Wood speed, a step not shown. It holds: with $\rho e=p/(\gamma-1)$,
$\big(\frac{p}{\rho\sqrt{2e}}\big)^2=\frac{(\gamma-1)p}{2\rho}$, while Wood's formula with
$\rho_kc_k^2=\gamma_kp$ gives $c^2=p/(\rho\sum_k\phi_k/\gamma_k)$; since
$\sum_k\phi_k/\gamma_k<\sum_k\phi_k/(\gamma_k-1)=1/(\gamma-1)$, the ratio of the two is below $\tfrac12$.

# Printed slips

- Ex. 4.10 (Richtmyer–Meshkov): the post-shock air state $(\rho,v_1,p)=(1.411,-0.39,1.628)$ for a Mach 1.24 shock into air at $\rho=1$, $p=1$, $\gamma=1.4$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* the Rankine–Hugoniot relations give $\rho=1.4112$ and $p=1.6272$, matching the printed 1.411 and 1.628 to rounding, but a post-shock speed $\frac{2c}{\gamma+1}(M-1/M)=0.4275$, not 0.39.
- Ex. 4.11 (helium bubble): post-shock air $(1.92691,-113.5,1.5698\times10^5)$ for Mach 1.22 into air at $\rho=1.4$, $p=10^5$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* density and pressure match the Rankine–Hugoniot values, the post-shock speed is 105.5; 113.5 is the speed for air at $\rho=1.225$, $p=101\,325$ (113.52), the pre-shock state of Ex. 4.12.
- Ex. 4.9, "Mach 200 gas jet": the jet is fluid 2 ($\rho_2=100$, $\gamma_2=1.67$, $p=1/1.4$, $v_1=200$). *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* the jet gas has sound speed $\sqrt{1.67\cdot(1/1.4)/100}=0.109$, so $v_1=200$ is Mach 1831 relative to the jet and Mach 200 relative to the ambient sound speed ($c=1$); the label does not say which.
- Ex. 4.12: "$\gamma_1=1.249$ and $\gamma_2=1.4$ for air and R22 bubble, respectively", while in the printed initial data fluid 1 is R22 ($\rho_1=3.863$, $\phi_1=1-10^{-6}$ inside the bubble) and fluid 2 air; the labels are swapped.
- p. 2586, proof of Thm. 3.10: the second line of the $\Xi_4\cdot n$ estimate has the prefactor $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}$ where the first line and the definition of $\Xi_4$ have $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}$.
- p. 2587, Eq. (3.34): the floor is written $\epsilon_{\rho e}$ but defined in the same step as $\epsilon_3$ (1D Eq. 2.40 uses $\epsilon_3$). Eqs. (2.39)/(3.33): $\theta_{\min}=\min\{\theta_{z_1},\dots\}$ with the factor defined as $\theta_z$.
- Thm. 2.12 says "the scheme (2.30) preserves $\bar U^{n+1}_j\in\mathcal G_*$", but (2.30) is the volume-fraction equation only; the proof works from (2.9)/(A.1).
- §3.1: $N_K=K(K+3)/2$ is called "the total number of degrees of freedom", with the basis index running $k=0,\dots,N_K$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* the dimension of 2D $\mathbb P^K$ is $(K+1)(K+2)/2=N_K+1$, so $N_K$ is the highest index.
