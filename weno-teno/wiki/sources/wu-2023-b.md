# Summary

The paper proposes [geometric quasilinearization (GQL)](/methods/geometric-quasilinearization.md),
a framework for proving and designing [bound-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md)
schemes when the admissible set $G=\{u:g_i(u)\succ0\}$ is cut out by
nonlinear, possibly implicit, constraints: positive pressure, subluminal
velocity, positive-definite pressure tensors, a minimum entropy principle.
Because a convex $G$ is the intersection of its supporting halfspaces, each
nonlinear constraint can be replaced by a family of constraints affine in $u$,
$\varphi_i(u;\zeta_{i*})\succ0$ for all free auxiliary variables
$\zeta_{i*}$ independent of $u$; the auxiliary variables parametrize the
boundary points $u_*$ and normals $n_*$. Convexity is the minimal
assumption; the $g_i$ need not be concave.

The theory has a constructive criterion (Thm 3.2), a theorem for open or
closed $G$ with differentiable boundary (Thm 3.9), a general theorem for
piecewise-$C^1$ boundaries proved through KKT conditions (Thm 3.10), and three
construction methods (gradient, cross-product, constructive). Explicit GQL
representations are derived for Euler and Navier–Stokes (with and without the
minimum entropy principle), the M1 radiation model, special relativistic
hydrodynamics, the ten-moment Gaussian closure, ideal MHD and relativistic MHD.
Linearity then gives short bound-preservation proofs of first-order
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) and gas-kinetic schemes,
of a Navier–Stokes scheme without convex splitting, and of the ten-moment
Lax–Friedrichs scheme.

For multicomponent ideal MHD, GQL ties bound preservation to a discrete
[divergence-free](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) condition.
High-order finite-volume and [discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md)
schemes that discretize the Godunov-symmetrized MHD system with a dedicated
source term, combined with a locally divergence-free basis and a
[Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md), are
provably bound-preserving under a CFL condition with the Gauss–Lobatto weight
$\varpi$; [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stepping keeps the
property. Two numerical tests (a two-species MHD blast and a two-species MHD
[jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md)) use third-order DG. The
introduction surveys the weak-bound-preserving-plus-limiter framework, flux
limiters and [convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md) as the existing
alternatives.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): vectors are not bold, the state
dimension $N$ is $m$, $\Gamma$ is $\gamma$, the Lorentz factor $\gamma$ is
$W$, the DG degree $K$ is $N$, $\sigma\alpha_n$ is the CFL number $\nu$, the
Gauss–Lobatto weight $\widehat\omega_1$ is $\varpi$, the free auxiliary
variables $\theta_{i*}$ are $\zeta_{i*}$.

1. For convex $G$, every nonlinear constraint has an equivalent linear representation obtained by adding $u$-independent free auxiliary variables; e.g. $u_2-u_1^2>0\iff u_2-2u_1\zeta_*+\zeta_*^2>0$ for all $\zeta_*\in\mathbb R$ (§1, Eqs. (1.8)–(1.9), Ex. 3.5). **Proved**
2. Constructive criterion: if each $\varphi_i$ is affine in $u$ and $\min_{\zeta_{i*}\in\Theta_i}\varphi_i(u;\zeta_{i*})=\lambda_i(u)g_i(u)$ with $\lambda_i>0$ on the region of the linear constraints, then the GQL set equals $G$ (Thm 3.2). **Stated** (said to follow "immediately" from Def. 3.1)
3. A GQL representation exists only if $G$ is convex, since an intersection of halfspaces is convex; convexity with nonempty interior is the minimal assumption, and the $g_i$ need not be concave (§3, Assumption 3.4). **Derived**
4. If $G$ is convex, open or closed, with differentiable boundary, then $G=\{u:(u-u_*)\cdot n_*\succ0\ \forall u_*\in\partial G\}$ with $n_*$ the inward normal (Thm 3.9, proof in App. A). **Proved**
5. For convex $G$ with piecewise-$C^1$ boundary pieces $\mathcal S_i=\partial G\cap\partial G_i$ and condition (3.7), $G=\{u:(u-u_*)\cdot n_{i*}\succ0\ \forall u_*\in\mathcal S_i,\ 1\le i\le I\}$, with $>$ for strict and $\ge$ for non-strict constraints; $G$ need be neither open nor closed (Thm 3.10, proof by KKT conditions and Slater's condition). **Proved**
6. At non-smooth junction points the GQL representation needs at most $m$ supporting halfspaces, unlike the intersection of all supporting halfspaces (Remark 3.12). **Stated**
7. Gradient method: if $\nabla g_i(u_*)\ne0$ on $\mathcal S_i$, then $\varphi_i(u;u_*)=(u-u_*)\cdot\nabla g_i(u_*)$ gives the GQL representation (Thm 4.1). **Derived** (from Thm 3.10)
8. Cross-product method: with a $C^1$ parametrization of $\mathcal S_i$, the normal is the $(m-1)$-ary cross product of the tangent vectors; this handles implicit $g_i$ such as $p(u)$ in relativistic hydrodynamics and MHD (Thm 4.2). **Derived**
9. Euler and Navier–Stokes, $\rho>0$, $\rho e>0$: $G=\{\rho>0,\ E-\rho v\,v_*+\tfrac12\rho v_*^2>0\ \forall v_*\in\mathbb R\}$, shown by all three methods; $\varphi=\tfrac12\rho(v-v_*)^2+\rho e$ is the total energy in a frame moving at $v_*$ (Thm 4.4, Remark 4.6). **Proved**
10. Euler with Tadmor's minimum entropy principle $p\rho^{-\gamma}\ge\exp(s_{\min})$: GQL with two auxiliary variables $(\rho_*,v_*)$ and $\tilde\varphi=u\cdot n_*+\exp(s_{\min})\rho_*^{\gamma}$ (Thm 4.7). **Proved**
11. M1 radiative transfer: $E_r-\mathcal F_r\cdot\zeta_*\ge0$ for all unit vectors $\zeta_*$ (Thm 4.8); 1D relativistic hydrodynamics: $E-m v_*-D\sqrt{1-v_*^2}>0$ for all $v_*\in(-1,1)$ (Thm 4.9); relativistic hydrodynamics with the entropy principle (Thm 4.10). **Proved**
12. Ten-moment Gaussian closure: positive definiteness of $E-m\otimes m/(2\rho)$ ($E$ the energy tensor) is equivalent to $z^\top(E-m\otimes v_*+\tfrac12\rho\,v_*\otimes v_*)z>0$ for all $v_*\in\mathbb R^2$, $z\ne0$ (Thm 4.11). **Proved**
13. Ideal MHD: $G=\{\rho>0,\ u\cdot n_*+\tfrac12\lvert B_*\rvert^2>0\ \forall v_*,B_*\in\mathbb R^3\}$, recovering by the gradient method the representation first found algebraically by Wu (2018) (Thm 4.12). **Proved**
14. Relativistic MHD (positive $D$, positive $p$, $\lvert v\rvert<1$) has an explicit linear GQL representation in $(v_*,B_*)$, Eqs. (4.19)–(4.21), although $p(u)$ and $v(u)$ are implicit (Thm 4.13). **Proved** (some steps "can be verified")
15. The first-order finite-volume scheme with the Lax–Friedrichs flux for 1D Euler is bound-preserving under $\nu=a\Delta t/\Delta x\le1$, $a=\max_i(\lvert v\rvert+c)$ (§5.1, Ex. 1.1). **Proved**
16. The first-order gas-kinetic (kinetic flux-vector splitting) scheme is bound-preserving under $\nu\le1$, with a proof much shorter than the traditional one of Tang & Xu because $v_*$ commutes with the velocity integrals (§5.1, Ex. 1.2, Remark 5.2). **Proved**
17. The first-order Navier–Stokes scheme (5.9) (bound-preserving Euler flux plus central diffusion) is bound-preserving under $a\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^2}\frac{2}{\bar\rho_i^n\mathrm{Re}}\max\{\eta_v,\gamma/\mathrm{Pr}\}\le1$ (5.11), without the convex decomposition of convection and diffusion that the paper says gives stricter time-step limits (§5.2, Remark 5.3). **Proved**
18. The 2D ten-moment Lax–Friedrichs scheme is bound-preserving under $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}a_1+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}a_2<1$, $a_\ell=\max(\lvert v_\ell\rvert+\sqrt{p_{\ell\ell}/\rho})$, by AM–GM on the GQL form (§5.3, Eqs. (5.16)–(5.18)). **Proved**
19. Multicomponent MHD: the first-order 2D Lax–Friedrichs scheme satisfies $\varphi(\bar u^{n+1}_{ij})>-\Delta t\,(v_*\cdot B_*)\,\mathrm{div}_{ij}\bar B$ under $\nu=\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}a_1+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}a_2\le1$, so it is bound-preserving if a discrete divergence vanishes (Thm 6.1). **Proved**
20. High-order ($N\ge1$) finite-volume/DG cell-average schemes with Gauss-quadrature Lax–Friedrichs fluxes satisfy the analogous estimate (6.15) under $\nu\le\varpi$ if the point values at the mixed Gauss/Gauss–Lobatto points lie in $G$; bound preservation then requires the discrete divergence-free condition (6.16) (Thm 6.2). **Stated** (proof "omitted", by analogy with Wu 2018)
21. Standard high-order schemes do not preserve the discrete divergence-free condition; globally divergence-free techniques are incompatible with the local scaling limiter, and locally divergence-free DG gives only the interior part $\mathrm{div}^-_{ij}B=0$ (Remark 6.3). **Stated**
22. Adding the discretized Godunov-symmetrization source $\widehat S_{ij}$ (6.22), built on the normal-$B$ jumps, gives provable bound preservation under $(1+\varepsilon_B)\nu\le\varpi$, provided $\mathrm{div}^-_{ij}B=0$ and the point values satisfy (6.12); $\varepsilon_B$ depends on the normal-$B$ jumps (Thm 6.4). **Proved**
23. Bound preservation is retained with SSP Runge–Kutta stepping, a convex combination of forward-Euler steps (§6.2, after Thm 6.4). **Stated**
24. The scaling limiter (App. B) enforces (6.12), keeps the cell average and "does not lose the high-order accuracy"; the concave $E-\frac12(\lvert\rho v\rvert^2/\rho+\lvert B\rvert^2)$ replaces the non-concave pressure (App. B). **Stated** (accuracy cited)
25. In the two-component MHD jet, removing any one of the scaling limiter, the locally divergence-free basis or the source term (6.22) makes the method "fail quickly due to nonphysical numerical solutions", even with the WENO limiter (§7, Ex. 7.2). **Shown** (text only, no failure figure)
26. The bound-preserving DG method stays robust on the blast problem ($400\times400$) and keeps the admissible set (6.4) throughout (§7, Ex. 7.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- The unit ball is defined as open after (1.10), "$\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\lVert x\rVert<1\}$", and as closed in Thm 4.13 (p. 16), "$\lVert x\rVert\le1$"; the closed ball admits $\lVert v_*\rVert=1$, where the Lorentz factor of the parametrization is undefined.
- Thm 4.10, (4.14): "$\widetilde G_*=\{u=(D,m,E)^\top:\ \rho>0,\dots\}$"; the linear constraint is $D>0$ as in (2.9), $\rho$ is not a component of $u$.
- Thm 4.10: (4.14) ranges over $v_*\in(-1,1)$, (4.15) prints "$\forall v_*\in\mathbb R$".
- Thm 4.10: the scaling factor is printed with $(1-v_*)^{5/2}$, while every other factor of the parametrization is in $1-v_*^2$ (not verified by recomputation).
- After (5.6): "with $\rho$ being the fluid velocity, $v$ being the fluid velocity"; $\rho$ is the density.
- (5.5): the third component is printed $\frac w2(w^2+\xi^2)$ with a vector $\xi$; $\lVert\xi\rVert^2$ is meant, as in (5.6).
- §5.1, Ex. 1.2: the bounds use "$a(u)$" while the conclusion uses $\alpha(\bar u^n_j)$ for the same spectral radius.
- (6.8): "$\alpha_{1,n}\ge\max_{i,j,\mu}$" has the index $\mu$ where $q$ is meant (cf. $\alpha_{2,n}$).
- (6.21) prints "$\varphi(u;\,;v_*,B_*)$" and the bound "$\le\lvert b\rvert\rho^{1/2}\varphi$"; the definition of $\beta_1$ in Thm 6.4 also carries $(\rho^{\pm,q})^{1/2}$. The bound that holds has the exponent $-1/2$: $(v-v_*)\cdot(B-B_*)\le\rho^{-1/2}\big(\tfrac12\rho\lvert v-v_*\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert B-B_*\rvert^2\big)\le\rho^{-1/2}\varphi$. *(compiler check, not in the sources: Cauchy–Schwarz and AM–GM, with $\varphi=u\cdot n_*+\tfrac12\lvert B_*\rvert^2=\rho e+\tfrac12\rho\lvert v-v_*\rvert^2+\tfrac12\lvert B-B_*\rvert^2$ expanded from Thm 4.12's $n_*$.)* Whether the version of record corrects it is not known.
- (2.7) writes $p(U)$, $v(U)$ with capital $U$; the state is $u$ throughout.
- App. B: $\theta_1=(\bar\rho-\epsilon_1)/(\bar\rho-\min\rho)$ is printed without the cap $\min\{1,\cdot\}$ that Cui et al. (2024, Rem. 8.1) print, and $\theta_2$ has no cap and no rule for an empty set of violating points.
- Ex. 7.2: the domain is stated as $[-0.5,0.5]\times[0,1.5]$, the computation uses "200×600 uniform cells in $[0,0.5]\times[0,1.5]$" with no stated symmetry condition, and Fig. 6 shows the full width.
- Fig. 5 caption: "from let to right". Ref. [5] prints the author list "S. Boyd, S. P. Boyd, and L. Vandenberghe".
