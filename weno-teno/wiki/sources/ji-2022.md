# Summary

The paper carries [targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md) from Cartesian finite
differences to cell-centred finite volumes on triangular, mixed and tetrahedral meshes. The
schemes are implemented in the open-source code [UCNS3D](/software/ucns3d.md). The candidate
set has the layout of [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md): one large central-biased stencil carries a
polynomial of the target order (third to sixth), and several small directional stencils carry
quadratic (third-order) polynomials. Every polynomial is a constrained least-squares fit of
cell averages in a reference space, the stencils holding twice as many cells as the polynomial
has coefficients. The second-order case of such fits is on the page on
[gradient reconstruction on unstructured meshes](/methods/unstructured-gradient-reconstruction.md).

The selection departs from the Cartesian recipe. The measure is $\gamma_k=(\beta_k+\epsilon)^{-6}$
with $\epsilon=10^{-12}$ and no $\tau$ term, built on reference-space
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md), and it is normalised and cut off
at $C_T=10^{-6}$. If the large stencil survives, the reconstruction is its polynomial, i.e. the
linear high-order scheme. Otherwise the cut-off is repeated among the small stencils only,
and the reconstruction is the unweighted mean of the survivors. No linear weights are needed,
unlike [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md)-type unstructured WENO or CWENO. Fluxes are
[HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) in the rotated face frame; time stepping is
[SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) at CFL 0.4.

The evidence is numerical only. In smooth linear advection the TENO errors are identical to
the linear-scheme errors. The other tests are the [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md),
[solid-body rotation](/benchmarks/solid-body-rotation.md),
[shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[2D explosions](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md),
[Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and a 3D spherical explosion.
At equal order TENO is less dissipative than WENO, which is in turn less dissipative than
CWENO. Unstructured WENO5/6 fail on double Mach reflection without an extra limiter. TENO and
CWENO of equal order cost the same.

# Key claims

Registry notation. Here the large stencil is written $S_{\mathrm L}$ with polynomial
$P_{\mathrm L}$, and the $n_s$ small stencils $S_0,\dots,S_{n_s-1}$ (the source's $S_K$ and
$K$; see the Notation map).

1. TENO is extended to unstructured meshes "for the first time", "as far as the authors' knowledge" (§1, §6). **Stated**
2. The candidates are one large central-biased stencil $S_{\mathrm L}$ with the target-order polynomial and $n_s$ small directional stencils with third-order polynomials. For TENO6, $n_s=3$ on triangles and $n_s=4$ on the mixed mesh of Fig. 1 (§3.1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
3. The scale-separation measure is $\gamma_k=1/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^6$, $\epsilon=10^{-12}$, for every candidate (Eq. 23). A WENO-Z-type form "can be applied" but is not used, to save cost (§3.2). **Stated**
4. Selection in two steps with the same $C_T=10^{-6}$ (Eqs. 24–28). If $S_{\mathrm L}$ is smooth ($\delta_{\mathrm L}=1$), the reconstruction is exactly $P_{\mathrm L}$. Otherwise $\chi_k$ and $\delta_k$ are recomputed among the small stencils, and the reconstruction is $\sum_{k<n_s}\omega_kP_k$ with $\omega_k=\delta_k/\sum_{l<n_s}\delta_l$ (§3.3). **Stated** (definition)
5. $C_T$ "is rather flexible between $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-7}$", citing Fu et al. 2016 (§3.3). **Stated**
6. Neither WENO linear weights $d_k$ nor the CWENO parameter $d'_{\mathrm L}$ has to be specified (§3.4). **Stated** (holds by construction)
7. In smooth regions the nonlinear TENO reproduces the linear scheme exactly: TENO3–TENO6 give errors identical to the linear schemes for $h=1/13$ to $1/160$ ($374$ to $58{,}330$ triangles) (Tables 1–4, §5.1). **Shown**
8. Observed orders at the finest pair ($h=1/80\to1/160$), $L_\infty$/$L_2$: 2.92/3.01 (third order), 3.61/3.83 (fourth), 4.93/5.01 (fifth), 5.97/5.90 (sixth) (Tables 1–4). The fourth-order scheme stays below 4 at the two finest pairs (3.75 and 3.61 in $L_\infty$), yet the text says that "the desired accuracy order is restored for all the considered TENO schemes". **Shown**
9. Isentropic vortex: "the desired convergence order is achieved" for TENO3–6 in $L_\infty$ (Fig. 2; numbers only in the figure). **Shown**
10. Solid-body rotation ($h=1/80$, 14,860 triangles): TENO4–6 are much less dissipative than TENO3 on the slotted cylinder, and TENO5 overshoots less than WENO5 and dissipates less than CWENO5 (Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
11. Shock tubes on a Cartesian mesh ($h=1/100$, conservative-variable reconstruction): no spurious oscillations, slight overshoots near the contact of the Lax tube. On 1967 uniform triangles the Sod and Lax tubes keep their one-dimensional [symmetry](/properties/symmetry-preservation.md) (Figs. 5–7). **Shown**
12. Shu–Osher on 18,282 triangles ($h\approx1/20$): higher order resolves the entropy waves better, and TENO5/6 beat CWENO5/6 of the same order (Figs. 8–10). **Shown**
13. CWENO with larger $d'_{\mathrm L}$ ($10^3$, $10^7$, $10^{15}$) resolves the Shu–Osher waves better but loses robustness, "as evidenced by additional numerical experiments" (not shown); double Mach reflection "typically fails with too large $d'_K$". $d'_{\mathrm L}=10^4$ is adopted for all orders (§2.4, §5.5, Fig. 11). **Shown** (Fig. 11) / **Stated** (the failures)
14. Explosion EP01 (Sod-type): TENO3–6, WENO5 and CWENO5 all stay monotone. EP02: TENO and CWENO5 are alike, while WENO5 oscillates near the discontinuities (Figs. 12–17). **Shown**
15. Kelvin–Helmholtz ($h\approx1/500$): TENO3 is much more dissipative than higher-order TENO, and TENO5/6 "perform much better than the CWENO5 and CWENO6 schemes in terms of preserving the kinetic energy" (Figs. 18–20). **Shown** (density only) / **Stated** (kinetic energy)
16. Double Mach reflection on 151,850, 369,794 ($h\approx1/200$) and 752,708 ($h\approx1/280$) triangles: higher-order TENO resolves more small scales, without spurious oscillations at the discontinuities (Figs. 21–23). **Shown**
17. On double Mach reflection at equal order, TENO is the least dissipative, then WENO, then CWENO (Figs. 24–25). **Shown**
18. WENO5 and WENO6 with equal-order candidates "typically fail" on double Mach reflection without the extended-bounds limiter of Tsoutsanis (2018): both fail at $h\approx1/200$, WENO6 at $h\approx1/280$. TENO and CWENO are robust at every resolution (§5.8). **Shown**
19. TENO5 resolves the Mach-stem roll-up better than unstructured [WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md) at twice the resolution per direction, and at $h\approx1/200$ is comparable to [multi-resolution WENO5](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) at $h\approx1/320$ (§5.8). **Stated** (by comparison with other papers' figures)
20. 3D spherical explosion on tetrahedra ($h\approx1/30$): no obvious artefacts, and TENO3–6 "agree with the analytical solutions well" (Fig. 26). **Shown**
21. Cost: the normalised reconstruction time grows "by roughly 65%" from TENO3 to TENO6 (DMR $1.36\times10^{-6}\to2.24\times10^{-6}$, KHI $1.32\times10^{-6}\to2.20\times10^{-6}$ s per iteration per element). TENO and CWENO of equal order cost about the same (DMR: TENO5 $1.96\times10^{-6}$ against CWENO5 $1.98\times10^{-6}$; TENO6 $2.24\times10^{-6}$ against CWENO6 $2.20\times10^{-6}$). Intel Xeon E5-2690, Intel Fortran 19, 8 MPI ranks × 7 OpenMP threads (Table 7). **Shown** (the ratios $2.24/1.36=1.65$ and $2.20/1.32=1.67$ agree; *compiler check, not in the sources*)
22. CWENO is much cheaper than equal-order WENO, because every WENO candidate carries the target order, and its compact small stencils make a smooth candidate near shocks more likely (§2.4). **Stated**
23. The stencil arrangement gives a narrower full stencil than equal-order unstructured WENO (§6). **Stated**
24. On unstructured meshes the optimal linear weights of a high-order WENO combination can be negative or fail to exist; hence the WENO comparator uses target-order candidates with free weights (§1, §2.3). **Stated** (cited)
25. Reconstruction in characteristic variables suppresses oscillations better than in primitive or conservative variables, as is "generally believed" (§2.3). The reconstructed variables are stated only for the shock tubes (conservative). **Stated**
26. HLL is "free from Carbuncle problem and the necessity of entropy fix", and the choice of solver "does not affect our conclusion" (§5). **Stated**
27. One source of WENO [dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) is the smooth weighting of candidates, which starts the nonlinear adaptation already at low wavenumbers. TENO avoids it by using a stencil with its full weight or discarding it (§3.4, §6). **Stated**

*Compiler check, not in the sources.* The second step always keeps at least one small
stencil: the largest of the $n_s$ normalised measures is at least $1/n_s>C_T$, so the equal
weights in claim 4 are always defined.

Near discontinuities the [ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) rests on the explicit
exclusion of non-smooth stencils, not on any linear-weight structure (§3.3–§3.4).

# Printed slips

- Table 6 (shock-tube data) has a column shift. The rows read ST01 "1.0 0.0 1.0 1.0 0.125 0.0", ST02 "0.445 0.698 3.528 3.528 0.5 0.0" and ST03 "1.0 0.0 1000 1000 1.0 0.0" under $\rho_L,u_L,p_L,\rho_R,u_R,p_R$: in each row the printed $\rho_R$ repeats $p_L$ and $p_R=0$. The intended right states cannot be recovered from the table.
- Eq. (6) counts $N_k=\frac1{n_d!}\prod_{m=1}^{n_d}(r+m)$ coefficients, which includes the constant term ($N_k=3$ for $r=1$, $n_d=2$). Eq. (7) then writes $\bar U_0+\sum_{l=1}^{N_k}a_l\psi_l$, with $N_k$ non-constant terms. The two counts differ by one.
- §5.10: "For DMR, we measure the total computing time at t = 2", while the DMR end time is $t=0.2$ (§5.8).
- §5.4–§5.5: the 1D-strip domains are printed $[0,0.2]\times[0,1]$ and $[0,2]\times[0,10]$, with $x$ as the second factor, while the data are given in $x\in[0,1]$ and $x\in[0,10]$ and the DMR domain $[0,4]\times[0,1]$ puts $x$ first.
- Eq. (33) gives the middle HLL state for "$S_L\le0$", without the "$\le S_R$" of Eq. (34).
- §3.3: "the measured smoothness indicator $\chi_k$ is renormalized as $\chi_k=\gamma_k/\sum\gamma_k$" (the quantity renormalised is $\gamma_k$), and step (ii) writes "the largest candidate stencil $S_k$" for $S_K$.
- The weights are written $w_k$ in Eqs. (15) and (19) and $\omega_k$ in Eqs. (16) and (20).
- §5.2: "the gas constant γ = 1.4" ($\gamma$ is the ratio of specific heats).
- §5.7 claims better kinetic-energy preservation, but Fig. 20 shows density profiles only.
- §5.9 compares the 3D explosion with "the analytical solutions", but gives no analytical solution or its source.
- The Fig. 24 caption says that WENO5 and WENO6 "fail at this resolution without the additional limiter", yet shows WENO5/6 results without saying whether the limiter was used for them.
- Ref. [42] has no journal name, and ref. [14] has no year.
- Typos: "Riemman" (§4.2, §5), "shcmes" (§5.5), "typologies" for topologies (captions of Figs. 6–7).
