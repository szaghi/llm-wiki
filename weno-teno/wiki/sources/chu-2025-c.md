# Summary

Chu et al. (2025c) revise the
[low-dissipation central-upwind (LDCU) schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-central-upwind-scheme.md)
of Kurganov & Xin (2023), which modify the
[central-upwind](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) scheme of Kurganov & Lin. The
authors state that the LDCU schemes resolve contact waves and 2D wave interactions
much more sharply than central-upwind schemes, but are sometimes more oscillatory,
especially near the boundaries of the computational domain.

The change is in the projection step of the fully discrete derivation. The original
LDCU scheme approximates the solution in the Riemann fan
$[x_{i+1/2}+a^-_{i+1/2}\Delta t,\,x_{i+1/2}+a^+_{i+1/2}\Delta t]$ by a piecewise
constant function whose jump sits at $x_{i+1/2}$. The new scheme moves the jump to
$x_{i+1/2}+\tilde v_{i+1/2}\Delta t$, with $\tilde v_{i+1/2}\in(a^-,a^+)$ an extra
degree of freedom. For the Euler equations, continuity of velocity and pressure across
the jump forces the velocity to be constant in the fan, and $\tilde v$ is set to that
intermediate velocity: the jump moves with the contact. The semi-discrete limit is the
central-upwind flux plus a modified built-in anti-diffusion term, which weights the
density differences by $v^{\mathrm{HLL}}-a^-$ and $a^+-v^{\mathrm{HLL}}$ and carries
an upwind factor switched on the sign of $v^{\mathrm{HLL}}$. The 2D scheme is built
dimension by dimension, with anti-diffusion also on the transverse momentum and a
matching energy term.

All tests are second order: generalized-minmod piecewise-linear reconstruction
($\theta=1.3$), [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) at CFL 0.475. On a
shock–entropy wave of [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) type the original
scheme is inaccurate near the left boundary (which uses zero-gradient ghost cells; the
paper does not treat [boundary closures](/methods/shock-capturing-boundary-closure.md)),
the new one is not. On a stationary contact with a shock and a rarefaction the
original scheme's oscillations do not decay with refinement. On
[2D Riemann](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) Configuration 3 and the
[explosion and implosion problems](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md) the
new scheme is less oscillatory at comparable resolution. Evidence is visual: no
accuracy test and no theorem.

# Key claims

Registry notation: cells $I_i$ (source $C_j$), averages $\bar u_i$, one-sided values
$u^\pm_{i+1/2}$, speeds $a^\pm_{i+1/2}$, velocity $v$ (source $u$), HLL state
$u^{\mathrm{HLL}}_{i+1/2}$ (source $U^*$, not named HLL in the paper), with
$v^{\mathrm{HLL}}=(\rho v)^{\mathrm{HLL}}/\rho^{\mathrm{HLL}}$. Page-local:
$\bar u^{\mathrm{int}}_{i+1/2}$ the intermediate average over the Riemann fan,
$\bar u^{\mathrm{int},L}$, $\bar u^{\mathrm{int},R}$ the subcell states left and right
of the jump, $\tilde v_{i+1/2}$ the jump speed, $v^{\mathrm{int}}$ the intermediate
velocity, $\mathsf q_{i+1/2}$ the built-in anti-diffusion and $\alpha^*$ its upwind
factor.

1. The LDCU schemes of Kurganov & Xin achieve much higher resolution of contact waves and 2D wave-interaction structures than central-upwind schemes but sometimes produce more oscillatory results, especially near the domain boundaries (Abstract, §1). **Stated**
2. The modification is "very simple, yet systematic", being based on a more accurate projection of the evolved solution onto the original mesh (§1). **Stated**
3. The original conservation requirement $a^+\bar u^{\mathrm{int},R}-a^-\bar u^{\mathrm{int},L}=(a^+-a^-)\bar u^{\mathrm{int}}$ (Eq. (2.3)) leaves $m$ degrees of freedom, and one more can be introduced to improve the projection (§2, p. 3). **Derived**
4. The new projection puts the jump at $x_{i+1/2}+\tilde v_{i+1/2}\Delta t$, $\tilde v_{i+1/2}\in(a^-,a^+)$ (Eq. (2.4)); conservation becomes $(a^+-\tilde v)\bar u^{\mathrm{int},R}+(\tilde v-a^-)\bar u^{\mathrm{int},L}=(a^+-a^-)\bar u^{\mathrm{int}}$ (Eq. (2.5)), which reduces to (2.3) for $\tilde v=0$ (§2.1). **Derived**
5. For 1D Euler, continuity of $v$ and $p$ (Eq. (2.6)) with (2.5) on $\rho$, $\rho v$, $E$ (Eq. (2.7)) gives $v^{\mathrm{int},L}=v^{\mathrm{int}}=v^{\mathrm{int},R}$ (Eq. (2.8)), which motivates $\tilde v_{i+1/2}=v^{\mathrm{int}}_{i+1/2}$ (§2.2). **Derived**
6. The density jump is made as large as possible without new extrema: $\delta_{i+1/2}=\mathrm{minmod}(S^-,S^+)$ with $S^-=(v^{\mathrm{int}}-a^-)(\bar\rho^{\mathrm{int}}-\rho^{\mathrm{int}}_\ell)$, $S^+=(a^+-v^{\mathrm{int}})(\rho^{\mathrm{int}}_r-\bar\rho^{\mathrm{int}})$, and $\bar\rho^{\mathrm{int},L/R}=\bar\rho^{\mathrm{int}}+\delta/(a^\mp-v^{\mathrm{int}})$ (Eq. (2.9)). **Derived**
7. The fully discrete new LDCU scheme (Eqs. (2.11)–(2.12)) adds anti-diffusive corrections $\alpha^{\mathrm{int}}_{i\mp1/2}\delta_{i\mp1/2}(1,v^{\mathrm{int}},\tfrac12(v^{\mathrm{int}})^2)^\top$, with $\alpha^{\mathrm{int}}=a^+/(a^+-v^{\mathrm{int}})$ if $v^{\mathrm{int}}<0$ and $a^-/(a^--v^{\mathrm{int}})$ otherwise (§2.2.1). **Derived**
8. The semi-discrete limit is $\hat f_{i+1/2}=\frac{a^+f(u^-)-a^-f(u^+)}{a^+-a^-}+\frac{a^+a^-}{a^+-a^-}(u^+-u^-)+\mathsf q_{i+1/2}$ with $\mathsf q_{i+1/2}=\alpha^*\,\mathrm{minmod}\big((v^{\mathrm{HLL}}-a^-)(\rho^{\mathrm{HLL}}-\rho^-),\,(a^+-v^{\mathrm{HLL}})(\rho^+-\rho^{\mathrm{HLL}})\big)(1,v^{\mathrm{HLL}},\tfrac12(v^{\mathrm{HLL}})^2)^\top$, $\alpha^*=a^+/(a^+-v^{\mathrm{HLL}})$ if $v^{\mathrm{HLL}}<0$ and $a^-/(a^--v^{\mathrm{HLL}})$ otherwise (Eqs. (2.13)–(2.16), §2.2.2). **Derived**
9. Desingularization: if $a^+<\varepsilon$ and $a^->-\varepsilon$ the flux is replaced by $\tfrac12(f(u^-)+f(u^+))$ (Remarks 2.1, 2.2), $\varepsilon=10^{-12}$ in all tests (§3). **Stated**
10. The 2D scheme (§2.3) is derived on the 1D restrictions (Eq. (2.20)) with continuity of $v_1$ and $p$ and sharp non-oscillatory jumps in $\rho$ and the transverse momentum $\rho v_2$; the $x$-anti-diffusion is $\alpha^*(q^\rho,v_1^{\mathrm{HLL}}q^\rho,q^{\rho v_2},q^E)^\top$ (p. 8), the $y$-term its mirror image (p. 9), and the fully discrete 2D scheme is "not shown here for the sake of brevity" (p. 8). **Derived**
11. All results use piecewise-linear reconstruction with the generalized minmod limiter ($\theta=1.3$), three-stage third-order SSP-RK, CFL 0.475 and $\gamma=1.4$ (§2, §2.3, §3). **Stated**
12. Example 1 (shock–entropy wave, $\Delta x=1/80$, $t=5$): the original scheme is "very inaccurate near the left boundary", the new one accurate throughout (Fig. 3.1). **Shown**
13. Example 2 (stationary contact, travelling shock and rarefaction, $\Delta x=1/200$ and $1/8000$, $t=0.012$): both resolve shock and contact similarly; the new scheme is non-oscillatory, the original has small oscillations "whose magnitude does not decay when the mesh is refined" (Fig. 3.2). **Shown**
14. 2D Riemann Configuration 3 ($\Delta x=\Delta y=0.001$, $t=1$): the new scheme is substantially less oscillatory, with comparable or slightly better resolution (Fig. 3.3). **Shown**
15. Explosion ($\Delta x=\Delta y=3/800$, $t=3.2$): the original scheme oscillates along the free boundaries $x=1.5$ and $y=1.5$, the new one much less; the original "achieves a slightly better resolution" here (Fig. 3.4). **Shown**
16. Implosion ($\Delta x=\Delta y=1/2000$, $t=2.5$): the new scheme is slightly less oscillatory and its jet propagates further, "slightly higher resolution" (Fig. 3.5). **Shown**
17. The new schemes contain almost the same small amount of [numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) as the original LDCU schemes but give substantially "cleaner", non-oscillatory solutions (§1). **Shown** (by the figures)

*(Compiler check, not in the sources: for $v^{\mathrm{HLL}}=0$, $\alpha^*=1$ and claim 8
