# Summary

The paper builds [artificial-viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md)
shock capturing for nodal [discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md)
schemes (Hesthaven–Warburton, LGL nodes in 1D, straight-sided triangles in 2D,
[local Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) interface flux,
Laplacian viscosity in an LDG-type mixed form). The
[smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) is the modal
decay rate $\mathfrak a_K$ of the Klöckner–Warburton–Hesthaven model,
$\lvert\hat u_m\rvert\simeq c\,m^{-\mathfrak a_K}$, but it is predicted by a
feedforward neural network instead of a least-squares fit of the modal
coefficients. The network (11 inputs, hidden layers of 32, 16 and 8 ReLU
neurons, one output, 1057 parameters) maps the element solution, normalized
by its maximum nodal magnitude and resampled at 11 uniform points, to
$\mathfrak a_K$. Training data are canonical functions with known regularity:
jumps ($\mathfrak a_K=1$), kinks ($\mathfrak a_K=2$), and constant, noisy-constant,
linear and trigonometric functions labelled $\mathfrak a_K=4$. The viscosity is
full for $\mathfrak a_K<1$, zero for $\mathfrak a_K>3$ and linear in between, scaled
by $\mu_{\max}=c_{\max}(\mathcal H/N)\max\lvert f'\rvert$ with a length scale
switched to the maximum edge jump, and made $C^0$ by P2 nodal averaging. In 2D
the network is applied along each triangle edge and the edge rates are
combined. The approach is a sibling of [learned WENO weights](/methods/learned-weno-weights.md)
and of the modal-decay [troubled-cell indicators](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md)
used for [hp-adaptation](/methods/hp-adaptation.md).

Evidence is numerical. Against the averaged modal-decay (MDA) estimate the
network is more reliable in constant regions; in convergence tests
(linear transport, [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md))
the viscosity acts only on the coarsest grids; shock tests
([Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md),
[Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[2D Riemann case 12](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md),
[Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) on unstructured
triangles, [high-Mach jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md))
are free of spurious oscillations, with $N=2$ the most fragile degree (it fails
on the jet). Time stepping is the five-stage fourth-order low-storage
Runge–Kutta method of Carpenter & Kennedy.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the polynomial degree $P$ is $N$ ("P2" is $N=2$), the
source's decay rate $\tau$ is the page-local $\mathfrak a_K$ (it is not the WENO-Z
$\tau$), its conserved unknown $q$ is $u$, and its weight-penalty coefficient
$\omega$ is renamed $\omega_{\mathrm{pen}}$.

1. A feedforward network can replace the least-squares modal-decay estimate of Klöckner et al. as the smoothness indicator of DG artificial viscosity, mapping element values to $\mathfrak a_K$ (abstract, §1, §4.1). **Stated** (design), **Shown** (§4.1.3, §5)
2. Compactness of the training data (inputs normalized by $\max_m\lvert u_m\rvert$, output restricted to the effective interval $\mathfrak a_K\in[1,3]$) is "critical to the success of training the network" (abstract, §4.1.1, §6). **Stated**
3. Because the viscosity (4.3) is constant for $\mathfrak a_K<1$ and for $\mathfrak a_K>3$, any $\mathfrak a_K$ outside $[1,3]$ can be reset to the nearer limit without changing the viscosity, so the output is guaranteed compact (§4.1.1). **Derived**
4. The Persson–Peraire indicator, based on the highest mode under the assumption that the $N$th mode scales as $1/N^2$, gives limited information on the decay rate and "tends to underestimate the smoothness"; a two-highest-mode variant and the all-mode least-squares fit of Klöckner et al. partly relieve this (§1, footnote 1). **Stated**
5. The MDA model is less reliable than the network: in constant regions its decay rate is close to $N$ because of the baseline modal decay, so it over-dissipates for degrees below 4, and skyline pessimization alters the function and makes $\mathfrak a_K$ uncertain at low degree (§4.1.3, Fig. 4, test function (4.7) on 65 elements). **Shown**
6. On the test function (4.7) the network predicts $\mathfrak a_K\approx1$ around discontinuities and $\mathfrak a_K>3$ in smooth regions; it slightly underestimates the smoothness of the sharp pulse near $x=0.15$ on the coarse mesh, while the better-resolved peak at $x=0.8$ gets $\mathfrak a_K>3$ (§4.1.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
7. On coarse meshes there is "no essential distinction between spurious oscillations and physical fluctuations", so the model treats every oscillation as non-smoothness; this improves with refinement (§4.1.3, §6). **Stated** (Figs. 4, 10 as support)
8. Architecture: 11 inputs, three hidden layers of 32, 16 and 8 neurons, one output, ReLU activation, 1057 trainable parameters (§3.1). **Stated** (the count is $11\cdot32+32+32\cdot16+16+16\cdot8+8+8+1=1057$)
9. Training: Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$, cost = mean squared error plus $\omega_{\mathrm{pen}}=1$ times size-normalized squared Frobenius norms of the weights, 80/10/10 split, weights and biases initialized from $\mathcal U[0,1]$, best of 20 trainings kept, early stop when the validation error stagnates (§3.2, §4.1.2). **Stated**
10. 1D linear transport: from $N_g=20$ elements onwards the scheme with network viscosity has errors identical to the inviscid scheme for $N=2,3,4$; at $N_g=10$ its errors are larger ($L^2$: $2.49\times10^{-3}$ against $1.71\times10^{-3}$ for $N=2$, $1.16\times10^{-4}$ against $6.93\times10^{-5}$ for $N=3$, $2.70\times10^{-6}$ against $2.18\times10^{-6}$ for $N=4$); asymptotic orders 3.00, 4.00, 5.03 (Table 2). **Shown**
11. 2D isentropic vortex ($t=10$): the viscosity is triggered only on the coarsest grid ($N_g=40$; $N=4$ $L^2$ error $1.38\times10^{-3}$ against $2.40\times10^{-5}$ inviscid), which inflates the first observed orders ($N=4$: 10.2 in $L^2$, 9.95 in $L^\infty$; $N=3$: 7.13, 8.06); afterwards the errors equal the inviscid ones. The asymptotic $L^2$ orders of both schemes are about 2.1–2.2 ($N=2$), 3.9 ($N=3$) and 4.06 ($N=4$) (Table 3). **Shown**
12. Burgers ($f=u^2$, $u_0=\sin2\pi x$, 80 elements, $t=0.3$): the discontinuity is captured without spurious oscillations and the peak viscosity decreases with increasing degree; a slight asymmetry of the viscosity is attributed to the randomness of the training samples (§5.2, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
13. Sod ($t=0.2$) and Lax ($t=0.13$) shock tubes on 100 elements: spurious oscillations suppressed (§5.3.1, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
14. Shu–Osher (200 elements, $t=1.8$): shocks sharp and oscillation-free; at a fixed number of degrees of freedom the accuracy decreases with increasing degree on coarse resolution (attributed to the coarse-mesh underestimation of smoothness), while the degrees almost coincide on finer meshes (§5.3.2, Figs. 9–11). **Shown**
15. 2D Riemann case 12 ($h=1/160$, $t=0.25$): oscillations suppressed and contact lines preserved (§5.4.1, Fig. 12). **Shown**
16. Shock–vortex interaction ($h=1/50$): stabilized "with reasonable wiggles", the vortex well resolved and only marginally affected by the artificial dissipation (§5.4.2, Figs. 13–15). **Shown**
17. Sedov blast wave (element size $1.1/160$, $t=1$): spurious oscillations suppressed and the viscosity follows the symmetric structure (§5.4.3, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
18. Double Mach reflection ($h=1/204$, $t=0.2$): strong shocks captured with little spurious oscillation, contact roll-up more visible at higher degree; at comparable degrees of freedom ($N=2$: 1,259,712; $N=4$: 1,248,480) shock thickness is comparable and contacts are slightly sharper for $N=2$ (§5.4.4, Figs. 18–20). **Shown**
19. Mach 3 forward-facing step on fully unstructured triangles ($h=1/80$, $t=3$, threshold 2.5 and the minimum rule (4.8)): shocks stabilized without special treatment of the corner; the entropy layer along the step wall is triggered by the corner and decreases with degree; agreement with Chaudhuri et al. (§5.4.5, Figs. 21–23). **Shown**
20. Astrophysical jet ($h=1/320$, $t=0.001$, threshold 2.5, minimum rule): $N=3$ and $N=4$ stable, $N=2$ goes unstable for lack of dissipation, attributed to the insufficient smoothness information of low-degree polynomials (§5.4.6, Figs. 24–25, §6). **Shown**
21. The explicit time step obeys $\Delta t\sim\mathrm{CFL}\big/\big(aN^2/h+\mu N^4/h^2\big)$, so artificial viscosity imposes a stringent restriction; an implicit scheme is left to future work (§2.3, Eq. 2.23). **Stated**
22. The 2D extension follows Yu & Hesthaven (2020): a discontinuity in a triangle intersects at least one edge, so edge-wise 1D estimates characterize the element (§4.2). **Stated**
23. The method extends to arbitrary 2D/3D and curved elements "at least in principle"; this is not validated (Remark 4). **Stated**
24. A more robust discontinuity switch than the jump-based length scale $\tilde{\mathcal H}$ is "clearly desirable" (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Title: "discontinuous Galekin methods" (page 1 and Crossref) for "Galerkin".
- Eq. (2.9): the right-hand side of the gradient equation carries $w^*_{v1}$, $w^*_{v0}$, whereas the text says that the centred flux is used for $g^*$ and $q^*$ and the 2D counterpart (2.20) carries $q^*$; integration by parts of the gradient equation gives the trace of $q$.
- §4.2, step 2: "the average of the largest two decay rates among the three edges", but Eq. (4.9), $\mathfrak a_K=\tfrac12\big(\sum_e\mathfrak a_e-\max_e\mathfrak a_e\big)$, is the mean of the two smallest.
- §5.1.1: exact solution $\sin(\pi(x-t))$ on "$[0,2\pi]$", error "after two periods, i.e., $t=4$"; with unit speed $t=4$ is two periods only on a domain of length 2, and $\sin(\pi x)$ is not periodic on $[0,2\pi]$ ($\sin(2\pi^2)\approx0.78\ne0$). *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- Eq. (5.7), case 12: the first state is assigned to "$0.5<x<1$, $0.5<y<1.5$" while the other tiles of the enlarged periodic domain span $0.5\le x<1.5$; as printed, the strip $1\le x<1.5$ falls into the "otherwise" state.
- §5.4.5: "agree well with the flow structures in Fig. 23 (see Fig. 20)"; Fig. 20 is the double-Mach comparison, the forward-step density is Fig. 22.
- Tables 2–3 captions say "$L_2$ errors and orders" but also list $L_\infty$.
