# Summary

Fan et al. (2025b) address the accuracy loss of earlier
[learned-weight](/methods/learned-weno-weights.md) schemes, which the authors
attribute to the network having to predict, for every low-order sub-stencil,
weights that meet the smooth-region accuracy condition at once. They build a
network around the third-order [weighted compact nonlinear scheme](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md)
with the [multi-resolution](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) weights of Zhu & Shu in
the modified form of [Qin et al. (2023)](/sources/qin-2023.md), whose
[smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) of the three-point
stencil carries an amplifying parameter $\lambda_{\mathrm s}$.

The nested sub-stencils are $\{x_i\}$ and $\{x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1}\}$. The
multi-resolution combination is rewritten on the plain sub-stencil interpolants,
whose optimal weights are then $(0,1)$; the network only has to drive the weight
of the three-point interpolant to 1. With a softmax output this is a half-line
condition on the logit difference, where weights with nonzero optimal values need
an interval of width $O(\epsilon_{\mathrm a})$ (Appendix A).

The network has three parts. A feature layer takes the normalized
Galilean-invariant differences of WENO3-NN plus a deliberately scale-variant
feature $\log_{10}\Delta_{\max}$, which, the authors argue, is what separates smooth
extrema from jumps on three points under refinement (see
[scale invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md) and
[critical-point accuracy](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)). Then three hidden
layers of 16 nodes with a softmax output. Last, at inference only, a TENO-style
hard cut-off at $C_T=2\times10^{-4}$. The loss pulls the weights towards a
low-dissipation and, at small scales, a high-dissipation multi-resolution target.

Third order holds to $N=10240$ for small-amplitude advection and a
[density wave](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md), where WCNS versions using the
network of [Bezgin et al. (2022)](/sources/bezgin-2022.md) reach about second order.
The [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
shows less dissipation than WCNS3-JS and WCNS3-MR. Shock tests
([Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[123](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md), [LeBlanc](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[2D Riemann](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[shock–bubble](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md),
[double Mach](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[Mach-80 jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md)) show resolution
between WCNS3-MR and WCNS5-JS, at the cost of WCNS3-MR.

# Key claims

Registry notation: grid points $x_i$ (source $x_j$, solution points) and flux
points $x_{i+1/2}$, point values $u_i$, left-biased interpolated value
$u_{i+1/2}$. Sub-stencil interpolants are $u^{(1)}$, $u^{(2)}$ in the source's
numbering (source $q_1,q_2$), their nonlinear weights $\omega_k$, the
multi-resolution weights on the hierarchical values $\omega^{\mathrm p}_k$.
Page-local: $d^{\mathrm{MR}}_k=b_{\mathrm{MR}}^{k-1}/\sum_lb_{\mathrm{MR}}^{l-1}$
the multi-resolution linear weights (source $\gamma_k$, base $b$),
$\tau_{\mathrm{MR}}=\lvert\beta_2-\beta_1\rvert^2$ (source $\tau$),
$\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_k$ the network weights, $\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}$ the softmax
logit difference (source $\delta$), $\epsilon_{\mathrm a}$ the accuracy tolerance
(source $\epsilon$), $\kappa_{\mathrm d}=\tanh(2\Delta_{\max})$ (source $\kappa$).

1. Earlier learned schemes lose accuracy: Stevens & Colonius' scheme is first order overall, and WENO3-NN, ML-WENO5 and a data-driven WCNS3 lose accuracy as the grid is refined, because the predicted weights of all low-order sub-stencils must meet the accuracy condition at once (§1). **Stated**
2. In the multi-resolution form the largest sub-stencil carries the optimal-accuracy candidate, so in smooth regions the network only has to give that stencil a weight close to 1 (§1, §3, Eqs. (19)–(21), Remark 3). **Derived**
3. $\sum_k\omega^{\mathrm p}_kp_k=\sum_k\omega_ku^{(k)}$ with $[\omega_1\ \omega_2]=[\omega^{\mathrm p}_1\ \omega^{\mathrm p}_2]\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\-d^{\mathrm{MR}}_1/d^{\mathrm{MR}}_2&1/d^{\mathrm{MR}}_2\end{bmatrix}$ (Eqs. (19)–(20)). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: with $p_1=u^{(1)}$, $p_2=(u^{(2)}-d^{\mathrm{MR}}_1u^{(1)})/d^{\mathrm{MR}}_2$ the identity holds, and since $\omega^{\mathrm p}_1+\omega^{\mathrm p}_2=1$ it gives $\omega_1=(\omega^{\mathrm p}_1-d^{\mathrm{MR}}_1)/d^{\mathrm{MR}}_2$, which is negative whenever $\omega^{\mathrm p}_1<d^{\mathrm{MR}}_1$; the paper's targets are produced in this form, and Remark 3 asks for $\omega_k\ge0$.)*
4. With $u^{(k)}=u(x_{i+1/2})+O(\Delta x^{2k-1})$ (Eq. (29)), $u^{\mathrm{NN}}_{i+1/2}=u(x_{i+1/2})+O(\Delta x^3)$ iff $\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_1=O(\Delta x^2)$ and $\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_2=O(1)$ (Eq. (30)). **Derived**
5. With a softmax, $\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_1=(1+e^{\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}})^{-1}$, and $\lvert\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_1-d_1\rvert\le\epsilon_{\mathrm a}$ is an interval of width $O(\epsilon_{\mathrm a})$ around $\ln(1/d_1-1)$ for Jiang–Shu weights ($d_1=1/4$) and for the hierarchical form ($d_1=1/11$, $b_{\mathrm{MR}}=10$), but the half-line $\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}\ge\ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm a}-1)$ for the rewritten form ($d_1=0$) (App. A, Eqs. (A.4)–(A.6), Table A.6). At $\epsilon_{\mathrm a}=10^{-6}$: $\pm5.33\times10^{-6}$ around $\ln3$, $\pm1.21\times10^{-5}$ around $\ln10$, and $\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}\ge13.82$. **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: the half-widths are $\epsilon_{\mathrm a}/(d_1(1-d_1))$ to first order, $16/3$ and $121/10$, and the row $\epsilon_{\mathrm a}=10^{-2}$ gives $[-0.0526,+0.0541]$, $[-0.1154,+0.1275]$ and $\ln99=4.595$; all agree with the table.)*
6. Only the rewritten form keeps optimal $L^\infty$ accuracy on the advection test, and it has the smallest weight errors $\lvert\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_1-d_1\rvert$ at $N=40$ (App. A, Fig. A.19). **Shown**
7. On three points, smooth extrema and discontinuities give nearly the same normalized data, so the scale-invariant features of WENO3-NN alone contaminate accuracy; $\Delta_{\max}$ shrinks under refinement for smooth data but stays constant at a jump, hence the added feature $\log_{10}\Delta_{\max}$ (§3.1.1, Eq. (26), Fig. 4). **Shown** (qualitatively)
8. A single hidden layer of 8 nodes gives similar results; 3×16 is kept for comparison with WENO3-NN (Remark 4). **Stated**
9. The cut-off layer, $\hat\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_k=\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_k\delta_k/\sum_l\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_l\delta_l$ with $\delta_k=0$ if $\omega^{\mathrm{NN}}_k<C_T=2\times10^{-4}$ (as in Bezgin et al.), stops small fluctuations from degrading accuracy as the error nears machine zero (§3.1.3, Eq. (31)). **Stated** (no ablation shown)
10. The loss is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{wl}}+c_h\mathcal L_{\mathrm{wh}}+c_W\lVert W\rVert_2^2$, $c_h=1.2$, $c_W=10^{-5}$: $L^1$ distances to WCNS3-MR weights with $b_{\mathrm{MR}}=30$, $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=1$ (low dissipation) and, weighted by $1-\kappa_{\mathrm d}$, with $b_{\mathrm{MR}}=10$, $\lambda_{\mathrm s}=50$ (high dissipation); the $L^1$ norm avoids conflicting constraints (§3.1.4, Eqs. (32)–(35)). **Stated**
11. Training data: six function families, 4000 samples each, rescaled by $10^a/\Delta_{\max}$ with $a\sim\mathcal U(-4,4)$; 4:1 split, Adam, learning rate $10^{-4}$, batch 500, 100 epochs on an RTX 4070 Ti (§3.2, Table 1, Eq. (36)). **Stated**
12. Advection of $0.001\sin(2\pi x)$ to $t=1$: order 3.00 from $N=80$ to $N=5120$ in all three norms, $L^1$ error $8.28\times10^{-6}$ at $N=20$ and $6.29\times10^{-14}$ at $N=10240$ (§4.1.1, Table 3). **Shown**
13. In the same test WCNS3-NN1 (Jiang–Shu form) and WCNS3-NN2 (multi-resolution form), both using the network of Bezgin et al. inside the WCNS, reach about second order in $L^1$ and $L^2$ and first order in $L^\infty$ (§4.1.1, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**; these are re-implementations in a point-value WCNS, not the finite-volume WENO3-NN of Bezgin et al.
14. Density wave $\rho=2+0.01\sin(2\pi x)$, $v=p=1$, $t=1$: third order to $N=10240$ ($L^1$ $3.94\times10^{-12}$), with $L^\infty$ orders 2.72 and 3.51 at the two coarsest refinements (§4.1.2, Table 4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
15. Weight analysis at $N=20$ and $N=500$: all schemes detect the discontinuities; at a smooth extremum WCNS3-MR-NN deviates least from $\omega_2=1$ on the coarse grid and separates extrema from jumps on the fine grid as WCNS3-MR does, while WCNS3-NN2 still drops to first order at extrema (§4.1.3, Fig. 8). **Shown**
16. Spectral analysis: better dispersion over a wide wavenumber range and lower dissipation than WCNS3-JS and WCNS3-MR (§4.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
17. Lax and Sod ($N=400$): resolution comparable to WCNS5-JS, sharper discontinuities and no overshoot compared with WCNS3-MR (§4.3.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
18. The 123 problem ($N=400$) and LeBlanc ($N=1000$, $t=10^{-4}$) run through (§4.3.1, Fig. 11). **Shown**
19. Shu–Osher ($N=400$): WCNS3-JS barely resolves the structure; WCNS3-MR-NN is slightly better than WCNS3-MR (§4.3.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
20. Woodward–Colella ($N=800$, $t=0.038$): clearly better than the other third-order schemes and close to WCNS5-JS (§4.3.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. 2D Riemann configurations 3 and 5, shock–bubble, double Mach reflection and Mach-80 jet: better resolution than WCNS3-JS and WCNS3-MR, comparable to the fifth-order scheme in the Riemann cases (§4.4, Figs. 14–18). **Shown**
22. Wall-clock times are comparable to WCNS3-MR, e.g. configuration 5 at $400^2$: 2808.29 s (MR) and 2800.06 s (MR-NN); Shu–Osher $N=200$: 1.48 and 1.16 s; Sod $N=400$: 1.15 and 1.16 s, on an i5-13600KF CPU with an RTX 4070 Ti GPU (§4.5, Table 5). **Shown**; the paper does not say which part runs on the GPU.
23. A larger base $b_{\mathrm{MR}}$ gives higher resolution but poorer shock capturing, a smaller one the reverse, and no single choice suits the whole domain (Remark 1); a larger $\lambda_{\mathrm s}$ improves shock capturing "without sacrificing the design accuracy" (Remark 2, citing Qin et al.). **Stated**
24. The approach extends to higher orders and to WENO (§2, §5). **Stated** (not shown)

The scheme (§2, §4): staggered finite differences with the fourth-order derivative $f'_i=\frac1{24\Delta x}(f_{i-3/2}-27f_{i-1/2}+27f_{i+1/2}-f_{i+3/2})$, characteristic interpolation, Roe flux, SSP-RK3 at CFL 0.5. Its multi-resolution weights are $\omega^{\mathrm p}_k\propto d^{\mathrm{MR}}_k\big(1+\tau_{\mathrm{MR}}/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)$, $\epsilon=10^{-10}$, with $\beta_1=\min\{(u_i-u_{i-1})^2,(u_{i+1}-u_i)^2\}$ and $\beta_2=\sum_{n=1}^2\big(\lambda_{\mathrm s}^{n-1}\psi_2^{(n)}(x_i)\big)^2$ (Eqs. (13)–(17)).

# Printed slips

- Table 1 lists the smooth family "$\tan(kx)$", $k\sim\mathcal U(5,30)$ on $[-1,1]$, while §3.2 says "tanh functions … represent smooth flows". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\tan(kx)$ has poles at $\lvert x\rvert=\pi/(2k)\le0.314$ inside the interval for every $k\ge5$.)*
- §3.2 says "polynomials up to degree three"; Table 1 has $n\in\{0,1,2\}$.
- §4.1.2 and §4.4: "$E=\rho u^2/2+p/(\gamma-1)$ denotes the total energy per unit mass"; the formula is energy per unit volume.
- App. A, case 3: "the optimal weight is simply $d_1=\gamma_1=0$"; §2.2 requires $\gamma_k\ne0$, and $\gamma_1=1/11$ in case 2.
- Eq. (13) prints $\beta_2$ with no power of $\Delta x$, while $\beta_1$ is an undivided squared difference; the paper does not say whether the derivatives $\psi_2^{(n)}$ are taken in index space.
- §4.4.3 compares "WCNS3-NN and WCNS3-MR"; no scheme named WCNS3-NN is defined (Table 2 defines NN1 and NN2).
- Table 4 caption cites "Euler equations (43)", the initial condition (the equations are (41)); Fig. 18 caption cites "(56)", the ambient state.
- Ref. [12]: authors printed "G.W. Huaibao Zhang, F. Zhang".
