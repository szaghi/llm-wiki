# Summary

Luo et al. (2026) (Luo, Zhao, Qiu & Chen) carry the compact "simple"
[Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) with one-stage [ADER](/methods/ader-scheme.md) time stepping of
their Part I (structured meshes) to triangular meshes. The scheme is a third-order finite volume that
stores, per triangle $K$, the averages of $u$, $\partial_xu$ and $\partial_yu$. Point values at the edge
Gauss points are expanded to second order in time, the time derivatives being turned into space
derivatives of the reconstruction polynomial by the
[Cauchy–Kowalewski procedure](/methods/cauchy-kowalewski-procedure.md); the flux is a local Lax–Friedrichs
flux integrated in time and along the edges.

The derivative averages are not evolved by their own equations. At $t^{n+1}$ they are recomputed by the
divergence theorem, $\bar v_K=\frac1{|K|}\oint_{\partial K}u\,n_x\,ds$, from the central average of the
interior and exterior Taylor values at the edge Gauss points, an idea taken from Part I and from compact
gas-kinetic schemes ([unstructured gradient reconstruction](/methods/unstructured-gradient-reconstruction.md)).
The reconstruction is "a direct two dimensional procedure" on the target and its three edge neighbours:
a quadratic that matches the four averages exactly and six derivative conditions in least squares, and
three linear polynomials on the pairs $\{K_0,K_\ell\}$, combined in the [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md) form with
positive linear weights $(0.94,0.02,0.02,0.02)$, barycentre-derivative
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type weights
normalized by the mean of the averages (taken from Zhao & Qiu 2025). One reconstruction per time step
serves the fluxes, the time derivatives and the derivative averages.

Against the Runge–Kutta moment-based HWENO on triangles of Zhao & Qiu (RK-HWENO), which evolves extra
equations and reconstructs at every stage, the authors claim smaller errors and lower cost; against
ADER-WENO, a more compact stencil at equal order. The evidence is numerical: third order on 2D
[Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md), the Euler [density wave](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md)
and the [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md); an error–CPU plot; and the Lax problem
([shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md)), [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), two
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) set-ups and the
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md). On the density wave the RK-HWENO $L^1$ errors are
the smaller ones on the three finest meshes, against the abstract's general claim.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's $W$ is $u$; its derivative averages $\bar V_K$, $\bar Y_K$ are $\bar v_K$,
$\bar w_K$ (page-local); linear weights $\gamma_\ell$ are $d_\ell$; $\bar\omega$ is $\alpha$; the global
indicator $\kappa$ is written $\tau$ and $W_{ave}$ is $\bar u_{\mathrm{ave}}$ (page-local); $\epsilon$ is
kept; velocities $\mu,\nu$ are $v_1,v_2$ and $P$ is $p$; the LLF speed $\alpha$ is $a$. Stencil and
polynomial indices are the source's: $T_1$, $p_1$ big, $T_{2,3,4}$, $p_{2,3,4}$ small.

1. ADER-HWENO is a compact third-order HWENO finite volume on triangles with a one-stage ADER step, the time derivatives of $u$ converted to space derivatives by the Lax–Wendroff procedure (Abstract, §1, §2). **Stated**
2. The averages $\bar v_K$, $\bar w_K$ at $t^{n+1}$ come from the Taylor-extrapolated edge values by the divergence theorem, $\bar v_K^{n+1}\approx\frac1{|K|}\sum_e\sum_{G_e}\hat u(\mathbf x_{G_e},t^{n+1})n_x\mathsf w_{G_e}|e|$ with $\hat u=\tfrac12(u^{\mathrm{int}}+u^{\mathrm{ext}})$, "instead of by the evolution solution directly in the conventional HWENO methods" (Abstract, §2.3, Eqs. (2.11)–(2.15)). **Derived**
3. Against RK-HWENO, no additional equations are solved and the reconstruction is done once per time step instead of once per stage (Abstract, §1, §4). **Stated** (design)
4. "Because of these advantages the new method is more efficient than the RK-HWENO method with smaller numerical errors and less computational costs" (Abstract). **Shown** (partly; see claim 10)
5. Against ADER-WENO at the same order, the stencil is more compact because function and derivative averages are both used (Abstract, §1, §4). **Stated**
6. The reconstruction is "a direct two dimensional procedure ... different from the dimension-by-dimension strategy" of the structured-mesh schemes (§2.2). **Stated**
7. Step 1–3: the quadratic $p_1$ on $T_1=\{K_0,K_1,K_2,K_3\}$ matches the four averages exactly and the derivative averages on the unions $K_0\cup K_\ell$ in least squares; the linear $p_{\ell+1}$ on $\{K_0,K_\ell\}$ keep $\bar u_{K_0}$ exactly and fit $\bar u_{K_\ell}$, $\bar v_{K_\ell}$, $\bar w_{K_\ell}$ in least squares; $p_1=d_1\big(\tfrac1{d_1}p_1-\sum_{\ell=2}^4\tfrac{d_\ell}{d_1}p_\ell\big)+\sum_{\ell=2}^4d_\ell p_\ell$ holds for any $d_1\ne0$, and $d=(0.94,0.02,0.02,0.02)$ is chosen "based on a balance" between non-oscillation and accuracy (§2.2, Eq. (2.7)). **Derived** (identity) / **Stated** (choice)
8. Indicators $\beta_\ell=\sum_{|s|=1}^{k}|K_0|^{|s|}\big(\partial^{|s|}p_\ell(x_{K_0},y_{K_0})/\partial x^{s_1}\partial y^{s_2}\big)^2$, $k=2$ for $\ell=1$ and 1 otherwise, at the barycentre; weights $\alpha_\ell=d_\ell\big(1+\tau/(\epsilon\,\bar u_{\mathrm{ave}}^4+\bar u_{\mathrm{ave}}^2\beta_\ell)\big)$, $\tau=\big(\tfrac13\sum_{\ell=2}^4|\beta_1-\beta_\ell|\big)^2$, $\bar u_{\mathrm{ave}}$ the mean of the four averages plus $10^{-30}$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ (Eqs. (2.8)–(2.9), from Zhao & Qiu). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: under $u\mapsto\sigma u$ the numerator and both denominator terms scale as $\sigma^4$, so the weights are invariant to scaling but not to a shift $u\mapsto u+\mu$, which changes $\bar u_{\mathrm{ave}}$; the paper does not call them [scale-invariant](/properties/scale-invariance.md).)*
9. 2D Burgers ($T=0.5/\pi$, 268 to 274432 triangles): ADER-HWENO $L^1$ orders 3.25, 3.13, 2.93, 2.87, 2.91 and $L^\infty$ orders 2.22, 2.95, 2.56, 2.67, 2.76; finest-mesh errors $L^1=1.85\times10^{-7}$, $L^\infty=3.34\times10^{-6}$ against $2.70\times10^{-7}$, $4.94\times10^{-6}$ for RK-HWENO, and smaller than RK-HWENO's on every mesh (Table 3.1). **Shown**
10. Euler density wave (268 to 68608 triangles): ADER-HWENO $L^1$ orders 3.24, 2.95, 2.98, 2.99; the $L^\infty$ errors are smaller than RK-HWENO's at every mesh (text); the $L^1$ errors are larger on the three finest meshes ($6.27\times10^{-5}$ against $5.81\times10^{-5}$ at 4288, $7.95\times10^{-6}$ against $6.26\times10^{-6}$ at 17152, $1.00\times10^{-6}$ against $7.93\times10^{-7}$ at 68608) (Table 3.2). **Shown**
11. ADER-HWENO is more efficient in $L^\infty$ error against CPU time, with "substantially less" CPU time at equal mesh (Fig. 3.3; the figure carries the unexplained labels 739 and 2554 at error $10^{-5}$; no hardware stated). **Shown**
12. Isentropic vortex on GMSH meshes (244 to 62464 triangles): density $L^1$ orders 2.08, 2.62, 2.92, 2.98, $L^\infty$ 2.43, 2.46, 2.87, 2.97 (Table 3.3). **Shown**
13. Lax problem: resolution "comparable" with RK-HWENO; Shu–Osher and blast waves: "higher resolution results" than RK-HWENO (Figs. 3.5–3.7). **Shown** (line plots)
14. Double Mach reflection on 589,312 triangles: comparable with RK-HWENO; on a 30° wedge the resolution improves from $h=1/160$ (209,938 triangles) to $h=1/320$ (837,673) (Figs. 3.8, 3.10). **Shown**
15. Forward step on 164,608 triangles, without corner modification or local refinement: comparable with RK-HWENO (Figs. 3.11–3.12). **Shown**
16. Solutions are oscillation-free at discontinuities, e.g. Burgers at $T=1.5/\pi$ on 4288 triangles (Fig. 3.2; Abstract, §4). **Shown**
17. "In some extreme cases" the Zhang–Shu scaling limiter modifies the reconstructed polynomials; the cases are not identified (§3). **Stated**
18. CFL 0.5 in all computations (§3). **Stated**
19. With a Gauss–Lobatto rule in time the $t^{n+1}$ edge values are temporal nodes and serve (2.13) directly, which "can further reduce the computational cost" (§2.3). **Stated**
20. Earlier HWENO schemes have three drawbacks: least-squares linear weights for 2D point values, two reconstructions on two stencil sets, and extra evolution equations; arbitrary-linear-weight WENO removes the first, unified stencils ([Fan et al. 2025a](/sources/fan-2025.md)) the second, and a GRP two-stage HWENO the third at the price of several reconstructions per step (§1). **Stated**
21. Extensions to moving meshes and multi-component flows are "underway" (§4). **Stated**

The number of temporal nodes, the temporal rule and the number of edge Gauss points are not stated, nor is
any characteristic decomposition, stability analysis or hardware.
