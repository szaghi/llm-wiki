# Summary

The paper builds a high-order
[bound-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md) local
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) (LDG) scheme for
the compressible Navier–Stokes equations of a multi-species gas with
finite-rate chemistry, the viscous reacting extension of the
[multicomponent Euler equations](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md).
It keeps $\rho>0$, $p>0$ and $0\le Y_k\le1$, and names three difficulties.

First, positivity for the convection–diffusion system: following X. Zhang
(2017), advective minus diffusive flux is treated as one flux in a
[Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)-type numerical flux whose
penalty, derived anew for many species (Lemma 4.1), includes the viscous
stress and heat flux. Second, the upper bound $Y_k\le1$ is not limited:
the model and the scheme conserve the linear invariant $\rho-\sum_k\rho Y_k$
through a "consistent" numerical flux, so $\sum_kY_k=1$ holds exactly at the
semi-discrete and fully discrete levels and positivity of every partial
density then gives the upper bound. Third, stiff chemistry: the authors'
third-order conservative modified exponential Runge–Kutta (CMERK) method
replaces [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Its artificial linear
damping makes every stage a convex combination of a convective and a source
structure. Positivity of the cell averages is proved under a CFL bound built
on the Gauss–Lobatto
[cell-average decomposition](/methods/optimal-cell-average-decomposition.md)
and a lower bound on the damping rate, in 1D and on 2D polygons. A three-step
density, mass-fraction and pressure
[Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) enforces
pointwise admissibility. Stiffness is handled by exponential integration, not
by an [asymptotic-preserving](/properties/asymptotic-preservation.md)
construction, and the alternative route of
[positivity-preserving flux limiters](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md)
is cited, not used.

Numerics (mostly $\mathbb P^2$) show third order with and without the limiter,
and convergence for stiff sources up to $c=10^6$ where SSP-RK3 at the same
$\Delta t$ fails. They include He/N₂ and H₂/O₂/Ar
[shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), on which the code blows
up without the limiter, a 1D reflected-shock H₂/O₂/Ar detonation and a 2D
cellular H₂/O₂/Ar [detonation](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md) at
$\mathbb P^1$.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's conserved vector
$\mathbf w$ is $u$, its velocity $(u,v)$ is $(v_1,v_2)$, its partial densities
$r_i=\rho Y_i$ are $\rho Y_k$, its DG degree $k$ is $N$, its Lax–Friedrichs
penalty $\beta_{i+1/2}$ is $a^{\mathrm R}_{i+1/2}$, its Gauss–Lobatto end weight
$\hat\omega$ is $\varpi$, its constant conservation vector $\mathbf v$ is
$\mathsf v$ and its damping rate $\mu$ is $\mu_{\mathrm R}$.

1. No previous work addresses bound preservation for multicomponent chemically reacting flows governed by Navier–Stokes; the most common practice is a cut-off, which loses mass (§1). **Stated**
2. Wang, Zhang, Shu & Ning made density, pressure and mass fractions positive for inviscid reacting flows but cannot guarantee $Y_k\le1$ theoretically, and their SSP time integration limits the step for stiff sources (§1). **Stated**
3. Modified Patankar Runge–Kutta methods preserve mass conservation and positivity of density and mass fractions but "cannot theoretically yield positive pressure for multicomponent flows" (§1). **Stated**
4. The model (2.1) is conservative in the sense of Def. 2.1 with $\mathsf v=[1,0,0,0,-1,\dots,-1]$: advective, diffusive and source terms are all orthogonal to $\mathsf v$ (Eq. 2.8), using the diffusion-velocity correction (2.5), which gives $\sum_kY_k\mathbf V_k=0$ when $\sum_kY_k=1$, and $\sum_k\dot\omega_k=0$ (§2.4). **Derived**
5. The semi-discrete LDG scheme (3.2)–(3.3) satisfies $\frac{d}{dt}(u\cdot\mathsf v)=0$ whenever the numerical flux is consistent, $\hat f_{i+1/2}\cdot\mathsf v=0$ (Thm. 3.1; 2D Thm. 5.2). **Proved**
6. The flux (3.4) is consistent provided $u\cdot\mathsf v=0$ at the current time (after Def. 3.1; 2D Thm. 5.1). **Stated** ("easy to check")
7. The fully discrete LDG–CMERK scheme preserves $u^{n+1}\cdot\mathsf v=u^n\cdot\mathsf v=0$, i.e. $\sum_kY_k=1$ at every time level (Thm. 3.2; 2D Thm. 5.3); the proof is written for stage 1, the others "the same". **Proved** (partially written out)
8. Replacing the exponentials by the fourth-order rational Taylor approximation $e^{A\mu_{\mathrm R}\Delta t}\approx\big[1-A\mu_{\mathrm R}\Delta t+\tfrac12(A\mu_{\mathrm R}\Delta t)^2-\tfrac16(A\mu_{\mathrm R}\Delta t)^3+\tfrac1{24}(A\mu_{\mathrm R}\Delta t)^4\big]^{-1}$ keeps conservation and approximates better for large $\mu_{\mathrm R}$ (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
9. For calorically perfect gases the mixture $\gamma-1>0$ when $Y_k\ge0$, so $p\ge0$ iff $P(u)=E-\sum_k\rho Y_kh_k(0)-\tfrac12\rho\lvert v\rvert^2\ge0$ (Eq. 4.1); $P$ is concave and the admissible set $G=\{\rho\ge0,\ P\ge0,\ \rho Y_k\ge0\}$ is convex (§4.1). **Derived** (concavity "one can check")
10. Because $\sum_kY_k=1$ is preserved, only the lower bounds $\rho Y_k\ge0$ need enforcing; $Y_k\le1$ follows (§4.1, §5.2). **Derived**
11. Each CMERK stage of the cell-average update is a convex combination of the structures $\mathbf C_i(\Delta t)$ (a forward-Euler flux update) and $\mathbf D_i=\bar S_i/\mu_{\mathrm R}+\bar u_i$ (Eqs. 4.3–4.6) (§4.2). **Derived**
12. Lemma 4.1: for $u\in G$ and the total flux $f=f^a-f^d$ with $p$, $\tau_{xx}$, $q_x$ and the diffusion velocities arbitrary, $u\pm f/a^{\mathrm R}\in G$ iff $a^{\mathrm R}\ge\max\big\{\lvert v_1+\tfrac{b}{2\rho P}\rvert+\tfrac{\sqrt{\mathfrak D}}{2\rho P},\ \lvert v_1+v_{1,k}\rvert\big\}$, with $b$ and $\mathfrak D=b^2+2\rho P(p-\tau_{xx})^2$ defined in Eq. (3.5) (§4.2). **Proved** *(compiler check, not in the sources: the discriminant of the quadratic (4.11) reduces to $b^2+2\rho P(p-\tau_{xx})^2$ as printed.)*
13. Theorem 4.1: with $L$ Gauss–Lobatto points ($L$ the smallest integer with $2L-3\ge N$), if the interface traces and interior Gauss–Lobatto values lie in $G$, then $\mathbf C_i(\Delta t)\in G$ under $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\max_ia^{\mathrm R}_{i+1/2}\le\varpi=\frac1{L(L-1)}$; a weaker condition (4.13) on the interior convex combination suffices (§4.2). **Proved** (weaker version referred to Zhang 2017)
14. Theorem 4.2: if the values at the $M$ Gauss points lie in $G$, then $\mathbf D_i\in G$ when $\mu_{\mathrm R}$ exceeds the bound (4.14), built from the production rates, the formation enthalpies $h_k(0)$ and $P$; experiments use $M=N+1$ (§4.2). **Proved**
15. Theorem 4.3: under (4.16) for $u^n,u^{(1)},u^{(2)}$ and the CFL condition (4.17) $\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\max a^{\mathrm R}\le\min\{\alpha_{10}/\beta_{10},\dots,\alpha_{32}/\beta_{32}\}\varpi$, $\bar u^{n+1}_i\in G$ (§4.2). **Proved** (proof omitted as straightforward). *(compiler check, not in the sources: with the printed coefficients $\alpha_{10}/\beta_{10}=1.41404$, $\alpha_{21}/\beta_{21}=\alpha_{32}/\beta_{32}=0.79298$, and the ratios with $\beta_{jl}=0$ are unbounded, so the CMERK factor is $0.79298$.)*
16. Lemma 5.1 and Theorems 5.4–5.6 give the same conclusions on polygonal cells with a cell quadrature that contains the edge Gauss points, under $\Delta t\frac{\lvert e_i\rvert}{\lvert K\rvert}\max a^{\mathrm R}_{e_i}\le\min\{\alpha/\beta\}\min_{\nu,i}a_{\nu,i}/b_\nu$ (§5.2). **Proved** (Lemma 5.1 sketched; Thms. 5.5–5.6 omitted)
17. On rectangles with Gauss × Gauss–Lobatto tensor quadrature the CFL condition becomes $\Delta t\big(\frac1{\Delta x}+\frac1{\Delta y}\big)\max a^{\mathrm R}_{e_i}\le\frac1{P(P-1)}$, $P=\lceil(N+3)/2\rceil$ (Remark 5.1). **Stated**
18. In practice $\mu_{\mathrm R}$ is computed from $u^n$ only, $\Delta t=\min\{a_\Delta\Delta x/a^*,\ b_\Delta\Delta x^2/\eta^*\}$, $a^*$ a wave speed without the viscous and heat-flux terms of the penalty,, and on a non-physical cell average the step is recomputed with $\mu_{\mathrm R}$ doubled and $\Delta t$ halved; Theorem 4.3 guarantees termination and "very few recomputations are observed" (§6, Eqs. 6.1–6.2). **Stated**
19. 1D accuracy test ($\mathbb P^2$, $c=100$ and $10^4$): third order in $L^2$ and $L^\infty$ with and without the limiter; the fraction of limited cells falls from 6.88% ($N_x=10$) to 8.58E-3% ($N_x=320$) for $c=100$ (Table 6.1). **Shown**
20. Stiff test $c=10^6$: CMERK with $\Delta t$ from (6.1) converges ($L^2$ 1.84E-03 at $N_x=10$ to 1.28E-08 at $N_x=640$, final order 2.96); SSP-RK3 with the same $\Delta t$ gives NaN at $N_x=10,20$, and RK3 with $\tfrac25\Delta t$ works (Table 6.2). **Shown**
21. 2D accuracy test ($\mathbb P^2$): third order with and without the limiter, e.g. $L^2$ 8.66E-08 at $N_x=320$, order 3.00, for $c=100$ without the limiter (Table 6.3). **Shown**
22. He/N₂ shock tube ($\mathbb P^2$, 1000 cells): positivity and both bounds of $Y$ preserved, agreeing with Houim & Kuo; without the limiter the code blows up and $Y_2$ is already negative at 0.5085 μs (Figs. 6.1–6.2). **Shown**
23. H₂/O₂/Ar shock tube ($\mathbb P^2$, 400 cells): bounds preserved; the code blows up without the limiter (Fig. 6.3). **Shown**
24. 1D reacting H₂/O₂/Ar reflected-shock problem (9 species, 37 irreversible reactions, $\mathbb P^2$, 400 cells): bounds preserved, with "some numerical oscillations" since only the bound-preserving limiter is used (Figs. 6.4–6.5). **Shown**
25. 2D H₂/O₂/Ar detonation ($\mathbb P^1$, $\Delta x=\Delta y=0.001$ m, 9 species, 34 reactions): a detonation forms after the initial shock meets two hot spots, with transverse waves and a cellular front (Figs. 6.6–6.7); higher-order runs need a further oscillation limiter, left to future work (§6.6). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- §2 (p. 3) and §3 (p. 6): "the primitive variables and the advection fluxes are given by" for $\mathbf w=(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E,r_1,\dots)$, which are conserved variables (so called in §3.1 and §5.1).
- §1 (p. 2): "mass fractures are designed to be positive" (fractions).
- Wilke viscosity (p. 4) printed as $\eta=\sum_i\frac{X_i\eta_i}{\sum_jX_i\phi_{ij}}$; with $X_i$ inside the sum over $j$ the mole fractions cancel and the mixture viscosity would not depend on composition, so $X_j$ is meant *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Theorem 5.4 (p. 18): "A sufficient condition for $\mathbf C_K(\Delta t)\in G$ in (4.6)"; (4.6) is the 1D definition, the 2D $\mathbf C_K$ is (5.13).
- Eq. (3.5) uses a strict ">" for the penalty, Lemma 4.1 and (5.4) use "≥".
- Example 6.6 (p. 27): the hot-spot "regions" are printed as equalities $\sqrt{(x-0.019)^2+(y-0.015)^2}=0.0025$ m (a circle); the text's "within" implies "<".
- Example 6.6 prints the compositions as mass-fraction ratios, $Y_{Ar}:Y_{O_2}:Y_{H_2}=7:1:2$, while Examples 6.3–6.4 give the same mixture as a 2/1/7 molar ratio of H₂/O₂/Ar; the paper does not say which is meant.
- §2.3: the Arrhenius rate is printed $k_r=A_rT^{\beta_r}\exp\frac{E_{a,r}}{R_uT}$, with a positive exponent; the sign convention of $E_{a,r}$ is not stated.
- Ref. [25] (Johnson & Kercher) is printed without a journal name ("423 (2020) 109826").
