# Summary

Zuo & Zhu (2024a) make the finite-difference
[multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) (MR-WENO) of Zhu & Shu into a
[hybrid scheme](/methods/hybrid-weno.md), MMR-WENO ("modified MR-WENO") of orders 5, 7 and 9. A cell
uses the linear upwind scheme of the same order unless it is troubled; only troubled cells pay for
characteristic projection and nonlinear weights.

The contribution is a [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md). On the big
stencil of $n_{\mathrm g}+1$ points the nodal density is interpolated by a polynomial of degree $n_{\mathrm g}$ (the source's $2r$), written in
monomials centred at $x_{i-1/2}$ and at $x_{i+1/2}$. The cell is troubled if that polynomial can have
an extremum inside $[x_{i-1/2},x_{i+1/2}]$, decided without root finding. Each monomial term of the
scaled derivative is monotone on the cell, so summing termwise maxima and minima gives an upper and a
lower bound, and the cell is flagged when the bounds, shifted by $\pm\Delta x$, have opposite signs.
The earlier extremum indicators of Zhu & Qiu, Zhao et al. and Wang, Tian, Zhu & Zhao (2023) searched wider intervals
with exact roots or sign tests and stopped at fifth order. The authors call the new one parameter
free and valid at any order.

The evidence is numerical. On the 1D, 2D and 3D
[density-wave advection](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md) MMR-WENO reaches orders 5, 7 and 9,
while MR-WENO5 shows erratic orders in 2D and 3D. The other tests are the Lax problem
([shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md)), a
[two-dimensional Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and the inviscid
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md). Flagged-cell maps are compared with the
SHUS-WENO of Wang, Tian, Zhu & Zhao. CPU time falls to 20.7–36.8% of MR-WENO. The sibling paper
[Zuo & Zhu (2024b)](/sources/zuo-2024-b.md) replaces the bounds by an exact root count.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's $h$ is $\Delta x$; its velocities $\mu,\nu,w$ are $v_1,v_2,v_3$; its
linear weights $\gamma_{\ell,\tilde\ell}$ are $d_{\ell,\tilde\ell}$; its $\bar\omega$ is $\alpha$;
its $\tau_{\ell_2}$ is $\tau_{2\ell_2+1}$ (subscript = points of the big stencil); its indicator
polynomials $g_1,g_2$ are $\mathsf g_{\mathrm L},\mathsf g_{\mathrm R}$, with coefficients
$a^{\mathrm L}_k,a^{\mathrm R}_k$ in powers of $X_{\mathrm L}=(x-x_{i-1/2})/\Delta x$ and
$X_{\mathrm R}=(x-x_{i+1/2})/\Delta x$, and its $MAX_l$, $MIN_l$ are $\overline B_l$, $\underline B_l$
(page-local).

1. MMR-WENO "could save at most 63%-79% CPU time" against MR-WENO of the same order; the ratios MMR/MR are 36.8, 31.2, 35.7% (Lax, orders 5/7/9), 32.5, 23.6, 23.2% (2D Riemann), 31.1, 28.4, 24.9% (double Mach) and 31.6, 24.2, 20.7% (Taylor–Green) (Abstract, §3, Table 3.1; no hardware stated). **Shown**
2. The indicator is "a first time" design based on two high-degree polynomials and "can automatically identify the discontinuous solutions without manually adjusting the parameters related to the problems" (Abstract, §1, §4). **Stated** (illustrated by flagged-cell maps, Figs. 3.5, 3.7, 3.10)
3. Cell $I_i$ is troubled iff the termwise bounds of $\Delta x\,\mathsf g'_{\mathrm L}$ or of $\Delta x\,\mathsf g'_{\mathrm R}$ on $I_i$ satisfy $(\overline B_l\pm\Delta x)(\underline B_l\pm\Delta x)<0$; troubled cells (Flag $=0$) use MR-WENO, the others (Flag $=1$) the linear upwind scheme (Eq. 2.33, Algorithm 1). **Derived** (construction)
4. Each term of $\Delta x\,\mathsf g'_{\mathrm L}$ is monotone on the cell, so $\overline B_1=\max(0,4a^{\mathrm L}_4)+\max(0,3a^{\mathrm L}_3)+\max(0,2a^{\mathrm L}_2)+a^{\mathrm L}_1$ and $\underline B_1$ with $\min$ bound the derivative from above and below (§2.2 Step 3, Eqs. 2.29–2.30). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $X_{\mathrm L}\in[0,1]$ on $I_i$, so the bounds are correct; they are over-estimates, so opposite signs do not imply an extremum, and only equal signs prove that there is none.)*
5. The shift $\pm\Delta x$ is used "following the analysis in [28]" against round-off sign changes of near-zero bounds (§2.2 Step 3). **Stated**
6. If the first polynomial flags the cell, the second is not evaluated (Remark 1, Algorithm 1). **Derived**
7. Testing the smallest interval $[x_{i-1/2},x_{i+1/2}]$ instead of $[x_{i-3/2},x_{i+3/2}]$ flags fewer cells, and the second polynomial is what guarantees "the stability of the modified MR-WENO schemes" (Remark 1). **Stated** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\mathsf g_{\mathrm L}$ and $\mathsf g_{\mathrm R}$ interpolate the same five nodal values with degree 4, so they are one polynomial expanded about two points; solving both interpolation systems reproduces all ten printed coefficients (2.25)–(2.26), with $a^{\mathrm L}_0$, $a^{\mathrm R}_0$ the interpolated values at $x_{i\mp1/2}$. The second test differs from the first only through its termwise bounds.)*
8. The earlier indicators of Zhu & Qiu, Zhao et al. and Wang, Tian, Zhu & Zhao "had a fatal flaw": for quintic or higher-degree polynomials, finding the extreme points in the big interval is "not only time-consuming but also unavailable", so they served only hybrid schemes of at most fifth order (§1, §2.2). **Stated**
9. The construction extends to seventh order (two sextics on seven points) and ninth order (two octics on nine points), and to 2D and 3D dimension by dimension (Remark 2). **Stated** (formulas given for fifth order only; the announced third-order schemes are not given)
10. In smooth cells the characteristic projection and the computation of linear weights, smoothness indicators and nonlinear weights are skipped for systems (§1). **Stated**
11. 1D density wave ($t=2$, $N_x=100$–150): MMR-WENO5/7/9 reach orders 5, 7, 9 with errors equal to or smaller than MR-WENO's; MR-WENO5 has erratic $L^\infty$ orders (7.10, 5.29, 5.65, 6.53, 5.41), MMR-WENO5 5.19 to 4.96 (Table 3.2). **Shown**
12. 2D density wave: MR-WENO5 $L^1$ orders 2.52, 7.58, 5.98, 8.66, 5.55 from $10^2$ to $60^2$, MMR-WENO5 4.92–5.00 with smaller errors on every grid ($L^1$ at $10^2$: MR-WENO5 $4.74\times10^{-3}$, MMR-WENO5 $1.98\times10^{-3}$) (Table 3.3). **Shown**
13. 3D density wave: MR-WENO5 $L^\infty$ orders 4.73–6.41 against 4.88–5.00 for MMR-WENO5; at orders 7 and 9 the errors coincide to three digits on fine grids (Table 3.4). **Shown**
14. MMR-WENO reaches the design order "with less CPU time and smaller numerical errors" (Figs. 3.1–3.3). **Shown**
15. Lax problem: MMR-WENO "perform better overall" than SHUS-WENO; both indicators find the discontinuities, "while the method proposed in this paper is more accurate" (Example 3.4, Figs. 3.4–3.5). **Shown** (qualitative)
16. 2D Riemann problem: "more delicate roll-up structures", "smaller numerical dissipations" than MR-WENO (Example 3.5). **Shown** (qualitative)
17. Double Mach reflection: "more sophisticated flow structures" near the Mach stems, and more accurate flagging than SHUS-WENO (Example 3.6, Figs. 3.8–3.10). **Shown** (qualitative)
18. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex ($128^3$): the kinetic energy of MMR-WENO stays closer to its initial 0.125 than that of MR-WENO, "substantially lower dissipations" (Example 3.7, Figs. 3.11–3.12). **Shown**
19. MR-WENO background: the linear weights may be "any positive numbers as long as their sum is one", here proportional to $10^\ell$ (§2.1). **Stated**
20. CFL 0.6 and third-order TVD Runge–Kutta in all tests (§3, Eq. 2.20). **Stated** (no $\Delta t$ refinement is stated for the seventh- and ninth-order accuracy tests)

*Compiler check, not in the sources:* the accuracy tables are almost identical, MR-WENO and hybrid
columns alike, to those of the HMR-WENO paper [Zuo & Zhu (2024b)](/sources/zuo-2024-b.md), whose
indicator is different. Of the 216 error entries of Tables 3.2–3.4, 15 differ, all in the seventh-
and ninth-order part of Table 3.2 (MR-WENO and hybrid columns), by at most 3 % relative
($2.53\times10^{-16}$ against $2.60\times10^{-16}$); Tables 3.3–3.4 agree digit for digit. The six
MR-WENO CPU times of double Mach reflection and of the Taylor–Green vortex (orders 5, 7, 9) are the
same in the two papers (9509.17, 17058.2, 26535.5 s and 378045, 659238, 962971 s; Table 3.1 of
each).

# Printed slips

- Eqs. (2.31)–(2.32): the bounds of the second polynomial are printed $\overline B_2=\max(-4a^{\mathrm R}_4,0,\mathcal A^{max})+\max(3a^{\mathrm R}_3,0,\mathcal B^{max})+\max(-2a^{\mathrm R}_2,0,\mathcal C^{max})+\max(a^{\mathrm R}_1,\mathcal D^{max})$ (and $\underline B_2$ with $\min$), mixing in the termwise extremes $\mathcal A,\dots,\mathcal D$ of the first polynomial; Step 4 says "As shown in Step 3". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: on $I_i$, $X_{\mathrm R}\in[-1,0]$, so the bounds of the second polynomial alone are $\max(-4a^{\mathrm R}_4,0)+\max(3a^{\mathrm R}_3,0)+\max(-2a^{\mathrm R}_2,0)+a^{\mathrm R}_1$ and its $\min$ counterpart; the printed forms are still valid bounds but looser, and flag more cells.)*
- The criterion "$(MAX1\pm h)\cdot(MIN1\pm h)<0$" never says which sign pairing applies.
- Step 3 calls each term, including the constant $a^{\mathrm L}_1$, "strictly monotonic"; Eq. (2.28) prints $\tilde g'_l$ for $\tilde g'_1$; "overdetermined" for over-estimated bounds.
- Eq. (2.15): "$m=1,2,3,4$" without saying which $m$ goes with which order.
- Table 3.1, Taylor–Green, order 7: $160044/659238=24.28\%$, printed 24.2%, while the other eleven ratios are rounded *(compiler check)*.
- Example 3.4: "save nearly 60% CPU time", while Table 3.1 gives 63.2%, 68.8% and 64.3% for the Lax problem; "at most 63%-79%" for savings between 63.2% and 79.3% *(compiler check)*.
- Example 3.6: contours of "$[0,3]\times[0,1]$ on $1200\times400$ grid points" in the domain $[0,4]\times[0,1]$; which interval the 1200 points cover is not said.
- Typos: "Broges et al." (Borges), "Admas et al." (Adams), "CUP time", "gird points", "deference", "octave polynomials" (octic).
