# Summary

Fu, Hu & Adams make the cut-off $C_T$ of five- and six-point
[targeted ENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) vary locally, and so found the
[adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) schemes TENO5-A and TENO6-A. A first-order
discontinuity measure "following" Ren, Liu & Zhang (2003) is mapped by a smoothing kernel
onto the exponent of $C_T=10^{-\lfloor b\rfloor}$, so that $C_T$ is small (near-linear
scheme) on smooth broadband content and large (strong nonlinear adaptation) at
discontinuities. Two further changes come with it. A new five-point global indicator
$\tau_5$ of sixth order replaces the WENO-Z one, after Fu et al. (2017), who state that
sixth order is needed for robustness with SSP-RK3 at CFL numbers above 0.4. The linear
backgrounds are designed in spectral space with the
[approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md): the
five-point weights minimize Arshed & Hoffmann's joint objective with a small
anti-dissipative target (third order), and the six-point scheme optimizes dispersion and
dissipation separately (fourth order).

The schemes are run with one parameter set on the
[Sod and Lax tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), the
[Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md), the inviscid and viscous
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) as implicit LES,
[compressible isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/compressible-isotropic-turbulence.md) and a
[steady shock reflection](/benchmarks/steady-shock-problems.md). The comparators are
[WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md), WENO5-JS and (Rayleigh–Taylor only)
[WENO5-Z](/methods/weno-z.md); TENO5 and TENO6 with a fixed cut-off are not run, so the
effect of the adaptive cut-off is not separated from that of the new backgrounds.

# Printed slips

- **Sign of $\tilde\xi_I$ switches inside §IV.** Eq. (27) defines $\tilde\xi_I=+\sum_jc_j\cos(j\xi)$, and the text calls a positive $\alpha$ dissipation and a negative one anti-dissipation (positive = dissipative). Eq. (39), the remark after it ("a negative value corresponds to positive numerical dissipation"), the $-\tilde\xi_I$ of Eq. (40) and Fig. 2 use the opposite, registry, convention. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with $\tilde\xi_I=\sum b\cos$ as in Eq. (27) the six-point dissipative part would be $+\tfrac25b_0(1-\cos\xi)^3$; Eq. (39) is its negative.)* The "tiny positive hump" is positive only in the Fig. 2 convention.
- **Eq. (25) lacks the absolute value** of Eq. (15); the printed quadratic form is indefinite.
- **TENO5-A weights not reproducible** from Eq. (27) and the printed parameters (Key claim 6); the stated procedure, "solving the linear system $(\partial E_5/\partial a_j)=0$, $j=-3,\dots,2$ and imposing $\sum d_k=1$", imposes six stationarity equations on coefficients that depend on two free weights. Fu (2021) attributes the same weights to Arshed and Hoffmann (see [adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md)). Recorded as unverifiable, not as wrong.
- **"$C_T\approx10^{-\alpha_1}$"** for TENO6-A ($\alpha_1=10.5$): the floor gives $10^{-10}$.
- **Comparators announced and used.** §V names WENO5-Z and WENO-CU6 as the comparators; WENO5-Z appears only in Rayleigh–Taylor, and WENO5-JS is used in double Mach reflection, the blast-wave reference and the steady reflection.
- **"Approximate dispersion relation" in two senses.** In §IV.B the name is given to Hu et al.'s dispersion–dissipation condition (footnote § to arXiv:1204.5088); in §IV.C to Pirozzoli's numerically evaluated spectrum of a nonlinear scheme.
- §VI says the schemes "employ the same full-stencil points as the classical fifth-order WENO5-JS scheme"; TENO6-A uses six points.
- §III.B: "the numerical dissipation of TENO schemes can be further deduced" (presumably "reduced").
- §V.G: "as observed, e.g., by Yee et al. [38]"; ref. [38] is Kotov, Yee, Wray, Sjögreen & Kritsuk (2016).
- Nomenclature: $\tau$ listed twice (eddy turnover time, viscous stress tensor) besides the global indicator $\tau_K$; $S$ "strain rate sensor" (tensor); $k$ both conductivity and wavenumber; $q$ both heat flux and the TENO exponent.
- Ref. [28] prints Woodward alone for Woodward & Colella (1984). Refs. [19] and [24] print an issue DOI and a journal-ISSN DOI, not article DOIs.
