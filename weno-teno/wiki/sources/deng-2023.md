# Summary

The paper works on finite-volume reconstruction of the left state
$u^-_{i+1/2}$ from the three cell averages $\bar u_{i-1},\bar u_i,\bar u_{i+1}$
of a 1D scalar law, with [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)
stepping. Building on Leonard's normalised-variable diagram, it writes every
such scheme as a map from the normalised cell average
$\tilde u_i=(\bar u_i-\bar u_{i-1})/(\bar u_{i+1}-\bar u_{i-1})$ to the
normalised face value $\tilde u_{i+1/2}$, and recasts in this plane linear
schemes, RBF reconstructions, the [TVD limiters](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md),
the NVD schemes CUBISTA, TOPUS and SMART,
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md), [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)
and WENO-P third-order weights, the third-order analogue of
[WENO-Z+](/methods/weno-z-plus.md), LDLR and [THINC](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md).

Three facts are proved: a normalisable scheme is
[scale-invariant](/properties/scale-invariance.md); a linear operator is at
least second order iff it passes through the accuracy point $(1/2,3/4)$; the
line $\tilde u_{i+1/2}=\tfrac56\tilde u_i+\tfrac13$ (P3) is the only uniformly
third-order linear operator. A sign analysis of the leading error splits the
plane into regions of [numerical diffusion and anti-diffusion](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md),
and the location of critical points in normalised space is derived
([accuracy at critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)). From
these results the paper induces the
[Unified Normalised-variable Diagram (UND)](/definitions/normalised-variable-diagram.md):
the accuracy point and P3 line, diffusion and anti-diffusion regions, a
CFL-dependent CBC-TVD region, an ENO region and a high-resolution region.

On the UND it designs a non-symmetric shape-preserving TVD limiter, a
scale-invariant third-order WENO whose weight depends on $\tilde u_i$ alone,
and the [ROUND schemes](/methods/round-scheme.md) (dissipative,
low-dissipative and TVD/shape-preserving formulations). Evidence is numerical:
1D advection and Euler problems (Sod, Lax, [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), Leblanc), 2D
rotation, a [2D Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
the [double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and a
shock diffraction in a finite-difference immersed-boundary code with
[flux splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md). Low-dissipative ROUND is
claimed comparable to fifth-order WENO-JS with a three-cell stencil. No
observed-order table is printed; [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
plots and the contrast with [BVD](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md)
complete the analysis; [mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md) and
[CWENO](/methods/cweno.md) appear in the survey of third-order variants.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's $\phi$, $h$,
$\hat\phi^L_i$, $\hat\phi^L_{i+1/2}$ are $u$, $\Delta x$, $\tilde u_i$,
$\tilde u_{i+1/2}$; its normalised operator $\hat R$ is $\mathcal R$ and its
operator $R$ is written as the face value $u^-_{i+1/2}[\bar u]$, as on the
[normalised-variable diagram](/definitions/normalised-variable-diagram.md) page;
the intercept and slope $\alpha_0$, $\alpha_1$ of a linear operator are
$\mu_0$, $\mu_1$.

1. Lemma 1: with no critical point in the stencil, $\tilde u_i=\tfrac12-\frac{u''(x_c)\Delta x}{4u'(x_c)}+O(\Delta x^2)$ for any $x_c=x_{i-1/2}+\delta\Delta x$, $-1\le\delta\le2$ (§2.2, Eq. 2.7). **Proved**
2. Lemma 2: at a first-order critical point $x_c=x_{i-1/2}+\delta\Delta x$, $\delta\ne1/2$, $\tilde u_i=\delta/(2\delta-1)+O(\Delta x)$ (Eq. 2.8). **Proved**
3. Proposition 1: on a fine enough mesh a first-order critical point in $I_i$ gives $\tilde u_i<0$ or $>1$, one in $I_{i-1}$ gives $0<\tilde u_i<1/2$, one in $I_{i+1}$ gives $1/2<\tilde u_i<1$ (§2.2). **Proved** (from Lemma 2, truncating $O(\Delta x)$)
4. Proposition 2: a normalisable scheme is scale-invariant, $u^-_{i+1/2}[\lambda\bar u]=\lambda\,u^-_{i+1/2}[\bar u]$ (§2.3). **Proved**
5. Every consistent linear reconstruction is normalisable, $\mathcal R=\mu_1\tilde u_i+\mu_0$ (Eq. 3.7). **Stated** ("easy to prove")
6. Proposition 3: a linear operator is at least second-order accurate iff it passes through $(1/2,3/4)$ (Eq. 3.9). **Proved**
7. Proposition 4: uniform third order holds iff $\mu_0=1/3$, $\mu_1=5/6$ (P3); hence QUICK ($\mu_0=3/8$, $\mu_1=3/4$) is not uniformly third order in a cell-average finite-volume method (§3.1). **Proved**
8. Proposition 5 and Table 1: for a second-order linear operator the leading error $4(\tfrac13-\mu_0)(\tfrac12-\tilde u_i)u'(x_c)\Delta x+O(\Delta x^3)$ (Eq. 3.12) is diffusive for $\mu_0>1/3,\tilde u_i>1/2$ or $\mu_0<1/3,\tilde u_i<1/2$, anti-diffusive otherwise (Eq. 3.10). **Derived**
9. Multiquadric RBF compact reconstructions are not necessarily consistent (coefficients need not sum to 1), hence not normalisable; plotted with $\bar u_{i-1}=0$, $\bar u_{i+1}=1$ they are singular at $\tilde u_i=-0.4$ (§3.2, Fig. 1). **Derived/Shown**
10. Symmetric TVD limiters (van Leer, van Albada, OSPRE) are dissipative for $\tilde u_i<1/2$ and anti-dissipative for $\tilde u_i>1/2$, which distorts transported shapes; since P3 violates the symmetry condition (Eq. 4.15), symmetric TVD limiters cannot be third order (§4.1, Remark 4.1, Fig. 3). **Derived/Shown**
11. SuperBee is anti-dissipative for $\tilde u_i$ between 0.3 and 0.8; MinMod is always dissipative (§4.1). **Derived** (read off the diagram)
12. CUBISTA and TOPUS do not fully retrieve P3 on $0<\tilde u_i<1$ and lie below the slope-$1/0.4$ line, so they suit explicit Runge–Kutta; SMART is designed for implicit stepping (§4.2, Fig. 4). **Stated/Shown**
13. Classical third-order WENO weights (JS, Z, P) are not normalisable, hence not scale-invariant; in the special-case plane ($\bar u_{i-1}=0$, $\bar u_{i+1}=1$) WENO-JS3 is close to MinMod on $0<\tilde u_i<1$ and WENO-P is closer to P3 than JS or Z (§4.3, Fig. 5). **Shown** (special-case plot)
14. WENO-P+ is "neither scale-invariant nor mesh-size-independent" because its parameter $s$ is tied to $\Delta x$; with $s=1$ it leaves the CBC region, matching the oscillations reported by its authors (§4.4, Fig. 6). **Shown**
15. LDLR with $q=1$ lies outside the CBC region, matching its oscillations; with $q=2$ it is diffusive (§4.5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
16. Normalised THINC has a closed form (Eq. 4.33); with $\beta_{\mathrm T}=1.0$ it lies below P3 (diffusive), with $\beta_{\mathrm T}=1.3$ above P3 near $1/2$ (anti-diffusive, "as the SuperBee TVD limiter"), and with $\beta_{\mathrm T}=1.1$ it passes close to $(1/2,3/4)$, "almost" second order; consistent with $\beta_{\mathrm T}<1.1$ diffusive and $\beta_{\mathrm T}>1.2$ compressive in earlier work (§4.6, Fig. 8). **Derived/Shown**
17. The UND (§5, Fig. 9): accuracy point and P3 line; diffusion region below P3 and anti-diffusion region above; a CBC-TVD region whose left boundary $\tilde u_{i+1/2}=k\tilde u_i$ restricts the CFL number, "a safe choice for the CFL number is CFL < 1/k"; an ENO region; a high-resolution region. It applies to conservative, primitive or characteristic variables. **Stated** (induced from §§3–4, not proved)
18. A UND-based ENO region is needed because "classical ENO constraints are not enough to ensure that reconstruction operators can achieve the ENO property or obtain the optimal accuracy" (§5 IV, §6.2). **Stated**
19. New TVD limiter $\psi(r)=(2r+4r^2)/(1+2r+3r^2)$ for $r\ge0$ (Eqs. 6.1–6.2): tangent to P3 at $\tilde u_i=1/2$ and to the left CBC-TVD boundary at 0, fully in the dissipative second-order TVD region; lower $L_1$ error than van Leer, van Albada, OSPRE, SuperBee and MinMod, van Leer slightly better in $L_\infty$, SuperBee "loses the accuracy as the grid is refined" (Fig. 11), better shape preservation than van Leer in 2D rotation (Fig. 12). **Derived** (tangency) / **Shown**
20. Proposition 6: if the nonlinear weight depends only on $\tilde u_i$, the WENO operator is normalisable and thus scale-invariant (§6.2, Eqs. 6.3–6.5). **Proved**
21. The new scale-invariant WENO (Eq. 6.6, parameters $m=1$, $n=2$ and a free parameter $=25$) keeps the scale-invariance error at machine rounding ($\approx10^{-16}$) for scalings $\lambda\in[10^{-19},10^{19}]$, whereas WENO-JS3 with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ depends clearly on $\lambda$, with $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ its error grows for $\lambda<10^{-10}$, and WENO-Z3 and WENO-P3 with $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ also show errors (Fig. 13, $N_x=320$). **Shown**
22. The new WENO is "more accurate than existing WENO schemes" (van Leer, WENO-Z3, WENO-P3, Fig. 14) and less dissipative than WENO-P on Sod, Lax, Shu–Osher and the blast wave (Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. Dissipative ROUND (Eqs. 7.1–7.3) lies fully in the dissipative region, is less dissipative than existing second-order shock-capturing schemes in the accuracy test (Fig. 17), improves dispersion in the approximate-dispersion-relation analysis (Fig. 18), shows no oscillations and resolves critical points better than WENO-P (Figs. 19–23), with "no remarkable difference" between the three formulations; on the 2D Riemann problem ($600\times600$) it resolves the main structures "with less grid number" than WENO-JS5, and on the double Mach reflection ($\Delta x=1/300$) it resolves the second contact. **Shown**
24. Low-dissipative ROUND (Eqs. 7.4–7.6) has spectral properties "close to the linear third-order scheme" (Fig. 28), better resolution than WENO-JS5 on the blast wave (Fig. 33), is "much less dissipative and less oscillatory" than WENO-JS5 on Leblanc (Fig. 34), and "comparable and even better" than WENO-JS5 on 2D Riemann and double Mach on a coarser mesh (Figs. 35–36). **Shown**
25. Remark 7.2: unlike BVD algorithms, which minimise the absolute dissipation error at a cell boundary, ROUND minimises the overall error in a cell by "introducing both positive values and negative values of the second order term" (§7.2). **Stated**
26. Remark 7.3: the parameters of the low-dissipative formulations (e.g. $5,300,800,20$ for Eq. 7.4) are already large; larger values "are not recommended". **Stated**
27. Fine-tuned ROUND (Eq. 7.7) is "fully in the TVD region", less dissipative and more shape-preserving than van Leer in the 2D rotation test (Fig. 37). **Shown**
28. The extension to conservative finite differences with flux splitting is "direct" (the normalised variable is built from the split flux, possibly in characteristic space); in a finite-difference immersed-boundary code the compact stencil needs fewer ghost nodes and "speeds up" the method; in the shock diffraction over a cylinder ($500\times500$) dissipative ROUND is comparable to WENO-JS5 and low-dissipative ROUND resolves structures WENO-JS5 needs a finer mesh for (§8, Fig. 38). **Shown** (the speed-up is **Stated**, no timings)
29. The CFL < 0.5 restriction from the TVD bound ($k=2$) is "overly stringent when the high-order SSP-RK time scheme is adopted"; four-stage second-order SSP-RK allows a larger CFL than three-stage third-order SSP-RK (§4.1, §5 III). **Stated**
