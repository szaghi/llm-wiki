# Summary

Fan et al. (2025a) propose HWENO-U, a fifth-order moment-based finite-volume
[Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) scheme with unified stencils, in 1D and 2D
on structured meshes. The unknowns are the cell average $\bar u_i$ and the first
normalized moment $\bar u^{(1)}_i$, both evolved by the $\mathbb P^1$ DG weak form.
The reconstruction on $I_i$ uses only $\bar u_{i-1},\bar u_i,\bar u_{i+1}$ and
$\bar u^{(1)}_{i-1},\bar u^{(1)}_{i+1}$, not the cell's own moment. The authors prove
by von Neumann analysis that this alone makes the linear scheme unstable for every
time step, with forward Euler or SSP-RK3. Following the Lax–Friedrichs idea of
replacing $u_i^n$ by a neighbour average, they replace $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ by its HWENO
modification $\hat u^{(1)}_i$, the first moment of the reconstructed polynomial,
in the [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) stages only. The linear
scheme is then stable for $\Delta t/\Delta x\lesssim0.824$.

The efficiency gain is that one HWENO procedure (a quartic Hermite polynomial on the
big stencil, two linear polynomials in 1D and four in 2D, their
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and nonlinear weights)
gives both the quadrature values and $\hat u^{(1)}_i$. The earlier HWENO-M of Zhao &
Qiu (2020) needs two sequential HWENO procedures on different stencils. The linear
weights are any positive numbers summing to one, in the WENO-ZQ manner. The weights
are made [scale-invariant](/properties/scale-invariance.md) by a local average
$\mathsf m$ that normalizes the global indicator, the $\beta_k$ and $\epsilon$,
extending Don et al. (2022), who normalized only $\epsilon$.

The tests show fifth order on [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) and
[density-wave advection](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md) in 1D and 2D, a
smaller error × CPU product than HWENO-M and WENO-ZQ, and a CPU cost about 50% above
WENO-ZQ. The shock tests are the [Lax tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
the [blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md), 1D and 2D
[Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md), the
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), the
[forward step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) and a
[Mach 2000 jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md). They resolve as well as
or better than HWENO-M, and better than WENO-ZQ. Runs on data scaled by $10^{\pm7}$
show that the new weights remove the oscillations and the extra dissipation of the
original ones.

# Key claims

Registry notation. Page-local: $\bar u^{(1)}_i=\frac1{\Delta x}\int_{I_i}u\,\frac{x-x_i}{\Delta x}\,dx$
the first normalized moment (in 2D also $\bar u^{(1,y)}_{i,j}$ in $y$),
$\hat u^{(1)}_i$ its HWENO modification; $p_0$ the quartic Hermite
polynomial on the big stencil and $p_k$ ($k=1,2$; $1,\dots,4$ in 2D) the linear
candidates; $\tau_{\mathrm{ZQ}}=\big(\tfrac12(\lvert\beta_0-\beta_1\rvert+\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert)\big)^2$
the WENO-ZQ global indicator; $\mathsf m$ the mean of $\lvert\bar u\rvert$ on
the stencil plus $\mu_0=10^{-40}$; $\sigma$ the scale factor of the data
($u\mapsto\sigma u$, §10 of the [notation](/notation.md)); $\lambda=\Delta t/\Delta x$
for unit speed (see the Notation map).

1. HWENO-U uses a single HWENO reconstruction on unified stencils that yields both the point values and $\hat u^{(1)}_i$; earlier moment-based HWENO schemes, e.g. HWENO-M, need two stencil sets and two HWENO procedures (Abstract, §1, Rem. 2.3, Fig. 1). **Stated**
2. Thm 2.1: for $u_t+u_x=0$, the linear reconstruction from $\bar u_{i-1},\bar u_i,\bar u_{i+1},\bar u^{(1)}_{i-1},\bar u^{(1)}_{i+1}$ gives a scheme that is linearly unstable for every $\lambda>0$, with forward Euler or SSP-RK3; e.g. $\rho(G_1)\approx1+3.158\times10^{-12}$ at $\lambda=10^{-12}$ (Eqs. 2.25–2.31, Fig. 3a). **Proved** (spectral radius evaluated numerically)
3. Thm 2.2: with the modified SSP-RK3 (2.13), in which $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ is replaced by $\hat u^{(1)}_i$ in each stage, the necessary stability condition is $0<\lambda\lesssim0.824$, found by sampling $10^4$ values of $\vartheta\in[0,2\pi]$ (Eq. 2.33, Fig. 3b). **Proved** (spectral radius evaluated numerically)
4. The moment modification acts like the Lax–Friedrichs averaging that stabilizes the forward-time centred scheme (§1, Eq. 1.1; §4). **Derived** (by analogy, then by Thm 2.2)
5. The weights (2.11), (2.22), $\alpha_k=d_k\big(1+\tau_{\mathrm{ZQ}}/(\beta_k\mathsf m^2+\epsilon \mathsf m^4)\big)$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, give the same $\omega_k$ for $u$ and $\sigma u$ when $\mu_0=0$ (§2.1). **Derived**
6. The original WENO-ZQ and HWENO-M weight is the case $\mathsf m=1$; for $\sigma u$ it becomes $d_k\big(1+\sigma^4\tau_{\mathrm{ZQ}}/(\sigma^2\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)$, so it depends on the scale (Rem. 2.1). **Derived**
7. Adding $\mathsf m$ does not destroy accuracy or resolution, since $\mathsf m=O(1)$; when the three cell averages vanish the weights fall back to the linear ones (Rem. 2.1). **Stated**
8. The scale-dependent weights produce overshoots and undershoots in the Lax problem (WENO-ZQ, Fig. 8), and oscillations at small scale and extra dissipation at large scale in the scaled Lax problem (Fig. 9) (Ex. 3.5). **Shown**
9. HWENO-U, HWENO-M and WENO-ZQ reach fifth order on 1D Burgers (Table 1), 1D Euler (Table 3), 2D Burgers (Table 4) and 2D Euler (Table 6); the first moments of HWENO-U are fifth order too (Tables 2, 5). **Shown**
10. Ex. 3.1 (meshes $\ge200$): the CPU ratio HWENO-U/WENO-ZQ is about 1.516 for an $L^1$ error ratio about 1/12.800; HWENO-M/WENO-ZQ about 2.319 for about 1/11.777 (§3.1). **Shown**
11. Ex. 3.2: CPU ratio HWENO-U/WENO-ZQ about 1.711, $L^1$ ratio about 1/11.871; HWENO-M/WENO-ZQ about 2.488 and 1/11.904. **Shown**
12. Ex. 3.3 (2D Burgers): CPU ratio HWENO-U/WENO-ZQ about 1.312, $L^1$ ratio about 1/59.905; HWENO-M/WENO-ZQ about 1.601 and 1/75.842. **Shown**
13. Ex. 3.4 (2D Euler): CPU ratio HWENO-U/WENO-ZQ about 1.463, $L^1$ ratio about 1/59.034; HWENO-M/WENO-ZQ about 1.547 and 1/76.366. The HWENO cost rises by no more than 55% over WENO-ZQ despite two extra moment equations, because the interface fluxes are reused (§3.1). **Shown**
14. In 2D HWENO-U and HWENO-M have similar errors and CPU times, because HWENO-M modifies the moments dimension by dimension, which is hard to extend to unstructured meshes; HWENO-U uses truly 2D reconstructions and suits unstructured meshes (Ex. 3.4, §4). **Stated**
15. The total CPU times of Examples 3.5–3.13 (Table 7) always put HWENO-U between WENO-ZQ and HWENO-M, e.g. double Mach reflection $6.339\times10^4$ s (HWENO-U), $7.582\times10^4$ s (HWENO-M), $5.181\times10^4$ s (WENO-ZQ); Fortran 95 on an Intel Xeon Gold 6130 at 2.10 GHz (§3). **Shown**
16. Blast waves (Ex. 3.6): HWENO-U resolves better than HWENO-M and WENO-ZQ (Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. Shu–Osher (Ex. 3.7): HWENO-U and WENO-ZQ are similar and both better than HWENO-M (Fig. 11). **Shown**
18. Double rarefaction (Ex. 3.8): all three schemes work without positivity limiters (Fig. 12). **Shown**
19. 1D and 2D Sedov (Ex. 3.9, 3.12) and the Mach 2000 jet (Ex. 3.13) need the positivity-preserving limiter; in 2D Sedov HWENO-M and WENO-ZQ with the original weights oscillate even with the limiter, while HWENO-U and HWENO-M with the scale-invariant weights (2.22) do not (Fig. 17). **Shown**
20. The double Mach reflection with data scaled by $\sigma\in\{10^{-7},1,10^7\}$ gives consistent results with the weights (2.22) (Fig. 15). **Shown**
21. OF-HWENO (Zhao & Qiu 2024) also uses unified stencils and reaches sixth order with less storage, but its damping parameter is empirical and problem dependent, and stiff damping in extreme problems (1D Sedov) forces small steps or exponential Runge–Kutta (Rem. 2.2, §1). **Stated**
22. A positivity-preserving limiter is needed for HWENO-U and HWENO-M in some extreme 2D problems; without it negative densities or pressures arise (§3). **Stated**
23. All tests use CFL number 0.6 (§3), below the linear bound 0.824 of Thm 2.2. **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (2.9)**, first term: printed $(c_{0,1}+\frac14c_{0,4})^2$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* evaluating $\sum_{l=1}^4\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}(d^lp_0/d\xi^l)^2d\xi$ for $p_0=\sum_lc_{0,l}\xi^l$ gives $(c_{0,1}+\frac14c_{0,3})^2+\frac{13}3(c_{0,2}+\frac{63}{130}c_{0,4})^2+\frac{781}{20}c_{0,3}^2+\frac{1421461}{2275}c_{0,4}^2$. The index of the first square is 3, not 4: $\int\xi^2d\xi=1/12$ couples $c_{0,1}$ with $c_{0,3}$, and $\int\xi^3d\xi=0$ leaves no $c_{0,1}c_{0,4}$ term. The other three terms agree.
- **§2.3, after Eq. (2.25):** printed "$u^-_{i+1/2}=\frac{269}{456}\bar u_{i-1}+\frac{7}{12}\bar u_i-\frac{79}{456}\bar u_{i+1}+\frac{177}{76}\bar v_{i-1}+\frac{63}{76}\bar v_{i+1}$". *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* from the Appendix-A coefficients this is $u^+_{i-1/2}=p_0(x_{i-1/2})$; the value at $x_{i+1/2}$ is $u^-_{i+1/2}=-\frac{79}{456}\bar u_{i-1}+\frac7{12}\bar u_i+\frac{269}{456}\bar u_{i+1}-\frac{63}{76}\bar u^{(1)}_{i-1}-\frac{177}{76}\bar u^{(1)}_{i+1}$. The printed matrices $\mathbf A$–$\mathbf D$ are consistent with the correct value, so the slip is confined to the display.
- **Table 6:** the HWENO-M CPU time at $120\times120$ is printed "1.30E+03", smaller than its value at $80\times80$ (2.29E+03), while the HWENO-U and WENO-ZQ times grow with the mesh (1.43E+04 and 8.83E+03 at $120\times120$).
- **Table 4:** the HWENO-M $L^\infty$ errors and orders at $160^2$, $200^2$, $240^2$ (7.18E−08, 2.38E−08, 9.52E−09; 5.02, 4.95, 5.02) are identical to those printed for HWENO-U at the same meshes, while the $L^1$ errors differ.
- **Ex. 3.11:** the domain is printed $[0,0.6]\times[0,1]\cup[0.6,1]\times[0.2,1]$, of length 1, but the uniform $960\times320$ mesh has aspect ratio 3:1.
- **Eq. (2.11):** $u_{ave}=\frac{\sum_{l=1}^3|\bar u_l|}3+\mu_0$ indexes the stencil cells $l=1,2,3$ while the 1D text uses $i-1,i,i+1$. **Eq. (2.23):** "$\sum_{m=1}^4\omega_np_m(x,y)$", index $n$ for $m$.
