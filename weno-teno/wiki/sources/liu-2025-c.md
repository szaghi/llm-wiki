# Summary

Liu et al. (2025c) build an arbitrarily high-order nodal
[DG](/methods/dgsem.md) method on Cartesian meshes for 2D ideal MHD. It
discretizes the conservative form directly, not the Godunov-symmetrized form
with the non-conservative source $\phi_{\mathrm G}'(w)^\top\,\nabla\cdot B$, and it is both
[globally divergence-free](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) (GDF)
and [entropy stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md). The cell scheme is the
Chen–Shu entropy-stable collocation DG on $N+1$ Gauss–Lobatto nodes, with
[SBP operators](/operators/summation-by-parts-operator.md), the
Chandrashekar–Klingenberg [entropy-conservative flux](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md)
in [flux-differencing](/methods/flux-differencing.md) form, and an
[HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) surface flux with the 3-wave speed
estimates of Bouchut, Klingenberg and Waagan. Theorem 1 is the key step. If the
nodal field is divergence-free at every node and its normal component is
continuous across faces, the Godunov source contributions cancel exactly, and the
conservative scheme is entropy conservative in each cell and entropy stable
globally.

To supply such a field, the normal components are evolved on faces as univariate
polynomials of degree $N-1$ by 1D DG weak forms of the induction equation, with a
2D HLL vertex electric field, after Balsara, Kumar and Chandrashekar
([constrained transport](/methods/constrained-transport.md) in DG form). A new
least-squares reconstruction then rebuilds the interior $\mathbb Q^N$ field from
the face data: it minimizes the mass-weighted distance to the DG-evolved field
under the divergence and trace constraints, solved through an SVD row reduction
and a KKT system. A nodal energy correction keeps the pressure, hence the entropy,
unchanged; this gives up local conservation of total energy. For strong shocks a
scaling limiter $\theta=\exp(-\sigma_K\Delta t)$, inspired by
[OEDG](/methods/oedg.md), shrinks the nodal values towards the cell mean. Face
moments are scaled with the smaller factor of the two neighbours, so face averages
and the cell-average constraint survive. Time stepping is Ketcheson's 10-stage
fourth-order [SSP-RK](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md).

Tests are the [MHD vortex](/benchmarks/magnetized-vortex.md), a rotated
[Brio–Wu tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md), the
[field loop](/benchmarks/field-loop-advection.md), MHD
[Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md), the rotor and
[blast wave](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) and the
[shock–cloud interaction](/benchmarks/mhd-shock-cloud-interaction.md). They are
compared with the earlier entropy-stable nodal DG of Liu, Shu and Zhang (2018)
for the symmetrized system.

# Key claims

Registry notation: DG degree $N=k+1$ with $N+1$ Gauss–Lobatto nodes $\xi_j$ and
weights $\mathsf w_j$; $M$, $D$, $Q=MD$, $B$ the SBP matrices; $u$ the conserved
variables, $E$ the total energy, $\eta=-\rho s/(\gamma-1)$ with fluxes
$F^{(1)}_\eta,F^{(2)}_\eta$, $w=\eta'(u)$, $\psi$ the flux potentials; $f^{\mathrm{ec}}$
the two-point volume flux; $s^\pm$ the HLL speeds; $\mathcal E_z$ the electric
field. Page-local: $\phi_{\mathrm G}(w)=\rho\,(v\cdot B)/p$ the degree-one function whose gradient is the Godunov source vector (as on [Godunov\xe2\x80\x93Powell source term](/definitions/godunov-powell-source-term.md));
$b_1$, $b_2$ the face normal-field polynomials; $N_d$, $N_c$ unknowns and
conditions of the reconstruction; $\theta$ (the registry Zhang–Shu factor),
$\sigma_K$ and $c_{0,\mathrm L}$ the limiter factor, jump strength and user
constant.

1. The method is globally divergence-free, semi-discretely entropy stable per cell, and locally conservative in mass, momentum and magnetic field, but not in total energy; the energy error depends on the change of $B$ in the reconstruction step (§1). **Stated**; energy-error expression **Derived** (Remark 2).
2. The Godunov-modified system is equivalent to the conservative one only if $\nabla\cdot B=0$; entropy-stable schemes for the modified system with a non-conservative source can conserve mass but may fail to conserve momentum, total energy and magnetic field (§3, citing Chandrashekar–Klingenberg, Liu–Shu–Zhang, Tóth, Wu–Shu). **Stated**
3. Without the GDF property the conservative nodal DG scheme (8) "cannot achieve the entropy stability" (§3.1, after Def. 3). **Stated**
4. Theorem 1: with periodic, compactly supported or reflective boundaries, if the nodal field is divergence-free at every node (Eq. (11)) and normal-continuous across faces (Eq. (12)), scheme (8) is entropy conservative in each element (Eq. (13)) and entropy stable on $\Omega$ (Eq. (14)) (§3.1, proof App. B). The entropy-conservative flux satisfies the MHD-modified Tadmor condition $[\![w]\!]\cdot f^{\mathrm{ec}}=[\![\psi]\!]-[\![\phi_{\mathrm G}]\!]\,\{\!\{B_1\}\!\}$ (Def. 2, Eq. (9)), with the potential defined in §2 as $\psi=w\cdot f-F_\eta$. **Proved**
5. At a reflective wall the HLL flux with $s^+=-s^-=s$ reduces to Lax–Friedrichs and the wall entropy production is $-2(\rho/p)\,\rho v_1^2s\le0$ (App. B). **Proved**
6. Reconstruction existence: $N_d=2(N+1)^2$ unknowns against $N_c=4N+(N+1)^2-2$ independent conditions, so $N_d\ge N_c$ and a non-unique reconstruction exists; face polynomials of degree $N$ instead of $N-1$ could give $N_c>N_d$, which is why degree $N-1$ is chosen (§3.3). **Stated** (counting argument; see Printed slips)
7. The KKT system (25) has a unique solution because the reduced constraint matrix $A_1$ has full row rank (§3.3). **Stated**
8. For $N=1$ the reconstruction system is solvable iff the cell-average constraint holds, and its unique solution (C.2) takes each face's constant on the two nodes of that face, consistent with Balsara et al. (App. C). **Proved** (explicit computation)
9. The cell-average constraint $\Delta y(\bar b_1^{i+1/2,j}-\bar b_1^{i-1/2,j})+\Delta x(\bar b_2^{i,j+1/2}-\bar b_2^{i,j-1/2})=0$ (Eq. (21)) is kept in time by the face updates (16)–(17) if it holds initially (§3.2, citing Balsara et al.). **Stated**
10. Scaling all nodal values of a cell towards the weighted mean with one $\theta\in[0,1]$ does not increase the discrete cell entropy (Lemma 3, recalled from Chen–Shu; Jensen's inequality). **Stated**
11. The face-moment limiter keeps the face averages, hence Eq. (21), hence a GDF field can still be reconstructed after limiting; the entropy is kept by the energy correction (§4.1). **Derived**
12. The energy correction (27), $E^{n+1}=E^*+\tfrac12(\lvert B^{n+1}\rvert^2-\lvert B^*\rvert^2)$ at each node, keeps the nodal pressure, hence the entropy, unchanged by the reconstruction; without it there is no guarantee that the total entropy does not increase (Remark 2). **Derived**
13. The per-cell energy conservation error is $\sum_{j,l}\tfrac18\mathsf w_j\mathsf w_l\big(\lvert B^{n+1}_{jl}\rvert^2-\lvert B^*_{jl}\rvert^2\big)$, small to high order if the reconstruction is high-order accurate (Remark 2). **Derived**
14. With $N+1$ Gauss–Lobatto points the truncation error is of order $N$ at each collocation point (§3.1, from Chen–Shu). **Stated**
15. MHD vortex at $T=20$, $L^2$ errors for $N=2,3,4$ on $32^2$ to $256^2$: finest-mesh orders $N=2$: 2.48 ($\rho$), 2.29 ($\rho v_1$), 2.57 ($B_1$), 2.35 ($E$); $N=3$: 3.09, 3.03, 2.91, 3.05; $N=4$: 4.47, 4.21, 4.00, 4.33 (Table 1); the authors say the schemes "maintain the designed accuracy". **Shown**
16. Smooth vortex: both ES and ES-GDF fail to conserve total energy "at the same level"; ES-GDF keeps the magnetic-field conservation error at machine accuracy, ES does not (Fig. 2, $N=3$, $64^2$ and $128^2$). **Shown**
17. Rotated Brio–Wu (angle $\arctan0.5$, $512\times2$, $T=0.2/\sqrt5$): without the entropy-stable treatment the standard nodal DG and the GDF nodal DG "break down in the first several steps"; without limiter ES-GDF is more stable, while ES is unstable in $0.45<x<0.62$, which the authors attribute to the non-constant parallel field (Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
18. Field loop ($240\times120$, $T=2$, no limiter): ES-GDF captures the features better than ES; total entropy does not increase and the divergence norm stays near machine error (Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
19. MHD Kelvin–Helmholtz ($256\times512$, $N=2,3,4$, $T=5,12,20$): ES-GDF runs without any limiter; non-ES DG and ES DG without GDF blow up without limiter; higher $N$ gives faster growth of the poloidal magnetic energy in the transition, read by the authors as smaller numerical dissipation (Figs. 7–9). **Shown**
20. Rotor: no Mach-number distortion at $T=0.295$; ES-GDF conserves momentum and magnetic field, dissipates entropy and stays GDF ($400\times400$; Figs. 10–12). **Shown**
21. MHD blast (plasma beta $\beta_{\mathrm p}\approx2.51\times10^{-4}$, $200\times200$, $T=0.01$): with the limiter, $c_{0,\mathrm L}\ge0.08$ appears adequate and smaller values may fail; the pressure stays non-negative without any positivity limiter, minimum about 0.0952 at the final time (Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
22. Cloud–shock interaction resolved without negative pressure at $T=0.06$ on $600\times600$, matching earlier results (Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. The limiter constant $c_{0,\mathrm L}\in[0.01,0.1]$ in all tests; "its selection has no significant impact on most of the tests and only slightly affects the solution resolution" (§4.1). **Stated** (no sensitivity study beyond the blast remark)
24. The TVB limiter with characteristic decomposition cannot guarantee entropy non-increase (§4.1, citing Liu–Shu–Zhang). **Stated**
25. HLL with the Bouchut–Klingenberg–Waagan 3-wave relaxation speeds is entropy stable; HLL and LF are entropy stable if $s^-$ and $s^+$ bound the true extreme speeds (App. A.2, citing Bouchut et al. and Chen–Shu). **Stated**

App. C prints the constraint matrix sizes $12\times8$, $21\times18$, $32\times32$, $45\times50$ for $N=1,\dots,4$, the KKT matrix sizes $35\times35$, $60\times60$, $91\times91$ for $N=2,3,4$, and an $8\times8$ full-rank $A_1$ for $N=1$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the KKT size is $N_d+\operatorname{rank}A_1$, so the printed sizes imply ranks 8, 17, 28, 41 for $N=1,\dots,4$; the rank of the constraint matrix built from Eq.
