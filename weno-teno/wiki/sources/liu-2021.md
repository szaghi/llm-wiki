# Summary

The paper builds a cell-centred finite-volume scheme of second and third
order for 2D ideal MHD with the Godunov–Powell source term
$-(\nabla\cdot B)\,(0,B,v,v\cdot B)^\top$. Its reconstruction is
[WLS-ENO](/methods/wls-eno.md) (Liu & Jiao 2016): a single-stencil weighted
least-squares fit whose row weights $W_j=1/[(\bar u_j-\bar u_i)^2+\epsilon h_i^2]$
shrink the influence of neighbours across a discontinuity. Two changes are
made. The polynomial is written in a zero-mean basis, so that its cell
average equals $\bar u_i$ exactly (conservative WLS-ENO). The magnetic field
is then reconstructed **locally divergence-free** (LDF): $B_2$ is fitted
first, the coefficients of $B_1$ that enter $\partial_xB_1$ are fixed by
matching the coefficients of $\partial_xB_1+\partial_yB_2\equiv0$ (a
nonsingular upper-triangular system for any degree $n$), and the remaining
coefficients come from a reduced least-squares fit. The roles of $B_1$ and
$B_2$ are then swapped and the two LDF fields averaged. This addresses the
[divergence-free magnetic field](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md)
constraint inside each cell only; the normal-field jump across faces remains
and is controlled by the Powell term.

The reconstruction is placed in the positivity-preserving framework of Wu &
Shu (2019) ([bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md)): an
[HLL flux](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md), the Powell term upwinded with the
HLL speeds, a [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md)
on density and pressure, and [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)
stepping (SSP-RK2 for $n=1$, SSP-RK3 for $n=2$). Least-squares systems are
solved by truncated SVD. No other limiter is used: the
[ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) is claimed for the WLS weights,
not proved. The tests, on uniform Cartesian grids only, are a smooth Alfvén
wave (orders about 2 and 3), the
[Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md), the rotor and the
low-$\beta_{\mathrm p}$ blast wave of the
[MHD blast wave and rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) page, and a Mach 800
dense MHD jet ([high-Mach astrophysical jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md)),
which fails for $n=2$ unless the SVD truncation threshold is raised. No CFL
number is printed anywhere.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's WLS row weights
$\omega_j$ are written $W_j$ (they weight least-squares
rows, not candidate polynomials), its HLL speeds $\sigma^\pm$ are $s^\pm$, its
edge-quadrature weights $\omega_q$ are $\mathsf w_q$, its plasma beta is
$\beta_{\mathrm p}$; $n$ is the reconstruction degree. These are the symbols of the
[WLS-ENO](/methods/wls-eno.md) page, which also writes the field components $B_1$, $B_2$.

1. WLS-ENO computes each row weight once from cell averages, $W_j=1/[(\bar u_j-\bar u_i)^2+\epsilon h_i^2]$, $\epsilon=10^{-2}$, $h_i=\sqrt{|A_i|}$; it has higher accuracy than WENO near discontinuities and is less sensitive to mesh quality (§1, §2.3). **Stated** (cited from Liu & Jiao 2016)
2. The central-cell weight is $W_i=\alpha_{\mathrm c}\max_jW_j$ over edge neighbours, $\alpha_{\mathrm c}>1$ (e.g. 1.5); $W_j=O(1/h_i^2)$ in smooth regions and $O(1)$ across a discontinuity (§2.3). **Stated**
3. Writing the reconstruction in the basis $(x-x_i)^{p_1}(y-y_i)^{p_2}-\overline{x^{p_1}y^{p_2}}_i$ conserves the cell average exactly; the conservative system (12) eliminates the constant term and leaves $N_s\times(n_{2D}(n)-1)$ unknowns, $N_s$ the number of stencil cells (§3.1–3.2, Eqs. 9–12). **Derived**
4. Componentwise reconstruction does not satisfy $\partial_xB_1^R+\partial_yB_2^R\equiv0$ (Eq. 16); for $n=2$ the 3×3 system (17) gives $\partial_xB_1|_i$, $\partial_x^2B_1|_i$, $\partial_{xy}B_1|_i$ from the $B_2$ coefficients (§3.1). **Derived**
5. The remaining coefficients $\partial_yB_1|_i$, $\tfrac12\partial_y^2B_1|_i$ come from the reduced weighted least-squares problem (18), with weights built from the $B_1$ cell averages (§3.1). **Derived**
6. The one-round construction (only $B_1$ constrained) gives "slightly higher" $B_1$ than $B_2$ errors on the smooth Alfvén wave; the two-round average (19)–(20) removes the bias and is slightly more stable (§3.1). **Shown** (asserted from tests, no data printed)
7. The averaged reconstruction is LDF and "retains ENO property" (§3.1, §5). **Stated** (LDF follows by linearity; the ENO property is not proved)
8. For general degree $n$ the coefficient-matching system has size $n_{2D}(n-1)\times n_{2D}(n-1)$, $n_{2D}(n-1)=n(n+1)/2$, and is upper triangular with diagonal entries $1/((p_1-1)!\,p_2!)$, hence uniquely solvable; the remaining fit is an $N_s\times(n_{2D}(n)-n_{2D}(n-1)-1)$ weighted least-squares problem (§3.2, Eqs. 21–26). **Derived**
9. The least-squares matrices can become ill-conditioned under refinement; truncated SVD (threshold $\sigma_1\times10^{-10}$ unless stated, LAPACK DGESVD) gives a minimum-norm solution and "has good numerical stability in our tests" (§3.1, §3.3.2). **Stated** / **Shown**
10. Stencils should be balanced around the cell, with about 1.5–2 times as many cells as unknown coefficients; larger stencils "seem to be more stable". On Cartesian grids the 1/2-ring (4 edge neighbours) is used for $n=1$ and the 1-ring (8 vertex neighbours) for $n=2$ (§3.3.1). **Stated**
11. With an LDF reconstruction, the interior term of the split discrete divergence (33) vanishes analytically by Gauss's theorem when the edge quadrature has $2N_g-1\ge n$ (Eq. 34); in floating point it does not vanish and "may lead to a failed simulation", so the code omits it and uses Eq. (35) (§3.3.3). **Derived** (plus an implementation choice)
12. The remaining interface term of the discrete divergence (the normal-field jump) is "globally controlled by the Godunov–Powell source term" (§3.3.3). **Stated**
13. The positivity limiter (36)–(38) scales the reconstructed density and thermal-pressure polynomials, $\tilde u^R_i=\theta(u^R_i-\bar u_i)+\bar u_i$, with floor $\epsilon_{\min}=\min\{10^{-13},\bar u_i\}$ on a Gauss ⊗ Gauss–Lobatto point set, $Q=1,L=2$ for $n=1$ and $Q=2,L=3$ for $n=2$ (§3.3.4). **Stated** (background from Zhang–Shu and Wu–Shu)
14. Smooth Alfvén wave ($t=2$, angle $\pi/4$, $\gamma=5/3$, $16^2$ to $256^2$): $L_1(v_x)$ orders 2.09, 1.99, 2.43, 2.38 for $n=1$ and 2.88, 3.00, 2.82, 3.02 for $n=2$; $L_1(v_x)$ at $256^2$ is $3.7754\times10^{-5}$ ($n=1$) and $1.5896\times10^{-6}$ ($n=2$) (§4.1, Table 1). **Shown**
15. On 64 cores, $256^2$ takes 580.81 s for $n=1$ and 6450.53 s for $n=2$; below an error of about $10^{-3.6}$, $n=2$ is cheaper at equal error (§4.1, Table 1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
16. Orszag–Tang ($t=3$, $200^2$): shocks sharp, divergence norm controlled, relative total-energy deviation within $10^{-5}$ for $n=2$ over $0\le t\le3$ (§4.2, Figs. 5–8). **Shown**
17. Rotor ($t=0.295$): no negative pressure, no distortion of the central Mach-number contours (attributed in the literature to divergence error), divergence norm below $10^{-3}$ for $n=1,2$; wall times 333.94 s ($n=1$, $200^2$), 3731.19 s ($n=2$, $200^2$) and 8787.56 s ($n=1$, $600^2$, 16 cores), "which highlights the superiority of higher-order methods" (§4.3, Figs. 9–12). **Shown**
18. Blast wave ($\beta_{\mathrm p}\approx2.51\times10^{-4}$, $t=0.01$, $200^2$): no negative pressure; density "significantly smoother" than in Balsara–Spicer, Christlieb et al. and Wu–Shu (§4.4, Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. Mach 800 dense MHD jet ($\beta_{\mathrm p}=10^{-4}$, $200\times300$, to $t=0.002$): density and pressure stay positive for $n=1,2$; for $n=2$ the pressure turns negative at $t=4.45\times10^{-4}$ with SVD threshold $\sigma_1\times10^{-10}$ and the run succeeds with $\sigma_1\times10^{-6}$, the failure "possibly attributed to ill-conditioned matrix" (§4.5, Figs. 14–17). **Shown**
20. No limiter beyond the positivity limiter is used in any test: the scheme "can keep both the divergence-free constraint and ENO property for the magnetic field without using any limiter" (Abstract, §4). **Shown** (qualitatively)
21. Spatial order: $N_g=1$ Gauss point per edge for $n=1$ (second order), $N_g=2$ for $n=2$ (third order) (§3.3.4, §4). **Stated**
22. LDF does not imply globally divergence-free: a globally divergence-free scheme would also make the normal component continuous, so that the Powell term vanishes; this, and a 3D extension for solar-wind modelling, is future work (§5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §3.2, p. 6: "conservative WLE-ENO scheme" for WLS-ENO.
- §3.1, p. 6: "slightly stable than the only one round implementation" (more stable), and "these three reconstructed methods" where only the one-round and the two-round averaged procedures are described.
- Eq. (17) solves for $\partial_x^2B_1|_i$ while the text after it says it gives "$\tfrac12\partial_x^2B_1|_i$".
- The SVD threshold is $\sigma_1$ (subscript) in Eq. (29) and "$\sigma^1\times10^{-6}$", "$\sigma^1\times10^{-10}$" (superscript) in §4.5.
- §4.5: the jet domain is printed $[0,0.5]\times[0,1.5]$ while the nozzle is $-0.05<x<0.05$ at $y=0$, on the edge of that domain; either the domain is misprinted (Wu & Shu use $[-0.5,0.5]\times[0,1.5]$) or a half domain with an unstated symmetry boundary is used.
- §4.2 cites "Orszag-Tang (1979)" without a reference number; the original paper is not in the reference list.
- §5: "rotor vortex problem".
- Ref. [22] (Waagan 2009): pages "8609–9626"; ref. [40] (Christlieb et al.): "SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 37 (4) (2014)", a volume–year mismatch as printed.
