# Summary

The paper extends the high-order [entropy-stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md)
[adaptive moving-mesh](/methods/adaptive-moving-mesh.md) finite-difference schemes
of Duan and Tang, first built for special relativistic (M)HD, to the 2D and 3D
single- and two-component compressible Euler equations
([multicomponent Euler equations](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md))
closed by the [stiffened-gas equation of state](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md).
The equations are written in conservative form in time-dependent curvilinear
coordinates, together with the volume and surface geometric conservation laws.
The scheme is a [conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
on point values in computational space.

The construction has four steps. (i) A sufficient condition (Prop. 3.1) on a
curvilinear two-point flux for an entropy-conservative semi-discretization. The
LeFloch–Mercier–Rohde linear combination gives a $2n_{\mathrm o}$th-order flux, and the same
combination applied to the metric terms gives discrete geometric conservation
laws, hence [free-stream preservation](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md).
(ii) Explicit symmetric [entropy-conservative fluxes](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md)
for one and two species with the stiffened gas, built on logarithmic means of the
densities and of $1/T$, and rotated to arbitrary directions. (iii) An
entropy-stable flux of [TeCNO](/methods/tecno-scheme.md) type. It adds
dissipation on jumps of the scaled entropy variables reconstructed by fifth-order
[multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md)
([WENO-AO family](/methods/weno-ao.md)), whose
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) are given in closed
form. A sign switch zeroes the components that violate the sign property, and
explicit entropy-scaled eigenvectors are given for
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md).
(iv) Variational mesh redistribution with a gradient monitor, plus third-order
[SSP-RK](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) time stepping.

There are 11 tests: the 2D and 3D [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md),
a stiffened-gas and a spherical [shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
two [2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md), a
two-material triple-point problem, and 2D and 3D
[shock–bubble interactions](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md). The scheme
reaches fifth order on moving meshes. The entropy-conservative variant keeps the
total entropy constant, and the entropy-stable variant decays it. The moving-mesh
runs match or beat uniform meshes two to three times finer at 18–41 % of their CPU
time, and the multi-resolution dissipation resolves more structure than
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) "with a slight increase in the
computational cost" *(compiler check, not in the sources: 0.1 % to 12 % in
the four runs of Tables 5.1–5.2 that compare both)*. Prop. 3.1 and
the entropy inequality are quoted from Duan and Tang; no proof is printed.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the direction index $k$ of the computational coordinates
$\xi_k$ kept: $f^{(k)}$ is the flux, $w$ the entropy variables, $F_\eta^{(k)}$ the
entropy flux, $\phi=w\cdot u-\eta$ the entropy potential, $\psi^{(k)}=w\cdot f^{(k)}-F^{(k)}_\eta$,
$N_s$ the number of species, $\gamma_\ell$ the species adiabatic indices and
$\mathsf R_\ell$ the gas constants (see the Notation map).

1. A consistent two-point flux $\hat f^{(k)}$ that satisfies the jump condition (3.5), $[\![w]\!]\cdot\hat f^{(k)}=\{\!\{J\partial_t\xi_k\}\!\}[\![\phi]\!]+\sum_j\{\!\{J\partial_{x_j}\xi_k\}\!\}[\![\psi^{(j)}]\!]$, makes scheme (3.1) entropy-conservative (Prop. 3.1, §3.1). The proof is deferred to the analogous result of Duan and Tang. **Stated**
2. If the two-point entropy-conservative flux is symmetric, the combination (3.6) with coefficients $c_n$ satisfying $\sum_nn\,c_n=1$ and $\sum_nn^{2s-1}c_n=0$ ($s=2,\dots,n_{\mathrm o}$), together with the same combination (3.7) of the metric terms, gives $2n_{\mathrm o}$th-order entropy-conservative schemes (3.1)–(3.2), with the numerical entropy flux printed after (3.7) (§3.1). **Stated**
3. Discretizing $J\partial_{x_j}\xi_k$ in the cross-product form (3.9) with the $2n_{\mathrm o}$th-order central operator $\delta^c_k$, and combining with (3.7), gives the discrete surface conservation laws (3.3) (§3.2). **Derived**
4. Computing $(J\partial_t\xi_k)_i=-\sum_j(\dot x_j)_i(J\partial_{x_j}\xi_k)_i$ (3.10) and combining with (3.7) gives the semi-discrete volume conservation law (3.2) (§3.2). **Derived**
5. The fully discrete schemes (3.1)–(3.2) with explicit SSP-RK3 preserve a free stream; "It is proved [14]" (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
6. For one species with the stiffened gas, $\eta=-\rho_1S_1$ with $S_1=c_{v,1}\ln T-\mathsf R_1\ln\rho_1$, and $F_\eta^{(k)}=\eta v_k$, is a convex entropy pair that symmetrizes the system; $\phi=\mathsf R_1\rho_1-p_{\infty,1}/T$ and $\psi^{(k)}=\phi v_k$ (3.13)–(3.14). **Stated** ("one can verify")
7. An explicit symmetric two-point entropy-conservative flux for the one-species stiffened gas (the state $u^{\mathrm{ec}}$ and the flux $f^{\mathrm{ec},(1)}$ on p. 10) follows from the jump identities in the parameter vector $z=(\rho_1,v,1/T)$ (§3.3.1). **Derived**
8. For two species, the mixture entropy is $\rho S=\sum_\ell\rho_\ell S_\ell$, $\eta=-\rho S$ and $\phi=\sum_\ell(\mathsf R_\ell\rho_\ell-p_{\infty,\ell}/T)$; explicit $u^{\mathrm{ec}}$ and $f^{\mathrm{ec},(1)}$ are printed (pp. 10–12). **Derived**
9. The entropy-conservative flux in an arbitrary direction is $f^{\mathrm{ec}}_{\varphi,\theta}(u^-,u^+)=\mathsf T^{-1}f^{\mathrm{ec},(1)}(\mathsf Tu^-,\mathsf Tu^+)$ (3.19). It satisfies the directional entropy-conservation condition because $w(\mathsf Tu)=\mathsf Tw(u)$ (§3.3.2, p. 13). **Derived**
10. The entropy-stable flux (3.21), the entropy-conservative flux minus $\tfrac12\mathsf T^{-1}\tilde R\lvert\tilde\Lambda\rvert\mathcal Y[\![\tilde w]\!]^{\mathrm{MR}}$, with the sign switch $\mathcal Y$, makes the scheme satisfy the semi-discrete entropy inequality (§3.4, following Duan and Tang, with the sign property of Biswas and Dubey). The proof is not reproduced. **Stated**
11. Entropy-stable moving-mesh schemes built on multi-resolution WENO "better capture the localized structures" and "outperform" those built on WENO-JS, "with a slight increase in the computational cost" (Remark 3.2; §5). **Shown**
12. For the multi-component equations the monitor function must carry more solution information than in the single-component case to give a good mesh (§1; Example 5.9 monitors five variables). **Stated** (illustrated, no ablation)
13. 2D isentropic vortex on a moving mesh: MM-WENOMR reaches fifth order in the $\ell^1$ and $\ell^\infty$ errors of $\rho_1$ at $t=4$ with $N_x=40$ to $320$. The entropy-conservative scheme keeps the total entropy almost constant; with the entropy-stable scheme it decays, to about $-4.2\times10^{-6}$ at $t=4$ for $N_x=160$ (Ex. 5.1, Fig. 5.2). **Shown**
14. 3D isentropic vortex: fifth order at $t=0.1$ with $N_x=40$ to $320$; the entropy-stable total entropy decays to about $-1\times10^{-7}$ by $t=0.1$ (Ex. 5.5, Fig. 5.6). **Shown**
15. Quasi-2D stiffened-gas shock tube ($100\times5$ cells): MM-WENOMR with $N_x=100$ is better than UM-WENOMR with $N_x=100$. It is better than MM-WENOJS near the contact, and better than UM-WENOMR with $N_x=300$ near the rarefaction head and the contact (Ex. 5.2, Fig. 5.3). **Shown**
16. 2D Riemann problem I, 4 cores: MM-WENOMR $200^2$ takes 2m56s, MM-WENOJS $200^2$ 2m37s, UM-WENOMR $200^2$ 28s and UM-WENOMR $500^2$ 7m9s. MM-WENOMR uses 41.0 % of the CPU time of UM-WENOMR $500^2$ and gives sharper shocks (Ex. 5.3, Table 5.1). **Shown**
17. 2D Riemann problem II, 4 cores: MM-WENOMR $200^2$ takes 2m50s, MM-WENOJS 2m38s, UM-WENOMR $200^2$ 36s and UM-WENOMR $600^2$ 13m10s. MM-WENOMR uses 21.5 % of UM-WENOMR $600^2$ and 7.6 % more than MM-WENOJS, with more small-scale roll-up (Ex. 5.4, Table 5.1). **Shown**
18. 3D spherical shock tube, 32 cores: MM-WENOMR $100^3$ takes 13m53s, MM-WENOJS $100^3$ 13m52s, UM-WENOMR $100^3$ 2m45s and UM-WENOMR $200^3$ 37m19s. MM-WENOMR uses 37.2 % of UM $200^3$, with comparable results (Ex. 5.6, Table 5.2). **Shown**
19. Triple-point problem, 32 cores: MM-WENOMR $350\times150$ takes 3m21s, MM-WENOJS 3m3s, UM-WENOMR $350\times150$ 49s and UM-WENOMR $1050\times450$ 18m7s. MM-WENOMR beats UM $1050\times450$ and MM-WENOJS and resolves the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up; the text gives 18.9 % and 9.8 % more (Ex. 5.7, Table 5.2; see Printed slips). **Shown**
20. 2D shock–helium bubble ($M_s=1.22$): MM-WENOMR $800\times160$ takes 1h4m, UM $800\times160$ 9m12s and UM $2400\times480$ 4h4m. MM-WENOMR uses 26.2 % of the fine uniform run "when it obtains even better results" (Ex. 5.8, Table 5.3). **Shown**
21. 2D Mach-10 shock and ideal-gas bubble in a stiffened gas: MM-WENOMR $800\times160$ takes 9m28s, UM $800\times160$ 1m47s and UM $2400\times480$ 40m11s (23.6 %) (Ex. 5.9, Table 5.3). **Shown**
22. 3D shock–helium bubble: MM-WENOMR $400\times80\times80$ takes 11h6m and is comparable to UM $800\times160\times160$, which takes 38h2m (29.2 %); UM $400\times80\times80$ takes 2h34m (Ex. 5.10, Table 5.3). **Shown**
23. 3D shock and ideal-gas bubble in a stiffened gas: MM-WENOMR $400\times80\times80$ takes 2h9m and beats UM $800\times160\times160$, which takes 11h58m (18.0 %); UM $400\times80\times80$ takes 46m32s (Ex. 5.11, Table 5.3). **Shown**
24. On the same cell count, a moving-mesh run costs 4.3 to 7 times a uniform-mesh run: 2m56s against 28s, 1h4m against 9m12s, 11h6m against 2h34m (Tables 5.1–5.3). *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: the paper does not state the ratio; the printed timings give 6.3, 7.0 and 4.3.
25. A larger linear weight on the five-point sub-stencil of multi-resolution WENO gives sharper transitions at discontinuities, but the solution "may tend to oscillate" (App. A, step 3). The default is $(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(1,10,100)/111$; $(0.95,0.045,0.005)$ is used in Ex. 5.3, 5.10 and 5.11. **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Ex. 5.7, p. 24: "MM-WENOMR with 350 × 150 cells only takes 18.9% CPU time of MM-WENOMR with 1050 × 450 cells". Table 5.2 has no MM run at $1050\times450$; the comparison is with UM-WENOMR. *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: 3m21s/18m7s $=201/1087=18.5\,\%$, not 18.9 %.
- Ex. 5.4, p. 21: the monitor function and linear weights are said to be "the same as those used in Example 5.2", but Example 5.2 states no linear weights; Example 5.3 is where $(0.95,0.045,0.005)$ is set.
- Ex. 5.9, p. 27: the monitored variable "$u$" in $(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_5)=(\Phi,u,\rho_1,\rho_2,p)$ is never defined; the velocity is $v$ throughout.
- Ex. 5.3, p. 19: the initial data are printed "(0.5313, 0, 0, 0.4))", with a doubled parenthesis.
- Appendix A reconstructs "for the given cell average values $\{W_j\}$" and evaluates $h_m(x_{j+1/2})$, although the scheme works on point values and $h_m$ are functions of $\xi$.
- §3.3.2 is headed $N=2$, but the rotation argument (3.19)–(3.20) is written for general $N$.
- The order $2n_{\mathrm o}$ of the entropy-conservative part is never stated.
- Typos: "Entropy stablity" (keywords); ref. [33] prints "arbitraty".
