# Summary

High-order shock-capturing schemes for steady Euler problems with strong shocks usually
stall: the residue hangs at the truncation-error level, because of small post-shock
oscillations and switching nonlinear weights. Zhu & Shu's [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md)
scheme WENO-ZQ (here "CWENOZ") and their [multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md)
(MRWENO) converge better, but they behave almost linearly near shocks and can oscillate. The
paper carries the authors' earlier unsteady [hybrid strategy](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) over to
steady computation. The base is a fifth-order
[conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md) scheme
with [Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md),
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) and
[SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) pseudo-time marching.

The scheme is named WENO-H. A detector $\mathfrak n=\tau_5/(\epsilon+\bar\beta)$, built from
the [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md)
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) and the [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)
$\tau_5$, acts as a [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) with
three regions. Smooth stencils use the linear fifth-order polynomial. Non-smooth stencils use
a CWENOZ-type reconstruction with a grid- and amplitude-dependent weight factor $C_R$, blended
with its linear counterpart so that the blended smoothness indicator stays bounded.
Transition stencils use a smooth weight $M_{\mathrm t}(\mathfrak n)$ between the two, and
the paper calls it essential for convergence.

[Approximate dispersion relations](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md) show WENO-H
with less [dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) and dispersion than CWENOZ,
MRWENO and WENO-JS. Fifth order is shown on 2D and 3D steady
[manufactured solutions](/definitions/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md) and on the
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md). Fourteen steady benchmarks follow,
among them a shock reflection, the [forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md),
the supersonic [flow past a cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md), flows past
plates and square columns, and a 3D plate. In all of them WENO-H reaches residues of
$10^{-12}$ to $10^{-14}$, while CWENOZ or MRWENO stall in several, and its profiles oscillate
less. [Mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md), [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md)-based detectors
and the [ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) appear only in passing. The only analysis
is an elementary bound on the blended smoothness indicator.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the WENO-JS stencils $S_0$ (most upwind), $S_1$, $S_2$ and
$\tau_5=\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert$. The page-local symbols are $\bar\beta=\frac13\sum_k\beta_k$,
the detector $\mathfrak n$, the CWENOZ polynomials $P_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (five cells),
$P_{\mathrm L}$ and $P_{\mathrm R}$ (two cells) with indicators $\beta_{\mathrm{opt}}$,
$\beta_{\mathrm L}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm R}$, and the blending quantities $C_R$, $W_1$,
$a_{\mathrm H}$, $Q_{\mathrm H}$, $K_{\mathrm H}$ and $M_{\mathrm t}$. See the Notation map.

1. Residues of classical and hybrid WENO schemes for steady problems with strong shocks usually hang at the truncation-error level, even after long iteration (§1). **Stated**
2. The mapped WENO of Henrick et al. costs "about 20 %" more CPU time (§1). **Stated**
3. The Zhu–Shu CWENO and MRWENO steady schemes "showed the lovely convergence property", but "some oscillations may appear near discontinuities because the schemes perform similarly to a linear scheme here" (§1). **Stated** (partly shown in Ex. 4.4 and 4.7–4.11)
4. WENO-ZQ belongs to the central WENO class, following Cravero, Semplice & Visconti, and is renamed CWENOZ. Its fluxes are evaluated at the cell boundaries, not at the cell midpoint (§2.3). **Stated**
5. Unlike two-region hybrids, the domain is split into three regions by $\mathfrak n=\tau_5/(\epsilon+\bar\beta)$: smooth for $\mathfrak n\le\frac12$, non-smooth for $\mathfrak n\ge1$ and transition in between (§3). **Stated** (design). Away from critical points it rests on $\tau_5=O(\Delta x^5)$ and $\bar\beta=O(\Delta x^2)$. **Derived** (Eqs. 2.18–2.20)
6. In the non-smooth region the weights are $\alpha_k=d_k\big(1+\tau_{\mathrm Z}/((\epsilon+\beta_k)C_R)\big)$ with $C_R=C_s\Delta x+\beta_{\mathrm{opt}}+C_b$ and $C_s=(\sum_{|m|\le2}\lvert u_{i+m}\rvert)^2$ (Eqs. 3.2–3.4). The reason given is that $C_R=1$ "seems to cause some oscillations near discontinuities". The first two terms dominate on coarse grids and the last two on fine grids, "to provide proper linearity for steady-state convergence" (§3.1). **Stated**
7. If the residue hangs high in a small region, increasing $C_b$ helps. All tests use $C_b=0.01$ and the five-cell indicator in $C_R$ (§3.1). **Stated**
8. The blend is $P^{\mathrm C}=W_1P^{\mathrm L}+(1-W_1)P^{\mathrm N}$ with $W_1=\min(1,F)$, $F=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+(a_{\mathrm H}+1)(Q_{\mathrm H}-1)}}{a_{\mathrm H}+1}$ and $a_{\mathrm H}=\beta^{\mathrm L}/(\beta^{\mathrm A}+10^{-40})$, where $\beta^{\mathrm A}=\min(\beta_{\mathrm{opt}},\beta_{\mathrm L},\beta_{\mathrm R})$. Then the blended indicator satisfies $\beta^{\mathrm C}\le2Q_{\mathrm H}\beta^{\mathrm N}$ (Eqs. 3.6–3.13). **Derived** (from $(A+B)^2\le2(A^2+B^2)$ and $\beta^{\mathrm A}\le\beta^{\mathrm N}$)
9. $F$ increases with $Q_{\mathrm H}$ and decreases with $a_{\mathrm H}$. For $a_{\mathrm H}\le Q_{\mathrm H}$, $W_1=1$, because $F(Q_{\mathrm H},Q_{\mathrm H})=1$, and the linear polynomial dominates (§3.1). **Derived**
10. With $Q_{\mathrm H}=1+K_{\mathrm H}\min_k\beta_k/(\beta^{\mathrm A}+10^{-40})$ and $K_{\mathrm H}=3$, the paper concludes $\beta^{\mathrm C}\le2(\beta^{\mathrm A}+K_{\mathrm H}\min_k\beta_k)$ (end of §3.1). **Derived** as printed (see Printed slips)
11. The indicator $\beta^{\mathrm L}$ of the linear combination $P^{\mathrm L}$ has a cheap closed form in cell averages and $\beta_{\mathrm{opt}}$ (Eq. 3.9). The absolute value guards against round-off negatives (§3.1). **Derived**
12. The transition weight, linear $M_{\mathrm t}=2-2\mathfrak n$ (3.14) or the exponential bump (3.15), "is essential for the steady-state convergence in our hybrid scheme". Both work; only (3.15) is shown (§3.2). **Stated** (no sharp-switch ablation)
13. By ADR (Fig. 3.2), WENO-H has lower dissipative and dispersive errors than CWENOZ, MRWENO and WENO-JS. The settings are $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ and $C_b=0.01$, with linear weights $(0.8,0.1,0.1)$ for WENO-H, $(0.98,0.01,0.01)$ for CWENOZ and $(1,10,100)/111$ for MRWENO (§3). **Shown**
14. Remark 3.1: the same hybrid strategy with MRWENO, second-order TVD or first-order blending in the non-smooth region also converges in all the tests; details are not shown. **Stated**
15. 1D stationary Mach 2 shock on 400 points: all schemes reach machine zero, and WENO-H oscillates less near the shock (Ex. 4.1, Figs. 4.1–4.2). **Shown**
16. 2D steady manufactured solution: UW5 and WENO-H give identical, fifth-order errors ($L^1$ from $1.86\times10^{-3}$ at $20^2$ to $3.69\times10^{-6}$ at $70^2$; orders 4.94–4.99) (Ex. 4.2, Table 4.1). **Shown**
17. Isentropic vortex ($T=0.2$, $\Delta t\approx\Delta x^{5/3}$): the paper states that all schemes reach fifth order. The WENO-H orders are irregular at intermediate grids ($L^\infty$ 2.42 at $80^2$ and 8.19 at $160^2$) (Ex. 4.3, Table 4.2). **Shown** (with this caveat; *compiler check, not in the sources: the printed orders agree with the printed errors, e.g. $\log_2(2.42\times10^{-3}/2.22\times10^{-4})=3.45$ in $L^1$*)
18. Shock reflection on $120\times30$: with the LF speed $\max(\lvert v\rvert+c)$, only WENO-H reaches about $10^{-12}$, also with $\epsilon=10^{-10}$. With per-field speeds all three schemes converge, but CWENOZ and MRWENO oscillate (Ex. 4.4, Figs. 4.4–4.6). **Shown**
19. Forward-facing step, $90\times30$: all schemes converge to about $10^{-13}$ except MRWENO. Multiplying the LF speed by 1.1, MRWENO(1.1), fixes it (Ex. 4.5). **Shown**
20. Supersonic cylinder: on $60\times80$ all schemes converge to about $10^{-14}$, on $120\times160$ only WENO-H reaches round-off, and WENO-H is sharper (Ex. 4.6). **Shown**
21. One plate at 15° and two plates: on $200^2$ all schemes reach about $10^{-13.5}$. On $400^2$ CWENOZ and WENO-H reach about $10^{-13}$, while MRWENO stalls near $10^{-3.5}$, and MRWENO(1.1) does not fix the one-plate case (Ex. 4.7–4.8). **Shown**
22. Three plates, a long plate and three long plates: all schemes converge to about $10^{-13}$, and WENO-H oscillates less than CWENOZ and MRWENO (Ex. 4.9–4.11). **Shown**
23. Square column at Mach 4: on $60\times108$ all reach about $10^{-14}$; on $120\times216$ all except CWENOZ, which needs CWENOZ(1.1). Two columns on $160\times288$: all except CWENOZ, fixed by CWENOZ(1.1) (Ex. 4.12–4.13). **Shown**
24. 3D steady manufactured solution: fifth order, with identical UW5 and WENO-H errors ($L^1$ from $3.90\times10^{-3}$ to $7.06\times10^{-6}$; orders 5.01–5.07) (Ex. 4.14, Table 4.3). **Shown**
25. 3D Mach 3 flow past a plate on $100^3$: CWENOZ fails with negative pressure and works with weights $(0.8,0.1,0.1)$. All schemes converge except MRWENO (Ex. 4.15). **Shown**
26. In every benchmark the WENO-H average residue settles at round-off level (§5). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- End of §3.1: "we can conclude that $\beta^C\le2(\beta^A+K\min\{\beta_1^{(1)},\beta_2^{(1)},\beta_3^{(1)}\})$" *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. From $\beta^{\mathrm C}\le2Q_{\mathrm H}\beta^{\mathrm N}$ one gets $\beta^{\mathrm C}\le2\beta^{\mathrm N}(1+K_{\mathrm H}\min_k\beta_k/\beta^{\mathrm A})$. Replacing $\beta^{\mathrm N}$ by $\beta^{\mathrm A}$ would need $\beta^{\mathrm N}\le\beta^{\mathrm A}$, the reverse of the inequality $\beta^{\mathrm A}\le\beta^{\mathrm N}$ used in the proof. So the printed bound does not follow.
- §3.1: $C_R$ is introduced "for the sake of reducing the linearity", but in (3.2) a larger $C_R$ makes the weights more linear. The wording only makes sense for $C_R<1$ on coarse grids, relative to $C_R=1$.
- Ex. 4.4 writes $\varepsilon=10^{-10}$ (a different glyph from $\epsilon$) and does not say whether the detector's $\epsilon$ changes too.
- Typos: "the radio of specific heats" (§1), "Euler quations" (Ex. 4.14).
- Symbol reuse: $\epsilon$ is the regularization and the vortex strength (Ex. 4.3); $\eta$ is the detector and the computational coordinate (Ex. 4.6); $L$ is the left-eigenvector matrix and the spatial operator.
- References: [1] prints the authors as "F. Acker, R.B. de, R. Borges, B. Costa"; [9] glues the title word "TVB" to the author list; [23] (Harten et al., ENO III) prints "J. Comput. Phys. 131 (1) (1987) 3–47": volume 131, pages 3–47 are those that Ye et al. (2022) print for the 1997 J. Comput. Phys. reprint (bibliography entry `harten-1997`), here with the 1987 year of the original.
