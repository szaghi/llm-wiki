# Summary

The paper develops MDG+ICE, a form of
[implicit shock tracking](/methods/implicit-shock-tracking.md) that follows the
MDG-ICE method of Corrigan, Kercher and Kessler, for the compressible Euler
equations. The flow state and the grid geometry are both unknowns. The
equations are discretized by a space-time
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) method (1D
unsteady problems in the $x$–$t$ plane, 2D steady problems in $x$–$y$), and the
vertex coordinates are fixed by enforcing weakly the interface condition
$[\![F(u_h)\cdot n]\!]=0$ on element faces, the space-time form of the
[Rankine–Hugoniot condition](/definitions/rankine-hugoniot-condition.md).
Unlike Corrigan et al., the condition is tested in a **continuous** (vertex,
CG) trace space rather than the discontinuous one, the mesh may not move in
time, and one space-time slab is solved per step; this cuts the interface
equations from $n_{\mathrm{face}}n_{\mathrm{basis}}n_{\mathrm{eqns}}$ to
$n_{\mathrm{poin}}n_{\mathrm{eqns}}$ and the geometric unknowns to the
$x$-coordinates of the vertices at $t^{n+1}$.

Since the interface condition holds at convergence, no Riemann solver is used:
interior fluxes are the arithmetic mean of the two one-sided fluxes, and the
paper calls the extension of upwind fluxes (such as the
[HLL family](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md)) to space-time DG an open problem. No limiter, WENO
reconstruction or [artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md) is
used. The over-determined nonlinear system is solved in the least-squares
sense (Levenberg–Marquardt and Dennis–Gay–Walsh), and degenerate elements are
removed by edge swap, collapse and split. Two elementary lemmas show that the
jump condition on a straight space-time face gives the contact speed (linear
advection) and the Rankine–Hugoniot shock speed (Burgers).

With 4 to 16 space-time cells the method fits shocks and contacts of the
[Sod and Lax problems](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) and places
rarefaction heads and tails almost exactly at $N=2,3$; $p$-refinement slopes of
the density error are 8.75 and 10.48 (Sod) and 6.59 and 8.44 (Lax). The
[123 problem](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md) and the Noh problem
are computed without wall overheating, and steady oblique and bow shocks (Mach 3
wedge, Mach 2 diamond wedge, Mach 8 and 15 half cylinder) are fitted with no
[carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md). There is no comparison with
any other scheme.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the DG degree $p$ is $N$ (the
paper's "MDG(P$N$)" elements are tensor-product $\mathbb Q^N$ in space-time),
the jump is $[\![a]\!]=a^+-a^-$ with $u^-$ the left and $u^+$ the right trace,
the velocity is $v$ and the total energy per unit volume $E$.

1. Shock-capturing schemes "have a fatal flaw in that the interface conservation is not considered and can never be achieved for discontinuities"; neglecting interface conservation is called "the root cause" of both the numerical-flux question and the spurious-oscillation question of high-order methods (§I, p. 2). **Stated**
2. Interface conservation $[\![F(u_h)\cdot n]\!]=0$ (Eq. 3.5) holds automatically for resolved smooth flows and cannot be reached for discontinuous flows by refinement: it holds "if and only if the mesh interfaces are aligned with discontinuities" (§3.2, p. 5). **Stated**
3. Corrigan et al.'s MDG-ICE tests the interface condition in the discontinuous trace space (Eq. 3.6), giving $n_{\mathrm{face}}\times n_{\mathrm{basis}}\times n_{\mathrm{eqns}}$ equations ($n_{\mathrm{eqns}}=d+2$) for $2n_{\mathrm{poin}}$ space-time geometric unknowns, a large over-determined system (§3.2, pp. 5–6, Fig. 2). **Stated**
4. Testing in a continuous trace space (Eq. 3.7) with a mesh fixed in time gives $n_{\mathrm{poin}}\times n_{\mathrm{eqns}}$ equations for $n_{\mathrm{poin}}$ geometric unknowns (the $x$-coordinates); for a scalar law the interface system is square (§3.2, p. 6, Fig. 3). **Stated**
5. With one element in time per slab $[t^n,t^{n+1}]$, the unknowns per step are $n_{\mathrm{degr}}n_{\mathrm{eqns}}n_{\mathrm{elem}}+n_{\mathrm{poin}}/2$; the interface condition is not enforced on time-slab faces, where the flux is upwind "according to the causality principle" (§3.2, pp. 6–7, Fig. 4). **Stated**
6. Lemma 1: for linear advection with speed $a_0$ and Riemann data, the interface condition on a straight face holds iff the face moves with the contact, $x^{n+1}_i=x^n_i+a_0\Delta t$ (§3.2, Eqs. 3.8–3.10). **Derived** (see Printed slips for the sign of Eq. 3.9)
7. Lemma 2: for Burgers' equation with Riemann data, the interface condition holds iff the face moves with the Rankine–Hugoniot speed, $x^{n+1}_i=x^n_i+\tfrac12(u_l+u_r)\Delta t$ (§3.2, Eqs. 3.11–3.13). **Derived** (no entropy condition is imposed)
8. The interface flux is uniquely defined by the interface condition (Eq. 3.14), so no Riemann-solver flux is needed for linear stability; the central mean $\tfrac12\big(F(u_h^-)+F(u_h^+)\big)\cdot n$ (Eq. 3.15) is used "to enhance the stability and coupling for under-resolved flows" (§3.3, p. 8). **Stated**
9. "How to extend an upwind flux to the space-time DG formulation, that satisfies the consistence requirement (3.14), remains an open problem", although most upwind fluxes can be used in the steady formulation (§3.3, p. 8). **Stated**
10. Because discontinuities are fitted, no limiter, ENO/WENO reconstruction or artificial viscosity is needed for nonlinear stability (§I, §V). **Stated**; no oscillations appear in Figs. 9–28 (**Shown**, qualitatively)
11. The over-determined system $R(U)=0$, $m>n$, is solved as $\min\tfrac12\lVert R\rVert^2$ by Levenberg–Marquardt and the adaptive nonlinear least-squares algorithm of Dennis, Gay and Walsh (as printed) (§3.4, Eqs. 3.16–3.17). **Stated** (no damping, regularization or stopping criteria given)
12. MDG+ICE runs on tangled meshes with zero- or negative-volume elements, but such solutions are "either non-physical or meaningless" (a multiple-valued Burgers solution was once obtained); elements near unfitted interfaces flatten and collapse as the residual decreases (§3.5, p. 9). **Stated**
13. Degenerate elements (Jacobian determinant negative or below a tolerance) are removed by edge swap with cap removal and by edge collapse, each collapse followed by a split of the longest edge so that the numbers of triangles, points and boundary faces stay fixed (§3.5, pp. 9–10, Figs. 6–7). **Stated**
14. The space-time formulation makes solution transfer after a topology change free of conservative-interpolation concerns (§3.1, §3.5). **Stated**
15. Sod problem, 8 space-time quadrilaterals (six degenerate at the origin): MDG($N=1,2,3$) fits shock and contact as true discontinuities, the rarefaction head and tail are fitted "very accurately" at $N=1$ and "virtually resolved exactly" at $N=2,3$; an initial grid with a negative-volume element gives no significant difference (§IV.A, Figs. 8–11). **Shown**
16. Sod $p$-refinement: $\log_{10}\lVert\rho-\rho_e\rVert_{L_2}=-2.82255,-4.36399,-5.67351$ for $N=1,2,3$, slopes 8.75 and 10.48 against $\log_{10}(1/\sqrt{\mathrm{DOFs}})$, described as "exponential rate of convergence for p-type refinement" (§IV.A, Table 1, Fig. 12). **Shown**
17. Lax problem $p$-refinement: density errors $-3.37635,-4.53702,-5.59210$, slopes 6.59 and 8.44; velocity and pressure errors in Fig. 17 (§IV.B, second "Table 1"). **Shown**
18. MDG+ICE achieves "the formal rate of p-convergence even for discontinuous solutions and solutions with singularities, that no other numerical methods can to the best of our knowledge" (§IV.A, §IV.B, §V). **Stated** (three points per problem)
19. 123 problem, 16 cells, $N=2$: the initial discontinuity is removed by the collapse of its two adjacent cells, which carry zero volume and negative density before removal without breaking the solver; density, velocity, pressure and internal energy then match the exact solution "without overheating" (§IV.C, Figs. 18–21). **Shown** (figures only)
20. Noh problem ($\gamma=5/3$, 4 cells, $N=1$): one element collapses and is removed; density and pressure turn negative during the iterations without affecting the result; residuals reach about $10^{-8}$–$10^{-9}$ in about 50 iterations; the solution is "virtually identical to the exact solution", with no overheating (§IV.D, Figs. 22–24). **Shown**
21. Mach 3 flow past a 15° wedge (189 elements), started from a $N=0$ DG solution: the oblique shock is fitted in about 20 iterations, PDE and geometry residuals converge at the same rate, and the density along $y=0.3625$ matches the exact solution (§IV, Figs. 25–26). **Shown**
22. Mach 2 flow past a diamond wedge in a channel (500 elements): shock reflection, shock–shock interaction and expansion fan are "well resolved" at $N=1$ (§IV, Fig. 27). **Shown** (qualitatively)
23. Hypersonic flow past a half cylinder at $M_\infty=8$ (315 elements) and $15$ (497 elements): no shock instability or carbuncle at $N=1$ (§IV.E, Fig. 28). **Shown** (qualitatively)
24. MDG+ICE is "extremely robust": tangled meshes and negative density or pressure during iterations do not break the solution process (§V). **Stated** (supported by claims 15, 19, 20)
25. A formulation of MDG-ICE with a determined (square) system was given for scalar laws in Luo, Jiang & Nourgaliev (AIAA 2020-1316); the extension to systems is announced (§I, p. 2). **Stated**
26. Future work: efficient, reliable nonlinear least-squares solvers and extension to viscous, reactive and multi-material flows (§V). **Stated**

(3.10) and (3.13) print $+$; the conclusions are right, and the intermediate equations need a minus between the two terms (the normal of a face with tangent $(\Delta x,\Delta t)$ is $\propto(\Delta t,-\Delta x)$). *(compiler check, not in the sources: solving the printed Eqs. 3.9 and 3.12 for $x^{n+1}_i$.)*
