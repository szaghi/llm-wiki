# Summary

The paper builds a density-based finite-volume scheme on a compact four-cell
stencil for compressible multi-component flows (liquid, liquid–vapour mixture and
up to two non-condensable gases), aimed at diesel jets in dual-fuel engine
conditions, inside the TU München code CATUM. The model is a single-fluid
mass-fraction four-equation model (mixture mass, momentum, energy and one species
equation per gas), a form of the
[multicomponent Euler equations](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md)
contrasted with the [five-equation model](/definitions/five-equation-model.md),
closed by a barotropic equilibrium cavitation model (linearized liquid,
homogeneous-equilibrium vapour mixture, isothermal ideal gases); the appendices add
[stiffened-gas](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md) and Peng–Robinson
closures with an energy equation.

The contribution, **All-Mach THINC-TDU**, has three parts. At a detected
liquid–gas interface the gas mass fraction is reconstructed by
[THINC](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md) with $\beta_{\mathrm T}=1.6$; elsewhere a
second-order MUSCL reconstruction with a [TVD](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md)
limiter is used. The interface density is not reconstructed independently but
recomputed from the reconstructed mass fractions and the interface pressure through
the equation of state (the "thermodynamic-dependent update", TDU); at
liquid–vapour interfaces THINC acts on the density and the vapour fraction follows
from the barotropic law. The interface flux is the all-Mach flux of Schmidt et al.,
$p^*=\tfrac12(p^-+p^+)$ with an impedance-weighted interface velocity. Surface
tension uses the continuum-surface-force model with a curvature computed from a
Gaussian-filtered indicator, decoupled from the gradient of the Heaviside
approximation. Appendix D blends a fourth-order central, the upwind MUSCL and the
THINC face values through a switch built on the
[Ducros sensor](/definitions/ducros-shock-sensor.md) and on gas and vapour
volume-fraction variations, giving an implicit-LES variant.

Evidence is numerical and mostly visual: one-dimensional
[material-interface advection](/benchmarks/material-interface-advection.md), the
air–helium [shock–bubble interaction](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md)
against Quirk & Karni, Hysing rising bubbles, an
[oscillating droplet](/benchmarks/oscillating-droplet.md) (period and Laplace
jump, 4.24% error), shape relaxation, parasitic currents, Spray A break-up, gas and
vapour [bubble collapse near a wall](/benchmarks/bubble-collapse-near-wall.md),
and a near-critical shock–droplet case. The sharpening costs 2–7% extra.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's gas mass fraction
$\xi_{Gas}$ is $Y_k$, its volume fractions $\alpha_\Phi$ are $\phi_k$, its THINC
steepness $\beta$ is $\beta_{\mathrm T}$, its surface-tension coefficient and
curvature $(\sigma,k)$ are $(\sigma_{\mathrm{st}},\kappa_{\mathrm c})$, its face
states $(\cdot)_L,(\cdot)_R$ are $(\cdot)^-,(\cdot)^+$.

1. The mass-fraction four-equation model handles a species front that forms during the computation (reaction, cavitation, condensation), an advantage over level-set formulations, and needs one equation per extra species against two for the five-equation model (§1.1). **Stated**
2. Quasi-conservative models conserve species masses poorly and produce artificial temperature spikes [15,31–33]; Γ-transport four-equation models suit the stiffened-gas closure, mass-fraction four-equation models suit real-fluid closures (§1.1). **Stated**
3. The proposed four-equation model "is fully conservative when no additional source terms are introduced" (§1.3, item 1). **Stated** (no conservation test is shown)
4. [BVD](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md) reconstruction needs extra computation and communication; the paper restricts itself to a compact four-cell stencil and excludes it (§1.2). **Stated**
5. Existing THINC methods for compressible flow mostly reconstruct the volume fraction of a five-equation model, do not consider the Riemann solver at the face and decouple pressure, density and internal energy in the reconstruction (§1.2). **Stated**
6. The all-Mach flux $p^*=\tfrac12(p^-+p^+)$, $v^*=\big(\rho^-c^-v^-+\rho^+c^+v^++p^--p^+\big)/(\rho^-c^-+\rho^+c^+)$ of Schmidt et al. [104] suits both low-Mach pure liquid and high-Mach liquid–vapour mixtures; for supersonic or hypersonic gas flows [HLLC](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) or [Roe](/methods/roe-riemann-solver.md) should replace it (§3.2, Eqs. (3–1), (3–2)). **Stated**
7. $\beta_{\mathrm T}=1.6$ "was found to give suitable results"; the literature uses 1.6 to 3 (§3.3). **Stated**
8. With TDU the interface density $\rho^*=f(p^*,Y^*_{1},Y^*_{2})$ is recomputed from the interface pressure and the THINC mass fractions, so the density is sharpened without its own THINC reconstruction and thermodynamic consistency is kept (§3.3, Eq. (3–5); §3.6). **Stated** (illustrated by the tests)
9. One-dimensional two-component interface advection (100 cells, four periods, $t=0.16$ s, CFL 1.4): all MUSCL-TVD-TDU limiters smear the interface, Koren's least; All-Mach THINC-TDU is noticeably sharper; pressure and velocity stay at their initial values after four periods (§4.1, Figs. 4.2–4.3), a [pressure-equilibrium](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md) observation. **Shown**
10. Shock ($M_s=1.22$)–helium cylinder on a $14400\times1600$ mesh agrees with Quirk & Karni in schlieren images and in the space–time diagram of three interface points (§4.2, Figs. 4.5–4.7). **Shown**
11. Hysing rising bubbles (Case 1 $100\times200$, Case 2 $160\times320$, CFL 1.4): MUSCL-TVD-TDU smears the interface heavily; THINC-TDU gives "excellent results" in agreement with [123,124] (§4.3, Figs. 4.9–4.10). **Shown**
12. Oscillating ellipsoidal drop ($158\times158$): period $T=2\times10^{-5}$ s, matching Fyfe et al.'s expression (4–3); averaged pressure jump 241017 Pa against the theoretical 231214 Pa, relative error 4.24% (§4.4, Figs. 4.11–4.12). **Shown** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: Eq. (4–3) with $a=6\times10^{-4}$ m, $b=8\times10^{-4}$ m, $\sigma_{\mathrm{st}}=80$ N/m, $\rho_1+\rho_2=122.091$ kg/m³ gives $T=2.04\times10^{-5}$ s; $80/(0.346\times10^{-3})=2.312\times10^5$ Pa and $(241017-231214)/231214=4.24\%$.)*
13. Square-to-circle bubble relaxation ($150\times150$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{st}}=800$ N/m, density ratio 1000) agrees "very well" with Schmidmayer et al. [129]; a cube-to-sphere case is also run (§4.5, Fig. 4.13). **Shown**
14. Static spherical drop ($40^3$, density ratio 10): maximum parasitic speed about 0.004 m/s at $t\approx0.001$ s and 0.11 m/s at $t\approx0.05$ s, called "comparable to best results 0.0855m/s and 0.386m/s" of Williams et al. [136] (App. C, Fig. C.1). **Shown**
15. 2D Spray A shear layer (60 MPa, 0.75 μm cells): THINC-TDU prevents interface smearing and resolves the liquid tip at cost comparable to the standard model; THINC-TDU-iLES gives sharper interfaces than the original iLES (§4.6, Fig. 4.15). **Shown**
16. 3D Spray A primary break-up (55 million cells, minimum 0.04 μm, $\Delta t=0.1$ ns) runs robustly; it is called an under-resolved DNS kept stable by inherent numerical dissipation (§4.7, Fig. 4.17). **Shown**
17. 3D turbulent jet (6 MPa, wall cell 0.1 μm): THINC-TDU-iLES gives sharper interfaces than the original iLES (§4.8, Fig. 4.19). **Shown**
18. Gas and vapour bubble collapse near a wall ($R=400$ μm, wall distance 440 μm, 4 μm cells): interfaces stay sharp for both liquid–gas and liquid–vapour (§4.9, Figs. 4.21–4.23). **Shown**
19. The sharpening costs about 6.65% (turbulent jet) and 2.06% (gas bubble collapse) more than the original scheme (§5). **Shown** (numbers stated, no timing table)
20. Weak scaling is nearly constant up to 1372 cores and drops to about 76% efficiency at 1792 cores; strong scaling is good for 51.2K and 12.8K cells per core (§5, Fig. 5.1). **Shown**
21. Near-critical shock–n-dodecane droplet with the Peng–Robinson closure: at 0.115 mm cells the results agree with Boyd & Jarrahbashi [135] and are "superior to these of WENO3(HLLC) and OWENO3(HLLC) scheme, especially near the two phase interface" (App. A.2.2, Fig. A.2.1). **Shown** (contour images; OWENO3 is not defined or cited)
22. Diffuse-interface methods are cheaper and easier to parallelize than sharp-interface methods (§5, citing [14]). **Stated**
23. In the finite-volume scheme the flux of a face is the same for its two cells, so all variables are conserved exactly (App. B). **Stated**
24. The switch (D.4) with thresholds 0.95 on the Ducros ratio $\Phi_{\mathrm D}$ (Egerer et al.) and 0.4 on the gas sensor (Trummler et al.) "could help to avoid pressure oscillations while keeping contact waves crisp" (App. D). **Stated**
