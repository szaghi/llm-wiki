# Summary

Yang & Qiu (2026) build CHWENO, a finite-difference [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) for
nonlinear degenerate parabolic equations. The paper is peripheral to this bundle (its equations are not
hyperbolic) and is recorded for its reconstruction, which transfers unchanged to hyperbolic laws.

**Degenerate parabolic equations** (inline here, no page). The model is
$\partial_tu+\partial_xf(u)=\partial_x^2g(u)$ (in 2D plus $\partial_yF(u)=\partial_y^2G(u)$), with
$\partial_x^2g(u)=\partial_x\big(g'(u)\partial_xu\big)$ and $g'(u)\ge0$ allowed to vanish on a set of
states (§1, Eqs. (1.1)–(1.2)). Where $g'=0$ the equation is hyperbolic, so solutions can be weak, with sharp
fronts moving at finite speed; this is why a nonlinear, WENO-type treatment of the diffusion term is
wanted. The porous medium equation $\partial_tu=\partial_x^2(u^m)$, $m>1$, is the prototype; its Barenblatt
solution $B_m(x,t)=t^{-k}\big[\big(1-\tfrac{k(m-1)}{2m}\tfrac{x^2}{t^{2k}}\big)_+\big]^{1/(m-1)}$,
$k=1/(m+1)$, has compact support with fronts at $x=\pm t^k\sqrt{2m/(k(m-1))}$ (Eqs. (1.3)–(1.4)).

**Scheme.** The [conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
form differences the convection through the Shu–Osher function and the diffusion through a second implicit
function whose double cell average is $g(u)$, following the DWENO scheme of Liu, Shu & Zhang (2011), so
that $\partial_x^2g$ at $x_i$ is an exact difference of interface values (Eqs. (2.1)–(2.5)). Both fluxes
use one Hermite polynomial (a quartic for the convective flux, fifth order; a quintic for the diffusive
flux, sixth order) and two low-degree polynomials in the [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md) combination with
arbitrary positive linear weights (Zhu & Qiu 2016), with [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type weights and
Jiang–Shu [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) given in closed form. The
contribution: the derivative data are not evolved by an auxiliary equation, as in the HWENO of Ahmat et al.
(2023), but computed at every stage by Lele's sixth-order tridiagonal compact difference (CCD6), an idea
taken from Ma & Wu (2018). A von Neumann analysis with [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) and a
modified-wavenumber comparison ([approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md))
complete §2.

**Tests** (all at CFL 0.15; inline here, no page). Heat and manufactured stiff reaction–diffusion problems
show sixth order in 1D and 2D. Porous-medium tests: the Barenblatt solution for $m=2,4,6,8$ with front
tracking, 1D two-box collisions (equal boxes, $m=5$; unequal heights), and a 2D two-bump collision for
$m=2$; all are captured without visible oscillations, CHWENO sharpest on the Barenblatt zooms and, with HWENO,
closer to the reference than DWENO in the two-box collisions.
Further: the viscous [Buckley–Leverett](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md) equation with and without
gravity in 1D and 2D, strongly degenerate convection–diffusion in 1D and 2D, and the Schnakenberg system.
CHWENO runs in 0.53–0.73 (1D) and 0.45–0.64 (2D) of the CPU time of the HWENO of Ahmat et al.; it is more
accurate than DWENO but slower.

# Key claims

Registry notation: the source's linear weights $\gamma_k$ are $d_k$; $\bar\omega_k$ is $\alpha_k$; the
regularization $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ is $\epsilon$, while the diffusion coefficient $\varepsilon$ of the
model problems is kept (page-local); the advective CFL ratio $a\Delta t/\Delta x$ is $\nu$; the reduced
wavenumber $\alpha=k\Delta x$ is $\vartheta$, and the source's $k_i$, $k_r$ are $\tilde\vartheta_r$ and
$-\tilde\vartheta_i$. The diffusion potentials $g$, $G$, the CCD6 derivatives $h^+_i\approx\partial_xf^+$,
$\phi_i\approx\partial_xg$ and the global indicators $\tau$, $\varsigma$ are page-local.

1. CHWENO removes the auxiliary derivative equation of earlier HWENO schemes: $h^+_i$ and $\phi_i$ come from the CCD6 system $\tfrac13h^+_{i-1}+h^+_i+\tfrac13h^+_{i+1}=\tfrac7{9\Delta x}(f^+_{i+1}-f^+_{i-1})+\tfrac1{36\Delta x}(f^+_{i+2}-f^+_{i-2})$ (and the same for $\phi$, $g$), solved by circulant LU or the Thomas algorithm (§1, §2.1 Step 3, Eq. (2.19)). **Stated** (design)
2. With $f(u(x))$ the cell average of $\Phi$ and $g(u(x))$ the double cell average of $\Omega$, $\Psi(x)=\Omega(x+\tfrac{\Delta x}2)-\Omega(x-\tfrac{\Delta x}2)$, the derivatives are exact differences, $\partial_xf|_{x_i}=\frac{\Phi(x_{i+1/2})-\Phi(x_{i-1/2})}{\Delta x}$ and $\partial_x^2g|_{x_i}=\frac{\Psi(x_{i+1/2})-\Psi(x_{i-1/2})}{\Delta x^2}$; if $\hat f=\Phi+O(\Delta x^r)$ and $\hat g=\Psi+O(\Delta x^s)$ the scheme has order $r$, $r\le s$ (Eqs. (2.1)–(2.5)). **Derived**
3. The convective flux is fifth order: a quartic matching $f^+_{i-1},f^+_i,f^+_{i+1}$ as cell averages and $h^+_{i\pm1}$, and two linear polynomials, give $|\hat f_{i+1/2}-\Phi(x_{i+1/2})|=O(\Delta x^5)$ in smooth regions (Step 1, Eqs. (2.7)–(2.13)). **Stated**
4. The diffusive flux is sixth order: a quintic on $x_{i-1},\dots,x_{i+2}$ with $\phi_i,\phi_{i+1}$ under double-average conditions, and two quadratics, give $O(\Delta x^6)$; the linear interface value is $-\tfrac1{36}g_{i-1}-\tfrac94g_i+\tfrac94g_{i+1}+\tfrac1{36}g_{i+2}-\tfrac{2\Delta x}3(\phi_i+\phi_{i+1})$ (Step 2, Eqs. (2.14)–(2.18)). **Stated**
5. The linear weights may be any positive constants summing to one; the tests use $(0.98,0.01,0.01)$. The weights are $\alpha_k=d_k\big(1+\tau/(\epsilon+\beta_k)\big)$ with $\tau=\tfrac14\big(|\beta_0-\beta_1|+|\beta_0-\beta_2|\big)^2$ (and $\varsigma$ "defined similarly" for the diffusive flux), $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ (Eqs. (2.12)–(2.16), §3). **Stated**
6. Closed-form indicators: for the convective quartic $\beta_0=(a_{0,1}+\tfrac14a_{0,3})^2+\tfrac{13}3(a_{0,2}+\tfrac{63}{130}a_{0,4})^2+\tfrac{781}{20}a_{0,3}^2+\tfrac{1421461}{2275}a_{0,4}^2$, $\beta_{1,2}=a_{1,1}^2,a_{2,1}^2$; for the diffusive candidates on $[x_i,x_{i+1}]$ the analogous (2.18) (Eqs. (2.10)–(2.11), (2.17)–(2.18); page-local coefficients $a_{k,l}$, $b_{k,l}$ in powers of $(x-x_i)/\Delta x$). **Derived**
7. Linear von Neumann analysis of $u_t+au_x=\varepsilon u_{xx}$ with linear weights and TVD-RK3: stability for $\nu\le0.4159$ and $\varepsilon\Delta t/\Delta x^2\le0.2827$; the authors take $\Delta t\big(\max|f'|/\Delta x+\max|g'|/\Delta x^2\big)\le0.2827$ (§2.3, Eqs. (2.22)–(2.28), p. 10). **Derived** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the symbols (2.27) agree with the Fourier transform of the operators (2.23) with the CCD6 symbol (2.26) to $10^{-15}$; with $G=1+z+\tfrac{z^2}2+\tfrac{z^3}6$, $|G|\le1$ holds up to $\varepsilon\Delta t/\Delta x^2=0.2827$, reproduced, and up to $\nu\approx1.114$ for advection, not 0.4159. The adopted diffusive bound is the binding one either way.)*
8. The modified wavenumber of the linear CHWENO first-derivative operator follows the exact one over a wider band than linear DWENO (Fig. 1, §2.3). **Shown**
9. Resolved band: CHWENO keeps the dispersion error $1-\tilde\vartheta_r/\vartheta$ below 0.5 % up to $\vartheta=1.2274$ (5.12 points per wavelength) and below 0.1 % up to 0.9285 (6.77 PPW), DWENO up to 0.9717 (6.47 PPW) and 0.7333 (8.57 PPW); the dissipation thresholds are 1.0704 and 0.8055 (CHWENO), 0.8430 and 0.6364 (DWENO) (Table 1). **Shown** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $2\pi/0.9285=6.77$, $2\pi/0.7333=8.57$.)*
10. 1D heat equation ($T=2$): all three schemes sixth order; CHWENO $L^1$ $3.365\times10^{-7}$ to $3.222\times10^{-13}$ for $N_x=20$–160; CPU at $N_x=160$ 0.552 s (CHWENO), 0.900 s (HWENO), 0.505 s (DWENO) (Table 2). **Shown**
11. 1D stiff reaction–diffusion ($T=0.5$): sixth order for all three; CHWENO errors between HWENO (smallest) and DWENO; CPU at $N_x=160$ 4.01 s, 5.05 s, 2.68 s (Table 3). **Shown**
12. Barenblatt solutions ($m=2,4,6,8$, $N_x=200$): no visible oscillations, CHWENO sharpest in the zooms; fronts tracked on $[-9,9]$ (Figs. 2–3, Example 3). **Shown**
13. Two-box collisions: no visible oscillations; CHWENO and HWENO closer to the reference than DWENO (Figs. 4–7, Example 4). **Shown**
14. Viscous Buckley–Leverett with and without gravity and a strongly degenerate convection–diffusion equation: large gradients and hyperbolic–parabolic transitions captured stably, in agreement with HWENO, DWENO and references (Figs. 8–10, Examples 5–6). **Shown**
15. CHWENO/HWENO CPU ratio 0.53–0.73 over Examples 3–6, "approximately 30%" saving on average (means over 1000 runs; Table 4). **Shown**
16. 2D heat ($160^2$): CHWENO error about 1.1 times HWENO's, DWENO's more than 2.6 times CHWENO's; CPU 922.01 s, 1167.3 s, 487.7 s (Table 5). 2D stiff reaction–diffusion: CPU 1872.8 s, 2650.3 s, 1034.5 s (Table 6). **Shown**
17. 2D CHWENO/HWENO CPU ratio 0.45–0.64 over Examples 9–12 (means over 10 runs; Table 7). **Shown**
18. Schnakenberg system: CHWENO and HWENO give similar patterns, "in excellent agreement" with Hu et al. (2024) (Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. CHWENO "may not match the efficiency of the DWENO scheme" but is more accurate and resolves better (§4). **Shown** (Tables 2–6)
20. The CCD6 step is linear and adds no nonlinear-weight cost; the authors credit the CPU saving to it (§2.1 Step 3). **Stated**
21. Liu, Shu & Zhang's (2011) WENO for the diffusion term has [negative linear weights](/methods/negative-linear-weights.md), which Abedian et al. (2013) avoid (§1). **Stated**
22. Future work: explicit–implicit–null time marching to remove $\Delta t=O(\Delta x^2)$ (§4). **Stated**

The comparisons run every scheme at $C_{\mathrm{cfl}}=0.15$ (equal $\Delta t$), not at each scheme's own
stability limit. The sixth-order tables are pure diffusion and reaction–diffusion problems; no smooth
convection-dominated accuracy test exercises the fifth-order convective flux.
