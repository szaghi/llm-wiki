# Summary

Fu et al. (2016) introduce the [targeted ENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md) (TENO) family of
[conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
shock-capturing schemes. The paper gives three motivations. Classical WENO is too
[dissipative](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) for turbulence. Very-high-order WENO
loses robustness when every equal-width candidate is crossed by nearby discontinuities.
[WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md) produces spurious waves at marginally resolved
wavenumbers.

TENO has four ingredients. First, the $K$-point stencil is covered by $K-2$ candidates
of [incrementally increasing width](/methods/incremental-stencil-weno.md),
$\{3,3,3,4,\dots\}$, the first three being those of
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md). Second, a strong scale separation
$\gamma_k=(C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q$ with $C=1$ and $q=6$, built on the
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md). Third, an ENO-like
selection: the normalized $\chi_k$ is compared with a sharp cut-off $C_T$, giving
$\delta_k\in\{0,1\}$. Fourth, the surviving candidates are combined with their
renormalized linear weights. A smooth region therefore uses the linear background scheme
exactly, and the scheme is said to keep the [ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md)
at any order. The sufficient condition for formal order is
$\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^s)$, $s>0$. The paper states that this condition is
much weaker than those of [mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md),
[WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) and WENO-CU6-M (its refs. [19,20,24]), and that it holds "even near
[critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)".

TENO5 and TENO6 are analysed with the
[approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md). The
linear background is then optimized by blending two lower-order sub-schemes under the
dispersion–dissipation condition $\zeta\approx O(10)$ of Hu et al. This gives TENO5-opt
(fourth order) and TENO6-opt (fifth order). With one parameter set the schemes are run on
Gaussian-pulse advection, the [Sod and Lax tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
the [Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), the
[Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), Rayleigh–Taylor and the
viscous shock tube. TENO resolves more small-scale structure than WENO-JS, WENO-Z and
WENO-CU6. WENO-CU6-M fails the blast-wave and double-Mach cases. The paper contrasts
TENO with [hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) switches, whose threshold it calls "likely to
be problem dependent".

# Key claims

Registry notation. The source writes $\hat f_{k,i+1/2}$, $w_k$, $\varepsilon$ and
$\beta_{k,r}$; here they are $\hat f^{(k)}_{i+1/2}$, $\omega_k$, $\epsilon$ and $\beta_k$,
the indicator of $S_k$ of width $r_k$. Its $\xi,\xi_R,\xi_I$ are
$\vartheta,\tilde\vartheta_r,\tilde\vartheta_i$. TENO candidates follow the paper's TENO
numbering, which differs from the registry's "most upwind first":
$S_0=\{i-1,i,i+1\}$, $S_1=\{i,i+1,i+2\}$, $S_2=\{i-2,i-1,i\}$, $S_3=\{i,\dots,i+3\}$,
$S_4=\{i-3,\dots,i\}$, $S_5=\{i,\dots,i+4\}$. The WENO equations of §2 (Eqs. (9)–(19)) use
WENO-JS numbering ($S_0$ upwind, $S_1$ central, $S_2$ downwind, $S_3$ the far-downwind
CU6 stencil). Page-local symbols are $\zeta$ (as on the
[ADR page](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)), $\eta_{\mathrm b}$ (the
blending parameter; source $\eta$), $g^{(1)}$, $g^{(2)}$ (the blended sub-schemes) and
$\mathsf K$ (the lowest derivative index of Appendix A).

1. A $K$-point TENO scheme assembles $K-2$ candidate stencils of incrementally increasing width $\{r_k\}=\{3,3,3,4,\dots\}$. Every candidate other than $S_0$ has one end node at $x_i$ and contains at least one upwind point, "decreasing the strong anti-dissipation induced by a pure downwind stencil" (§3.1.1, Fig. 1, Eq. (21)). **Stated**
2. For two closely located shocks every candidate of classical seventh-order WENO is crossed, whereas the three-point $S_0$ of seventh-order TENO is not and can be singled out. TENO therefore "can recover the robustness of fifth-order WENO scheme even for very-high-order reconstructions" (§3.1.1, Fig. 2). **Stated** (schematic argument)
3. An order-$K$ reconstruction is obtained from $\bigcup_{k=0}^{K-3}S_k$, and an order-$(K+1)$ one by adding the upwind- or downwind-biased $S_{K-2}$. Odd orders are upwind, even orders central. The linear weights up to $K=8$ are printed in Table 2: $K=4$: $(\tfrac36,\tfrac36)$; $K=5$: $(\tfrac6{10},\tfrac3{10},\tfrac1{10})$; $K=6$: $(\tfrac9{20},\tfrac6{20},\tfrac1{20},\tfrac4{20})$; $K=7$: $(\tfrac{18}{35},\tfrac9{35},\tfrac3{35},\tfrac4{35},\tfrac1{35})$; $K=8$: $(\tfrac{30}{70},\tfrac{18}{70},\tfrac4{70},\tfrac{12}{70},\tfrac1{70},\tfrac5{70})$ (§3.1.1). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with the Table 1 candidates, the $K=6$ weights give $\tfrac1{60}(f_{i-2}-8f_{i-1}+37f_i+37f_{i+1}-8f_{i+2}+f_{i+3})$, the six-point central flux.)*
4. Scale separation: $\gamma_k=(C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q$ with $C=1$ and "typically $q=6$" (Eq. (22)). Larger $q$ and smaller $C$ separate resolved from non-resolved scales more strongly. Relative to WENO-CU6-M's $C=1000$, $q=4$, "the discontinuity-detection capability is significantly enhanced rather than sacrificed" (§3.1.2). **Stated**
5. ENO-like selection: $\chi_k=\gamma_k/\sum_l\gamma_l$ (Eq. (25)) and $\delta_k=0$ if $\chi_k<C_T$, else 1 (Eq. (26)). A stencil is either used with its linear weight or removed entirely (§3.1.3). **Stated** (definition)
6. Because $\delta_k$ is binary, "a smooth-transition from high-order to low-order through the weighting strategy is prevented". The scheme can nevertheless reach "arbitrarily high-order accuracy while satisfying the ENO property", hence the name "targeted ENO" (§3.1.4). The final weights are $\omega_k=d_k\delta_k/\sum_ld_l\delta_l$ and the flux is $\hat f_{i+1/2}=\sum_k\omega_k\hat f^{(k)}_{i+1/2}$ (Eqs. (27)–(28)). **Stated**
7. A sufficient condition for formal order in smooth regions is $\chi_k\ge C_T$ for all $k$. It holds if every $\gamma_k\to C$, which is "equivalent to" $\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)=O(\Delta x^s)$, $s>0$ (Eq. (29)). This is "a much weaker condition than those required for previous WENO schemes [19,20,24]" (§3.1.5). **Derived** (one-paragraph argument, no proof)
8. $\tau_5$ (WENO-Z) and $\tau_6$ (WENO-CU6) satisfy condition (29) "even near critical points" ("It is easy to find", §3.2, §3.3). The general $\tau_K$ of Appendix A also does (App. A). **Stated** (no critical-point test is shown)
9. ADR of TENO5: the dispersion and dissipation of the fifth-order linear scheme are recovered up to $\vartheta=1.75$, $1.85$ and $1.95$ for $C_T=10^{-5}$, $10^{-6}$, $10^{-7}$. Nonlinear dissipation is confined to high wavenumbers (§3.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. ADR of six-point schemes: WENO-CU6 departs from the linear scheme at $\vartheta=1.2$, and TENO6 delays the transition "significantly". TENO6 with $C_T=10^{-6}$ has the largest high-wavenumber dissipation, then WENO-CU6. WENO-CU6-M has the lowest dispersion and dissipation (§3.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. Dispersion–dissipation relation of Hu et al. [25]: $\zeta=\lvert d\tilde\vartheta_r/d\vartheta-1\rvert/(-\tilde\vartheta_i)\approx O(10)$ in the non-resolved range estimates the dissipation needed to damp spurious high-wavenumber errors (Eq. (30), absolute value printed). Its application to nonlinear schemes is "fairly straightforward" (§4.1). **Stated** (from [25])
12. For the nonlinear five-point schemes $\zeta\le5.5$: free from spurious waves, but over-dissipative. For six-point schemes $\zeta\approx O(100)$ between $\vartheta=1.1$ and $1.8$, the central background being non-dissipative (§4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
13. WENO-SYMBO (Weirs & Candler) and WENO-BO (Arshed & Hoffmann) produce anti-dissipation in the low-to-intermediate range and "apparently fail to satisfy the dispersion–dissipation relation" (§4.1, citing [22], [29]). **Stated**
14. Optimization: an order-$K$ background is replaced by $\eta_{\mathrm b}g^{(1)}+(1-\eta_{\mathrm b})g^{(2)}$ of two order-$(K-1)$ sub-schemes (Eq. (31)). For even $K$ the two sub-schemes have the same dispersion error, so only dissipation is tuned. For odd $K$ extra dispersion error is introduced but tolerated (§4.1). The optimization costs one order of accuracy (§1, item 4; §5.1). **Derived/Shown**
15. Five-point: $\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.4$ recovers the classical upwind fifth-order scheme. Decreasing $\eta_{\mathrm b}$ from 0.35 to 0.2 improves dissipation, the extremum of $\zeta$ rising from about 5.5 to 16.5, "maintaining order $O(10)$". The weights of Table 3 are $(0.5875,0.325,0.0875)$, $(0.575,0.35,0.075)$, $(0.5625,0.375,0.0625)$ and $(0.55,0.4,0.05)$ for $\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.35,0.30,0.25,0.20$ (§4.2, Table 3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
16. The nonlinear TENO5-opt ($\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.2$) adds dissipation only at high wavenumbers and is less dissipative than the classical fifth-order linear scheme at intermediate wavenumbers (§4.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
17. Six-point: $\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.5$ gives the non-dissipative central scheme. The optimized scheme uses $\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.54$, obtained "by enforcing the constraint $\zeta=10$ at $\xi=1.302$". The nonlinear optimized TENO6 has $\zeta\le35$ throughout and below 10 at high wavenumbers (§4.3, Table 4, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
18. One parameter set serves all computations (Table 5), with $C=1$, $q=6$ and CFL 0.6 throughout: TENO5 $d=(0.6,0.3,0.1)$, $C_T=10^{-5}$; TENO5-opt $d=(0.55,0.4,0.05)$, $C_T=10^{-5}$; TENO6 $d=(0.45,0.3,0.05,0.2)$, $C_T=10^{-7}$; TENO6-opt $d=(0.462,0.3,0.054,0.184)$, $C_T=10^{-7}$ (§5). No value of $\epsilon$ is printed anywhere in the paper; §2.2 calls it "a small value". **Stated**
19. On Gaussian-pulse advection the $L_\infty$ convergence of every TENO scheme coincides with its linear background. "Order-degeneration and instability of the classical WENO schemes, as shown in [36], are overcome", and the optimized schemes are one order less accurate (§5.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
20. Sod ($N_x=100$): WENO-Z, TENO5 and TENO5-opt resolve the contact better than WENO-JS. WENO-CU6-M shows slight oscillations at the contact, and TENO6 and TENO6-opt perform as well as WENO-CU6 (§5.2, Fig. 11). Lax: WENO-JS is marginally more dissipative and WENO-CU6-M overshoots at the contact (Fig. 12). **Shown**
21. Interacting blast waves ($N_x=400$, Roe flux with entropy fix): TENO5 and TENO5-opt resolve density, pressure and velocity better than WENO-JS and WENO-Z, which overshoot in velocity near $x=0.86$. WENO-CU6 produces spurious waves near $x=0.75$ and $0.86$, and WENO-CU6-M fails this case (§5.3, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
22. Shu–Osher ($N_x=200$): TENO5-opt is the best of the five-point schemes. The text gives an entropy-wave amplitude "up to 4.52" for TENO6 and "about 4.4" for TENO5-opt. WENO-CU6 and WENO-CU6-M oscillate slightly near the shock, TENO6 and TENO6-opt do not (§5.4, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
23. Double Mach reflection (effective $1024\times256$, adaptive multiresolution): the small-scale structures of TENO5-opt are "comparable to" fifth-order WENO-JS at $\Delta x=1/960$, and those of TENO6 to ninth-order WENO-JS at $1/960$ (Fig. 1 of [41]). WENO-CU6-M fails (§5.5, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
24. Rayleigh–Taylor ($128\times512$): TENO5 and TENO5-opt resolve richer vortices than WENO-JS and WENO-Z and break the flow symmetry, which is attributed to their low dissipation. At $128\times512$ they are comparable to WENO-JS and WENO-Z at $256\times1024$ (§5.6, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
25. 2-D viscous shock tube ($\mathrm{Re}=1000$, $t=1$, $1280\times640$, reference WENO-JS at $5120\times2560$): TENO5 and TENO5-opt predict the vortex and shock patterns well, where WENO-JS and WENO-Z differ "significantly" from the reference. TENO6-opt fits the reference best. WENO-CU6-M is slightly distorted "due to the lack of dissipation in high wave-number range" (§5.7, Figs. 21–23). **Shown**
26. TENO's arithmetic cost is "similar to" classical WENO, "although not specially studied" (§6). **Stated**
27. The positivity-preserving flux limiter of [31] "can also be applied to the TENO schemes to further improve robustness" (§6). **Stated**
28. The cut-off threshold is "problem independent", determined by spectral analysis (§6). $C_T$ also offers a direct means to control the spectral properties for specific problems (§3.1.3). **Stated**
29. "Eighth- or even higher-order TENO schemes may provide further improved performances" (§6). **Stated** (outlook)

Background printed in §2. The WENO-JS weights are $\alpha_k=d_k/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^q$ with
$d=(0.1,0.6,0.3)$ (JS numbering) and "$q\ge1$, generally defined as $q=2$" (Eq. (11)).
$\epsilon$ "indeed acts as a cutoff of the smoothness measure [33]". Following Henrick et
al., WENO-JS drops to third order where the first derivative vanishes, and to second
order if the second vanishes too. The WENO-Z weights are
$\alpha_k=d_k\big(1+(\tau_5/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q\big)$ with $\tau_5=\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert$
(Eq. (14)), and $q=1$ is "suggested" by Borges et al. The WENO-CU6 weights are
$\alpha_k=d_k\big(C+\tau_6/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)^q$ with $C=20$, $q=1$ and
$d=(\tfrac1{20},\tfrac9{20},\tfrac9{20},\tfrac1{20})$ (Eq. (17)). The downwind candidate is
$\tfrac16(11f_{i+1}-7f_{i+2}+2f_{i+3})$ (Eq. (16)), $\beta_3=\beta_6$ is the
full-stencil indicator (Eq. (18)), and $\tau_6=\beta_6-\tfrac16(\beta_0+\beta_2+4\beta_1)$
is printed without absolute value (Eq. (19)). WENO-CU6-M uses $C=1000$, $q=4$ (§2.3,
after Hu & Adams [24]). The general reference indicator of Appendix A is
$\tau_K=\sum_{j=\mathsf K}^{K-1}\Delta x^{2j-1}\int_{x_{i-1/2}}^{x_{i+1/2}}\big(\hat f_K^{(j)}\big)^2dx$,
with $\mathsf K=(K+1)/2$ for odd $K$ and $K/2$ for even $K$, so that
$\tau_K=\Delta x^{2\mathsf K}\big(f^{(\mathsf K)}(x_i)\big)^2+O(\Delta x^{2\mathsf K+2})\approx O(\Delta x^K)$
(Eqs. (A.1)–(A.3)). TENO5 uses the WENO-Z $\tau_5$ "taken from Eq. (14)", and TENO6 the
$\tau_6$ of Eq. (19).

Numerical setup (§5): $\gamma=1.4$ ($5/3$ for Rayleigh–Taylor). Roe-averaged characteristic
decomposition and global Lax–Friedrichs flux splitting, with the Roe flux with entropy fix
for the blast waves. Third-order (1-D) and second-order (2-D) "strongly stable"
Runge–Kutta, and wavelet-based adaptive multiresolution in 2-D. The Shu–Osher problem is
set on $[0,10]$ with the shock at $x=1$. Double Mach reflection is on $[0,4]\times[0,1]$,
$t=0.2$. The Rayleigh–Taylor final time is not printed.

# Printed slips

- **Lax left state** (§5.2): printed $(\rho,u,p)=(0.445,0.689,3.528)$; the other sources on the [Sod and Lax shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) page print the left velocity $0.698$.
- §3.2: "$\{\beta_{0,3},\beta_{1,3},\beta_{2,3}\}$ are calculated from Eq. (13), and the fifth-order undivided difference $\tau_5$ is taken from Eq. (14) in WENO-Z". Eqs. (13)–(14) use WENO-JS numbering ($S_0$ upwind, $S_2$ downwind), while TENO's $S_0,S_1,S_2$ are central, downwind and upwind. Read in TENO labels, $\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert$ would pair the central and upwind stencils; the words "taken from ... WENO-Z" name the WENO-Z outer-stencil difference. The same mismatch affects $\tau_6$ in §3.3.
- §3.3: "$\{\beta_{0,3},\beta_{1,3},\beta_{2,3},\beta_{3,4}\}$ is evaluated from Eq. (22) without exception"; Eq. (22) is the $\gamma_k$ formula, and the width-$r$ indicator is Eq. (23).
- Fig. 6 labels the optimized five-point curves $\eta=0.65,0.70,0.75,0.80$, whereas Table 3 and §4.2 give $\eta=0.35,0.30,0.25,0.20$ (the legend values equal $1-\eta$).
- §6: "Extensive one-, two- and three-dimensional benchmark tests ... have been performed"; the paper contains 1-D and 2-D tests only.
- §5.7: the initial data are given for $0\le x<1/2$ and $1/2\le x\le1$, but the domain is printed as $[0,0.5]\times[0,1]$, with resolutions $1280\times640$ and $5120\times2560$.
- §5: "WEMO-Z" for WENO-Z.
- Attributions in the text: [23] (Hu, Wang & Adams) is "Hu et al. [23]" in §1 and "Hu and Adams [23]" in §2.3; [24] (Hu & Adams) is "Hu et al. [24]" in §3.1.2; [31] (Hu, Adams & Shu) is "Hu and Adams [31]" in §6.
- Reference list: [5] and [43] are the same work (Daru & Tenaud, Comput. Fluids 30:89–113), printed with the years 2001 and 2000. [39] cites "P. Woodward" alone while the text says "Woodward and Colella [39]". [30] prints "H. Huynhb".
