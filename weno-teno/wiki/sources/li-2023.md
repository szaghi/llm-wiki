# Summary

The paper revisits the fifth-order finite-difference multi-resolution
[alternative WENO](/methods/alternative-weno.md) scheme (MR-AWENO) of Wang, Zhu, Yang and Zhao
(2021). MR-AWENO combines the A-WENO flux form (a
[Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) on interpolated interface states plus a
Taylor correction) with a [multi-resolution](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) interpolation.
That interpolation nests central Lagrange interpolants of degree 0, 2 and 4, with linear
weights $\{1/111,10/111,100/111\}$ and [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md)-type weights whose
regularization $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ is absolute.

Theorems 1–3 state when an MR-AWENO operator is homogeneous of degree one,
$\mathcal W[\sigma u]=\sigma\mathcal W[u]$, which the paper calls
[scale invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md). This holds iff the nonlinear weights are
invariant, iff the unnormalized weights all scale by one common factor. The 2021 weights
fail for every $\sigma\ne0,\pm1$. A scaled Lax problem with $\sigma=10^{-7}$ shows the
resulting oscillations. The fix (MR-AWENO-Si, Theorem 4) borrows from
[affine-invariant WENO](/methods/affine-invariant-weno.md). It has a new global
[smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) $\tau^*$, the square of the fifth
undivided difference on six points, and a descaler $\mathsf m$, the mean of $\lvert u_j\rvert$
on the five-point stencil, that multiplies the regularization as $\epsilon\mathsf m^2$.
Quadruple-precision tests confirm the invariance for $\sigma\in[10^{-19},10^{19}]$. Euler tests
show fifth order and fewer oscillations than MR-AWENO: Einfeldt, Sod, Lax,
[Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) with the
[positivity-preserving limiter](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) and a
[Mach 2000 jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md).

The second half applies scale invariance to [well-balancing](/properties/well-balancing.md) of
the Euler equations with gravity, following Li & Gao (2021). The Lax–Friedrichs dissipation
acts on $u-u_{\mathrm e}$, the source is rewritten as $(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm e})\partial_xp_{\mathrm e}$
and discretized with the same interpolation and correction, and the scheme interpolates
characteristic variables with the Si operator. At equilibrium those variables are multiples
of $p_{\mathrm e}$. Theorem 5 proves that the hydrostatic state is preserved exactly. The
[hydrostatic test suite](/benchmarks/hydrostatic-equilibrium-tests.md) (isothermal and
polytropic, 1D and 2D, double and quadruple precision, perturbations down to $10^{-12}$)
confirms preservation to machine precision.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $u$ the interpolated variable (the source's $q$), $\sigma$ the scaling
factor (the source's $\lambda$), $d_k$ the linear weights (the source's $\gamma_k$), $\mathsf m$
the descaler (the source's $\mu$), $a$ the Lax–Friedrichs speed, $u_{\mathrm e}$,
$\rho_{\mathrm e}$, $p_{\mathrm e}$ the equilibrium state.

1. The MR-AWENO scheme of Wang et al. (2021) is not scale-invariant. It produces visible oscillations near strong shocks when $\sigma$ is small (e.g. $\sigma=10^{-7}$), for $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ and for $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ (abstract, §1, Figs. 1 and 4). **Shown**
2. Theorem 1: for $u\in L^2(\Omega)$ and $\sigma\ne0$, the MR-AWENO operator (25) satisfies $\mathcal W[\sigma u]=\sigma\mathcal W[u]$ iff $\omega_k[\sigma u]=\omega_k[u]$, $k=0,1,2$, because the candidate interface values are linear in $u$ (§2.3, Eqs. 36–37). **Proved**
3. Theorem 2: for $\epsilon>0$, $\omega_k[\sigma u]=\omega_k[u]$ iff $\alpha_k[\sigma u]=C\alpha_k[u]$ with $C$ independent of $k$ (§2.3, Eqs. 38–39). **Proved**
4. Theorems 1 and 2 give necessary and sufficient conditions for checking whether existing WENO-type schemes are scale-invariant. The examples named are WCNS, TENO, Hermite WENO, WENO-Z+M, finite-volume WENO with unequal substencils on unstructured meshes, and approximations of high-order derivatives (§2.3). **Stated**
5. Theorem 3: the MR-AWENO operator (25) is not scale-invariant unless $\sigma=0,\pm1$. The proof works on the set $\{u:\tau[u]\ne0\}$ through the ratio $C_k=\alpha_k[\sigma u]/\alpha_k[u]$ of Eq. (41), which depends on $k$ (§2.3, Eqs. 40–41). The paper locates the cause in the different scalings of $\beta_k$, $\tau$ and $\epsilon$ (§2.3). **Proved**
6. The global indicator $\tau^*=(-u_{i-2}+5u_{i-1}-10u_i+10u_{i+1}-5u_{i+2}+u_{i+3})^2$ (42), on the six-point stencil of the five-point stencil plus $x_{i+3}$, improves smooth-region accuracy. It recovers the order at first-, second- and third-order [critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md) because $\tau^*=O(\Delta x^{10})$ there, and the weights "satisfy the conditions for achieving the formal fifth-order accuracy" (§2.3). **Stated**
7. $\tau^*$ reduces oscillations: if $u$ is smooth on the five-point stencil and a discontinuity sits at $x_{i+3}$, then $\tau^*=O(1)$ and the scheme becomes "more upwind beforehand" (§2.3). **Stated**
8. Theorem 4: the MR-AWENO operator with $\tau^*$ and the weights $\alpha^*_k=d_k\big(1+\tau^*/(\beta_k+\epsilon\mathsf m^2)\big)$, $\mathsf m=\frac15\sum_{j=i-2}^{i+2}\lvert u_j\rvert+\mathsf m_0$, is scale-invariant ($\mathsf m_0=0$ in the analysis; $\mathsf m_0=10^{-100}$ in the code) (§2.3, Eqs. 43–45). **Proved**
9. In quadruple precision the scale-invariance error of MR-AWENO-Si is essentially zero for every $\sigma\in[10^{-19},10^{19}]$ (sine, ReLU and modified Heaviside data, $N=20,80,320$, $\epsilon=10^{-6}$). The error of MR-AWENO depends on $\sigma$ and vanishes only at $\sigma=1$ (§2.4, Eq. 46, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. Scaled Lax problem ($N=200$, $t=1.3$): MR-AWENO with $\tau$ oscillates at the contact and the shock for $\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=10^{-7}$. With $\tau^*$ alone the oscillations disappear for $\sigma=1$ but not for $\sigma=10^{-7}$. MR-AWENO-Si is non-oscillatory in both cases: "the ENO-property of the solution cannot be satisfied by using the new $\tau^*$ alone" (§2.5, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. 1D density advection: both schemes reach fifth order, with $L^1(\rho)=6.44\times10^{-11}$ at $N=320$ for both (Table 1). **Shown**
12. Einfeldt ([double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md)), [Sod and Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) problems ($N=200$): MR-AWENO shows strong spurious oscillations near the shock, the contact and the rarefaction; MR-AWENO-Si does not (§3.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
13. Sedov blast wave (pressure ratio $O(10^{21})$, ambient $p=4\times10^{-13}$), both schemes with the positivity-preserving limiter: MR-AWENO oscillates near the shocks at $x\approx\pm1.5$; MR-AWENO-Si is sharper (§3.3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. 2D [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md): both schemes are fifth order once the vortex is resolved. At $160^2$ the MR-AWENO-Si error is $3.09\times10^{-6}$ against $1.05\times10^{-7}$ for MR-AWENO; from $320^2$ on the errors are identical, so the printed first-refinement order of MR-AWENO-Si is 9.87 (§3.4, Table 2). **Shown**
15. Double Mach reflection ($1600\times400$, $t=0.2$): both schemes capture the large structures; the small scales along the slip line are "much better resolved" by MR-AWENO-Si (§3.5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
16. Mach 2000 jet ($800\times400$, $\gamma=5/3$, $t=0.001$) with the positivity-preserving limiter: only the pressure limiter acts, at the jet front, and the density stays positive; MR-AWENO is noisier along the rear and above the jet tail (§3.6, Fig. 8). **Shown**
17. Theorem 5: the semi-discrete scheme (61) preserves the hydrostatic equilibrium $v=0$, $\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\phi$ exactly. It uses (i) the modified Lax–Friedrichs flux (62), whose dissipation acts on $u-u_{\mathrm e}$, (ii) the source rewritten as $(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm e})\partial_xp_{\mathrm e}$ and approximated by (64), and (iii) a scale-invariant interpolation. The sufficient condition (71) is $\{\!\{\mathcal W[\sigma_{i+1/2}p_{\mathrm e}]\}\!\}=\sigma_{i+1/2}\{\!\{\mathcal W[p_{\mathrm e}]\}\!\}$ with $\sigma_{i+1/2}=(2\tilde c^2_{i+1/2})^{-1}$ (§4.2, Eqs. 65–71, App. A). **Proved** (1D)
18. The third ingredient "is the crucial novel idea". Scale invariance is what lets the [characteristic-wise](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) scheme be well-balanced, because at equilibrium the characteristic variables are multiples $\sigma_{i+1/2}p_{\mathrm e}$ of the equilibrium pressure (§4.1–4.2). **Derived**
19. Accuracy with an isothermal equilibrium: the non-well-balanced (NWB) and well-balanced (WB) schemes are both fifth order, $L^1(\rho)=3.83\times10^{-12}$ at $N=512$ (Table 3). With a polytropic equilibrium, the WB error at $N=32$ is $7.49\times10^{-4}$ and then coincides with the NWB error (printed order 12.55 at the first refinement), which the paper calls "super-convergence behavior like the spectral method" (Table 6). **Shown**
20. 1D isothermal steady state ($\phi=x$, $t=2$): WB errors are $O(10^{-16})$ in double and $O(10^{-34})$ in quadruple precision for $N=10$–$160$. NWB errors fall from $O(10^{-10})$ at $N=10$ to machine epsilon in double for $N\ge80$, but only to $O(10^{-19})$ at $N=160$ in quadruple (Table 4). **Shown**
21. 2D isothermal steady state ($\rho_0=1.21$, $\phi=x+y$, $t=1$): the same picture (Table 5). **Shown**
22. Small perturbations of isothermal states: NWB resolves an amplitude $10^{-4}$ but fails for $10^{-11}$ (1D, $N=90$) and $10^{-12}$ (2D, $N=30$); its oscillations start at the left boundary before the perturbation reaches it. WB has none (Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
23. Sod problem under gravity ($\phi_x=1$, $N=100$, $t=0.2$): NWB oscillates near the shock ($x\approx0.8$) and the contact ($x\approx0.65$); WB does not. Contact discontinuity under gravity ($N=100$, $t=0.6$): both are non-oscillatory (Figs. 9, 12). **Shown**
24. Polytropic steady states (1D, $\phi=3.15x$, $t=10$; 2D Lane–Emden polytrope, $\gamma=2$, $t=14.8$): WB is at machine precision in both precisions. NWB reaches machine epsilon in double only at $N=160$ (1D), and decreases only to $O(10^{-17})$ in quadruple (Tables 7–8). **Shown**
25. Small perturbations of polytropic states: an amplitude $10^{-3}$ is indistinguishable between the schemes; $10^{-12}$ (1D, $N=50$) and $10^{-10}$ (2D, $N=50$) are resolved only by WB (Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
26. A-WENO admits any monotone flux, whereas WENO flux reconstruction needs a smooth flux splitting. It has higher resolution and smaller dissipation and dispersion errors, and preserves free streams automatically on curvilinear meshes (§1). **Stated** (cited)
27. Multi-resolution WENO is compact, robust on extreme problems, converges well to steady states and is efficient on unstructured meshes. MR-AWENO has a smaller truncation error and is more efficient than MR-WENO (§1). **Stated** (cited)

*Compiler checks, not in the sources.* The second- and fourth-derivative formulas of the
Taylor correction (28)–(29) are exact on $x^2$ and $x^4$ about $x_{i+1/2}$, and (29) annihilates
$x^2$. $\tau^*$ is the square of a fifth undivided difference, so it is $O(\Delta x^{10})$ for
any smooth $u$, not only at critical points: claim 6 turns on the ratio
$\tau^*/(\beta_k+\epsilon\mathsf m^2)$, which the paper does not analyse. The descaler makes the
weights invariant under $u\mapsto\sigma u$ but not under $u\mapsto u+\mu$: the paper's
property is scale invariance only, not affine invariance. The order 12.55 of claim 19 is
$\log_2(7.49\times10^{-4}/1.25\times10^{-7})$, computed from the $N=32$ error, after which the WB
errors equal the NWB ones (Table 6). Eq. (41) is exact: with $\beta_k$ quadratic and $\tau$ (23)
quartic in $u$, $\alpha_k[\sigma u]/\alpha_k[u]=\big(1+\sigma^4\tau/(\sigma^2\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)/\big(1+\tau/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)$
equals the printed right-hand side identically, and it depends on $k$ even for $\epsilon=0$.

# Printed slips

- Table 4 (NWB, $N=20$): double precision prints $L^1(\rho)=2.75\times10^{-13}$ and $L^1(\rho v)=2.43\times10^{-13}$, quadruple $3.75\times10^{-13}$ and $3.43\times10^{-13}$, while $L^1(E)$ and all $N=10$ entries agree between the two precisions. The paper does not explain why two entries of one row differ between the precisions while the others agree.
- Table 2: the first-refinement order of MR-AWENO is printed 4.98 *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\log_2(1.05\times10^{-7}/3.31\times10^{-9})=4.987$, a rounding-level difference)*.
- §5.2 against Table 8: the text calls the coarse-mesh NWB errors "about $O(10^{-9})$" where the table prints $1.14\times10^{-8}$, $6.71\times10^{-8}$, $2.09\times10^{-8}$, and says the quadruple NWB errors "decrease to $O(10^{-17})$" where the momentum error at $N=160$ is $7.66\times10^{-16}$.
- Eq. (37) writes the $k=2$ term with $P^{(2)}$, whereas in (25) $\omega_2$ multiplies the combination $\frac1{d_2}(P^{(2)}-d_0P^{(0)}-d_1P^{(1)})$. The "only if" direction of Theorem 1 then needs these combinations to be linearly independent, which is not argued.
- Eq. (76) gives the domain $x\in[-1,2]$, while the parameters are said to be "kept the same as those in (75)", where $x\in[0,1]$.
- Theorem 3 is stated without qualification, but is proved only on $\{u:\tau[u]\ne0\}$; for $\tau=0$ the weights are linear and the operator is trivially scale-invariant.
- Ref. [32] fuses affiliation letters into the author names ("Parésa", "Parés-Pulidob").
