# Summary

The paper adds a volume-penalization (Brinkman-type)
[immersed boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) to Huynh's
[flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md) (FR) on Cartesian grids, for the
compressible Navier–Stokes equations past static and rigidly moving bodies. The FR scheme
is a tensor-product Legendre nodal scheme with solution and flux points at Gauss points. It
uses a [Rusanov](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) inviscid interface flux, an LDG viscous
treatment and the three-stage TVD (SSP)
[Runge–Kutta scheme](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Its introduction reviews
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) immersed-boundary and cut-cell work (§1).
A sharp mask $\chi_{\mathrm s}$ (1 in the solid) marks the solution points covered by the
body. A source $\frac{\chi_{\mathrm s}}{\eta_{\mathrm p}}(\dots)$ relaxes the momentum and
the kinetic energy towards the solid velocity (adiabatic wall). This stiff source is handled
by Strang splitting: half a source step, a full RK3 convective step, then half a source step.
The source sub-steps use either explicit Euler or a linearized backward-Euler ("implicit")
forcing, whose Jacobian and inverse the paper gives in closed form. The authors claim this
forcing as new. The guideline $\eta_{\mathrm p}=\Delta t$ is used throughout.

Other ingredients are a ray-casting mask for convex bodies given by marker points,
back-mapping of solution points to the body frame for rigid motion, and inverse-distance
surface reconstruction from fluid points only (IDW-IP). A 1D penalized advection–diffusion
problem shows three things: the penalization error is $O(\sqrt{\eta_{\mathrm p}})$; the rate
$N_e^{-(N+1)}$ is recovered when the exact solution is imposed in the solid; and the global
rate is only about $N_e^{-1}$ when the solid carries the boundary value. The tests are the
[flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md) (Re 40, also
compressible Re 40/100 cases), a NACA0012, a sphere and a pitching–plunging NACA0012. They
agree with body-fitted FR and with the literature. Local p-refinement
([hp-adaptation](/methods/hp-adaptation.md)) with mortar coupling reproduces the uniform
degree-3 cylinder with about 28 % of the degrees of freedom. The paper has no shock
capturing, and all tests have $M\le0.5$. The 1D accuracy test uses an analytical exact
solution, not a [manufactured solution](/definitions/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation: $N$ is the FR polynomial degree (the source's $P$), $N_e$ the number of
elements in a convergence study, and $u=(\rho,\rho v,E)$. The page-local symbols are
$\chi_{\mathrm s}$ (solid mask), $\eta_{\mathrm p}$ (penalization parameter), $v_{\mathrm s}$
(solid velocity), $\Delta t_1=\Delta t/2$ (the Strang source sub-step) and $D_{\mathrm c}$
(diameter); see the Notation map.

1. The paper states that volume penalization had not been studied with high-order methods, and that combining it with FR is the "aim and novelty" of the work (§1). **Stated**
2. Local p-refinement of FR near the wall improves the accuracy and smoothness of the IBM solution, and raising $N$ improves the moving-boundary results. The authors say neither advantage was reported in earlier IBM work (§1; §5.2, §5.5). **Shown**
3. The penalization source (Eq. 26) is $S(u)=\frac{\chi_{\mathrm s}}{\eta_{\mathrm p}}\big(0,\ \rho v_{\mathrm s}-\rho v,\ \tfrac12\rho\lvert v_{\mathrm s}\rvert^2-\tfrac12\rho\lvert v\rvert^2\big)$, following Abgrall et al. for adiabatic walls. No-slip is $v_{\mathrm s}=0$, and the continuity equation is not penalized (§4.1). **Stated**
4. Time integration is Strang splitting (Eqs. 27–29): source half-steps $\Delta t/2$ around a full TVD-RK3 convective step. With this splitting the penalization terms are said to be "computed exactly" for momentum and energy, after Piquet et al. (§4.1). **Stated**
5. The implicit (linearized backward-Euler) forcing of the source sub-steps is "a new approach proposed in this paper". At a given CFL number it allows $\eta_{\mathrm p}$ about one or two orders of magnitude smaller than explicit forcing. No table or figure quantifies this (§4.1). **Stated**
6. The Jacobian $\partial S/\partial u$ (Eq. 34) and the inverse of $I-\Delta t_1\partial S/\partial u$ (Eq. 35) are given in closed form, so the implicit step needs no linear solve. The momentum diagonal of the inverse is $\eta_{\mathrm p}/(\Delta t_1+\eta_{\mathrm p})$ (§4.1). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: (35) is the exact inverse of $I-\Delta t_1\partial S/\partial u$ with (34); (34) is the Jacobian of (26) in the solid only for $v_{\mathrm s}=0$, since for $v_{\mathrm s}\ne0$ the $\rho$-column gains $\chi_{\mathrm s}v_{\mathrm s}/\eta_{\mathrm p}$ in the momentum rows and $\chi_{\mathrm s}\lvert v_{\mathrm s}\rvert^2/(2\eta_{\mathrm p})$ in the energy row. The paper does not say which Jacobian its moving-body runs use.)*
7. Explicit treatment of the source requires $\Delta t<\eta_{\mathrm p}$. Strang splitting relaxes this restriction, and implicit forcing relaxes it further (§4.6). **Stated**
8. The error splits into a penalization error $\propto\eta_{\mathrm p}^{\alpha_{\mathrm p}}$ and a discretization error $\propto N_e^{-\beta_{\mathrm d}}$ (Eqs. 44–46). Here $\alpha_{\mathrm p}=1/2$ for Dirichlet conditions and $1$ for Neumann conditions, and $\beta_{\mathrm d}$ is the smaller of the scheme order and the regularity of the penalized solution at the boundary (§4.6). **Stated**
9. The error saturates at $\Delta t\approx\eta_{\mathrm p}$, which justifies $\eta_{\mathrm p}=\Delta t$. The guideline is to fix $\eta_{\mathrm p}=\Delta t$ first and then set $\Delta t$ from the CFL condition, which for high-order methods scales as $1/N^2$ (§4.6). **Stated** (saturation is shown for NACA0012, claim 16)
10. In 1D penalized advection–diffusion the $O(\sqrt{\eta_{\mathrm p}})$ rate is recovered at large $\eta_{\mathrm p}$, and higher $N$ needs a smaller $\eta_{\mathrm p}$ for optimal accuracy. For $N>1$ the paper reports a "super-convergence" printed as "$O(\eta^{-1})$", against "$O(\eta^{-0.5})$" for low-order schemes (§5.1, Fig. 8). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
11. When the solid points are penalized with the analytical solution, FR converges at $N_e^{-(N+1)}$: penalization does not degrade high-order convergence by itself (§5.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
12. When the solid points are penalized with the boundary value $u=0$, the rate on $x\in[0.2,0.8]$ stays $N_e^{-(N+1)}$, but over the whole fluid domain $[0,1]$ it drops to about $N_e^{-1}$ for every $N$. The paper attributes this to low regularity across the boundary (§5.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. Cylinder, Re 40, $M=0.2$. $C_p$ agrees with body-fitted FR; the wall vorticity agrees less well, because only the surface velocity is penalized. Meshes 1/2/3 give $L/D_{\mathrm c}$ = 2.30/2.30/2.27, separation angle 51.2°/52.0°/52.0° and $C_d$ = 1.50/1.51/1.52; body-fitted FR gives 2.24, 51.0° and 1.53 (§5.2, Table 1). **Shown**
14. Local p-refinement on mesh 1 uses degree 1 in the far field, degree 2 in a buffer and degree 3 within $0.25D_{\mathrm c}$ of the surface. It is very close to uniform degree 3 with 148296 instead of 532576 degrees of freedom ("nearly one quarter"), and fewer than the 256000 points of an $800\times320$ finite-difference IBM grid (§5.2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
15. Interpolating with the element polynomial at the marker points uses nonphysical solid values: it gives an oscillatory $C_p$ and an under-predicted, oscillatory wall vorticity. IDW-IP from fluid points only is smooth and agrees with body-fitted results (§4.4, App. C, Fig. C.24). **Shown**
16. NACA0012 at Re 5000, $M=0.5$, 2° angle of attack, $h=0.004$ chord, $N=2$, $\Delta t=10^{-4}$. A smaller $\eta_{\mathrm p}$ is more accurate until $\eta_{\mathrm p}<\Delta t$. At $\eta_{\mathrm p}=10^{-5}$ the solution no longer improves and becomes more oscillatory (§5.3, Fig. 15). **Shown**
17. $C_f$ improves with near-wall resolution, through either $N$ (degree 4 at $h=0.004$ chord) or $h$ ($0.001$ chord at degree 2). Low-order schemes are said to need about $5\times10^{-4}$ chord (§5.3, Fig. 16). **Shown** (the low-order comparison is **Stated**)
18. Sphere, Re 100, $M=0.2$, $N=2$, $\Delta t=\eta_{\mathrm p}=5\times10^{-5}$. $C_p$ and wall vorticity agree with the literature apart from slight oscillations near separation; $C_d\approx1.06$, "only about 2 % error" against 1.08 and 1.09 (§5.4). **Shown**
19. Pitching–plunging NACA0012 (HiOCFD5 Case 2, Re 1000, $M=0.2$). The forces for $N=1,2,3$ agree with the body-fitted reference, and the results converge and become smoother towards $N=3$ (§5.5, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
20. Smoothing the mask with a Gaussian (Eq. B.1, widths $h/3$ and $h/8$) reduces the near-wall oscillations of $C_p$ but adds error in $C_f$ near the leading edge, with no accuracy gain; the wider smoothing is worse. All results use a sharp mask (App. B, Fig. B.23). **Shown**
21. Compressible cylinder (the Canuto & Taira benchmark, $M=0.1$–$0.5$, Re 40 steady and Re 100 unsteady). Mean drag, wake geometry, maximum lift and Strouhal number agree with the reference, and the trend with Mach number is captured (App. D, Fig. D.25). **Shown**
22. Literature values of the incompressible drag coefficient at Re 40 range from 1.48 to 1.59 (App. D). **Stated**
23. The simplified ray-casting mask applies only to convex bodies, which have two intercepts per ray; non-convex bodies need intersection counting (§4.2). **Stated**
24. The moving-body treatment (back-mapping to the body frame at $t=0$) is restricted to rigid motion. Too large a motion per step can lose mass through fast fluid–solid transitions (§4.3). **Stated**
25. Volume penalization is a diffuse-interface method: only inside and outside are known, not the exact wall position (§4.6). **Stated**
26. IDW-IP uses $2N_p$ to $3N_p$ nearest fluid points, where $N_p$ is the number of solution points per element. The paper says this choice balances accuracy and cost "after extensive testing" (§4.4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §5.1, p. 14: "the convergence rate becomes $O(\eta^{-1})$. This is different from … low-order schemes, where $O(\eta^{-0.5})$ is observed". The exponents have the wrong sign. Convergence as $\eta\to0$ is $O(\eta)$ and $O(\eta^{0.5})$, which agrees with the same paragraph's "$O(\sqrt\eta)$" and with Eq. (45), $\alpha=1/2$.
- §5.5 against Eq. (50): the text says "The airfoil keeps moving upward", while the printed plunge $b(t)=t^3(-8t^3+51t^2-111t+84)/16$ is not monotone on the simulated interval, whose snapshots (Fig. 21) reach $t=2.0$ *(compiler check, not in the sources: $b'(t)=-\tfrac{3}{16}t^2(t-2)^2(16t-21)$, so $b$ rises to $b(21/16)\approx1.142$ and falls back to $b(2)=1$)*. Either the description is loose or the formula is misprinted.
- Eq. (6): "The heat flux vector $\nabla q$ is given by $\partial q/\partial x_i=\lambda\,\partial T/\partial x_i$". This is garbled: the components $q_x,q_y,q_z$ used in Eqs. (3)–(5) need $q_i=\lambda\,\partial T/\partial x_i$.
- §3 calls the viscous system (3)–(5) "the following hyperbolic system".
- §3: "FR was first introduced by Hyunh" (Huynh).
- Eqs. (40)–(41): the IDW-IP weights are $1/d_1$, where $d_1$ is the distance to the normal line. A solution point on the normal gives $d_1=0$, and the paper does not say how this case is treated.
- §5.2 "nearly one quarter": 148296/532576 = 0.278.
- Eqs. (42)–(43) print $(\tau_yn-P_y)$ and $(\tau_xn-P_x)$ without defining these terms.
