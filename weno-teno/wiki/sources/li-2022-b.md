# Summary

The paper generalizes the fifth-order [WENO-ZN](/methods/weno-zn.md) scheme
of Shen, Zhang, Li & Peng (2020) to arbitrary odd order $2r-1$, with seventh
($r=4$) and ninth ($r=5$) order worked out. WENO-ZN modifies the
[WENO-Z weights](/methods/weno-z.md) twice. The global smoothness indicator is
no longer built from the [smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md)
$\beta_k$ but is the square of the $(2r-2)$th undivided difference on the
whole stencil, $\tau_{4r-4}=[\Delta^{2r-2}f_i]^2=O(\Delta x^{4r-4})$ in smooth
regions. The constant 1 in $d_k[1+(\tau/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q]$ is replaced by
an adaptive function $\Theta$ of the two extreme indicators
$\beta_0$, $\beta_{r-1}$, large on smooth stencils (weights pulled to $d_k$)
and small when a discontinuity lies in $S_0$ or $S_{r-1}$, which is the
heuristic for the [ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md).

A Taylor analysis of $\tau/\beta_k$ predicts that the $(2r-1)$th-order scheme
improves [accuracy at critical points](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)
up to order $2r-4$, while seventh-order WENO-Z fails at third-order critical
points; convergence tests on $x^ke^x$ confirm seventh and ninth order at
several critical-point orders. Spectral analysis with the
[approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
shows lower errors at medium wavenumbers. The background reviews
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md), [mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md),
[WENO-Z+](/methods/weno-z-plus.md), [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md) and
OWENO. The framework is the [conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
with [Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) in
[characteristic fields](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) and
[SSP RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Seventh-order tests (composite-wave
advection, [Lax](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md),
[shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md),
[forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) and the inviscid
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md)) show WENO7-ZN less
dissipative than WENO7-JS and WENO7-Z. WENO-Z$\eta$ and WENO-Z with
$\epsilon=\Delta x^m$ are excluded from the comparison as unable to "keep the
ENO property well".

# Key claims

Registry notation (linear weights $d_k$, candidate fluxes $\hat f^{(k)}$,
adaptive function $\Theta$; "$m$th-order critical point" means
$f'=\dots=f^{(m)}=0$ there; "order $n$" means $\alpha_k=d_k+O(\Delta x^n)$).

1. Fifth-order WENO-ZN attains fifth order at first- and second-order critical points with $q=2$ but cannot improve convergence at third- and higher-order critical points, limited by the smoothness indicators (Abstract, §1, §3.1). **Stated** (from Shen et al. 2020); **Shown** in Table 6
2. With $q=1$, fifth-order WENO-ZN gives fifth and fourth order at first- and second-order critical points (§3.1). **Stated** (from Shen et al. 2020)
3. If the leading term of the WENO-Z global indicator $\tau_{2r-1}$ is of the same order as that of $\beta_k$, WENO-Z reduces to the convergence rate of $r$th-order ENO (§2, below Eq. (15)). **Derived**
4. For seventh-order WENO-Z, $\tau_7=\lvert\beta_0-\beta_1-\beta_2+\beta_3\rvert=\lvert\tfrac43f_i'f_i^{(5)}-\tfrac{13}3f_i''f_i^{(4)}\rvert\Delta x^6+O(\Delta x^8)$ (Eq. (17), from the expansions of Box I, Eq. (16)). **Derived**
5. Seventh-order WENO-Z cannot improve the order at third-order critical points for $q=1$ or $q=2$ ($n=0$) (Table 4); Table 7 shows WENO7-Z orders 3.55, 3.39, 3.24 there. **Derived** + **Shown**
6. The $(2r-1)$th-order WENO-ZN weight is $\alpha_k=d_k\big[\Theta+\big(\tau_{4r-4}/(\beta_k+\epsilon)\big)^q\big]$, $\tau_{4r-4}=[\Delta^{2r-2}f_i]^2$, $\Theta=C_\Theta\big[(\beta_0+\beta_{r-1}-\lvert\beta_0-\beta_{r-1}\rvert+\epsilon)/(\lvert\beta_0-\beta_{r-1}\rvert+\epsilon)\big]^2$ (Eqs. (27)–(32)). **Stated** (definition)
7. $q=1$ is widely used at fifth order (less dissipation), $q=2$ suggested for higher-order WENO for robustness (§3.2). **Stated**
8. $C_\Theta=10$ in $\Theta$ efficiently increases the contribution of less-smooth stencils in a reasonable region and improves the resolution of high-frequency waves on coarse grids (§3.1, below Eq. (25)). **Stated** (from Shen et al. 2020)
9. The WENO-ZN global indicator is $O(\Delta x^{4r-4})$, that of WENO-Z$\eta$ with the optimal-order indicator $O(\Delta x^{2r+2})$; hence $(2r-1)$th-order WENO-ZN improves convergence at critical points up to order $2r-4$, WENO-Z$\eta$ only up to order $r-1$ (§3.3 (1), Table 5). **Derived**
10. Table 5 orders $n$ ($q=1$ / $q=2$): $r=3$: first-order critical point 4/8, second 2/4, third 0/0; $r=4$: 8/16, 6/12, 4/8, 2/4, 0/0 (first to fifth); $r=5$: 12/24, 10/20, 8/16, 6/12, 4/8, 2/4, 0/0 (first to seventh) (Table 5). **Derived**
11. ENO property: $S_0$ and $S_{r-1}$ cover the global stencil without overlap, so a discontinuity falls in one of them, $\Theta\ll1$, and the relative weights are governed by $(\tau_{4r-4}/(\beta_k+\epsilon))^q$, whose $k$-dependence comes only from $\beta_k$; on smooth stencils $\beta_0$ and $\beta_{r-1}$ share leading terms and $\Theta\gg1$ (§3.3 (2)). **Derived** (heuristic, no theorem)
12. For the test function of Eq. (33) (a jump at $x=0$), $\Theta\gg1$ and $\tau_7\gg\tau_{12}$ in smooth regions, and $\tau_{12}\sim\tau_7\gg\Theta$ at the discontinuity (Fig. 1; $\Theta\sim10^{10}$–$10^{15}$ in smooth parts). **Shown**
13. WENO-ZN keeps low dissipation and dispersion errors over a large range of medium wavenumbers compared with WENO-JS and WENO-Z, at fifth and seventh order, with the linear upwind schemes as references (§3.3 (3), Fig. 2). **Shown**
14. $f(x)=x^ke^x$, $x=0$ a $(k-1)$th-order critical point, $\Delta x=1.25\times10^{-2}\to1.56\times10^{-3}$: WENO5-ZN orders 4.99/5.00/5.00 (first order), 4.82/5.84/5.97 (second), 1.38/2.38/2.81 (third); WENO5-Z 2.11–2.32 at the second; WENO5-JS 2.00 (Table 6). **Shown**
15. WENO7-ZN orders 6.99/7.00/7.00 (second-order critical point), 6.99/7.00/7.00 (third), 7.43/7.80/7.91 (fourth); WENO7-Z 5.38/5.80/5.92, 3.55/3.39/3.24, 3.99/4.00/4.00; WENO7-JS 2.87–3.87, 3.24–3.55, 3.99–4.00; finest-grid WENO7-ZN errors $2.73\times10^{-20}$, $1.36\times10^{-19}$, $6.99\times10^{-16}$ (Table 7). **Shown**
16. WENO9-ZN orders 8.99/9.00/9.00 (third-order critical point), 8.99/9.00/9.00 (fourth), 9.00/9.00/9.00 (fifth), 7.04/7.07/9.09 (sixth); WENO9-Z 7.91–8.60, 3.11–3.84, 4.90–4.97, 5.88–5.89; WENO9-JS 6.30–7.12, 4.00, 5.23–5.54, 6.00 (Table 8). **Shown**
17. The numerical orders of Tables 6–8 agree with the analysis of Tables 4–5 (§4.1). **Stated** (Table 5 predicts $n=2$ or $4$ at the sixth-order critical point of $r=5$, where Table 8 observes 7.04–9.09)
18. WENO-Z with $\epsilon=\Delta x^m$ (Don & Borges) satisfies a sufficient condition for optimal order, but because $\beta_k$ and $\Delta x$ have different dimensions, such schemes may give oscillatory or dissimilar solutions depending on the reference values used for nondimensionalization (§4.1). **Stated** (from Shen et al. 2020)
19. High-order WENO-Z$\eta$ (Fan) produces apparent or strong oscillations in shock-tube problems (§1, §2, §4.1, §5). **Stated** (cited, not run here)
20. Composite-wave advection ($N_x=200$, $t=10$): WENO7-ZN gives the best result, especially on the square and half-ellipse waves; WENO7-Z gives an apparently asymmetric solution near the square wave (§4.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
21. Lax problem ($N_x=200$, $t=1.3$): WENO-ZN reduces the numerical dissipation near shocks (§4.3.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
22. Shu–Osher ($N_x=200$, $t=1.8$): WENO-ZN resolves the short waves better than WENO-JS and WENO-Z (§4.3.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
23. Blast waves ($N_x=400$, $t=0.038$): all three schemes capture the strong shock, WENO-ZN with less dissipation (§4.3.3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
24. Rayleigh–Taylor ($120\times480$, $t=1.95$): WENO-ZN gives richer unstable structures (§4.4.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
25. Shock–vortex interaction ($200\times100$, $t=0.6$): WENO-ZN is closest to the $2000\times1000$ WENO7-Z reference for the pressure along $y=0.5$ (§4.4.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
26. Forward-facing step ($600\times200$, $t=4$): all schemes capture the shocks; WENO-ZN gives a clearer roll-up of the vortex sheet (§4.4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown**
27. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex ($64^3$, $128^3$): WENO-JS dissipates the most kinetic energy, more at $128^3$ than the seventh-order linear upwind scheme at $64^3$, yet its enstrophy grows faster than that upwind run; WENO-ZN dissipates least of the three WENO schemes (§4.5, Fig. 11). **Shown**
28. Only seventh-order results are shown in §4; ninth-order schemes yield "similar conclusions" (§4). **Stated** (not shown)

- Eq. (36): $F(x,\alpha,a)=\sqrt{\max(1-\alpha^2(x-\alpha)^2,0)}$, with "$(x-\alpha)$" for "$(x-a)$".
- §4.4.1: "The computational domain is [0, 0.25] × [0.1]", while Eq. (45) has $0\le y\le1$.
- §4.4.1: gravity is introduced "by adding ρ and ρv to the flux of the y-momentum and the energy equations"; these are source terms.
- Table 5 footnote "A, B, and C are independent of k", while the $r=3$ rows print $B_k\Delta x^6$, $C_k\Delta x^8$, the $r=4$ row prints $C_k\Delta x^8$, and Table 4 gives $C_0=C_3\ne C_1=C_2$.

- Eqs. (12)–(13): $A_k$ is not defined.
- Eqs. (6)–(7) use $j$ for the cell index, $i$ elsewhere.
- Eq. (8) calls the Legendre-type basis on $[-1/2,1/2]$ "Hermite polynomials", while the Introduction says "Balsara et al. [16,17] then utilized Legendre polynomials".
- §4.4.3: "The forward-facing step problem is also originally from the paper of [30]", without a preceding citation of [30].
