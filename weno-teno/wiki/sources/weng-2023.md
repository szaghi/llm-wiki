# Summary

The paper presents **RSDFoam** (Real gas Shock and Detonation Foam), an
OpenFOAM 9 solver for unsteady inviscid shock and detonation simulation in one
to three dimensions with non-ideal thermodynamics at high pressure. It couples
blastFoam v5.0, which supplies the discretization (MUSCL reconstruction with
limiters, HLL-family and other approximate Riemann solvers, SSP Runge–Kutta
time stepping, 1D–3D adaptive mesh refinement), with Cantera v2.4, which
supplies the Redlich–Kwong and Noble–Abel equations of state, departure-function
thermodynamics, a fugacity-corrected mass-action law and a constant-volume
reactor. The reactive multispecies Euler equations are advanced by
[operator splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md): an explicit convection
step, an update of the primitive variables, and an implicit chemistry step
whose production rates are forward differences of the species concentrations
returned by a constant-volume reactor integrated over $\Delta t$. The real-gas
strength is measured by the non-dimensional attraction and covolume parameters
$A_{\mathrm{RK}}$ and $B_{\mathrm{RK}}$. The software architecture (Cantera
classes and four OpenFOAM–Cantera interfaces) is described.

Validation covers six cases, each against an equation-of-state-independent
theory solved with in-house SDToolbox and Cantera codes: a
[Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) with Redlich–Kwong gas
and a sweep of $B_{\mathrm{RK}}$ and $A_{\mathrm{RK}}/B_{\mathrm{RK}}$; an
oblique shock over a wedge with 2D AMR against the real-gas shock polar;
constant-volume ignition of H$_2$-air and DME-O$_2$; Chapman–Jouguet detonation
speeds of H$_2$-air up to 10 MPa against experiments; ZND structure with a
one-step reaction; and 2D [cellular detonation](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md)
with the Noble–Abel equation of state at 5 MPa. The reported agreement includes
detonation-speed errors below 0.1% (ideal gas) and at most 0.5%
(Redlich–Kwong), and mean cell widths of $18.3\,l_{1/2}$ (ideal gas) and
$20.2\,l_{1/2}$ (Noble–Abel). Real-gas runs take 2 to 6 times as long as
ideal-gas ones. The numerics are second order and taken unchanged from
blastFoam; the contribution is the thermodynamic coupling and its validation,
not a new discretization.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $p$ is the pressure (the source's $P$), $v$ the velocity
(the source's $\mathbf u$), $Y_k$ the mass fractions; $a_{\mathrm{RK}}$,
$b_{\mathrm{RK}}$ are the Redlich–Kwong attraction and covolume parameters and
$A_{\mathrm{RK}}=a_{\mathrm{RK}}p/(R^2T^2)$, $B_{\mathrm{RK}}=b_{\mathrm{RK}}p/(RT)$
their non-dimensional forms (page-local; see the Notation map).

1. RSDFoam is built in OpenFOAM 9 by connecting blastFoam v5.0 and Cantera v2.4: time integration, reconstruction and fluxes come from blastFoam; equations of state, thermodynamic functions and kinetics from Cantera (§3). **Stated**
2. blastFoam offers MUSCL reconstruction (upwind, linear, quadratic) with limiters (van Leer, minmod, superbee and others), the approximate Riemann solvers HLL, HLLC, HLLC-P, AUSM+, Tadmor and Kurganov, and SSP Runge–Kutta up to fourth order; the validation mainly uses quadratic MUSCL, the van Leer limiter, HLLC and second-order SSP Runge–Kutta (§3.1). **Stated**
3. Convection and chemistry are split: Eq. (12) for convection, then the primitive update, then an implicit chemistry step (Eq. (13)) whose rates are the forward difference $\dot R_k=(c_{k,1}-c_{k,0})/\Delta t$ of a Cantera constant-volume-reactor integration (Eq. (14)) (§3.1). **Stated**
4. The constant-volume reactor holds internal energy and density fixed, so its code is independent of the equation of state and needs no change for real gas; it uses adaptive steps with relative and absolute tolerances $10^{-12}$ and $10^{-15}$ (§3.2). **Stated**
5. The 1D AMR of Mao et al. and the 2D, axisymmetric and 3D AMR of Rettenmaier et al. (blastFoam's default) are merged and selected at run time (§3.1). **Stated**
6. Under the Redlich–Kwong equation of state the equilibrium constant equals the ideal-gas one (Eq. (9)), so Cantera's mass-action kinetics with fugacity coefficients (Eq. (8)) apply unchanged (§2.2, §3.2). **Stated** (cited)
7. Real-gas simulations take 2 to 6 times the run time of the same ideal-gas simulation (§4). **Stated** (no timing table)
8. Sod shock tube ($p_5/p_1=20$, $T_5/T_1=1$, $\gamma^0=1.4$, $A_{\mathrm{RK}}=B_{\mathrm{RK}}=0.1$, $\Delta x=1$ mm, CFL 0.1, $t=0.05$ s): the real gas gives a much smaller density ratio $\rho_5/\rho_1$; the shock speed changes only slightly ("relative difference is only 2.1%"), with shock Mach numbers 1.83 (ideal) and 1.76 (real); the real gas gives a wider expansion fan and a slower contact; the computations agree with the analytical solution (§4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. Sweeping $B_{\mathrm{RK}}$ from $6\times10^{-4}$ to $0.3$ with $A_{\mathrm{RK}}/B_{\mathrm{RK}}\in\{0.5,1,2\}$: the real-gas shock speed is below the ideal-gas one for small parameters and above it for large enough ones, and at fixed $B_{\mathrm{RK}}$ a larger $A_{\mathrm{RK}}/B_{\mathrm{RK}}$ lowers it: attraction decreases and covolume increases the shock speed (§4.1, Fig. 6, Table 1). **Shown**
10. Oblique shock (inflow $v_1/\sqrt{RT_1}=5$, $A_{\mathrm{RK}}=B_{\mathrm{RK}}=0.1$): the real-gas polar has a smaller maximum deflection, its strong branch below and its weak branch above the ideal-gas ones; the simulations (four AMR levels) recover only weak-shock solutions, close to the analytical polar (§4.2, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
11. Constant-volume reactor at 800 K, 10 MPa: the Redlich–Kwong gas ignites 3.4% (H$_2$-air) and 5.3% (DME-O$_2$) earlier than the ideal gas, with the same equilibrium state, and RSDFoam reproduces Cantera's profiles (§4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown**
12. Over temperature ranges the Redlich–Kwong ignition delay is always shorter, by 3.4%–7.1% (H$_2$-air) and 3.9%–6.4% (DME-O$_2$); the negative-temperature-coefficient behaviour of DME-O$_2$ at ambient pressure disappears at high pressure; first- and second-stage delays are reproduced (§4.3, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. Chapman–Jouguet speed of stoichiometric H$_2$-air at 300 K, 0.1–10 MPa: the Redlich–Kwong value exceeds the ideal-gas one from about 1 MPa, increasingly with pressure, and agrees better with Bauer's experiments (§4.4, Fig. 11). **Shown**
14. The 1D unsteady detonation speed of RSDFoam (base mesh equal to the induction length, three AMR levels) deviates from the Chapman–Jouguet value by less than 0.1% (ideal gas) and at most 0.5% (Redlich–Kwong); the transient speed at 7 MPa has a standard deviation of 41.0 m/s (§4.4). **Shown**
15. Detailed H$_2$-air chemistry above 1 MPa makes 1D detonations highly unstable, so the ZND validation uses a one-step reaction with $E_{a,1}=10R_uT_1$, $q=50R_uT_1$, $\gamma^0=1.4$, $A_{\mathrm{RK}}=B_{\mathrm{RK}}=0.1$ and a pre-exponential factor set for $l_{1/2}=1$ (§4.5). **Stated**
16. ZND: the real gas gives a higher post-shock pressure and a much smaller post-shock density, a 45% shorter half-reaction length and similar temperatures; the computation at $\Delta x=l_{1/2}/8$ agrees with ZND except for an under-resolved density peak (§4.5, Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
17. An effective resolution of $l_{1/2}/32$, by $\Delta x=l_{1/2}/4$ with three AMR levels or by a uniform mesh, resolves the density peak; the AMR run is at least 10 times faster (§4.5, Fig. 14). **Shown**
18. 2D cellular detonation (setup of Taileb et al., 300 K, 5 MPa, $\gamma=1.2$, Noble–Abel covolume $b=9.38\times10^{-4}$ m$^3$/kg, i.e. $B_{\mathrm{RK}}=0.023$, $E_{a,2}=20R_uT_1$, $q=50R_uT_1$): the Noble–Abel cells are quite regular and the ideal-gas cells significantly more irregular, so finite molecular volume enhances regularity, as Taileb et al. found (§4.6, Fig. 15). **Shown**
19. Cell widths measured by hand over $1000<x/l_{1/2}<1500$ (about 180 samples) range over 6–32 $l_{1/2}$ (ideal) and 10–27 $l_{1/2}$ (Noble–Abel), against Taileb's 9–37 and 7–35; the means are 18.3 and 20.2 $l_{1/2}$, confirming Taileb's value of about 20 $l_{1/2}$ for both (§4.6, Fig. 16). **Shown**
20. The differences from Taileb et al. are attributed to the order of the scheme (their ninth-order monotonicity-preserving reconstruction of characteristic variables against second-order MUSCL) and to AMR against a uniform mesh (§4.6). **Stated**
21. A frozen sound speed was added to Cantera and to OpenFOAM's thermophysical classes, and the gas state is updated from sensible internal energy, pressure and mass fractions by Newton–Raphson iteration (§3.2–§3.3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §2.2, after Eq. (9): "$\Delta G^0$ is the standard enthalpy change of the $j$th reaction"; the symbol and its use in $\exp(-\Delta G^0/R_uT)$ denote a Gibbs-energy change, as the same paragraph's "total Gibbs free energy" says.
- Eq. (9): the exponent $\sum_kv''_{i,j}-v'_{i,j}$ sums over $k$ a term indexed by $i$.
- §4.3 refers to "Eq. (20)" for the estimate of the reaction rate, which is Eq. (14).
- §4.1 places $A=B=0.1$ "in region 5" and converts it to $(T_1,P_1)$ values, and the Table 1 caption calls the conditions $(T_1,P_1)$. Since $B=bP/(RT)$ is proportional to $p$ and $p_5=20p_1$ at $T_5=T_1$, the two regions cannot both have $B=0.1$. Table 1 itself is self-consistent: with Eq. (5), $A/B=(0.42748/0.08664)(T_c/T)^{1.5}$, and its temperatures 711.2, 448.0 and 282.2 K are those of $A/B=0.5$, $1$, $2$ for $T_c=154.6$ K (computed 711.3, 448.1, 282.3 K) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §4.1: "The relative difference is only 2.1%. Quantitatively, the Mach number for IG and RG cases are 1.83 and 1.76": the Mach numbers differ by $(1.83-1.76)/1.83=3.8\%$, so the 2.1% is not their difference *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §4.3 names the second mixture "dimethyl ether (DME)-air" once, against "DME-O$_2$" in the captions of Figs. 9–10 and later text.
- Typos: "ℎ detonates the enthalpy" (§4.1), "RSFDFoam" (§4.3), "MUSLC" (§4.6), "an example pf oblique shock" (§4.2), "would to needed" (§4.4).
- Reference list: [44] prints "Combust Flame 2021;243:111945" and [34] gives pages 1–11 without a volume.
