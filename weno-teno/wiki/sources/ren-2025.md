# Summary

Ren et al. (2025) study the shock instability ([carbuncle](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md))
of fifth-order finite-volume WENO schemes for the 2D Euler equations. Earlier
[matrix stability analyses](/methods/matrix-stability-analysis.md) covered first-order
schemes (Dumbser, Moschetta and Gressier) and the authors' own second- and
third-order extensions. Here the analysis is extended to fifth-order
[WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) reconstruction with frozen nonlinear weights: the
perturbation of a cell couples to 13 cells with mid-point flux integration and to
45 with two Gauss points, and stability means that every eigenvalue of the
linearized operator has non-positive real part. The analysis is validated against
the measured growth rate of the transverse velocity on an $11\times11$
[steady normal shock](/benchmarks/steady-shock-problems.md) at $M_0=20$.

The findings: the fifth-order scheme is unstable with the
[Roe](/methods/roe-riemann-solver.md) and HLLC solvers, with a growth rate between
the first- and second-order ones; it is unstable with the dissipative
[HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) and van Leer fluxes and with the shock-stable
HLLC-ADC, hybrid SLAU and HLLC-EC fluxes, all stable at low order. The cause is
the reconstruction inside the numerical shock structure: every three-point
substencil there contains the discontinuity, all weights are non-zero and the
[ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) is lost. Even the smoothest single
quadratic stays unstable, while first-order reconstruction on the faces near the
shock is stable. The instability needs dissipation on both normal and transverse
faces and originates in the shock structure.
[Characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) mitigates
it without curing it.

From this the authors build MR-WENO-SD: the Zhu–Shu
[multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) with the
smoothness-indicator weights replaced by weights set by a pressure-jump and Mach
number shock detector, paired with an HLLC/HLL hybrid flux switched by the same
detector. It reaches fifth order on [density-wave advection](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md)
and is stable on the normal shock, the inviscid $M_0=20$
[cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), the
[Sedov blast](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) and an $M_0=8.1$ viscous cylinder,
whose stagnation heat flux is 0.81% off the Fay–Riddell value.

# Key claims

Registry notation: cell averages $\bar u_{i,j}$, one-sided states $u^\mp_{i+1/2,j}$
(source $\mathbf U^L$, $\mathbf U^R$), linear weights $d_k$, indicators $\beta_k$,
weights $\omega_k$, $\tau_5=\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert$, velocity $(v_1,v_2)$ with
$v_1$ normal to the shock. Page-local: $\delta u$ the perturbation about the steady
mean state; $\mathbb S$ the stability matrix and $\lambda(\mathbb S)$ its
eigenvalues (not the flux eigenvalues $\lambda_l$); $\lambda_{\mathrm{num}}$ the
measured growth rate; $\mathsf s\in[0,1]$ the numerical shock position;
$\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}$ the eigenvalue-smoothing threshold; $d_{m,n}$ and
$\bar d_{m,n}$ the multi-resolution linear weights; $\omega^{\mathrm P}_m$ the
weights of the polynomials; $f_{\mathrm{SD}}$ the shock detector; $\kappa_{\mathrm h}$
the HLLC/HLL blending factor.

1. The matrix stability analysis of Dumbser et al. assumes $u^-_{i+1/2,j}=\bar u_{i,j}$ and $u^+_{i+1/2,j}=\bar u_{i+1,j}$ (Eq. (23)), so it holds only for first-order schemes; a high-order analysis must include the reconstruction (§3.1). **Derived**
2. With nonlinear weights frozen (perturbations do not change $\omega_k$), the fifth-order linear model (27) couples $\delta u_{i,j}$ to 13 cells, $(i\pm1,\dots,i\pm3,j)$, $(i,j\pm1,\dots,j\pm3)$ and itself (Fig. 3c); with two Gauss points per face it couples 45 cells (Fig. 7, Eq. (C.12)) (§3.1, §3.4). **Derived**
3. Stability criterion: $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathbb S)\le0$ (Eq. (31)) for the linear time-invariant system $\frac{d}{dt}\delta u=\mathbb S\,\delta u$ (Eqs. (29)–(30)). **Derived**
4. Smoothing $\lvert\lambda\rvert$ by Eq. (32) with $\delta_{\mathrm{sm}}=10^{-4}$ makes the analysis reliable for non-differentiable fluxes: on the $11\times11$, $M_0=20$, Roe, fifth-order case with $\mathsf s=0$, the measured rate $\lambda_{\mathrm{num}}=2.17904$ is predicted as 2.65580 without smoothing (error 21.88%) and 2.19187 with it (0.59%) (Table 1, §3.3). **Shown**
5. Over $\mathsf s\in\{0,0.1,\dots,0.9,0.99\}$, $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathbb S)$ agrees with $\lambda_{\mathrm{num}}$ in both the stable and the unstable ranges (Fig. 6); the authors state that similar agreement holds for other solvers and conditions. **Shown** (Roe); **Stated** (others)
6. Mid-point and two-point Gauss flux integration give the same verdicts for Roe and HLL with WENO-Z on the $11\times11$, $M_0=20$ normal shock for $\mathsf s=0,\dots,1$: Roe is stable only at $\mathsf s=0.8,0.9$, HLL only at $\mathsf s=0.6,0.7$ (Table 2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
7. With Roe or HLLC the fifth-order scheme is unstable; its $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathbb S)$ is smaller than the second-order (MUSCL, van Albada limiter) value and larger than the first-order one (Figs. 9–10, §4.1.1). **Shown**
8. Roe, $\mathsf s=0.1$: the fifth-order mid-point run collapses (NaN) at $t=47.8$; the fifth-order Gauss run has a lower growth rate but collapses earlier, at $t=42.2$ (Fig. 13, §4.1.1). **Shown**
9. The authors attribute the slower growth at fifth order to the "smearing" of perturbations by the weighted combination of stencils; the smearing slows the growth but does not decide stability (§4.1.1, §4.1.3). **Stated**
10. With HLL and van Leer, believed to capture strong shocks stably at first and second order, the fifth-order scheme has eigenvalues with positive real part on the normal shock and the shock profile distorts (Figs. 11–12); inviscid blunt-body runs ($M_0=20$, $240\times40$) are unstable too (Fig. 15, §4.1.2). **Shown**
11. HLLC-ADC, hybrid SLAU and HLLC-EC, shock-stable at first and second order, are unstable in the fifth-order scheme (Figs. 16–19, §4.1.2). **Shown**
12. Inside the numerical shock structure every three-point substencil contains a discontinuity, so all nonlinear weights are non-zero: for $\rho^-_{6+1/2}$ (HLL, Fig. 20), $\omega=(0.21135,0.62458,0.16407)$; the reconstruction loses the ENO property near shocks (Table 3, §4.1.3). **Shown**
13. Using only the smoothest quadratic (the ENO choice) on the faces near the shock structure still gives $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathbb S)>0$ (Fig. 21); zeroth-degree reconstruction on those faces is stable (Fig. 22); high-order schemes "should degenerate to employing zeroth-degree polynomials to reconstruct variables near shocks" (§4.1.3). **Shown**
14. Wider stencils carry perturbations into undisturbed cells; the authors call this the "more decisive" factor that makes second-, third- and fifth-order schemes worse than first order with low-dissipation solvers (§4.1.3). **Stated**
15. The mid-point and Gauss verdicts agree because the Gauss-point reconstruction inside a cell uses the same WENO procedure and meets the same problem in the shock structure (§4.1.3). **Stated**
16. Hybrid-1 (fifth-order Roe on transverse faces, first-order van Leer on normal faces) and hybrid-2 (the reverse) are both unstable, on the normal shock and on the blunt body (Eq. (40), Figs. 24–26): the fifth-order instability is a "multidimensional coupled problem", as at low order (§4.2). **Shown**
17. The eigenvector of the most unstable mode ($\lambda=0.67356$, Roe, fifth order, $M_0=20$, $\mathsf s=0.1$) has a sawtooth distribution at $i=6$, the numerical shock structure (Fig. 27); snapshots show the perturbation appearing there and convecting downstream, while upstream it "always remains zero" (Fig. 28, §4.3). **Shown**
18. Local characteristic decomposition (App. B) lowers $\max\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda(\mathbb S)$ against primitive-variable reconstruction at every Mach number tested, but it stays positive (Figs. 29–30, §4.4). **Shown**
19. Plain MR-WENO with $\bar d_{m,n}=10^m$ puts weights $(0.21940,0.22749,0.55311)$ on the zeroth-, second- and fourth-degree polynomials for $\rho^-_{6+1/2}$ and gives a "subtle" non-carbuncle anomaly (Table 4, Fig. 32a); with $\bar d_{m,n}=1$ the weights are $(0.85337,0.08849,0.05815)$ and the normal shock is stable (Table 5, Fig. 32b), but the cylinder residual does not reach machine zero (Figs. 33–34, §5.1.1). **Shown**
20. The MR-WENO-SD weights (48)–(49) give $\omega^{\mathrm P}=(0.99999999,\ 1.98\times10^{-14},\ 1.27\times10^{-8})$ for $\rho^-_{6+1/2}$, i.e. zeroth-degree reconstruction in the shock structure (Table 6). **Shown**
21. MR-WENO-SD with HLLC/HLL reaches fifth order on 1D density-wave advection ($n=10,\dots,320$): $L_1$ orders 4.9055–5.0290, $L_\infty$ 4.8084–4.9973, $L_2$ 5.4265–5.5007 (Table 7, §5.2.1). **Shown**
22. On the $50\times25$ normal shock ($M_0=20$, $\mathsf s=0.1$) all eigenvalues of MR-WENO-SD + HLLC/HLL have negative real part and the density contour is stable (Fig. 35, §5.2.2). **Shown**
23. Inviscid $M_0=20$ cylinder (CFL 0.5): WENO + HLLC is severely unstable, while MR-WENO-SD + HLLC/HLL is stable and its residual converges better than plain MR-WENO; MR-WENO-SD is also used for the Gauss-point values inside cells (Figs. 36–37, §5.2.3). **Shown**
24. Double Mach reflection (Mach 10): WENO + HLLC gives a kinked Mach stem; the proposed scheme does not (Fig. 39, §5.2.4). **Shown**
25. Sedov blast ($960\times960$): WENO + HLLC shows grid-aligned instabilities; MR-WENO-SD + HLLC/HLL is stable (Fig. 40, §5.2.5). **Shown**
26. Viscous $M_0=8.1$ cylinder ($160\times120$, first cell $5.0\times10^{-5}$ m): WENO + HLLC is unstable with an unphysical heat flux; MUSCL + HLLC/HLL is stable but its heat flux is unsatisfactory on this grid; MR-WENO-SD + HLLC/HLL gives a stagnation heat flux of 2.44 against the Fay–Riddell 2.46 (0.81%) (Figs. 41–42, §5.2.6). **Shown**
27. "When the state within the numerical shock structure becomes closer to the downstream region, the computation tends to be more stable" (§6). **Stated** (the stable cases of Table 2 sit at $\mathsf s=0.6$–$0.9$)

Scheme details (§5.1, Eqs. (41)–(51)). The detector is
$f_{\mathrm{SD},i+1/2,j}=\big(1-\exp(-\max(M_{i,j},M_{i+1,j})\,\lvert p_{i,j}-p_{i+1,j}\rvert/(p_{i,j}+p_{i+1,j}))\big)^4$,
small in smooth flow and near 1 at shocks. A detected shock on the faces
$i\pm1/2$ of the reconstruction direction, or on the transverse faces, selects the
constant; one at $i\pm3/2$ selects the quadratic; otherwise the quartic is used.
The flux is $(1-\kappa_{\mathrm h})h^{\mathrm{HLLC}}+\kappa_{\mathrm h}h^{\mathrm{HLL}}$,
with $\kappa_{\mathrm h}$ the maximum of $f_{\mathrm{SD}}$ over the face and its four
transverse neighbours.

# Printed slips

- §2.3: "As advocated by Titarev and Toro [58]" and "[58]" again for the Gauss-point procedure; ref. [58] is Coralic & Colonius (J. Comput. Phys. 274, 2014), and Titarev & Toro is ref. [94], cited correctly in App. C.
- Eq. (13): the WENO-Z weight is $a^z_m/\sum_l\alpha^z_l$, with $\alpha$ in the denominator for the numerator's $a$.
- Eq. (27), last line (checked on the page image, p. 7): "$\boldsymbol\theta^R_{i,j+1/2}\mathbf U_{i,j+3}$" and "$\boldsymbol\theta^L_{i-1/2,j}\mathbf U_{i-3,j}$" lack the $\delta$; the same omission recurs in (A.4) and (B.6).
- App. C: in (C.11) and the matching line of (C.12) the right-state term reads "$\alpha^R_{n,j+3}\kappa^k_{n,j+2}\delta\mathbf U_{n,j+2}$" ($j+3$ against $j+2$); in (C.8) "$\kappa^k_{i-2,n}\delta\mathbf U^k_{i-2,j}$" mixes the Gauss-point value with the cell index of the sum, and "$\alpha^R_{i+3,n}$" stands for $\alpha^{R,k}_{i+3,j}$.
- §4.2: "$\mathbf F_{\text{hybrid/2}}$" for hybrid-2. §5.2.4: the double Mach reflection text twice names "the MR-WENO scheme with the HLLC/HLL solver" for the proposed MR-WENO-SD scheme.
- Table 7: the $L_2$ orders are 5.43–5.50 at every level while the $L_1$ and $L_\infty$ orders are about 5, and the $L_2$ error is smaller than the $L_1$ error at every $n$ (e.g. $n=10$: $L_1=1.1983\times10^{-3}$, $L_2=4.2921\times10^{-4}$). The paper does not define its norms. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: for norms normalized by the number of cells, $L_1\le L_2\le L_\infty$ by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, so the printed $L_2$ column is not of that kind.)*
- §4.4 states that §§4.1–4.3 reconstruct primitive variables, while §2.2 and §3.1 present reconstruction and analysis in conservative variables and Table 3 labels its weights for $\rho$.
- Eq. (44) writes the weight regularization as $\varepsilon$, the symbol of the shock position of §3.2; Eq. (9) uses $\epsilon$.
- Ref. [72] (Roe 1981) prints the DOI 10.1006/jcph.1997.5705, whose suffix names 1997; recorded as printed.
