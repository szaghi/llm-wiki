# Summary

Ding & Wu (2024a) build a finite-volume method for 2D ideal MHD that is both
discretely [divergence-free](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) (DDF)
and provably positivity-preserving for density and internal energy, a combination
the authors call unique among existing methods. The scheme discretizes the
Godunov–Powell form $\partial_tu+\nabla\cdot f(u)=-(\nabla\cdot B)S(u)$ with
$S(u)=(0,B,v,v\cdot B)^\top$, a [Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md)
and Gauss or Gauss–Lobatto edge quadrature.

The paper has three ingredients. First, a DDF projection: a minimal-norm
correction applied to each cell's own reconstructed interface values of the normal
field, so that the quadrature-based discrete divergence of the cell vanishes
exactly. It needs no reconstruction polynomial, so it fits finite-volume WENO,
which returns only point values; it is proved to keep $k$-th order accuracy.
Second, a source discretization built from the face jumps $[\![B_\ell]\!]$ and
averages $\{\!\{u\}\!\}$ at the quadrature nodes, which the authors say gives a
milder CFL condition than Wu & Shu's earlier ones. Third, a
[Zhang–Shu-type scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) on point
values (density, then the full state for internal energy), which keeps the DDF
condition because the discrete divergence is linear.

The main theorem ([bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md)) uses the
[geometric quasilinearization](/methods/geometric-quasilinearization.md) of the MHD
admissible set: forward Euler, hence SSP-RK3, stays in $G$ when
$\Delta t(a_1/\Delta x+a_2/\Delta y)$ is below the endpoint Gauss–Lobatto weight,
with a Lax–Friedrichs speed enlarged by a jump term. In the proof the divergence
terms cancel exactly once the DDF condition holds.

The tests use second-order (van Albada) and fifth-order (finite-volume WENO-Z,
characteristic, four Gauss–Lobatto nodes) schemes: a low-pressure
[magnetized vortex](/benchmarks/magnetized-vortex.md) for the orders, the
[Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md), the rotor and a strongly
magnetized [blast wave](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md), and a strongly magnetized
[Mach-800 jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md). With the projection the
divergence error stays at round-off. Without the projection or the limiter, the
fifth-order WENO code oscillates or crashes.

# Key claims

Registry notation. Page-local: $\mathsf w_\mu$, $\mu=1,\dots,Q$, the normalized edge
quadrature weights ($\sum_\mu\mathsf w_\mu=1$); $\hat\omega_1=1/(N_{\mathrm{GL}}(N_{\mathrm{GL}}-1))$
the endpoint weight of the $N_{\mathrm{GL}}$-point Gauss–Lobatto rule, $N_{\mathrm{GL}}=\lceil(k+2)/2\rceil$;
$k$ the design order; $\nabla_h\cdot B_{ij}$ the discrete divergence (9); $a_\ell$ the
Lax–Friedrichs speed in direction $\ell$, $\hat a_\ell$ its wave-speed lower bound
and $\beta_\ell$ its jump correction; $\rho e$ the internal energy density; $v_*$,
$B_*$ the free auxiliary variables of the GQL (see the Notation map).

1. The method preserves both a discrete divergence-free condition and the positivity of density, pressure and internal energy, which the authors call unique among existing methods (§1). **Stated**
2. Violating the discrete divergence-free condition even slightly can make the cell averages of finite-volume or DG schemes for the conservative MHD system lose positivity; exact smooth solutions of the conservative system may lose positivity if $\nabla\cdot B=0$ is slightly violated, while those of the Godunov–Powell system cannot (§1, citing Wu 2018 and Wu & Shu 2018, 2019). **Stated**
3. The high-order positivity-preserving schemes of Balsara and of Cheng et al. were not rigorously proved positivity-preserving; only 1D methods of Cheng et al. were, under assumptions on exact Riemann solutions, and the multidimensional ones were only conjectured (§1). **Stated**
4. Positivity requires the interface values to satisfy at once the DDF condition (9) and the admissibility conditions (10): all quadrature-node values in $G$ and, for $k\ge3$, the interior remainder state $\Pi_{ij}$ of the Gauss–Lobatto decomposition in $G$ (§2.1, Eqs. 9–10). **Proved** (as hypotheses of Thm 2.1)
5. Thm 2.1: if $\bar u_{ij}\in G$ and (9)–(10) hold, the forward-Euler update of scheme (6) with source (12) stays in $G$ under $0<\Delta t(a_1/\Delta x+a_2/\Delta y)<\hat\omega_1$ (Eq. 15), with $a_\ell\ge\hat a_\ell+\beta_\ell$ (Eq. 16) and $\beta_1=\max\lvert[\![B_1]\!]\rvert/(2\sqrt{\{\!\{\rho\}\!\}})$ over $x$-faces and nodes (§2.1, proof §3.2). **Proved**
6. Each of the three ingredients (DDF projection, limiter, source (12)) is essential; removing any of them may lose positivity (§2.1). **Stated** (supported in part by Examples 4.2–4.5: **Shown**)
7. The source discretization (12) is new and differs from Wu & Shu's; as a result the CFL condition (15) is milder (§2.1). **Stated** (no side-by-side constants given)
8. Positivity extends to SSP-RK3 because SSP methods are convex combinations of forward-Euler steps and $G$ is convex (Rem. 2.2). **Derived**
9. With the three-point Gauss rule, negative linear weights appear in WENO reconstruction at the midpoint (Shi, Hu & Shu 2002); the fifth-order scheme therefore uses the four-point Gauss–Lobatto rule (§2.2.2). **Stated**
10. At second order, the minimal change of the slopes (26) gives (27)–(28); the induced change of the point values (29)–(30) is the Euclidean projection of the origin on a line, and the map is a matrix $P$ with $P^2=P$, $P^\top=P$ (Prop. 2.3, Rem. 2.4, Eq. 32). **Proved**
11. At second order, the slope modification (28) is equivalent to the point-value projection (29) (Prop. 2.5). **Proved**
12. Thm 2.6: the high-order DDF projection (33)–(34), the same corrections $A_1$ at all nodes of the $x$-faces and $A_2$ at all nodes of the $y$-faces of a cell, gives $\nabla_h\cdot\tilde B_{ij}=0$ exactly. **Proved**
13. Thm 2.7: if $B\in C^k$ is exactly divergence-free and the reconstructed point values are $O(h_\Delta^k)$ accurate, the projected values stay $O(h_\Delta^k)$ accurate; the key step is $\lvert\nabla_h\cdot\hat B_{ij}\rvert=O(h_\Delta^{k-1})$, so $\lvert A_\ell\rvert=O(h_\Delta^k)$ (Eq. 36). **Proved**
14. The DDF projection uses only point values, so it suits finite-volume WENO and is "very easy to implement"; polynomial-based divergence-free techniques cannot be applied when only point values exist (Rem. 2.8, §2.3.2). **Stated**
15. Thm 2.9: the limiter (37)–(38) keeps the DDF condition (9) and enforces (10). **Proved**
16. The limiter does not destroy high-order accuracy (Example 4.1). **Shown**
17. The coupling of the point values by the DDF condition invalidates the classical convex-decomposition argument of Zhang & Shu for the positivity analysis; the GQL is used instead (§3). **Stated**
18. Lemma 3.1 (from Wu & Shu): $G=G_*$, the set of states with $u\cdot n_1>0$ and $u\cdot n_*+\lvert B_*\rvert^2/2>0$ for all $v_*,B_*\in\mathbb R^3$, $n_*=(\lvert v_*\rvert^2/2,-v_*,-B_*,1)^\top$ (§3.1). **Stated** (proved in the cited work)
19. In the proof the divergence remainder reduces to $\Delta t(v_*\cdot B_*)(\tfrac12\widehat{[\![B]\!]}_{ij}-\nabla_h\cdot\{\!\{B\}\!\}_{ij})=-\Delta t(v_*\cdot B_*)\nabla_h\cdot B_{ij}$, which vanishes by (9): "the DDF condition and the proper approximation to the source terms eliminate the impact of magnetic divergence terms on the PP property" (§3.2, Abstract). **Proved**
20. Example 4.1 (vortex, $\gamma=5/3$, $t=0.05$, $N_x=20,\dots,1280$): the second-order scheme reaches orders 2.42–2.59 on the finest grids, the fifth-order scheme 4.80–5.05 at $N_x=1280$, with $L^1$ errors $2.50\times10^{-11}$ ($v_2$), $1.61\times10^{-11}$ ($B_1$), $2.04\times10^{-11}$ ($p$) (Table 4.1). **Shown**
21. Example 4.2 (Orszag–Tang, $200\times200$): without the DDF projection the fifth-order solution shows "obvious oscillations" and severe instability at $t=4$; with it the divergence error stays at round-off; without both projection and limiter the fifth-order WENO code breaks down at $t=3.11$ (Figs. 4.1, 4.2a). **Shown**
22. Example 4.3 (rotor, $400\times400$, $t=0.295$): the divergence errors are about $10^{-13}$ with the projection and grow quickly without it; without it nonphysical structures appear in the Mach number (Figs. 4.2b, 4.3, 4.4). **Shown**
23. Example 4.4 (blast, $B_1=100/\sqrt{4\pi}$, pressure $10^3$ inside and $0.1$ outside, $200\times200$): the DDF positivity-preserving schemes are robust; fifth-order WENO without the projection shows oscillations and nonphysical structures; with the limiter off the code breaks down on a negative pressure (Figs. 4.2c, 4.5). **Shown**
24. Example 4.5 (jet, $v=800$, $B_2=\sqrt{200}$, $200\times600$): with the limiter off the code blows up quickly; with it the results are comparable with those of Wu & Shu and Wu, Jiang & Shu (Fig. 4.6). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (55):** "$\lambda_2=\frac{\alpha_2^{LF}\Delta t}{\Delta x}$"; the definition on p. 6 is $\lambda_2=\alpha_2^{LF}\Delta t/\Delta y$.
- **Eq. (59)**, last-but-one line: "$-\frac12\frac{\hat\alpha_2^{LF}\Delta t}{\Delta x}[\dots]$" should carry $\Delta y$, as the line above it in the same display does.
- **Eq. (61)**, first two lines: the sums "$-\frac{\beta_1\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sum_{\mu=1}^Q[(\{\{U\}\}^\mu_{i+\frac12,j}+\{\{U\}\}^\mu_{i-\frac12,j})\cdot n^*+|B^*|^2]$" (and the $\beta_2$ term) omit the quadrature weight $\omega_\mu$, which the result in the next line requires.
- **End of the proof of Thm 2.1** (p. 19): "$\lambda_2\ge(\hat\alpha_2^{LF}+\beta_2)\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}$" and "$\frac{2\hat\omega_2\lambda_2}{\lambda}>\dots$": $\hat\omega_2$ should be $\hat\omega_1$ (the only one defined, and the one used in the preceding display), and $\Delta x$ should be $\Delta y$ in both.
- **Example 4.3:** "$\phi=(r_2-r)/(r_2-r_0)$"; $r_0$ is not defined, only $r_1=0.1$ and $r_2=0.115$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* continuity of $\rho=1+9\phi$ with the inner value $10$ at $r=r_1$ needs $\phi(r_1)=1$, that is $r_0=r_1$; the velocity $\phi\,(\cdot)/r$ then also matches the inner $(\cdot)/r_1$ at $r=r_1$.
- **Example 4.4:** the final time is not stated in the text.
- Typos: "the theoretical CFL condition (15) for PP property milder than" (missing "is", §2.1); "it satisfied by" (§1); "fist-order" (§3); ref. [9] "magnetodydrodynamic".
