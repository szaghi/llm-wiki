# Summary

The paper proposes a new [split convective form](/methods/split-convective-forms.md),
**Div-rot**, for the Navier–Stokes convective term. It is a product-form
discretization plus a telescoping, "zero-consistent" correction to the
pressure gradient, so it conserves momentum a priori. For a discretely
divergence-free velocity it reduces to the classical rotational form
$-v\times\omega+\tfrac12\nabla\lvert v\rvert^2$. The earlier
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
splittings (Morinishi, Feiereisen, Kennedy–Gruber, Coppola, Rozema) reduce
instead to the skew-symmetric form. Div-rot is obtained by writing the
advective term with the rotation tensor and substituting it into the product
form; the strain-tensor analogue is EMAC.

The analysis uses diagonal-norm finite-difference
[summation-by-parts operators](/operators/summation-by-parts-operator.md)
and the two-point-flux ([flux differencing](/methods/flux-differencing.md))
representation of split forms, together with a telescoping "splitting error
operator". With these tools the paper proves that incompressible Div-rot is
KEP and helicity preserving, and that EMAC is not discretely KEP because the
discrete product rule fails. It introduces Div-str, a KEP and
angular-momentum-preserving rendition of EMAC, and a one-parameter KEP family
that contains Morinishi's form. The compressible extension uses
square-root-density variables $\sqrt\rho\,v_i$, which give geometric density
averages; its one-parameter family contains Rozema et al.'s cubic splitting. It
is an alternative to the arithmetic-average family of Coppola et al.
([entropy-conservative and KEP two-point fluxes](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md);
no [logarithmic mean](/operators/logarithmic-mean.md) appears). Div-rot's
implied kinetic-energy flux does not average velocities from both sides of an
interface, so a total-energy flux exactly consistent with an internal-energy
flux exists.

The numerics use sixth-order compact central differences and RK4: an
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) run over 100 vortex
widths and the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at
Re 1600. Div-rot is more robust than the divergence form but aliases more than
Feiereisen or Kennedy–Gruber. With tangent-filter solution filtering it gives
the lowest density error. An appendix reads filtering as
[artificial dissipation](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md) acting on
[entropy](/properties/entropy-stability.md) and proposes momentum-only
filtering, which promotes entropy stability. The
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) is measured
with the Schranner et al. metric.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's velocity $u_i$ is $v_i$,
its pressure $P$ is $p$, its SBP norm $\mathcal P$, $\mathcal Q$ and boundary
operator $E$ are $M$, $Q$ and $B$, its derivative operator $\mathcal D_j$ is
$D_j$, its splitting error operator $\mathcal E_j$ is $\mathcal S_j$ (as on the
concept pages) and its diagonal matrices of nodal data ($U_j$, $R$) are kept. The
two-point function $\tilde F(a,b)_{mn}=\tfrac12(a_mb_n+a_nb_m)$, written
$(\!(a;b)\!)$ (the product mean) on the concept pages, equals
$2\{\!\{a\}\!\}\{\!\{b\}\!\}-\{\!\{ab\}\!\}$, and $(\!(1;a)\!)=\{\!\{a\}\!\}$.

1. Incompressible Div-rot, $V_jD_jv_i+V_iD_jv_j-\tfrac12\delta_{ij}\mathcal S_j(v_k,v_k)$, conserves momentum a priori and, for $D_jv_j=0$, preserves kinetic energy discretely with kinetic-energy flux $\tfrac12(\!(v_j;v_iv_i)\!)$ (§3.1, Table 1, Eq. 15). **Proved** (SBP matrix algebra)
2. For a discretely solenoidal velocity Div-rot coincides with the classical rotational form (§3.1, after Eq. 15). **Derived**
3. Kinetic-energy preservation of the rotational form and of Div-rot holds only in the summed multi-dimensional sense (index relabeling under the summation convention), not component by component (Remark 3). **Derived**
4. The strain-based EMAC of Charnyi et al. is not discretely KEP under diagonal-norm SBP algebra: a residual $\int_\Omega v_j(2v_i\delta_jv_i-\delta_jv_iv_i)\,d\Omega\approx O(\Delta x^p)$ remains, because $(\!(v_j;v_j)\!)\ne(\!(1;v_jv_j)\!)$; the residual is indefinite and destroys the $L^2$ energy estimate (Eq. 16). **Proved** (algebra; indefiniteness **Stated**)
5. Div-str, twice Morinishi minus Div-rot, $D_jV_jv_i+\tfrac12\delta_{ij}\mathcal S_j(v_k,v_k)$, is KEP as a weighted combination of two KEP splittings and preserves angular momentum; it re-interprets EMAC in a form that is KEP under discrete SBP algebra (Eq. 17, footnote 1, Table 1). **Derived**
6. $\alpha\,$Div-rot $+(1-\alpha)\,$Div-str is a one-parameter family of incompressible KEP splittings, characterized by the small-scale pressure-gradient correction $\frac{1-2\alpha}2\delta_{ij}\mathcal S_j(v_k,v_k)$; $\alpha=1/2$ recovers Morinishi (Eq. 18). **Derived**
7. Discrete global preservation for the six incompressible splittings (Div, Prod, EMAC, Morinishi, Div-rot, Div-str): momentum a priori for all; kinetic energy (if $D_jv_j=0$) for Morinishi, Div-rot and Div-str; angular momentum a priori only for Div and Div-str; vorticity (if solenoidal) for all; enstrophy for none; helicity (if solenoidal) only for Div-rot (Table 2, Appendix A). **Proved**/**Derived**
8. The rotational form, hence Div-rot, does not discretely preserve enstrophy, locally or globally (App. A.3, Eq. A.10). **Derived**
9. Div-rot preserves helicity with an explicit discrete helicity flux plus a pressure flux (Eq. A.12); Div-str and the others carry a spurious term. **Derived**
10. All splittings preserve global vorticity; Div-rot induces the fewest local error terms, the others carry extra telescoping terms (Eqs. A.7, A.8). **Derived**
11. Compressible Div-rot, with mass flux $(\!(\sqrt\rho;\sqrt\rho v_j)\!)$ and the momentum form of Table 3, preserves kinetic energy with flux $\tfrac12(\!(\sqrt\rho v_j;v_i\sqrt\rho v_i)\!)$ and needs no solenoidal condition; it is "the first analogue to the rotational form for compressible flows" (Table 3, Eq. 24). **Proved** (SBP algebra; priority claim **Stated**, "to the author's knowledge")
12. The square-root density weighting must be applied to both the mass and the momentum equations, and it yields geometric, not arithmetic, density averages in the fluxes (§3.2). **Derived**
13. The compressible EMAC of Both et al. is not KEP under the discrete calculus (Eq. 25). **Derived**
14. Compressible Div-str is twice Rozema minus Div-rot; $\alpha\,$Div-rot $+(1-\alpha)\,$Div-str is a one-parameter compressible KEP family with geometric density averages that recovers Rozema et al.'s cubic splitting at $\alpha=1/2$ (Eq. 26), an alternative to the Coppola et al. family (convex combination of the Feiereisen and Coppola splittings, arithmetic density averages, Kennedy–Gruber at even weighting). **Derived**
15. Div-rot is, to the author's knowledge, the only KEP method whose kinetic-energy flux squares the same velocity variable rather than averaging the velocities on the two sides of the interface, argued to promote realizability when they have opposite sign; hence $(\!(\breve v_j;\breve e_{tot})\!)-\tfrac12(\!(\breve v_j;\breve v_iv_i)\!)=(\!(\breve v_j;\breve e)\!)$ with $\breve\phi=\sqrt\rho\phi$ (§3.2, Remark 4). **Stated** (realizability) / **Derived** (identity)
16. Because Div-rot uses a product form in the momentum equation it is expected to alias without numerical or physical dissipation, so de-aliasing by solution filtering may be needed (§3.2, §4). **Stated**, then **Shown**
17. The total energy of the KEP methods is built as an internal-energy flux plus the kinetic-energy flux induced by mass and momentum (following Kuya et al.), with pressure work in product form and internal energy split like the kinetic-energy flux; the discrete equations then satisfy a neutral $L^2$-type stability statement in $[\sqrt\rho,\sqrt\rho v_i,\sqrt{\rho e}]$ (§4, Table 4, footnote 3). **Stated**
18. Isentropic vortex without filtering: the divergence form becomes unstable within five vortex widths and Div-rot within fifteen; Feiereisen and Kennedy–Gruber stay stable for 100 widths but are noisy (Fig. 1a). **Shown**
19. Isentropic vortex with solution filtering: all forms are stabilized; Div-rot has the lowest density error over 100 widths; Kennedy–Gruber with all-variable filtering keeps kinetic energy but decreases entropy and increases the density RMS; after 100 widths $\rho_{\min}$ is 0.5479 (exact), 0.5069 (Feiereisen), 0.4490 (Kennedy–Gruber), 0.6261 (Div-rot) (Figs. 1, 2). **Shown**
20. Without filtering the divergence form shows kinetic-energy growth, entropy decay and density-RMS growth (Fig. 1b–d). **Shown**
21. Taylor–Green vortex at Re 1600: the divergence form is unstable on coarse grids (stable on $512^3$, footnote 5); the KEP forms (Kennedy–Gruber, Div-rot) are robust even when severely under-resolved; at $64^3$ Div-rot has larger density variability and numerical dissipation, attributed to aliasing; the KEP forms agree as the grid is refined (Fig. 3). **Shown**
22. Taylor–Green vortex at $t\approx9t_c$: Div-rot recovers the expected vortical structures with less noise at low resolution than Kennedy–Gruber (Fig. 4). **Shown** (qualitative)
23. Filtering the total-energy variable tends to decrease global entropy, filtering momentum to increase it, and filtering mass is indefinite (App. B, Eqs. B.2–B.5). **Derived** (filter operator non-positive, conservative, periodic, identity norm, semi-discrete limit)
24. Momentum-only, entropy-stability-promoting filtering gives strict kinetic-energy decay and entropy increase for all KEP splittings; it admits more vortex incoherence than all-variable filtering but avoids vortex-core deepening; $\rho_{\min}$ after 100 widths 0.5677 (divergence), 0.6663 (Feiereisen), 0.6671 (Kennedy–Gruber), 0.6981 (Div-rot) (Figs. B.5, B.6). **Shown**
25. The implied two-point fluxes are general and usable in finite-volume, DG and flux-reconstruction schemes (§5). **Stated**
26. Discrete kinetic-energy preservation in time requires special (implicit symplectic, or relaxation-type) integrators; temporal errors are not studied and are presumed negligible at small $\Delta t$ (§4.1.1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §4.2.1: "time step size of $\Delta t=t_c/10^4$" and, two sentences later, "$\Delta t=10^{-3}$ … yields a $CFL_{V_0,1D}\approx0.01$ relative to a $N=64^3$ grid"; the two differ by a factor 10. *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\Delta x=2\pi L/64=0.098L$, so a CFL of 0.01 needs $\Delta t\approx10^{-3}t_c$; $t_c/10^4$ is the inconsistent value.)*
- Table 3, EMAC row: the momentum term is printed with $\tfrac12\delta_{ij}\mathcal E_j(u_k,u_k)$, its flux with $\tilde\epsilon^{(j)}(\breve u_k,\breve u_k)$; Eq. (25) expands the correction as $\tfrac12\mathcal E_i(\breve u_k,\breve u_k)$, so the breves are missing in the first.
- Eq. (23), Coppola kinetic energy: the subtracted mass term is printed $-\tfrac12\mathbf u_i^\top U_i\mathcal P(U_j\mathcal D_j\boldsymbol\rho+\mathcal D_j\mathbf u_j)$; Table 3 gives the mass term $R\mathcal D_j\mathbf u_j+U_j\mathcal D_j\boldsymbol\rho$, and the stated result needs the $R$.
- Eq. (24): "$U_k\breve U_k\mathcal D_i\sqrt R$" applies $\mathcal D_i$ to the diagonal matrix where the vector $\sqrt{\boldsymbol\rho}$ is meant.
- Appendix B, after (B.1): the filter re-scaling is referred to "Equation (32)", the Taylor–Green parameter list; the filter is Eq. (29).
- §4.2.1: domain printed "$-\pi L\le x,y,x\le\pi L$" ($z$ meant).
- Fig. 3 caption lists three panels for a four-panel figure (panel (d) is the numerical dissipation); the caption of Fig. B.5 likewise lists three items for four panels.
- §4.1.1: vortex "diameter of approximately 2.1 [m]", identified by $\delta T/T_\infty=0.001$. *(compiler check, not in the sources: with $\alpha=4$, $\phi=1$ and assuming $\gamma=1.4$, which the paper does not print for this case, Eq. (27) gives $\delta T/T_\infty=0.02895\,e^{2(1-r^2)}$, which equals $0.001$ at $r=1.64$, a diameter of about 3.3; the same formula reproduces the printed $\rho_{\min}=0.5479$.)* Either the threshold or the diameter is misprinted, or the diameter is defined differently.
- Remark 3 ends "as already acknowledged by Morinishi et al. [3] (see Table 3)"; this paper's Table 3 is the compressible splitting table, so Table 3 of [3] is presumably meant.
- Typos: "skew-symmetric from" (p. 2), "a product from … expected the yield" (p. 9), "standard divergence from" (footnote 5), "entrophy" (A.3), "Kennedy-Grubber" (p. 8), "complimentary" (§5).
