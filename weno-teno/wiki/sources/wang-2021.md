# Summary

The paper modifies the fifth-order finite-difference
[multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) of Zhu & Shu (2018), here
WENOZS5, a relative of [WENO-AO](/methods/weno-ao.md). In a
[conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
with [Lax–Friedrichs splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) and
[characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md), the split flux
$f^+$ is reconstructed from three nested central stencils of 1, 3 and 5 points, with
polynomials of degree 0, 2 and 4 written in a local Legendre basis. Any positive linear
weights summing to one are admitted. Four things change. The Jiang–Shu
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) $\beta_2,\beta_3$ are
computed on the stencil polynomials themselves, as sums of squares of Legendre
coefficients, not on the weight-dependent "equivalent polynomials" of Zhu & Shu. The
constant's indicator is $\beta_1=\min\big((f_i-f_{i-1})^2,(f_{i+1}-f_i)^2\big)$. The
nonlinear weights are of [WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) type with
$\tau=\big(\tfrac12(\lvert\beta_3-\beta_1\rvert+\lvert\beta_3-\beta_2\rvert)\big)^2$. The
interface value is assembled as in [CWENO](/methods/cweno.md). Taylor expansions give
$\omega_k=d_k+O(\Delta x^4)$, also at first-order critical points
([critical-point accuracy](/properties/critical-point-accuracy.md)).

The authors claim that the original scheme loses accuracy for some linear weights
because its indicators depend on them, and that the new scheme reaches fifth order for
any weights. The method is extended to Navier–Stokes with sixth-order central viscous
fluxes and third-order [SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md). Evidence is
numerical: the [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md),
smooth accuracy tests in 1D to 3D, and shock tests from Shu–Osher to the Taylor–Green
vortex, against [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md), WENO-AO(5,3) and WENOZS5.
Resolution is close to WENOZS5 and sharper than WENO-JS, with lower
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md). The cost is about 0.7
times that of WENOZS5. Near-vacuum problems need the original linear weights. The
introduction also recalls [mapped](/methods/mapped-weno.md),
[Hermite](/methods/hermite-weno.md) and [hybrid](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) WENO.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's linear weights $\gamma_n$ are
$d_k$, its unnormalized weights $\bar\omega_n$ are $\alpha_k$, its $\varepsilon$ is
$\epsilon$, its mesh size $h$ is $\Delta x$, its split point flux $F^{x,+}_{c,l}$ is
$f_l$. As on the [multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) page, the nested
central stencils of 1, 3, 5 points are written $T_1,T_2,T_3$, the stencil polynomials
$P_1,P_2,P_3$ and the equivalent polynomials of Zhu & Shu $\tilde P_m$.

1. The new multi-resolution WENO5 reaches fifth order on smooth problems with any positive linear weights summing to one (tested: $(0.95,0.049,0.001)$, $(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, $(0.001,0.049,0.95)$) (§3.1.1, §5.1, Figs. 5.2, 5.4, 5.6). **Shown**
2. The smoothness indicators of the original multi-resolution WENO depend on the linear weights through its equivalent polynomials (Eqs. (3.23)–(3.28)), so for some weights (e.g. $d_{1,2}=10/11$, $d_{2,2}=1/11$, $d_{1,3}=100/111$, $d_{2,3}=10/111$, $d_{3,3}=1/111$) the smoothness of $P_2,P_3$ "will be misjudged", "resulting in only first-order accuracy for the smooth problems" (§3.1.1, p. 5). **Stated** (not shown for those weights; §5.1 shows a failure for the third weight type)
3. With the third weight type the original WENOZS5 "cannot achieve the fifth-order accuracy" (§5.1, p. 8) and "does not converge for the three-dimensional viscous accuracy test problem" (§5.1, p. 12; omitted from Fig. 5.6(b)). **Shown** (figures only)
4. Taylor expansions: $\beta_{10},\beta_{11},\beta_2,\beta_3$ all equal $(f')^2\Delta x^2+O(\Delta x^3)$; $\lvert\beta_3-\beta_1\rvert=O(\Delta x^3)$ ($f'\ne0$) or $O(\Delta x^4)$ ($f'=0$, $f''\ne0$); $\lvert\beta_3-\beta_2\rvert=O(\Delta x^4)$ or $O(\Delta x^6)$; hence $\tau=O(\Delta x^6)$ or $O(\Delta x^8)$ and $\omega_k=d_k+O(\Delta x^4)$ in smooth regions (Eqs. (3.13)–(3.20)). **Derived** (no theorem; see Printed slips for Eq. (3.18))
5. The recommended linear weights are $d_1=1/111$, $d_2=10/111$, $d_3=100/111$ "for all examples in this paper"; "the larger the linear weights corresponding to the five-point sub-stencil is, the closer it is to the optimal quartic linear polynomial, but it also tends to oscillate" (§3.1.1, Step 3). **Stated** (but §5 defines "New WENO5" with the first weight type; see Printed slips)
6. In smooth problems the errors of New WENO5 and WENOZS5 equal those of the optimal linear Upwind5 scheme on fine grids and are smaller than WENO-JS5 (1D Euler, 320 points: $L_1$ error $2.93\times10^{-11}$ for New WENO5 and Upwind5, $3.26\times10^{-11}$ for WENOZS5, $1.93\times10^{-10}$ for WENO-JS5) (Tables 5.1–5.6). **Shown**
7. 1D Euler CPU times at 320 points: Upwind5 0.9217 s, WENO-JS5 1.5809 s, WENOZS5 3.0171 s, New WENO5 1.9766 s (Table 5.1). **Shown**
8. 3D Euler accuracy test: New WENO5 costs "less than 10%" more than WENO-JS5, WENOZS5 about 45% more; at $100^3$, 15170 s, 14526 s and 22054 s; New WENO5 costs "about 0.7 times" the original (Example 5.3, Table 5.5). **Shown**
9. WENO-AO(5,3) costs more than the three-stencil fifth-order schemes because it uses four sub-stencils (§5.1, p. 7). **Stated**
10. In the approximate dispersion relation WENOZS5, WENO-AO5 and New WENO5 have better dispersion and dissipation than WENO-JS5 (§4, Fig. 4.1). **Shown**
11. Shu–Osher (200 points): New WENO5 is better than WENOZS5 and WENO-JS5, WENO-AO5 best; WENO-JS5 0.2028 s (281 steps), WENO-AO5 0.2340 s (282), New WENO5 0.2184 s (283), WENOZS5 0.2808 s (282) (Example 5.4, Fig. 5.7). **Shown**
12. Woodward–Colella blast waves (400 points, $t=0.038$): New WENO5 is similar to WENO-AO5, both better than WENO-JS5 and WENOZS5; WENOZS5 1.14 s against New WENO5 0.76 s, "about 0.67 times" (Example 5.4, Fig. 5.8). **Shown**
13. For the near-vacuum problems (Sedov, double rarefaction, LeBlanc) the paper's weight ratio "is so large that it cannot directly and robustly solve such extreme problems", so the linear weights of Zhu & Shu are used instead; no positivity-preserving limiter is used (Example 5.5). **Stated** (as a limitation)
14. Sedov (400 points): WENO-JS5 and both multi-resolution schemes are similar and worse than WENO-AO5 near the peak; "the reason may be that in the vicinity of strong shock waves with a large pressure ratio, the algorithm in this paper is rapidly reduced to the first order accuracy" (Example 5.5, Fig. 5.9). **Shown** (cause conjectured)
15. 2D Riemann problem ($400\times400$, $t=0.8$): both multi-resolution schemes capture more roll-up than WENO-JS5 and WENO-AO5 (Example 5.6, Fig. 5.12). **Shown**
16. Double Mach reflection and Mach 3 forward-facing step ($1200\times400$): the multi-resolution schemes resolve the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-ups better than WENO-JS5 (Examples 5.7–5.8, Figs. 5.13–5.14). **Shown**
17. Rayleigh–Taylor ($240\times960$, $t=1.95$): the multi-resolution schemes give more roll-up than WENO-JS5 and WENO-AO5 (Example 5.9, Fig. 5.15). **Shown**
18. Viscous shock tube, $\mathrm{Re}=200$ ($1000\times500$, $\nu=0.3$): triple point and primary-vortex height New WENO5 $(0.579,0.138)$ / $0.172$ against the reference $(0.580,0.137)$ / $0.166$; WENO-JS5 $(0.579,0.139)$ / $0.170$, WENO-AO5 $(0.578,0.140)$ / $0.173$, WENOZS5 $(0.579,0.135)$ / $0.171$ (Example 5.11, Table 5.7). **Shown**
19. Taylor–Green vortex ($128^3$, inviscid to $t=10$, $\mathrm{Re}=1600$ to $t=20$): New WENO5 "performs best among the four considered WENO schemes" against the reference DNS (Example 5.13, Fig. 5.22). **Shown**
20. Cost relative to WENO-JS5 over Examples 5.6–5.13: WENO-AO5 1.29–1.80, WENOZS5 1.40–1.83, New WENO5 1.09–1.60 (Table 5.8); the text says 25%–80%, 40%–83%, 9%–30% and "about 0.7 times" WENOZS5 (§5.4). **Shown** (the text range for New WENO5 disagrees with the table)
21. On unstructured meshes the multi-resolution approach needs only three sub-stencils for an order above three, whereas classical unstructured WENO "needs more than nine sub-stencils" (§6). **Stated**
22. The original multi-resolution WENO costs about 45% more than classical WENO on a uniform Cartesian grid (§1, Remark 2). **Stated** (consistent with Table 5.5)

# Printed slips

- Linear weights: Step 3 recommends $1/111$, $10/111$, $100/111$ "for all examples in this paper", but §5 says "this new ... scheme with first linear weights is abbreviated as the New WENO5 scheme", the first type being $(0.95,0.049,0.001)$, and Example 5.5 switches to "the same as the original method", whose weights §5 gives as $1/111$, $10/111$, $100/111$. Which weights "New WENO5" uses is ambiguous.
- Failure case: §3.1.1 says the original fails when "the linear weight of the five-point sub-stencil is much less than that of the one-point sub-stencil"; §5.1 shows the failure with the third type, $0.95$ on the five-point stencil; the introduction speaks of "the linear weight of the largest reconstruction sub-stencil is much larger".
- Eq. (3.18): the printed $\Delta x^4$ bracket of $\lvert\beta_3-\beta_1\rvert$, $\tfrac14(f'')^2+\tfrac13f'f'''$, is the $\Delta x^4$ term of $\beta_{10}$ itself. *(compiler check, not in the sources: (3.13) and (3.16) give $\beta_3-\beta_{10}=f'f''\Delta x^3+\big(\tfrac56(f'')^2-\tfrac13f'f'''\big)\Delta x^4+O(\Delta x^5)$; the orders $O(\Delta x^3)$, $O(\Delta x^4)$ are unaffected, and (3.19) agrees with (3.15)–(3.16): $\tfrac{781}{720}-\tfrac1{36}=\tfrac{761}{720}$, $-\tfrac1{360}-\tfrac{13}{72}=-\tfrac{11}{60}$, $-\tfrac1{15}-\tfrac1{60}=-\tfrac1{12}$.)*
- §5.4: New WENO5 costs "9%–30%" more than WENO-JS5, but Table 5.8 gives 1.60 for Example 5.10; WENO-AO5 "25%–80%" against a table minimum of 1.29; the ratio to WENOZS5 ranges 0.70–0.87 (Example 5.10: $486.44/556.07=0.87$).
- Example 5.1: initial $\rho=1+0.2\sin(\pi x)$ but exact solution printed $\rho=1+0.2\sin(x-ut)$ (missing $\pi$); likewise Example 5.2; "periodic boundary conditions are used in both directions" for a 1D problem.
- Table 5.5: grid row "80 × 80 × 60".
- "Example 5.4 to 5.4" (§5, p. 6); Example 5.12 is the "extension of the previous Example 4.10" (should be 5.10).
- Example 5.13: "$w_0(x,y,z)-0$"; kinetic energy printed "$E_k=\frac{1}{\rho_0V}\int_V\frac12\rho u_i\cdot u_i\,dV$".
- §3.1.2: the viscous discretization is said not to exceed the WENO stencil, but the derivative (3.29) spans $i\pm3$ and the interpolation (3.30) spans $i-2,\dots,i+3$.
- Typos: "shock senor", "probelm", "WNEO5"; hardware for the CPU times not stated.
