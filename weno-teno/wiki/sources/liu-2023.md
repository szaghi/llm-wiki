# Summary

The paper takes the approximate HLL solver for the
[generalized Riemann problem](/methods/generalized-riemann-problem.md) (HLL-GRP) of Goetz,
Balsara & Dumbser and Balsara, Li & Montecinos, so far applied only to 1D systems, and
implements it in a multidimensional finite-volume scheme for ideal MHD. The route is
rotational invariance: at each edge midpoint the reconstructed states and gradients of the two
neighbouring cells are rotated into the edge-normal frame by a rotation matrix $\mathsf T_{ij}$,
and only the normal gradient enters a 1D GRP with piecewise-linear data. The authors claim as
contributions a way to compute the normal flux integral of a rotated scheme and an explicit
expression of the "rotational gradient". The HLL-GRP flux is one-step and second order in
time: the [HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) flux of the associated Riemann problem plus a
correction $-A^2\partial_xu\,\Delta t/2$ in the relevant region of the wave fan, with the star
gradient from a least-squares system based on the Rankine–Hugoniot conditions.

Around this flux the scheme uses locally divergence-free [WLS-ENO](/methods/wls-eno.md)
reconstruction of the primitive variables (one linear polynomial per cell, from a weighted
least-squares fit on 4 neighbours), a Barth–Jespersen-type slope limiter on density and pressure
that the authors call a "PP limiter", the Godunov–Powell source discretized as by Wu and Shu
and evaluated at the time midpoint with a Cauchy–Kowalewski predictor
([divergence-free magnetic field](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md)), the Wu–Shu
wave-speed estimates and time-step condition, and truncated SVD for every least-squares problem.

HLL-GRP (one stage) is compared with forward-Euler and two-stage SSP-RK2 HLL schemes with the
same reconstruction, on Cartesian grids only: [Brio–Wu](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md), the
smooth [circularly polarized Alfvén wave](/benchmarks/circularly-polarized-alfven-wave.md), the
[Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md) to $t=100$, and the 2D
[blast wave and rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) and a 3D blast wave. HLL-GRP reaches
about second order with errors close to RK2, produces no negative density or pressure in any
run, and has divergence norms comparable to or below the other schemes; it costs about 2.5
times as much as RK2 at $256^2$, which the authors attribute to the SVD solves. No positivity
proof is claimed.

# Key claims

Registry notation: conservative variables $u=(\rho,\rho v,B,E)$ with cell averages $\bar u_i$,
$x$-flux $f^{(1)}$ with Jacobian $A$ ($A^{(2)}$ in $y$), interface states $u^-$ (cell $i$) and
$u^+$ (cell $j$) in the rotated frame, one-sided speeds $a^\pm$ (including 0) and HLL speeds
$s^\pm$ (without 0), HLL star state $u^{\mathrm{HLL}}$, WLS row weights $W_j$; page-local
$\mathsf T_{ij}$ (rotation), $S_{\mathrm P}(u)$ (Powell vector), $\partial_nu$ (normal gradient),
$\mathsf l_i$ (limiter factor).

1. HLL-type GRP solvers (HLL-, HLLC-, HLLD-, HLLI-GRP) "have only been implemented for 1D hyperbolic systems and not for multidimensional numerical simulations"; this paper is "the first attempt" to use HLL-GRP in multidimensional MHD finite volumes (§1, p. 2). **Stated**
2. The paper formulates "for the first time" the rotational gradient explicitly and gives a way to compute the normal flux integral of a rotated scheme (§1, p. 2; Eqs. (45), (47), (55)–(56)). **Derived**
3. By rotational invariance $f(u)\cdot n_{ij}=\mathsf T_{ij}^{-1}f^{(1)}(\mathsf T_{ij}u)$ (Eq. (41)); the data of the local 1D GRP depend only on the states and the normal gradients, $\partial_xu^\mp=\mathsf T_{ij}\big(\partial_xu\,n_{ij,x}+\partial_yu\,n_{ij,y}\big)$, "and the tangential gradient values have no effect on the normal flux" (§3.1.3, p. 7, Eqs. (52)–(56)). **Stated**
4. The edge GRP omits the Godunov–Powell source, "following the usual rotation schemes", because adding it "may affect the stability and accuracy of the scheme" (§3.1.3, p. 8). **Stated**
5. The HLL-GRP flux is $f^{(1)}(u^-)-A(u^-)^2\partial_xu^-\,\Delta t/2$ if $s^->0$, $\hat f^{\mathrm{HLL}}-A(u^{\mathrm{HLL}})^2\partial_xu^{\mathrm{HLL}}\,\Delta t/2$ if $s^-\le0\le s^+$, and $f^{(1)}(u^+)-A(u^+)^2\partial_xu^+\,\Delta t/2$ if $s^+<0$ (§3.2.1, Eq. (66)), from the flux expansion (65) and the evolved states (61)–(63). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: on $x/t=0$ the evolved state $u+t(\tfrac xt-A)\partial_xu$ has $\partial_t\tilde u=-A\partial_xu$, so the correction $A\,\partial_t\tilde u\,\Delta t/2$ of (65) is $-A^2\partial_xu\,\Delta t/2$, as printed.)*
6. The star gradient $\partial_xu^{\mathrm{HLL}}$ solves, "according to the Rankine–Hugoniot jump conditions", the overdetermined system $\big((A(u^{\mathrm{HLL}})-s^\mp I)^2\big)\partial_xu^{\mathrm{HLL}}=(A(u^\mp)-s^\mp I)^2\partial_xu^\mp$ (two blocks, $16\times8$ for MHD; Eq. (64)), solved by truncated SVD (§3.2.1). **Stated** (from Goetz et al. and Balsara et al.)
7. The least-squares matrices of (20), (24) and (64) "may be ill-conditioned as the mesh is refined"; truncated SVD with threshold $\sigma^1\times10^{-10}$ gives a minimum-norm solution, and the threshold "is typical and not universal" (§3.3, Eqs. (71)–(77)). **Stated**
8. The WLS-ENO row weight $W_j=1/[(\bar w_j-\bar w_i)^2+\varepsilon h^2]$ ($\varepsilon=10^{-2}$, $h=\sqrt{|A_i|}$, 4 neighbours) is $\mathcal O(1/h^2)$ where the function is smooth and $\mathcal O(1)$ where it is discontinuous (§3.1.1, Eq. (20)). **Stated**
9. Imposing the local divergence-free condition on $\partial_yB_2$ instead of $\partial_xB_1$, or on both separately and averaging, "didn't make much difference to the simulation results" (§3.1.1, p. 5). **Stated** (no data)
10. The "PP limiter" enforces $\bar\rho_{\min}\le\tilde\rho_i(x_{ij,c})\le\bar\rho_{\max}$, and the same for $p$, at all edge midpoints, "which also ensures the positivity of the reconstructed density and pressure" (§3.1.2, Eq. (29)). **Derived** (at the edge midpoints, given positive averages)
11. The CFL-type condition (16)–(18) is that of Wu and Shu, for whom (with the HLL flux, their limiter and their Godunov–Powell discretization) it yields a provably positivity-preserving scheme; this paper "only appl[ies] this CFL-type condition … without making a rigorous proof" (§2.2, p. 4). **Stated**
12. The time step from (16) is "much smaller for the same CFL value" than that of common methods (§4, p. 12). **Stated** (no numbers)
13. The default CFL number is 0.8; the smooth Alfvén test uses 0.2 (§4; Table 1 caption). **Stated**
14. Brio–Wu at $t=0.1$ on 800 cells: HLL-GRP and RK2 "basically coincide" (§4.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
15. Smooth Alfvén wave, $t=2$, CFL 0.2: the HLL-GRP $L^1$ errors in $v_1$ are 1.7210E-02, 3.9797E-03, 1.0327E-03, 2.0078E-04, 5.5446E-05 on $16^2$ to $256^2$, orders 2.1125, 1.9463, 2.3627, 1.8565; RK2's orders are 2.0953, 1.9895, 2.4408, 2.3915 (error 3.6834E-05 at $256^2$); forward Euler's decay to 1.0034. HLL-GRP "achieves the expected convergence order" with errors close to RK2 (§4.2, Table 1). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: the HLL-GRP orders follow from the printed errors, e.g. $\log_2(1.7210/0.39797)=2.1125$.)*
16. CPU wall time at $256^2$ on 64 cores of Tianhe-1A (two six-core Xeon X5670 per node): 714.7742 s (forward Euler), 4202.0244 s (HLL-GRP), 1681.4516 s (RK2); HLL-GRP "is found to be very time-consuming compared to RK2 scheme, because the update of the flux requires solving LS problem" by truncated SVD (§4.2, Table 1). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: the ratio HLL-GRP/RK2 is 2.50.)*
17. Orszag–Tang: citing Jiang & Wu that "negative pressure may be generated beyond t = 3.9", the authors run to $t=100$ on $200\times200$ "(can continue to run)" without negative pressure (§4.3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
18. The Orszag–Tang divergence norm of HLL-GRP is "almost relatively low compared to other schemes" (§4.3, Fig. 7). **Shown**
19. Blast wave ($\beta_{\mathrm p}\approx2.51\times10^{-4}$): no negative pressure; HLL-GRP and RK2 profiles "almost identical"; the HLL-GRP divergence norm lies between those of forward Euler and RK2, and divergence concentrates at the shock front where $\beta_{\mathrm p}$ is very low (§4.4, Figs. 8–11). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\beta_{\mathrm p}=2\cdot0.1/(100^2/4\pi)=2.513\times10^{-4}$.)*
20. Rotor: discontinuities captured crisply, without spurious oscillations or distortion of the rotating velocity field; HLL-GRP divergence slightly below the other schemes (§4.5, Figs. 12–15). **Shown**
21. 3D blast wave on $200^3$, $t=0.2$: HLL-GRP profiles almost identical to RK2; the HLL-GRP and RK2 divergence norms coincide, below forward Euler, "controlled to the $10^{-3}$ magnitude"; the results agree with CESE computations (§4.6, Figs. 16–20). **Shown**
22. "No negative pressure or negative density was generated in all simulations" (§5). **Shown**
23. The scheme "cannot model multidimensional wave propagation as faithfully and accurately as genuine multidimensional schemes", and the SVD makes it time-consuming (§5). **Stated**
24. "In theory, the scheme can be applied to both structured and unstructured meshes"; only Cartesian grids are tested (§5). **Stated**
25. GRP solvers can reduce the number of SSP-RK stages and guide mesh motion in ALE codes (§1). **Stated**
