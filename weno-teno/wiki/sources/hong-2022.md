# Summary

The paper proposes TENO-M, a variant of the [targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md)
family that costs less. Six- and eight-point TENO, with the hierarchical voting and the
optimised background schemes of Fu et al. (2018, the source of
[adaptive TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md)), need the
[smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) of four- and five-point
stencils and a full-stencil $\tau_K$, which is a 36-term quadratic form for $K=8$. TENO-M
first runs TENO5 on the three three-point stencils. TENO5 uses the
[WENO-Z](/methods/weno-z.md) indicator $\tau_5=\lvert\beta_1-\beta_2\rvert$ (source numbering, see the Notation map) and works as an
explicit detector of where the discontinuity lies. The initial target stencil is then
enlarged one point at a time, left or right, using only three-point extension stencils. An
extension is accepted when the inner neighbour on the same side was accepted and a
relative-difference test between two three-point indicators, both taken at the shared node,
falls below a threshold $C_s$. The final flux is the maximum-order linear scheme on the
accepted union. When every stencil is accepted, this is the dissipation-optimised
background scheme: fifth order for six points, sixth order for eight.

An [approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md)
analysis shows that $C_s$ moves the spectral properties between TENO5 ($C_s=0$) and the
linear background ($C_s=1$). The paper adopts $C_s=0.8$. The tests are Gaussian-pulse
advection, [Sod and Lax tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md),
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md),
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md), a
[2D Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) and decaying
compressible isotropic turbulence. They put TENO-M about on par with TENO, and far ahead of
[WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md), WENO7 and WENO9; [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md)
overshoots in places. In the measured timings, TENO6 costs about 25 % more than TENO-M6 and
TENO8 about 44 % more than TENO-M8; TENO-M8 costs about as much as WENO-CU6 and WENO9.
Nothing is proved: the evidence is the construction, the spectral analysis and the numerical
tests.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with the source's stencil numbering ($S_0$ central, $S_1$ downwind, $S_2$
upwind three-point stencils; see the Notation map). $\eta_{\mathrm b}$ is the blending
parameter of the six-point background (renamed from the source's $\eta$).

1. TENO-M starts from TENO5 run on $S_0,S_1,S_2$. It then enlarges the initial target stencil point by point toward each side where no discontinuity is detected, so every candidate stencil has three points. The fifth-order TENO-M is TENO5 (§3, §3.1–3.2, Eq. 21). **Stated** (construction)
2. With the hierarchical voting strategy there are 6, 9 and 17 admissible combinations of the $\delta_k$ for the five-, six- and eight-point schemes, against $2^{K-2}-1$ without it. TENO-M6 and TENO-M8 admit the same 9 and 17 combinations (Tables 2–3) (§2.5, §4.1–4.2). **Derived** (enumeration)
3. Extension test: for $k>2$, $\delta_k=1$ iff $\lvert\beta^*_{(k-2,k)}-\beta^*_{(k,k-2)}\rvert/\big(\max(\beta^*_{(k-2,k)},\beta^*_{(k,k-2)})+\epsilon\big)<C_s$. Both three-point indicators are evaluated with the derivatives taken at the node that the two stencils share. The test applies only when the adjacent inner stencil $S_{k-2}$ has been accepted (Eqs. 21–26). **Stated** (definition)
4. In the six-point configurations where TENO5 has already found a discontinuity on $S_1$ (Fig. 4c, f, h), $S_3^*$ is not computed and TENO-M6 equals TENO5. TENO6 always evaluates every candidate stencil (§4.1). **Stated**
5. For TENO-M8, only 3 of the 16 discontinuity configurations (Fig. 5a, g, h) need all stencils, and in Fig. 5n the scheme reduces to TENO5. TENO8 always evaluates the four-point stencils $S_3,S_4$ and the five-point stencil $S_5$ (§4.2). **Stated**
6. The six-point background is the blend $\eta_{\mathrm b}\hat f^{(5,\mathrm{up})}+(1-\eta_{\mathrm b})\hat f^{(5,\mathrm{down})}$ with $\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.57$, of fifth order. The eight-point background (Eq. 29) is of sixth order. Both are taken from Fu et al. 2018 (Eqs. 27, 29; §5.1). **Stated**
7. Spectral analysis: $C_s=0$ recovers the spectral properties of TENO5. Raising $C_s$ moves the dissipation of TENO-M toward that of the linear background in the low-to-intermediate wavenumber range, and $C_s=1$ recovers it there. Larger $C_s$ lowers the high-wavenumber dissipation, which works against stable shock capturing (§4.3, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
8. In the intermediate-to-high wavenumber range, TENO-M has better dispersion than TENO, for both the six- and the eight-point schemes (§4.3, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
9. Parameters: $C_s=0.8$; $C_T=10^{-5}$ for TENO-M and $C_T=10^{-7}$ for TENO6 and TENO8 (as in Fu et al. 2018); $C=1$, $q=6$, $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ ($10^{-4}$ for Rayleigh–Taylor) (§2.2, §4.3, §5, §5.5). **Stated**
10. Gaussian-pulse advection: the $L_\infty$ errors of TENO and TENO-M are almost those of the linear backgrounds, with orders 5 (six-point) and 6 (eight-point) because of the dispersion–dissipation optimisation (§5.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
11. Sod and Lax tubes ($N=100$): every scheme is stable; WENO-CU6 oscillates slightly near the contact. TENO-M6 matches TENO6 and resolves the contact and the expansion better than WENO-JS, and TENO-M8 resolves the discontinuities better than WENO9 (§5.2, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
12. Interacting blast waves ($N=400$): TENO6 and TENO-M6 are alike and much better than WENO-JS near $x=0.75$ and $x=0.78$. WENO-CU6 overshoots near the left peak at $x=0.65$. TENO8 and TENO-M8 are less dissipative than WENO7 and slightly better than WENO9 (§5.3, Fig. 11). **Shown**
13. Shu–Osher ($N=200$): WENO-CU6 overshoots near the valleys. TENO6, TENO-M6 and WENO-CU6 resolve the entropy waves alike and much better than WENO-JS. TENO8 and TENO-M8 improve markedly on WENO7 and WENO9 (§5.4, Fig. 12). **Shown**
14. Rayleigh–Taylor ($64\times256$): TENO and TENO-M resolve far more small-scale structure than WENO-JS, WENO7 and WENO9. TENO is "slightly better" than TENO-M, and WENO-CU6 is comparable to TENO-M6 (§5.5, Fig. 13). **Shown**
15. Double Mach reflection ($960\times240$, Van Leer flux-vector splitting): TENO and TENO-M resolve the slip-line structures equally well and far better than the WENO schemes. WENO9 oscillates near the Mach stem (§5.6, Fig. 14). **Shown**
16. 2D Riemann problem ($512\times512$, Van Leer): TENO-M resolves small-scale structures slightly better than TENO, and both are much better than the WENO schemes (§5.7, Fig. 15). **Shown**
17. Decaying compressible isotropic turbulence ($128^3$, initial turbulent Mach number 0.5, $\mathrm{Re}_\lambda=72$): TENO6 and TENO-M6 predict the turbulent Mach number and the rms density much better than WENO-JS and are slightly less dissipative than WENO-CU6. TENO8, TENO-M8 and WENO9 are alike and less dissipative than WENO7 (§5.8, Fig. 16). **Shown**
18. CPU time per 1000 steps (Table 4; Fortran, double precision, Intel Core i9-10980XE at 3.00 GHz), in s, for Rayleigh–Taylor / DMR / 2D Riemann: TENO6 125.75 / 1747.42 / 2034.43; TENO-M6 100.84 / 1406.65 / 1632.51; WENO-JS 88.64 / 1252.31 / 1432.34; WENO-CU6 105.80 / 1489.53 / 1746.42; TENO8 163.41 / 2277.26 / 2626.74; TENO-M8 113.08 / 1588.62 / 1838.13; WENO7 100.41 / 1398.94 / 1623.05; WENO9 113.45 / 1577.73 / 1838.68 (§6, Table 4). **Shown**
19. TENO6 takes "about 25%" more time than TENO-M6 and WENO-CU6 "about 6%" more. TENO8 takes "44%" more than TENO-M8 on average, and TENO-M8 is "comparable" to WENO-CU6 and WENO9 (§6, §7). **Shown** (the ratios computed from Table 4 agree: 1.24–1.25, 1.05–1.07, 1.43–1.45, and TENO-M8/WENO9 0.997–1.007; *compiler check, not in the sources*)
20. The timings include flux splitting and [characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md), which cost the same for every reconstruction. The saving of TENO-M over TENO in the reconstruction alone is therefore "much higher" than the totals show (§6). **Stated** (not measured)
21. Fu et al. 2018 (their Table 4) report that eight-point TENO took nearly 70 % more time than WENO-CU6 on DMR at $1024\times256$ (§1). **Stated** (cited)
22. The cost of TENO comes mainly from the large-stencil smoothness indicators and the full-stencil $\tau_K$ (Eqs. 19–20), and from finding the target stencil on the full stencil (§1, §3.2). **Stated**

*Compiler checks, not in the sources.* The six-point background (Eq. 27) with
$\eta_{\mathrm b}=0.57$ reproduces polynomials up to degree 4 only (fifth order). With
$\eta_{\mathrm b}=1/2$ it would be the central sixth-order scheme
$\frac1{60}(f_{i-2}-8f_{i-1}+37f_i+37f_{i+1}-8f_{i+2}+f_{i+3})$. The eight-point coefficients
(Eq. 29) sum to 1 and are exact up to degree 5 (sixth order). The seven-point target
$\hat f_1$ of Eq. (30), used when only the leftmost point is cut off, is of seventh order:
near a discontinuity, TENO-M8 can switch to a target of higher formal order than its
smooth-region background. The paper does not comment on this. Table 4 gives
TENO8/WENO-CU6 $=1.50$–$1.54$, against the "nearly 70 %" quoted from Fu et al. 2018 (claim 21),
on a different grid, code and machine.

# Printed slips

- Eq. (2) prints $\partial u_i/\partial t=\partial f/\partial x\vert_{x=x_i}$; the minus sign that Eqs. (1) and (3) require is missing.
- Eq. (19), first square: "$(-25f_i+48f_{i+1}-36f_{i+2}+16f_{i+3}-3f)^2$" — the last $f$ has no subscript. With $f_{i+4}$, Eq. (19) equals the Jiang–Shu indicator of the five-point stencil $\{x_i,\dots,x_{i+4}\}$ exactly *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (20) is labelled $\tau_8$, but Eq. (10) defines $\tau_K$ with absolute-value bars, which Eq. (20) does not print.
- Eq. (21) is circular as printed: the condition for $\delta_k=1$ is a product that contains $\delta_k$ itself. The prose makes the meaning clear: $S^*_k$ is accepted iff every inner stencil on the same side is accepted and $S^*_k$ passes Eq. (26).
- §5.5 prints the domain as "$[0,0.25]\times[0,1][0,1]$".
- Abstract, first sentence: "Fu et al. (J Comput Phys 374:724–751, 2018), a class of adaptive targeted ENO (TENO) schemes have been proposed" — a leading "In" is missing.
- Eq. (29) prints $f^{opt,8}_{i+1/2}$ without the hat used elsewhere.
- Ref. [24] (Roe) prints J. Comput. Phys. 43, 357–372 (1981) with DOI 10.1006/jcph.1997.5705, which on Crossref is the DOI of the 1997 reprint (J. Comput. Phys. 135:250–258); the bibliography keeps the 1981 entry without that DOI. Ref. [14] is truncated (title only), and ref. [23] has no volume or pages.
