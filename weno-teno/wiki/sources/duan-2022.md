# Summary

The paper extends the authors' second-order entropy-stable moving-mesh
schemes to high order for 2D and 3D
[special-relativistic hydrodynamics and MHD](/definitions/relativistic-hydrodynamics.md).
RMHD is taken in the symmetrizable form of Wu & Shu: a Godunov–Powell-type
source proportional to $\nabla\cdot B$ makes $\eta=-\rho Ws/(\gamma-1)$,
$F^{(k)}_\eta=\eta v_k$ an entropy pair, so the
[divergence-free constraint](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md)
is handled without cleaning or constrained transport. The equations are
written in time-dependent curvilinear coordinates with the volume and surface
conservation laws, and the
[adaptive moving mesh](/methods/adaptive-moving-mesh.md) is redistributed
by Winslow variable diffusion solved with Jacobi iterations.

The contributions are: a sufficient jump condition for a two-point
[entropy-conservative flux](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md) in moving
curvilinear coordinates, realized as a metric-averaged Cartesian EC flux plus
a new "EC state" built from [logarithmic means](/operators/logarithmic-mean.md);
a $2p$th-order EC flux as a LeFloch–Mercier–Rohde linear combination of
two-point fluxes ([flux differencing](/methods/flux-differencing.md) in
[conservative finite-difference](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md)
form), applied identically to the source term, the volume law and the
metrics, so the discrete surface laws hold exactly and the
[SSP Runge–Kutta](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) scheme is
[free-stream preserving](/properties/free-stream-preservation.md); and a
[TeCNO-type](/methods/tecno-scheme.md) entropy-stable flux of order $2p-1$
whose dissipation acts on [WENO-JS](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md)
jumps of rotated, scaled entropy variables (a
[characteristic-like](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) projection)
with the Biswas–Dubey sign switch, proved
[entropy stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md).

Numerics on [isentropic vortices](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) confirm
orders six (EC) and five (ES). On three RHD
[2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md), the
relativistic [MHD blast](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md), RMHD shock–cloud
problems, a 3D spherical Riemann problem and a 3D
[shock–bubble](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md), the fifth-order
moving-mesh scheme matches uniform meshes 2–4 times finer per direction at
7%–31% of the CPU time, and beats the second-order moving-mesh scheme at
comparable cost.

# Key claims

Registry notation (see the notation map): the source's adiabatic index
$\Gamma$ is $\gamma$, its entropy variables $V$ are $w$, its entropy flux
$q_k$ is $F^{(k)}_\eta$, its $\psi_k$ is kept (it carries an extra $\Phi B_k$
term), and its ES order $w=2p-1$ is written $2p-1$ (the concept pages write
$n_{\mathrm o}$ for $p$ and $c_n$ for $\alpha_{p,n}$).

1. For $B\not\equiv0$ the pair $(\eta,F_\eta)$ does not satisfy the compatibility condition and RMHD cannot in general be symmetrized; adding the source $-\Phi'(w)^\top\sum_k\partial_{x_k}B_k$, with $\Phi$ homogeneous of degree one, makes it an entropy pair of the modified system (§2, Eqs. 2.3–2.5, citing Wu & Shu). **Stated**
2. Under the geometric conservation laws (2.9) the curvilinear form has an entropy identity for smooth solutions (2.10) and an inequality otherwise (2.11); discrete geometric conservation laws are needed for discrete entropy conservation (Remark 2.1). **Derived** (derivation deferred to the authors' earlier paper)
3. A two-point flux satisfying the curvilinear jump condition (3.1) is entropy conservative; (3.1) reduces to the RHD condition for $B\equiv0$ and to the Cartesian RMHD condition of Wu & Shu on a fixed mesh (Def. 3.1, Remark 3.1). **Stated** (used in the proof of Thm. 3.2)
4. The flux $\tilde{\mathcal F}_k=\mathring U+\sum_j\mathring F_j$ (3.2)–(3.3), metric averages of an "EC state" $\tilde U$ and Cartesian EC fluxes $\tilde F_j$, satisfies (3.1) when $[\![w]\!]^\top\tilde U=[\![\phi]\!]$ and $[\![w]\!]^\top\tilde F_j=[\![\psi_j]\!]-\{\!\{B_j\}\!\}[\![\Phi]\!]$; an explicit $\tilde U$ for RMHD is given (§3.1). **Derived**
5. With coefficients satisfying $\sum_nn\alpha_{p,n}=1$ and $\sum_nn^{2s-1}\alpha_{p,n}=0$, $s=2,\dots,p$ (3.11), the linear combinations (3.6)–(3.8) are $2p$th-order approximations of the flux divergence, the source term and the volume-law derivative (Prop. 3.1, Eq. 3.12). **Proved** (Lemma 3.1 and Taylor expansion)
6. Lemma 3.1: for a consistent symmetric two-point function $\tilde F$, $2\partial_{\zeta_r}\tilde F\big(u(\zeta_l),u(\zeta_r)\big)\big|_{\zeta_r=\zeta_l}=\partial_\zeta f(u(\zeta))\big|_{\zeta_l}$, and likewise for products of such functions (§3.2; the first identity attributed to Chan). **Proved**
7. Metrics computed in conservative cross-product form (3.17) with the same $2p$th-order central operator as the flux satisfy the discrete surface conservation laws exactly, because the central operators of different directions commute (§3.3, Thomas–Lombard). **Proved**
8. The temporal metrics $J\partial_t\xi_k=-\sum_j\dot x_jJ\partial_{x_j}\xi_k$ (3.18), with (3.5) and (3.8), give the semi-discrete volume conservation law (§3.3). **Derived**
9. The fully discrete scheme with explicit SSP Runge–Kutta preserves free-stream states (Prop. 3.2); the proof is for forward Euler and extends by convex combination. **Proved**
10. Theorem 3.2: the semi-discrete schemes (3.4)–(3.5) with fluxes (3.6)–(3.8) are $2p$th-order accurate in space and entropy conservative, $\frac{d}{dt}J_i\eta(u_i)+\sum_k\frac1{\Delta\xi_k}\big[(\tilde F_\eta^{(k)})_{i,k,+1/2}-(\tilde F_\eta^{(k)})_{i,k,-1/2}\big]=0$, with the consistent numerical entropy flux (3.20)–(3.21). **Proved**
11. The $2p$th-order EC flux on moving curvilinear meshes "can be regarded as a refinement" and extension of the arbitrarily high-order Cartesian EC fluxes of LeFloch, Mercier & Rohde (§1, §3.2). **Stated**
12. The ES flux (4.1) of order $2p-1\ge3$ subtracts $\tfrac12D\,Y\langle\!\langle\tilde w\rangle\!\rangle^{\mathrm{WENO}}$ from the EC flux, with $D=\hat\lambda T^{-1}R(Tu)$ and $\langle\!\langle\tilde w\rangle\!\rangle$ the jump of the left and right Jiang–Shu WENO reconstructions of the rotated, scaled entropy variables $\tilde w=R^\top T\,w$ (§4). **Stated** (construction)
13. Theorem 4.1: with the sign switch $Y_{ll}=1$ iff $\operatorname{sign}\langle\!\langle\tilde w_l\rangle\!\rangle=\operatorname{sign}[\![\tilde w_l]\!]$ and $0$ otherwise, the scheme (4.2)–(4.3) satisfies the semi-discrete entropy inequality with the numerical entropy flux (4.4). **Proved**
14. The dissipation vanishes for a constant state, so the ES schemes preserve free streams (Remark 4.1). **Stated**
15. 2D RMHD isentropic vortex ($t=4$, $40$–$160$ points per direction for EC, up to $200$ for ES): MM-O6 reaches sixth order on the prescribed moving mesh (6.2) and MM-O5 fifth order on the adaptive mesh with monitor (6.3) (Ex. 6.1, Fig. 6.3); the swapped pairings also reach their orders, results omitted. **Shown** (swapped pairings **Stated**)
16. The discrete total entropy $\sum J\eta/N^2$ stays constant for EC and decays monotonically for ES, in 2D at $t\le4$ on $160^2$ (Fig. 6.4) and in 3D at $t\le0.1$ (Fig. 6.14); the paper gives the curves only, no printed values. **Shown**
17. RHD Riemann problem I (four vortex sheets): MM-O5 on $200^2$ (1m02s) is very close to UM-O5 on $500^2$ (5m49s) at 17.8% of the CPU time; MM-O5 on $150^2$ (29s) beats MM-O2 on $200^2$ (30s) (Ex. 6.2, Fig. 6.5). **Shown**
18. RHD Riemann problem II (four rarefactions): MM-O5 $200^2$ (1m25s) is close to UM-O5 $500^2$ (4m38s) at about 30.6% CPU; MM-O5 $150^2$ (35s) beats MM-O2 $200^2$ (38s) (Ex. 6.3, Fig. 6.6). **Shown**
19. RHD Riemann problem III (two contacts, two shocks): MM-O5 $200^2$ (1m16s) agrees with UM-O5 $600^2$ (9m16s) at 13.7% CPU; MM-O5 $150^2$ (32s) beats MM-O2 $200^2$ (39s) (Ex. 6.4, Fig. 6.7). **Shown**
20. 2D RMHD blast ($\gamma=4/3$): MM-O5 $150^2$ (2m03s) is much better than UM-O5 $150^2$ (30s) and comparable to UM-O5 $600^2$ (28m14s), 7.26% CPU (Ex. 6.5, Table 6.1, 4 cores). **Shown**
21. 2D RMHD shock–cloud: MM-O5 $210\times150$ (4m16s) is comparable to UM-O5 $560\times400$ (40m28s), 10.5% CPU (Ex. 6.6, Table 6.1). **Shown**
22. 3D RMHD isentropic vortex ($t=0.1$, $N\times N\times5N$ points): MM-O5 (adaptive) reaches fifth order and MM-O6 (prescribed mesh 6.4) sixth order (Ex. 6.7, Fig. 6.13). **Shown**
23. 3D RHD spherical Riemann problem: MM-O5 $100^3$ (5m40s) is comparable to UM-O5 $200^3$ (41m08s), 13.8% CPU, and beats MM-O2 $100^3$ (2m51s) near the head and tail of the rarefaction (Ex. 6.8, Table 6.2, 32 cores). **Shown**
24. 3D RHD shock–bubble: MM-O5 $325\times90\times90$ (2h14m44s) is comparable to UM-O5 $650\times180\times180$ (12h34m43s), 17.8% CPU, and gives sharper bubble interfaces than MM-O2 (1h10m29s) "since the high-order accurate scheme has lower dissipation" (Ex. 6.9, Table 6.2). **Shown**
25. 3D RMHD shock–cloud: MM-O5 $210\times150\times150$ (3h9m57s) is better than UM-O5 on the same grid (2h8m44s) and comparable to UM-O5 $420\times300\times300$ (34h46m49s) at a printed 9.06% CPU (see Printed slips) (Ex. 6.10, Table 6.2). **Shown**
26. The high-order ES adaptive moving-mesh schemes outperform both their uniform-mesh counterparts and the second-order ES moving-mesh schemes (Abstract, §7). **Shown** (by the comparisons above)

# Printed slips

- §5, mesh-movement limiter (p. 24): "$\Delta_\tau\le-\frac{1}{2(\delta_\tau x_k)_i}[(x_k)^n_i-(x_1)^n_{i,k,-1}]$ … $+\frac{1}{2(\delta_\tau x_k)_i}[(x_k)^n_{i,k,+1}-(x_1)^n_i]$" mixes $(x_1)$ and $(x_k)$; $(x_k)$ is evidently meant in both places.
- Ex. 6.10: the text says "The iso-surfaces of ρ = 1.52", the caption of Fig. 6.18(a) "Iso-surface of ln ρ = 1.52"; the same paragraph says "near the bubble" for the cloud.
- Ex. 6.10: "only takes 9.06% CPU time", but Table 6.2 gives $11397\,\mathrm s/125209\,\mathrm s=9.10\%$. *(compiler check, not in the sources: 3h9m57s = 11397 s, 34h46m49s = 125209 s; the other printed percentages of Exs. 6.2–6.9 agree with the tables.)*
- Exs. 6.1 and 6.7, prescribed meshes (6.2) and (6.4): $\mathring x_k=2i_kR/(N-1)$, $i_k=0,\dots,N-1$, covers $[0,2R]$, not the stated domain $[-R,R]$ (likewise $\mathring x_3=10i_3R/(5N-1)$ covers $[0,10R]$, not $[-5R,5R]$); a $-R$ offset is missing.
- Ex. 6.7: the set $\Omega_0$ in "$(\tilde x_1,\tilde x_2)\in\Omega_0$" is never defined.
- Exs. 6.2–6.4 and 6.8 state neither the physical domain nor the boundary conditions; discontinuity positions and cut lines imply $[0,1]^2$ and $[0,1]^3$.
- Typos: "aare obtained" (Fig. 6.4 caption), "nay not be unique" (§1), "arbitraty order" (ref. [38]), "Riemman" (ref. [46]).
