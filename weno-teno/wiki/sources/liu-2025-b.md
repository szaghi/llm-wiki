# Summary

Liu et al. (2025b) extend the authors' entropy-stable oscillation-free DG
scheme (ES-OFDG, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 2024) to the ideal MHD equations. The
conservative system is not symmetrizable, so the authors use the form with
the [Godunov–Powell source term](/definitions/godunov-powell-source-term.md)
$S(u)\,\nabla\cdot B$, $S=(0,B,v,v\cdot B)^\top$. With the entropy
$\eta=-\rho s/(\gamma-1)$ this system is symmetrized by the entropy variables,
and the source is the gradient of a degree-one homogeneous function of them.

The spatial base is the nodal [entropy-stable](/properties/entropy-stability.md)
[DG](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) scheme of Liu, Shu and Zhang on
Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto points: a [summation-by-parts](/operators/summation-by-parts-operator.md)
difference matrix, [flux differencing](/methods/flux-differencing.md) with the
Chandrashekar–Klingenberg [entropy-conservative flux](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md)
(built on the [logarithmic mean](/operators/logarithmic-mean.md)), the
non-conservative term discretized as in Cheng and Shu's DG for
Hamilton–Jacobi equations, and an entropy-stable interface flux with a
[Lax–Friedrichs-type](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) dissipation in entropy
variables. To this the authors add a damping term
$-\sigma_{\mathrm d}(u_h-\bar u_h)$ whose new coefficient uses interface jumps
of the solution and of its first derivative, the local maximum wave speed,
and the squared local oscillation as normalizer. The last two are taken from
[OEDG](/methods/oedg.md); the normalizer is what the authors call affine
invariance ([scale invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md)). Theorem 3.2
proves semi-discrete entropy stability and a local truncation error of at
least $O(\Delta x^N)$. Each forward-Euler stage of [SSP-RK3](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md)
is [split](/methods/operator-splitting.md) into an explicit step and an
implicit damping step in closed form, so the damping does not restrict the
time step.

The tests use $N=2$ with CFL $0.6/(2N+1)$: a [density wave](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md),
a [circularly polarized Alfvén wave](/benchmarks/circularly-polarized-alfven-wave.md),
the [Brio–Wu](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md), a Ryu–Jones and a
high-pressure-ratio tube, an MHD [2D Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
[Orszag–Tang](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md), the rotor and the
Balsara–Spicer [blast](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md), an MHD
[Kelvin–Helmholtz instability](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md),
the [shock–cloud interaction](/benchmarks/mhd-shock-cloud-interaction.md),
and Mach 800 and 10000 [jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md),
the jets with the [Zhang–Shu limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md).
The scheme is not conservative, not globally
[divergence-free](/properties/divergence-free-magnetic-field.md) and not
provably [positivity-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md), which
the authors state.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $u=(\rho,\rho v,B,E)$ (source $\mathbf W$), $\eta$ and
$F_\eta$ the entropy pair (source $U$, $F$), $w=\eta'(u)$ the entropy
variables (source $\mathbf V$), $\psi$ the entropy flux potential (source
$\psi_m$), $f^{\mathrm{ec}}$ (source $\mathbf f_{1S}$), $h$ the interface flux
(source $\hat{\mathbf f}_1$), $N$ the degree (source $k$), $D$, $M$, $Q=MD$,
$B$-matrix, $\mathsf w_i$ the LGL weights. Page-local: $N_q+1$ the number of
LGL points (source $k_q+1$); $\mathsf b=\rho/(2p)$ (source $\beta$);
$\phi_{\mathrm G}(w)=2\mathsf b\,(v\cdot B)$ the potential of the source;
$\sigma_{\mathrm d}$ the damping coefficient (source $\sigma$); $\mathcal M_j$
the local-oscillation normalizer; $\mathcal H$ the discrete entropy Jacobian.

1. The conservative ideal MHD system "can hardly be symmetrized"; with the source $S(u)\nabla\cdot B$ (2.7) it is symmetrized by the entropy variables of $\eta=-\rho s/(\gamma-1)$ (§2, Eqs. (2.7)–(2.9)). **Stated** (cited to Godunov, Powell, Chandrashekar–Klingenberg)
2. The source equals $\phi_{\mathrm G}'(w)$ with $\phi_{\mathrm G}=-(w_2w_5+w_3w_6+w_4w_7)/w_8=2\mathsf b\,(v\cdot B)$, homogeneous of degree one; the potential is $\rho+\mathsf b\lvert B\rvert^2$ and the flux potential $\rho v_m+\mathsf b v_m\lvert B\rvert^2$ (§2, Eq. (2.9)). **Stated** *(compiler check, not in the sources: with $w_{2..4}=2\mathsf b v$, $w_{5..7}=2\mathsf b B$, $w_8=-2\mathsf b$ the ratio is $2\mathsf b\,v\cdot B$)*
3. The nodal DG scheme (3.11) with an entropy-conservative volume flux and an entropy-stable interface flux is entropy stable (Theorem 3.1; proof in Liu, Shu and Zhang). **Stated**
4. A flux is entropy conservative for the 1D model if $[\![w]\!]\cdot f^{\mathrm{ec}}+[\![\phi_{\mathrm G}]\!]\{\!\{B_1\}\!\}=[\![\psi]\!]$ (Def. 3.1, Eq. (3.12)); entropy stable with "$\le$" (Def. 3.2, Eq. (3.13)). **Stated** (definitions)
5. The Chandrashekar–Klingenberg flux, written out with logarithmic means of $\rho$ and $\mathsf b$, is the volume flux (§3.1). **Stated** (cited)
6. The interface flux is $h=f^{\mathrm{ec}}-\tfrac12\mathbf D[\![w]\!]$ with $\mathbf D=a\,\mathcal H$, $[\![u]\!]=\mathcal H[\![w]\!]$ (Derigs et al.), which reduces to the Lax–Friedrichs flux; the local speed $a=\max(\lvert\lambda^-_{\max}\rvert,\lvert\lambda^+_{\max}\rvert)$ is recommended (Eq. (3.14)). **Stated**
7. Damping coefficient (3.16): $\sigma_{\mathrm d}=a_j\,\mathcal M_j^{-2}\max_{1\le s\le8}\sum_{l=0}^{1}\tfrac12\Delta x^{2l}\big([\![\partial_x^lu_s]\!]^2_{j-1/2}+[\![\partial_x^lu_s]\!]^2_{j+1/2}\big)$ if $\mathcal M_j>\varepsilon$, else 0, with $a_j$ the largest MHD wave speed in $I_j$ and $\mathcal M_j=\max_s\lVert u_s-\mathrm{avg}_{\Omega_j}u_s\rVert_{L^\infty(\Omega_j)}$, $\Omega_j=I_{j-1}\cup I_j\cup I_{j+1}$ (§3.2). **Stated** (design)
8. The coefficient "introduces the local characteristic speed, is free of the characteristic decomposition, and is evolution-invariant", is affine-invariant through $\mathcal M_j^2$, and is "more robust, efficient, and easy to implement" than that of the authors' ES-OFDG paper (§3.2). **Stated** (invariance not proved; no side-by-side comparison)
9. **Theorem 3.2**: with an entropy-conservative volume flux and an entropy-stable interface flux, the damped scheme (3.15) is entropy stable, $\frac{d}{dt}\sum_j\sum_i\frac{\Delta x}2\mathsf w_i\eta(u^i_h)\le0$ (periodic boundaries), and its local truncation error is at least $O(\Delta x^N)$ (§3.2). **Proved**
10. The damping is entropy-dissipative cell by cell: by orthogonality of the cell-mean projection its contribution equals $-\sigma_{\mathrm d}\sum_i\mathsf w_i\big(\eta'(u^i_h)-\eta'(\bar u)\big)\cdot(u^i_h-\bar u)\le0$, by convexity (proof of Thm. 3.2, Eq. (3.18)). **Proved**
11. On a smooth exact solution the jumps of $u$ and $\partial_xu$ vanish, so $\sigma_{\mathrm d}=0$ and the interface source correction vanishes, giving the $O(\Delta x^N)$ truncation error (Eq. (3.19)). **Proved**
12. Positivity is assumed, not proved: it is linked to a discrete divergence-free condition (Wu) that the scheme does not satisfy, and a provably positivity-preserving entropy-stable DG scheme "remains an open challenge"; the Zhang–Shu limiter with adaptive time-step reduction is used heuristically (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
13. The method is non-conservative because of the source; a DG method that is at once conservative, globally divergence-free, entropy stable and positivity-preserving "remains open" (Remark 3.2). **Stated**
14. Explicit damping inside TVD-RK3 "may lead to restricted CFL conditions"; each stage is split into an explicit step and an implicit damping step with closed form $u^{n,1}_h=\frac1{1+\alpha_{\mathrm d}}\tilde u^{n,1}_h+\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm d}}{1+\alpha_{\mathrm d}}\overline{\tilde u^{n,1}_h}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm d}=\frac{2\Delta t}{\Delta x}\sigma_{\mathrm d}(\tilde u^{n,1}_h)$, so no nonlinear iteration is needed and the CFL condition is unchanged (§3.3, Eq. (3.21)). **Derived** (CFL claim not quantified)
15. The splitting is at most first order but does not pollute accuracy because $\sigma_{\mathrm d}$ is a high-order term in smooth regions; the proof for linear scalar laws is that of OEDG (§3.3). **Stated**
16. Parameters: $N=2$, $N_q=N+2$, CFL $0.6/(2N+1)$, $\varepsilon=10^{-12}$ (§4). **Stated**
17. Density wave ($N_x=12$ to 384, $T=1.3$): orders tend to 3.000 in $L^1$, $L^2$, $L^\infty$, with $L^2$ error $2.760\times10^{-9}$ at 384 cells (Table 1). **Shown**
18. Circularly polarized Alfvén wave ($T=5$, errors of $B_\perp$): $L^1$ orders 3.622, 5.065, 3.910, 3.086, 2.897 from $16^2$ to $512^2$, $L^1$ error $2.478\times10^{-8}$ at $512^2$; the authors write that "the optimal order of error accuracy is obtained" (Table 2). **Shown**
19. Brio–Wu, Ryu–Jones and the high-pressure-ratio tube ($N_x=800$) are resolved "without generating significant spurious oscillations" (Ex. 2, Figs. 1–3). **Shown**
20. Orszag–Tang: the discrete total entropy of the fully discrete scheme does not increase, although only semi-discrete stability is proved (Ex. 5, Fig. 6). **Shown**
21. Rotor: unlike standard DG, which shows a distortion of the Mach number around the central region (Li & Shu), the scheme shows none, which the authors attribute to entropy stability; density and pressure stay positive without a positivity limiter (Ex. 6, Fig. 7). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
22. Balsara–Spicer blast: captured "without resorting to artificial nonlinear limiters", with positive density and pressure near the shock front (Ex. 8, Fig. 9). **Shown**
23. Jets with $B_2=\sqrt{2000}$ (Mach 800) and $\sqrt{20000}$ (Mach 10000) need the positivity limiter and CFL $0.2/(2N+1)$, and "align well" with earlier results (Ex. 10, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
24. In [Liu & Wu (2025)](/sources/liu-2025.md) these jets needed the LDF OE step, positivity limiting and the upwind Godunov–Powell source, and disabling any one made the code fail; the authors read this as showing "the good performance and robustness of the proposed method" (Ex. 10). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Energy components of the fluxes (p. 3): "$u_1\big(E+p+\frac12\lvert\mathbf B\rvert\big)$" and likewise for $u_2$, $u_3$; the magnetic pressure lacks the square, which (2.1) prints as $\lvert\mathbf B\rvert^2/2$.
- Def. 3.2, Eq. (3.13): the entropy-stability inequality for the flux $\hat{\mathbf f}$ is written with $\mathbf f_S$ in place of $\hat{\mathbf f}$; read literally it is the condition (3.12) with $\le$.
- Proof of Theorem 3.2 (p. 8): the cell mean in the orthogonality step is printed $\frac12\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\mathbf W^\ell_hw_\ell$ with upper limit $k$ instead of $k_q$, and the inequality "$\le0$" is printed for a single node $i$, while the orthogonality argument holds for the sum over $i$.
- §1 cites "[11, Remark 4.4]" and §3.2 cites "[11, Remark 3.4]" for the same statement.
- Example 8: the text gives $T=0.01$, the caption of Fig. 9 "$T=0.6$".
- Example 7 prints the initial field $\mathbf B=[0,0,0]^T$, while the text describes the growth of the magnetic field and Fig. 8 shows magnetic pressure. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with $B\equiv0$ at $t=0$ the ideal induction equation $\partial_tB=\nabla\times(v\times B)$ keeps $B=0$, so the initial data as printed are incomplete.)*
- Example 3 does not give the propagation angle $\alpha$ numerically.
- Fig. 11 caption: "density logarithm $\rho\in[-1.58,1.12]$", where Fig. 12 prints $\log_{10}\rho$.
- "Sjögrenn" (Ex. 7) against "Sjögreen" (Ex. 4, ref. [56]); "Alfvên" (Ex. 2).
- Ref. [4] (Brackbill & Barnes, J. Comput. Phys. 35, 426–430) is printed with the year 1988 and the initial "F."; the bibliography holds this volume and page as a 1980 work, cited so by other sources.
