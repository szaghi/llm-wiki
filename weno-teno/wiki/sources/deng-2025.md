# Summary

The paper extends the [ROUND](/methods/round-scheme.md) family of three-cell compact
finite-volume reconstructions, introduced on uniform grids by
[Deng (2023)](/sources/deng-2023.md), to arbitrary non-uniform one-dimensional
grids. It then uses the result as the finite-volume scheme of a hard-switching
[DG subcell limiter](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-limiting.md) whose subcells are
non-uniform, sized by the Gauss–Legendre weights.

On a non-uniform stencil $\{I_{i-1},I_i,I_{i+1}\}$ the paper writes the upwind,
central and third-order quadratic reconstructions in normalised variables. It
proves three results for a linear normalised operator $\mathcal R(\tilde u)=\mu_1\tilde u+\mu_0$.
It is at least second order if and only if it passes through a grid-dependent
accuracy point (Proposition 1). Exactly one choice of $(\mu_0,\mu_1)$ gives third
order (Proposition 2). Its dissipative and anti-dissipative regions follow from the
signs of two differences (Proposition 3). The result is a non-uniform
[normalised-variable diagram](/definitions/normalised-variable-diagram.md) with ENO
and high-resolution regions. It also gives a piecewise-linear ROUND operator with
four constants. That operator needs no smoothness indicators and lies in the
[dissipative](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) region for $0<\tilde u_i<1$.

Coupled to a modal Legendre DG scheme, the subcell values are the Gauss-point
values, the subcell widths are the Gauss weights, and a mass-matrix projection maps
subcell data back to modal coefficients. The
[troubled-element indicator](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) is a relaxed
[BVD](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md) criterion. It compares the
total boundary variation of the first-order upwind and the third-order linear
reconstructions.

The tests are:

- a rotating scalar ([solid-body rotation](/benchmarks/solid-body-rotation.md)) on uniform and strongly non-uniform grids;
- third order on the [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md), and conservation to round-off;
- the [Woodward–Colella blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md);
- [Shu–Osher](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) and Titarev–Toro;
- the [Lax tube](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md);
- two [2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md), the second with [Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) roll-ups;
- [double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md);
- the [viscous shock tube](/benchmarks/viscous-shock-tube.md) at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$;
- a hypersonic type IV shock–shock interaction on a [cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md), compared with Wieting–Holden experiments.

An appendix gives preliminary unstructured OpenFOAM results.

# Key claims

Registry notation, with $\Delta x_i$ the width of $I_i$ (the source's $h_i$),
$\tilde u_i=(\bar u_i-\bar u_{i-1})/(\bar u_{i+1}-\bar u_{i-1})$ and
$\tilde u_{i+1/2}=(u^-_{i+1/2}-\bar u_{i-1})/(\bar u_{i+1}-\bar u_{i-1})$, and $\mu_0$,
$\mu_1$ the intercept and slope of a linear normalised operator. Page-local:
$\Sigma_i=\Delta x_{i-1}+2\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1}$; $\tilde u_A=(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i)/\Sigma_i$
the abscissa of the accuracy point; $c_{\mathrm P}=\Delta x_i(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i)/\big((\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})\big)$
the third-order intercept; $\beta^{\mathrm R}_1,\dots,\beta^{\mathrm R}_4$ the ROUND
constants; $\mathrm{TBV}$ the total boundary variation.

1. Proposition 1: a linear normalised operator is at least second-order accurate if and only if its graph passes through the accuracy point $\big(\tilde u_A,\ (\Delta x_{i-1}+2\Delta x_i)/\Sigma_i\big)$ (§2.2, Taylor expansion (2.20)–(2.21)). **Proved**
2. Proposition 2: third order holds only for $\mu_0=c_{\mathrm P}$ and $\mu_1=\frac{\Delta x_{i+1}(\Delta x_{i-1}^2+3\Delta x_{i-1}\Delta x_i+3\Delta x_i^2+\Delta x_{i-1}\Delta x_{i+1}+2\Delta x_i\Delta x_{i+1})}{(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i)(\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})}$, the normalised form of the quadratic reconstruction (2.12) (§2.2). **Proved**
3. Proposition 3: for a second-order linear operator, and no critical point in the stencil, the leading error is numerical diffusion when $\mu_0-c_{\mathrm P}$ and $\tilde u_i-\tilde u_A$ have the same sign, and anti-diffusion when they have opposite signs (§2.2, Eqs. (2.22)–(2.25)). **Derived**
4. For positive widths the slopes order as upwind > quadratic > central and the intercepts as central > quadratic (Eqs. (2.16)–(2.17)); all three operators pass through the accuracy point (Eq. (2.18)). **Stated**
5. For equal widths the operators and propositions reduce to the uniform-grid ones of Deng (2023) (Remark 2.1). **Stated**
6. The non-uniform diagram has the accuracy point and the third-order line, the diffusion and anti-diffusion regions, an ENO region bounded by first-order downwind ($\tilde u_{i+1/2}=1$), first-order upwind ($\tilde u_{i+1/2}=\tilde u_i$) and $\tilde u_{i+1/2}=c\,\tilde u_i$ with CFL $<1/c$ "a safe choice", and high-resolution regions outside $[0,1]$ (§2.3, Fig. 1(a)). **Stated**
7. The ROUND operator (2.26) with $\beta^{\mathrm R}_1=1/4$, $\beta^{\mathrm R}_2=3/2$, $\beta^{\mathrm R}_3=2/5$, $\beta^{\mathrm R}_4=6/5$, used in every test, recovers the quadratic reconstruction away from $\tilde u_i\approx0,1$ and lies entirely in the dissipative region for $0<\tilde u_i<1$ (§2.4, Fig. 1(b)). **Stated** (by construction)
8. Unlike non-uniform WENO, non-uniform ROUND needs no smoothness indicators, which the authors give as the reason it is cheaper (§1, §2.4, §5). **Stated** (no timings)
9. Rotating complex waves ($200\times200$ cells): ROUND is far less dissipative than TVD limiters (minmod, van Leer, …) and keeps the shape of the scalar on a strongly non-uniform grid, while the van Leer limiter distorts it even on a uniform grid (§4.1, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
10. Along $y=3.25$, ROUND on the non-uniform grid is less dissipative than WENO-JS5 on the uniform grid and has no overshoot, while WENO-JS5 overshoots (§4.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
11. ROUND stays comparable on grids with larger subcell-width ratios, those of fifth-order DG on $40\times40$ and sixth-order DG on $34\times34$ elements (§4.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
12. Third-order DG/FV-ROUND on the isentropic vortex gives $L^2$ density orders 2.69, 3.40, 3.41 and 2.65 from 10 to 50 elements per side, with 1–10 % troubled elements, more on coarse meshes (§4.2, Table 1). **Shown**
13. With 5 % of the elements forced to be troubled at random every step (no indicator), the orders are 2.60, 2.42, 2.47 and 2.13 (§4.2, Table 2). **Shown**
14. The maximum conservation deviation over all steps on $40\times40$ elements is $2.44\times10^{-16}$ ($\rho$), $3.63\times10^{-16}$ ($\rho v_1$), $0.84\times10^{-16}$ ($\rho v_2$) and $5.09\times10^{-16}$ ($E$) (§4.2, Table 3). **Shown**
15. Woodward–Colella blast waves: third-order DG/FV-ROUND with 130 elements (390 degrees of freedom) at $t=0.038$ is competitive with ninth-order DG with first-order subcell correction (Vilar) at 540 degrees of freedom, and slightly better at the contact at $x=0.6$ (§4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown**
16. Shu–Osher: third-order DG/FV-ROUND with 300 degrees of freedom is better than or comparable to the subcell approach of Hennemann et al. (fifth-order DG with first-order subcells) at 320 and 640; fifth order with 400 degrees of freedom is comparable with fifth-order CPR with second-order CNNW subcell limiting at 400 (§4.3, Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
17. Titarev–Toro shock–density-wave test at 750 degrees of freedom: fifth order on 150 elements resolves slightly better than third order on 250 (§4.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
18. Lax tube (180 degrees of freedom): DG/FV-ROUND is less dissipative and less oscillatory than third-order DG with WENO-JS5 on uniform subcells (§4.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. 2D Riemann case 1 ($200\times200$ fourth-order elements, $800\times800$ degrees of freedom, $t=0.8$): symmetry is preserved "without introducing extra reconstruction strategies", which the authors credit to the scalar-structure-preserving property of ROUND and to the indicator; the shock lies within fewer than four subcells, inside one element (§4.4, Fig. 14). **Shown**
20. 2D Riemann case 2: fourth order on $400\times400$ elements is comparable to the sixth-order finite volumes with space-time AMR of Dumbser et al. (§4.4, Fig. 15). **Shown**
21. Double Mach reflection (fourth order, $480\times120$ elements): the troubled-element distribution is comparable to [Mossier et al. (2022)](/sources/mossier-2022.md); resolution is better than Hennemann et al. at similar cost and comparable to sixth-order Sonntag–Munz with $480\times120\times6^2$ degrees of freedom. Small acoustic waves are attributed to excessive DG/FV switching and imperfect coupling (§4.5, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
22. The viscous shock tube at $\mathrm{Re}=1000$ (fourth order, $750\times375$ elements) failed at the start with negative density or pressure until an a posteriori fix was added: subcells where negativity appears at Gauss points are recomputed with the first-order Godunov scheme. The fix acts only at the initial stage, and the results agree with Daru–Tenaud along the wall and at $y=0.05$ (§4.6, Figs. 18–19). **Shown**
23. Type IV shock–shock interaction (fourth order, $43\times94$ curvilinear elements, cubic boundary): the time-averaged wall pressure agrees well with Wieting–Holden; the heat flux follows the trend but its peak is under-predicted (§4.7, Figs. 20–23). **Shown**
24. The finite-volume metrics come from the DG mapping, which "may lead to the violation of the free-stream preservation or the geometric conservation law"; this is not fixed (§4.7). **Stated**
25. ROUND is scale-invariant, but DG/FV-ROUND is not, because the indicator threshold is scale-dependent (§3.3). **Stated**

The indicator (§3.3, Eqs. (3.7)–(3.9)) marks subcell $i$ when
$\mathrm{TBV}^{\mathrm P}_i-\mathrm{TBV}^{\mathrm U}_i>\max\big(10^{-6},10^{-4}(\lvert\bar u_{i-1}\rvert+\lvert\bar u_i\rvert+\lvert\bar u_{i+1}\rvert)\big)$,
with $\mathrm{TBV}^{\mathrm U}_i=\lvert\bar u_i-\bar u_{i-1}\rvert+\lvert\bar u_i-\bar u_{i+1}\rvert$
and $\mathrm{TBV}^{\mathrm P}_i$ the sum of the interface jumps of the quadratic
reconstruction. In 1D an element is troubled if one subcell is marked, in 2D if more
than half are. The indicator is applied at every Runge–Kutta stage.

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* Propositions 1 and 2 and the accuracy point
(2.18) were re-derived symbolically from the cell averages of a cubic on the
non-uniform stencil: second order is equivalent to passing through
$(\tilde u_A,(\Delta x_{i-1}+2\Delta x_i)/\Sigma_i)$, and third order fixes $\mu_0=c_{\mathrm P}$
and the $\mu_1$ of claim 2.

# Printed slips

The corrections to Eqs. (2.20)–(2.24) below are *(compiler check, not in the sources)*: a symbolic Taylor
expansion of $\bar u_{i-1}+\mu_1(\bar u_i-\bar u_{i-1})+\mu_0(\bar u_{i+1}-\bar u_{i-1})$
about $x_{i-1/2}$, from the cell averages of a cubic.

- **Eq. (2.20):** the first-order bracket prints $\mu_0\big(1+\frac{\Delta x_{i+1}}{\Delta x_i}+\frac{\Delta x_{i-1}}{\Delta x_i}\big)$; the expansion gives $\mu_0\big(2+\frac{\Delta x_{i+1}}{\Delta x_i}+\frac{\Delta x_{i-1}}{\Delta x_i}\big)$, as Eq. (2.21) also has.
- **Eq. (2.21):** the first-order bracket prints $(-1+\mu_1+\mu_0)\frac{\Delta x_{i-1}}{\Delta x_{i+1}}$; the expansion has $\frac{\Delta x_{i-1}}{\Delta x_i}$. The condition used in the proof of Proposition 1 and Propositions 1–2 themselves are correct.
- **Eq. (2.24):** printed $\frac{\phi''}{\phi'}=\frac{3(2h_i+h_{i-1}h_i+h_{i+1}h_i)^2}{(h_{i-1}+h_i)(h_{i-1}+h_{i+1})(h_{i-1}+h_i+h_{i+1})}(\theta_A-\hat\phi^L_i)$, whose numerator mixes lengths and squared lengths. The expansion of $\tilde u_i$ for a quadratic gives $\frac{3\Sigma_i^2}{(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i)(\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})}(\tilde u_A-\tilde u_i)$, which is $4/\Delta x$ on a uniform grid. The prefactor is positive either way, so Proposition 3 stands.
- **Eq. (2.23):** with the second-order condition substituted, the coefficient of the second derivative in the error is $\tfrac16(c_{\mathrm P}-\mu_0)(\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})(\Delta x_{i-1}+\Delta x_i+\Delta x_{i+1})$ (sign as in the source's convention), where (2.23) prints $\tfrac16(c_P-\alpha_0)h^2$. The factor is positive, so the sign argument is unaffected. (2.23) writes $\phi'(x_c)h^2$ and (2.25) $\phi'(x_c)h$ without explanation.
- **§4.1:** "$\beta=log_2(36\delta^2)$" with $\delta=0.01$. This is negative ($\log_2 0.0036\approx-8.12$), so $G(r,\beta)=\exp(-\beta r^2)$ would exceed 1 inside its support. [Deng (2023)](/sources/deng-2023.md) prints $\beta=\log2/(36\delta^2)$ for the same rotation test with $\delta=0.01$, $\alpha=5$.
- **§4.1:** the run goes to $t=2$ "(after one period)"; the field $v=(0.5y-1,\ 1-0.5x)$ rotates about $(2,2)$ with angular speed $1/2$, so one period is $4\pi\approx12.6$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Eq. (4.5):** the Shu–Osher post-shock state is assigned "if $0\le x\le-4$", an empty set on $[-5,5]$.
- **Eq. (4.3):** the temperature perturbation is printed "$\delta=-\frac{(\gamma-1)\varepsilon^2}{4\alpha\gamma}e^{2\alpha(1-\tau^2)}$", without $T$.
- **Eq. (2.8):** the left side is printed $d\phi_i/dt$ without the bar of (2.7).
- **§4.7:** the lower-inlet $v_\infty=0.35\times10^3$ is printed without units.
- Not printed: the CFL number of the rotation test, the Riemann solver, and the Prandtl number and $\gamma$ of the viscous shock tube.
