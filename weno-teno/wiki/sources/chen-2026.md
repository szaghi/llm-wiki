# Summary

Chen & Chen (2026) build a 2D finite-difference solver for compressible Euler and
Navier–Stokes flows past obstacles on uniform Cartesian grids from three existing
ingredients. The first is the fifth-order [TENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md)
reconstruction of Lax–Friedrichs-split characteristic fluxes, with the cut-off $C_T$
chosen locally by the [adaptive-TENO](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) rule of Fu, Hu and
Adams, driven by the first-order discontinuity measure of Ren et al. rather than by
a high-order [smoothness indicator](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md). The second
is the Hu–Adams–Shu
[positivity-preserving flux limiter](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md),
applied at every stage of Ruuth's five-stage third-order SSP Runge–Kutta method. The
third is a ghost-cell sharp-interface
[immersed-boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) with image points and
body-intercept points and bilinear interpolation of conservative variables.

The contribution is the coupling. Every interface flux whose stencil contains a ghost
point uses $C_T=10^{-1}$, the largest value, to add
[dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) next to the body; elsewhere
$C_T$ ranges over $[10^{-9},10^{-1}]$. The bilinear interpolation at body-intercept
points, and at image points whose four bounding vertices are fluid points, is written
as two successive convex combinations, so by convexity of the admissible set it keeps
density and pressure positive. Where a bounding vertex is replaced by a
body-intercept point the bilinear fit is not convex, and a blend with the grid-only
interpolation, its weight found by bisection, restores positivity. The paper prints
no proof of its "Theorem 1", no rules for the ghost-point density and pressure, and
no CFL number, on which the limiter's guarantee depends: the claims on
[bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md) rest on these parts.

Validation is numerical: second-order wall error on a Mach 3 cylinder, maps of $C_T$
and of limiter activity on the Mach 3 [cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md)
of Guermond et al. (where [Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md)
roll-up of the contact lines appears), three cylinders at Mach 2, Schardin's
[shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) problem against WENO-Z and
experiment, wedges at Mach 2–6 against oblique-shock theory, and viscous cylinders at
$\mathrm{Re}=2\times10^5$ and $M=1.7,3,5$. No timings are given.

# Key claims

Registry notation: conserved state $u=(\rho,\rho v,E)$ (source $Q$), velocity $v$
(source $u_i$), point values $u_i$, split fluxes $f^\pm$ in characteristic variables,
TENO symbols $S_k$, $\hat f^{(k)}_{i+1/2}$, $\beta_k$, $\gamma_k$, $\chi_k$, $\delta_k$,
$C_T$, $d_k$, $q$, $\epsilon$, and the adaptive-TENO symbols $\sigma_i$, $\mu_d$,
$\varphi(\mu_d)$, $b_1$, $b_2$, $\xi_{\mathrm A}$ of the
[adaptive-TENO page](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) (source $\eta_i$, $m$, $g(m)$,
$\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, $\xi$). Page-local: $\theta_{i+1/2}$ the limiter factor
($\theta=1$ keeps the high-order flux), $\alpha^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ik}$, $\beta^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ik}$
the Shu–Osher coefficients of SSPRK(5,3), $G$ the admissible set (source $\mathcal A$).

1. The framework couples a ghost-cell sharp-interface immersed-boundary method with a dissipation-adaptive TENO scheme that raises $C_T$ near immersed boundaries and uses a first-order indicator to adapt dissipation elsewhere (Abstract, §1, §2.3). **Stated**
2. Interface fluxes whose stencil contains at least one ghost point use $C_T=10^{-1}$, "ensuring numerical stability while maintaining the second-order accuracy of the IBM" (§2.3.2, Fig. 3). **Stated** (stability **Shown** in §3)
3. With $b_1=9$, $b_2=8$ in $C_T=10^{-\lfloor b_1-b_2(1-\varphi(\mu_d))\rfloor}$, $\varphi(\mu_d)=(1-\mu_d)^4(1+4\mu_d)$, $C_T$ ranges over $[10^{-9},10^{-1}]$, "notably broader than the range of $10^{-10}$ to $10^{-5}$ reported by Fu et al." (§2.3.2, Eq. (26)). **Derived**
4. Increasing $C_T$ allows larger CFL numbers without losing boundedness across discontinuities, as found by Deng et al. (2025) (§1, §2.3.2). **Stated** (cited)
5. The first-order Lax–Friedrichs flux is positivity preserving under CFL $<1/2$, citing Zhang & Shu (2010) (§2.4, Eq. (27)). **Stated**
6. Each SSPRK(5,3) stage is a forward-Euler step $u^{s+1}_i=u^s_i+\lambda(\hat f_{i-1/2}-\hat f_{i+1/2})$, $\lambda=\Delta t\sum_k\beta^{\mathrm{RK}}_{sk}/\Delta x$, which splits into the mean of the half-states $u^s_i\pm2\lambda\hat f_{i\mp1/2}$, so positivity of both half-states gives positivity of the stage (§2.4, Eqs. (28)–(30)). **Derived**
7. The limiter, applied to density and then to pressure with floors $\min(10^{-13},\rho^0_{\min})$ and $\min(10^{-13},p^0_{\min})$, is enforced at each sub-stage of SSPRK(5,3) to guarantee positive density and pressure (§2.4, Eqs. (31)–(32)). **Derived** (after Hu, Adams & Shu)
8. "Theorem 1": pressure is "convex" in the conservative variables, so positivity of $p$ is kept under convex combinations, and $G=\{\rho>0,p>0\}$ is convex (§2.4, Eqs. (33)–(34), citing Zhang & Shu). **Stated** (no proof)
9. Bilinear interpolation at a body-intercept point is the two-step convex combination $u_{I_1}=\omega_xu_{N_1}+(1-\omega_x)u_{N_2}$, $u_{I_2}=\omega_xu_{N_4}+(1-\omega_x)u_{N_3}$, $u_P=\omega_yu_{I_1}+(1-\omega_y)u_{I_2}$, hence keeps positive density and pressure when the four grid points have them (§2.5). **Derived**
10. The bilinear fit through a body-intercept point and three grid points (Eqs. (35)–(36)) "does not guarantee the positivity of density and pressure"; the blend $u_P=(1-\omega)u^{\mathrm{BGP}}_P+\omega u^{\mathrm{OGP}}_P$, $\omega\in[0,1]$ by bisection (Eq. (37)), fixes it, and "even in the most challenging case, the weighted correction is rarely activated" (§2.5). **Stated** (frequency not quantified)
11. The interpolation "ensures density and pressure remain positive at every grid point for all time, provided the initial data are physical" (Abstract). **Stated**: ghost values are extrapolated as $V_{GP}=2V_{BI}-V_{IP}$ in primitive variables (Eq. (38)); only the velocity and temperature rules are printed (Eq. (40)), and no CFL number is printed for any test.
12. Wall-normal velocity $L_2$ error on the Mach 3 cylinder, grids $800\times400$ to $6400\times3200$: $7.60\cdot10^{-2}$, $1.86\cdot10^{-2}$, $2.64\cdot10^{-3}$, $5.54\cdot10^{-4}$, orders 2.03, 2.81, 2.25, "the expected second-order accuracy" (§3.1, Table 3). **Shown**
13. On $700\times350$ points (close to the 252,820 of Guermond et al.) the Mach 3 cylinder shows the same two triple points, and the roll-up of the contact lines "is more clearly visible", attributed to the low dissipation of adaptive TENO (§3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. Inviscid simulations do not converge strongly under refinement, and very fine grids can produce scheme-dependent fine structures (§3.1, cited). **Stated**
15. With $C_T\in[10^{-9},10^{-1}]$ the limiter is rarely active; with $C_T\in[10^{-15},10^{-5}]$ (and $10^{-1}$ at the wall) activations increase near the shock; with $C_T=10^{-14}$ fixed (and $10^{-1}$ at the wall) they spread into the wake: smaller $C_T$ resolves more high-wavenumber content but lowers stability (§3.1, Fig. 8). **Shown** (maps, no counts)
16. Schardin's problem: WENO-Z and adaptive TENO, both with the immersed boundary, agree with the experiment of Chang & Chang at $t=3.09$ and $4.26$, and adaptive TENO "exhibits lower numerical dissipation in resolving the fine structures" (§3.2, Figs. 11–13). **Shown** (visual)
17. Wedge shock angles against oblique-shock theory: $15^\circ$ at $M=2$, $45.19^\circ$/$45.27^\circ$/$45.41^\circ$ on $250\times125$/$1200\times600$/$1800\times900$ against $45.34^\circ$; $M=4$, $26.89^\circ$ against $27.06^\circ$; $M=6$, $22.38^\circ$ against $22.67^\circ$; $20^\circ$ at $M=3.5$, $34.80^\circ$ against $34.58^\circ$ (§3.3, Table 4). **Shown**
18. At $M=2$ the method "captures shocks more sharply and resolves finer details in the wake region using fewer grid points" than Mo et al. (§3.3, Fig. 14). **Shown** (visual)
19. Viscous cylinder, $\mathrm{Re}_\infty=2\times10^5$: at $M_\infty=1.7$ separation angle $115^\circ$, stand-off $0.950D$, recirculation length $1.20D$ (Turner et al.: $113^\circ$, $1.030$, $1.17$); at $M_\infty=5$, $120^\circ$, $0.240$, $1.76$ (Turner et al.: $118^\circ$, $0.245$, $1.75$) (§3.4, Table 5). **Shown**
20. At $M_\infty=3$ the stand-off is $0.33D$, against $0.34$ from the correlation $0.2\exp(4.7/M^2)$ and $0.37$ from Mao et al. (§3.4, Table 6, Eq. (43)). **Shown**
21. Agreement is better at $M_\infty=5$ than at $1.7$, where the Mach stem became unsteady because the bow-shock reflection angle exceeds the critical value for Mach reflection, and a larger domain ($2000\times1200$) was used (§3.4). **Shown** / **Stated**
22. The $C_p$ discrepancy "may be attributed to the finite-box effect" of interpolation at the solid boundary (§3.4, Fig. 20(b)). **Stated**
23. Mean wake profiles at $M_\infty=5$, $x/D=0.55$–$2.5$, agree closely with Turner et al. (§3.4, Fig. 21). **Shown**
24. The immersed boundary is formally second order, yet using the high-order TENO scheme up to the boundary "is more convenient and does not compromise the robustness" (§2.5). **Stated**
25. TENO ingredients as printed: $\gamma_k=1/(\beta_k+\epsilon)^q$, $q=6$, $\epsilon=10^{-40}$ (Eq. (19), without the $C+\tau_K/(\beta_k+\epsilon)$ form of the TENO5 measure recorded on [targeted ENO](/methods/targeted-eno.md)); target schemes $\tfrac1{10},\tfrac6{10},\tfrac3{10}$ for $\delta=(1,1,1)$, $(\tfrac14,\tfrac34)$ for $(1,1,0)$, $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ for $(0,1,1)$ and the single candidate otherwise (Table 2); viscous fluxes by fifth-order central differences (§2.2.2, §2.3.1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Concave or convex**: the abstract says "pressure is concave in the conservative variables", "Theorem 1" (§2.4) that it is "convex". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $p=(\gamma-1)\big(E-\lvert\rho v\rvert^2/(2\rho)\big)$ and $\lvert\rho v\rvert^2/\rho$ is jointly convex for $\rho>0$, so $p$ is concave, which is what the conclusion on convex combinations needs; the Theorem's wording is the slip.)*
- Eq. (2): the momentum flux is printed $\rho u_iu_j+p\sigma_{ij}$, with $\sigma_{ij}$ the viscous stress of Eq. (3), for $p\delta_{ij}$.
- Eq. (23): the measure is printed $\min(\eta_{i-1},\eta_i,\eta_{i+2})$, without $\eta_{i+1}$. [Fu (2023)](/sources/fu-2023.md) and [Fu (2021)](/sources/fu-2021.md) print the four-point minimum $\min(\sigma_{i-1},\sigma_i,\sigma_{i+1},\sigma_{i+2})$, [Huang et al. (2023)](/sources/huang-2023-b.md) the three-point $\min(\sigma_{i-1},\sigma_i,\sigma_{i+1})$.
- Eq. (27): the Lax–Friedrichs flux is written with $Q^n_i$, $Q^n_{i+1}$ although it is applied at every stage.
- Table 3: the second order is printed 2.81. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\log_2(1.86\cdot10^{-2}/2.64\cdot10^{-3})=2.817$.)*
- Fig. 8, middle column: the $b_1$, $b_2$ that give $C_T\in[10^{-15},10^{-5}]$ are not printed.
- §3.1: "the same two shock-wave triple points in the forward facing step" for the cylinder test; §3.4 refers to "Fig. 16(a), (c) and (e)" and "Figs. 16(b), (d) and (f)" for the six-panel Fig. 18 (Fig. 16 is the Mach 3.5 wedge).
- §2.2.1 "proposed by Ruuth and Steven", ref. [66] being Ruuth alone; ref. [59] is cited as "Deng" and "Deng et al."; "Courant-Friedriches-Levy", "viscousity", "conversation equations", "specific heat ration", "progates", "evoles", "continious", "maximun".
- References: [71] duplicates [57] (Fu, Comput. Phys. Commun. 244:117–131, 2019); [62] prints "X. Zhang, C.-W. Shu, et al." for a two-author paper; [78] prints its DOI twice.
