# Summary

Duan et al. (2025) work with the generalized (method-of-lines)
[Active Flux](/methods/active-flux.md) method of Abgrall and Barsukow. The degrees
of freedom are cell averages and point values at interfaces (in 2D also at corners),
joined by a globally continuous piecewise-parabolic reconstruction. Averages are
updated by the finite-volume formula with fluxes taken at the point values; point
values by a finite-difference-like formula; both with SSP-RK3.

The point-value update of that method applies Jacobian splitting, $A^\pm=R\Lambda^\pm L$, to
upwind derivatives of the reconstruction. The authors identify two defects. For
Burgers' equation, at a transonic jump with point values of opposite sign, both
point values freeze while the average grows linearly (a stagnation spike). In 2D,
for grid-aligned flow, the corner and horizontal-face values decouple from the
vertical-face values and the averages (a mesh-alignment defect). Their cure is
[flux vector splitting](/methods/flux-vector-splitting.md): $f=f^++f^-$ with
$\pm$ eigenvalues of $\partial f^\pm/\partial u$ non-negative, each split flux
interpolated by a parabola through its values at the two interfaces and at the
cell-centred point value. Three splittings are tested:
[local Lax–Friedrichs](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md), Steger–Warming (upwind) and
van Leer–Hänel.

[Bound preservation](/properties/bound-preservation.md) needs both kinds of
unknowns in $G$. Averages are limited by [convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md)
against first-order LLF fluxes written through intermediate states, with density
then pressure for Euler and a linear sufficient condition for the quadratic
pressure constraint. Point values are limited by a
[Zhang–Shu-type scaling](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) towards a
first-order LLF point update on a staggered stencil. A shock-sensor blend
$\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm s}\varphi_{\mathrm J}\Psi_{\mathrm D})$, a Jameson pressure
sensor times a compression-only [Ducros sensor](/definitions/ducros-shock-sensor.md),
moves the limited flux further towards LLF behind strong shocks.

Tests: [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md), [LeBlanc](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md),
[Woodward–Colella](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md), a near-vacuum
[isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md), quasi-2D
[Sod](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md), [Sedov](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md),
the [forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md), Mach 80 and 2000
[jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md), and in the appendix the
[double rarefaction](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md), a
[2D Riemann problem](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) and the
[double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md). Jacobian splitting,
LLF and van Leer–Hänel reach third order, Steger–Warming about second; LLF
splitting is judged best for CFL and robustness.

# Key claims

Registry notation: conserved $u$ (source $U$), averages $\bar u_i$, Active Flux
interface point values $u_{i+1/2}$ (single-valued: the reconstruction is
continuous), cell-centred value $u_i=(-u_{i-1/2}+6\bar u_i-u_{i+1/2})/4$ (not an
unknown), Jacobian $A$ (source $J$), spectral radius $a$ (source $\varrho$),
sound speed $c$ (source $a$), admissible set $G$. Page-local: $\mathsf e_{i+1/2}\in[0,1]$
the convex-limiting flux blend (source $\theta$; 1 = Active Flux, 0 = LLF; as on [convex limiting](/methods/convex-limiting.md)), $\theta$
the point-value scaling factor as in the registry, $\varphi_{\mathrm J}$ the
Jameson pressure sensor, $\Psi_{\mathrm D}$ the modified Ducros sensor,
$\kappa_{\mathrm s}$ the sensor strength, $\ell^{\mathrm s}$ the sensor blend,
$\mathcal E$ the vortex strength.

1. Jacobian-splitting Active Flux stagnates for Burgers' equation: with $u_{i-1/2}=2$, $u_{i+1/2}=-1$ at the initial jump the upwind derivatives (8) vanish, both point values stay fixed and $\bar u_i$ grows linearly (§2.1, Example 5.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
2. Evolving the point values by flux vector splitting "borrows the information from the neighbors naturally" and removes the spike, with the continuous reconstruction kept (§2.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
3. For linear systems the upwind splitting $f^\pm=\tfrac12(f\pm\lvert A\rvert u)$, $\lvert A\rvert=R(\Lambda^+-\Lambda^-)L$, reduces exactly to Jacobian splitting, provided the split-flux differences and the reconstruction derivatives come from the same polynomial (§2.2.2). **Derived**
4. The differences $(\tilde D^+f^+)_{i+1/2}=\frac1{\Delta x_i}(f^+_{i-1/2}-4f^+_i+3f^+_{i+1/2})$ and its mirror for $f^-$, from the parabola through the two interface values and the centre value, are third order; their coefficients differ from those of Abgrall & Barsukow because they use $u_i$, not $\bar u_i$ (§2.2, Eq. (11)). **Stated**
5. The Steger–Warming split fluxes need not be differentiable in $u$ for nonlinear systems; following van Leer, the authors say this "might explain" the order loss (Remark 2.1, Examples 5.6, D.1). **Stated** (order loss **Shown**)
6. Mesh alignment: on a quasi-2D Sod problem on $100\times2$ cells, Jacobian splitting gives large density deviations between contact and shock, and the corner and horizontal-face values decouple from the vertical-face values and averages; all flux-vector-splitting variants agree with the exact solution once limiting is on (§3.2, Example 5.7, Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
7. Lemma 4.1: the 2D first-order LLF scheme (28) is a convex combination of intermediate states, hence bound preserving, under the step condition (30) (§4.1). **Proved** (by citation plus Appendix B)
8. Lemmas B.1–B.2: the LLF intermediate state $\tfrac12(u_L+u_R)+\frac1{2a}\big(f(u_L)-f(u_R)\big)$ lies in $G$ for $a\ge\max\{a(u_L),a(u_R)\}$, for scalar laws and for Euler (App. B). **Proved**
9. Proposition 4.1: if $\bar u^n_{i,j}$ and the limited intermediate states lie in $G$, the limited average update (32) is bound preserving under (30), and so is SSP-RK3 by convexity (§4.1). **Proved**
10. The limited average update is conservative, its flux being $\mathsf e\hat f^{\mathrm H}+(1-\mathsf e)\hat f^{\mathrm L}$ (Remark 4.1). **Derived**
11. Condition (30) is checked at $t^n$ only; if it fails at a later Runge–Kutta stage, the step is restarted with halved $\Delta t$ until the states (29) are admissible and (30) holds (Remark 4.2). **Stated**
12. The pressure constraint on the limited intermediate states is quadratic in $\mathsf e$, $A_{\mathsf e}\mathsf e^2\pm B_{\mathsf e}\mathsf e\le C_{\mathsf e}$ (36); following Kuzmin it is replaced by the linear sufficient condition $(\max\{0,A_{\mathsf e}\}+\lvert B_{\mathsf e}\rvert)\mathsf e\le C_{\mathsf e}$, so $\mathsf e=\min\{1,C_{\mathsf e}/(\max\{0,A_{\mathsf e}\}+\lvert B_{\mathsf e}\rvert)\}$ (§4.1.2). **Derived**
13. Limiting the averages alone does not keep the bounds; point-value limiting is needed too. In 2D advection $[0,1]$ is kept only with both limitings, for global or local bounds (§4.2, Example 5.4, Table 1). **Shown**
14. Lemma 4.2: the first-order LLF point-value updates (39)–(41) are bound preserving under the six-term condition (42) (§4.2). **Stated** ("straightforward", by analogy with Lemma 4.1)
15. The centre value $u_i$, and its 2D form (24), is not a convex combination and can leave $G$; harmless for scalar laws, for Euler it is scaled towards $\bar u_{i,j}$ at the start of every Runge–Kutta stage (Remarks 4.3, 4.4). **Stated**
16. Proposition 4.2 (2D) and Theorem C.1 (1D): with data in $G$ and steps satisfying (30) and (42) (1D: (50) and (60)), Active Flux with SSP-RK3 and both limitings keeps the maximum principle for scalar laws and positive density and pressure for Euler (§4.2, App. C). **Proved** (assembled from the lemmas)
17. On uniform meshes, $\Delta t\le\tfrac14\min\{\Delta x/\lVert a^{(1)}\rVert_\infty,\Delta y/\lVert a^{(2)}\rVert_\infty\}$ satisfies (30) and (42) (Remark 4.5), and $\Delta t\le\Delta x/(2\lVert a\rVert_\infty)$ satisfies (50) and (60) in 1D (Remark C.1). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: substituting uniform spacing and the global spectral radii into (30) and into the six terms of (42) gives the factor $\tfrac14$ in each.)*
18. The shock-sensor blend $\ell^{\mathrm s}=\exp(-\kappa_{\mathrm s}\varphi_{\mathrm J}\Psi_{\mathrm D})\in(0,1]$, with $\Psi_{\mathrm D}=\max\{-\nabla\cdot v/\sqrt{(\nabla\cdot v)^2+(\nabla\times v)^2+10^{-40}},0\}$ and face values the maximum of the two cells, strongly reduces oscillations behind strong shocks and keeps bound preservation because $\ell^{\mathrm s}\le1$; $\kappa_{\mathrm s}$ is problem dependent and "its optimal choice needs further investigation" (§4.1.3; Examples 5.9, D.6, D.7). **Shown**
19. LeBlanc: stable at CFL 0.4 with Jacobian, LLF and Steger–Warming splitting, 0.1 with van Leer–Hänel; 6000 cells, $\kappa_{\mathrm s}=10$; without bound-preserving limiting the run stops on negative density or pressure (Example 5.2). **Shown**
20. Blast waves: CFL 0.4 (Jacobian, LLF, Steger–Warming) and 0.36 (van Leer–Hänel), 800 cells, $\kappa_{\mathrm s}=1$; the run fails without bound-preserving limiting (Example 5.3). **Shown**
21. Isentropic vortex with $\mathcal E=10.0828$: minimum density about $7.83\times10^{-15}$ and pressure about $1.78\times10^{-20}$; with limiting, Jacobian, LLF and van Leer–Hänel reach third order in $\ell^1$ and Steger–Warming about second; CFL 0.2 (Example 5.6, Fig. 9). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: at $r=0$ the printed vortex gives $k_0=\mathcal Ee^{0.5}/(2\pi)=2.6457$, $T_0=1-\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}k_0^2=2.278\times10^{-6}$ for $\gamma=1.4$, $\rho=T_0^{2.5}=7.834\times10^{-15}$ and $p=T_0\rho=1.785\times10^{-20}$.)*
22. 1D Euler accuracy ($\gamma=3$, $\rho_0=1+(1-10^{-7})\sin(\pi x)$): largest stable CFL about 0.47 (Jacobian), 0.43 (LLF), 0.32 (Steger–Warming), 0.18 (van Leer–Hänel); at CFL 0.18 all but Steger–Warming reach third order, Steger–Warming second, with density defects where the velocity vanishes, "similar to" the sonic point glitch (Example D.1, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
23. The largest CFL numbers are found experimentally; their reduction comes from the stability of the point-value updates and is "not related to the BP property" (Remarks 2.2, 5.1). **Stated**
24. LLF splitting is "the best among all the three FVS in terms of the CFL number and non-oscillatory property for high-speed flows involving strong discontinuities" (Remark 2.2, Conclusion). **Shown** (on the tests)
25. Forward-facing step at $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/160,1/320$: the method captures the vortices better than $\mathbb P^2$ DG with the TVB limiter on the same mesh, with fewer unknowns; on the double Mach reflection at $1/480$ the roll-ups are comparable to $\mathbb P^2$ DG–TVB, "4 versus 6" unknowns per cell (Examples 5.9, D.7). **Shown**
26. The power-law reconstruction of Barsukow et al. reduces oscillations but remains more oscillatory than the shock-sensor blend and lowers the CFL to 0.1; the authors "do not recommend" it for generalized Active Flux (Example D.3, Fig. 20). **Shown**
27. In an oblique shock reflection the shock-sensor blend ($\kappa_{\mathrm s}=0.5$) speeds up steady-state residual convergence after the reflected shock forms (Example D.5, Fig. 24). **Shown**
28. Mach 80 and Mach 2000 jets ($\gamma=5/3$, $400\times200$ cells) run with both limitings and the sensor ($\kappa_{\mathrm s}=1$, $10$), with results "comparable to" those of Zhang & Shu (Example 5.10, Fig. 16). **Shown**

The van Leer–Hänel splitting is printed as $f^\pm=\big(\pm\tfrac14\rho c(M\pm1)^2,\ \pm\tfrac14\rho c(M\pm1)^2v+p^\pm,\ \pm\tfrac14\rho c(M\pm1)^2H\big)$ with $p^\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma M)p$, without a case distinction for $\lvert M\rvert\ge1$ (§2.2.3).
