# Summary

In [special relativistic hydrodynamics](/definitions/relativistic-hydrodynamics.md)
the primitive variables $(\rho,v,p)$ are implicit functions of the conserved
$u=(D,m,E)$. A physical-constraint-preserving scheme keeps $u$ in the
admissible set $G=\{D>0,\ g(u)=E-\sqrt{D^2+\lvert m\rvert^2}>0\}$. Even for
$u\in G$, though, the root-finding step that recovers the pressure can
diverge or return a negative pressure. The main contribution (§2) is three
Newton–Raphson recoveries, each proved to have only positive iterates and to
converge quadratically to the physical pressure $p(u)$ for every $u\in G$.
NR-I applies Newton to a quartic obtained by squaring the pressure equation,
whose smallest positive root is proved to be $p(u)$, from an initial guess
chosen by a convexity analysis. NR-II applies Newton to $\psi(p)=(E+p)\Phi(p)$
from $0$ or a closed-form guess. A hybrid uses NR-I by default and switches to
NR-II where the quartic is proved ill-conditioned (clustered roots as $D\to0$
or $\gamma\to1$). A closed-form Ferrari root is shown to be costly and
inaccurate. In $10^8$-sample tests the hybrid is fast and accurate, and no
algorithm returns a negative pressure. The recoveries of
[Chen & Wu (2022)](/sources/chen-2022-b.md) converge only linearly.

The application is a [bound-preserving](/properties/bound-preservation.md)
finite-volume [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) scheme in 1D, 2D and
axisymmetric coordinates. It evolves zeroth and first moments, reconstructs
linearly in smooth cells and with HWENO in cells flagged by the KXRCF
indicator, and uses WENO-Z-type weights claimed to have
[scale invariance](/properties/scale-invariance.md). Interface values are
reconstructed in [characteristic variables](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md)
with rescaled ("matching") eigenvectors. A two-stage
[Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) acts at all
quadrature points, with the global
[Lax–Friedrichs flux](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) and SSP-RK3. The fully
discrete scheme is proved to preserve $G$ under $\Delta t\le\Delta x/(12a)$
by a Gauss–Lobatto convex decomposition. The tests show sixth (1D) and fifth
(2D) order on ultra-relativistic smooth data, a shock-heating problem with
$v_0=1-10^{-10}$, [2D Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md),
a near-vacuum [shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md)
and an axisymmetric [relativistic jet](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md).

# Key claims

Registry notation: $u=(D,m,E)$ the conserved variables, $v$ the velocity, $W$
the Lorentz factor, $\gamma\in(1,2]$ the ratio of specific heats, $G$ and $g$
the admissible set and its concave constraint, $a$ the global Lax–Friedrichs
speed, $d_k$, $\beta_k$, $\omega_k$ the HWENO linear weights, indicators and
weights, $\bar u_i$ and $\bar u^{(1)}_i$ the zeroth and first moments (see the
notation map).

1. For $u\in G$ the pressure function $\Phi(p)=\frac{p}{\gamma-1}-E+\frac{\lvert m\rvert^2}{E+p}+D\sqrt{1-\frac{\lvert m\rvert^2}{(E+p)^2}}$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$, with $\Phi(0)<0$ and $\Phi\to+\infty$, so $p(u)$ is its unique positive root (§2.1, Eq. 2.3, after Wu & Tang). **Stated**
2. The quartic $\phi(p)=(\gamma-1)^2[h_1(p)-h_2(p)]=c_0+c_1p+c_2p^2+c_3p^3+p^4$ (Eqs. 2.7–2.8) has $c_0>0$, $c_1<0$ and $c_3\ge0$, with $c_3=0$ iff $\gamma=2$ (Lemma 2.1). **Proved**
3. $\phi$ has two distinct positive roots and either two negative or two complex roots (Theorem 2.1); its smallest positive root is $p(u)$ (Theorem 2.2). **Proved**
4. Newton monotonicity lemmas: from below the root with $f'<0$, $f''\ge0$ (or $f'>0$, $f''\le0$) the iterates increase monotonically to the root; from above with $f'<0$, $f''\le0$ (or $f'>0$, $f''\ge0$) they decrease monotonically to it (Lemmas 2.4–2.5). **Proved** (Lemma 2.5 "similarly", proof omitted)
5. NR-I (Algorithm 2.1) starts from $p_0=0$ if $c_2>0$ and otherwise from the largest root of $\phi''$, $(-3c_3+\sqrt{9c_3^2-24c_2})/12$; it converges monotonically to $p(u)$, has positive iterates (Theorem 2.5) and converges quadratically (Theorem 2.6). **Proved**
6. A closed-form root by Ferrari's method (Algorithm 2.2) is "very costly and sometimes of low accuracy" because of its trigonometric, inverse-trigonometric, cube-root and square-root operations; it takes 367–370 s per $10^8$ samples, with maximum errors up to 2.39 (§2.2, Tables 1–3). **Shown**
7. For $\psi(p)=(E+p)\Phi(p)$, $\psi'>0$ and $\psi'''>0$ on $p\ge0$ and $\psi''\to2/(\gamma-1)$, so the convexity of $\psi$ falls into exactly three cases (§2.3, Eq. 2.18). **Derived**
8. In two of these cases Newton on $\psi=0$ converges from any $p_0\ge0$; in the third it converges from any $p_0\in[0,p(u)]$, can fail from $p_0>p(u)$, and any failure produces a negative iterate (Theorem 2.7). **Proved**
9. Newton on $\psi=0$ from $p_0=0$ is always convergent with positive iterates (Theorem 2.8); if $D<(E^2-\lvert m\rvert^2)/E$ (Eq. 2.19), it is so from any $p_0\ge0$ (Theorem 2.9). **Proved**
10. The guess $p_c^u$ of Eq. (2.16), root of $h_1(p_c^u)=h_2(0)$, satisfies $p(u)<p_c^u<p_b^u<p_a^u$, with $p_b^u$ the positive root of $h_3=\sqrt{h_1}$ and $p_a^u$ the larger positive root of $\phi$ (Lemma 2.6). **Proved**
11. NR-II (Algorithm 2.3) starts from $p_0=0$ if $D\ge(E^2-\lvert m\rvert^2)/E$ and from $p_c^u$ otherwise; it always has positive iterates and converges quadratically (Theorem 2.10). **Proved**
12. The quartic becomes ill-conditioned because its two positive roots cluster: $\lvert p_a^u-p(u)\rvert\to0$ as $D\to0^+$ (Lemma 2.7), and both roots tend to 0 as $\gamma\to1^+$ (Lemma 2.8). **Proved**
13. The hybrid recovery (Algorithm 2.4, Eq. 2.22) uses NR-I if $\gamma\ge1+\epsilon_1$ and $D^2/(E^2-\lvert m\rvert^2)\ge\epsilon_2$, and NR-II otherwise, with $\epsilon_1=0.01$, $\epsilon_2=10^{-4}$; it has positive iterates and converges quadratically (Remark 2.4). **Stated** (from Theorems 2.5, 2.6, 2.10)
14. In $10^8$ random samples per set no algorithm produced a negative pressure; the hybrid is called "the most effective algorithm overall". On set 1 it takes 38.775 s against 120.152 s for Chen & Wu's Hybrid-linear, 62.906 s for Riccardi & Durante's Vel-Proxy, 36.946 s for NR-I and 60.776 s for NR-II, with maximum error 1.15E-09 against 1.81E-04 for NR-I (§5.1, Table 1). **Shown**
15. On the extreme set 2 NR-I loses accuracy and speed (13.1856 iterations on average, maximum error 2.46E-06), while NR-II and the hybrid reach 2.10E-11 in about 3.2 iterations (Table 2). **Shown**
16. On set 3 (large density, small velocity) Hybrid-linear is fastest (33.876 s, 2.07 iterations); the hybrid NR takes 37.629 s (Table 3). **Shown**
17. After the limiter, the point values at the four Gauss–Lobatto points and the interior value $\Pi_i$ lie in $G$ (Lemma 3.2); the proof is "similar to" Chen & Wu (2022) and not given. **Stated**
18. The 1D scheme with the global Lax–Friedrichs flux maps $\bar u_i\in G$ into $G$ under $\Delta t\le\mathsf w_1\Delta x/a$, with $\mathsf w_1=1/12$ the endpoint weight of 4-point Gauss–Lobatto quadrature ($N_{\mathrm{GL}}=4$), by the decomposition $\bar u_i=(1-2\mathsf w_1)\Pi_i+\mathsf w_1u^+_{i-1/2}+\mathsf w_1u^-_{i+1/2}$ and the splitting property $u\pm f(u)/a\in G$ for $a$ at least the spectral radius; SSP Runge–Kutta keeps the property by convexity (Theorem 3.1, Lemma 3.1). **Proved**
19. The 2D analogue holds under $\Delta t\le\mathsf w_1/(a^{(1)}/\Delta x+a^{(2)}/\Delta y)$ (Theorem 4.1, Eq. 4.10). **Proved**
20. In axisymmetric coordinates the scheme with the geometric source is bound-preserving under a combined restriction (Eq. 4.12), built on the source lemma of Wu & Tang (Lemma 4.2); a special splitting parameter turns it into an explicit bound (§4.3). **Derived**
21. The HWENO weights $\alpha_k=d_k\big(1+\tau^2/(\beta_k^2+\epsilon)\big)$, $\tau=\tfrac12(\lvert\beta_0-\beta_1\rvert+\lvert\beta_0-\beta_2\rvert)$, are "scale-invariant": unchanged under $(\bar u,\bar u^{(1)})\to\lambda(\bar u,\bar u^{(1)})$ for any $\lambda\ne0$ (§3.1.2, Eq. 3.8). **Stated** (exact only for $\epsilon=0$: $\tau^2$ and $\beta_k^2$ both scale as $\lambda^4$, while $\epsilon$ does not *(compiler check, not in the sources)*)
22. Unrescaled or "unitized" eigenvectors in the characteristic decomposition give "serious oscillations" on 2D Riemann problem II; the "matching" rescaling $(\mu_{\mathrm r}\mathsf r,\mathsf l/\mu_{\mathrm r})$ of a right and left eigenvector pair $(\mathsf r,\mathsf l)$, with $\mu_{\mathrm r}=\sqrt{\lvert\mathsf l\rvert/\lvert\mathsf r\rvert}$, is best; "unitization" takes $\mu_{\mathrm r}=\lvert\mathsf l\rvert$ (Remark 3.3, Example 5.7, Fig. 7). **Shown**
23. The 1D scheme reaches sixth order on Example 5.2 ($L^1$ orders 6.00, 5.99, 5.97 at $N=120,150,180$), and the limiter does not destroy accuracy (Table 4). **Shown**
24. The 2D scheme reaches fifth order on Example 5.5 ($L^1$ orders 5.35, 5.20, 5.08 at $N=120,150,180$) (Table 5). **Shown**
25. Without the limiter the code breaks down after one time step on the shock-heating problem (Example 5.4) and cannot complete one step on the demanding shock–vortex test (Example 5.8, vortex strength $\mathcal E=10.0828$). **Shown**
26. With the limiter only two cells are limited in the 1D Riemann problem (Example 5.3, Fig. 4) and only a few cells near shocks in the other tests. **Shown**
27. Pointwise bound preservation is needed at every point where a flux is evaluated, inner Gauss–Lobatto points included, because the recovery converges only for $u\in G$; this "differs from the Euler equations" (Remark 3.4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §5.1 says the hybrid "exhibits superior performance in terms of speed, robustness, efficiency, and accuracy across all tests", but Table 3 has Hybrid-linear (33.876 s) and NR-I (35.504 s) faster than the hybrid (37.629 s), and Table 2 has NR-II marginally faster (49.493 s against 49.734 s).
- The tests use "CFL number 0.6" (§5) without defining it, while Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 guarantee bound preservation only for $\Delta t\le\Delta x/(12a)$; if the CFL number is taken with respect to $\Delta x/a$, 0.6 exceeds the proved bound $1/12$ by a factor of 7.2 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §1 dates Harten's TVD concept "1985" while ref. [12] is printed as J. Comput. Phys. 49:357–393, 1983.
- Theorem 2.4, proof of (ii): "$\phi'(0)=a_1<0$"; the coefficient is $c_1$.
- Eq. (3.1): the 1D flux is printed $(Dv_i,\ m_1v_1+p,\ m_1)^\top$, with $Dv_i$ for $Dv_1$.
- §4.1: "$y_{j+b}=y_j+b\Delta x$", and the $y$-moment $\bar W_{i,j}$ is weighted by $(y-y_j)/\Delta x$, where $\Delta y$ is meant; App. A, Eq. (A.2), divides $(y-y_j)$ by $\Delta x$ while (A.1) uses $\Delta y$.
- §4.1, Step 3: $\mu_2=\frac{\lambda_2\alpha_2}{\lambda_1\alpha_1+\lambda_2\alpha_2)}$ has a stray parenthesis.
- §3.1.3, Step 3: $D_{\min}$ is printed with the vector states, $(\bar D_i-\hat\omega_1U^*_{i\oplus(-\frac12)}-\hat\omega_4U^*_{i\oplus\frac12})/(1-2\hat\omega_1)$, where their density components are meant.
- Example 5.8: "a shock at $x=-6$", then "the initial data in $x>6$ … and the post-shock state in $x<6$"; $x>-6$ and $x<-6$ are meant.
- Fig. 6 caption: "within the domain $[0,1]^2$", against $[-0.5,0.5]^2$ in the text and the axes.
- App. A: "the night cells" for nine cells. App. B indexes the polynomials with $r$, which elsewhere is the degree, and gives the coefficient range $s,r=1,2$ where the polynomials run from 0 to 2.
- The values of the linear weights, of $\epsilon$ and of the KXRCF threshold are not printed, and Example 5.4 prints no grid size.
