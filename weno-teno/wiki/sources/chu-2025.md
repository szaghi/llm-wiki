# Summary

Chu et al. (2025a) build second-order semi-discrete finite-volume adaptive
[low-dissipation central-upwind (LDCU) schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-central-upwind-scheme.md)
for the Euler equations in 1D and 2D. The numerical flux is the LDCU flux of
Kurganov & Xin (2023), a [central-upwind](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) flux with
a built-in anti-diffusion term based on the HLL intermediate state. The new part is
the reconstruction of the one-sided values $u^\pm_{i+1/2}$: piecewise linear in local
characteristic variables ([characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md),
as in [Chertock et al. (2023)](/sources/chertock-2023.md)), with slopes limited by the
two-parameter SBM [limiter family](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md) of Lie
& Noelle.

The SBM parameter $\tau_{\mathrm S}$ is chosen cell by cell. Where an indicator marks
the solution as rough, the overcompressive limiter ($\theta_{\mathrm S}=2$,
$\tau_{\mathrm S}=-0.25$) keeps shocks and contacts sharp. Elsewhere the dissipative
one ($\tau_{\mathrm S}=0.5$, the Minmod2 limiter) avoids the kinks, staircases and
spurious jumps that overcompression causes in smooth flow; the trade-off is one of
[numerical dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md). Two
[indicators](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) are tested: a minmod-based
shock indicator modified from Wang et al. (2015) (scheme A-MM), and the weak local residual
of the density equation of Kurganov & Liu (scheme A-WLR), compared with a threshold
$C_{\mathrm W}\Delta x^2$.

Tests (SSP-RK3, CFL 0.4) are a shock–entropy wave, the
[Shu–Osher problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md), the
[Woodward–Colella blast wave](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md),
[2D Riemann](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) Configuration 3, the
Liska–Wendroff [implosion](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md) and the
[Rayleigh–Taylor instability](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md). Both
adaptive schemes resolve more than the plain LDCU scheme with Minmod2 everywhere.
A-WLR can be sharper, but its constant is problem dependent and does not carry from
coarse to fine meshes. The authors judge the minmod indicator robust. Evidence is
visual: no error tables, no timings, no theorem.

# Key claims

Registry notation: cells $I_i$ (source $C_j$), averages $\bar u_i$, one-sided values
$u^\pm_{i+1/2}$, speeds $a^\pm_{i+1/2}$, HLL state $u^{\mathrm{HLL}}_{i+1/2}$
(source $U^*_{j+1/2}$), characteristic variables $\zeta=L_{i+1/2}u$ (source
$\Gamma$), with $R_{i+1/2}$ from the Jacobian at the arithmetic mean of
$\bar u_i,\bar u_{i+1}$. Page-local: $\mathsf q_{i+1/2}$ the LDCU anti-diffusion
term; $\psi^{\mathrm{SBM}}_{\theta_{\mathrm S},\tau_{\mathrm S}}(r)$ the SBM limiter;
$s_i$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{MM}}$ the minmod indicator and its threshold;
$\varepsilon^{\mathrm{WLR}}_{i+1/2}$ the weak local residual, $\bar\varepsilon^{\mathrm{WLR}}$
its smoothed value and $C_{\mathrm W}$ its constant.

1. The adaptive LDCU schemes "contain substantially smaller amount of numerical dissipation and achieve much higher resolution" than LDCU schemes with Minmod2 everywhere (§1, §5, Figs. 4.1–4.10). **Shown**
2. Compressive and overcompressive limiters sharpen discontinuities but give kinks, staircases or non-physical jumps in smooth regions, so limiters must be switched (§1, after Lie & Noelle). **Stated**
3. The LDCU flux is the central-upwind flux $\frac{a^+\,f(u^-)-a^-\,f(u^+)}{a^+-a^-}+\frac{a^+a^-}{a^+-a^-}(u^+-u^-)$ plus $\mathsf q_{i+1/2}$; for 1D Euler $\mathsf q_{i+1/2}=\mathrm{minmod}\big(-a^-(\rho^{\mathrm{HLL}}-\rho^-),\,a^+(\rho^+-\rho^{\mathrm{HLL}})\big)(1,v^{\mathrm{HLL}},\tfrac12(v^{\mathrm{HLL}})^2)^\top$, all at $x_{i+1/2}$ (§2.1, quoting Kurganov & Xin). **Stated**
4. SBM classification: $\tau_{\mathrm S}\ge0.5$ dissipative (contacts smeared in time), $0\le\tau_{\mathrm S}<0.5$ compressive (contacts sharp within a few points, smooth extrema compressed into kinks), $\tau_{\mathrm S}<0$ overcompressive (contacts very sharp, smooth solutions overcompressed with artificial $O(1)$ jumps); larger $\theta_{\mathrm S}\in[1,2]$ is less dissipative but more oscillatory, and $\theta_{\mathrm S}=2$ throughout (§2.1.1). **Stated**
5. SBM with $\theta_{\mathrm S}=2$, $\tau_{\mathrm S}=0.5$ is the Minmod2 limiter $\mathrm{minmod}(2\Delta_-,\Delta_c,2\Delta_+)$ of the characteristic slopes (§2.2). **Derived**
6. The adaptive rule uses SBM with $\tau_{\mathrm S}=-0.25$ in rough cells and Minmod2 elsewhere; in 2D with the minmod indicator the $x$- and $y$-slopes switch separately on the indicator of their own direction (§2.2, §3.2). **Stated** (design)
7. The reconstruction is done in local characteristic variables to reduce the oscillations that either limiter can produce near shocks and contacts (§1, §2.1.1, App. A). **Stated**
8. Minmod indicator: $s_i=\mathrm{minmod}(\bar\rho_{i+1}-\bar\rho_i,\bar\rho_i-\bar\rho_{i-1})$, and $I_i$ is rough if $\lvert s_i\rvert>\max\{\lvert s_{i-1}\rvert,\lvert s_{i+1}\rvert\}+\delta_{\mathrm{MM}}$, $\delta_{\mathrm{MM}}=10^{-4}$ in all examples, to which it is "not very sensitive" (§2.2.1, §3.2.1). **Stated**
9. The weak local residual of the density equation over $[t^{n-2},t^{n-1}]$ (Simpson weights in space, trapezoid in time, Eq. (2.7)) is expected to scale as $\Delta$ near shocks, $\Delta^{\alpha_{\mathrm W}}$ near contacts ($1<\alpha_{\mathrm W}\le2$) and $\Delta^4$ in smooth regions, $\Delta=\max\{\Delta t,\Delta x\}$ (Eq. (2.8), from Kurganov & Liu); a cell is rough if $\bar\varepsilon^{\mathrm{WLR}}>C_{\mathrm W}\Delta x^2$, with $C_{\mathrm W}$ tuned per problem (Eq. (2.9)). **Stated**
10. Which interface value enters (2.7), $u^-$, $u^+$ or their average, makes "no visible advantages or disadvantages" (Remark 2.2). **Stated**
11. Tuning $C_{\mathrm W}$ on a coarse mesh and reusing it on a fine one, the strategy of Kurganov & Liu for adaptive artificial viscosity, is not robust for A-WLR: with $C_{\mathrm W}=0.2$ the solution is smooth at $\Delta x=1/40$ but shows staircases at $1/200$ (Example 2, Fig. 4.3). **Shown**
12. Example 1 (shock–entropy wave, $\Delta x=1/80$, $t=5$): both adaptive schemes resolve the oscillatory region much better than LDCU, A-WLR best (Fig. 4.1). **Shown**
13. Example 2 (Shu–Osher, $\Delta x=1/40$, $t=5$, $C_{\mathrm W}=0.35$): both adaptive schemes beat LDCU; A-MM resolves the smooth parts better than A-WLR, which the authors attribute to the large $C_{\mathrm W}$ (Fig. 4.2). **Shown**
14. Example 3 (blast wave, $\Delta x=1/400$, $t=0.038$, $C_{\mathrm W}=0.1$): A-MM beats LDCU and A-WLR resolves the contact near $x=0.6$ best; A-WLR is, to the authors' knowledge, "the first Riemann-problem solver-free scheme" to resolve it sharply (§4.1, Fig. 4.4). **Shown** (priority **Stated**)
15. Example 4 (Configuration 3, $\Delta x=\Delta y=3/2500$, $t=1$, $C_{\mathrm W}=4$): the adaptive schemes capture the sideband instability of the jet and the instability along its neck better; A-WLR uses the sharp limiter only in a small region, mostly at shocks (Figs. 4.5–4.6). **Shown**
16. Example 5 (implosion, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/2000$, $t=2.5$, $C_{\mathrm W}=5$): the diagonal jet travels much further with the adaptive schemes, A-MM even further than A-WLR, which flags only cells along the shocks; smaller $C_{\mathrm W}$ sharpens A-WLR on coarse meshes but gives "severe instabilities" at $1/2000$ (Figs. 4.7–4.8). **Shown**
17. Example 6 (Rayleigh–Taylor, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/1024$, $C_{\mathrm W}=3$, $t=1.95$ and $2.95$): the adaptive schemes resolve more small structures; across the 2D tests A-MM flags more cells than A-WLR (Figs. 4.9–4.10, §4.2). **Shown**
18. The symmetry of the implosion (about $y=x$) and of the Rayleigh–Taylor flow (about $x=0.125$) is lost to round-off in these low-dissipation schemes and is enforced by averaging mirrored cell values after each step, after Wang et al. (2020) (Remarks 4.1, 4.2). **Stated** (procedure)
19. The weak-local-residual indicator helps in some examples but depends on a hard-to-tune constant; the minmod indicator gives a robust adaption strategy (§5). **Shown**
20. All runs use three-stage third-order SSP-RK at CFL 0.4, with $\gamma=1.4$ in Examples 1–5 and $\gamma=5/3$ in Example 6 (§4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Example 1, shock Mach number** (§4.1, p. 10): the text calls the incident shock "a forward-facing shock wave of Mach number 1.1", with post-shock state $(\rho,v,p)=(1.51695,0.523346,1.805)$ running into $(1,0,1)$, $\gamma=1.4$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the Rankine–Hugoniot relations give $(1.169082,0.188239,1.245)$ for Mach 1.1 and $(1.515695,0.523346,1.805)$ for Mach 1.3. The printed velocity and pressure are those of a Mach 1.3 shock, and the printed density 1.51695 differs from 1.515695 by a dropped digit.)*
- Appendix A, p. 19: "the SBM function, defined in (2.3)"; it is Eq. (2.6), and (2.3) is the 1D equation of state.
- Eq. (2.9) flags the cell $C_j$ through $\bar\varepsilon^{n-3/2}_j$, but $\bar\varepsilon$ is defined only at interfaces $x_{j+1/2}$ (p. 6); the map from interface to cell is not stated. No absolute value is printed although the residual is signed.
- 2D weak local residual (p. 9): the formula uses $\mathcal F^{n-3/2}_{j,k}$ where the quantity defined is $\mathcal F^{n-3/2}_{j+1/2,k+1/2}$. In the bracket with weight 4 of $\mathcal F$ (and of $\mathcal G$) the $t^{n-2}$ terms are printed "$-(\rho u)^{n-2}_{j+3/2,k+1/2}-(\rho u)^{n-2}_{j-1/2,k+1/2}$". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the corner brackets of the same formula and the 1D Eq. (2.7) carry the $t^{n-2}$ flux difference as $+(\cdot)_{j+3/2}-(\cdot)_{j-1/2}$, so the printed signs do not form a flux difference at $t^{n-2}$.)*
- Eq. (2.6) (SBM limiter): the cases are $r<0$, $0<r\le1$ and "otherwise" with $r\,\phi(1/r)$, so $r=0$ falls in the third case, where $1/r$ is undefined.
- Remark 4.2: the mirror cell of $j=1,\dots,M$ is written $M-j$; $M$ is not defined, and with $M$ cells the mirror of $j$ would be $M+1-j$.
- "Examples 2 in §4" (p. 7); "the point values of the computed solution $\bar U_j(t)$" for cell averages (§2.1, p. 3); "contact discontinues" (p. 18).
- References: "A. Kuganov" for Kurganov in [23] and [24]; [24] prints "P. Noelle" where [30] prints "S. Noelle"; "B. Wendrof" and "diference" in [31]; "Classifcation" in [36]; "fow" in [38]; ref. [39] (Shu, Acta Numer.) printed with volume 5 for 2020 (the bibliography records volume 29, which [Chertock et al. (2023)](/sources/chertock-2023.md) also prints as 5); ref.
