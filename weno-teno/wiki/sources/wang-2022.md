# Summary

The paper treats rigid, geometrically complex walls on Cartesian grids for
the two-dimensional Euler equations with a sharp-interface ghost-cell
[immersed-boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md),
implemented in the block-structured [AMR](/methods/adaptive-mesh-refinement.md)
framework [AMROC](/software/amroc.md); the wall is a stationary signed-distance
[level set](/methods/level-set-method.md). The baseline AMROC method reflects
each ghost point across the wall to a mirror point at the same normal distance,
interpolates the fluid state there bilinearly and reflects the normal (and, for
no-slip, the tangential) velocity. When the ghost cell lies close to the wall
the mirror point's four-node stencil contains solid nodes ("incomplete bilinear
interpolation"), and accuracy drops.

The contribution is a bilinearly complete reconstruction: an image point at
normal distance $\delta=\sqrt2\,\Delta x$ from the wall, where a complete
bilinear stencil exists, a second extrapolation point a further $r_E$ along the
normal, and a quadratic Taylor expansion along the normal that gives the mirror
value for Neumann variables and for the normal velocity (Dirichlet
$v_n=0$). Applied to every ghost cell this is BCGCM; the hybrid HBCGCM keeps
the baseline method wherever the mirror stencil is complete. The interior
solver is second-order [MUSCL–Hancock](/methods/muscl-hancock.md) with a
primitive-variable Riemann solver (PVRS), and an
[HLL](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) solver for the reactive case.

Evidence is an algebraic unit test (observed local orders about 3 for pure
extrapolation, about 2 with interpolated inputs) and Euler tests: a stationary
rotating vortex inside a circular wall, Prandtl–Meyer expansions over straight
and curved walls, a ramp-variant [double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md),
Schardin's moving-shock/triangle problem and blunt-body
[shock-induced combustion](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md). HBCGCM
gives the smallest errors among the ghost-cell variants and an observed
boundary-region entropy order about 1.35, costs little more than the baseline and
less than a cut-cell method in AMReX. The "second order" of the title is a
formal local Taylor remainder; the paper proves no global convergence
result. The paper has
no WENO or TENO content; it enters the bundle under immersed boundaries.

# Key claims

Registry notation: velocity components are $v_1,v_2$ ($v_n$, $v_t$ normal and
tangential), the sound speed is $c$, the primitive-variable vector of the
ghost-cell reconstruction is written $\mathsf q$ (page-local), and the
geometric distances $r_G$, $r_E$, $\delta$ are kept from the source.

1. The baseline AMROC ghost-cell method (mirror point at $r_M=r_G$, bilinear interpolation, Eqs. 16–18) yields an incomplete bilinear interpolation when the ghost cell is close to the wall, which "significantly reduces" the interpolation accuracy (§2.3.1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
2. An image point at normal distance $\delta=\sqrt2\,\Delta x$ from the wall is surrounded by four fluid cell centres in 2D; a larger, unrestricted $\delta$ produces severe extrapolation errors (§2.3.1, after Chi et al.). **Stated**
3. The mirror value follows from the image, projection and extrapolation values by a quadratic Taylor expansion along the normal (Eq. 23, remainder Eq. 24); for Neumann variables it reduces to Eq. 30, for the normal velocity with $v_n\vert_p=0$ to Eq. 32 (§2.3.1). **Derived**
4. The error of the improved reconstruction is $R\propto o\big((\delta+r_E)^2\big)$ (from Eq. 31), so its accuracy depends on the distance of the extrapolation points from the wall; the paper calls the second order "theoretically proven" (abstract, §2.3.2). **Derived** (formal Taylor argument, no theorem)
5. The improved reconstruction "requires no assumption regarding flow properties", unlike the Chi et al. method, whose simplified model assumptions introduce uncertainty (§2.3.1, §2.3.2). **Stated**
6. Pure-extrapolation unit test (exact image and extrapolation values): the improved method is exact to rounding for the linear function 1; mean observed orders 2.97 against 1.92 (linear extrapolation) for the quartic function 2 and 3.02 against 1.96 for the trigonometric function 3; at least one order of magnitude better than the Chi and linear methods (§3.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
7. In the same test the Chi method is worse than linear extrapolation in all cases, because the test functions violate its assumptions (§3.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
8. Complete-interpolation unit test (image and extrapolation values bilinearly interpolated): mean orders 2.02 against 1.09 for function 2 and 2.11 against 0.96 for function 3 (improved against baseline; see Printed slips) (§3.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. At grid size of order $10^0$ the baseline beats the improved method for function 2, because the image and extrapolation points lie where the quartic varies sharply (§3.1, Fig. 5b). **Shown**
10. Scanning the mirror-point position (grid size 0.01), the improved method beats the baseline at most positions but is slightly worse when the mirror point is close to a fluid node, where the incomplete-interpolation error (Eqs. 37–38) vanishes (§3.1, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
11. Stationary rotating vortex, case 1 (wall at the vortex edge, $R_G=R$): HBCGCM has the smallest mass loss and errors among the ghost-cell methods; the $L_1$ gap closes with refinement (§3.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
12. Stationary rotating vortex, case 2 ($R_G=R/2$, wall at maximum shear velocity): HBCGCM has the lowest mass loss and the lowest $L_1$ and (on fine grids) $L_\infty$ errors among the ghost-cell methods, but its advantage over BCGCM is "not fully realized" in $L_2$ (§3.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. The cut-cell method has smaller mass loss than every ghost-cell method because it is conservative, but a worse mass-loss convergence rate than HBCGCM; the paper reads this as "HBCGCM has higher convergence than CCM" (§3.2, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
14. Prandtl–Meyer expansion over a straight wall (deflections 20°, 30°, 40°, 50°; grids $30^2$–$600^2$): observed boundary-region $L_2$ entropy orders 1.25, 1.13, 1.17, 1.16 (baseline), 1.25, 1.21, 1.20, 1.16 (BCGCM) and 1.36, 1.37, 1.35, 1.33 (HBCGCM), against about 1.14 reported by Pember et al. (§3.3, Table 1). **Shown**
15. BCGCM's improvement over the baseline in the straight-wall Prandtl–Meyer case is at most 37% (§3.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
16. Curved-wall Prandtl–Meyer case ($N_x=150$): HBCGCM gives the lowest relative Mach error at every deflection; BCGCM gains "about 5 to 100 times" over the baseline at small deflections and is comparable at large ones; boundary-region $L_2$ entropy errors are $1.01\times10^{-4}$ (baseline), $8.78\times10^{-5}$ (BCGCM) and $2.32\times10^{-5}$ (HBCGCM) (§3.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
17. Double Mach reflection off a 36° ramp: all four methods place the discontinuities identically on the $6000\times4800$ grid and differ in the wall jet; the improved method gives smaller-scale vortices on the slip line and jet and a smoother distribution than the cut-cell method (§3.4, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
18. Density errors on $1500\times1200$ against the baseline $6000\times4800$ reference: HBCGCM has the lowest $L_1$, $L_2$ and $L_\infty$ errors, total and in the boundary region, of all four methods ($L_1$ $2.3\times10^{-4}$ / $5.0\times10^{-4}$, $L_2$ 0.0020 / 0.0018, $L_\infty$ 0.072 / 0.044; baseline $7.2\times10^{-4}$ / 0.0030, 0.0049 / 0.011, 0.12 / 0.10; BCGCM $2.5\times10^{-4}$ / $6.2\times10^{-4}$, 0.0021 / 0.0024, 0.074 / 0.047; cut-cell $4.2\times10^{-4}$ / 0.0021, 0.0040 / 0.010, 0.25 / 0.25) (§3.4, Table 2). **Shown**
19. It is "generally thought" that the numerical scheme influences the [Kelvin–Helmholtz](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) roll-up on the slip line of the double Mach reflection more than the immersed-boundary method (§3.4). **Stated**
20. Schardin's problem (shock $M=1.34$ on a triangular obstacle): HBCGCM schlieren agree with Chang & Chang's experiment, and the triple-point and vortex-centre trajectories agree with experiment, ray-shock theory and Boukharfane et al.'s simulations (§3.5, Figs. 16–18). **Shown**
21. Blunt-body shock-induced combustion: pressure-oscillation frequency 707.2 kHz (baseline) and 714.8 kHz (HBCGCM) against 725 kHz measured by Lehr, errors 2.5% and 1.4%; only HBCGCM reproduces the alternating entropy/compression-wave structure between bow shock and combustion front (§3.6, Fig. 19). **Shown**
22. Single-core cost: the cut-cell method is much more expensive than any ghost-cell method and less accurate than HBCGCM; HBCGCM costs negligibly more than the baseline and less than BCGCM, because not every ghost cell needs the extra extrapolation (§3.7, Fig. 20; no timings printed in the text). **Shown**
23. The extension to 3D (trilinear interpolation along the normal) is "straightforward" and in progress, as is the coupling with AMR (§4). **Stated**
24. Cut-cell methods are strictly conservative but suffer from the small-cell problem: an excessive time-step restriction for explicit and ill-conditioning for implicit schemes (§1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (23): the coefficient of the projection value is printed $\mathsf q_p\,r_E(r_G-\delta)(r_E-r_G+\delta)$; quadratic Lagrange interpolation through the three normal positions of Eqs. (19)–(21) gives $r_E(\delta-r_G)(r_E-r_G+\delta)$. For $r_G=0$ the printed coefficient is $-1$ instead of $+1$, and the printed coefficients sum to $1-2c_p$ instead of 1. Eq. (24) carries the same sign pattern. Eqs. (30) and (32) are unaffected. *(compiler check, not in the sources: Lagrange coefficient of the node $-r_G$ evaluated at 0.)*
- Eq. (41), vortex pressure: printed bracket $2r^2/R^2+1-\ln2$ ($r<R/2$) and $r^2/R^2+3-4r/R+\ln(r/R)$ ($R/2\le r\le R$). Integrating $dp/dr=\rho U^2/r$ with Eq. (40), $\rho_0=1$ and $p(R)=p_0$ gives $r^2/R^2+1-2\ln2$ and $r^2/R^2-4r/R+3+2\ln(r/R)$; the printed profile fails the radial balance on both pieces and is discontinuous at $r=R/2$ (0.807 against 0.557). *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- §3.4: the post-shock velocity is printed $u_{n,s}=1566.2$ m/s behind the $M=4.5$ shock; $1566.2=4.5\,c_0$ is the shock speed. The Rankine–Hugoniot gas velocity is $\frac{2c_0}{\gamma+1}(M-1/M)\approx1240.7$ m/s; the printed $\rho_s$ and $P_s$ are consistent with $M=4.5$. *(compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- Eq. (44), curved Prandtl–Meyer wall: the pieces cover $x\le0$, $0\le x\le0.6$ and $x\ge0.7$, leaving $(0.6,0.7)$ undefined, and the slope jumps from $+0.36$ (the derivative of $0.3x^2$ at $x=0.6$) to $-0.36$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §2.3.1: "based on the Cheng et al. stencil"; no Cheng is in the reference list, and the stencil is attributed elsewhere to Pan & Shen and Chi et al.
- §3.1: "the mean orders of the improved method and linear method are 2.02 and 1.09" (also 2.11 and 0.96) in the complete-interpolation test, where the comparison is with the baseline method.
- §2.3.2 repeats "The accuracy of the improved GCM is dependent on the distance between the extrapolation points and the boundary." twice in a row.
- Eq. (3): $E=p/(\gamma-1)+\tfrac12\rho u^2$ omits $v^2$ in the 2D system.
- Eq. (37): first denominator $\Delta x$ where the weight $(\Delta y-\Delta k)/\Delta y$ of the same three-node interpolation needs $\Delta y$ (immaterial for $\Delta x=\Delta y$) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §3.2: the vortex wall is a "reflective slip boundary condition" and, two paragraphs later, a "radially symmetric no-slip boundary"; by Eq. (40) the vortex velocity at $R_G=R/2$ (case 2) is $\alpha\ne0$, so the two conditions differ there *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Remainders of second-order Taylor expansions are written $o(h^2)$; for a smooth function the remainder is $O(h^3)$, which is also what the pure-extrapolation unit test measures (orders 2.97 and 3.02) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Captions: Figs. 9–10 "Comparison of three methods" over four curves; Fig. 14 "Counters of density"; Fig. 18 "$b=h\cdot\tan(\pi/3)$ is the edge length" of an equilateral triangle of height $h$, whose side is $2h/\sqrt3$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
