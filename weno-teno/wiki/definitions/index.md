# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Approximate dispersion relation](/definitions/approximate-dispersion-relation.md) — The modified-wavenumber characterization of a possibly nonlinear spatial scheme: its sign convention for dissipation, the Hu-type dispersion-to-dissipation ratio, its fully discrete Runge–Kutta form and its critique, optimization on the nonlinear ADR, and what the sources read off it for WENO, TENO, THINC, ROUND, MDCD, WCNS, gradient-based, hybrid and multi-resolution schemes and central schemes with artificial dissipation.
* [Boundary variation diminishing (BVD)](/definitions/boundary-variation-diminishing.md) — BVD is the selection principle that picks, cell by cell, the candidate reconstruction (polynomial versus THINC) with the smallest jump of interface values, hence the least Riemann-flux dissipation; the page records its definitions, an interface-wise variant, a compact DG-limiter form, a relaxed troubled-element test and a neural-network predictor of the selection.
* [Carbuncle phenomenon](/definitions/carbuncle-phenomenon.md) — The carbuncle is a numerical instability that low-dissipation Riemann solvers such as HLLC, HLLEM and Roe develop near strong shocks; linear analyses trace it to undamped transverse perturbations and, at fifth order, to the reconstruction inside the numerical shock structure, which destabilizes even HLL; the remedies are HLL, flux splitting, multidimensional fluxes, extra dissipation or shock fitting.
* [Conservative finite-difference scheme](/definitions/conservative-finite-difference-scheme.md) — A conservative finite-difference scheme evolves point values by differences of numerical fluxes at half points, du_i/dt = −(f̂_{i+1/2} − f̂_{i−1/2})/Δx, with the flux built so that the difference approximates ∂_x f to high order: by reconstructing split fluxes, interpolating states with corrections, central fluxes with high-order dissipation, implied fluxes of compact schemes, or in fluctuation form.
* [Ducros shock sensor](/definitions/ducros-shock-sensor.md) — The Ducros sensor flags compressions by the share of dilatation in the velocity-gradient magnitude; the page gives the classical squared ratio, the modified compression-only form that switches hybrid central/TENO and central/WENO schemes, its use as a weight towards a first-order flux (a WENO-Z blend, an exponential Active Flux limiter), its regularization and thresholds, and its recorded failures and repairs.
* [ENO property](/definitions/eno-property.md) — A reconstruction has the ENO property when, near a discontinuity, it effectively draws only on candidate stencils that do not cross it, so the solution is captured essentially non-oscillatorily; for weighted schemes this means the weights of discontinuous stencils vanish asymptotically, a condition that a too-large or absolute ε can silently defeat.
* [Five- and six-equation diffuse-interface models](/definitions/five-equation-model.md) — Single-velocity diffuse-interface models of compressible two-phase flow: the five-equation model (two partial densities, mixture momentum and energy, a non-conservative volume-fraction equation, one pressure) in its Kapila and Allaire forms, and the six-equation pressure-disequilibrium model with instantaneous relaxation; their sound speeds, discretization of the non-conservative term, interface equilibrium and admissible states.
* [Godunov–Powell source term](/definitions/godunov-powell-source-term.md) — The non-conservative term proportional to div B that Godunov and Powell add to ideal MHD: it makes the system symmetrizable by the entropy variables and lets bound-preserving and entropy-stable schemes tolerate divergence errors. The page gives the term, its entropy structure, its interface-jump discretizations and its cost in conservation.
* [Method of manufactured solutions](/definitions/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md) — Code verification by a chosen smooth solution whose source term is added to the solver, the observed order being compared with the design order; the sources apply it to a full solver on mapped adaptive meshes, a steady shock-capturing solver, curved-boundary conditions on unfitted meshes, a semi-discrete operator, a gradient reconstruction, and viscous or curvilinear DG and FR schemes.
* [Multicomponent Euler equations](/definitions/multicomponent-euler-equations.md) — The fully conservative Euler system for a mixture of several gases or stiffened fluids in thermal and mechanical equilibrium, written with partial densities, mass fractions or molar concentrations, and its reacting Navier–Stokes extension; its closures, admissible set, wave structure and mixture entropy, and the entropy-stable, positive and bound-preserving schemes built on it, with their behaviour at material interfaces.
* [Normalised-variable diagram](/definitions/normalised-variable-diagram.md) — The normalised-variable diagram plots the normalised interface value of a three-cell compact reconstruction against the normalised cell average; Deng's unified version (UND), extended to non-uniform grids, adds the accuracy point, the third-order line, diffusion and anti-diffusion regions, a CFL-dependent CBC-TVD region and ENO and high-resolution regions, on which TVD, NVD, WENO3, THINC, ROUND and learned schemes are compared.
* [Numerical dissipation and anti-dissipation](/definitions/numerical-dissipation.md) — Numerical dissipation is the part of a scheme's error that damps resolved content, anti-dissipation the part that amplifies or steepens it; the page fixes three measures (flux viscosity term, leading truncation error, imaginary part of the modified wavenumber), the design levers of the sources, including scale-adaptive control, the non-dissipative alternative, and the implicit-LES evidence on dissipation order.
* [Rankine–Hugoniot jump condition](/definitions/rankine-hugoniot-condition.md) — The Rankine–Hugoniot condition ties the speed of a discontinuity to the jumps of state and flux across it; in this bundle it is the interface condition that a moving space–time DG method enforces to fit shocks and contacts, and, componentwise and regularized, the source of sharper one-sided speeds in central-upwind fluxes.
* [Scale sensors and the effective scaled wavenumber](/definitions/scale-sensor.md) — A scale sensor estimates the local reduced wavenumber of the data, exactly for a sinusoid, so that dissipation, the TENO cut-off or the linear scheme can follow the resolved scale; the page gives the six-point sensors and their phase-independent form, the five-point TENO5-A sensor, a spectral-difference shock detector and a bounded second-derivative sensor.
* [Shallow-water equations](/definitions/shallow-water-equations.md) — The one- and two-layer, one- and two-dimensional shallow-water (Saint-Venant) equations with bottom topography and friction, their energy entropy and Lagrangian form, and their steady states — lake at rest, constant energy, and the invariants $q$ and global flux $K$ that survive hydraulic jumps — which decide what well-balancing must preserve.
* [Six-equation two-phase model](/definitions/six-equation-model.md) — The single-velocity pressure-disequilibrium model of compressible two-phase flow: two partial densities, mixture momentum, two phasic energies and a volume fraction relaxed toward pressure equilibrium. With infinite relaxation it recovers the Kapila five-equation model while keeping a monotone frozen sound speed; the page gives its forms, relaxation solvers, phase-change and surface-tension extensions.
* [Smoothness indicators](/definitions/smoothness-indicator.md) — A smoothness indicator β_k is a quadratic functional of the data on candidate stencil S_k, O(Δx²) on smooth data and O(1) across a jump; the Jiang–Shu form underlies WENO, TENO, CWENO and Hermite-WENO weights, with variants for one-point bases, L¹-type and symmetric forms, Hermite data, multi-dimensional and time stencils, global indicators and pairwise tests; the term also names unrelated sensors.
* [Special relativistic hydrodynamics and MHD](/definitions/relativistic-hydrodynamics.md) — The special relativistic (magneto)hydrodynamics equations in lab-frame conservation form, whose primitives and fluxes are implicit functions of the conserved variables; the page fixes the variables, general equations of state, the implicit recovery equations and their solvers, the convex admissible set and its GQL form, homogeneity, wave speeds, entropy and symmetrization, and extensions.
* [Stiffened-gas equation of state](/definitions/stiffened-gas-equation-of-state.md) — The stiffened-gas law, an ideal gas shifted by a constant stiffening pressure, which models liquids in compressible multiphase solvers: its pressure, energy and temperature forms, its pressure–temperature form with entropy and Gibbs function, sound speed, mixture rules, the convexity of its admissible set, its relation to the Tait law and the parameter values the sources use.
* [Total variation diminishing (TVD) and slope limiters](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md) — TVD reconstruction limits the interface value of a second-order scheme by a limiter of the slope ratio inside Sweby's region; the page gives the incremental-form TVD conditions for explicit and implicit Euler steps, the limiter formulas of the sources, their normalised-variable reading, the first-order barrier at extrema, and the roles of limiters in WENO, central and DG schemes.
* [Troubled-cell and shock indicators](/definitions/troubled-cell-indicator.md) — A troubled-cell (shock, discontinuity) indicator is a cheap local functional of the discrete solution that decides where a high-order scheme is limited, blended or switched; the page defines modal, KXRCF-type, TVB, minmod-jump, BVD, admissibility, time-reversibility, residual, WENO- and TENO-type, undivided-difference, derivative-ratio, divergence and physical (discontinuity-feedback) indicators, with parameters and limits.
