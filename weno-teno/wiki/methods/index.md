# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Active Flux](/methods/active-flux.md) — Active Flux evolves cell averages conservatively with fluxes from point values shared at cell interfaces, giving a globally continuous reconstruction without Riemann solvers; the page covers arbitrary order in 1D and on 2D Cartesian grids, flux-vector-split point updates, bound-preserving limiting, adaptive refinement and bicharacteristic operators for systems.
* [Adaptive moving mesh finite differences](/methods/adaptive-moving-mesh.md) — An r-adaptive scheme moves a fixed number of grid points, by variational redistribution with a monitor function, a prescribed mesh Jacobian or an optimal-transport Monge–Ampère map, and discretizes the conservation law on the moving or adapted mesh; the sources make it entropy stable, fast or shock-resolving and measure its cost against uniform meshes.
* [Adaptive TENO (TENO-A, TENO-AA)](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) — Adaptive TENO varies the TENO cut-off locally through a discontinuity measure, a wavenumber scale sensor, a global smoothness factor or a jump-variance indicator (TENO-A, TENO-LAD, TENO-AZ, TENO-E), relays band-wise linear schemes and cut-offs (TENO6-SA) and, in TENO-AA, tests nested large stencils largest-first; the page gives formulas, parameters, orders and evidence.
* [Adaptive-diffusion central-upwind Rankine–Hugoniot flux](/methods/adaptive-diffusion-central-upwind-flux.md) — Wang, Don, Kurganov and Liu tighten the one-sided speeds of the anti-diffused central-upwind flux with regularized discrete Rankine–Hugoniot speeds, replace them by fluid-velocity speeds where a relative-jump detector flags smoothness, and damp acoustic speeds near contacts in 2D; used inside fifth-order A-WENO.
* [ADER schemes](/methods/ader-scheme.md) — ADER schemes advance a DG or finite-volume solution in one step: an element-local space–time predictor, solved by fixed-point iteration without neighbour data, feeds a corrector that integrates fluxes in time by quadrature; the page gives the two ADER-DG formulations, their time-step limits, a posteriori subcell limiting, the uses of the predictor and GRP-based finite-volume ADER to tenth order.
* [AENO and AENO-C reconstruction](/methods/aeno-reconstruction.md) — AENO averages, coefficient by coefficient, the Taylor coefficients of two candidate polynomials of one degree through a joining function of their magnitude ratio; AENO-C pairs the ENO polynomial with the centred one and uses a symmetric exponential joining function, giving design order to 10 in ADER finite volumes at two-stencil cost.
* [Affine-invariant WENO weights](/methods/affine-invariant-weno.md) — Wang & Don's Ai-WENO weights multiply the sensitivity parameter ε by the squared mean absolute deviation of the stencil data, which makes the nonlinear weights, hence the WENO operator, commute with every affine map f ↦ σf+μ; this restores the ENO property for small-amplitude offset data and gives intrinsic well-balancing for shallow water.
* [AFTENO — alternative-formulation TENO](/methods/afteno.md) — AFTENO places a TENO interpolation of point values inside the alternative finite-difference flux formulation, a monotone two-point flux on interpolated states plus even-derivative corrections, so that any Riemann solver and any variable set can be used at arbitrary odd or even order; this page gives its stencils, linear weights, correction coefficients, tested fifth-order performance and cost.
* [Alternative WENO (A-WENO)](/methods/alternative-weno.md) — The alternative formulation of finite-difference WENO evaluates a two-point finite-volume flux on WENO-interpolated interface states and adds a Taylor series of even-order flux derivatives, so that any monotone, Riemann-solver or central-upwind flux and any set of variables can be used while keeping the high order of the conservative finite-difference scheme.
* [APEC flux for approximate pressure equilibrium](/methods/apec-scheme.md) — APEC is a fully conservative kinetic-energy-preserving central flux for multi-species and real-fluid Euler equations whose internal-energy half-point value adds a correction to the arithmetic mean, so pressure equilibrium holds to second order for any equation of state; the page gives the derivation, error analysis, PEqC variant and tests.
* [Artificial compression](/methods/artificial-compression.md) — Artificial compression adds a limited anti-diffusive correction in linearly degenerate fields so that contact discontinuities stop spreading; the page gives Harten's switch in the modified-flux TVD scheme, Yang's compression in the point-value form Jiang and Shu attach to WENO, their measured costs and effects, and the alternatives the reviews list.
* [Artificial viscosity and spectral viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md) — Artificial viscosity adds solution-dependent dissipation instead of overwriting the solution; the page covers the high-order Jameson–Schmidt–Turkel blend, graph viscosity, Laplacian DG viscosities driven by a time-reversibility defect, a learned modal decay or a time-evolved SPH-type switch, residual-based finite-element viscosity, spectral and quasi-spectral eddy viscosities, C-function viscosity on moving meshes, a posteriori cellwise viscosity, and modal damping read as dissipation.
* [Bassi–Rebay viscous discretizations (BR1, BR2)](/methods/bassi-rebay-viscous-discretization.md) — BR1 and BR2 discretize the viscous terms of DG and flux-reconstruction schemes with lifted gradients: BR1 with a global lifting and central interface averages, BR2 with face-local liftings and a penalty factor; entropy-variable gradients and an extra projection make them entropy stable, but neither guarantees positivity.
* [Block-structured adaptive mesh refinement](/methods/adaptive-mesh-refinement.md) — Hierarchical h-refinement of Cartesian or mapped meshes in nested patches or fixed-size blocks (AMROC, PARAMESH, ForestClaw, ADAM, AthenaK, AMReX): refinement criteria, coarse–fine transfer from second-order to WENO, divergence-preserving and Active Flux transfers, refluxing and its conservation analysis, sub-cycling with and without time interpolation, and measured savings and accuracy against uniform meshes.
* [Cauchy–Kowalewski procedure](/methods/cauchy-kowalewski-procedure.md) — The Cauchy–Kowalewski (Lax–Wendroff) procedure expands the solution in time and replaces every time derivative by spatial derivatives through repeated use of the PDE; the page gives Harten et al.'s ENO flux built on it, its Euler form, its role in generalized Riemann problems and ADER, its cost at high order, and the case made for Runge–Kutta instead.
* [Central WENO (CWENO)](/methods/cweno.md) — Central WENO schemes subtract weighted low-order candidates from one central high-order polynomial and recombine them nonlinearly, so the linear weights may be any positive numbers summing to one and the result is a full polynomial on the cell; the page gives CWENO3, unstructured CWENO, WENO-ZQ and its scale-invariant form on triangles, CWENOZ as a DG limiter, predictor-based weights and costs.
* [Central-upwind schemes](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) — Central-upwind fluxes use only one-sided local speeds $a^\pm_{i+1/2}$ and no Riemann solver; the flux is algebraically the HLL form, it reduces to upwind only when all eigenvalues share a sign, and its dissipation is reduced by built-in anti-diffusion, characteristic-wise diffusion, Rankine–Hugoniot speeds or smoothness switches.
* [Characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) — Local characteristic decomposition projects stencil data onto the eigenvectors of the flux Jacobian frozen at one state, usually an interface average, reconstructs each field separately and projects back; WENO, TENO and central-upwind schemes use it against oscillations, multiphase sources project primitive variables at material interfaces, entropy-stable schemes scale the eigenvectors, and some schemes do without it.
* [Conservative phase-field interface methods](/methods/phase-field-method.md) — A phase-field flux added to the volume-fraction equation of a diffuse-interface model, and carried consistently into the mass, momentum and energy equations, holds material interfaces at a fixed thickness against numerical diffusion; the page records Huang and Johnsen's N-phase model, its admissibility constraints, and their reduction-consistent, bound-preserving discretization.
* [Conservative remapping for ALE schemes](/methods/conservative-remapping.md) — Conservative remapping transfers cell averages from a Lagrangian mesh to another mesh; the page records van Leer's 1979 least-squares remap of a Lagrangean step back to the Eulerian grid in MUSCL, and Gu et al.'s ALE remap as a moving-mesh solve of a trivial equation with third-order multi-resolution WENO and proved positivity.
* [Conservative semi-Lagrangian finite-volume WENO](/methods/semi-lagrangian-finite-volume.md) — A semi-Lagrangian finite-volume scheme updates each cell average as the integral of the old solution over its upstream cell; Zheng et al. reach fourth order in 2-D without dimensional splitting by cubic-curved upstream cells, a WENO-ZQ polynomial reconstruction and Green's-theorem clipping, with mass conservation, positivity and large CFL numbers.
* [Constrained transport for the induction equation](/methods/constrained-transport.md) — Constrained transport stores face averages of the normal magnetic field and updates them with edge averages of the electric field by a discrete Stokes theorem, so the face-based discrete divergence is constant to round-off; the page gives the update, high-order face-to-cell interpolation, multidimensional and upwind edge fields, implicit variants and their limits.
* [Converged ENO (CENO)](/methods/converged-eno.md) — Converged ENO is a TENO-type reconstruction for steady flows whose stencil flags remember the previous step through a hysteretic cut-off and are frozen when the global indicator falls below the mesh width, with a prioritized fifth-order or third-order choice, so that steady residuals can fall to machine zero.
* [Convex limiting and invariant-domain-preserving schemes](/methods/convex-limiting.md) — Convex limiting blends a high-order update with a bound-preserving low-order update, adding as much antidiffusive flux as target bounds allow; the page gives the FCT form, graph viscosity and bar states, sparse low-order DG operators, Zalesak and convex limiters, element- versus subcell-wise blending, bound relaxation, subcell Rusanov finite volumes as sparse IDP DG, and semi-discrete monolithic convex limiting.
* [COS(DG): convex oscillation-suppressing DG](/methods/cos-dg.md) — COS(DG) replaces each DG cell polynomial, once per time step after the last Runge–Kutta stage, by a convex combination of itself and its cell average with an exponential factor driven by entropy-induced distances to the neighbours; it conserves, is locally scale- and evolution-invariant, L2-nonexpansive, entropy non-increasing and accuracy-preserving.
* [Deferred correction (DeC) time integration](/methods/deferred-correction.md) — Deferred correction reaches a high-order implicit collocation-type time discretization by a fixed number of iterations of a cheap low-order operator; the page records the two-operator iteration, its contraction estimate, the explicit kinetic variant whose stiff source stays implicit but is computed explicitly, its linear stability, and the fully discrete entropy-conservative residual-distribution use.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) — The discontinuous Galerkin (DG) semi-discretization of a conservation law with piecewise polynomials of degree N coupled only through numerical fluxes, its modal, nodal, space–time and central (overlapping-mesh) variants, its Runge–Kutta time stepping and CFL limit, the cell-average update behind bound preservation, and the stabilization and adaptation strategies the sources attach to it.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method (DGSEM)](/methods/dgsem.md) — The DGSEM is nodal collocation DG on tensor-product Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto nodes with a diagonal mass matrix and an SBP derivative matrix; the page gives its strong, weak and split (flux-differencing) forms, the curvilinear metric treatment, non-conservative and viscous terms, the subcell flux form used for limiting, positivity of its cell averages, and the codes that implement it.
* [Discrete equations method (DEM)](/methods/discrete-equations-method.md) — The discrete equations method builds a multiphase Godunov scheme for the non-equilibrium Baer–Nunziato model from pairwise single- and two-phase Riemann problems in flow channels, with the non-conservative terms given by Lagrangian fluxes across contact waves; the page records the effective-cell-size time-step limit of its high-order extension and Paula et al.'s RDEMIC, which removes it.
* [Discrete kinetic relaxation schemes](/methods/discrete-kinetic-relaxation-scheme.md) — A discrete kinetic (Jin–Xin, BGK-type) scheme replaces a system of conservation laws by a linear transport system with diagonal velocities relaxing to a Maxwellian; combined with deferred correction it is arbitrarily high order, asymptotic preserving and computationally explicit, because the projection on the conserved variables annihilates the stiff source, and it runs at CFL numbers near one.
* [Dissipation-based WENO stabilization of continuous finite elements](/methods/weno-stabilized-continuous-galerkin.md) — The CG-WENO method of Kuzmin and Vedral uses a Hermite WENO reconstruction only to measure how far a continuous finite element solution is from smooth, and turns that measure into a cellwise blend of high-order projection stabilization and low-order artificial viscosity; the solution is never overwritten, and the stabilization is O(h^(N+1/2)) consistent for smooth solutions.
* [Divergence-preserving prolongation and reconstruction for AMR](/methods/divergence-preserving-prolongation.md) — Prolongation of a face-collocated vector field with a divergence constraint onto newly refined zones of an adaptive mesh, so that every fine zone satisfies the constraint to round-off; the page gives Balsara et al.'s analytically exact fourth-order reconstruction, the WENO-ADP face interpolation with its least-squares touch-up (WENO-ADPT), the counts of the constrained problem, and the verification.
* [Double-flux method](/methods/double-flux-method.md) — The double-flux method removes the spurious pressure oscillations of conservative schemes at multicomponent material interfaces by computing each interface flux twice, once with the frozen mixture properties of each neighbouring cell; the page records Abgrall and Karni's algorithm as embedded in a weighted compact scheme, and the overspecification and conservation objections raised against it.
* [ENO reconstruction (essentially non-oscillatory schemes)](/methods/eno-reconstruction.md) — ENO schemes build, in each cell or at each interface, one interpolating polynomial on an adaptive stencil grown point by point towards the side with the smaller divided difference, so that the stencil avoids discontinuities; the page gives the cell-average (Harten et al.) and point-value flux (Shu–Osher) constructions, their accuracy notions, systems, cost and the WENO successors.
* [Entropy correction terms](/methods/entropy-correction-term.md) — An entropy correction term adds to each element of a residual-distribution, DG or SBP scheme a perturbation that keeps local conservation and enforces linear constraints such as entropy conservation, entropy dissipation or kinetic-energy preservation: in Abgrall's form the smallest one proportional to the mean-free entropy variables, in modal DG also Laplacian-like or entropy-viscosity spreadings.
* [Entropy filtering for DG and FR](/methods/entropy-filtering.md) — Entropy filtering applies, after every Runge–Kutta stage, an elementwise exponential modal filter whose strength is the smallest making the nodal values positive and above a local entropy minimum, assuming the element mean already is; the page gives the filter, constraints, floors and tolerances, algorithm, Navier–Stokes split, ideal-MHD split, faster strength solve and multi-species pressure-sensor switch.
* [Entropy projection](/methods/entropy-projection.md) — Entropy projection evaluates the nonlinear terms of a DG or FR scheme at conservative states mapped back from the L2 projection or interpolation of the entropy variables; it lets Tadmor's condition close the entropy balance on uncollocated or Gauss nodes, takes 4–14 % of the run time in Ranocha et al.'s tests, and cannot be built for kinetic-energy variables.
* [Entropy-conservative and kinetic-energy-preserving two-point fluxes](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md) — Two-point fluxes satisfying Tadmor's jump condition conserve entropy, and entropy-dissipative ones satisfy it as an inequality; the page states the condition in Cartesian, directional, moving-mesh and non-conservative form and gives the Ranocha, Shima, asymptotically entropy-conservative algebraic, multicomponent, vibrating-air, stiffened-gas, GLM-MHD, relativistic and scalar fluxes and their entropy-stable dissipation.
* [Exact Riemann solver](/methods/exact-riemann-solver.md) — The exact Riemann solver resolves a jump between two states into its waves by solving the nonlinear wave relations for the resolved pressure; the page gives van Leer's pressure equation, acoustic start, Godunov's chord and Bedijn's tangent iterations with the cavitation floor, the scalar Osher formula, and where later sources use or avoid it.
* [Exponential nonlinear weights (WCNS-EXP)](/methods/exponential-nonlinear-weights.md) — Chen and Deng's exponential nonlinear weights d_k exp(-β_k^λ/λ), with a stencil-local λ that tends to 1 on smooth data and to 10⁻⁶ at discontinuities, keep fifth-order interpolation at critical points of any order using local data only; the page gives the construction, the overflow-free ratio form, tests and the scale dependence.
* [Fluctuation-form finite-difference WENO for non-conservative systems](/methods/fluctuation-form-fd-weno.md) — Balsara, Bhoriya, Shu and Kumar write point-value FD-WENO as an interior quasilinear term plus HLL or HLLI fluctuations at zone boundaries; it equals Lax–Friedrichs FD-WENO for linear systems, treats non-conservative products, preserves stationary linearly degenerate jumps and needs one reconstruction per zone.
* [Flux differencing and split-form DG](/methods/flux-differencing.md) — Flux differencing replaces the DG volume term D f by symmetric two-point volume fluxes weighted by 2D; on diagonal-norm SBP elements and finite-difference grids it recovers split forms, inherits entropy or kinetic-energy properties from the volume flux, reaches high order through two-point flux combinations, and costs about N+1 times the strong form's flux evaluations.
* [Flux globalization for balance laws](/methods/flux-globalization.md) — Flux globalization absorbs the integral of the source or non-conservative product into a global flux $K$, so that steady states, moving and discontinuous ones included, are states of constant $K$; Kurganov et al. build a well-balanced path-conservative central-upwind scheme on it, Ciallella et al. an arbitrary-order WENO finite-volume scheme, Chu et al. (2025b) a variant with field-wise characteristic diffusion.
* [Flux reconstruction and energy-stable FR](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md) — Flux reconstruction corrects the discontinuous interpolated flux of each element with correction functions that carry the interface flux jumps, recovering nodal DG for one choice; energy-stable FR is a DG scheme filtered by a one-parameter correction operator, and Cicchino et al. make its split form nonlinearly stable, up to 3D Euler on curvilinear grids, by filtering the volume terms too.
* [Flux vector splitting](/methods/flux-vector-splitting.md) — Flux vector splitting writes the flux as a sum of parts whose Jacobians have non-negative and non-positive eigenvalues, so each part is upwinded on its own; the page gives the Lax–Friedrichs, Steger–Warming and van Leer–Hänel splittings, their use in Active Flux and finite differences, and the advective–pressure splittings of all-Mach schemes.
* [Force-free splitting for relativistic MHD](/methods/force-free-splitting-rmhd.md) — Komissarov & Phillips split the electromagnetic field of ideal relativistic MHD into a force-free component and a plasma-inertia perturbation, integrate both conservation subsystems together and recombine them every step, with a thresholded energy transfer, so that conversion to primitive variables survives magnetizations far beyond those of unsplit schemes.
* [Gas-kinetic schemes](/methods/gas-kinetic-scheme.md) — Gas-kinetic schemes take the interface flux, and its time derivative, from moments of a BGK solution instead of a Riemann solver; the bundle's sources build a compact high-order scheme on it that evolves cell-averaged gradients with a nonlinearly limited two-stage fourth-order step, a discrete-velocity variant coupling convective and viscous fluxes on unstructured grids, and a robustness-switched fifth-order scheme.
* [Generalized Riemann problem (GRP) solvers](/methods/generalized-riemann-problem.md) — A generalized Riemann problem has piecewise-linear (or piecewise-smooth) data across a jump; its solution gives the interface state and its time derivative, hence a one-step second-order flux; van Leer's 1979 MUSCL computes such derivatives from characteristic equations, Liu, Feng and Wang carry the HLL-GRP solver to multidimensional ideal MHD, and the Toro–Titarev solver reaches arbitrary order through the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya procedure.
* [Geometric quasilinearization (GQL)](/methods/geometric-quasilinearization.md) — Geometric quasilinearization rewrites every nonlinear constraint of a convex admissible set as a family of constraints affine in the conservative variables, indexed by free auxiliary variables; the page gives the representation theorems, the three construction methods, the explicit representations and the bound-preservation proofs built on them.
* [GLM divergence cleaning](/methods/glm-divergence-cleaning.md) — Generalized-Lagrange-multiplier cleaning adds a scalar field coupled to the magnetic-field divergence, so that divergence errors propagate away at a finite speed and are damped instead of accumulating; the page gives the augmented system, the cleaning speeds and damping rates the sources choose, its conservative and non-conservative forms, and its comparison with constrained transport and projection.
* [Godunov scheme](/methods/godunov-scheme.md) — Godunov's first-order scheme replaces the data by cell averages, solves the Riemann problem at every interface exactly for one step and averages back; the page gives the reconstruct–evolve–average form, the scalar Godunov flux, its Roe replacement, van Leer's Lagrangean reading, and the evidence that second-order extensions beat it.
* [Gradient reconstruction on unstructured meshes](/methods/unstructured-gradient-reconstruction.md) — Cell-centred finite volumes on unstructured meshes reconstruct a cell gradient, or a higher-degree polynomial, from neighbouring averages; this page records Green–Gauss and least-squares gradients, the vertex-based VWLSQ, boundary treatments up to the ghost-cell-constrained WLSQ-GCC, face-gradient decoupling, a WENO-type weighting replacing the limiter, GPU face loops, the constrained least-squares polynomials of high-order WENO, TENO and hybrid DG–FV, and RBF-quadrature derivatives.
* [Gradient-based reconstruction (MEG/MIG schemes)](/methods/gradient-based-reconstruction.md) — Gradient-based reconstruction interpolates point values to the interfaces of a conservative finite-difference scheme from the cell-centre value and high-order first and second derivatives, the first derivatives being shared with the viscous fluxes; it is fourth order (MEG6, MIG4) or seventh order (MEG7) for linear problems, MP-limited at shocks, and needs flux reconstruction for nonlinear order.
* [h- and hp-adaptation for DG](/methods/hp-adaptation.md) — Adaptive DG refines the mesh (h) or raises the polynomial degree (p) where an estimator is large: Basile et al.'s hp loop with a modal-decay smoothness test on shock-free flows, Bai and Li's quadtree h-refinement, Mossier et al.'s p-adaptation with finite-volume subcells on a static mesh, Kou et al.'s static p-refinement, and Abgrall et al.'s entropy-stable degree transfer.
* [Harten's high-resolution TVD scheme](/methods/harten-tvd-scheme.md) — Harten's 1983 scheme applies a first-order total-variation-nonincreasing three-point scheme to a modified flux whose minmod-limited correction cancels the leading viscous error, giving a five-point second-order scheme that keeps the original CFL bound; the page gives the scalar construction, its lemmas, the characteristic extension to systems, the choices of viscosity, and the Euler tests.
* [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) — Hermite WENO (HWENO) builds WENO candidates from cell averages plus derivative or first-moment data on compact stencils; the page covers moment-based finite-volume HWENO with moment modification, unified stencils (HWENO-U), positivity preservation and the OE-HWENO filter, Hermite candidates for spectral-difference limiting, finite-difference HWENO-R, and HWENO as a finite-element sensor.
* [HLL-family approximate Riemann solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) — HLL, HLLE, HLLC, HLLEM/HLLI, HLLD and relaxation two-point fluxes as the bundle's sources use them: the single-state HLL form and its non-conservative and anti-diffusive extensions, HLLC star states and wave-speed estimates for single- and multi-component and curvilinear flows, positivity proofs, the entropy-stable relaxation solver, radiative and relativistic resistive-MHD HLL, carbuncle behaviour, dissipation on turbulence, and solver switching.
* [Hybrid central-WENO schemes](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) — Hybrid schemes use a cheap linear (central, energy-preserving or optimal upwind) reconstruction where a sensor declares the stencil smooth and a nonlinear WENO or TENO reconstruction elsewhere, or switch between flux and state reconstruction; they cut dissipation and cost, restore order or add robustness by a first-order fallback, but hinge on the sensor and its threshold.
* [IMEX Runge–Kutta schemes](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) — Implicit–explicit Runge–Kutta schemes pair an explicit tableau for the non-stiff terms with a diagonally implicit one for the stiff terms; the page records the A/CK/ARS types, stiff accuracy, the partitioned semi-implicit form, the SI-IMEX(4,4,3) and (3,3,2) tableaux and their order check, and additive schemes with stiff local sources solved cell by cell, and a fourth-order split with implicit point-value stages.
* [Immersed-boundary and cut-cell methods](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) — Treatments of walls and interfaces off the mesh: ghost-cell immersed boundaries and the bilinearly complete reconstruction, positivity-preserving convex interpolation with adaptive TENO, cut cells and the small-cell problem, volume penalization with flux reconstruction, image lines, ghost points with a wall model, signed-distance extrapolation on adaptive meshes, cut against ghost cells at cold walls, and true-boundary reconstruction for embedded DG.
* [Implicit shock tracking and moving DG (MDG-ICE)](/methods/implicit-shock-tracking.md) — Implicit shock tracking makes the mesh geometry an unknown and moves element faces onto discontinuities, by weakly enforcing the flux-jump condition (MDG-ICE; the MDG+ICE variant of Luo, Absillis and Nourgaliev) or by minimizing an enriched DG residual constrained by the DG equations (HOIST, made robust by Huang and Zahr, run slab by slab in space–time by Naudet and Zahr).
* [Incremental-stencil WENO](/methods/incremental-stencil-weno.md) — Incremental-stencil WENO (WENO-IS, WENO5IS) of Wang, Xiang and Hu combines two two-point and two three-point candidates into the five-point value with linear weights 15/32, 5/32, 5/16, 1/16, using WENO-Z-type weights with the TENO reference indicator; it serves as WCNS interpolation and as a multiphase base scheme.
* [Lax–Friedrichs (Rusanov) flux and flux splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) — The Lax–Friedrichs two-point flux and the Lax–Friedrichs flux splitting $f^\pm=\tfrac12(f\pm au)$, global or local, per field or scalar: the default upwinding of finite-difference WENO and TENO, the surface flux of DG, the low-order flux whose bar-state structure makes invariant-domain and bound-preserving proofs work, its positivity-preserving extension to the total Navier–Stokes flux, and its entropy-stable variant on entropy-variable jumps.
* [LCD central-upwind scheme](/methods/lcd-central-upwind-scheme.md) — Chertock et al. replace the scalar diffusion coefficients of the central-upwind flux by field-wise ones built from the one-sided characteristic speeds, in a local characteristic decomposition; the scheme becomes exactly upwind for every linear system and resolves contacts and instabilities more sharply; Chu et al. (2025b) extend it to non-conservative balance laws through flux globalization.
* [Learned WENO weights](/methods/learned-weno-weights.md) — Learned WENO weights replace the smoothness-indicator formula of a WENO-type scheme by a trained network that outputs the nonlinear weights; the page describes WENO3-NN, the five-point WENO5-NN, WCNS3-MR-NN, the constrained DSP-WENO, learned MUSCL slopes and scheme selection, a learned entropy-stable flux, the ROUND operators fitted to WENO3-NN, and a learned DG smoothness indicator.
* [Level-set and ghost-fluid interface methods](/methods/level-set-method.md) — Methods that locate a material interface or rigid wall by the zero contour of a signed-distance level set: transport, reinitialization, ghost-fluid methods and their loss of conservation, a conservative sharp-interface finite-difference scheme with cell merging, a DG cut-cell scheme with real–ghost mixing, a conservative cut-cell finite-volume method extended to liquid–vapour phase change, and level-set–phase-field coupling.
* [Low-dissipation central-upwind schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-central-upwind-scheme.md) — The low-dissipation central-upwind (LDCU) flux adds to the central-upwind flux an anti-diffusion built from the HLL intermediate state; Chu et al. (2025a) make it adaptive by switching a characteristic-wise SBM slope limiter with a roughness indicator, and Chu et al. (2025c) move the subcell jump with the contact to remove spurious oscillations.
* [Mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md) — Mapped WENO (WENO-M, Henrick, Aslam & Powers) passes the Jiang–Shu weights through a mapping that fixes the linear weights, restoring the fifth-order condition at first-order critical points but not where f'' also vanishes, at a higher CPU time than WENO-JS and WENO-Z in Borges et al.'s timings; another source maps second-order TVD weights for a third-order WENO.
* [Matrix stability analysis of shock-capturing schemes](/methods/matrix-stability-analysis.md) — Matrix stability analysis linearizes a semi-discrete shock-capturing scheme about a steady shocked state, assembles the stability matrix of the perturbations and declares the scheme stable when no eigenvalue has positive real part; the page gives the first-order and fifth-order WENO versions, their validation, and the saw-tooth analysis used beside it.
* [Minimized-dispersion and controllable-dissipation (MDCD) schemes](/methods/mdcd-scheme.md) — MDCD schemes are six-point fourth-order finite-difference fluxes whose dispersion and dissipation are set by two parameters; Li, Chen and Ren make the dissipation follow a scale sensor (MDAD), weight it like WENO-CU6 and hybridize it, Li et al. tune both parameters on the fully discrete scheme (ADADF), and Zhou et al. carry the same two parameters into a WCNS interpolation.
* [Monotonicity-preserving (MP) limiter](/methods/monotonicity-preserving-limiter.md) — The Suresh–Huynh monotonicity-preserving limiter keeps a high-order interface value unless it leaves a monotonicity bound and otherwise clips it into an interval built from median, upper-limit and large-curvature values; the page records its form in the gradient-based MEG/MIG schemes, the gradient-based curvature that replaces the three-point one, and MP3/MP5 convergence in a relativistic code.
* [MOOD a posteriori limiting](/methods/mood-a-posteriori-limiting.md) — MOOD-type limiting computes a candidate high-order update, checks it for admissibility (positivity, NaN, maximum principle), and recomputes only flagged cells with a robust low-order scheme; the page records a kinetic scheme, deferred correction, a posteriori subcell limiters of ADER-DG and unstructured DG, artificial-viscosity recomputation, PAD and NAD used a priori, and the a priori contrast.
* [Multi-resolution WENO](/methods/multi-resolution-weno.md) — Multi-resolution WENO combines polynomials on nested central stencils, down to a one-point base, with any positive linear weights; the page records the Zhu–Shu hierarchy to ninth order, ghost candidates, the Wang et al. variant, scale-invariant MR-AWENO, WCNS, learned, shock-detector (MR-WENO-SD), hybrid, moving-mesh, Hermite, entropy-stable and steady-state uses.
* [MUSCL and MUSCL–Hancock schemes](/methods/muscl-hancock.md) — MUSCL was named in 1979 for Woodward's code of van Leer's second-order sequel to Godunov's method, a Lagrangean step with stored limited slopes and characteristic interface derivatives followed by a remap; later sources use the name for limited piecewise-linear reconstruction in a κ family and for the MUSCL–Hancock predictor scheme that the bundle's AMR, immersed-boundary and multiphase solvers build on.
* [Negative linear weights](/methods/negative-linear-weights.md) — When accuracy on the full stencil fixes the linear weights of a WENO combination, some can be negative or fail to exist, and the combination stops being convex; the page records where this happens (integration, Gauss points, unstructured and semi-Lagrangian stencils, spectral optimisation) and the remedies the sources use or cite.
* [Operator splitting for stiff source terms](/methods/operator-splitting.md) — Operator splitting advances transport and a stiff term in separate steps: chemistry as a constant-volume reactor per cell, two-phase relaxation as infinite-rate projections, bubble sources by Strang splitting, DG damping as an implicit stage; a Dirac-delta source is never well-balanced; the founding TVD and MUSCL papers split space directions, the ENO paper rejects that above second order.
* [Optimal cell-average decomposition](/methods/optimal-cell-average-decomposition.md) — The cell-average decomposition writes a cell average as a positive quadrature that includes the interface points and sets the bound-preserving CFL number of Zhang–Shu schemes; the page states the optimization problem, the proofs that the Gauss–Lobatto decomposition is optimal in 1D and for Q^N, and the optimal and quasi-optimal decompositions for P^N.
* [Oscillation-eliminating discontinuous Galerkin (OEDG)](/methods/oedg.md) — OEDG follows every Runge–Kutta stage of a DG scheme with an exactly solved damping step that multiplies each modal coefficient by a solution-dependent exponential factor, scale- and evolution-invariant and conserving cell averages; the page covers OE-HWENO, bound-preserving two-phase OEDG, locally divergence-free OEDG for MHD and the entropy-stable oscillation-free DG relative.
* [PAMPA scheme](/methods/pampa-scheme.md) — PAMPA (Point-Average-Moment PolynomiAl-interpreted) is an Active-Flux-type method that evolves cell averages conservatively and interface point values by a non-conservative residual form; Abgrall et al. make its third-order 1D version bound preserving by blending fluxes and residuals with low-order ones, with closed-form coefficients and GQL positivity.
* [Path-conservative schemes](/methods/path-conservative-scheme.md) — Path-conservative schemes discretize a non-conservative product $\mathcal C(u)\partial_xu$ by integrating it along a path between interface states and over each cell; the bundle's central-upwind (PCCU) and HLL/HLLI forms use a straight-line path in $u$ or in equilibrium variables, reduce to conservative fluxes when $\mathcal C=0$, and also evolve conservation laws in primitive variables.
* [Piecewise parabolic method (PPM)](/methods/piecewise-parabolic-method.md) — The piecewise parabolic method reconstructs face values from cell averages with limited parabolas. In this bundle it is a reconstruction option of two GPU finite-volume codes, applied to primitive variables in two limiter families; the page records its observed orders on linear waves, its dissipation and its loss of convergence on MHD waves.
* [Polynomial viscosity matrix (PVM) Riemann solver](/methods/polynomial-viscosity-matrix-solver.md) — PVM solvers write the numerical flux as a central average minus a viscosity matrix applied to the interface jump and replace the Roe matrix |A| by a polynomial in A that approximates the absolute value; evaluated by Horner's rule with finite-difference Jacobian–vector products they need neither eigenvectors nor separate wave-speed estimates per subsystem.
* [Positivity-preserving flux limiter (Hu–Adams–Shu)](/methods/positivity-preserving-flux-limiter.md) — A positivity-preserving flux limiter blends, interface by interface, a high-order flux with a first-order admissible flux so that the forward-Euler update stays admissible; the page gives Wong et al.'s HLLC construction, the Hu–Adams–Shu form at every Runge–Kutta stage, a lower-bound Active Flux variant, Xiong–Qiu–Xu's parametrized limiter and the schemes relying on it.
* [Positivity-preserving numerical fluxes for Navier–Stokes](/methods/positivity-preserving-navier-stokes-flux.md) — Zhang's Lax–Friedrichs-type Navier–Stokes flux, with a coefficient that includes viscous stress and heat flux, keeps density and internal energy positive; the page gives its total-flux finite-volume, finite-difference-splitting and LDG forms, Ching et al.'s multicomponent form on the viscous flux alone, graph-viscosity use in nodal DG, CFL conditions, adaptive time stepping and evidence.
* [Quasi-spectral viscosity](/methods/quasi-spectral-viscosity.md) — Quasi-spectral viscosity (QSV) is one explicit eddy/artificial viscosity for high-order compact finite differences that serves both as subfilter model in LES and as shock capturing: filter residuals estimate the energy at the grid cutoff and impose a plateau-cusp wavenumber shape on the dissipation; the page gives the closure, its constants and the evidence.
* [Quinpi — implicit CWENO time stepping](/methods/quinpi.md) — Quinpi is an implicit finite-volume framework that freezes CWENO weights on a first-order implicit predictor, integrates with a diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta method, limits in time by a CWENO-type blend with the predictor and restores conservation by an interface correction; its scalar third-order version Q3P1 converges at third order for time steps of one and ten cell widths.
* [RBF differential-quadrature finite volumes](/methods/rbf-differential-quadrature.md) — The implicit RBF differential-quadrature finite-volume method (IRBFDQ-FV) of Liu et al. evolves cell-centre point values with a cubic Taylor polynomial whose derivatives come from meshless multiquadric RBF quadrature, so a non-diagonal mass operator makes it implicit; it uses Roe or gas-kinetic fluxes and a PEEB scaling limiter on unstructured grids.
* [RBF-based WENO](/methods/rbf-weno.md) — RBF-based WENO and TENO replace polynomial candidates by radial-basis-function interpolants: Jeong, Yang and Yoon's WENO-G fits the Gaussian shape parameter to reach sixth order on five points with RBF ideal weights; Liang and Fu's Gaussian-RBF TENO selects one target stencil by TENO flags, needs no linear weights and adapts the shape parameter to the local scale.
* [Riemann solvers with phase change](/methods/phase-change-riemann-solver.md) — The liquid–vapour Riemann problem with phase change has four waves and jump conditions with latent heat and surface tension, closed by a mass-flux model; Long et al. approximate it by a four-wave HLL-type solver reduced to a scalar iteration on the mass flux from the states adjacent to the phase interface, the interfacial flux of a fully conservative cut-cell scheme.
* [Roe approximate Riemann solver](/methods/roe-riemann-solver.md) — Roe's solver upwinds every characteristic field of a linearized flux Jacobian at the Roe-averaged state; in the bundle's sources it is the least diffusive option of finite-volume codes, the default flux of several WENO and TENO tests, used with a Harten–Hyman-type entropy fix, prone to the carbuncle, and, in a Lagrangian acoustic form, a particle-pair solver.
* [ROUND schemes](/methods/round-scheme.md) — ROUND schemes are three-cell compact finite-volume reconstructions written directly as functions of the normalised cell average, hence scale-invariant, with dissipation, TVD and ENO behaviour placed by construction; the page gives the dissipative, low-dissipative, fine-tuned, learned-weight-inspired and non-uniform-grid forms, their DG-subcell use, cost, evidence and order caveat.
* [Semi-implicit all-Mach schemes](/methods/semi-implicit-all-mach-scheme.md) — Semi-implicit all-Mach schemes split the Euler or MHD equations into explicitly treated material waves and implicitly treated acoustic (and, in one MHD scheme, magnetic) waves, so the time step follows the flow speed; the page compares splittings by hyperbolicity, asymptotic consistency and contact preservation, and implicit parts solved by one linear pressure equation or Picard iteration.
* [Shock-capturing boundary closure for multi-block finite differences](/methods/shock-capturing-boundary-closure.md) — Qin, Chen, Lin and Deng close a fifth-order staggered WCNS at block boundaries with nonlinear multi-resolution interpolation on one-sided nested stencils built on 'conservative solution points', so that the multi-block scheme is globally conservative, fifth order and shock-capturing up to the boundary, and slow shocks cross block interfaces.
* [Sign-preserving WENO](/methods/sign-preserving-weno.md) — Sign-preserving WENO reconstructions (SP-WENO, SP-WENOc, DSP-WENO) are third-order four-point WENO forms whose weight perturbations keep the sign property that TeCNO entropy-stable dissipation needs; the page gives the form, the sign constraint, the zero-jump problem at shocks, and the learned feasible-region variant.
* [Spectral difference method](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md) — The spectral difference method is a nodal high-order discontinuous scheme in differential form: a degree-N solution polynomial on solution points, a degree-(N+1) flux polynomial on staggered flux points with common interface fluxes, and flux derivatives at the solution points; sources limit it with TENO/WENO Hermite reconstructions or entropy filters.
* [Split convective forms](/methods/split-convective-forms.md) — Split forms write the convective terms of mass and momentum as weighted mixtures of divergence and advective forms; the page gives the Coppola–Veldman family and conditions, Edoh's rotational forms, De Michele and Coppola's flux form and energy splittings, the Burgers split of DG and FR, an optimized eight-point cubic split in a TENO hybrid, and face and two-point fluxes.
* [SSP Runge–Kutta time stepping](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) — Strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta methods are convex combinations of forward-Euler steps, so bound-preserving, invariant-domain and limiter proofs need only forward Euler; the three-stage third-order Shu–Osher scheme, mostly at CFL numbers 0.3 to 0.6, is the default stepper of the bundle, and the page records how sources size time steps and state the positivity CFL.
* [Subcell finite-volume limiting for DG](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-limiting.md) — Subcell finite-volume limiting makes a nodal DGSEM robust by convexly blending it, per element or per subcell interface, with a compatible low-order finite-volume scheme on the LGL subcells; the page gives the flux-differencing form, the four mix-and-match ingredients, the entropy-stable blending lemma, semi-discrete monolithic convex limiting, switching on equidistant or Gauss-weight subcells, Gauss-node blending, and measured effects.
* [Targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md) — TENO builds a K-point reconstruction from candidate stencils of incremental width, separates smooth from non-smooth stencils by a strongly scale-separated measure and a sharp cut-off, and combines the surviving stencils with their renormalized linear weights, so that smooth regions recover the background linear scheme exactly; this page gives the basic recipe, its parameters, variants and recorded disagreements.
* [TeCNO schemes — entropy-stable reconstruction with the sign property](/methods/tecno-scheme.md) — A TeCNO-type flux adds to an entropy-conservative flux a dissipation acting on jumps of reconstructed scaled entropy variables; it is entropy stable whenever the reconstruction has the sign property, which minmod and ENO have by construction and WENO reconstructions get from a componentwise sign switch or from constrained weights.
* [TENO-THINC](/methods/teno-thinc.md) — TENO-THINC uses TENO5 everywhere and replaces its interface flux by a tanh jump flux in cells that a parameter-free detector, built from the binary TENO stencil flags, marks as discontinuous; the page gives the original detector, Liang et al.'s improved detector and two-steepness flux, a TENO-THINC limiter for DG, the evidence and the cost.
* [Tensor-train WENO](/methods/tensor-train-weno.md) — Tensor-train WENO evolves the fields of a three-dimensional finite-difference WENO5-JS Euler solver in low-rank tensor-train format, with shift operators for differences, cross interpolation for the nonlinear flux and weight steps, rounded SSP-RK3 and a mesh-dependent truncation tolerance; it keeps fifth order and is fast on low-rank flows.
* [THINC reconstruction](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md) — THINC reconstructs a cell's data by a hyperbolic-tangent jump profile fitted to the cell average and bounded by the neighbouring values; this page gives its finite-volume, finite-difference and normalised closed forms, the role of the steepness parameter (diffusive, nearly second order, anti-diffusive), a genuinely multi-dimensional form with time averaging for multi-stage Runge–Kutta, and how it is hybridized with polynomial reconstructions.
* [Unstaggered central schemes](/methods/unstaggered-central-scheme.md) — Unstaggered central schemes evolve a piecewise-linear solution on a single grid without Riemann solvers by passing through implicit staggered dual cells — forward projection, Nessyahu–Tadmor predictor–corrector on the dual cells, back projection — and, in the source of this bundle, carry a deviation-based well-balancing and a constrained-transport correction for ideal MHD with gravity.
* [Wall-modelled large-eddy simulation](/methods/wall-modeled-les.md) — Equilibrium wall-modelled LES solves thin-boundary-layer ODEs between the wall and an exchange point and imposes the modelled wall stress and heat flux through an effective wall viscosity and conductivity, keeping no-slip; De Vanna et al. show that shock-capturing dissipation decides whether wall turbulence survives in it, and couple it with immersed boundaries.
* [Weighted compact nonlinear schemes (WCNS)](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md) — WCNS are finite-difference shock-capturing schemes in three steps — nonlinear node-to-interface interpolation, an interface flux, and high-order interface-to-node differencing; the page records explicit, TENO-weighted, spectrally optimized adaptive-dissipation, exponential-weight, multi-resolution, learned, hybrid and mapped members, and an open disagreement on conservation.
* [WENO limiters for discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/weno-limiter-dg.md) — A WENO limiter replaces the DG polynomial of a troubled cell by a nonlinear, cell-average-preserving combination of polynomials from the cell and its neighbours; the page gives the simple WENO limiter, a direction-wise variant, a same-order CWENOZ replacement on mixed meshes, a TENO-weighted compact limiter, a TENO-THINC limiter with a compact BVD switch, and critiques.
* [WENO-AO (adaptive-order WENO)](/methods/weno-ao.md) — WENO-AO nonlinearly hybridizes one large central high-order stencil with the three small stencils of third-order WENO; the sources raise the power of its global indicator for orders 7 and 9, measure the cost of its four sub-stencils, use it in compact gas-kinetic schemes and expose its loss of robustness when all stencils are non-smooth.
* [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md) — WENO-CU6 (Hu et al.) is a sixth-order central-upwind WENO that adds a downwind three-point stencil to the fifth-order set, uses a six-point global smoothness indicator of order Δx⁶ and a WENO-Z-type weight with C = 20, q = 1 as recalled by Fu et al. (2016); in the bundle it is the main low-dissipation comparator of the TENO papers.
* [WENO-JS reconstruction](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) — The Jiang–Shu weighted ENO reconstruction: a convex combination of r-point candidate polynomials with weights d_k/(β_k+ε)^q, giving order 2r−1 in smooth regions and ENO behaviour at discontinuities; the baseline every later weight design modifies.
* [WENO-Z weights](/methods/weno-z.md) — WENO-Z replaces the Jiang–Shu weights by α_k = d_k[1+(τ/(β_k+ε))^q], with a global smoothness indicator τ built from the local β_k; the weights approach the linear ones faster in smooth regions, and the less-smooth stencil keeps more weight than in WENO-JS.
* [WENO-Z+ and WENO-Z+M](/methods/weno-z-plus.md) — WENO-Z+ adds to the WENO-Z weight a term proportional to (β_k+ε)/(τ_5+ε) that raises the weight of the less-smooth stencil; Luo & Wu's WENO-Z+M takes its square root and an adaptive coefficient, removing the negative dissipation that WENO-Z+ shows in its approximate dispersion relation.
* [WENO-ZN schemes](/methods/weno-zn.md) — WENO-ZN weights replace the WENO-Z global indicator by the square of the (2r−2)th undivided difference over the whole stencil, of order Δx^(4r−4), and the constant 1 by an adaptive function of the two extreme indicators; at order 2r−1 they keep the design order up to (2r−4)th-order critical points.
* [WLS-ENO reconstruction](/methods/wls-eno.md) — WLS-ENO is a single-stencil finite-volume reconstruction: a weighted least-squares fit whose row weights fall where neighbouring averages jump; Liu et al. make it conservative with a zero-mean basis and locally divergence-free for the magnetic field, and run it in second- and third-order ideal-MHD schemes, with a Zhang–Shu or Barth–Jespersen limiter and an HLL or generalized-Riemann-problem flux.
* [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) — The Zhang–Shu limiter scales a high-order polynomial linearly toward its cell average until the point values of a cell-average decomposition lie in the admissible set; it conserves the cell average and, with a BP first-order flux and a CFL bound, makes the update bound-preserving; a one-parameter blend toward a low-order update reuses its algebra.
* [α-damping viscous flux discretization](/methods/alpha-damping-viscous-flux.md) — The α-damping viscous flux computes the interface gradient as the average of two cell-centre gradients plus a damping jump of extrapolated states; with sixth-order explicit or fourth-order compact cell gradients and damping coefficient 4 it is a fourth-order conservative viscous discretization free of odd–even decoupling, whose gradients are shared with the inviscid reconstruction.
