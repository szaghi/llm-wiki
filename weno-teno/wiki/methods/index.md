# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Active Flux](/methods/active-flux.md) — Active Flux evolves cell averages conservatively with fluxes from point values shared at cell interfaces, giving a globally continuous reconstruction without Riemann solvers; Abgrall and Barsukow extend it to arbitrary order through larger stencils, extra point values or extra moments.
* [Adaptive TENO (TENO-A, TENO-AA)](/methods/adaptive-teno.md) — Adaptive TENO varies the TENO cut-off locally through a low-order-undivided-difference discontinuity measure (TENO-A, adaptive dissipation) and, in TENO-AA, also tests nested symmetric large stencils largest-first so that the accuracy order drops recursively near discontinuities; this page gives the formulas, parameters, orders and evidence of both.
* [Adaptive-diffusion central-upwind Rankine–Hugoniot flux](/methods/adaptive-diffusion-central-upwind-flux.md) — Wang, Don, Kurganov and Liu tighten the one-sided speeds of the anti-diffused central-upwind flux with regularized discrete Rankine–Hugoniot speeds, replace them by fluid-velocity speeds where a relative-jump detector flags smoothness, and damp acoustic speeds near contacts in 2D; used inside fifth-order A-WENO.
* [Affine-invariant WENO weights](/methods/affine-invariant-weno.md) — Wang & Don's Ai-WENO weights multiply the sensitivity parameter ε by the squared mean absolute deviation of the stencil data, which makes the nonlinear weights, hence the WENO operator, commute with every affine map f ↦ σf+μ; this restores the ENO property for small-amplitude offset data and gives intrinsic well-balancing for shallow water.
* [AFTENO — alternative-formulation TENO](/methods/afteno.md) — AFTENO places a TENO interpolation of point values inside the alternative finite-difference flux formulation, a monotone two-point flux on interpolated states plus even-derivative corrections, so that any Riemann solver and any variable set can be used at arbitrary odd or even order; this page gives its stencils, linear weights, correction coefficients, tested fifth-order performance and cost.
* [Alternative WENO (A-WENO)](/methods/alternative-weno.md) — The alternative formulation of finite-difference WENO evaluates a two-point finite-volume flux on WENO-interpolated interface states and adds a Taylor series of even-order flux derivatives, so that any monotone, Riemann-solver or central-upwind flux and any set of variables can be used while keeping the high order of the conservative finite-difference scheme.
* [Artificial viscosity and spectral viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity.md) — Artificial viscosity controls oscillations by adding solution-dependent dissipation instead of overwriting the solution; the page covers the nonlinear blend of high-order projection stabilization and low-order isotropic viscosity in continuous finite elements, and the damping of DG modes that acts as an exponential modal filter linked to spectral viscosity.
* [Central WENO (CWENO)](/methods/cweno.md) — Central WENO schemes combine candidate polynomials of unequal size, including a central high-order one, with linear weights that may be any positive numbers summing to one, independent of the stencil geometry; the bundle's sources recall them as the stability-oriented branch of WENO from which WENO-AO and WENO-ZQ descend.
* [Central-upwind schemes](/methods/central-upwind-scheme.md) — Central-upwind fluxes use only one-sided local speeds $a^\pm_{i+1/2}$ and no Riemann solver; the flux is algebraically the HLL form, it reduces to upwind only when all eigenvalues share a sign, and its dissipation is reduced by characteristic-wise diffusion, Rankine–Hugoniot speeds or smoothness switches.
* [Characteristic decomposition](/methods/characteristic-decomposition.md) — Local characteristic decomposition projects stencil data onto the eigenvectors of the flux Jacobian at an interface state, reconstructs each field separately and projects back; the bundle's WENO, TENO and central-upwind schemes rely on it to suppress oscillations, while OEDG shows a method that does without it.
* [Convex limiting and invariant-domain-preserving schemes](/methods/convex-limiting.md) — Convex limiting blends a high-order update with a bound-preserving low-order update, adding as much antidiffusive flux as target bounds allow; the page gives the FCT form, the Zalesak limiter, Guermond–Popov bar states and invariant-domain bounds, element- versus subcell-wise blending, and the equivalence of subcell Rusanov finite volumes with sparse IDP DG.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin.md) — The discontinuous Galerkin (DG) semi-discretization of a conservation law with piecewise polynomials of degree N coupled only through numerical fluxes, its Runge–Kutta time stepping and CFL limit, the cell-average update behind bound preservation, and the stabilization families the sources attach to it.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method (DGSEM)](/methods/dgsem.md) — The DGSEM is nodal collocation DG on tensor-product Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto nodes with a diagonal mass matrix and an SBP derivative matrix; the page gives its strong, weak and split (flux-differencing) forms, the curvilinear metric treatment, the subcell flux form used for limiting, and the codes that implement it.
* [Dissipation-based WENO stabilization of continuous finite elements](/methods/weno-stabilized-continuous-galerkin.md) — The CG-WENO method of Kuzmin and Vedral uses a Hermite WENO reconstruction only to measure how far a continuous finite element solution is from smooth, and turns that measure into a cellwise blend of high-order projection stabilization and low-order artificial viscosity; the solution is never overwritten, and the stabilization is O(h^(N+1/2)) consistent for smooth solutions.
* [Entropy-conservative and kinetic-energy-preserving two-point fluxes](/methods/entropy-conservative-flux.md) — Two-point fluxes that satisfy Tadmor's jump condition between entropy variables and flux potentials conserve entropy, and entropy-dissipative fluxes satisfy it as an inequality; the page states the condition in Cartesian and directional form, gives the Ranocha, Shima et al. and scalar KPP fluxes, and the logarithmic mean with its division-reduced evaluation.
* [Fluctuation-form finite-difference WENO for non-conservative systems](/methods/fluctuation-form-fd-weno.md) — Balsara, Bhoriya, Shu and Kumar write point-value FD-WENO as an interior quasilinear term plus HLL or HLLI fluctuations at zone boundaries; it equals Lax–Friedrichs FD-WENO for linear systems, treats non-conservative products, preserves stationary linearly degenerate jumps and needs one reconstruction per zone.
* [Flux differencing and split-form DG](/methods/flux-differencing.md) — Flux differencing replaces the DG volume term D f by a sum of symmetric two-point volume fluxes weighted by 2D; on diagonal-norm SBP (LGL) elements it recovers split forms, inherits entropy conservation or kinetic-energy preservation from the volume flux, admits a local flux form, and costs about N+1 times the flux evaluations of the strong form.
* [Geometric quasilinearization (GQL)](/methods/geometric-quasilinearization.md) — Geometric quasilinearization rewrites every nonlinear constraint of a convex admissible set as a family of constraints affine in the conservative variables, indexed by free auxiliary variables; the page gives the representation theorems, the three construction methods, the explicit representations and the bound-preservation proofs built on them.
* [Hermite WENO](/methods/hermite-weno.md) — Hermite WENO (HWENO) builds WENO candidates from both cell averages and derivative information, which on element methods means extending each neighbour's polynomial into the cell and correcting its mean; Kuzmin & Vedral use such a reconstruction only to measure smoothness and drive the stabilization of continuous finite elements, never to overwrite the solution.
* [HLL-family approximate Riemann solvers](/methods/hll-riemann-solvers.md) — HLL, HLLE, HLLC and HLLEM/HLLI two-point fluxes as the bundle's sources use them: the single-state HLL form and its non-conservative and anti-diffusive extensions, positivity and CFL properties, carbuncle behaviour, and the switching of low- and high-dissipation solvers in high-order schemes.
* [Hybrid central-WENO schemes](/methods/hybrid-weno.md) — Hybrid schemes use a cheap linear (central or optimal upwind) reconstruction where a sensor declares the stencil smooth and switch to a nonlinear WENO or TENO reconstruction elsewhere; they cut dissipation and cost, but their quality hinges on the sensor, which the sources call case- and parameter-sensitive unless it is built from the TENO cut-off flags.
* [Lax–Friedrichs (Rusanov) flux and flux splitting](/methods/lax-friedrichs-flux.md) — The Lax–Friedrichs two-point flux and the Lax–Friedrichs flux splitting $f^\pm=\tfrac12(f\pm au)$, global or local, per field or scalar: the default upwinding of finite-difference WENO and TENO, the surface flux of DG, and the low-order flux whose bar-state structure makes invariant-domain and bound-preserving proofs work.
* [LCD central-upwind scheme](/methods/lcd-central-upwind-scheme.md) — Chertock et al. replace the scalar diffusion coefficients of the central-upwind flux by field-wise ones built from the one-sided characteristic speeds, in a local characteristic decomposition; the scheme becomes exactly upwind for every linear system and resolves contacts and instabilities more sharply.
* [Learned WENO weights](/methods/learned-weno-weights.md) — Learned WENO weights replace the smoothness-indicator formula of a WENO scheme by a trained neural network that outputs the nonlinear weights; this page describes WENO3-NN (architecture, invariance layer, ENO cut-off layer, loss, training data), what its normalised-variable diagram reveals, its cost, and the closed-form ROUND operators fitted to it.
* [Mapped WENO](/methods/mapped-weno.md) — Mapped WENO (WENO-M, Henrick, Aslam & Powers) passes the Jiang–Shu nonlinear weights through a mapping function, restoring the fifth-order condition at first-order critical points at a higher cost than WENO-JS; the bundle's sources recall it, without printing the mapping, as the first remedy for critical-point order loss.
* [Optimal cell-average decomposition](/methods/optimal-cell-average-decomposition.md) — The cell-average decomposition writes a cell average as a positive quadrature that includes the interface points and sets the bound-preserving CFL number of Zhang–Shu schemes; the page states the optimization problem, the proofs that the Gauss–Lobatto decomposition is optimal in 1D and for Q^N, and the optimal and quasi-optimal decompositions for P^N.
* [Oscillation-eliminating discontinuous Galerkin (OEDG)](/methods/oedg.md) — OEDG follows every Runge–Kutta stage of a standard DG scheme with an exactly solved damping step that multiplies each modal coefficient by a solution-dependent exponential factor; its damping coefficients are scale- and evolution-invariant, it conserves cell averages, and it carries an optimal fully discrete error estimate for linear advection.
* [ROUND schemes](/methods/round-scheme.md) — ROUND (Reconstruction Operators on the Unified Normalised-variable Diagram) schemes are three-cell compact finite-volume reconstructions designed directly as functions of the normalised cell average, which makes them scale-invariant and lets dissipation, TVD/CBC and ENO behaviour be placed by construction; this page gives the dissipative, low-dissipative, fine-tuned and neural-network-inspired formulations, their cost and evidence, and their order caveat.
* [Split convective forms](/methods/split-convective-forms.md) — Split forms write the convective terms of the mass and momentum equations as weighted mixtures of divergence and advective forms; the page gives the Coppola–Veldman family, the weight conditions and duality relations under which a discretization conserves mass, momentum and kinetic energy, the dual-sided variant and the finite-volume face fluxes they correspond to.
* [SSP Runge–Kutta time stepping](/methods/ssp-runge-kutta.md) — Strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta methods are convex combinations of forward-Euler steps; this is why the bundle's bound-preserving, invariant-domain and limiter proofs need only forward Euler, and the three-stage third-order Shu–Osher scheme, at CFL numbers from 0.15 to 0.8, is the default stepper of its semi-discrete schemes.
* [Subcell finite-volume limiting for DG](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-limiting.md) — Subcell finite-volume limiting makes a nodal DGSEM robust by convexly blending it, per element or per subcell interface, with a compatible low-order finite-volume scheme on the LGL subcells; the page gives the flux-differencing form that makes the two compatible, the four mix-and-match ingredients, and the measured effect of each choice.
* [Targeted ENO (TENO)](/methods/targeted-eno.md) — TENO builds a K-point reconstruction from candidate stencils of incremental width, separates smooth from non-smooth stencils by a strongly scale-separated measure and a sharp cut-off, and combines the surviving stencils with their renormalized linear weights, so that smooth regions recover the background linear scheme exactly; this page gives the standard recipe, its parameters, variants and recorded disagreements.
* [TENO-THINC](/methods/teno-thinc.md) — TENO-THINC is a finite-difference hybrid that uses TENO5 everywhere and replaces its interface flux by the THINC jump reconstruction in cells that a parameter-free detector, built only from the binary TENO stencil flags, identifies as containing a discontinuity; this page gives the detector, the switching rule, the evidence and the cost.
* [THINC reconstruction](/methods/thinc-reconstruction.md) — THINC reconstructs a cell's data by a hyperbolic-tangent jump profile fitted to the cell average and bounded by the neighbouring values; this page gives its finite-volume, finite-difference and normalised closed forms, the role of the steepness parameter (diffusive, nearly second order, anti-diffusive), and how it is hybridized with polynomial reconstructions.
* [Weighted compact nonlinear schemes (WCNS)](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md) — WCNS are finite-difference shock-capturing schemes in three steps — nonlinear node-to-interface interpolation of the variables, a two-point flux at the interfaces, and high-order compact interface-to-node flux differencing; this page records the family as the chunk-1 sources recall it, its TENO-weighted members (TCNS, WCNS-T), its relation to the alternative WENO formulation, and its stated weaknesses.
* [WENO-AO and multi-resolution WENO](/methods/weno-ao.md) — WENO-AO nonlinearly hybridizes one large central high-order stencil with the three small stencils of third-order WENO, and multi-resolution WENO uses nested central stencils down to a low-order base; Balsara et al. show that both need fixes to reach orders 7 and 9 and their third-order version, and use them in fluctuation-form FD-WENO.
* [WENO-CU6](/methods/weno-cu6.md) — WENO-CU6 (Hu et al.) is a sixth-order central-upwind WENO that adds a downwind three-point stencil to the fifth-order set, uses a six-point global smoothness indicator of order Δx⁶ and a WENO-Z-type weight with constant C = 20; in the bundle it is the standard low-dissipation comparator of the TENO papers.
* [WENO-JS reconstruction](/methods/weno-js-reconstruction.md) — The Jiang–Shu weighted ENO reconstruction: a convex combination of r-point candidate polynomials with weights d_k/(β_k+ε)^q, giving order 2r−1 in smooth regions and ENO behaviour at discontinuities; the baseline every later weight design modifies.
* [WENO-Z weights](/methods/weno-z.md) — WENO-Z replaces the Jiang–Shu weights by α_k = d_k[1+(τ/(β_k+ε))^q], with a global smoothness indicator τ built from the local β_k; the weights approach the linear ones faster in smooth regions, and the less-smooth stencil keeps more weight than in WENO-JS.
* [WENO-Z+ and WENO-Z+M](/methods/weno-z-plus.md) — WENO-Z+ adds to the WENO-Z weight a term proportional to (β_k+ε)/(τ_5+ε) that raises the weight of the less-smooth stencil; Luo & Wu's WENO-Z+M takes its square root and an adaptive coefficient, removing the negative dissipation that WENO-Z+ shows in its approximate dispersion relation.
* [WENO-ZN schemes](/methods/weno-zn.md) — WENO-ZN weights replace the WENO-Z global indicator by the square of the (2r−2)th undivided difference over the whole stencil, of order Δx^(4r−4), and the constant 1 by an adaptive function of the two extreme indicators; at order 2r−1 they keep the design order up to (2r−4)th-order critical points.
* [Zhang–Shu scaling limiter](/methods/zhang-shu-scaling-limiter.md) — The Zhang–Shu limiter scales a high-order polynomial linearly toward its cell average until the point values used by a cell-average decomposition lie in the admissible set; it conserves the cell average and, with a BP first-order flux and a CFL bound, makes the cell-average update bound-preserving.
