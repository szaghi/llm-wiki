# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [Buckley–Leverett problem](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md) — Scalar conservation law with the non-convex S-shaped flux used to model porous-media flow, run as Riemann or step data against a fine first-order reference; used to show that explicit DG with subcell convex limiting and implicit CWENO at Courant numbers near ten stay free of overshoots.
* [Burgers equation tests](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) — The inviscid Burgers equation as test bed: sine data for accuracy before the shock forms and shock capturing after, energy and entropy tests of split forms, discontinuous data, a two-dimensional four-quadrant Riemann problem, and special Euler data with gamma = 3 whose solution is a Burgers solution; records setups, breaking times and reported orders.
* [Double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark in which a Mach 10 shock at 60° to a reflecting wall forms a double Mach stem, slip line and wall jet; used to rank the numerical dissipation of shock-capturing schemes by the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up along the slip line.
* [Double rarefaction (123) problem](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md) — One-dimensional Euler Riemann problem of two gases receding from each other, whose two rarefactions leave a near-vacuum (Toro-type 123 data), a vacuum point or a vacuum region at the centre; a positivity and internal-energy robustness test, run by capturing schemes with positivity limiters and by a shock-tracking DG method.
* [Gas–liquid Riemann problems](/benchmarks/gas-liquid-riemann-problems.md) — One-dimensional Riemann problems between an ideal gas and a stiffened-gas liquid, with exact solutions: compressed air against water (Cocchi et al.), water at a billion pascals against air, water at two gigapascals moving into air, a gas–water double rarefaction and a rarefaction reflected into water; they test interface location, contact oscillations and robustness.
* [High-Mach astrophysical jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md) — Mach 80 and Mach 2000 gas jets injected into a light ambient medium, and their relativistic, MHD and gas–liquid relatives; near-vacuum, extreme-Mach-number robustness tests that most high-order schemes pass only with a positivity- or invariant-domain-preserving limiter.
* [Isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) — Smooth exact solution of the Euler equations, an isentropic vortex advected by a uniform stream (or held steady) on a periodic box; used for design-order convergence studies, which expose the time-step scaling and multidimensional quadrature high orders need, and, with an amplified vortex strength, as a stress test of entropy-conservative fluxes.
* [Kelvin–Helmholtz instability](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) — Periodic two-dimensional shear-layer benchmark: Euler variants with sharp, exponentially smoothed and tanh-smoothed layers serve as a visual dissipation gauge, a positivity stress test for high-order DG and a probe of the non-convergence of inviscid shear flow; a smooth guiding-center Vlasov variant serves as a convergence test.
* [KPP rotating wave](/benchmarks/kpp-rotating-wave.md) — Two-dimensional scalar conservation law with the nonconvex flux (sin u, cos u) and piecewise-constant data, whose entropy solution is a rotating spiral; high-order schemes that are non-oscillatory but not entropy stable converge to a wrong, nonentropic solution.
* [Leblanc shock tube](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md) — One-dimensional Euler Riemann problem with a pressure ratio of 10^9 and a density ratio of 10^3 or 2×10^3, printed in two inequivalent versions under one name; a robustness test of near-vacuum, extreme-ratio shock tubes in which positivity limiters and low dissipation are judged against a fine reference.
* [Mach 3 forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark of Mach 3 flow in a wind tunnel with a step, run to t=4; it tests robustness at the expansion corner and the resolution of the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up of the vortex sheet from the triple point.
* [MHD blast wave and rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) — Two-dimensional ideal-MHD robustness tests: the Balsara–Spicer blast wave at plasma beta about 2.5×10^-4, its relativistic counterpart, and the first and second rotor problems, which probe positivity of pressure and the effect of divergence errors, plus a weak, smooth magnetic blast used in three dimensions to verify entropy conservation and stability of DG schemes.
* [Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md) — Periodic two-dimensional ideal-MHD benchmark in which smooth vortical velocity and magnetic fields steepen into interacting shocks and current sheets; used for high-order MHD shock capturing, density-bound limiting, entropy stability and divergence control, in two normalizations that differ by a rescaling.
* [Rayleigh–Taylor instability](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark with gravity, in the Xu–Shu setting: a heavy and a light gas, γ=5/3, a single-mode interface perturbation on [0,1/4]×[0,1] run to t=1.95; used to compare the small-scale structure and the left–right symmetry of low-dissipation schemes.
* [Richtmyer–Meshkov instability](/benchmarks/richtmyer-meshkov-instability.md) — A planar shock crossing a sinusoidally perturbed interface between two gases or a metal and a gas: the single-mode air–SF6 case at Mach 1.2 against front-tracking and growth models, a heavy-to-light copper–gas case with phase reversal, and the Brouillette–Sturtevant air–acetone/SF6 case with re-shock; it tests interface sharpness, growth rates and the resolution of the mixing zone.
* [Sedov blast wave](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) — Point-explosion benchmark in which the energy of one cell drives a strong shock into cold gas of near-zero pressure, in one dimension and in a two-dimensional corner, with an exact similarity solution; a robustness test of positivity preservation at pressure ratios near 10^20, with a gas–liquid variant.
* [Shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark of a Mach 5.09 shock diffracting around a backward-facing corner into gas at rest, with a relativistic counterpart; the expansion behind the corner drives density and pressure towards zero, so the sources use it as a robustness test of positivity-preserving and limited high-order schemes.
* [Shock-induced combustion and detonation](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md) — Reacting-flow validation cases with detailed hydrogen chemistry: Lehr's ballistic blunt-body experiments, where the oscillation frequency of the shock-induced combustion front is measured; a one-dimensional reflected-shock detonation; and two-dimensional cellular H2–O2–Ar detonations judged by front speed and cell size.
* [Shock–bubble interaction](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md) — A planar shock hitting a circular or spherical region of different density or material: one-dimensional analogues, the Mach 1.22 air–helium and air–R22 experiments of Haas and Sturtevant, stronger air–helium and air–hydrogen cases, gas bubbles in water or stiffened gas, a relativistic version, and Baer–Nunziato and shallow-water variants; tests interface sharpness, robustness at material contrasts and the resolved roll-up.
* [Shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) — An isentropic vortex advected through a stationary Mach 1.1 shock on [0,2]×[0,1]; the reference setup tests the resolution of the shocked vortex through pressure profiles against fine-grid references, and amplified, strong-vortex, relativistic or non-Euler variants test bound preservation, shock-capturing dissipation and non-conservative multiphase schemes.
* [Shock–water-column interaction](/benchmarks/shock-water-column.md) — A planar shock in air hitting a cylindrical water column (or a spherical droplet), the liquid counterpart of the shock–bubble problem: validation against the interferograms of Igra and Takayama and the schlieren images of Sembian et al. at Mach 1.47 and 2.4, a column with an air cavity, and a Mach 10 case that tests positivity of high-order schemes.
* [Shu–Osher shock–entropy-wave problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) — One-dimensional Euler benchmark of a Mach 3 shock running into a sinusoidal density (entropy) wave, with its Titarev–Toro high-frequency variant; the bundle's most widely run test of how well a shock-capturing scheme resolves the short waves generated behind a shock without oscillating at the shocklets.
* [Sod and Lax shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) — The two most often run one-dimensional Euler Riemann problems with exact solutions, Sod's mild tube and Lax's stronger one, run by most sources as the first check of shock and contact capturing; records each source's placement, grid and final time, the printed variants and the multicomponent, stiffened-gas and relaxation relatives.
* [Solid-body rotation](/benchmarks/solid-body-rotation.md) — LeVeque's two-dimensional linear-advection test in which a smooth hump, a cone and a slotted cylinder rotate rigidly for one revolution; the exact solution is the initial data, so L1 errors and the solution range measure together the dissipation and the bound violations of limited high-order finite element schemes.
* [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) — Three-dimensional periodic vortex flow that transitions to turbulence: inviscid at low Mach number to compare kinetic-energy loss and enstrophy growth of shock-capturing schemes, viscous at Re=1600 or 5000 and Mach numbers up to 1.2 to compare dissipation rates with DNS, and in two dimensions a robustness test of entropy- and kinetic-energy-preserving DG.
* [Two-dimensional Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) — Four-quadrant Riemann problems of the two-dimensional Euler equations in the Lax–Liu classification, chiefly configurations 3 and 12 plus a four-contact spiral; used as visual tests of resolution, of the jet's sideband and neck instabilities, of scale invariance and of moving-mesh efficiency, with relativistic, MHD, multispecies and Burgers relatives.
* [Underwater explosion](/benchmarks/underwater-explosion.md) — A gas bubble at a gigapascal and 1250 kg/m³, 0.24 m in diameter, 0.3 m below a free water–air surface: the two-dimensional test of compressible two-phase solvers at density ratios near 1000 and pressure ratios near 10⁴, run by a sharp-interface DG solver and a pressure–temperature-equilibrium finite-volume model.
* [Woodward–Colella interacting blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md) — One-dimensional Euler problem of two strong blast waves between reflecting walls, pressures 1000, 0.01 and 100, run to t=0.038; a one-dimensional robustness test whose resolution is measured by the resolution of the density peaks and contacts after the blasts collide.
