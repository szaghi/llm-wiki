# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [Brio–Wu MHD shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md) — One-dimensional ideal-MHD Riemann problem with a constant normal field and a reversing transverse field, whose solution contains a compound wave; used to validate MHD Riemann solvers, continuous finite elements, unstaggered central schemes, positivity-filtered DG, all-Mach IMEX WENO, a one-step HLL-GRP finite-volume scheme, and, in a relativistic two-fluid variant, entropy-stable finite differences.
* [Bubble collapse near a wall](/benchmarks/bubble-collapse-near-wall.md) — A gas or vapour bubble in high-pressure liquid next to a rigid wall collapses asymmetrically and forms a wall-directed micro-jet; with pressure ratios of about 350 to 4300 and density ratios of 1000 and above it tests interface sharpness, tensile stability and conservation of compressible multiphase schemes.
* [Buckley–Leverett problem](/benchmarks/buckley-leverett-problem.md) — Scalar conservation law with the non-convex S-shaped flux used to model porous-media flow, run as Riemann or step data against fine reference solutions; used to show that explicit DG with subcell convex limiting, implicit CWENO at Courant numbers near ten and a multi-block WCNS crossing a block interface converge without overshoots or a spurious solution.
* [Burgers equation tests](/benchmarks/burgers-equation-tests.md) — The inviscid Burgers equation as test bed: sine data for accuracy before the shock and shock capturing after, energy and entropy tests of split forms, discontinuous data, a slow shock across a block interface, 2D Riemann and sine data, the latter on adaptive Active Flux grids, and gamma = 3 Euler data reducing to Burgers; setups, breaking times, orders.
* [Circularly polarized Alfvén wave](/benchmarks/circularly-polarized-alfven-wave.md) — Smooth exact travelling-wave solution of ideal and relativistic MHD in which the transverse velocity and magnetic field rotate along the propagation direction at constant density and pressure; advected over one period in 1D or along a rotated direction in 2D and 3D, it is a convergence-order test of MHD schemes, from fifth-order WENO to second-order GRP finite volumes.
* [Decaying compressible isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/compressible-isotropic-turbulence.md) — Triply periodic box of side 2π initialized with a random solenoidal velocity field of prescribed spectrum and turbulent Mach number 0.07–0.6, left to decay; the time histories of kinetic energy, turbulent Mach number, density fluctuations, enstrophy and dilatation against DNS or spectral references measure the numerical dissipation of a scheme on under-resolved turbulence with eddy shocklets.
* [Density-wave advection](/benchmarks/density-wave-advection.md) — Smooth density (entropy) wave carried at uniform velocity and pressure, the exact Euler solution being pure translation; used as a 1D and 2D order-of-accuracy test of Euler and Navier–Stokes schemes, as a long-time dissipation test, and as a probe of pressure-equilibrium and entropy conservation of kinetic-energy-preserving fluxes.
* [Double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark in which a Mach 10 shock at 60° to a reflecting wall forms a double Mach stem, slip line and wall jet; used to rank the numerical dissipation of shock-capturing schemes by the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up along the slip line.
* [Double rarefaction (123) problem](/benchmarks/double-rarefaction-problem.md) — One-dimensional Euler Riemann problem of two gases receding from each other, whose two rarefactions leave a near-vacuum (Toro-type 123 data), a vacuum point or a vacuum region at the centre; a positivity and internal-energy robustness test, run by capturing schemes with and without positivity limiters and by a shock-tracking DG method.
* [Flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md) — Cylinder flows in four regimes: supersonic and hypersonic inviscid flow with a detached bow shock (steady shock capturing, limiters, immersed walls); hypersonic viscous flow over isothermal or catalytic walls in perfect or reacting gas (wall heat flux); subsonic inviscid flow, whose zero drag and symmetry test wall treatments; viscous flow at Re 40 and 100 (wake, separation, drag).
* [Gas–liquid Riemann problems](/benchmarks/gas-liquid-riemann-problems.md) — One-dimensional gas–liquid Riemann problems with exact or reference solutions: compressed air against water, water at a billion pascals and at a terapascal against air, a weak water shock through an air interface, water at two gigapascals moving into air, gas–water rarefactions and liquid–vapour tubes with phase change; they test interface location, contact oscillations and robustness.
* [Gresho vortex](/benchmarks/gresho-vortex.md) — Low-Mach two-dimensional vortex in exact equilibrium between a piecewise-linear azimuthal velocity and its pressure field, advected through a periodic square; used to test whether a compressible scheme's dissipation stays bounded as the Mach number goes to zero, for semi-implicit all-Mach schemes and learned WENO weights.
* [High-Mach astrophysical jets](/benchmarks/high-mach-astrophysical-jet.md) — Mach 80 and Mach 2000 gas jets injected into a light ambient medium, and their relativistic, MHD and gas–liquid relatives; near-vacuum, extreme-Mach-number robustness tests that most high-order schemes pass only with a positivity- or invariant-domain-preserving limiter.
* [Hydrostatic equilibrium tests under gravity](/benchmarks/hydrostatic-equilibrium-tests.md) — Suite of Euler-with-gravity tests for well-balanced schemes: isothermal, isentropic and polytropic hydrostatic steady states in one and two dimensions, small perturbations of them, and shock tubes and contacts in a gravitational field, judged by whether the steady state is kept to round-off and the perturbation resolved.
* [Isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) — Smooth exact solution of the Euler equations, an isentropic vortex advected by a uniform stream (or held steady) on a periodic box; used for design-order convergence studies, which expose the time-step scaling and multidimensional quadrature high orders need, and, with an amplified vortex strength, as a stress test of entropy-conservative fluxes.
* [Kelvin–Helmholtz instability](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) — Periodic two-dimensional shear-layer benchmark: Euler variants with sharp, exponentially smoothed and tanh-smoothed layers serve as a visual dissipation gauge, a positivity stress test for high-order DG and a probe of the non-convergence of inviscid shear flow; low-Mach Euler and MHD variants check all-Mach schemes; guiding-center Vlasov and viscous Navier–Stokes variants serve as convergence tests.
* [KPP rotating wave](/benchmarks/kpp-rotating-wave.md) — Two-dimensional scalar conservation law with the nonconvex flux (sin u, cos u) and piecewise-constant data, whose entropy solution is a rotating spiral; high-order schemes that are non-oscillatory but not entropy stable converge to a wrong, nonentropic solution.
* [Leblanc shock tube](/benchmarks/leblanc-shock-tube.md) — One-dimensional Euler Riemann problem with pressure ratio 10^9 and density ratio 10^3 or 2×10^3, printed in two inequivalent versions under one name, plus a rescaled copy and a gamma = 1.4 variant of the first; a near-vacuum robustness test for positivity limiters, DG blending and ALE remapping, judged against fine or exact references.
* [Mach 3 forward-facing step](/benchmarks/forward-facing-step.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark of Mach 3 flow in a wind tunnel with a step, run to t=4 or to a steady state; it tests robustness at the expansion corner, the resolution of the Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up of the vortex sheet from the triple point, shock stability of Riemann solvers and steady-state convergence of WENO residuals.
* [Magnetic field-loop advection](/benchmarks/field-loop-advection.md) — Two-dimensional ideal-MHD test in which a weak cylindrical magnetic loop, defined by a vector potential, is advected diagonally across a periodic box; with the magnetic field nearly passive, the loop shape and magnetic-energy decay measure the dissipation of constrained-transport schemes, here at fourth order and at low Mach number.
* [Magnetized (MHD) vortex](/benchmarks/magnetized-vortex.md) — Smooth exact equilibrium vortices of ideal and relativistic MHD, in which centrifugal force, magnetic tension and pressure balance radially, advected or held static on a periodic box as convergence-order tests: Balsara's iso-density vortex, a relativistic iso-enthalpy vortex, and the near-vacuum vortex used to test positivity-preserving DG.
* [Material-interface advection tests](/benchmarks/material-interface-advection.md) — Uniform-velocity, uniform-pressure advection of material interfaces between gases or between gas and liquid, in 1D and 2D with up to five materials, and of a single-gas contact over many crossings, with pure translation as exact solution; it checks that multi-component and multi-phase schemes keep pressure and velocity in equilibrium, conserve mass and keep interfaces thin.
* [MHD blast wave and rotor](/benchmarks/mhd-blast-wave.md) — Two-dimensional ideal-MHD robustness tests: the Balsara–Spicer blast wave (plasma beta about 2.5×10^-4), an extreme variant at pressure ratio 10^5, a 3D blast, relativistic, two-fluid and radiative counterparts, and the first and second rotor problems, which probe pressure positivity and divergence errors; plus a smooth weak magnetic blast verifying entropy conservation of DG.
* [MHD shock–cloud interaction](/benchmarks/mhd-shock-cloud-interaction.md) — Ideal and relativistic MHD robustness test in which a strong planar fast shock with a rotational discontinuity overruns a dense cloud ten to thirty times the ambient density, in two and three dimensions; used for positivity-preserving DG, order-reduced fourth-order finite volumes and entropy-stable moving meshes.
* [Noh implosion problem](/benchmarks/noh-implosion-problem.md) — Cold gas at unit speed converging on a point, line or plane forms an infinite-strength shock moving out at speed 1/3 with an exact solution; run in one to three dimensions by shock-capturing, shock-tracking, ALE and adaptive-moving-mesh schemes to test wall heating at the centre, symmetry and positivity.
* [Orszag–Tang vortex](/benchmarks/orszag-tang-vortex.md) — Periodic two-dimensional ideal-MHD benchmark in which smooth vortical velocity and magnetic fields steepen into interacting shocks and current sheets; used for high-order MHD shock capturing, density-bound limiting, entropy stability, divergence control and long-time robustness, in two normalizations that differ by a rescaling, and in relativistic single-fluid and two-fluid versions.
* [Oscillating droplet](/benchmarks/oscillating-droplet.md) — A two-dimensional elliptic drop at rest relaxes under surface tension and oscillates about the circle of equal area; the measured period, compared with the linear two-phase (Rayleigh mode-2) formula, and the mean pressure jump, compared with Laplace's law, test the surface-tension model of compressible interface-capturing schemes.
* [Rayleigh–Taylor instability](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-instability.md) — Two-dimensional Euler benchmark with gravity, in the Xu–Shu setting: a heavy and a light gas, γ=5/3, a single-mode interface perturbation on [0,1/4]×[0,1] run to t=1.95; used to compare the small-scale structure and the left–right symmetry of low-dissipation schemes; a diffuse-interface variant measures the speed-up of adaptive moving meshes, and three-dimensional magnetized and two-species viscous variants test positivity-preserving DG.
* [Richtmyer–Meshkov instability](/benchmarks/richtmyer-meshkov-instability.md) — A planar shock crossing a sinusoidally perturbed interface: single-mode air–SF6 at Mach 1.2 against front tracking and growth models, heavy-to-light copper–gas with phase reversal, Brouillette–Sturtevant air–acetone/SF6 with re-shock, a single-gas density interface ranking Riemann solvers and reconstructions, and its viscous air–SF6 version; also shocked N2 and He bubbles. It tests interface sharpness, growth rates and mixing-zone resolution.
* [Sedov blast wave](/benchmarks/sedov-blast-wave.md) — Point-explosion benchmark: the energy of one or a few cells drives a strong shock into cold gas of near-zero pressure, in 1D, in a 2D corner or centre and in 3D, with an exact similarity solution; tests positivity preservation at pressure ratios near 10^20, positivity-limited DG, ALE remapping and grid-aligned shock stability, with a gas–liquid variant.
* [Shallow-water flow over a bump and lake at rest](/benchmarks/flow-over-a-bump.md) — One-dimensional shallow-water tests over an isolated bottom bump: the lake at rest (with and without dry zones), its small perturbation, and sub-, super- and transcritical moving-water steady states, used to measure exact well-balancing, order of accuracy on moving equilibria and spurious waves at hydraulic jumps.
* [Shock diffraction](/benchmarks/shock-diffraction.md) — Shock-diffraction benchmarks: the 2D Mach 5.09 corner problem, its relativistic counterpart and a Mach 10 step variant, testing positivity-preserving and limited schemes in the near-vacuum expansion behind the corner; the 3D diffraction of a strong shock by a sphere, an adaptive-mesh GPU scaling test; and Schardin's weak shock over a wedge, checking hybrid DG–FV switching against experiment.
* [Shock-induced combustion and detonation](/benchmarks/shock-induced-combustion.md) — Reacting-flow cases: Lehr's blunt-body experiments with a measured combustion-front oscillation frequency, one-dimensional reflected-shock and Chapman–Jouguet detonations, ZND structure and two-dimensional cellular detonations with ideal and real-gas equations of state judged by front speed and cell size, and detonations used as GPU chemistry performance tests.
* [Shock–bubble interaction](/benchmarks/shock-bubble-interaction.md) — A planar shock hitting a circular or spherical region of different density or material: one-dimensional analogues, the Mach 1.22 air–helium and air–R22 experiments of Haas and Sturtevant, a helium–R22 three-phase bubble, stronger air–helium and air–hydrogen cases, gas bubbles in water or stiffened gas, a relativistic version, Baer–Nunziato and shallow-water variants; tests interface sharpness, robustness and roll-up.
* [Shock–mixing-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-mixing-layer-interaction.md) — Viscous two-dimensional (and three-dimensional) benchmark after Yee, Sandham & Djomehri (also run inviscid) in which an oblique shock impinges on a spatially developing, periodically forced mixing layer at Re 500, producing vortices, shocklets and acoustic waves; used to test shock detectors and the resolution of low-dissipation schemes.
* [Shock–vortex interaction](/benchmarks/shock-vortex-interaction.md) — An isentropic vortex advected through a stationary Mach 1.1 shock on [0,2]×[0,1]; the reference setup tests the resolution of the shocked vortex through pressure profiles against fine-grid references, and amplified, strong-vortex, relativistic or non-Euler variants test bound preservation, shock-capturing dissipation and non-conservative multiphase schemes.
* [Shock–water-column interaction](/benchmarks/shock-water-column.md) — A planar shock in air hitting a cylindrical water column or a droplet: validation against the interferograms of Igra and Takayama and the schlieren images of Sembian et al. at Mach 1.47 and 2.4, a column with an air cavity, a Mach 10 positivity test of high-order schemes, and Mach 1.5 shocks on water and n-dodecane droplets in their own vapour, with and without phase change.
* [Shu–Osher shock–entropy-wave problem](/benchmarks/shu-osher-problem.md) — One-dimensional Euler benchmark of a Mach 3 shock running into a sinusoidal density (entropy) wave, with its Titarev–Toro high-frequency variant; the bundle's most widely run test of how well a shock-capturing scheme resolves the short waves generated behind a shock without oscillating at the shocklets.
* [Sod and Lax shock tubes](/benchmarks/sod-and-lax-shock-tubes.md) — The two most often run one-dimensional Euler Riemann problems with exact solutions, Sod's mild tube and Lax's stronger one, run by most sources as the first check of shock and contact capturing; records each source's placement, grid and final time, the printed variants and the multicomponent, stiffened-gas and relaxation relatives.
* [Solid-body rotation](/benchmarks/solid-body-rotation.md) — LeVeque's two-dimensional linear-advection test in which a smooth hump, a cone and a slotted cylinder rotate rigidly for one revolution; the exact solution is the initial data, so L1 errors and the solution range measure dissipation and bound violations of limited high-order schemes and troubled-cell switches; smooth rotations test order on adaptive grids.
* [Supersonic flow in a diamond-wedge channel](/benchmarks/supersonic-diamond-wedge-channel.md) — Steady Mach 2 Euler flow through a channel containing a diamond-shaped wedge, whose solution is a network of oblique shocks reflecting from the walls and intersecting, expansion fans and slip lines; used to show that implicit shock-tracking methods fit discontinuity surfaces of non-trivial topology on coarse meshes.
* [Supersonic turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/supersonic-turbulent-boundary-layer.md) — Spatially developing flat-plate turbulent boundary layer at free-stream Mach 2 and 5.86 in a 90δ0×10δ0×6δ0 box with digital-filter inflow, run as DNS, wall-resolved and wall-modeled LES; its skin friction and profiles against DNS show how much numerical dissipation a shock-capturing convective scheme can inject before wall turbulence is damped.
* [Supersonic vortex between concentric arcs](/benchmarks/supersonic-vortex-between-arcs.md) — Steady isentropic, irrotational supersonic flow between two concentric circular arcs with an exact smooth solution; it measures the order of accuracy of boundary treatments on curved slip walls, where straight-sided meshes, ghost cells and inexact boundary gradients reduce unstructured finite-volume and DG schemes to first or second order.
* [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) — Periodic vortex flow that transitions to turbulence: inviscid at low Mach number to compare kinetic-energy loss and enstrophy growth, viscous at Re 1600 or 5000 and Mach up to 1.2 to compare dissipation rates with DNS, in 2D a robustness test of structure-preserving DG, with a density slab a two-phase test, and a GPU performance case.
* [Triple-point problem](/benchmarks/triple-point-problem.md) — Two-dimensional three-state Riemann problem on [0,7]×[0,3] in which a shock from a high-pressure strip runs along two regions of different density, producing a vortex at the triple point with Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-up; run with one material and with two or three materials of different specific-heat ratio, as a test of interface and small-scale resolution and of multi-phase discretizations.
* [Turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) — Pressure-driven compressible flow between two isothermal no-slip walls in a periodic 2πh×2h×πh box, run as DNS, wall-resolved LES and wall-modeled LES from friction Reynolds number 180 to 20000 and bulk Mach 0.1 and 1.5, and judged against incompressible and compressible DNS profiles and friction laws.
* [Two-dimensional explosion and implosion problems](/benchmarks/explosion-and-implosion-problems.md) — Multi-dimensional Euler tests with Sod-type circular, spherical and diamond-shaped initial discontinuities: the long-time cylindrical explosion and the Liska–Wendroff implosion used as gauges of numerical dissipation, and short-time radial and spherical explosions used for monotonicity, symmetry and adaptive-cost tests.
* [Two-dimensional Riemann problems](/benchmarks/two-dimensional-riemann-problems.md) — Four-quadrant Riemann problems of the two-dimensional Euler equations in the Lax–Liu classification, chiefly configurations 3 and 12, with configurations 7 and 13 and two four-contact configurations; used as visual tests of resolution, of the jet's sideband and neck instabilities and of slip-line instability, of scale invariance and of moving-mesh efficiency, with relativistic, MHD, multispecies and Burgers relatives.
* [Underwater explosion](/benchmarks/underwater-explosion.md) — A gas bubble at a gigapascal and 1250 kg/m³, 0.24 m in diameter, 0.3 m below a free water–air surface: a two-dimensional test of two-phase solvers at density ratios near 1000 and pressure ratios near 10⁴, run by a sharp-interface DG solver, an equilibrium model and a Lagrangian particle method; plus a spherically symmetric one-dimensional explosion bubble.
* [Viscous shock tube (Daru–Tenaud)](/benchmarks/viscous-shock-tube.md) — Two-dimensional Navier–Stokes shock tube of Daru and Tenaud in a closed box [0,1]×[0,0.5] with a 100:1 density and pressure jump at Reynolds number 200, 1000 and above; the Mach 2.37 shock reflects from the end wall, interacts with the boundary layer and forms a lambda-shock and a primary vortex, a test of viscous discretizations, shock capturing and resolution.
* [Woodward–Colella interacting blast waves](/benchmarks/woodward-colella-blast-wave.md) — One-dimensional Euler problem of two strong blast waves between reflecting walls, pressures 1000, 0.01 and 100, run to t=0.038; a one-dimensional robustness test whose resolution is measured by the resolution of the density peaks and contacts after the blasts collide, also run as a two-dimensional strip on triangles.
