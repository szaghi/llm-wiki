# Summary

Compares seven schemes for controlling $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}$ in
shock-capturing (Godunov-type) MHD codes, all framed as modifications of a
conservative second-order base scheme: Powell's
[eight-wave formulation](/methods/eight-wave.md) (constraint to truncation
error), the [projection scheme](/methods/projection-method.md) (constraint to
Poisson-solver tolerance), and five constrained-transport / central-difference
variants (constraint to roundoff in a scheme-specific discretization),
including the Godunov-CT family catalogued at [flux-CT](/methods/flux-ct.md).
Extensive numerical testing (ten problems, Versatile Advection Code) ranks the
projection scheme, the field-CD scheme, and flux-CT as the most accurate and
reliable.

# Key claims

1. **CT–finite-volume equivalence.** The field-, flux-, and
   transport-flux-interpolated CT algorithms "can be recast into pure finite
   volume schemes, and the staggered representation of the magnetic field is
   unnecessary": in terms of cell-centered variables the recast FV schemes are
   *identical* to the originals, and they conserve the corner-centered
   divergence $D_h^{\mathrm{k}} B_h$ (his eq. 27) exactly
   ([Result page](/results/ct-finite-volume-equivalence.md)).
2. **Conservation vs orthogonality impossibility** (Appendix A): a
   conservative scheme cannot also guarantee that the discrete Lorentz
   acceleration is exactly orthogonal to $B_h$ in every cell — proved by
   explicit counterexample
   ([Result page](/results/conservation-orthogonality-incompatibility.md)).
   Consequence drawn in §1.1: for conservative shock-capturing methods *no*
   choice of discrete divergence constraint avoids all unphysical effects;
   the choice of discretization "is always somewhat arbitrary."
3. **Eight-wave failure mode.** The non-conservative source terms of the
   eight-wave formulation can produce incorrect jump conditions across strong
   shocks (rotated shock-tube test); the error does not vanish with
   resolution.
4. **Projection-scheme accuracy.** The projection scheme is consistent and
   retains the base scheme's order of accuracy even for discontinuous flows
   (proved in the paper), costs only ≈6% extra (2D, CPU-normalized), and is
   the only scheme of the seven applicable to unstructured grids — at the
   price of a *global* Poisson solve.
5. **Constraint-placement taxonomy.** Which discrete divergence is held, and
   how strongly, differs per family: CT/CD hold a specific stencil
   ($D_h^{\mathrm{k}}$ for CT variants, $D_h^{\mathrm{c}}$ for the CD
   schemes) to roundoff; projection enforces $D_h^{\mathrm{c}}$ to solver
   tolerance; eight-wave bounds the constraint by truncation error only.
   Initial/boundary data must be compatible with the scheme's own stencil.
