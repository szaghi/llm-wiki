# Summary

Given how a Riemann solution is used inside a difference scheme, Roe argues
that an exact solution is wasted effort. He replaces the nonlinear Riemann
problem by the exact solution of a *linear* one, $u_t + \tilde A u_x = 0$,
whose constant matrix $\tilde A(u_L,u_R)$ must satisfy **Property U**: a
linear map, consistency with the Jacobian, the exact jump identity
$\tilde A(u_L - u_R) = F_L - F_R$, and a complete set of eigenvectors. The
jump identity makes the scheme conservative and resolves an isolated shock
exactly. The construction tool is a **parameter vector**
$\mathbf w = \rho^{1/2}(1, u, v, w, H)^T$, in which every component of the
Euler conserved vector and fluxes is quadratic; jumps then factor exactly
through arithmetic means of $\mathbf w$, which gives the
$\sqrt\rho$-weighted averages of velocity and total enthalpy. The paper
prints the eigenvalues, eigenvectors and wave strengths (unsteady 3D, and
steady supersonic marching). One shock-tube experiment inside a
first-order upwind scheme agrees with Godunov's exact solver to about three
significant digits at about half the CPU time. An entropy weakness at sonic
expansions is identified, with a cure stated in words only.

The paper has **no divergence-constraint content**: Euler equations only,
no magnetic field. It is ingested as the origin of the Roe matrix and
Property U, which the bundle's MHD solvers ([Brio & Wu (1988)](/sources/brio-wu-1988.md),
[Ryu & Jones (1995)](/sources/ryu-jones-1995.md),
[Powell (1994)](/sources/powell-1994.md), the
[eight-wave formulation](/methods/eight-wave.md)) call "Roe-type".

# Key claims

Sections are unnumbered; they are cited by page.

1. **Linearized Riemann problem** (p. 359). The approximate solution is the
   exact solution of $u_t + \tilde A u_x = 0$ with the data $(u_L, u_R)$
   and $\tilde A$ constant. The obvious candidates
   $\tilde A = \tfrac12(A_L + A_R)$ and $\tilde A = A(\tfrac12(u_L + u_R))$
   "in general" fail condition (iii) below.
2. **Property U** (p. 359; named on p. 360, "uniform validity across
   discontinuities"):
   (i) $\tilde A$ is a linear map from the space of $u$ to the space of $F$;
   (ii) $\tilde A(u_L,u_R) \to A(u)$ as $u_L, u_R \to u$, with
   $A = \partial F/\partial u$;
   (iii) $\tilde A(u_L,u_R)(u_L - u_R) = F_L - F_R$ for any $u_L, u_R$;
   (iv) the eigenvectors of $\tilde A$ are linearly independent.
   The paper states, with a pointer to Roe's conference paper [1], that
   (iii) suffices for conservation and that (iii)+(iv) are necessary and
   sufficient for the scheme to "recognize" a shock: if
   $F_L - F_R = S(u_L - u_R)$ (eq. 5), $S$ is an eigenvalue of
   $\tilde A$, the jump projects onto that single eigenvector, and the
   Riemann solution is exact (p. 360). These statements are **asserted, not
   proved here**.
3. **Heuristic** (p. 360). On a region with $O(N^2)$ points crossed by
   finitely many discontinuities, $O(N)$ points lie near a single
   discontinuity, which Property U handles; $O(1)$ points lie near
   interactions, where conservation (also assured by U) must suffice.
4. **Multi-D caveat** (p. 360). Applied operator by operator after
   dimensional splitting, the method works for grid-aligned shocks; oblique
   shocks, especially near a steady state, are not recognized as being in
   equilibrium by either split operator. "The concept of operator splitting
   becomes rather dubious." No remedy is given.
5. **Existence via the mean-value path** (p. 361):
   $\tilde A = \int_0^1 A(\theta)\,d\theta$ on the straight path
   $u(\theta) = u_L + \theta(u_R - u_L)$, which may lack a closed form or be
   costly.
6. **A rational construction, "far from unique"** (p. 361, eqs. 6a–6c, 7).
   With $\Delta(\cdot) = (\cdot)_L - (\cdot)_R$, the identities
   $\Delta(pq) \equiv \bar p\,\Delta q + \bar q\,\Delta p$ and
   $\Delta(1/q) \equiv -\Delta q/q^{*2}$ (bar arithmetic, star geometric
   mean) give an exact matrix satisfying (i)–(iii) (eq. 7). It is "far from
   unique": applying (6b) to $\Delta(pqr)$ gives a result that depends on
   the order of multiplication. The formulae are cumbersome for
   eigen-analysis.
7. **Parameter vector** (pp. 362–363, eqs. 8–12). For the 3D Euler
   equations with a perfect gas, every component of $u, F, G, H$ is
   quadratic in $\mathbf w = \rho^{1/2}(1, u, v, w, H)^T$ (eq. 11), e.g.
   $u_5 = \frac{w_1w_5}{\gamma} + \frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}(w_2^2+w_3^2+w_4^2)$
   (12a). Roe calls it "a simple device which has so far worked each time
   that it has been tried" (p. 362). Homogeneity is "not essential" (shallow
   water offered as an exercise).
8. **Exact jump factorization** (eqs. 13–16): $u_L - u_R \equiv \tilde B(w_L - w_R)$
   and $F_L - F_R \equiv \tilde C(w_L - w_R)$, with entries linear in the
   arithmetic means $\bar w_i$ (eqs. 14, 16).
9. **The Roe matrix and averages** (p. 364, eqs. 17–19).
   $\tilde A = \tilde C\tilde B^{-1}$; its eigenvalues solve
   $\det(\tilde C - \lambda\tilde B) = 0$ (eq. 18). The averaged state is
   $$u = \frac{\rho_L^{1/2}u_L + \rho_R^{1/2}u_R}{\rho_L^{1/2}+\rho_R^{1/2}} = \frac{\bar w_2}{\bar w_1},\quad v = \frac{\bar w_3}{\bar w_1},\quad w = \frac{\bar w_4}{\bar w_1},\quad H = \frac{\bar w_5}{\bar w_1},$$
   and eq. 18 reduces to
   $(\lambda-u)^3[(\lambda-u)^2 - (\gamma-1)\{H - \tfrac12(u^2+v^2+w^2)\}] = 0$
   (eq. 19). No averaged density is printed; the Euler eigenstructure does
   not need one.
10. **Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, wave strengths** (eqs. 20–22). With
    $q^2 = u^2+v^2+w^2$ and $a^2 = (\gamma-1)[H - \tfrac12q^2]$:
    $\lambda_1 = u - a$, $\lambda_{2,3,4} = u$, $\lambda_5 = u + a$, with
    eigenvectors $e_1 = (1, u-a, v, w, H-ua)^T$, $e_4 = (1, u, v, w, \tfrac12q^2)^T$,
    $e_5 = (1, u+a, v, w, H+ua)^T$ and two shear vectors (eq. 20). Strengths
    in eqs. 22a–e. Taking the factors $v, w$ out of $e_2, e_3$ keeps the
    strengths determinate; the eigenvector determinant is then
    $2a^3/(\gamma-1)$, "never small unless the Mach number is very large"
    (p. 365).
11. **Steady supersonic marching** (eqs. 23–28): eigenvalues (25) with
    $R^2 = (u^2+v^2)/a^2 - 1$ (26); the independence determinant
    $8a^2H(u^2+v^2)/R$ is troublesome only at very large Mach number or with
    the $x$-Mach number near 1. Pseudo-unsteady devices for steady states
    (eqs. 29–33): constant $H$ gives non-physical eigenvalues (31);
    $p_x = a^2\rho_x$ gives eigenvalues "just as in the real-time flow" (33).
12. **Flux-difference splitting by wave sign** (p. 370, unnumbered).
    $\Delta F = \sum_j \lambda_j\alpha_je_j$, split into a left-moving part
    over $\lambda_j < 0$ and a right-moving part over $\lambda_j > 0$. The
    scheme is "first-order upwind differencing passed through the mechanism
    of [1]" (p. 368). **The paper does not print the interface flux** in the
    form central average minus half the sum of $|\lambda_j|\alpha_je_j$;
    that form is an equivalent later restatement (Brio & Wu eq. 15; Harten,
    Lax & van Leer eq. 3.12b).
13. **Entropy weakness** (pp. 370–371). When a wave is a fan spanning
    $x = 0$, the linearization assigns all of it to one side; in the
    extreme case $F_i = F_{i+1}$, $u_i \ne u_{i+1}$ with an expansion
    expected, both split parts vanish and the unphysical discontinuity
    stays. The cure is stated in words only: "For each interval which
    contains a sonic point one creates artificially an equal and opposite
    contribution to $\Delta F^-$ and $\Delta F^+$". **No formula** is
    given. Transonic aerofoil runs needed no special treatment, but users
    are advised to use such a device or artificial viscosity. Unlike
    Harten & Lax's construction, the solver has no "naturally constructed
    entropy condition" (p. 359).
14. **Accuracy beyond first order** (p. 359): the mechanism of [1]
    transmits accuracy "at least to third order", on "unpublished numerical
    results"; not shown here.

# Tests

1. **Riemann-problem comparison** (Fig. 1, p. 368). Sod's shock tube,
   "density ratio 1:8, pressure ratio 1:10"; $\gamma$ is **not stated**.
   The linearized (all-discontinuity) density against the exact one over
   $x/t \in [-2, 2]$: the match "is not very close", but the areas under the
   two curves are identical, a consequence of conservation.
2. **First-order Godunov against the Roe first-order upwind scheme**
   (Fig. 2, Table I, pp. 368–369). 35 steps at $\Delta t/\Delta x = 0.411$
   ("Sod's standard comparison"), 99 intervals. Density in the shock region
   (indices 72–80) differs by at most $3\times10^{-4}$ (e.g. index 76:
   0.1878 exact, 0.1881 approximate): "insignificant everywhere", largest
   near the shock, far below the truncation error common to both.
3. **Timing** (DEC KL-10, optimized FORTRAN, I/O excluded; pp. 369–370).
   Exact solver: 1.6 iterations on average at 0.20 ms each, 1.11 s in
   Riemann solves, 2.02 s total. Approximate solver: 0.27 ms per problem,
   0.94 s in Riemann solves, **1.09 s total**; the gain comes from the
   output being directly usable as wave increments. Higher-order variants:
   second order 1.20 s, with monotonicity logic 1.78 s, third-order monotone
   1.90 s.
