# Summary

Before this paper, every "positivity-preserving" MHD scheme assumed that
its first-order cell-average update was positivity-preserving; none had
proved it in more than one dimension. Wu analyzes conservative FV and DG
schemes with the Lax–Friedrichs flux on uniform Cartesian meshes. The
result is the
[positivity–divergence coupling](/results/positivity-divergence-coupling.md):
in 2D and 3D, positivity is provable once a discrete divergence-free (DDF)
condition holds, and in general it fails without one.

The file read is the arXiv v2 preprint. Volume, issue and pages were
verified on Crossref.

# Key claims

1. **Linearized admissible set** (Lemma 2.2). The admissible set
   $\mathcal{G}$ ([notation](/notation.md)) is rewritten as the
   intersection of half-spaces that are linear in $U$, indexed by auxiliary
   vectors $(v^*, B^*)$. Convexity and orthogonal invariance follow
   directly.
2. **The classical LF splitting property fails** for MHD (Proposition 2.5).
3. **Generalized LF splitting** (Lemma 2.6, Theorems 2.11, 2.15, 2.16).
   * A two-state inequality has an extra term in
     $(B_i - \tilde B_i)(v^*\cdot B^*)$. Remark 2.9 shows the inequality
     is false without that term.
   * The term cancels exactly when the convex combination of LF splitting
     states satisfies a DDF condition. In 2D this is a quadrature form of
     the Gauss integral, eq. 29.
   * The viscosity bound is a computable two-state estimate, not the
     spectral radius.
4. **1D** (Theorems 3.1, 3.4).
   * The first-order LF scheme with the standard viscosity is not
     positivity-preserving at any CFL number.
   * With the proper viscosity it is, given a constant $B_1$.
   * High-order schemes are positivity-preserving under limiter-accessible
     conditions.
5. **2D, first order** (Theorems 4.1, 4.3, 4.5, 4.6).
   * Admissible data alone do not guarantee an admissible update, for any
     viscosity multiple and CFL number.
   * Under $D_h^{\mathrm{c}}\bar B_h = 0$ (eq. 45, central differences of
     cell averages over $2\Delta x$) the update is admissible.
   * The LF scheme never increases $\max|D_h^{\mathrm{c}}\bar B_h|$, so it
     preserves that condition exactly. This gives the first provably
     positivity-preserving multidimensional MHD scheme.
6. **2D, high order** (Theorem 4.7, Remarks 4.8–4.9).
   * The sufficient conditions are admissibility at Gauss–Lobatto points
     plus a vanishing face-quadrature divergence of the interface-averaged
     normal fields (eq. 50).
   * A *locally* divergence-free DG field is not enough. A globally
     divergence-free field satisfies eq. 50 by Green's theorem, but the
     scaling limiter can break that.
   * Enforcing both conditions together is left open; Wu & Shu's
     symmetrizable-MHD work addresses it.
7. **Lower bound when the condition fails** (Theorem 4.10):
   $\mathcal{E}(\bar U^{n+1}) > -\Delta t\,(\bar v\cdot\bar B)\,\mathrm{div}_{ij}B^n$.
   On a high-Mach jet with $B_a = \sqrt{200}$ ($\beta = 0.01$), a locally
   divergence-free third-order DG scheme with a positivity limiter fails at
   $t \approx 2.4\cdot10^{-4}$. The four failing cells lie where the
   predicted negative part dominates, and the failure persists on a refined
   mesh.
8. **3D** (Appendix B): the same results with the 3D DDF condition.
