# Summary

A review that runs ten schemes on one 1D Euler shock-tube problem, all on
the same grid ($\Delta x = 0.01$) at Courant number 0.9: Godunov (in the
Eulerian two-step form of Godunov, Zabrodin & Prokopov), two-step
Lax–Wendroff, MacCormack, Rusanov, upwind, the Harten–Zwas self-adjusting
hybrid, Boris–Book antidiffusion, Harten's artificial compression method
(ACM), Hyman's predictor–corrector, and Glimm's random choice method with
Chorin's modification. Zones across the contact and the shock, overshoots
and run time per step are reported per scheme. Glimm's method is given in
full: the iterative exact Riemann solver of Godunov as modified by Chorin
(eqs. 12–19) and the sampling procedure (Appendix, eqs. 46–62).

Conclusions (§4): without corrective procedures, Godunov's and Hyman's
methods are best; Glimm's gives the sharpest discontinuities but is noisy
in rarefactions and costs 2–3 times as much per step; ACM beats Boris–Book
antidiffusion when used with first-order or hybrid schemes; the
corrective methods and the hybrid carry problem-dependent parameters. The
shock-tube data of §3 are the [Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-shock-tube.md).

**Bearing on divergence constraints: none.** Purely 1D gas dynamics, no
magnetic field, no incompressibility. The bundle takes from it the
shock-tube data, the hydrodynamic base of the Brio–Wu MHD tube
([rotated shock tube](/benchmarks/rotated-shock-tube.md)).

# Key claims

1. **The shock-tube problem** (§3, p. 14, Figs. 2–3). Left: density 1,
   pressure 1, velocity 0. Right: density 0.125, pressure 0.1, velocity 0.
   $\gamma = 1.4$, $\Delta x = 0.01$, $\sigma = 0.9$ in all calculations.
   Scheme parameters: Rusanov $\omega = 1.0$, Boris–Book $\eta = 0.125$,
   Hyman $\delta = 0.8$, Lapidus viscosity $\nu = 1.0$ "in all but one
   case" (Godunov + ACM uses 2.0, p. 16). The diaphragm position is drawn
   symbolically (Fig. 2), not given. **No output time is printed** in the
   text or captions. Wave structure (Fig. 3): left rarefaction, contact
   (pressure and velocity continuous), right shock.
2. **Table I: standard schemes** (p. 4), order and stability limit, with
   $\sigma = \max(|u|+c)\,\Delta t/\Delta x$. Godunov, order 1,
   $\sigma \le 1$: a Lax–Friedrichs-type half step to $i+1/2$ followed by
   a conservative full step. Sod notes (p. 7) that Godunov's Lagrangian
   method replaces the half-step values by the Riemann-problem values
   $u_*$, $p_*$ (eqs. 18–19); **the "Godunov" scheme tested is therefore
   not the Riemann-solver Godunov scheme**. Two-step Lax–Wendroff and
   MacCormack, order 2, $\sigma \le 1$. Rusanov, order 1, $\sigma \le 1$
   and $\sigma \le \omega \le 1/\sigma$. Upwind, order 1, $\sigma \le 1$,
   with a centred pressure term.
3. **Glimm's random choice** (§2, eqs. 9–11). Exact Riemann solutions
   between piecewise-constant cells, sampled at an equidistributed random
   point; $\Delta t < \Delta x / 2(|u|+c)$ so that neighbouring Riemann
   problems do not interact. Glimm's choice of a new random number per
   point and per level is "disastrous" at finite $\Delta x$; Chorin's one
   number per time step fixes it (pp. 14–16).
4. **Iterative exact Riemann solver** (eqs. 12–19, pp. 6–7). Wave mass
   fluxes $M_l$, $M_r$ as functions of the star pressure (16a,b; 17), the
   star pressure (18) and velocity (19), iterated from a starting value
   given in Chorin and in Sod's ref. [25], not here.
5. **Lapidus artificial viscosity** (eq. 20, p. 7), added to Godunov,
   MacCormack and Lax–Wendroff; not applied to the mass equation, "so as
   to minimize the amount of mass diffused across the contact". Contact
   smearing is attributed to truncation error, citing Harten (§1, p. 2).
6. **Harten's ACM** (eqs. 21–27, 38, pp. 8–10). An artificial compression
   flux turns a contact into a shock of the modified equation; applied by
   operator splitting with a limited coefficient; restricted to
   first-order schemes; switched off in smooth regions in the hybrid.
7. **Harten–Zwas hybrid** (eqs. 28–37, pp. 9–10). Flux blend of a
   first-order and a $k$-th order flux by a switch $\theta$; MacCormack
   ($k = 2$) with an added first-order term; stability limit
   $\max(|u|+c)\Delta t/\Delta x \le 3^{1/2}/2$; switch built on density
   differences with $p = 1$.
8. **Boris–Book antidiffusion** (eqs. 39–44, pp. 11–12). Two-step
   Lax–Wendroff, diffusion $\eta$, then a limited antidiffusive flux
   (44d); $\max(|u|+c)\Delta t/\Delta x \le 1$.
9. **Hyman predictor–corrector** (eq. 45, pp. 12–13). Central differences
   for the flux, an improved-Euler step, a Rusanov-like viscosity and a
   switch "a type of artificial compression"; limit 1.
10. **High-order methods abandoned** (§1, p. 3). Fourth and sixth order
    were tried; with matching high-order viscosity the time step became
    prohibitive, and with the third-order Lapidus viscosity "all benefits
    obtained by using a high-order method are lost".
11. **Multi-D** (§4, p. 25). The 1D shock tube is a "sitting duck" for
    Riemann-based methods; "The true value of a numerical method should be
    measured by its ability to solve multidimensional problems."

# Tests

One problem, the shock tube of claim 1. Each figure plots density,
velocity, pressure and specific internal energy against the exact
solution; widths are counted in zones of $\Delta x = 0.01$.

| Fig. | Scheme | Contact (zones) | Shock (zones) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Godunov | 7–8 | 5–6 | rarefaction corners rounded |
| 5 | Godunov + ACM ($\nu = 2.0$) | 3–4 | 1–2 | slight undershoot at the rarefaction tail; oscillations at the contact |
| 6 | Lax–Wendroff | 6–8 | 4–6 | overshoots, larger at the shock |
| 7 | MacCormack | 7–8 | 5–6 | as Lax–Wendroff |
| 8 | Rusanov | 14–16 | 6–8 | "extremely diffusive" |
| 9 | Rusanov + ACM | 2–3 | 1–2 | "greatly improved" |
| 10 | Upwind | — | — | **nonphysical expansion shock** at the left end of the rarefaction, attributed to the centred pressure term |
| 11 | Glimm | 0 | 0 | positions exact only on average; rarefaction not smooth |
| 12 | Antidiffusion | 5–7 | 1–2 | slight overshoot at the rarefaction tail |
| 13 | Harten–Zwas hybrid | 8–9 | 5–6 | oscillation-free |
| 14 | Hybrid + ACM | 3–4 | 2–3 | |
| 15 | Hyman | 6–8 | 3–4 | oscillation-free |

* **Table II** (p. 20): Glimm with 9 interior points shows all waves.
* **Table III** (p. 20): over 20 Glimm steps mass (about 0.543–0.554) and
  energy (about 2.213–2.275) are conserved "on the average"; momentum
  grows linearly from 0.018 to 0.190 (the reader attributes this to the
  boundary pressure difference; the paper does not say).
* **Table IV** (p. 23): seconds per step on a CDC 6600 at 100 points,
  0.224–0.276 for most schemes, 0.364 for Glimm; ACM adds about
  0.011–0.021. The text's factor 2–3 for Glimm (pp. 19, 24) is not what
  Table IV shows (about 1.6), whose times include the exact solution,
  printing and plotting (footnote a).
