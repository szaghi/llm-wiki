# Summary

The canonical book of the edge-element school: computational
electromagnetism developed from variational formulations with **Whitney
elements** as the discretization substrate and **complementarity** — dual
formulations (e.g. via scalar and vector potentials) yielding two-sided
bounds on energy quantities — as an organizing methodological principle.
Bossavit's identification of Nédélec's edge elements with Whitney forms,
and his "Japanese papers" popularizing the geometric viewpoint, are the
historical bridge repeatedly credited by the sources in this bundle
(Hiptmair, AFW, Christiansen, Teixeira, Desbrun-school).

**Coverage of this ingestion (scoped):** front matter, series/editor
framing, and positioning; body chapters *not* compiled — their technical
content is represented in this bundle at research grade by
[Hiptmair (2002)](/sources/hiptmair-2002.md) and the FEEC line, which
postdate and systematize it. Ingested chiefly to give the corpus's most
frequently cited lineage anchor its own verifiable Source page. (No DOI;
ISBN is the permanent identifier.)

# Key claims

1. **Edge elements are Whitney forms** — the identification (Bossavit,
   1988 onward, consolidated in this book) that fused the FE and
   forms/lattice communities; compiled in this bundle at
   [Whitney forms](/definitions/whitney-forms.md).
2. **Complementarity as method**: solving both dual formulations brackets
   the true energy — a structure-exploiting error-control device
   distinctive of this school and not otherwise represented in the bundle.
