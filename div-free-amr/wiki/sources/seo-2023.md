# Summary

HOW-MHD is a Cartesian MHD code for adiabatic and isothermal flows. It
combines:
* the fifth-order finite-difference WENO of Jiang & Shu and Jiang & Wu,
  with characteristic decomposition and local Lax–Friedrichs splitting;
* a five-stage, fourth-order SSP-RK integrator at CFL about 1.5;
* a new high-order staggered CT that generalizes the second-order CT of
  Ryu et al. (1998).

The CT is the paper's contribution for this bundle. It is merged into
[constrained transport](/methods/constrained-transport.md),
[field-loop advection](/benchmarks/field-loop-advection.md) and
[exact discrete divergence](/properties/exact-discrete-divergence.md).

# Key claims

1. **The second-order CT bottleneck** (§1). With the second-order CT, the
   fifth-order WENO code converges at second order on 3D MHD waves, as
   Donnert et al. (2019) found for an earlier version of the code.
2. **The high-order CT** (§2.3).
   * The face field $b$ is staggered. The cell-centred $B$ in the state
     vector is a sixth-order interpolation of it (eq. 30).
   * The edge "advective fluxes" $\Omega$ ($E_h$) are built from the
     upwinding parts of the WENO-reconstructed magnetic fluxes,
     interpolated to edges at fourth order.
   * They are corrected to edge point values by transverse
     second-difference terms with weight $1/24$.
   * Faces are updated by $\nu$-order central differences of the edge
     values (eq. 27): $\nu = 4$ (CT4) or $\nu = 6$ (CT6, the default).
   * The divergence uses the same $\nu$-order differences (eq. 29). The
     paper states it is preserved "up to the numerical truncation error".
   * Lower-order interpolation lowers the convergence order: with
     fourth-order interpolation to cell centres, only fourth order is
     obtained.
3. **Accuracy** (§§3.1–3.2). On 3D linear Alfvén, fast and slow waves and
   on a 3D circularly polarized Alfvén wave, CT4 and CT6 give fifth-order
   convergence and the second-order CT gives second order. CT6 has
   slightly smaller errors at negligible extra cost.
4. **Diffusivity** (§§3.3–3.4).
   * Field loop: the high-order CT keeps the loop circular. At $t = 20$ and
     $N = 128$ it retains about 96% of the magnetic energy, against 95%
     with the second-order CT.
   * Reconnection in a current sheet proceeds more slowly with the
     high-order CT, closer to a higher-resolution run.
5. **Robustness** (§§3.5–3.8). Oblique shock tube, rotor, 3D blast and 3D
   jet launching all run. On the shock tests the CT order makes no visible
   difference. On the rotor the normalized maximum of $\nabla\cdot b$
   rises from zero and saturates "at the level of numerical truncation
   error" for all three CT variants (Fig. 11).
6. **Turbulence** (§§3.9–3.10). In ISM-like ($\mathcal M \approx 10$,
   $\beta = 0.1$) and ICM-like ($\mathcal M \approx 0.5$, $\beta = 10^6$)
   driven turbulence, CT6, CT4 and the second-order CT give identical
   spectra. The WENO code at $256^3$ reaches higher wavenumbers than a
   second-order TVD code, which looks like the WENO code at $128^3$.
   Isothermal and adiabatic runs at $\mathcal M \approx 1$ give similar
   statistics.
