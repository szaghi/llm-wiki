# Summary

Introduces what is now called the **Yee lattice** and the **FDTD scheme**:
Maxwell's curl equations discretized with the electric-field components at
edge midpoints and magnetic-field components at face centers of a cubic cell
(his Fig. 1), advanced by a leapfrog scheme with $E$ and $H$ staggered in
time by a half step. The stated motivation is the *boundary condition*: with
this placement, tangential-$E$ on a perfectly conducting surface (and hence
normal-$H$) vanishes by setting boundary DOFs to zero — the grid is chosen
"so as to approximate the condition … as accurately as possible."
Establishes the CFL-type stability bound
$\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2+(\Delta y)^2+(\Delta z)^2} > c\,\Delta t$, the 2D TE/TM
reduction, and a TM pulse-scattering computation validated against
Friedlander's closed-form wedge-diffraction solution.

Read visually, all 6 pages (text layer present but figures load-bearing).

# Key claims

1. **The placement** (Fig. 1): $E$-components at middles of cell edges,
   $H$-components at centers of cell faces — with the update for each face
   $H$ (his eq. 5) being the circulation of the four surrounding edge $E$
   values, and each interior edge value shared by adjacent faces.
2. **Leapfrog time staggering**: $E$ at integer, $H$ at half-integer time
   levels — staggering in time as well as space
   ([Yee scheme](/methods/yee-scheme.md)).
3. **Boundary-condition-driven design**: conducting surfaces approximated by
   cube faces; the field placement makes the PEC condition exact on the
   lattice; the scattering example imposes only $E_z = 0$ on the obstacle
   boundary.
4. **Stability criterion** (eqs. 7–8) for constant $\epsilon, \mu$, with
   $c_{\max}$ in inhomogeneous regions.
5. **What the paper does *not* claim**: divergence preservation is nowhere
   stated — Maxwell's divergence equations are not even written. The
   solenoidal property of the lattice is a later reading (see notation map).
