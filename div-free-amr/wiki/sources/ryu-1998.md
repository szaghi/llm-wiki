# Summary

The paper puts the staggered-mesh idea of Dai & Woodward (1998) into the
authors' second-order Harten-TVD MHD code (Ryu & Jones 1995; Ryu et al.
1995a). It replaces the code's earlier explicit Poisson divergence-cleaning
step. The face-centred field is the evolved field; the cell-centred field
used by the TVD step is the two-face average (eqs. 3–4).

Unlike Dai & Woodward, the edge "advective flux" (the $z$ electric field) is
built from the TVD upwind fluxes. For each direction only the advective
product is averaged centrally, and the full seven-wave TVD dissipation of
the magnetic component is added (eqs. 6–7). The edge value is the plain
average of two such fluxes per direction (eq. 15). Faces are updated by the
discrete Stokes differences (eqs. 16–17), so the net magnetic flux out of
each cell is exactly zero if it was zero before (eq. 18).

The scheme reduces exactly to the 1D code on grid-aligned problems, runs
about 20% faster than the cleaning version, and drops the
periodic-domain, regular-grid requirement of the Poisson cleaning. Tests:
two diagonal 2D shock tubes, Orszag–Tang, supersonic MHD clouds including
a new oblique-field case, and a cylindrical MHD jet. The authors name the
class "method of characteristics, constrained transport" (MoCCT, §4). No
divergence value is reported in any test.

# Key claims

1. **Why divergence errors matter** (§1). Conventional schemes conserve the
   advective fluxes of the induction equation, but "nothing maintains the
   magnetic fluxes in the sense of Gauss's law". Nonzero $\nabla\cdot\mathbf
   B$ "usually grows exponentially, causing an anomalous force parallel to
   the magnetic field" (attributed to
   [Brackbill & Barnes (1980)](/sources/brackbill-barnes-1980.md)); the
   exponential growth is asserted, not shown. The nonconservative momentum
   form of Brackbill & Barnes and Zachary et al. (1994) keeps the error
   small but risks "unphysical results because of the nonconservation of
   momentum" (citing LeVeque).
2. **Four approaches** (§1):
   * vector potential: second derivatives in the Lorentz force, third-order
     differencing needed to stay second order (Evans & Hawley 1988);
   * [Powell (1994)](/sources/powell-1994.md) source terms: "works well for
     some problems with open boundaries but not for others, including those
     with periodic boundaries" (no test given);
   * explicit cleaning after the fluid update (Zachary et al. 1994; Ryu et
     al. 1995a,b): adequate if boundary effects are negligible or the
     domain periodic and the grid "more or less regular", otherwise "not
     easily adaptable";
   * staggered-mesh CT (Evans & Hawley 1988; DeVore 1991; Stone & Norman
     1992), previously artificial-viscosity based and "unnatural" for
     Riemann-solver schemes until Dai & Woodward (1998).
**DeVore's member of the group: a qualification.** [DeVore (1991)](/sources/devore-1991.md) is flux-corrected transport (explicit low-order diffusion plus limited antidiffusion on edge fluxes, Appendix eqs. A3–A10), not an artificial-viscosity scheme; the grouping is Ryu et al.'s.

3. **Placement** (§2, Fig. 1, eqs. 3–4). Face components $b_x$, $b_y$ on
   cell interfaces; fluid variables and the intermediate cell-centred field
   at centres; the edge flux $\Omega$ at cell corners in 2D. The centred
   field is the two-face average, recomputed from the faces each step.
   Non-uniform grids need "an appropriate second-order interpolation" (not
   given).
4. **Fluid update** (eq. 5): Strang-split TVD sweeps, $L_yL_x$ then
   $L_xL_y$.
5. **Interface fluxes for the field** (eqs. 6–7). On an $x$-interface: the
   central average of $B_yv_x$ from the two adjacent cells, minus
   $\frac{\Delta x}{2\Delta t}\sum_{k=1}^{7}\beta_k R_k(5)$, the fifth
   ($B_y$) component of the seven-wave Harten-TVD dissipation; the
   $y$-interface flux is the analogue with $B_xv_y$. Rationale (p. 246):
   only the advective term "requires modification of fluxes to avoid
   numerical problems", while the dissipation keeps "information from
   seven characteristic waves". Computed in the TVD sweep "with little
   additional cost".
6. **Edge flux** (eq. 15), "a simple arithmetic average, which still keeps
   second-order accuracy":
   $$\bar\Omega_{i,j} = \tfrac12\left(\bar f_{y,i+1,j} + \bar f_{y,i,j}\right) - \tfrac12\left(\bar f_{x,i,j+1} + \bar f_{x,i,j}\right).$$
   *Compiler check:* $\bar f_x \sim B_yv_x$ and $\bar f_y \sim B_xv_y$, so
   $\bar\Omega \approx v_yB_x - v_xB_y = E_z$.
7. **CT update and exact constraint** (eqs. 16–18):
   $$b^{n+1}_{x,i,j} = b^n_{x,i,j} - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y}\left(\bar\Omega_{i,j}-\bar\Omega_{i,j-1}\right),\qquad b^{n+1}_{y,i,j} = b^n_{y,i,j} + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(\bar\Omega_{i,j}-\bar\Omega_{i-1,j}\right),$$
   and the net face flux of every cell,
   $(b_{x,i,j}-b_{x,i-1,j})\Delta y + (b_{y,i,j}-b_{y,i,j-1})\Delta x$, is
   zero at $n+1$ "if it is zero at step $n$". The field update is unsplit
   and follows the split fluid update.
8. **1D reduction** (pp. 246–247): on grid-aligned 1D problems the 2D code
   reproduces the 1D code exactly; "with Dai & Woodward's (1998) advective
   fluxes, that is not necessarily true".
9. **Cost** (p. 247): about 400 Mflops on a Cray C90, about
   $1.2\times10^5$ zones/s in 2D, "about 20% faster than the previous code,
   which is due to the absence of an explicit divergence-cleaning step".
10. **Scope.** 3D and other geometries are called "trivial" (§2; no 3D
    formula); cylindrical $(r,z)$ is used in §3.4. §4: the method "exactly
    conserves the surface integral of magnetic flux over a cell and does it
    in an upwind fashion". No projection and no source term are used.

# Tests

All with $\gamma = 5/3$ and Courant number 0.8; the entropy-fix parameters
are printed per wave family, and the results are "mostly not very
sensitive" to them (§3). **No divergence measure is reported in any test.**

1. **Diagonal shock tubes** (§3.1, Figs. 2a–b). $[0,1]^2$, $256^2$,
   propagation along the diagonal, compared with the exact solution of Ryu
   & Jones (1995) ([rotated shock tube](/benchmarks/rotated-shock-tube.md)
   at 45°).
   * Fig. 2a (Fig. 1 of Ryu et al. 1995a): fast shocks, a slow rarefaction,
     a slow shock and a contact, "all … correctly reproduced".
   * Fig. 2b (Fig. 2 of Ryu et al. 1995a), $t = 0.2\sqrt2$: fast shocks,
     rotational discontinuities, slow shocks and a contact. The plotted
     parallel field stays flat at about 0.564.
2. **Orszag–Tang vortex** (§3.2, Fig. 3). Periodic $[0,1]^2$, $256^2$,
   $\beta = 10/3$, $M^2 = 1$, shown at $t = 0.48$; structures "match
   exactly with those in Ryu et al. (1995a)". Visual comparison only.
3. **Supersonic MHD cloud** (§3.3, Figs. 4a–b). Aligned and transverse
   models of Jones et al. (1996) plus a new oblique (45°) case; Mach 10,
   $\beta = 4$, density contrast 10, 50 zones per cloud radius. Agreement
   with Jones et al. is "good"; the oblique case was "difficult to simulate
   with the old code because of its lack of a suitable periodic space for
   solving Poisson's equation in the explicit divergence-cleaning step".
4. **Cylindrical MHD jet** (§3.4, Figs. 5a–b). $(r,z)$ on $256\times1700$,
   Mach 20, density contrast 0.1, helical jet field, to $t = 2.2$; about
   20 CPU hours on a Cray C90. A robustness demonstration.
