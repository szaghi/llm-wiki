# Summary

The paper reports direct-connect experiments with non-premixed
ethylene–air continuous rotating detonation (CRD) in a hollow
(centerbody-less) cylindrical chamber, 100 mm in diameter and 90 mm long,
exhausting through a Laval nozzle; seven nozzles give contraction ratios 1,
2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12. With air at 540–560 g/s and equivalence ratio
0.33–1.35 it maps the operating domain (lean limit against contraction
ratio) and identifies three propagation modes: single-wave, two-wave
(homo-rotating, only at contraction ratio 12 and equivalence ratio at least
0.99) and a "sawtooth wave" near the lean limit at contraction ratios 1, 2
and 4. Propagation stability is quantified by the relative standard
deviation of the instantaneous frequency. The headline is a single-wave
ethylene–air CRD at 1915.40 m/s average speed (6.10 kHz), slightly above
the computed Chapman–Jouguet (C-J) speed, in contrast with the over-50%
velocity deficits and sawtooth waveforms of earlier ethylene–air annular
work.

# Key claims

1. **Operating domain** (§3, Fig. 4, Abstract). CRD is achieved over the
   tested equivalence ratios 0.33–1.35; the lean limit rises and the
   domain shrinks as the contraction ratio increases: 0.4 at contraction
   ratio 1, about 0.7 at 12 (read from the figure, approximate). The rich
   limit was not sought. Shown.
2. **Why CRD is easy here** (§3.1). Attributed to the hollow chamber
   satisfying the empirical criterion "annular width > half the detonation
   cell size" (Bykovskii et al.) and to a recirculation zone at the sudden
   enlargement that holds a deflagration acting as an ignition source; the
   lean-limit trend is "inferred" from lower main-stream velocity, lower
   wave height and a smaller required cell size. Stated, not measured.
3. **Test 1, single wave** (§3.2.1, Figs. 6–7, Eqs. 1–5). Contraction
   ratio 4, air 543 g/s, equivalence ratio 1.09: average frequency
   6.10 kHz (FFT 6.02 kHz), average speed 1915.40 m/s against a computed C-J
   speed of 1845.91 m/s (1 atm, 300 K), about 103.8% of C-J (ratio computed
   by the reader). Called the highest ethylene–air CRD speed reported to
   date; the excess is referred to Zhang et al. for "correction methods".
   Shown.
4. **Test 2, two waves** (§3.2.2, Figs. 8–9). Contraction ratio 12, air
   553 g/s, equivalence ratio 1.00: apparent speed 2775.76 m/s; the
   two-sensor time ratio of about 2/3 gives two waves, hence 1387.88 m/s per
   wave, 74.62% of C-J; average frequency 8.84 kHz (FFT 8.79 kHz). A
   secondary peak behind each main peak is attributed to oblique-shock
   reflection off the nozzle contraction, stronger at larger contraction
   ratio. Shown.
5. **Mode transition** (§3.1, Fig. 5b). At contraction ratio 12 the
   frequency steps at equivalence ratio about 0.99 from below the
   single-wave critical frequency (C-J speed over chamber circumference) to
   between the single- and two-wave critical frequencies. Shown.
6. **Sawtooth wave** (§3.2.3, Figs. 10–13). Very low pressure rise and no
   steep front. Test 3 (contraction ratio 2, equivalence ratio 0.43):
   self-sustains about 65 ms then quenches; 4.32 kHz, 1356.48 m/s, 92.97%
   of a C-J speed of 1459.11 m/s. Test 4 (contraction ratio 1, equivalence
   ratio 0.47): persists about 350 ms at 4.82 kHz; in blowdown the nitrogen
   purge pushes extra ethylene in and the wave becomes a high-pressure-rise
   CRD at about 6 kHz. Shown.
7. **Sawtooth as a critical condition** (§3.2.3). Called "a manifestation
   of high-frequency tangential instability in rocket combustor" and a
   critical condition with too little heat release to couple the inducing
   shock to the flame. Stated, not demonstrated.
8. **Stability metric** (§3.3, Figs. 14–15, Eqs. 6–7). The relative
   standard deviation of the instantaneous frequency decreases with
   equivalence ratio for contraction ratios 1–6 (attributed to the nozzle
   isolating back pressure) and increases for 8–12 (attributed to the
   reflected oblique shock); two near-lean-limit runs at 8 are below 1%;
   the two-wave mode is least stable; around stoichiometric, contraction
   ratios 2 and 4 give the lowest deviation and super-C-J speed. Conclusion:
   contraction ratio 2–4 is best. Trends shown, attributions interpreted.

# Methods

* **Rig** (§2, Fig. 2): NUDT direct-connect rig; hollow chamber 100 mm ×
  90 mm, then a 200 mm Laval nozzle (inlet-to-throat area ratio 1–12;
  ratio 1 is a straight cylinder). Non-premixed slit–orifice injection: air
  through a convergent–divergent radial slit with a 0.7 mm throat, ethylene
  through 90 orifices; a sudden enlargement downstream of the injector;
  exhaust to atmosphere.
* **Initiation**: tangential H2/O2 pre-detonator (hot tube), spark-ignited;
  blowdown by nitrogen purge.
* **Metering**: sonic nozzles; turbine flow meter within 1%; air 540–560
  g/s, ethylene 12.8–47.5 g/s. Chamber and plenum pressures not reported.
* **Diagnostics**: two PCB 113B24 sensors 120° apart at one axial station
  (about 40 mm from injection, read from the sketch), 2 MHz sampling,
  high-pass filtered; FFT on one sensor. Instantaneous frequency is the
  inverse peak-to-peak interval; average speed is average frequency times
  chamber circumference; the wave count follows from the ratio of the
  inter-sensor to the same-sensor peak interval and the sensor spacing
  (Eqs. 3–5).
* **C-J speed**: "computed" at 1 atm and 300 K; code or method not stated.
* No uncertainty beyond 1% flow and 1% timing; no numerical simulation.
