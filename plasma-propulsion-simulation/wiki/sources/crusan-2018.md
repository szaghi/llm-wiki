# Summary

A programme description of NASA's X-Hab Academic Innovation Challenge,
run by the Advanced Exploration Systems (AES) division with the National
Space Grant Foundation since 2011, in which university senior and
graduate design courses build prototypes of deep-space exploration
systems. It gives the proposal and award timelines (Tables 1–2), the
internal Project Idea Form, the submission and selection criteria
(§§3.1–3.3), and the systems-engineering review gates (system definition,
preliminary design, critical design, progress checkpoint) with their
success criteria (§§3.4–3.7). §4 reports programme statistics (49
projects, 19 organizations, 15 states) and narrates example projects in
habitation, in-space manufacturing, life support (CO₂ sorbents) and plant
growth. The paper is descriptive: no models, numerics or quantitative
engineering results, and no propulsion or plasma content.

# Key claims

All claims are stated; no data are shown.

1. X-Hab began in 2011 as a head-to-head competition to build an
   expandable habitat "loft" on the Deep Space Habitat Demonstration Unit
   (Oklahoma State, Wisconsin-Madison, Maryland); Oklahoma State won and
   the loft was used in the 2011 Desert RATS analog field test in Arizona
   (§1, Fig. 1).
2. As of writing, 49 projects were selected from 19 organizations in 15
   states (§1).
3. After the first year the format changed to individual-team
   challenges; in its second year X-Hab grew to 18 projects sponsored by
   10 NASA exploration projects (§4).
4. "This past year" 11 topics were offered and 11 proposals selected from
   10 universities, 4 in habitation (§4).
5. Awards are about \$10k–\$50k each (§3, §3.1.1).
6. Teams follow a systems-engineering approach per the NASA Systems
   Engineering Handbook (SP-2007-6105), with the system definition review
   one month after kickoff, the preliminary design review two months after
   project start and the critical design review in January (§§3.2–3.6,
   Table 2).
7. Outcomes: University of Puerto Rico studies (2016) helped NASA
   In-Space Manufacturing define requirements for a future ISS metallic
   manufacturing demonstration (§4.2); University of South Alabama (2015,
   2016) built multi-component gas-absorption instruments whose data NASA
   will use in CO₂-removal simulations (§4.3); Ohio State's passive Water
   Assurance Delivery System interfaces with Veggie and influences
   next-generation seed pillows (§4.4).
8. Recent AES ISS demonstrations showed polymer additive manufacturing to
   work in microgravity similarly to the ground (§4.2), stated without
   citation.

# Methods

Programme description only. Annual cycle: request for proposals in
February, proposals in April, awards in May (Table 1); design phase in
summer, kickoff in September, system definition review in October,
preliminary design review in November, critical design review in
January, progress checkpoint in March, completion and NASA evaluation in
May (Table 2). Topics come from an internal Project Idea Form (sponsor,
scope, expected product, relevance to exploration including technology
readiness level, effort, seed funding, infusion path). Proposals are
evaluated on alignment with NASA's human exploration directorate, work
plan, ABET alignment, systems engineering in the curriculum, CAD and
analysis tools, past performance, teaming, continuity, diversity, K-12
outreach, evaluation plan and budget (§3.3). The system definition and
preliminary design reviews have 9 and 10 success criteria (§§3.4–3.5).
Deliverables: work plan and schedule, education and outreach report,
demonstration articles, technical final report (§3.8).

# Tests and results

No tests in the engineering sense. §4 narrates the 2017 topic list (seven
topics: habitation commonality; in-space polymer 3-D printer and
recycler; condensed water on Resource Prospector; wastewater to plant
nutrient; microgravity plant watering; rotating food-production system;
CO₂-removal bed optimisation test stand) and example projects: Maryland
(deployable airlock and hatch, neutral-buoyancy tests), Oklahoma State
(MPLM-based transit habitat mock-up), Pratt Institute (Mars transit and
surface habitat concept with inflatable shell and VR station), University
of Puerto Rico (low-power metal 3-D printer), University of South Alabama
(sorbent instruments), Utah State (Eden plant chamber), University of
Colorado Boulder (LabOASIS monitoring), Ohio State (passive watering and
biomass recycling).
