# Summary

An experimental study, in a Mach 2.95 suction wind tunnel, of the
near-wall wake behind a sonic transverse nitrogen jet (2 mm circular
orifice in a flat plate) for seven jet-to-crossflow momentum flux ratios
$J=2.3$–28.9. Nanoparticle planar laser scattering (NPLS) side and top
slices are combined with surface oil-flow visualisation. The near-wall
wake is divided into three regions: (I) a V-shaped separation zone
induced by leeward "collision shocks", (II) a reattachment region with
inner and outer slip lines, (III) a mixing and recovery zone. The
included angle between the V-shaped separation lines is about 33–35° and
stated to be independent of $J$; the separation length is fitted as
$x_2/d=4.582J^{0.4135}$ (Eq. 3). The paper is a short follow-up to the
same group's shock-structure study (ref. [5], Liang et al. 2018).

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Collision shocks** (§3, Figs. 4–6). At $J=20.6$ and 28.9 V-shaped
   collision shocks are visible on the NPLS slices at $y=2$ and 4 mm (more
   clearly at 4 mm), read by the authors as evidence that the flow lateral
   to the jet is supersonic even at $y=2$ mm; at $J=7.7$ they are too weak
   to identify. $J=2.3$ and 5.5 behave like 7.7, $J=11.2$ and 16.0 like
   20.6 (images not shown). Shown (visualisation); interpretation stated.
2. **Three-dimensionality** (§3, Fig. 7). In averaged NPLS images at
   $J=20.6$ the collision shocks' influence area is much larger at $y=2$ mm
   than at 4 mm. Shown qualitatively.
3. **Separation topology** (§3, Fig. 8a,b). Oil flow at $J=20.6$ shows a
   V-shaped trailing recirculation made of two oblique separation zones,
   whose separation lines end on a V-shaped slip line produced by the
   collision-shock/boundary-layer interaction; the collision shock
   intersects the shock reflected from the Mach disk (attributed to
   ref. [5]). The Fig. 8b schematic (a half-cone shock surface forming
   because the flow behind the collision is blocked by the leeward
   separation bubble) is interpretation. Shown and stated.
4. **Three-region division** (§3, Fig. 8a), proposed by this paper:
   Region I, V-shaped separation zone; Region II, reattachment with an
   "outer slip line I" along the collision shock and an "inner slip line"
   between reattached wake and lateral flow; Region III, mixing and
   recovery, where the reflected shock generates "outer slip line II".
5. **Distinct from the bow-shock separation** (§3). The V-shaped zone
   differs in mechanism and position from the parabolic separation zone of
   Glagolev et al. [25], which the authors identify with the horseshoe
   separation of the jet bow shock. Stated reinterpretation.
6. **Low-$J$ behaviour** (§3, Fig. 9). At $J=7.7$ the collision and
   reflected shocks are weaker, the outer slip line merges into the inner
   one after a short distance, and the collision/reflected-shock
   intersection moves closer to the orifice; the separation is largest at
   $J=28.9$. Shown qualitatively.
7. **Separation-line angle** (§3, Figs. 8a, 9, 11a): 34.50° ($J=28.9$),
   33.20° ($J=20.6$), 34.20° ($J=7.7$); claimed independent of $J$ because
   the leeward collision-shock angle "is only dependent upon the free-stream
   Mach number" (stated justification).
8. **Power-law correlations** (§3, Eqs. 1–3, Fig. 11b), least-squares over
   eight conditions $J=0.0$, 2.3, 5.5, 7.7, 11.2, 16.0, 20.6, 28.9:
   $x_1/d=A_1J^{B_1}$, $x_2/d=A_2J^{B_2}$ with $A_1=0.3167$, $B_1=0.7697$,
   $A_2=4.582$, $B_2=0.4135$, so $x_2/d=4.582J^{0.4135}$ (Eq. 3); $x_1$ is
   the streamwise coordinate of the separation start, $x_2$ the length to
   where oil lines stop recirculating. The form follows jet-penetration
   correlations (Rothstein–Wantuck, Gruber). The width is said to follow
   from the constant angle; no width formula is printed. The reader checked
   that Eq. 3 with $d=2$ mm gives 12.9–36.8 mm over the seven ratios,
   consistent with Fig. 11b (about 13–36 mm, approximate).
9. **Engineering use** (§3, end; stated). Fuel and crossflow do not mix
   completely in the separation zone, so the correlations help to avoid it
   in combustion organisation and serve as validation data.

# Methods

* Experiment only. Mach 2.95 suction-type tunnel, test section 400 mm
  long, 200 × 200 mm²; $P_0=101325$ Pa, $T_0=300$ K, $U_\infty\approx605$
  m/s (estimated), $Re_\delta=3.5\times10^4$ at $x=-5$ mm (§2).
* Flat plate with a 10 × 190 mm² roughness trip 10 mm behind the leading
  edge, 250 mm of plate ahead of the 2 mm orifice. Incoming boundary layer
  (Fig. 3): $\delta_{99}=6.0$ mm at $x=-5$ mm, profile from 400 PIV images
  matching a Touber prediction, taken as fully developed turbulent.
* Jet (Table 1): N₂, $M_j=1.0$, $T_{0j}=300$ K; $J=2.3$, 5.5, 7.7, 11.2,
  16.0, 20.6, 28.9 at $P_{0j}=110$, 259, 375, 525, 750, 967, 1354 kPa and
  $Re_j=6.6\times10^4$ to $8.3\times10^5$ (length scale not stated);
  $J=\rho_jV_j^2/(\rho_\infty U_\infty^2)$.
* NPLS: 4000 × 2672 px CCD, TiO₂ particles (effective diameter 42.5 nm,
  relaxation time 66.3 ns), 0.0259 mm/px side view, 0.0332 and 0.0344
  mm/px top slices; the jet is unseeded. Laser parameters not stated.
* Oil flow with pigment in an unspecified oil. Separation lines extracted
  at 95% of peak brightness and fitted by straight lines (Fig. 10);
  stated systematic uncertainty 3% on angle, 5% on length.
