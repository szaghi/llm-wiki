# Summary

Two sliding-mode controllers for a chaser approaching a free-floating
(tumbling) target: one tracks the relative position, the other
synchronises the relative attitude. Both sliding manifolds lie along the
negative gradient of an artificial potential function (APF): a
hyperbolic attractive potential (after Badawy and McInnes, the paper's
ref. [40]) plus a repulsive potential (after Hu et al., ref. [34])
active only inside an avoidance zone of radius $\delta_0$ and divergent
at the danger-zone radius $d_0$, which gives collision avoidance. A
Lyapunov argument with the finite-time lemma of Bhat and Bernstein
(ref. [42]) shows that the sliding surface is reached in finite time
and that on it the system converges to the desired position and
attitude under bounded disturbances. Two simulations, a translation
manoeuvre with a 20 kg chaser and a rotation manoeuvre with a full
inertia matrix, reach their targets without the relative distance
falling below the danger-zone radius. No integrator, time step or
software is stated.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Position law** (Theorem 1, Eqs. 20, 27–31, proved). With sliding
   surface $s=\dot\rho+\alpha\nabla_\rho U_1$ and
   $u_f=-A_1\rho-A_2v-m_c\alpha\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla_\rho U_{att})-(\gamma_1+m_c\gamma_3)\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)-p\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)$,
   the function $V=\tfrac12s^Tm_cs$ satisfies
   $\dot V\le-\frac{\sqrt2\min\{p\}}{\sqrt{m_c}}V^{1/2}$, so the surface
   is reached in finite time,
   $T_{reach}\le V(x_0)^{1-\zeta}/(c(1-\zeta))$ (Lemma 1).
2. **Convergence on the manifold** (Eqs. 32–33). On $s=0$,
   $\dot U_1=-\alpha\|\nabla_\rho U_1\|^2$; since the unique stable
   equilibrium of $U_1$ is at $r_d$, $\rho\to r_d$ and $v\to0$.
3. **Collision avoidance** (Remark 3, argued). Because $U_1$ stays bounded
   while $U_{rep}$ diverges as $\|\rho-\rho_0\|\to d_0$,
   $\|\rho-\rho_0\|>d_0$ for all time. This is stated in a remark, not a
   theorem.
4. **Local minima** (Remark 2, stated without proof). The combined
   potential is axisymmetric about the desired-position direction, so it
   has only two equilibria on that axis, a global stable point at $r_d$
   and an unstable saddle; arrival is claimed to be guaranteed.
5. **Bounded approach speed** (Eq. 15).
   $\|\nabla_\rho U_{att}\|=C_h\sqrt{\|\rho-r_d\|^2/(\|\rho-r_d\|^2+1)}\le C_h$.
6. **Switching repulsive term** (Eqs. 24–26). The derivative of
   $\nabla_\rho U_{rep}$ is unbounded when the switch $k$ changes; it is
   handled by the equivalent-control method with a low-pass filter
   $\mu\dot z=z+\alpha\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla_\rho U_{rep})$, $\mu$ small,
   whose output is assumed bounded, $\|\dot z\|\le\gamma_3$ ("to be
   designed"). The bound is an assumption, not a result.
7. **Attitude law** (Theorem 2, Eqs. 35–41, proved).
   $u_\tau=N^*-C^*(\beta\nabla_{e_v}U_2)-J^*\frac{d}{dt}(\beta\nabla_{e_v}U_2)-\gamma_2\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)-m\,\mathrm{sgn}(s)$
   with $s=\dot e_v+\beta\nabla_{e_v}U_2$ and
   $U_2=C_h\sqrt{\|e_v\|^2+1}-1$; $V=\tfrac12s^TJ^*s$ gives
   $\dot V\le-\frac{\sqrt2\min\{m\}}{\sqrt{j_2}}V^{1/2}$, then
   $e_v,\dot e_v\to0$. The attitude law has no repulsive term.
8. **Chattering** (Remark 4, Eq. 34). The sign function is replaced by a
   saturation with boundary-layer width $\eta$.

# Methods

- **Frames** (§2): Earth-centred inertial, target orbital frame and
  principal-axis body frames of target and chaser; rotations as direction
  cosine matrices, $S(\cdot)$ the cross-product matrix.
- **Translation** (Eqs. 1–4): nonlinear relative motion in the target
  body frame,
  $m_c\ddot\rho+2m_cS(\omega)\dot\rho+m_c(S^2(\omega)+S(\dot\omega))\rho-g(r_c,r_t)-d_f=u_f$,
  with differential gravity and disturbance lumped and bounded by
  $\gamma_1$ (Assumption 1).
- **Attitude** (Eqs. 5–10): unit-quaternion kinematics and Euler equations
  (after Junkins and Schaub, ref. [38]); torque-free target; relative
  attitude $e=q_t^*\otimes q_c$; Euler–Lagrange-like form
  $J^*\ddot e_v+C^*\dot e_v+N^*=u_\tau+d_\tau$ (after Costic et al.,
  ref. [39]) with skew-symmetry of $\dot J^*-2C^*$, bounds $j_1$, $j_2$
  on $J^*$ and $\|d_\tau\|\le\gamma_2$ (Assumption 2).
- **APF** (§3.1, Eqs. 13–19): attractive
  $U_{att}=C_h\sqrt{\|\rho-r_d\|^2+1}-1$; repulsive
  $U_{rep}=k\,(\|\rho-\rho_0\|^2-\delta_0^2)^2/(\|\rho-\rho_0\|^2-d_0^2)^2$
  with $k=0$ outside the avoidance zone and 1 inside; for the position
  problem the obstacle is the target, $\rho_0=0$.

# Tests and results

- **Set-up** (§4.1, Table 1): target inertia diag(50, 70, 100) kg·m²;
  initial target attitude quaternion [1, 0, 0, 0] and rate
  [0.05, −0.06, 0.02] rad/s; target orbit with semi-major axis
  7178160 m, eccentricity 0.001, inclination 30°, ascending node 60°,
  argument of perigee 0°, initial true anomaly 0°. Fig. 3 illustrates the
  potential with $r_d=\rho_0=0$, $d_0=1.5$ m, $\delta_0=2$ m, $C_h=1$.
- **Translation** (§4.2): $r_d=[2,0,0]^T$ m, $\rho(0)=[-20,12,-7]^T$ m,
  $v(0)=[0.5,-0.7,1]^T$ m/s, $m_c=20$ kg; $C_h=0.5$, $\alpha=0.5$,
  $p=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,0.5,0.5)$, $\gamma_1=0.02$, $\gamma_3=0.05$,
  $\eta=5\times10^{-3}$; $\delta_0=2$ m, $d_0=1.9$ m; disturbance force
  $d_f=[0.01+0.02\sin(\omega_ot),\ 0.02-0.025\sin(\omega_ot+0.1),\ 0.01+0.03\sin(\omega_ot+0.5)]^T$ N,
  $\omega_o$ the target's mean orbital rate (gravitational parameter
  $3.986004\times10^{14}$ m³/s²). The distance always stays above the danger-zone radius
  (Fig. 4a) and $\rho\to r_d$, $v\to0$ (Figs. 4b, 5). The chaser enters
  the avoidance zone at about 89 s, where velocity, distance and sliding
  surface change sharply, and leaves it after about 92 s (Figs. 4–6). The
  control force is small at the start, attributed to the hyperbolic
  potential (Fig. 7).
- **Rotation** (§4.3): chaser inertia with rows (40, −12, 20), (−12, 80, 10),
  (20, 10, 50) kg·m²; $e(0)=[0.8,0.3464,0.3464,0.3464]^T$ (a unit
  quaternion), $\dot e_v(0)=[0.05,0.3,-0.1]^T$; disturbance torque
  $[0.001+0.002\sin(\omega_ot),\ 0.002-0.0025\sin(\omega_ot+0.5),\ 0.001+0.003\cos(\omega_ot+0.2)]^T$ N·m; $C_h=0.3$, $\beta=0.5$,
  $\gamma_2=0.005$, $m=\mathrm{diag}(0.3,0.3,0.3)$,
  $\eta=5\times10^{-4}$. Attitude synchronisation is reached (Fig. 8), the
  sliding surface reaches zero in finite time (Fig. 9); control torques
  in Fig. 10.
- No settling time or numerical accuracy is reported for either case.
