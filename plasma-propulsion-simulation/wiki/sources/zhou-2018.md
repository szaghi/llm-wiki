# Summary

The authors run hydrogen–air rotating detonation experiments in an annular
rotating detonation combustor (RDC; inner and outer diameters 94 and
106 mm) that discharges through a convergent section and a turbine guide
vane into an axial-flow turbine (outer diameter 66 mm), so that the RDC
drives a real rotating turbine in an open cycle. Two ignition devices are
compared, an automotive spark plug (test 1) and an H2/O2 pre-detonator
(test 2), at air mass flow 170 g/s and equivalence ratio 0.6–1.4. In both
cases a single stable rotating detonation wave forms, after a "formation
process" lasting on average about 0.159 s with the spark plug and 0.022 s
with the pre-detonator; the final state does not depend on the igniter.
With spark ignition the formation goes through two co-rotating waves, a
"two-peak" (bifurcated) wave, strong–weak alternation and finally one
stable wave. Measured wave speeds are about 20% below the CEA
Chapman–Jouguet (C-J) values.

# Key claims

1. **Stable single wave with a turbine** (§3.1, §3.2, Fig. 6; Conclusion
   1). A stable single hydrogen–air wave is sustained at every tested
   equivalence ratio except 0.6, with either igniter. Shown.
2. **Speed from frequency** (§3.1, Eq. 1). Average speed = π × outer
   diameter (106 mm) × FFT dominant frequency / number of waves; a standard
   kinematic relation.
3. **Composite operation** (§3.1, Fig. 3). At equivalence ratio 1.17 the
   dominant frequency 4.79 kHz with one wave gives 1.59 km/s; the head-end
   trace shows a mean of about 6 bar (0.6 MPa) with strong disturbances
   behind the peaks. Shown.
4. **Reflected shocks** (§3.1, Fig. 3d). Shocks are present in front of
   the guide vane; part of the oblique shock reflects back into the
   combustor from the concave blade surfaces (incidence about 25°), stated
   to have minor influence. A 64% pressure reduction across the vane and
   0.4 bar at its exit are attributed to the authors' earlier paper.
5. **Igniter sets the formation time** (§3.2, Figs. 4–5). At equivalence
   ratio 1: spark plug about 0.15 s, stable mean 4.73 kHz (1.57 km/s);
   pre-detonator 0.02 s, 4.7 kHz (1.56 km/s). The final state is
   independent of the igniter, in agreement with prior work. Shown.
6. **Formation time against equivalence ratio** (§3.2, Fig. 6). It falls
   as equivalence ratio rises; means 0.159 s (test 1) and 0.022 s (test 2);
   runs at 0.6 never stabilised (plotted at 0.4 s). Read from the figure
   (approximate): test 1 from about 0.21 s at 0.68 to about 0.12–0.13 s at
   1.33–1.41; test 2 from about 0.05 s to under 0.01 s. Shown.
7. **Turbine slows formation** (§3.2). The 0.15 s is compared with 2.23 ms
   in the authors' earlier turbine-free RDC (a different rig) to infer
   "relatively large effects of turbine", attributed to turbine spin-up;
   the steady turbine speed is 12,000 rpm (200 Hz) against a wave frequency
   of about 4.5 kHz. Hypothesis: no turbine-free control in this paper and
   no turbine-speed history.
8. **Spark-ignited mode sequence** (§3.3, Fig. 7). Phase 1: two
   co-rotating waves at 7.76 kHz (7.67 kHz on the Fig. 7a label), sensors
   90° apart showing about 180° phase. Phase 2, after about 20 ms: the
   peaks bifurcate into a "two-peak wave", still two waves, at 7.53 kHz
   (speculated as a split flame coupled with shocks). Phase 3: strong–weak
   alternation (one strong wave followed by two weaker ones) for about
   0.1 s at 4.91 kHz, one wave. One stable wave at 0.148 s. Shown.
9. **Reaction-zone postposition** (§3.3, Figs. 8–9). Just before the
   stable state the first sensor reads below the second for about 5 ms,
   interpreted as the reaction zone moving backward. Trace shown,
   interpretation stated.
10. **Repeatability** (§3.4, Fig. 10). Nine runs at equivalence ratio 0.85
    give 1.5–1.55 km/s. Shown.
11. **Velocity deficit** (§3.4, Fig. 11). Speed rises with equivalence
    ratio, about 1.32 km/s at 0.6 to about 1.65 km/s at 1.4 (read from the
    figure, approximate), against CEA C-J speeds of about 1.70–2.07 km/s
    and an isobaric sound speed of about 0.97–1.16 km/s. A deficit of about
    20% is stated and attributed, without proof, mainly to engine structure
    and reactant mixing.

# Methods

* **Rig** (§2, Fig. 1): H2 and air supplies, annular RDC (6 mm gap),
  convergent section, guide vane, axial-flow turbine with output shaft;
  axial lengths not given.
* **Injection**: slot–orifice; H2 through 120 orifices of 0.8 mm, air
  through a 1.5 mm annular slot; sonic-nozzle metering; reactants and
  ambient at 282 K, 1 atm; dry air composition stated.
* **Operation**: air 170 g/s, equivalence ratio 0.6–1.4, 0.4 s supply,
  about 0.05 s tail on residual plenum gas. Pre-detonator: H2/O2, 2 g/s,
  stoichiometric, in the same location as the spark plug.
* **Diagnostics**: three piezoresistive sensors (0.1% full scale) on the
  vane inlet and the two plenums; two PCB sensors at 550 kHz on the outer
  wall, 8 mm apart axially and 90° apart circumferentially; one PCB sensor
  before the vane; one ion probe. NI X-series acquisition.
* **Reduction**: FFT dominant frequency, per-cycle frequency from peak
  intervals, Eq. 1; C-J speed and isobaric sound speed from NASA CEA.
  Fig. 11 error bars undefined. No numerical simulation.
