# Summary

The paper first argues, from inviscid parent-flowfield computations at
freestream Mach 4 and mean exit Mach 1.4 (§2.3, Tables 1–2, Fig. 5), that
an inward-turning ("wavecatcher") compression built on the authors'
Internal Conical Flow C (ICFC, an internal conical flow A coupled with a
truncated Busemann flow) beats a 2-D multi-wedge compression in
total-pressure recovery (+28.0%), compression length (−5%), wetted area
(−15.7%) and pressure drag (−12.1%). It then argues qualitatively (§2.4,
Figs. 7–8) that the price is a harder shock-wave/boundary-layer
interaction. A section-controllable wavecatcher intake (design Mach 4,
osculating-axisymmetric design, contraction ratio about 5.96, internal
about 2.18, 2920 mm long) is simulated by steady RANS (k–ε) with and
without a 20 mm streamwise bleed slot at the reflected-shock impingement
(§3). Bleeding 2.528% of the captured mass removes the throat-region weak
normal shock and spanwise vortices, raises the throat recovery from 0.740
to 0.764, and raises the maximum sustainable back-pressure ratio from 73.4
to 76.7 with exit recovery 0.509 → 0.528 (Tables 4–5, Figs. 18–20); it
does not move the terminal shock at a given back pressure (Fig. 18).

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Parent-flow comparison** (§2.3.2, Table 1, Fig. 5; inviscid, own
   computation), wedge / isentropic / Busemann / ICFC: $\sigma$ 0.683 /
   0.740 / 0.984 / 0.874 (ICFC +28.0% vs wedge); $\pi$ 32.6 / 34.5 / 44.7 /
   40.5; CR 6.58 / 6.99 / 9.10 / 7.39; $L/H_0$ 3.10 / 4.17 / 4.38 / 2.94;
   $H_e/H_0$ 0.152 / 0.143 / 0.331 / 0.368; $L/H_e$ 20.4 / 29.3 / 13.2 /
   8.0; $A_{wet}/A_{capture}$ 4.72 / 5.73 / 5.34 / 3.98. Busemann has the
   highest recovery; the advantage claims compare ICFC with the wedge.
2. **One-dimensional relations** (§2.3.2, Eqs. 1–2, stated):
   $\sigma=P_e^*/P_0^*=CR\,q(M_0)/q(M_e)$ and
   $\pi=p_e/p_0=CR\,\frac{q(M_0)}{q(M_e)}\left(\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_0^2}{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_e^2}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$,
   so at fixed entrance and exit Mach numbers both depend only on CR.
3. **Pressure drag** (§2.3.4, Eq. 3, Table 2): $C_p=\sum P\,A_S/(0.5\rho_0V_0^2A_{capture})$;
   multi-wedge 0.430, isentropic 0.423, Busemann 0.401, ICFC 0.378
   (−12.1%). Pressure drag stated to be 70–80% of intake drag (attributed).
4. **Spillage** (§2.3.1, Figs. 3–4; qualitative): the enclosed section
   prevents lateral spillage at design; the 3-D intake's mass capture at
   Mach 4 is 0.997 (Table 4; 0.992 in §3.4).
5. **Harder interaction** (§2.4, Figs. 6–8; qualitative): no
   constant-pressure plateau ahead of the reflected shock, pressure there
   about twice the 2-D value, a "much more shrunken" boundary-layer profile
   at the shoulder (k–ε), strongest streamwise pressure gradient near the
   centreline; the vortex mechanism is attributed to refs. [37, 38].
6. **Ramjet design tension** (§2.2, stated): osculating planes cannot all
   keep one contraction ratio, so throat Mach number is non-uniform and
   some planes approach Mach 1; the design throat Mach must be raised,
   increasing terminal-shock loss (scramjet throats typically Mach 3.0 for a
   Mach 6 intake).
7. **Bleed and flow structure** (§3.3, Figs. 14, 16, 17; shown): without
   bleed the reflected shock hits the shoulder, re-reflects and induces a
   weak normal shock near the throat; with bleed the boundary layer thins,
   the normal shock disappears and the spanwise vortices (Q-criterion,
   $Q=2\times10^7$) are removed. Throat recovery 0.740 → 0.764, throat Mach
   1.461 → 1.622.
8. **Back-pressure characteristic** (§3.4, Tables 4–5, Figs. 18–20;
   shown): no bleed sustains $\pi_e=73.4$ ($\sigma_e=0.509$, $M_e=0.257$);
   with bleed up to 76.7 ($\sigma_e=0.528$, $M_e=0.237$, bleed 2.579%),
   "+3.73%". At equal back pressure the non-dimensional terminal-shock
   position is unchanged (Fig. 18). The unstart limit is not determined.
9. **Slot sizing** (§3.3, stated, attributed to Schulte et al.): slot
   length between one and two local boundary-layer thicknesses; here the
   undisturbed thickness at impingement is about 18.1 mm, so 20 mm; placed
   where the Prandtl–Meyer angle is below 45°. Hamed et al. (attributed):
   slot width at least the incoming boundary-layer thickness, removing
   20–30% of its mass flow, eliminates turbulent separation.
10. **Design method** (§2.1, Fig. 2, stated, details in ref. [31]):
    osculating-axisymmetric theory reduces the 3-D design to osculating-plane
    designs, each streamline-traced in an ICFC parent flow; Mölder (1967) is
    credited with showing that only four types of internal axisymmetric
    conical flows with straight shocks exist.

# Methods

* **Parent flows** (§2.3): inviscid, method not stated; Mach 4, exit Mach
  1.4, equal capture area; boundary-layer profiles of Fig. 7 with k–ε.
* **Intake geometry** (§3.1.1, Figs. 9–10, 15): length 2920 mm; capture
  area 88460 mm² (three straight lines and an ellipse, width 450.6 mm);
  throat area 14851 mm² (two ellipses); CR ≈ 5.96, CRI ≈ 2.18 (section at
  cowl closure 32381 mm²); divergent segment 1800 mm, exit diameter 240 mm,
  equivalent expansion angle 1.6°; shoulder smoothing and
  displacement-thickness correction. The reader checked these ratios.
* **RANS** (§3.1.2): steady compressible finite volume, calorically perfect
  gas, standard k–ε, "high resolution" advection (order and code not
  stated); half domain, structured mesh, cylindrical far field about 5
  entrance diameters; first cell 0.1 mm, $y^+\approx30$–60; adiabatic
  no-slip walls; freestream 220.6 K, Mach 4.0, 2930.7 Pa; converged when
  mass flow, recovery and exit Mach vary by less than 0.05%.
* **Grid study** (Table 3): 3.5, 5.21, 6.52 M cells give exit recovery
  0.621 / 0.622 / 0.622, exit Mach 2.642 / 2.644 / 2.644, pressure ratio
  3.958 / 3.956 / 3.956; the 5.21 M grid is used.
* **Validation**: stated only, through ref. [41] (wind-tunnel comparison
  on a wavecatcher intake); none shown here.
* **Bleed**: 20 mm slot on the upper wall; bleed ratio is an output; the
  bleed-exit boundary condition is not stated.
* **Vortex identification** (Eq. 4): $Q=\tfrac12(\|\Omega\|^2-\|S\|^2)$,
  after Jeong & Hussain.
