# Summary

The paper simulates the plume of the LIPS-200 xenon ion thruster striking a
300 mm aluminium plate with [EX-PWS](/software/ex-pws.md), Beihang
University's 3D hybrid PIC-DSMC code on unstructured tetrahedral grids.
Heavy species are macroparticles with [DSMC](/methods/dsmc.md) elastic and
[charge-exchange](/definitions/charge-exchange-ions.md) collisions; the
facility gas is a field of untracked temporary particles; electrons are a
quasi-neutral isothermal [Boltzmann fluid](/methods/boltzmann-electron-model.md).
The plate reflects with species-dependent accommodation coefficients
(Corey et al.) and sputters. The computed axial plume force at 0.6–1.0 m is
compared with a torsional micro-thrust balance in Beihang's PES chamber.
A vacuum case then maps how the plate perturbs the plume.

# Key claims

1. **Collisions** (§2.1, eqs. 1–6): Xe–Xe, Xe$^+$–Xe, Xe$^{2+}$–Xe and
   CEX$^+$–Xe elastic; single and double charge exchange, by DSMC (Bird).
   Cross sections are in the group's earlier paper (ref. 16). Triply and
   more highly charged ions are neglected, stated without demonstration to
   have no effect on accuracy.
2. **Background gas** (§2.1): temporary neutrals created in every cell at
   every iteration at uniform facility temperature and pressure, not
   tracked; only the simulated particle's velocity changes in a collision.
   $2\times10^{-3}$ Pa in the force cases, zero in §4.2.
3. **Electrons** (§2.1, eqs. 7–9): collisionless, unmagnetized,
   quasi-neutral momentum balance $en_e\mathbf E=-\nabla p_e$ with
   $p_e=n_eT_e$, giving the Boltzmann relation with reference density
   $1\times10^{16}$ m$^{-3}$ at $\phi=0$ and $T_e=3.5$ eV (attributed to
   Boyd).
4. **Surface** (§2.2, eqs. 10–14, Table 1): Maxwell reflection; normal and
   tangential momentum accommodation; Corey et al. angular formula for the
   normal coefficient; Table 1 values (normal-incidence normal coefficient,
   Corey constant, tangential coefficient): Xe 0.90, 0.846, 0.80; Xe$^+$
   0.96, 0.846, 0.90; Xe$^{2+}$ 0.97, 0.846, 0.95. Sticking coefficient 0,
   justified by the observed sputter erosion. Boundaries at potential 0 and
   300 K; sheaths ignored.
5. **Plate force** (§2.2, eq. 15, Table 2) = incident + reflected + sputtered
   momentum fluxes, sputtering by Yamamura's model (Xe on Al, sublimation
   energy 3.39 eV, threshold 69.20 eV normal and 72.25 eV oblique).
6. **Force validation** (§4.1, Fig. 4; shown): simulation generally above
   experiment, maximum relative error 16.36%; balance error 1.45% (§3).
   From the figure (approximate): simulation about 24.7 → 13.6 mN from 0.6 to
   1.0 m, experiment about 22.3 → 14.2 mN from 0.6 to 0.9 m.
7. **Momentum shares** (§4.1, Table 3): Xe$^+$ about 85%, Xe$^{2+}$ about
   13.6%, Xe 1.22–1.85%, CEX ions below 0.1%.
8. **Sticking sensitivity** (§4.1, Fig. 5): sticking coefficient 1 lowers the
   resultant force by about 0.1–0.25% and shifts the Xe$^+$ and Xe
   momentum ratios by up to about 1.25% (read from the figure).
9. **Plate perturbation, vacuum** (§4.2, Figs. 6–14): ion density behind the
   plate drops "around five magnitudes", from $10^{14}$ to $10^9$ m$^{-3}$ at
   1.0 m and $|y|<0.1$ m, with re-convergence on axis to about $10^{11}$
   m$^{-3}$ at 1.5 m; potential negative everywhere, maximum drop "only tens
   of volts" against about 1000 eV beam ions, with a "wings-shaped"
   structure from ion–atom elastic collisions; neutral density up to
   $5\times10^{16}$ m$^{-3}$ in front of the plate; the CEX wing is enlarged;
   higher-energy CEX ions form in the annular wing within about 0.5 m of the
   exit.
10. **Recommendation** (§5, stated): avoid plates or shelters downstream of
    the thruster.

# Models and numerics

* Hybrid particle–fluid: PIC push of ions in the field from the Boltzmann
  relation, DSMC collisions, no Poisson solve, no magnetic field. See
  [hybrid PIC–fluid models](/methods/hybrid-pic-fluid-model.md).
* Domain: cylinder 2.0 m long and 2.0 m in diameter, 1.5 m downstream and
  0.5 m upstream of the exit; neutralizer, chamber walls and mountings
  neglected; outer boundaries free outflow.
* Grid: unstructured tetrahedra, cells larger than $\lambda_D$ (about
  $10^{-5}$ m) and of order $\lambda_{\mathrm{mfp}}$ (about 0.1 m); 1, 2 and
  4 million cells tested, about 2 million used (§2.3, Fig. 2).
  $\Delta t=1\times10^{-7}$ s; $W=2\times10^8$; particles per cell, run
  times and processor count not stated. Parallel partitions are mentioned;
  MPI is implied, not stated.
* Inflow: Gaussian flux profiles from ref. 16, validated there against
  Faraday probes. LIPS-200: 200 mm diameter, $40\pm4$ mN, $I_{sp}$
  $3000\pm300$ s.

# Tests and results

* Grid convergence (§2.3, Fig. 2): axial current density on the axis
  coincides for the three grids beyond 0.3 m.
* Experiment (§3, Fig. 3): PES chamber 5.2 m × 12.6 m, about $2\times10^{-3}$
  Pa with the thruster on; fully elastic torsional balance with a 300 mm ×
  1 mm aluminium target and carbon anti-sputtering cover; strain gauge.
* Results: Key claims 6–9.
