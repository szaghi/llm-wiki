# Summary

The paper tests the single-region pseudo-2D PIC scheme that the same group
introduced in Faraji, Reza & Knoll (2022): the 2D Poisson equation is
replaced by two decoupled 1D Poisson equations on region-averaged charge
densities, and the potential is a weighted sum of the two 1D potentials.
The scheme is applied in all three Hall-thruster planes and compared with
1D PIC and with a 2D reference: the Charoy et al. (2019) benchmark
(axial–azimuthal), Croes' 2017 2D PIC (azimuthal–radial) and the Panelli
et al. (2021) 2D hybrid model of an SPT-100 (axial–radial). It resolves
instability-driven electron transport in the axial–azimuthal plane without
a Bohm model; reproduces the coupling between the azimuthal instability
and the secondary-electron-emission sheath in the azimuthal–radial plane;
and in the axial–radial plane improves $T_e$, ionization and $n_i$
profiles but produces an unphysically large anode-sheath drop. No new
formulation is given, and the cost saving is restated from the original
paper, not measured.

# Key claims

1. **Decomposition** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–7). Potential = weighted sum of a
   function of $x$ and a function of $y$ at the intersection of a
   horizontal and a vertical region; each obeys a 1D Poisson equation with
   the region-averaged charge density (Eqs. 6–7) as source; solved with the
   Thomas algorithm. Weights 0.5 throughout; the original study
   (axial–azimuthal only) found results "almost invariant" if the vertical
   weight is at least the horizontal one; equal weights adopted by analogy
   in the other planes. The authors call the formulation "preliminary" and
   lacking "a rigorous mathematical framework" (§1, §6).
2. **Cost** (§2.1, §3.2). "Up to two orders of magnitude reduction in the
   computational cost", full-size thrusters "without any speed-up factor".
   Stated, carried over from Faraji et al. (2022); no timings here.
3. **Axial–azimuthal accuracy** (§3.2, Fig. 3). Time-averaged $E_x$, $n_i$,
   $\phi$, $T_e$ "consistent with" the 2D benchmark and similar to a 1D
   axial run with a tuned Bohm profile; a 1D run without anomalous
   collisions cannot sustain the potential (about 30 V by $x\approx0.5$ cm).
   Shown, no error metric. Reader's plot readings (approximate): peak $E_x$
   ≈4.8×10⁴ V/m at ≈1.0 cm (2D: ≈5×10⁴ V/m at ≈0.85 cm); peak $n_i$
   ≈4.7×10¹⁷ m⁻³ (2D ≈3.6×10¹⁷); peak $T_e$ ≈49 eV (2D ≈54); plume $T_e$
   at 2.5 cm ≈18 eV (2D ≈12).
4. **Azimuthal waves** (§3.2, Fig. 5, Eq. 8). The spectrum follows the
   ion-acoustic relation in the ion frame, $\omega\approx\pm k_\theta
   c_s/\sqrt{1+k_\theta^2\lambda_D^2}$ (attributed); waves at ≈3 MHz and
   ≈2 mm; dominant mode near $k_\theta\lambda_D\approx0.25$–0.3,
   $\omega/\omega_{pi}\approx0.3$ (read from Fig. 5b). Shown.
5. **Electron azimuthal momentum budget** (§3.2, Fig. 6). The magnetic-force
   term is "remarkably similar" to the 2D reference; downstream of the
   field peak nearly all transport comes from the electric (wave) force;
   force densities up to about 7 N m⁻³. Shown.
6. **Bohm tuning with an accelerated PIC** (§3.1–§3.2, Figs. 2, 4). A
   three-zone $\nu_{\mathrm{ano}}$ equal to $\omega_{ce}$ divided by the paper's anomalous Hall parameter, 32
   ($0<x\le0.5$ cm), 300 ($0.5<x\le1.2$ cm), 10 ($1.2<x\le2.5$ cm), i.e.
   $\alpha_{\mathrm B}=16/(\text{that parameter})$ = 0.5, 0.053, 1.6, tuned with a 1D run
   with $f_m=100$ and $f_\varepsilon=20$ (≈300,000 steps against 4,000,000).
   The same coefficients in a real-scale 1D run reproduce the 2D profiles;
   the accelerated run shows "clear discrepancies" (reader's readings: peak
   $E_x$ ≈2.2×10⁴ against ≈5×10⁴ V/m, peak $T_e$ ≈78 against ≈54 eV).
   Shown; read by the authors as scaling altering the resolved physics.
7. **Wall-module verification** (§4.1, Table 1, Eq. 9, Fig. 7). A 1D
   radial source–collector sheath with constant SEE yield matches the
   sheath drop $\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{sh}}=T_{e}\ln[(1-\delta_{\mathrm{SEE}})\sqrt{m_i/(2\pi m_e)}]$
   ($T_e$ the parallel temperature, in eV) for yields 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.99;
   above the xenon critical yield 0.983 the sheath is space-charge
   saturated. Steady state after ≈2 µs. Vaughan model verification on the
   Sydorenko mini-benchmark and Schwager–Birdsall validation stated, not
   shown.
8. **Azimuthal–radial coupling** (§4.3.1, Figs. 8–9). The instability heats
   electrons, the yield passes the saturation threshold at ≈1 µs, a burst
   of near-wall electrons reaches the bulk at ≈4–7 µs and quenches $T_e$,
   then the sheath stabilizes in the saturated regime with yield slightly
   above 1. A 1D radial run stays stable. Shown (text only; figures missing
   in the version read).
9. **Radial physics damps the instability** (§4.3.2, Eqs. 11–13, Figs.
   10–11). Simulated cross-field mobility agrees with the
   instability-enhanced mobility of Lafleur et al. (2016) (classical
   $\mu_\perp$ times one minus $\Omega_e$ times the normalized correlation of
   density and azimuthal-field fluctuations); the 1D azimuthal run gives
   higher energy, mobility and wave energy; the gap between whole-radius and
   central mobility shows near-wall conductivity; SEE lowers electron energy.
   The ≈0.7 µs oscillations are flagged as an artefact of the finite axial
   length and reinjection (ion transit ≈1.1 µs). Shown.
10. **Axial–radial** (§5.2, Figs. 12–14). With Vaughan SEE resolved, $T_e$,
    ionization rate and $n_i$ agree better with the 2D hybrid reference than
    1D runs with or without an ad-hoc wall-collision model; but the anode
    sheath drop is unrealistically high and $E_x$ is overpredicted,
    attributed to wall physics driven by axially averaged bulk conditions
    (near-anode electron drift ≈−6×10⁵ m/s against ≈−1.7×10⁵ m/s in 1D, read
    from Fig. 13c). Mean ion exit velocity matches 1D. All runs share a
    two-zone Bohm model, so agreement was "expected" (§5). Shown.
11. **Conclusion** (§6). The scheme captures multi-dimensional coupling
    "closely similar" to full-2D in all three planes; single-region is not
    the best choice for the axial–radial plane. Stated.

# Models and numerics

* Code: the Imperial Plasma Propulsion Laboratory PIC code, Julia,
  Xoshiro256++ random numbers; explicit electrostatic; earlier verified on
  the Turner et al. (2013) capacitive discharge benchmark and a 1D
  azimuthal case of Lafleur et al. (2016).
* Electrons and ions kinetic; neutrals a fixed background
  (3.22×10¹⁹ m⁻³) in the azimuthal–radial case, kinetic in the
  axial–radial case; imposed cosine ionization in the axial–azimuthal case.
* Linear (CIC) weighting; leap-frog with Boris rotation for electrons;
  Box–Muller sampling. MCC with Biagi-v7.1 cross sections (ionization, four
  excitations, elastic); null collision not mentioned.
* Anomalous transport: none in the axial–azimuthal pseudo-2D run;
  three-zone Bohm-type collision (fictitious MCC event) in the 1D axial
  comparisons; two-zone Bohm-type with $\alpha_{\mathrm B}$ = 0.035
  (channel) and 5.0 (near plume) in all axial–radial runs.
* Walls: isotropic, diffuse or specular reflection; ion recombination; SEE
  constant; linear for boron nitride (Eq. 10, printed parameters: maximum
  yield 2.9, $\gamma_0=0.578$, $\omega^*=35.04$ eV; secondaries at 1 eV); or
  Monte Carlo Vaughan (half-Maxwellian at 2 eV, diffuse). Floating wall in
  §4.1, grounded walls in §4.2. Ad-hoc 1D wall collision (Hagelaar et al.
  2002, Eq. 14) as a fictitious MCC event: $\nu_w$ equal to 0.1 times a
  reference frequency "typically $10^7$ s⁻¹", with an energy loss per event
  set by a 20 eV parameter (Eq. 15).
* Azimuthal–radial set-up (Table 2): $\Delta t=4\times10^{-12}$ s, 12 µs;
  0.5 cm azimuthal × 2.0 cm radial, virtual axial length 1.0 cm; cells
  20 µm; 200/50 particles per cell in the two region grids; $n_0=3\times10^{17}$ m⁻³;
  $T_e$ 5 eV, $T_i$ 0.1 eV; axial field 2×10⁴ V/m; radial field 200 G;
  grounded walls; pair injection to compensate wall losses.
* Axial–radial set-up (Table 3): 3.0 cm axial (6 cm in the reference) ×
  1.5 cm radial, 20 µm cells, $\Delta t=2\times10^{-12}$ s, 30 µs; 35/65
  particles per cell; 300 V, 5 mg/s; peak radial field 16 mT; anode 750 K,
  walls 850 K.
* No scaling in the pseudo-2D runs. Weights, parallelization and run times
  not stated.
