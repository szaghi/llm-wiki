# Summary

A numerical study (no new experiment). Aleatory uncertainty in five
inputs, freestream Mach number, unit Reynolds number, angle of attack,
freestream temperature and wall temperature, is propagated through 3-D
RANS simulations with the SST k–ω model of the two-ramp hypersonic
inlet/isolator of Li et al. [42], at a baseline Mach 5.9. The baseline
lies inside the inlet's Mach-number hysteresis loop, so the started and
the unstarted flow are analysed at the same freestream condition. A
point-collocation non-intrusive polynomial chaos (NIPC) surrogate
(Legendre basis, optimal Latin hypercube sampling, oversampling ratio 2)
gives means, 95% intervals and Sobol' indices for the pressure-coefficient
field and for three isolator performance parameters at $x=316$ mm:
normalised mass capture rate, exit Mach number and total-pressure
recovery. Mach number and angle of attack dominate the pressure
uncertainty in nearly complementary regions; performance uncertainty in
the started flow is about twice that in the unstarted flow; Mach number
dominates the total-pressure-recovery uncertainty; wall temperature is
negligible. A grid study with a cell-Reynolds-number criterion and an
error-accumulation reliability check (Smirnov's $R_s$) support the runs.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map). Claims 1–6, 8–10 are
shown by the paper's own simulations.

1. **Hysteresis** (§4.5, Fig. 8). Varying $Ma_\infty$ between 3.3 and 7
   gives different unstart and restart paths for mass capture rate and
   $\sigma_p$; on restart a first step-up at $Ma_\infty=5$ does not start
   the inlet because an entrance separation bubble remains. $Ma_\infty=5.9$
   is chosen because both modes exist there, far from the critical Mach
   numbers.
2. **Initial-condition independence** (§4.5, Figs. 9–10). Two routes to
   Mach 5.9 (7→5.9 and 7→6.5→5.9) give identical wall $C_p$; the density
   residual drops by 9–10 orders of magnitude.
3. **Pressure-coefficient intervals** (§5.2, Figs. 14–16). Started flow:
   shock regions carry 95%-interval widths above 1.0. Unstarted flow: large
   widths at the separation-induced shock, just behind the cowl lip and at
   the isolator impingements; reflected shocks in the unstarted isolator
   have much smaller widths, attributed (interpretation) to weaker
   reflected shocks and to the entrance bubble. Downstream, large widths
   decrease and small ones grow, called a "cumulative effect of
   uncertainty due to the multiple reflections of shock waves".
4. **Performance parameters at $x=316$ mm** (§5.2, Table 6; baseline /
   NIPC mean / interval width): started $Ma_e$ 2.93 / 2.91 / 0.536, Φ
   4.42×10⁻³ / 4.40×10⁻³ / 1.06×10⁻³, $\sigma_p$ 0.445 / 0.442 / 0.111;
   unstarted $Ma_e$ 1.80 / 1.78 / 0.205, Φ 3.35×10⁻³ / 3.32×10⁻³ /
   1.00×10⁻³, $\sigma_p$ 0.104 / 0.103 / 0.053. Started widths are about
   twice the unstarted ones, except Φ (ratios 2.6, 2.1 and about 1.06).
5. **Sobol' indices of the pressure field** (§5.3, Figs. 17–18).
   $Ma_\infty$ and $\alpha_\infty$ dominate in "nearly complementary"
   regions: in the started flow $\alpha_\infty$ dominates post-shock regions
   and $Ma_\infty$ the regions ahead of and covered by shocks; in the
   unstarted flow $\alpha_\infty$ dominates the external flow, the bubble and
   the reflected shocks, $Ma_\infty$ the regions between reflected shocks.
   $Re_\infty$ matters at the unstarted reflected shocks, $T_\infty$ only at
   the separation-induced shock near the throat (started), $T_w$ nowhere.
   Near the separation bubble all five inputs are comparable (246–260 mm,
   Fig. 18a; 190–235 mm, Fig. 18b).
6. **Sobol' indices of performance** (§5.3, Fig. 19). $Ma_\infty$ and
   $\alpha_\infty$ dominate the mass-capture uncertainty; $Ma_\infty$
   dominates $\sigma_p$ and leads for $Ma_e$; $Re_\infty$, $T_\infty$, $T_w$
   weigh more for Φ and $Ma_e$ in the unstarted flow.
7. **Oblique-shock argument** (§5.3, Eqs. 24–26; stated approximate
   derivation): $\dfrac{\partial p_2}{\partial\theta}\Big/\dfrac{\partial p_2}{\partial Ma_1}\approx\dfrac{Ma_\infty\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\gg1$,
   used to explain why $\alpha_\infty$ dominates post-shock pressure. See
   Recorded as printed.
8. **Linear correlations** (§5.3, Figs. 20–21; coefficients from the Fig. 20
   annotations): $Ma_\infty$–Φ 0.8180 (start), 0.9005 (unstart);
   $Ma_\infty$–$Ma_e$ 0.8594, 0.6817; $Ma_\infty$–$\sigma_p$ −0.9117,
   −0.9665. Mass flow correlates negatively with $\alpha_\infty$; nothing
   correlates with $T_w$. Stated consequence: maximum mass flow and maximum
   recovery cannot both be reached by varying $Ma_\infty$ (or
   $\alpha_\infty$) alone.
9. **Error-accumulation reliability** (§4.4, Table 4, Eqs. 21–23, after
   Smirnov et al.): $R_s=n_{\max}/n$ with $S_{\max}=5\%$, $n=30000$ steps;
   coarse 6.62–7.13, medium 62.62–73.60, fine 282.82–392.14; $R_s\gg1$ taken
   as acceptable accumulated error. The reader checked the arithmetic.

Key formulas, read cleanly from the rendered page 3 (paper notation):
expansion $Y=\alpha^*(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\xi)\approx\sum_{i=0}^{P}\alpha_i(x_i)\Psi_i(\xi_i)$
(Eq. 13); sample count $N_s=n_p(P+1)=n_p\,(n+p)!/(n!\,p!)$ (Eq. 14);
moments $\mu_{\alpha^*}=a_0$, $\sigma_{\alpha^*}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{P}a_i^2\langle\Psi_i^2\rangle}$
(Eq. 16); Sobol' index $S_{i_1,\dots,i_s}=D_{i_1,\dots,i_s}/D_{\mathrm{Total}}$
from the partial sums of squared coefficients (Eqs. 17–19); cell Reynolds
number $Re_\Delta=\rho_\infty u_\infty\Delta/\mu_\infty$ (Eq. 20).

# Methods

* **Solver.** Unnamed in-house second-order finite-volume code (Beihang,
  Yan group), multi-block structured grids; 3-D compressible RANS
  (Eqs. 1–6), perfect gas, Sutherland viscosity, Pr = 0.72, Pr_t = 0.9.
* **Turbulence.** Menter SST k–ω (Eqs. 7–12), constants $a_1=0.31$;
  set 1: 0.85, 0.5, 0.075, 0.5332; set 2: 1.0, 0.856, 0.0828, 0.4403;
  vorticity-based production.
* **Discretisation.** Roe flux-difference splitting with second-order
  MUSCL (limiter not stated), central viscous fluxes; implicit LU-SGS,
  CFL 3.0, 30000 steps.
* **Geometry and boundaries.** Li et al. inlet: ramps at 9° and 14°,
  isolator 10 mm high and 194 mm long, computed as a spanwise-periodic 3-D
  slab; far-field, outflow, no-slip isothermal walls.
* **Grids** (Table 2): 360×120×20 (3,646,300 points, min $Re_\Delta=30$),
  450×150×30 (4,824,420, 20; adopted), 550×180×40 (6,204,660, 10); Table 3
  gives $Ma_e$ 2.92/2.93/2.93, mass flow 8.17/8.10/8.10 ×10⁻³ kg/s,
  $\sigma_p$ 0.444/0.445/0.445.
* **Validation.** DLR GK-01 inlet (Häberle & Gülhan): Mach 7, $4.0\times10^6$
  m⁻¹, 0°, 46.0 K, wall 300.0 K; wall $C_p$ within a ±5.6% band (Fig. 5,
  graphical only). Li et al. shock-tunnel data with ±5% (Figs. 6–7):
  agreement except a slight discrepancy downstream in the isolator.
* **UQ.** Point-collocation NIPC after Hosder et al.; Legendre basis,
  optimal LHS, $n_p=2$, least squares (Eq. 15); 95% interval taken as
  3.92σ; Sobol' indices from the coefficients; Pearson correlations by
  linear regression. **PCE order and sample count are not stated**
  (the reader infers about 42 samples from Fig. 20, order 2).
* **Inputs** (Table 5, uniform): $\alpha_\infty$ 0.0° ± 0.2°;
  $Ma_\infty$ 5.9 ± 1.5% [5.8115, 5.9885]; $Re_\infty$ $5.2\times10^6$ m⁻¹
  ± 15%; $T_\infty$ 101.7 K ± 10%; $T_w$ 283.0 K ± 10%. The authors note
  the coefficient-of-variation choice drives the sensitivities.

# Tests and results

* §4.2 GK-01 validation (Figs. 4–5): separation bubble, separation and
  reflected shocks captured; $C_p$ within the band.
* §4.3 grid study (Tables 2–3): medium and fine identical to three digits.
* §4.4 error accumulation (Table 4): all $R_s>6.6$.
* §4.5 hysteresis (Figs. 8–10): claims 1–2.
* §5.1 baseline structure (Figs. 11–12): started, cowl shock impinging
  just behind the throat with a small separation; unstarted, large
  separation bubble ahead of the throat, strong separation shock,
  recompression shock across the throat, second small bubble on the cowl,
  pressure plateau.
* §5.2–5.3 UQ and sensitivity: claims 3–8.
