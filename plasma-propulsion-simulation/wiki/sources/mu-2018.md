# Summary

The paper treats the re-planning of a space station's daily (24 h,
"detailed planning" level) activity schedule when unexpected events
("emergencies", represented as unexpected missions) break in, as a
dynamic constraint satisfaction problem. Missions, activities and
resources are described by an ontology-style domain model adapted from
Bu et al., with a mission priority in $[0,100]$, a mission class and an
execution-property type (non-interruptible, suspendable, restartable). A
preprocessing strategy stops the lowest-priority conflicting missions and
reconstructs them according to their execution type; an interval-based
greedy algorithm re-inserts activities with three insert strategies
(SIST stabilization, SISF shifting, SIDP displacement); a backtracking
iteration strategy resolves conflicts propagated among activities. A
notional 24 h scenario of the future Chinese space station (16 missions,
43 activities, 29 resources, three unexpected "UrgentAvoidance"
missions) shows all power, thermal, bandwidth and capacity violations
removed, with a higher mission accomplishment rate and a lower objective
than a no-re-planning baseline, at computation times below 0.2 s.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Problem class** (§2). On-orbit activity re-planning belongs to
   detailed planning with a one-day interval and is a dynamic constraint
   satisfaction problem: determine the start times of re-planned missions
   so that their activities satisfy all schedule constraints. Stated.
2. **Novelty** (§1). "To the best of the authors' knowledge, no efficient
   method exists" for this problem; prior space-station planning work is
   limited to nominal planning. Stated.
3. **Domain model** (§3.1, Eqs. 1–8). A mission has nine elements, a
   priority $E_{M.P.}\in[0,100]$, a class
   $S_{M.C.}\in\{v_{Cr},v_{Em},v_{Da},v_{Pa},v_{Un}\}$ and an execution type
   $S_{M.E.T.}\in\{v_{No},v_{Al},v_{Re}\}$; an activity has six elements and
   precedence types $\{v_{Eq},v_{Be},v_{Af}\}$; a resource has seven
   elements and a class $\{v_{St},v_{Pa},v_{Uk}\}$. Defined.
4. **Objective** (§3.2.1, Eqs. 9–10).
   $f=\min\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n-n_C}\delta_{m_i}(t_{r_i}-t_i)+\sum_{j=1}^{n_C}24\times3\times\delta_{m_j}\Bigr)$,
   $\delta_m\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}$: a cancelled mission is penalised as a delay
   of a full three-day cycle (72 h) weighted by importance. Importance
   coefficients by class: 1 undefined, 2 payload, 3 daily maintenance
   (printed $v_{Cr}$; see Recorded as printed), 4 important, 5 urgent.
   Defined.
5. **Variables and constraints** (§3.2.2–3.2.3, Eqs. 11–13). Design
   variables are mission start times within each mission's earliest-start /
   latest-end window; at each re-planning time the summed power, thermal
   load, bandwidth and capacity of the executing devices must not exceed
   rated values, and communication activities must start inside a
   communication window. Defined.
6. **Preprocessing** (§4.1, Fig. 2). At pop-up time the state is updated,
   constraints checked, and the lowest-priority conflicting missions are
   moved iteratively into a reconstruction list; non-interruptible ones are
   cancelled, suspendable ones reconstructed from their execution state,
   restartable ones delayed to the end of the conflict. Defined.
7. **Greedy insertion** (§4.2–4.3, Eqs. 14–15, Figs. 3–6). An activity
   neighbourhood interval (free time between adjacent activities with
   constraint conflicts), the three insert strategies, and a mission
   time-free degree $TTFD=t_{M.ET.}-t_{M.FT.}$ drive a greedy algorithm
   with priority search. Defined as a flowchart only: no pseudocode, no
   complexity analysis.
8. **Backtracking** (§4.4, Fig. 7). When a conflict found in simulated
   execution forces a lower-priority activity to be delayed, the
   simulation returns to the start of that mission and re-checks every
   constraint. Defined, illustrated only.
9. **Constraint satisfaction** (§5.3, Figs. 12–16). After re-planning all
   resources satisfy rated power, thermal load and bandwidth in the three
   cases; the preplanned schedule overruns bandwidth for M01 and M02 and
   power, thermal load and bandwidth for M03. Shown, for one notional
   scenario.
10. **Against the baseline** (§5.4, Table 6). Re-planning beats the
    "without re-planning strategies" baseline in accomplishment rate (M01,
    M02) and objective (all three cases), at higher but sub-second
    computation times. Shown; the text says "times less than 2 s … are
    acceptable".

# Methods

* **Model**: ontology-based domain model (after Bu et al., the authors'
  ref. [21]); single-objective minimisation (Eq. 9) over mission start
  times; resource constraints (Eq. 13).
* **Algorithm**: heuristic in three stages: preprocessing (Fig. 2),
  interval-based greedy insertion with SIST/SISF/SIDP (Figs. 3–6),
  backtracking conflict resolution (Fig. 7); time-stepped simulation of
  activity execution with a step of 0.09 h (§5.1).
* No implementation language, hardware or code release is stated. No
  comparison with another optimiser (genetic algorithm, constraint
  programming solver); the only baseline, "without re-planning
  strategies", is not defined (from Table 6 it cancels the conflicting
  mission).
* **Scenario** (§5.1, Tables 2–5): 24 h horizon; 16 missions, 43
  activities of 0.2–2.0 h, 29 resources with capacity, power (kW),
  thermal load (kW) and bandwidth (Mbps); rated power 4 kW, rated thermal
  load 2 kW, rated bandwidth 500 Mbps. Unexpected missions M01, M02, M03
  ("UrgentAvoidance", priority 95, class daily maintenance per Table 5,
  non-interruptible, one 1.6 h activity on resources R1, R2, R3, R14,
  R15), at 2.6 h, 8.8 h and 18.7 h.

# Tests and results

* **M01 at 2.6 h** (§5.2, Fig. 9): conflict with executing A4 on resource
  R2 (capacity overrun); SIST applied; M2 reconstructed and delayed
  between M3 and M4.
* **M02 at 8.8 h** (Fig. 10): A15 executing; SISF applied; M6 re-executed
  after M7; new capacity conflicts among M6, M8, M9, so M8 and M9 are
  delayed in priority order.
* **M03 at 18.7 h** (Fig. 11): A34 and A37 executing; SIDP applied; M13
  cancelled (non-interruptible), M12 re-executed after M03; new conflicts
  among M12, M14, M15: M14 and M15 delayed, M15 then cancelled for lack of
  an interval in its window.
* **Table 6** (16 executed and 1 unexpected mission in every row):

| Case | Strategy | Cancelled | Delayed | Accomplishment rate | Objective | Time [s] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | re-planning | 0 | 1 | 1.0 | 8.7 | 0.139 |
| M01 | without | 1 | 0 | 0.9375 | 216 | 0.012 |
| M02 | re-planning | 0 | 3 | 1.0 | 12.6 | 0.175 |
| M02 | without | 1 | 0 | 0.9375 | 144 | 0.019 |
| M03 | re-planning | 2 | 2 | 0.875 | 225.6 | 0.06 |
| M03 | without | 2 | 0 | 0.875 | 432 | 0.011 |
