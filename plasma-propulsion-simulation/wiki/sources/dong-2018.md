# Summary

The paper proposes a kinematic (position-level, incremental) visual-servo
control scheme for an eye-in-hand manipulator capturing a non-cooperative
target of unknown motion. The target pose and velocity in the camera
frame come from photogrammetry (pinhole model, four feature points,
iterative least squares), which initialises an adaptive extended Kalman
filter (AEKF) with a constant-velocity model; only the process-noise mean
and covariance are adapted (a windowed estimator, Eqs. 18–21), the
measurement-noise covariance being fixed from experiment. The controller
points the end-effector velocity at the target position predicted one
sampling step ahead, scales it by the largest factor admitted by the
joint-rate limits, and maps it to joint increments with the Jacobian
right pseudo-inverse, which avoids the multiple solutions of
position-level inverse kinematics. A Lyapunov argument (Appendix A)
claims asymptotic stability when the end-effector is faster than the
target. One laboratory experiment on a fixed-base 3-DOF manipulator (plus
2 grasping DOF) and a wire-suspended, stepper-driven target shows capture
at about 12 s. The paper has no plasma or propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Photogrammetry plus AEKF** (§2, Eqs. 2–3): photogrammetry alone is
   memoryless, noise-sensitive, depends on the initial guess and gives no
   target motion; using it to initialise an AEKF addresses this. Stated;
   the integrated method is attributed to the authors' earlier work [18].
2. **Adapting only the process noise** (§2, Eqs. 12–21): adapting $\mathbf q$
   and $\mathbf Q$ with $\mathbf R$ fixed experimentally is preferred because
   adapting both "may not be robust". Stated, attributed to [24]; the
   estimator equations are given, not derived.
3. **Predictive incremental law** (§3, Eqs. 28–36):
   $\dot{\boldsymbol\Theta}=\lambda\mathbf J^\dagger\mathbf n_{ET'}$ with the
   predicted target position $\mathbf X_{T'}=\mathbf X_T+\dot{\mathbf X}_Tt_s$
   and $\lambda=\max\{\lambda_1\cap\cdots\cap\lambda_n\}$ from the joint-rate
   box constraints (Eq. 33), with the null space neglected (Eq. 26).
   Avoidance of inverse-kinematics multiplicity is argued by construction.
4. **Stability** (Appendix A, Eqs. A.1–A.18): with
   $V=\tfrac12\mathbf E^T\mathbf E$, $\mathbf E=\mathbf X_T-\mathbf X_E$ and the
   assumption $\|\dot{\mathbf X}_T\|<\lambda$ (A.16),
   $\dot V=\|\mathbf E\|\|\dot{\mathbf X}_T\|\cos\varphi-\|\mathbf E\|\lambda\cos\phi\le0$,
   hence "asymptotically stable". Proved in the paper's continuous-time,
   geometric sense, relying on a small $t_s$ (see Recorded as printed).
5. **Capture** (§4, Figs. 4–9): capture of a non-cooperative target at
   about 12 s, with the error converging within tolerance and robustness
   to the unmodelled flexible coupling of the torso joint (Fig. 7). Shown
   by one run, qualitative plots, no error statistics.
6. **Transfer to free-floating manipulators** (§1, §3): via the
   generalized Jacobian [21], the virtual manipulator [22] or the
   dynamically equivalent manipulator [23]. Stated as future work, not
   shown.

# Methods

* Frames: global (inertial), camera (centre of the image plane, $y$ axis
  normal to it toward the target), target (Fig. 1).
* Camera model: homogeneous transform target→camera (Eq. 1) with the
  rotation from Euler angles $\theta_x,\theta_y,\theta_z$; pinhole
  projection $\{x_m,z_m\}^T=-\frac{f}{y_C-f}\{x_C,z_C\}^T$ (Eq. 2); two
  equations per feature point (Eq. 3), 6 unknowns, at least 3 points
  needed, 4 used to avoid ambiguity; iterative least squares.
* Estimator: 12-state constant-velocity model (positions, Euler angles and
  their rates, Eq. 4); $\mathbf X_k=\mathbf A\mathbf X_{k-1}+\mathbf B\boldsymbol\omega_{k-1}$
  with per-axis blocks $\begin{bmatrix}1&t_s\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$ and
  $\{t_s^2/2,\ t_s\}^T$ (Eqs. 5–7); process noise = accelerations,
  $\boldsymbol\omega\sim\mathscr N(\mathbf q,\mathbf Q)$; measurement noise
  $\boldsymbol\mu\sim\mathscr N(0,\mathbf R)$ (Eqs. 8–11). EKF predict and
  update (Eqs. 12–17); windowed update of $\mathbf q$, $\mathbf Q$ over $N$
  steps (Eqs. 18–21); camera-to-global transform (Eqs. 22–23).
* Controller: forward kinematics $\mathbf X_E=\mathbf f(\boldsymbol\Theta)$,
  $\dot{\mathbf X}_E=\mathbf J\dot{\boldsymbol\Theta}$ (Eqs. 24–25); right
  pseudo-inverse $\mathbf J^\dagger=\mathbf J^T(\mathbf J\mathbf J^T)^{-1}$ for
  full row rank (Eq. 27); prediction (Eq. 28), direction (Eqs. 29–30),
  scale from joint-rate limits (Eqs. 32–34), increment
  $\delta\boldsymbol\Theta=\dot{\boldsymbol\Theta}t_s$ (Eq. 36); loop until the
  position error is within tolerance, then capture. Assumptions: target
  always reachable, end-effector always faster than the target.
* Hardware (§4): fixed-base manipulator with torso, shoulder and elbow
  revolute joints in the control law (two more for grasping), shaft
  encoders, home angles 0°, 90°, 0°; links shoulder–elbow 0.4540 m and
  elbow–end-effector 0.4445 m. Target: plate on fishing wires driven by
  one stepper motor (translation in $x$, $z$, rotation about $y$), white
  background, four feature shapes; OpenCV optical flow after lock-on. The
  sampling time is not printed.

# Tests and results

* One experiment (§4, Figs. 4–9): the target starts 50 sampling steps
  (≈2 s) before the manipulator; tracking tolerance 0.01 m per axis.
* Fig. 5: target estimates in the camera frame are smooth while the
  camera is stationary and oscillate after the manipulator starts (camera
  motion coupling), most visibly in the velocities.
* Figs. 6–7: incremental joint inputs and encoder angles, near-uniform
  except the torso joint (flexible coupling).
* Figs. 8–9: end-effector and target positions in the global frame,
  capture at about 12 s; position errors converge.
* No error tables, no repeated trials, no baseline controller and no
  comparison with the earlier method [18].
