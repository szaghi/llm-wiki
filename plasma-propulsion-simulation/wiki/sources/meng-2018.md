# Summary

The paper parameterizes a 3-D variable-cross-section ("shape-transition")
curved scramjet isolator in two steps: entrance and exit sections are
linearly blended after Barger's geometric blending functions and each
intermediate section is rescaled to a prescribed area distribution; the
sections are then translated along an "offset line", a B-spline through
five control points with three free variables. These are optimized with
a multi-island genetic algorithm (MIGA, in Isight) coupled to 3-D RANS
(SST k–ω, second-order Roe) to maximize the exit total-pressure recovery
at no backpressure, at Mach 2. Three cases are optimized
(rectangular-to-circular, circular, rectangular), all 800 mm long with a
40 mm offset, and compared with an existing baseline. The authors argue
that, once the offset line is optimized, the remaining total-pressure
loss is governed by wetted area. The optimized rectangular-to-circular
isolator recovers 1.59% more total pressure than the baseline and keeps
its shock-train leading edge furthest downstream at a backpressure ratio
of 4. Backpressured runs show a switch between bottom- and top-wall
large-scale separation modes in the curved ducts.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Section blending** (§2.1, unnumbered equations p. 565, after Barger):
   $f_i(z_j,y_j)=(1-k_i)f_{in}(z_j,y_j)+k_if_{out}(z_j,y_j)$ with
   $k_i=(x-x_{in})/(x_{out}-x_{in})$; each section is scaled by $p_i$ so
   that $\int p_iy_j\,d(p_iz_j)=A(x_i)$, solved iteratively. Without
   scaling the blend overshoots the area by up to about 16% at mid-length
   (Fig. 3, approximate reading). Shown.
2. **Offset line** (§2.1, Fig. 5, Table 1): B-spline with control points
   $(x/l,y/l)$ = (0,0), ($v_1$,0), (0.5, $v_3h/l$), ($v_2$, $h/l$),
   (1, $h/l$); points 2 and 4 fix zero end slopes; stated to give a
   monotone line.
3. **Optimizer** (§2.2, Fig. 6): MIGA in Isight, loop geometry → grid →
   CFD → objective; objective the exit total-pressure recovery $\delta$ at
   no backpressure, chosen because backpressured runs are too costly.
4. **Validation** (§3, Fig. 7): Kawatsu et al. constant-area duct,
   30 × 30 mm, 290 mm long, Ma = 2.3, $T_0=298$ K, $P_0=100$ kPa,
   $P_b/P_i=3.8$. Wall pressure is overpredicted upstream of the shock
   train (maximum deviation 52%); the shock-train leading edge agrees, with
   a minimum deviation of 1.3% in the shock-train region. Shown.
5. **Grid and step studies** (§3, Fig. 7, Table 3): grids 41×41×81,
   65×65×100, 81×81×120 (coarse deviates at the pressure rise); $\delta$
   after 1000, 2000, 5000, 10000 steps 0.86201, 0.82751, 0.81987, 0.81985,
   printed errors 5.14%, 0.093%, 0.0024%, 0; 5000 steps used.
6. **Optimization histories** (§4.1, Figs. 8–9): for Case1 no variable
   converges ($v_1$ settles near 0.3 after 300 iterations) while $\delta$
   converges after about 200 iterations to about 0.89, taken as evidence
   of a nonlinear objective justifying a global algorithm; optimized
   $\delta$ 0.91 (circular, Case2) and about 0.87 (rectangular, Case3).
7. **No-backpressure ranking** (§4.2, Figs. 10–12): circular highest,
   rectangular lowest (0.87 at the outlet), rectangular-to-circular in
   between; Case1 is 1.59% above Case0. Case0 is slightly better for
   $x<0.2$ m, Case1 downstream (exit Mach 1.96, better uniformity). The
   wetted-area explanation is stated from a boundary-layer argument;
   wetted areas are never computed.
8. **Backpressure** (§4.3, Figs. 13–15): backpressure raised to
   $P_b/P_1$ = 1, 2, 3, 3.5, 4; separation and a shock appear at 2 in all
   configurations; shock trains are asymmetric, with a switch from
   bottom-wall (BLS) to top-wall (TLS) separation "without any changes of
   the boundary conditions" (attributed behaviour of refs. [31,32]); at 4
   the leading edge is most upstream for the circular case and Case0 and
   most downstream for Case1; $\delta$ is unaffected below 1 and the cases
   are "near equivalent" above 3. Shown.
9. **Waltrup–Billig correlation** (§1, Eq. 1, attributed, not used):
   $S_{CL}(M_2^2-1)\mathrm{Re}_\theta^{\alpha}/(D^{1/2}\theta^{1/2})=50(P_f/P_a-1)+170(P_f/P_a-1)^2$,
   with the exponent $\alpha$ printed only as a symbol.

# Methods

* 3-D RANS, finite volume, SST k–ω, second-order upwind Roe, implicit
  steady iteration (5000 steps). Solver name, gas model, wall thermal
  condition, inflow boundary layer, $y^+$ and the grid of the 800 mm
  isolators are not stated.
* Optimization inflow: Ma = 2, $P_0=100$ kPa, $T_0=300$ K; outflow "no
  backpressure", then prescribed static ratio $P_b/P_1$ ($P_1$ undefined,
  presumably the isolator-entrance static pressure).
* Configurations (Table 2), all $l=800$ mm and $\Delta y=40$ mm: Case0
  baseline rectangular-to-circular ("successfully applied in the
  scramjet"), Case1 optimized rectangular-to-circular (entrance 240 × 56
  mm), Case2 circular (R = 66), Case3 rectangular.
* No own experiment; uncertainty only through the step study and the
  validation deviation.
