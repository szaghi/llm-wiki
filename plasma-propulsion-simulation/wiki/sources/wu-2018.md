# Summary

The paper describes an atmospheric (non-vacuum) thermal test facility,
at the Xi'an Institute of Space Radio Technology, for testing large
deployable antennas (LDAs) in the deployed state, as a cheaper
alternative to thermal-vacuum chambers (JPL Space Simulator, ESTEC LSS,
the Chinese KM6, NASA's Space Power Facility). The 20 m × 20 m × 13 m
chamber is cooled by an air-standard (reverse Brayton) refrigeration
cycle with two turbines and heat exchangers instead of liquid nitrogen,
heated by finned-tube electric heaters at the outlets of the
recirculating fans, and kept dry by twin-tower adsorption dryers and a
slight overpressure. Uniformity and low disturbance come from returning
air at the bottom and supplying it through a perforated top plate, and,
during antenna tests, from cooling through wall heat sinks. The
acceptance tests (§3) show a −100 °C to 130 °C cycle, constant-
temperature holds, an inlet dew point near −100 °C, a maximum
temperature deviation of at most 2.5 °C between −40 and −100 °C, and a
maximum airflow speed of about 0.4 m/s. The paper is a facility
description with measured data and no equations; it has no plasma or
propulsion content.

# Key claims

1. **Specification** (§1, §2.1.7): effective test volume
   20 m × 20 m × 13 m; temperature range −100 °C to 130 °C; horizontal
   temperature deviation at most 2.5 °C, vertical at most 4 °C;
   cleanliness Class 100,000; airflow speed at most 5 m/s (stated only
   in §1). Stated as specification.
2. **Thermal cycle** (§3.1, Fig. 5): a −100 °C to 130 °C cycle was
   completed in about 120000 s. Shown.
3. **Constant temperature** (§3.1, Fig. 6): holds at ±20 °C and ±60 °C
   of about 10000 s each (plateaus +20, −20, −60, −20, +20, +60 °C).
   Shown; the sentence claiming constant temperature "ranging from
   −100 °C to 130 °C" is only stated.
4. **Dryness** (§3.1, Fig. 7): during cooling the chamber inlet dew point
   stayed between −100 °C and −95 °C; the outlet dew point fell to about
   −95 °C and kept decreasing. Shown.
5. **Uniformity** (§3.2.1, Figs. 8–12): per GB/T 10592-2008 [32], 27
   thermocouples in three layers (1, 3 and 6 m high): maximum temperature
   deviation 2.4 °C at −40 °C, 2.4 °C at −60 °C, 2.5 °C at −80 °C and
   2.5 °C at −100 °C. Shown.
6. **Airflow** (§3.2.2, Figs. 13–14): 2 layers × 25 points at 1 m and
   2 m height; maximum about 0.4 m/s at the recirculating-duct inlet at
   1 m, about 0.1 m/s at 2 m. Shown.
7. **CFD at design stage** (§2.2.2): simulations selected the
   bottom-return and perforated-top-plate supply. Stated only: no model,
   code, grid or result is reported.
8. **Equivalence** (§4): the atmospheric chamber "can be considered
   equivalent to thermal vacuum chamber in LDA thermal test" because the
   airflow disturbance is negligible. Stated, without a convective
   versus radiative heat-transfer comparison or antenna-distortion
   data.
9. **Comparison facilities** (§1, attributed): JPL Space Simulator 6 m
   diameter × 7.5 m [20]; ESTEC LSS 10 m × 15 m [27,28]; KM6 main
   chamber 12 m × 22.4 m, $45\times10^{-6}$ Pa, shrouds below 95 K with
   liquid nitrogen [29]; NASA Glenn SPF 22,653 m³, 30.5 m × 37.2 m,
   cryoshroud −160 °C to 60 °C, 930 m³ of liquid nitrogen, 5
   turbomolecular and 10 cryogenic pumps, $2.7\times10^{-4}$ Pa in less
   than 8 h [30,31].

# Methods

* Facility (§2.1): test chamber (insulated stainless-steel walls and
  floor, aluminium wall heat sinks, a recirculating duct with a 1.2 m
  inlet and four fans, heaters and filters at the fan outlets, a
  perforated top plate; a 6 m × 6 m double door; a spray room for
  dedusting); gas supply (6 compressors in two groups, 2 twin-tower
  regeneration adsorption dryers with an outlet dew point of −100 °C to
  −90 °C); refrigeration (two turbines, a water-cooled and an air-to-air
  heat exchanger recovering cold from the chamber exhaust, a turbine
  expander, a room-temperature bypass); heating (finned-tube electric
  heaters with a power regulator); clean system; pressure control (an
  outlet centrifugal fan and valves, the chamber held slightly above
  atmospheric); monitoring (IP cameras in purged covers, a 0.5 m
  window).
* Control (§2.2.1): PID controllers set the heater power when heating
  and the cold-air flow through the inlet valve when cooling; during
  antenna tests the cold air goes to the wall heat sinks rather than
  into the chamber volume.
* Drying (§2.2.3): dryer dew point −90 to −100 °C; precipitation of
  water upstream by the heat exchanger (about −100 °C) and the expander;
  long dry-air replacement before low-temperature tests; heat sinks as
  cold traps; overpressure.
* Measurements (§3): 27 thermocouples, inlet and outlet dew-point
  transmitters, 50 airflow-speed probes.

# Tests and results

* Thermal cycling (§3.1, Fig. 5): from about 30 °C down to −100 °C
  (reached at about 65000 s, read from the figure), then up to 130 °C
  (about 115000 s, read from the figure); about 120000 s in all (text).
* Constant temperature (§3.1, Fig. 6): ±20 °C and ±60 °C holds, about
  10000 s each.
* Dew point (§3.1, Fig. 7), 0–50000 s: inlet −100 °C to −95 °C; outlet
  from about −74 °C (read from the figure) to about −95 °C (text).
* Uniformity (§3.2.1, Figs. 9–12) at −40, −60, −80, −100 °C: maximum
  deviations 2.4, 2.4, 2.5, 2.5 °C; the main test area, below the
  gravity-compensation truss, is 6 m high.
* Airflow (§3.2.2, Fig. 14): about 0.4 m/s maximum at 1 m near the duct
  inlet, about 0.1 m/s at 2 m.
