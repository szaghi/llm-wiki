# Summary

An experimental study of how a coaxial semiconductor spark plug starts the
main discharge of an ablative (PTFE) [pulsed plasma thruster](/definitions/pulsed-plasma-thruster.md).
A parallel-plate thruster is fired with a normal propellant face, an
inclined face and no propellant, at 1130 and 1430 V, while electrode
voltage, Rogowski-coil current and spark-plug voltage are recorded. With no
propellant the thruster still discharges with near-identical waveforms,
and the light is confined to the ceramic side walls: a surface flashover.
With propellant the authors propose a three-step ignition and an algebraic
ionization criterion. There is no numerical simulation.

# Key claims

1. **Prior theories** (§1, Fig. 1; attributed): spark-plug plasma seeding
   secondary-electron multiplication until a channel forms (refs. 7–8), or
   field emission from the electrodes through a thinned potential barrier
   (ref. 10).
2. **No-propellant discharge** (§3, Figs. 4–6; shown): total discharge time
   and voltage and current waveforms differ "not obvious[ly]" between
   normal, inclined and no propellant; without propellant the light sits on
   the two side ceramics at the start of the channel, read as a surface
   flashover.
3. **Spark-plug duration** (§3, Fig. 7; shown): spark-plug voltage lasts
   about 15 µs against a discharge of about 5.5 µs; inferred that
   spark-plug electrons sustain the discharge.
4. **Ignition sequence with propellant** (§3; proposed, not proved): a
   voltage fluctuation marks the initial conductive path of spark-plug
   electrons; with circuit resistance about 10 mΩ the initial current of
   about 500 A ablates neutral gas, ionized within about 0.5 µs; the plasma
   enters the channel and closes the path, else the path is cut.
5. **Ionization criterion** (§3, Eqs. 1–4): in a field taken as uniform
   between the electrodes, an electron gains $e\lambda_{\mathrm{mfp}}V_d/d$
   over one mean free path, $d$ being the electrode gap; the mean free path
   uses hard-sphere cross sections of C and F weighted 1:2 and a neutral
   density from an ablated mass equal to about 5% of the mass shot; the
   path persists if the gain exceeds the C/F weighted ionization energy
   (one third C, two thirds F). Result: minimum discharge voltage about
   468 V. The inputs are not printed.
6. **Voltage scan** (§3; shown): success rate over 2000 ignitions falls from
   85% at 600 V to 39% at 450 V (tests at 450, 500, 550, 600 V; intermediate
   values not printed), claimed consistent with 468 V.
7. **Propellant inclination** (Table 2, 1430 V; shown): normal 4.23 µg / 99%
   (1500 ignitions) and 3.99 µg / 98% (2000); inclined 2/25 3.50 µg / 83%
   and 3.31 µg / 81%; inclined 5/25 3.01 µg / 72% and 2.88 µg / 70%. Stated
   cause: the inclined face covers more anode area, so fewer initial
   electrons reach the anode; with more ignitions, spark-plug contamination
   lowers the initial electron energy.

# Models and numerics

No simulation. A 0D algebraic criterion (Key claim 5). Set-up (§2,
Table 1): vacuum $5\times10^{-3}$ Pa; electrodes 25 mm long, 12 mm wide,
4 mm thick, gap 25 mm; spark plug 10 mm maximum diameter; 0.5 Hz; high-voltage
probe, Rogowski coil, oscilloscope and a precision balance for the mass
shot. Capacitance, stored energy and inductance are not stated; no thrust,
$I_{\mathrm{bit}}$, $I_{sp}$ or efficiency is reported.
