# Summary

The paper builds a phenomenological aging constitutive model for an HTPB
coating, the buffer and insulation layer between propellant and case of a
solid rocket motor. The aging state is characterized by the NMR crosslink
density rather than by time and temperature. Samples were aged
thermo-oxidatively at 323.15, 333.15, 343.15 and 353.15 K for 50 days, with
uniaxial tension and NMR crosslink density measured every 5 days. Of three
empirical kinetic models, a power law in time (exponent 0.3) fits the
crosslink density best; its rate constant follows a modified Arrhenius law
with a power-of-temperature factor. The stress is written as an aging
function of crosslink density and strain times an equilibrium stress; the
equilibrium stress is a second-order polynomial (five-parameter,
Mooney–Rivlin-type) hyperelastic law fitted to a multi-step relaxation
curve, and the aging function is a sum of two exponentials in strain whose
four coefficients are fitted as functions of crosslink density. A held-out
313.15 K aging test gives crosslink-density prediction errors of
0.19–0.58%; the tensile curves are reproduced qualitatively.

# Key claims

1. **Equilibrium curve from multi-step relaxation** (§2.2, Fig. 3).
   Single-step relaxation drops the stress from 0.23 to 0.12 MPa in 600 s
   and to 0.11 MPa at 1800 s, so the first 600 s carry 91.67% of the drop,
   which justifies 600 s holds; the stresses after each hold are taken as
   equilibrium stresses. Shown.
2. **Aging stiffens the coating** (§2.3, Figs. 4–5). Longer aging lowers
   maximum elongation and raises modulus and tensile strength, faster at
   higher temperature; curves show elastic, "yield" and stress-intensification
   stages. Shown; the micro-mechanical explanation is stated.
3. **Crosslink density rises monotonically** (§2.4, Fig. 7), fast then
   slower, with a similar shape at all temperatures, read as a single aging
   mechanism (post-curing and oxidative crosslinking early, oxidation
   slightly outweighing chain scission later, attributed). Read from the
   figure (approximate): from about 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³ to about 4.85,
   5.2, 6.1 and 6.55 × 10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³ at day 50 at the four temperatures.
   Shown.
4. **Kinetic model selection** (§3, Eqs. 1–3, Table 1). Logarithmic,
   power-law and "exponential" models fitted by least squares; the power
   law with exponent 0.3 correlates best ($r$ = 0.9684–0.9828, against
   0.8397–0.8887 logarithmic and 0.8734–0.9353 exponential). The exponent
   scan is not tabulated. Shown.
5. **Modified Arrhenius law** (Eq. 4, Table 2). The logarithm of the rate
   constant is linear in the logarithm of temperature and in inverse
   temperature; fitted values: logarithm of the pre-exponential 213.5,
   temperature exponent −30.21, activation energy 1.062 × 10⁵ (unit not
   printed, J mol⁻¹ implied), $r$ = 0.9971. The reader's recomputation from
   Table 2 (0.557, 0.726, 0.909, 1.095) agrees with the Table 1 rate
   constants (0.5875, 0.7417, 0.9772, 1.1370) within 2–7%. Form attributed.
6. **Hyperelastic part** (§4.1, Eqs. 12, 14, 15, Table 3). Incompressible
   uniaxial tension; second-order polynomial strain energy in the first two
   invariants; fitted coefficients 0.4001, −0.1957, −0.005041, −0.1390 and
   0.0333 (units not printed, MPa implied), $r$ = 0.9999, over strain 0–3.
   The second order rests on "application experience" (stated).
7. **Aging function** (§4.2, Eqs. 5, 16, 17, Table 4, Figs. 9–10).
   Measured as the ratio of tensile-test stress to multi-step equilibrium
   stress at equal strain and fitted as a sum of two exponentials in strain;
   its coefficients are quadratic or linear in crosslink density (in units
   of 10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³), with printed $r$ of −0.9417, −0.9023, 0.9147 and
   −0.8971. The reader's check at crosslink density 3 reproduces the Fig. 10
   values (about 0.918, −2.10, 1.05, 0.077). The full model is Eq. 18.
8. **Verification** (§5, Table 5, Fig. 11). Held-out aging at 313.15 K,
   crosslink density test against prediction (× 10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³): 3.771 /
   3.793 (0.58%) at 10 d, 3.997 / 3.981 (0.40%) at 20 d, 4.089 / 4.110
   (0.51%) at 30 d, 4.231 / 4.212 (0.45%) at 40 d, 4.289 / 4.297 (0.19%) at
   50 d. Tensile curves are called "accurately" predicted without an error
   metric; worse at 10 d and "not very good" near fracture (damage
   neglected). Shown, qualitatively for the curves.

# Methods

* **Material**: one batch of filled HTPB coating (State-owned No. 845
  Plant, Xi'an; composition not stated); 2 mm dumbbells to ASTM
  D624-00(R2007).
* **Aging** (§2.1): GB/T3512-2001 in DU288 oil-bath thermostats, ±1 K,
  humidity below 30%, 50 days; every 5 days 5 samples for tension and 5 for
  NMR; a fifth thermostat at 313.15 K for verification only.
* **Relaxation** (§2.2): INSTRON 5982 at 100 mm/min; multi-step with 10%
  strain increments and 600 s holds to fracture; single-step to 1800 s
  (strain not stated).
* **Tension** (§2.3): QJ916-85, 100 mm/min to fracture, 5 samples averaged;
  test temperature not stated.
* **NMR** (§2.4): VTMR20-010V-T system; 8 × 2 × 2 mm strips at
  293.15 ± 2 K, 10 repeats; inversion to crosslink density not stated.
* Least squares throughout, without confidence intervals; no numerical
  simulation.
