# Summary

The paper proposes using crew trash, compacted by a heat melt compactor
(HMC), as passive radiation shielding on deep-space crewed missions. A
2000 g model trash (a NASA-style composition, Table 2) becomes 1444 g,
2.2 cm thick and 0.395 g/cm³ after HMC processing (Table 1). Geant4
Monte Carlo transport ($10^6$ primaries per energy point) of protons,
alphas, C, O and Fe ions at 100, 550, 1000 and 2000 MeV/n through slabs
of 5 and 10 g/cm² of HMC trash, aluminium and water gives the emergent
primary energies (Figs. 6–10) and the secondary-particle yields
(Figs. 11–15, Tables 3–4). By primary-energy reduction trash lies
between aluminium and water; by secondary-neutron yield it is the lowest
of the three at 10 g/cm² (Table 4). The authors conclude that trash can
replace consumed water in a solar-particle-event storm shelter but is
ineffective against high-energy galactic cosmic-ray ions. The paper has
no propulsion content; its only pointer toward plasma is the suggestion
of active (magnetic or plasma) shielding [16,17] as future work.

# Key claims

1. **Compaction** (§2.2, Tables 1–2): HMC processing removes water and
   compacts the model trash from 2000 to 1444 g, 12.7 to 2.2 cm and
   0.095 to 0.395 g/cm³. Data, attributed to HMC tests [12,13].
2. **Low-energy attenuation** (§3.1, Figs. 6–7): at 100 MeV/n, protons
   and alphas emerging from 5 g/cm² keep about 67% (aluminium), 55%
   (water) and 58% (trash) of their incident energy; 10 g/cm² leaves
   about 5% in aluminium and stops them in water and trash. Shown by
   simulation (values read from the figures as stated in the text).
3. **Ranking by attenuation** (§3.1, summary bullet 1): by
   energy-reduction percentage, trash shields better than aluminium "but
   a little lower than that of water". Shown.
4. **High energy** (§3.1): above about 1 GeV/n the energy reduction
   hardly varies with thickness, especially for low-$Z$ primaries; the
   fractional reduction grows with the primary's atomic number. Shown.
5. **Secondaries in trash** (§3.2, Table 3): for O ions, neutrons and
   protons dominate, one to two orders of magnitude above heavier
   fragments, and grow with energy and thickness; at 1000 MeV/n and
   10 g/cm², 1,280,000 neutrons and 1,370,000 protons per million
   primaries. Shown.
6. **Secondary ratios** (§3.2, Table 4, 10 g/cm²): secondary neutrons,
   trash/aluminium from 28% (protons, 550 MeV/n) to 83% (Fe,
   1000 MeV/n), trash/water from 55% to 80%; secondary protons
   trash/aluminium up to 103% (O and Fe at 1000 MeV/n), the "around 3%"
   excess noted in the text. Shown.
7. **Harm** (§3.2): citing ICRP 2007 through [15], a proton radiation
   weighting factor of 2 and a neutron factor of "2.5 to over 20"; hence
   trash secondaries are inferred to be less harmful than those of
   aluminium or water. Attributed and inferred; no dose is computed.
8. **Conclusion** (§4): HMC trash is "superior" to aluminium and water,
   especially for low-$Z$ primaries below several hundred MeV; a storm
   shelter can start as water and be refilled with trash as the water is
   consumed; no obvious benefit against high-energy ions; active
   shielding [16,17] suggested.
9. **Context figures** (§2.2): launch cost \$20,000/kg (LEO) to
   \$200,000/kg (Mars); trash about 30% water by mass. Stated without a
   source.

# Methods

* Code (§2.3): Geant4 [14] with an ion physics list (version and list
  name not given); MCNPX, FLUKA and HZETRN named but not used. $10^6$
  primaries per energy point; "calculation errors being less than 1%".
* Primaries (§2.1): protons, alphas, C, O and Fe at 100, 550, 1000 and
  2000 MeV/n, chosen from solar-particle-event spectra (August 1972,
  October 1989; Fig. 1) and galactic cosmic-ray spectra (Figs. 2–3).
* Shields (§2.2): slabs of 5 and 10 g/cm² of HMC trash (Table 2:
  low-density polyethylene 460 g, food 180 g, aluminium pouches 260 g,
  tap water 200 g, paper 200 g, cotton cloth 400 g, towels 200 g, latex
  gloves 40 g, duct tape 60 g), aluminium and water. Beam geometry,
  slab size and the elemental composition of processed trash are not
  given; normal incidence is implied.
* Outputs: emergent primary-energy fraction (Figs. 6–10) and secondary
  counts per million primaries by species (Figs. 11–15, Tables 3–4). No
  dose, dose equivalent or LET spectrum.

# Tests and results

The percentages below are emergent primary energy as a fraction of the
incident energy, as stated in the text from Figs. 6–10.

* Protons and alphas (Figs. 6–7): about 97% (5 g/cm²) and 95%
  (10 g/cm²) kept at 550 MeV/n for all three materials, about 99% at
  1000 and 2000 MeV/n.
* C and O (Figs. 8–9): stopped at 100 MeV/n; 90–94% (5 g/cm²) and
  80–88% (10 g/cm²) at 550 MeV/n; 95–97% and 91–95% at 1000 MeV/n;
  about 98% and 96% at 2000 MeV/n.
* Fe (Fig. 10): stopped at 100 MeV/n; at 550 MeV/n 75/68/70%
  (aluminium/water/trash, 5 g/cm²) and 45/25/30% (10 g/cm²); 86–88% and
  72–77% at 1000 MeV/n; about 94% and 88% at 2000 MeV/n.
* O on trash (Table 3), secondaries per million primaries:
  550 MeV/n, 5 g/cm²: 628,343 neutrons, 689,374 protons, 174,299
  alphas; 1000 MeV/n, 5 g/cm²: 805,373, 850,035, 182,823;
  1000 MeV/n, 10 g/cm²: 1,280,000, 1,370,000, 325,825; Li to O
  fragments 13,000–75,000.
* Ratios at 10 g/cm² (Table 4; neutrons trash/aluminium,
  trash/water; protons trash/aluminium, trash/water): protons 550 MeV/n
  28/55/58/61%; protons 1000 MeV/n 34/57/69/67%; alphas 550 MeV/n
  52/69/93/77%; O 1000 MeV/n 65/80/103/88%; Fe 1000 MeV/n
  83/80/103/83%.
