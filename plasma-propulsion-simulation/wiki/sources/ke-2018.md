# Summary

An experimental hypervelocity-impact study of stuffed Whipple-type
debris shields whose stuffed layer combines basalt (ceramic) fabric with
an aramid fabric. Four aramid types were compared (US-made Aramid-II,
Chinese Aramid-II, Aramid-III and PBO), then three ways of compounding
Aramid-III with basalt: hybrid weaving, glue lamination and hand-sewing
with aramid thread. Seventeen targets were shot with 2A12 aluminium
spheres of about 5 mm at 4.55–4.96 km/s on the two-stage light-gas gun
of the CARDC Hypervelocity Impact Research Center (§3.2, Table 3). The
performance criterion is rear-plate damage: perforation size and bulge
height, the latter from laser shadowgraphy (§3.1). At equal areal
density Aramid-III and PBO beat Aramid-II. All three compound modes raise
the fabric "adhesion degree", and too much adhesion degrades
performance, which the authors attribute to reduced absorption of the
debris cloud's kinetic energy. There are no equations and no numerical
simulation of the impacts.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map). Every conclusion
rests on a single shot per configuration; the paper does not quantify
shot-to-shot scatter, and compared shots differ in speed by up to
0.41 km/s (4.55–4.96 km/s in Table 3; reader's arithmetic).

1. **Fabric type** (§4.3, Table 3, shown). At comparable stuffed-layer
   areal density ($\rho_{SL}\approx$ 1470–1500 g/m², targets 1–2, 2–1,
   4–1, 10–1), rear-plate damage is smallest for 10–1 (basalt + 2×PBO,
   bulge $h_R=9.1$ mm, no perforation) and worst for 2–1 (basalt +
   Chinese Aramid II-B, 0.5 mm perforation, $h_R=14.6$ mm). Aramid-III
   and PBO outperform Aramid-II.
2. **Strength ratio** (§4.3, stated). If fabric strengths add, the total
   yielding strength of the stuffed layer in 4–1 or 10–1 is "1.4 times"
   that of 1–2 or 2–1; no derivation is given.
3. **PBO against Aramid-III** (§2, Conclusions, stated). PBO performs
   about as well as Aramid-III at equal areal density but degrades
   quickly under space radiation, so Aramid-III was chosen to compound
   with basalt. The radiation statement is not supported by data in the
   paper.
4. **Amount of aramid** (§4.3, shown). Shielding improves as the aramid
   fabric in the stuffed layer increases: 4–1 (six Aramid-III-A layers)
   beats 3–2 (three layers), and 10–1 beats 5–1.
5. **Hybrid weave** (§4.4). Six layers of hybrid basalt/Aramid-III fabric
   (6–1) do not beat alternating basalt and Aramid-III-A layers (3–1,
   3–2) at equivalent areal density, although the summed tensile
   strength of 6–1 is "1.1 times" higher. The authors attribute this to
   the inter-layer spacing ("multiple shock shields"); with an aluminium
   honeycomb core that shortens the spacing (8–1 against 9–1) the hybrid
   fabric's benefit "is embodied". The mechanism is stated, not
   demonstrated.
6. **Placement order** (§4.5). Alternating aramid and basalt layers (3–2)
   outperform three basalt plus three aramid layers in blocks (12–1), in
   agreement with the paper's ref. [4]. A single thick Aramid-III-C layer
   (7–1, 200 tex against 44 tex for III-A) gives the poorest shielding of
   3–2, 7–1 and 12–1; the authors say it mainly intercepts the cloud
   rather than helping the basalt break it up.
7. **Compounding** (§4.5, Conclusions). Sewing (3A-1, 6A-1) and gluing
   (3B-1, 6B-1) increase adhesion but degrade performance relative to
   loose layers (3–2, 6–1); the glued targets show the largest bulges
   ($h_R=18.0$ mm and 17.1 mm, Table 3). The stated mechanism:
   over-adhesion raises rupture resistance, shrinks the rupture-drawing
   damage area and restricts absorption of the debris cloud's kinetic
   energy.
8. **Velocity trend** (§3.1, attributed to Christiansen, NASA/TM-2009-214785,
   the paper's ref. [17]): shield performance improves as impact velocity
   rises over 2.6–6.5 km/s.

# Methods

- **Targets** (§2, Fig. 1, Table 1): bumper and rear plate both 1 mm Al
  6061-T6, 200 mm × 200 mm, with the stuffed layer between them and a
  witness plate behind the rear plate. The spacings are drawn in Fig. 1
  but not given in the text. All fabrics are plain weave; only the
  hybrid fabric was purpose-woven (Fig. 2), the others were bought.
  Fabric thickness, areal density $\rho_{FL}$, thread density and
  pulling force are in Table 2; areal densities were measured on several
  1 m² samples with a 0.1 g scale.
- **Compound modes**: hybrid weave (Fig. 2); hand-sewing with aramid
  thread at about 10 mm thread spacing (Fig. 3); glue lamination, which
  makes the stack plate-like (Fig. 4). An aluminium honeycomb core of
  321.2 g/m² sits between fabric and rear plate in 8–1, 9–1 and 11–1 to
  fix the layer adhesion, following the authors' earlier work (ref. [8]).
- **Projectile**: 2A12 aluminium sphere, $d_P$ = 5.00–5.04 mm.
- **Facility** (§3.2, Fig. 5): two-stage light-gas gun, 7.6 mm bore,
  maximum 7.4 km/s, launch mass 0.0002 g to 1 g, test chamber below
  10 Pa; sabot acceleration may exceed 2,000,000 g above 7.0 km/s.
  Launch parameters are optimised with an in-house interior-ballistics
  CFD code (ref. [18]) of which no detail is given.
- **Diagnostics**: velocity from a light-screen system (uncertainty below
  0.3%, below 20 m/s); impact sequence and bulge height from an in-house
  sequenced laser shadowgraph with over 10,000,000 pixels (bulge
  uncertainty below 0.2 mm); perforations by vernier caliper (0.02 mm).
  Projectile integrity was checked from the bumper hole shape, the
  absence of small craters and one pre-impact shadowgraph (target 2–1,
  Figs. 6–7).
