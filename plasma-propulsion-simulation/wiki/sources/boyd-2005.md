# Summary

A review of the numerical simulation of the internal (device) flows of the
five electric-thruster types operating in space in 2005: resisto-jets,
arcjets, gridded ion thrusters, Hall thrusters (SPT and TAL) and pulsed
plasma thrusters (PPTs). Plumes, MPD, FEEP, colloid, helicon and laser
thrusters are excluded (§1). §2 frames the choice between continuum
(Navier–Stokes-type) and kinetic methods (DSMC for rarefied gas, MCC, PIC
for plasma, usually hybrid PIC ions with fluid electrons under
quasi-neutrality) by the Knudsen number and the Debye length. §3 surveys
models per thruster type, illustrated mostly by the author's group's
earlier work. §4 ranks the accuracy of performance prediction. No new
results.

# Key claims

All stated or attributed; the figures reproduce earlier work.

1. **Typical properties** (Table 1, no sources given):

   | Thruster | Thrust (mN) | $I_{sp}$ (s) | Density (m$^{-3}$) | Regime |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Resisto-jet | 2–100 | 200–300 | $10^{21}$–$10^{23}$ | continuum/kinetic |
   | Arcjet | 2–700 | 400–1500 | $10^{22}$–$10^{23}$ | continuum/kinetic |
   | Ion thruster | 0.01–200 | 1500–5000 | $10^{17}$–$10^{18}$ | kinetic |
   | Hall thruster | 0.01–1000 | 1500–6000 | $10^{18}$–$10^{19}$ | kinetic |
   | PPT | 0.05–10 | 500–2000 | $10^{20}$–$10^{22}$ | continuum/kinetic |

2. **Rarefaction** (§2.2.1): flow is rarefied for $\mathrm{Kn}>0.01$.
   DSMC (after Bird) needs $\Delta t$ well below the mean collision time and
   cells of the order of the mean free path; MCC (after Birdsall) serves
   heavy-species collisions in hybrid particle–fluid codes.
3. **Hybrid PIC and quasi-neutrality** (§2.2.2, eq. 2.4): PIC ions, fluid
   electrons; for $T_e$ of several eV quasi-neutrality holds down to about
   $10^{14}$ m$^{-3}$, except in the inter-grid region of ion thrusters;
   the field comes from the potential given by the electron momentum
   equation; DSMC collisions follow the move step.
4. **Resisto-jets and arcjets** (§§3.1–3.2): DSMC within 5 % of measured
   $I_{sp}$ for a H$_2$ resisto-jet and closer to Pitot data than
   Navier–Stokes; arcjet continuum models "typically" within 10 %; DSMC
   with kinetic Ohmic heating within 10 % for a 1.4 kW H$_2$ arcjet.
5. **Ion thrusters** (§3.3): ion optics is "one of the most mature areas";
   PIC with Monte Carlo charge exchange predicts accelerator-grid erosion
   (pits-and-grooves of the NSTAR 8200 h test needs a long downstream
   domain to capture backflowing charge-exchange ions); multi-aperture
   integration for NEXT agrees within 2 % on thrust, $I_{sp}$ and beam
   current; grid current and erosion remain "more problematic".
6. **Hall-thruster principle and configurations** (§3.4, pp. 678–680,
   Fig. 13): a "grid-less electrostatic acceleration device"; SPT with a
   channel of a few centimetres and ceramic walls, TAL with a few
   millimetres and metallic walls; $I_{sp}$ 1500–2500 s at overall
   efficiency about 50–60 %; 14 operational spacecraft (May 2005). SPT-100:
   density about $10^{17}$ m$^{-3}$, ion velocity about 16 km/s, annular
   diameter 100 mm, peak field about 200 G, so electrons are magnetized and
   ions are not. "Almost all numerical modeling studies of Hall thrusters
   have considered the SPT configuration."
7. **Hall oscillations and model lineage** (§3.4): oscillations of
   10–50 kHz observed, first predicted by Boeuf & Garrigues (1D); 2D hybrid
   PIC-ion/fluid-electron models (Fife & Martinez-Sanchez with electron
   sub-cycling, Koo & Boyd, the Hagelaar/Boeuf group) "represent the state
   of the art"; their accuracy "depends heavily" on the electron mobility.
8. **Anomalous mobility unresolved** (§3.4, Fig. 16): measured electron
   current exceeds the classical electron–atom value; near-wall
   conductivity and Bohm mobility are the candidates; "No general mobility
   model has yet been formulated." For the P5 thruster (Koo & Boyd) the
   measured centreline potential lies between the wall-collision and the
   Bohm simulations (drop too early with the first, too late with the
   second; read off the figure).
9. **Fluid and fully kinetic Hall models** (§3.4): steady 2D multi-fluid
   models run in minutes against many hours for unsteady hybrids; Yim et
   al.'s SPT-100 erosion model matches measured wall erosion at 160–1000 h
   (Fig. 17). Fully kinetic PIC needs an increased electron-to-ion mass
   ratio and an increased vacuum permittivity; Blateau et al. and Irishkov
   et al. over-predict thrust by about 10 % and efficiency by about 50 %.
   Fluid and hybrid schemes predict thrust and power only within about
   10 %.
10. **PPTs** (§3.5): MACH2 MHD sometimes predicts performance, but the
    agreement is "almost certainly fortuitous" because it under-predicts
    the ablated mass by an order of magnitude, hence late-time ablation;
    micro-PPT modelling chains an ablation model, a Knudsen-layer fluid
    model and a DSMC-PIC plume.
11. **Accuracy ranking** (§4): resisto-jets and ion thrusters within 5 %;
    arcjets, Hall thrusters and PPTs within 10–20 %. Needed: faster methods
    and better data (sputter yields, SEE coefficients, electron mobility,
    C/F ionization cross sections).

# Models and numerics

A review; it solves nothing new. Continuum: compressible Navier–Stokes
with source terms (eqs. 2.1–2.3). DSMC, MCC and PIC as in Key claims 2–3;
the PIC cycle is density from ions, field, accelerate, move, then DSMC
collisions. Hall hybrids in $r$–$z$ from a virtual anode line to a
cathode line in the near plume (Fig. 15). No cell sizes, time steps,
particle counts, weights, scaling factors or anomalous coefficients are
given, only qualitative run times. Codes named: MACH2; Koo & Boyd's model
is not named.
