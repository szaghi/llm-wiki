# Summary

The paper proposes a cable–rib tension deployable antenna structure:
radial ribs (centre, main, upper and lower beams joined by hinges)
stiffened by tensioned Kevlar cables, carrying a prime-focus parabolic
cable-net reflector. Closed-form geometry sizes an 18 m aperture with
focal ratio 0.5 and gives the unit height and beam lengths. A
geometrically nonlinear finite-element model (cable and beam elements,
tangent stiffness $K_T=K_E+K_G$) gives the natural frequencies.
Comparing 4, 6, 8 and 10 units on surface density and on the index
$\mu=f_1/\sigma$ selects six units. A genetic algorithm in MATLAB
minimises mass over lowest frequency under a frequency constraint and
buckling-motivated bounds, for three boundary-condition models. A 1.8 m
six-unit prototype was deployed and hammer-tested: measured mean
$f_1=21.30$ Hz against 21.81 Hz from the finite-element model.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Geometry** (§3.2, Eqs. 10–15, computed). For $D=18$ m and
   $f/D=0.5$, the minimum unit height is $H_{\min}=y^2/(4f)=2.25$ m
   (Eq. 11). With beam angles of 30° and the longest beam at most 6 m
   (the fairing limit), $H_{\max}=3$ m (Eq. 13). The design takes
   $H=2.25$ m, $a=b\approx5.1$ m, $c=d=4.5$ m and a flare angle of about
   53.13° (Eq. 15).
2. **Unit height** (§3.2.3, Eq. 14, Fig. 6, computed). The normalised mass
   $m=\rho s[18+(4-2\sqrt3)H]$ grows with $H$ while $f_1$ falls (from
   about 0.44 Hz at $H_{\min}$ to about 0.365 Hz at $H_{\max}$; Fig. 6b, plot reading), hence
   $H=H_{\min}$.
3. **Cable angle** (§3.2.2, attributed to Jones et al., ref. [24]). A
   cable gives maximum stiffness at 54.74°; the authors "round up" to 60°.
4. **Number of units** (§3.3, Table 1, Figs. 11–12, computed). Surface
   density $\sigma=m/S_e$ is lowest at six units (0.35 kg/m²); $f_1$
   decreases monotonically with the number of units (about 0.53, 0.44,
   0.38, 0.35 Hz; Fig. 11b, plot readings); the index $\mu=f_1/\sigma$ peaks at six units (about
   1.27 Hz·m²/kg; Fig. 12, plot reading).
5. **Tensioned cables** (§3.4, Table 2, computed). Adding them raises the
   first three frequencies from 0.44, 0.52, 0.69 Hz to 0.56, 0.72,
   1.02 Hz (ratios 1.27, 1.38, 1.48) for a mass increase from 59.12 to
   61.17 kg (ratio 1.03).
6. **Optimum per boundary condition** (§4.2, Tables 4, 5, 7). Model 1
   (fixed at the lowest point): $W=44.69$ kg, $f_1=1.43$ Hz, fitness
   31.3. Model 2 (fixed at the outer endpoint): 58.91 kg, 0.83 Hz, 70.98.
   Improved Model 2 (the constrained upper beam a separate variable):
   46.99 kg, 0.88 Hz, 53.40, i.e. 20% less mass and 6% more frequency
   than Model 2. Model 3 (with a spacecraft boom, required $f_1$ 0.4 Hz):
   52.95 kg, 0.43 Hz, 123.14. The highest $f_1$ comes with the centre
   beam fixed.
7. **Against the initial design** (§4.2.3, Table 6). Improved Model 2
   raises $f_1$ from 0.56 to 0.88 Hz (+57%) and lowers weight from 61.17
   to 46.99 kg (−23%); its surface density, 0.28 kg/m², is compared with
   about 0.32 kg/m² stated for AstroMesh of the same diameter and focal
   ratio (attributed, ref. [6]).
8. **Prototype test** (§5.3, measured). Five hammer tests of the 1.8 m
   prototype give $f_1$ from 20.79 to 21.95 Hz, mean 21.30 Hz; the
   finite-element value is 21.81 Hz, an error of "merely 2.4%". The
   6.5 kg prototype satisfies $f_1\ge20$ Hz.
9. **Aperture scaling** (§5.3, Fig. 24, figure only). $f_1$ decreases with
   aperture diameter for all three boundary conditions, ordered
   centre-fixed above outer-fixed above boom-fixed.

# Methods

- **Geometry** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–3): local nodal coordinates of one unit,
  rotated about the axis by $2\pi/n$ between adjacent units.
- **Dynamics** (§2.2, Eqs. 4–7): $M\ddot u+C\dot u+K_Tu=F$ with
  $K_T=K_E+K_G$, $K_G$ the prestress geometric stiffness; cable and beam
  element matrices (deferred to ref. [21]) assembled through a
  transformation matrix; undamped free vibration
  $(K_T-\omega^2M)\bar u=0$ solved by spectral decomposition of
  $M^{-1}K_T$. The finite-element code is written in MATLAB.
- **Reflector and edge cable** (§3, Eqs. 9–18): paraboloid
  $x^2+y^2=4fz$, net depth $h=D^2/(16f)$; catenary edge cable
  $T=w\sqrt{L^4/(64h_c^2)+x^2}$; inscribed and effective areas
  (Eqs. 16, 18). Inputs: distributed load 8 N/m, maximum catenary
  tension 200 N, beam diameter 100 mm and thickness 1 mm, cable diameter
  2 mm, hinge masses 2 kg and 0.5 kg; carbon-fibre tube beams and Kevlar
  cables (mass from Eq. 20).
- **Optimisation** (§4.1, Eqs. 22–26, Table 3): design vector of beam
  diameters, the cable diameter and the wall thickness; fitness
  $\xi(s)=W/f_1$ (plus hinge masses); constraint $[f_1]-f_1\le0$; bounds
  1–2 mm on thickness, 40–150 mm on beam diameters, 0–10 mm on the cable
  diameter, justified by Euler and wall buckling (ref. [27]) with
  $E=3.50\times10^{11}$ N/m². Cable pretension is disregarded. Genetic
  algorithm: population 50, 100 or 300 generations, stochastic-uniform
  selection, uniform crossover at 80%, mutation at 5%; MATLAB 2015a on a
  3.20 GHz i5 with 4 GB RAM, run times from 7 min 46 s (Model 1) to
  23 min 18 s (Model 3).
- **Prototype** (§5.1): 1.8 m, six units, carbon-fibre beams and
  aluminium-alloy hinges (modelled as point masses), a linkage beam
  parallel to the main beam; outer diameters 35, 14, 8, 10, 8 mm, all
  1 mm thick, cable diameter 1 mm; densities 1940 and 1440 kg/m³ for
  carbon fibre and Kevlar; total 6.5 kg with motor and gearbox. Tests: a
  deployment test (torsion springs, then motor) and a hammer modal test
  with accelerometers and an LMS system.
