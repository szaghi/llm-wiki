# Summary

The paper designs a linear time-varying model predictive controller
(LTV-MPC) for three-axis attitude control of small satellites that
carry only magnetorquers, in elliptic low Earth orbits, for nadir and
for inertial pointing. Nonlinear 3-2-1 Euler-angle kinematics and
rigid-body dynamics with gravity-gradient torque are linearised by small
angles into a state-space model; the time-varying disturbance (the
eccentricity-driven pitch torque for nadir pointing, a gravity-gradient
term for inertial pointing) is augmented into the state matrix
(Eqs. 65, 75). The constrained MPC (dipole-moment saturation) is solved
as a quadratic programme with MATLAB `quad` at each 10 s step. For
inertial pointing the conventional LTV-MPC diverges, and the authors add
exponential data weighting, to improve the Hessian conditioning at long
prediction horizons, and a Laguerre-function parameterisation of the
control increments, to reduce the on-line load. All evidence is
nonlinear simulation with noise-free measurements (§§3.2, 4.4, 5).

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Underactuation** (§§1, 2.3.2, stated, standard). A magnetorquer-only
   satellite is instantaneously underactuated: the control torque
   $\mathbf T_c=\mathbf m\times\mathbf B_B(t)$ (Eq. 16) cannot produce
   three independent torques at any instant.
2. **Elliptic-orbit disturbance** (Eqs. 40, 42, attributed to the paper's
   ref. [12]). In an elliptic orbit the orbit-frame rate $\omega_j$ varies
   in time, producing a known pitch torque
   $F_2=[0\ \dot\omega_j\ 0]^T$ with
   $\dot\omega_j=-2e\mu\sin f/R^3$ (as printed).
3. **Augmented state** (Eqs. 65–68, 75, constructive). Augmenting the
   disturbance into the state,
   $X'=[\phi\ \theta\ \psi\ \dot\phi\ \dot\theta\ \dot\psi\ 1]^T$, gives a
   standard LTV state-space form usable for the MPC prediction.
4. **Nadir pointing** (§3.2, Fig. 4, shown by simulation). With UMPSat2
   inertia in a POPACS-type orbit ($e=0.0727$), $N_p=60$, $N_c=10$, the
   conventional LTV-MPC converges in about two orbital periods with
   dipole moments within the 15 A·m² limit; X and Z dipoles are generated
   periodically to counter the pitch torque.
5. **Inertial pointing** (§§4.1, 4.4, Fig. 5, shown by simulation). With
   NPSAT1 inertia in the DTUSAT2 orbit ($e=0.0013$) the conventional
   LTV-MPC (Table 4) oscillates and diverges after three orbits; the
   variant with exponential weighting and Laguerre functions (Table 5:
   $N_p=150$, $N_c=100$, $N=100$, $\xi=0.9$, $\alpha=1.002$) converges within
   two orbits without steady-state error, with dipoles far below the
   30 A·m² limit.
6. **Exponential weighting** (Eq. 76, attributed to refs. [23, 27, 28]).
   Weights $\alpha^{-2j}$ with $\alpha>1$ reduce the growth of the Hessian
   condition number with $N_p$; $\alpha=1$ recovers the standard cost
   (Eq. 69). No condition numbers are reported.
7. **Laguerre parameterisation** (Eq. 83, §4.3, stated).
   $\Delta\mathbf u_i(k)=\mathbf L_i(k)^T\eta_i$ expresses prediction and
   cost in the coefficient vector $\eta$ and "reduces the on-line
   computational load"; no timing or operation count is given.
8. **Nadir parametric study** (§5, Table 6, Figs. 6–10, shown by
   simulation). Convergence improves with $N_p$; $N_c$ has a small
   effect; the conventional LTV-MPC becomes unstable at large $N_p$
   (see Recorded as printed), whereas the Laguerre-weighted version at
   $N_p=100$ does not, though it does not significantly improve
   convergence. The authors select $N_p=80$, $N_c=20$ for the
   conventional nadir controller.
9. **Inertial parametric study** (§5, Table 7, Figs. 11–13). The
   conventional LTV-MPC diverges in every tested case, so only the
   proposed controller is plotted; convergence improves with $N_p$, $N_c$
   matters little, and a nonzero reference attitude of 10°, 30°, 50° is
   reached.

# Methods

- **Frames and models** (§§2.1–2.3): inertial, local-vertical
  local-horizontal orbit and principal body frames. Tilted-dipole
  geomagnetic field (Eqs. 2–3, dipole strength
  $\mu_m=7.746\times10^{15}$ Wb·m, coelevation 170.0° for 2010, Earth rate
  360.99°/day); position integrated from TLE orbital elements. Euler's
  equation with control, gravity-gradient (gravitational parameter
  $3.986\times10^{14}$ m³/s²) and disturbance torques (solar radiation,
  drag, residual dipole).
- **Linearisation**: small angles, per-axis equations (Eqs. 26–28,
  53–55), control torque linearised with the orbit- or inertial-frame
  field (Eqs. 31, 57), inertia ratios
  $\sigma_1=(J_y-J_z)/J_x$ and cyclic (Eq. 41). The small-angle validity
  bound is delegated to the paper's ref. [19].
- **Discretisation and MPC**: MATLAB `c2d` at sampling interval 10 s;
  model matrices frozen within one horizon; prediction over $N_p$,
  increments over $N_c$, quadratic cost (Eqs. 69–70), input-amplitude
  constraints (Eqs. 71–74), receding horizon.
- **Laguerre functions** (Eq. 80, read from the rendered page):
  $\Gamma_m(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^2}}{z-\xi}\left(\frac{1-\xi z}{z-\xi}\right)^{m-1}$,
  $0\le\xi<1$, generated by a difference equation (Eq. 82) with
  $\beta=1-\xi^2$.
- **Simulation**: nonlinear plant, noise-free rate and attitude
  measurements, gravity-gradient and residual-dipole disturbances
  (Fig. 3); gains tuned by trial and error. The QP settings, `c2d`
  method and plant integrator are not given.

# Tests and results

- **Nadir baseline** (Tables 1–2): altitude 543.8832 km, period 6114 s,
  $e=0.0727$, semi-major axis 7227.5 km, inclination 81.0192°; inertia
  2.543, 5.525, 1.833 kg·m²; maximum dipole 15 A·m². $N_p=60$, $N_c=10$,
  4 orbits, step 10 s, control weight $10^{1.5}I_{3\times3}$, state weight
  $I_{7\times7}$. Initial Euler angles about 10°, rates $10^{-3}$ rad/s
  per axis, residual dipole 0.5 per axis (units not printed).
  Convergence in about two orbits, pitch hardest; plotted dipoles within
  about ±4 A·m².
- **Inertial baseline** (Tables 3–5): altitude 822.3718 km, period
  6076 s, $e=0.0013$, semi-major axis 6983.9 km, inclination 98.6854°;
  inertia 5.0, 5.1, 2.0 kg·m²; maximum dipole 30 A·m². Conventional:
  $N_p=60$, $N_c=20$, control weight $10^{-3}I$. Proposed: $N_p=150$,
  $N_c=100$, control weight $10^{3}I$, $N=100$, $\xi=0.9$, $\alpha=1.002$,
  3.5 orbits. Initial Euler angles 5°, zero rates, residual dipole 0.1
  per axis. The proposed controller keeps the Euler angles within a few
  degrees and plotted dipoles within about ±1.5 A·m².
- **Table 6** (nadir, ten cases): cases 1–5 NPSAT1 inertia, Euler angles
  −30°, 20°, 30°, rates 0.001 rad/s, $(N_p,N_c)$ = (10,5), (30,5),
  (60,5), (60,10), (60,20); cases 6–10 UMPSat2 inertia, Euler angles 15°,
  −15°, 15°, rates 0.004, 0.005, −0.004 rad/s, $(N_p,N_c)$ = (60,20),
  (80,20), (80,10), (80,40), (100,20), case 10 with Laguerre functions
  and exponential weighting.
- **Table 7** (inertial, five NPSAT1 cases): Euler angles 45°, −30°, 35°
  with $N_p$ = 150 or 180, $N_c=30$; Euler angles 5° per axis, rates
  ±0.003 rad/s, $N_p=150$, $N_c$ = 20 or 30 (rates converge within 1.5
  orbits); a POPACS-orbit case reaching the reference 10°, 30°, 50°.
- No computation time is reported for any controller variant. Plot
  values above are approximate readings.
