# Summary

The minimum-fuel, single-boost, planar exo-atmospheric ascent of a
launcher upper stage whose non-reignitable chemical engine
($I_{sp}=300$ s) thrusts continuously with a parametric thrust-level
profile, linear in time (Eq. 3) or bilevel (Eq. 4), the profile
parameters being optimisation variables. With the Pontryagin Maximum
Principle the paper proves that $p_mm$ is constant (Eq. 25), hence that
the velocity-costate modulus is constant (Eq. 26), which yields, through
the author's companion paper (ref. [63]), a closed-loop implicit law for
the thrust pitch angle (Eq. 27). From the transversality conditions it
proves that injection occurs at an apsis with horizontal thrust and zero
flight-path angle (Eqs. 40–41), approached from above for a perigee or
circular-orbit injection. The optimal control problem reduces to a
nonlinear program in 2 or 3 unknowns (Eq. 57). An analytical
gravity-loss and final-mass estimate (Eqs. 55–56) is checked on a GTO
example (§3, Table 1). The paper has no plasma or electric-propulsion
content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Constant $mp_m$** (§2.2, Eqs. 13–25, proved): for both thrust models
   the first-order conditions on the profile parameters (Eq. 12) give
   $p_m(t)m(t)=p_0m(t_f)$ on $[t_0,t_f]$, assuming $T>0$ throughout.
2. **Constant velocity costate** (Eq. 26, proved):
   $p_v(t)=p_0m(t_f)/v_e$.
3. **Closed-loop thrust direction** (Eqs. 27–29; derivation deferred to
   ref. [63]): $\sin(\gamma-\theta)=\frac{v_c}{v}\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt{1-3\sin^2\theta}}$,
   $v_c=\sqrt{\mu/r}$, with
   $\omega=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r^3}(1-3\sin^2\theta)}$; Eq. 27 is solved
   on line by dichotomy.
4. **Apsis injection** (§2.3, Eqs. 30–41, proved, assuming $v\ne0$ and
   $\cos\gamma\ne0$): the final condition $(v_c^2+\omega^2r^2)\sin\theta=0$
   gives $\sin\theta(t_f)=0$ and $\gamma(t_f)=0$: injection at perigee or
   apogee.
5. **Approach direction** (Eqs. 42–45): at the perigee of an ellipse
   $v>v_c$ gives $\dot\theta=(v-v_c)/r>0$, so the trajectory ends with
   negative $\gamma$ ("from above with a downward leg"); apogee injection
   is from below; for a circular orbit $\ddot\theta=T/(mr)>0$, again from
   above.
6. **Loss estimate** (§2.4, Eqs. 46–56, approximate): small angles give
   $2\theta\approx\gamma$; gravity loss
   $\Delta V_G\approx\frac23r[\omega_0-\omega_f]$ (Eq. 53) and
   $\Delta V_G\approx\frac14v_c\gamma_0^2$ with $v_c=\sqrt{\mu/r_0}$
   (Eq. 55); final mass
   $m_f\approx m_0e^{-(\Delta V_G+v_p-v_0)/v_e}$ (Eq. 56). Angle-of-attack
   losses are stated to stay small.
7. **Reduced problem** (§3, Eq. 57, Table 1, shown numerically):
   maximise $m(t_f)$ over $T_1,T_2,t_1$ subject to the target apogee and
   perigee, by a generalized reduced-gradient algorithm. The final mass is
   nearly independent of the thrust model (1422.5–1422.7 kg), and the
   analytical guess (1445 kg) is within 2%.
8. **Perigee-argument control** (§3, conclusion, stated): the angular
   range to injection depends on the thrust profile (69.3–79.2°), so the
   argument of perigee could be controlled "with reduced performance
   loss" by thrust-level tuning. A suggestion, not demonstrated.

# Methods

* **Dynamics** (Eq. 1): planar point mass in an Earth-centred inertial
  frame, central gravity only (drag, third body, $J_2$ and higher, solar
  pressure neglected, justified for 100–300 km altitude and kN-class
  thrust), $\dot m=-T/v_e$. Target orbit by specific energy and angular
  momentum (Eq. 2); orientation free.
* **Thrust models:** linear $T=T_1+(t-t_0)T_2$ (Eq. 3); bilevel, $T_1$
  on $[t_0,t_1)$ and $T_2$ on $[t_1,t_f)$ (Eq. 4). Unknowns
  $T_1,T_2,t_1,t_f$ and the thrust direction (Eq. 5).
* **Maximum principle:** Hamiltonian $H=H_0+T\Phi$ with switching
  function $\Phi=p_v/m-p_m/v_e$ (Eq. 8); thrust along the velocity
  costate (Eq. 7); transversality with multipliers (Eq. 10); $H\equiv0$
  (free final time, Eq. 11); parameter conditions
  $\int_{t_0}^{t_f}(\partial T/\partial T_i)\Phi\,dt=0$ (Eq. 12, ref.
  [34]); no interior-point constraint at $t_1$.
* **Numerics:** closed-loop propagation of Eq. 1 with Eq. 27 solved by
  dichotomy, stopped at zero flight-path angle (for perigee injection
  $\gamma$ first falls to a negative minimum, then returns to zero); outer
  nonlinear program Eq. 57 by generalized reduced gradient. Integrator,
  step and tolerances are not stated.

# Tests and results

One case (§3): coplanar GTO, apogee 36000 km, perigee 300 km altitude;
$I_{sp}=300$ s, $v_e=2942$ m/s; $m_0=10000$ kg, $h_0=150$ km,
$v_0=5000$ m/s, $\gamma_0=30$° (initial osculating orbit: apogee
661.7 km, perigee −5209.4 km); $R_E=6378137$ m,
$\mu=3.986005\times10^{14}$ m³/s². Preliminary guess (Eq. 58):
$v_p=10155$ m/s, $\Delta V_G\approx536$ m/s, $m_f\approx1445$ kg.

| Variable | Linear | Bilevel $t_1=250$ s | 500 s | 750 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $T_1$ | 26.467 kN | 37.942 kN | 26.339 kN | 23.378 kN |
| $T_2$ | −10.976 N/s | 12.842 kN | 13.799 kN | 14.015 kN |
| $m_f$ | 1422.5 kg | 1422.7 kg | 1422.5 kg | 1422.5 kg |
| $t_f$ | 1308.5 s | 1476.3 s | 1374.4 s | 1299.5 s |
| angular range $\varphi_f$ | 69.7° | 79.2° | 73.6° | 69.3° |
| $\Delta V_G$ | 555 m/s | 545 m/s | 555 m/s | 558 m/s |
| $\Delta V_T$ | 27 m/s | 37 m/s | 28 m/s | 25 m/s |

The bilevel solution is "rather insensitive to the switching time".
Figs. 2–4 plot thrust level, altitude and angle of attack against time
(linear and bilevel with $t_1=500$ s) without further numbers.
