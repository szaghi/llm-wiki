# Summary

The paper designs a kerosene-fuelled RBCC combustor whose expansion angle
and geometric-throat area are set by hydraulic actuators to suit the
inflow (design range flight Mach 2–7, Table 1), and tests it in ground
direct-connect experiments at simulated flight Mach 2, 3, 4 and 6 on the
NWPU RBCC bench. A low-flow primary (strut-mounted) rocket acts as a pilot
flame for the secondary kerosene; at Mach 4 and 6 shutting the rocket off
gives a smooth rocket-ramjet to ramjet mode transition. Steady 3D RANS
simulations in CFD++ (cubic k–ε, Eulerian dispersed-phase spray,
Westbrook–Dryer quasi-global kerosene mechanism) then compare the variable
geometry combustor (geometric throat) with a fixed geometry combustor
(thermal throat) at Mach 3 and 6. The variable combustor has a shorter,
more concentrated heat-release zone, a lower combustor Mach number, higher
combustion efficiency (about 0.90 against 0.81 at Mach 3; 0.92 against
0.78 at Mach 6), higher exit total-pressure recovery and higher specific
impulse (+18.6% at Mach 3, +26.2% at Mach 6).

# Key claims

1. **Design by isobaric constant-velocity heating** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–4).
   Under isobaric heating the velocity stays constant, which gives the exit
   to inlet Mach ratio from the total-temperature ratio (Eq. 1), the
   exit-to-inlet area ratio (Eq. 2, neglecting viscous drag and added fuel
   mass; the inverse square of Eq. 1), the total-pressure ratio (Eq. 3) and
   the geometric-throat area from the choked mass-flow formula (Eq. 4).
   Stated as design relations, not derived in the text.
2. **Configuration schedule** (Table 1). Expansion and throat area ratios
   decrease with flight Mach over 2–6; at Mach 7 the combustor switches to
   an expanded configuration with no geometric throat.
3. **Rocket pilot ignition** (§4.1, Fig. 6; Conclusion 1). The secondary
   kerosene does not ignite reliably without an ignition source; a primary
   rocket at 0.12 kg/s (at most 5% of the Mach 2 airflow) gives reliable
   ignition and stable, efficient combustion at Mach 2–6. Experiment.
4. **Mode transition at Mach 4 and 6** (§4.1, Figs. 6c–d, 7, Table 5).
   Turning the rocket off moves the operation from rocket-ramjet to ramjet
   mode; the pre-combustion shock train moves toward the combustor and
   stays in the isolator; thrust falls by 10.8% (Mach 4) and 12.2%
   (Mach 6); specific-impulse gains of 28.2% and 37.1% are stated;
   combustion efficiency rises from 0.898 to 0.947 (Mach 4) and from 0.890
   to 0.966 (Mach 6). Experiment.
5. **Geometric against thermal throat at Mach 3** (§4.2.1, Figs. 8–10).
   The variable combustor's heat release is concentrated between the fuel
   pylons and the throat, in a zone 20% shorter (stated as helping thermal
   protection), with isobaric combustion; the fixed combustor (2°
   expansion) has pressure rising before secondary injection and then
   falling. Both run in ramjet mode; the fixed combustor forms a thermal
   choke with a complex 3D sonic surface. Simulation.
6. **Mach 6** (§4.2.2, Figs. 11–13). The variable combustor forms a
   pre-combustion shock train in the isolator and runs fully in ramjet
   mode; the fixed combustor has supersonic flow outside the pylons, a
   "ramjet-scramjet mixed mode". Simulation.
7. **Total-pressure recovery** (§4.3, Fig. 14), mass-weighted section
   total pressure over the isolator-inlet total pressure: at the combustor
   exit, variable against fixed, 0.63 against 0.58 at Mach 3 (+8.6%) and
   0.32 against 0.24 at Mach 6 (+33.3%). The variable combustor loses more
   in the isolator (stronger shock train) and less to combustion (lower
   Mach number). Simulation.
8. **Combustion efficiency** (§4.3, Fig. 15), temperature-rise
   definition with the CEA adiabatic flame temperature: at Mach 3 heat
   release starts at the rocket exit (x/H = 10) and rises quickly after
   secondary injection (x/H = 14); exit 0.90 against 0.81; Mach 6: 0.92
   against 0.78. Simulation.
9. **Performance** (Table 6; Conclusion 3). Specific impulse 1096 against
   924 s at Mach 3 and 1016 against 805 s at Mach 6, attributed jointly to
   lower combustor Mach number and higher combustion efficiency.
   Simulation.
10. **Definitions** (§4.1). Specific impulse is thrust over standard
    gravity times total fuel flow (rocket propellant included); in the
    experiment the throat is assumed choked, so throat total pressure
    follows from static pressure and total temperature from mass flow; the
    pressure force is the integrated wall pressure and the drag is taken
    from CFD.

# Methods

* **Facility** (§2.2, Figs. 2–4): NWPU RBCC direct-connect bench;
  gas-oxygen-alcohol vitiated-air heater with oxygen made up to mass
  fraction 0.23; facility nozzle, isolator with a central strut housing the
  primary rocket, rectangular combustor (straight, fixed pylon and variable
  sections; the variable section is four hinged rods moved by two parallel
  hydraulic actuators), inner nozzle. The fixed combustor is the variable
  section set to an expansion without a throat.
* **Fuel**: liquid kerosene through streamwise-vortex pylons. Primary
  rocket (Table 4): 0.12 kg/s, O/F about 1.0, 1.5 MPa, 1850 K; species mass
  fractions (%) CO2 0.1, H2O 0.1, H2 5.7, CO 94.1.
* **Inflow** (Table 2):

  | Flight Mach | Air [kg/s] | Total temperature [K] | Total pressure [MPa] | Isolator Mach | Equivalence ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2 | 2.8 | 400 | 0.3 | 1.3 | 0.6 |
  | 3 | 4.7 | 600 | 0.6 | 1.6 | 0.6 |
  | 4 | 4.0 | 880 | 0.9 | 2.0 | 0.7 |
  | 6 | 3.3 | 1650 | 1.3 | 2.5 | 1.0 |

* **Diagnostics**: wall pressure (−0.1 to 1 MPa, 0.25% full scale, up to
  1000 Hz); outlet flame photographs. Total temperature and internal drag
  not measured.
* **Numerics** (§3): CFD++, steady 3D nonlinear RANS with cubic k–ε
  (setup validated earlier on HIFiRE-2); Eulerian dispersed-phase spray;
  turbulence–chemistry interaction neglected; C10H20 surrogate with the
  Westbrook–Dryer quasi-global mechanism (10 species, 12 reactions; e.g.
  C10H20 + 5O2 → 10CO + 10H2, $A$ = 4.50E+09, exponent 0,
  $E$ = 1.256E+08 J/kmol, orders 0.25 and 1.5). Half domain from facility
  nozzle to combustor exit; rocket inlet from CEA; adiabatic no-slip walls.
  ICEM grid of 1.2 million nodes, first cell 0.01 mm, maximum combustor
  wall unit 30; 64 processors, about 5 days per case. Scheme and order not
  stated; grid independence only from an earlier fixed-geometry study; the
  Fig. 5 validation against sidewall pressure at Mach 3 and 6 is judged in
  words ("well fits").

Simulations (Table 6, fixed / variable): Mach 3 pressure force 3163 /
3684 N, drag −274 / −257 N, thrust 2889 / 3427 N, specific impulse 924 /

2849 N, −582 / −471 N, 1845 / 2378 N, 805 / 1016 s, 0.78 / 0.92, 0.24 /
0.32.

* Fig. 6: in hot flow, boundary-layer separation and a normal shock train
  in the isolator make the combustor inlet flow subsonic.
* Fig. 7: a bright Mach disk at the outlet in ramjet mode.
* Fig. 11: at Mach 6 with the rocket off, recirculation behind the rocket
  lets the flame propagate upstream.
