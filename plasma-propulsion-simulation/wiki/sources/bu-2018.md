# Summary

A flight-control paper. It designs a longitudinal tracking controller for
a flexible air-breathing hypersonic vehicle (AHV) on the Bolender–Doman /
Parker et al. curve-fit model, working directly on **non-affine** models
in which the fuel equivalence ratio $\Phi$ and the elevator deflection
$\delta_e$ enter only through the force and moment fits. The dynamics
split into a velocity subsystem (input $\Phi$) and an altitude subsystem
(input $\delta_e$), both under a prescribed-performance error
transformation (Bechlioulis–Rovithakis). Each subsystem uses one radial
basis function neural network whose squared weight norm is adapted
(minimal-learning-parameter scheme), so the controller has only two
adaptive parameters. The altitude subsystem is rewritten as a third-order
pure-feedback chain in the flight-path angle, avoiding back-stepping, and
a high-order tracking differentiator (HTD) estimates the unmeasured
derivatives. Lyapunov arguments give semi-global uniform ultimate
boundedness (SGUUB). Two simulated climbs are compared, qualitatively
only, with the author's earlier neural back-stepping controller. The only
propulsion content is a curve-fit scramjet thrust model; the paper has no
plasma or electric-propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Non-affine decomposition** (§2.1, Eqs. 8–9, shown; the
   decomposition is attributed to refs. [18–25]): $\dot V=f_V(V,\Phi)$;
   $\dot h=V\sin\gamma$, $\dot x_1=f_1(x_1,x_2)$, $\dot x_2=x_3$,
   $\dot x_3=f_3(\mathbf x,\delta_e)$ with $x_1=\gamma$, $x_2=\theta$,
   $x_3=Q$.
2. **Prescribed performance** (§2.3, Eqs. 13–17, attributed to ref.
   [41]): $-\delta\rho(t)<e(t)<\rho(t)$ for $e(0)\ge0$ (mirrored
   otherwise), $\rho(t)=(\rho_0-\rho_\infty)e^{-lt}+\rho_\infty$;
   transformed error $\varepsilon=\tfrac12\ln\frac{e/\rho+\delta}{1-e/\rho}$
   for $e(0)\ge0$; a bounded $\varepsilon$ guarantees the prescribed
   performance (Remark 1).
3. **Velocity controller** (§3.1, Eqs. 26–27): a PI-type law in the
   transformed error with a neural term
   $-\tfrac12\varepsilon_1\hat\varphi_1\mathbf h_1^T\mathbf h_1$ and the
   adaptation $\dot{\hat\varphi}_1=\frac{\lambda_1}{2}\varepsilon_1^2\mathbf h_1^T\mathbf h_1-2\hat\varphi_1$.
   Theorem 1 (proved, Lyapunov function of Eqs. 30–33): SGUUB for
   $k_{V1}>\iota_{1M}^2/2$.
4. **Pure-feedback transformation** (§3.2, Eqs. 34–39): with
   $z_1=\gamma$, $z_2=\dot\gamma$, $z_3=\ddot\gamma$ the altitude dynamics
   become $\dot z_1=z_2$, $\dot z_2=z_3$, $\dot z_3=k_h\delta_e+F_h$; the
   flight-path command $\gamma_d$ (Eq. 34) gives
   $\dot\varepsilon_2+k_\gamma\varepsilon_2=0$ when $\gamma\to\gamma_d$.
5. **Altitude controller** (Eqs. 41, 48–49): error function
   $E=(d/dt+\mu)^3\int_0^te_0\,d\tau$, $e_0=\gamma-\gamma_d$; the elevator
   law uses HTD estimates and one adaptive parameter. Theorem 2 (proved):
   SGUUB for $k_{h1}>\iota_{2M}^2/2$. Remark 10 argues boundedness under
   HTD estimation errors.
6. **Computational cost** (Remarks 5, 6, 12, stated): one network per
   subsystem where ref. [24] needs two; 2 adaptive parameters against
   "thirteen online parameters" in ref. [33]. No timing or operation
   count is given.
7. **Simulation** (§4, stated from figures): "better transient and
   steady-state performance" than neural back-stepping (ref. [33]) and no
   high-frequency chattering; the back-stepping controller gives smoother
   attitude, flexible-state and input responses in Case 1. No error metric
   is tabulated.

# Methods

* **Plant:** the flexible longitudinal AHV model of refs. [38,39]
  (Eqs. 1–7): five rigid states ($V,h,\gamma,\theta,Q$) and two flexible
  modes; thrust linear in $\Phi$ with altitude- and dynamic-pressure-
  dependent fits; exponential atmosphere. Coefficient values are "the same
  as [39]" and not reprinted.
* **Neural approximation:** Gaussian RBF networks (Eq. 11; universal
  approximation attributed to ref. [40]) with 20 nodes each and evenly
  spaced centres; velocity network input $V\in[7700,7900]$ ft/s; altitude
  network input in $\mathbb R^6$ (velocity, flight-path, pitch, pitch
  rate and the two HTD estimates) on stated ranges.
* **Minimal learning parameter:** the scalar $\varphi_i=\|W_i^*\|^2$ is
  adapted instead of the weight vectors (refs. [21,22,37]), with Young's
  inequality bounding.
* **HTD** (Eq. 19, from ref. [42]): a fourth-order chain with parameter
  $R$ and coefficients $a_1$–$a_4$; estimation errors are bounded for a
  large but finite $R$ (Remark 3).
* **Setup** (§4): climb from trim ($V=7700$ ft/s, $h=85000$ ft,
  $\theta=1.6325$°, flexible states 0.9700 and 0.7967) to
  $h=85500$ ft, $V=7900$ ft/s; references from a second-order filter
  (natural frequency 0.1, damping 0.9, Eq. 55); gains $k_V=1$,
  $k_{V1}=0.2$, $k_{V2}=0.8$, $k_\gamma=2$, $k_h=1$, $k_{h1}=50$,
  $\mu=7$, $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=0.05$, $R=0.05$, $a_1=a_3=0.5$,
  $a_2=a_4=0.1$, $\delta_1=\delta_2=0.6$; every aerodynamic coefficient
  perturbed as $C_0[1+0.3\sin(0.05\pi t)]$ (up to 30%). Integrator, time
  step and software not stated; figures run to 80 s.

# Tests and results

* **Case 1:** initial velocity error 1 and altitude error 0.015 (units not
  stated); performance functions $\rho_1=(3-0.5)e^{-0.15t}+0.5$,
  $\rho_2=(0.1-0.01)e^{-0.3t}+0.01$. Tracking errors stay inside the
  bounds (Figs. 2–7); pitch rate oscillates at about ±0.5 deg/s, decaying
  over roughly 0–50 s and larger than with back-stepping (Fig. 4, figure
  reading); initial high-frequency content in the second flexible mode
  (Fig. 5); adaptive parameters bounded (Fig. 7).
* **Case 2:** initial errors −3 and −0.01; $\rho_1=(5-0.5)e^{-0.15t}+0.5$.
  Same qualitative conclusions (Figs. 8–13); robustness to the parameter
  uncertainty called "satisfactory".
* No quantitative comparison (RMS or maximum error, settling time, CPU
  time) is given.
