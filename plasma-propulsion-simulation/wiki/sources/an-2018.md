# Summary

A flight-control paper. It designs a disturbance-rejection controller
based on nonlinear dynamic inversion (NDI) for the longitudinal dynamics
of an air-breathing hypersonic vehicle (AHV), using the Wang–Stengel-type
7-state model of a 200 ft winged-cone configuration. A nonlinear
disturbance observer (the Yang–Li–Chen form) estimates the mismatched
lumped disturbance $d_l$; the estimate enters the NDI law through a
state-dependent compensation matrix $\Upsilon(x)$ that cancels the
disturbance from the velocity and altitude outputs in steady state
(Theorem 1). With a constant diagonal observer gain the closed loop is
shown to be globally input-to-state stable (ISS), where the earlier work
it builds on (ref. [24]) obtained local ISS. A MATLAB/Simulink simulation
compares the law with baseline NDI and integral NDI. The scramjet appears
only as a thrust-coefficient fit and as a source of thrust fluctuation
lumped into $d_l$; the paper has no plasma, electric-propulsion or
particle-simulation content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Model** (§2.1, Eq. 1; attributed to refs. [16,19]):
   $\dot x=f(x)+g(x)u+d_l$, $x=[V,h,\gamma,\alpha,q,\delta T,\dot{\delta T}]^T$,
   $u=[\delta T_{com},\delta E]^T$; thrust, lift and drag are
   $\bar qS$ times coefficient fits (Appendix A).
2. **Assumption 1:** $d_l$ and $\dot d_l$ bounded, and
   $\dot d_l\to0$ as $t\to\infty$.
3. **Observer** (Eqs. 2–3): $\dot z_{d_l}=-l(x)[\lambda(x)+z_{d_l}+f(x)+g(x)u]$,
   $\hat d_l=z_{d_l}+\lambda(x)$, $l=\partial\lambda/\partial x$; error
   dynamics $\dot e_{d_l}=-l(x)e_{d_l}+\dot d_l$. Lemma 1 (asymptotic
   convergence if the unforced error dynamics are asymptotically stable)
   is quoted from ref. [24].
4. **Bounded-derivative case** (Eqs. 4–9, shown): with $\lambda=Lx$,
   $L=\mathrm{diag}(l_1,\dots,l_7)$ and $-L$ Hurwitz, and only
   $\dot d_l$ bounded,
   $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|e_{d_l}\|\le(c_1/c_2)\sup_{t\ge t_0}\|\dot d_l\|$,
   so large gains make the residual arbitrarily small (the exponential
   bound is cited from Khalil, Theorem 3.9).
5. **Relative degrees** (§3.1): input relative degree (3, 4) for the
   velocity and altitude errors; the disturbance is mismatched (input
   relative degree exceeds disturbance relative degree).
6. **Control law** (Eq. 12): $u=G^{-1}(x)[v(x)+\Upsilon(x)\hat d_l-F(x)]$,
   with $G$ the 2×2 matrix of Lie derivatives.
7. **Theorem 1** (proved, Eqs. 13–21): with the baseline linear law of
   Eq. 13, $\Upsilon$ chosen as the negative gradient of the stated Lie
   derivative combinations (Eq. 14), and Hurwitz characteristic
   polynomials $P_V(s)$ (cubic) and $P_h(s)$ (quartic), the effect of
   $d_l$ on velocity and altitude is completely compensated in steady
   state. The velocity case is proved in full; for altitude the proof is
   said to be "similar".
8. **Linear interpretation** (Eqs. 22–24, shown): the compensation term
   equals the disturbance-compensation gain $D_V=[-k_{V1},-k_{V2},-1]$ of
   the linear design of ref. [18] applied to an equivalent disturbance.
9. **Invertibility of $G$** (Appendix B): $|G|$ is printed in closed form;
   it vanishes only if $\cos\gamma=0$ or if
   $T+\cos\alpha\,\partial L/\partial\alpha+\sin\alpha\,\partial D/\partial\alpha=0$,
   which "cannot hold in the admissible flight envelope". Asserted, not
   shown.
10. **Stability** (§3.2, Eqs. 25–27; proved via Khalil Lemma 5.5): with
    constant $l=L$ the cascade is ISS, needing only bounded $d_l$ and
    $\dot d_l$ (Remark 2). Remark 3 claims global ISS against the local ISS
    of ref. [24]; the working point is a trajectory, not a fixed state.
11. **Performance recovery** (Remark 2, stated with refs. [27,28]): when
    $d_l=0$ the loop recovers the baseline NDI behaviour.

# Methods

* **Plant:** longitudinal rigid-body AHV, 7 states (Appendix A):
  $\mu=1.39\times10^{16}$ ft³/s², $m=9375$ slugs,
  $I_{yy}=7\times10^6$ slug·ft², $\bar c=80$ ft, $S=3603$ ft²,
  $R_E=20{,}903{,}500$ ft; exponential atmosphere with
  $\rho_0=6.7429\times10^{-5}$ slugs/ft³ at $h_0=85{,}000$ ft and scale
  height $h_s=21{,}358$ ft. Coefficient fits (read from the rendered
  page): $C_L=\alpha(0.493+1.91/M)$,
  $C_D=0.0082(171\alpha^2+1.15\alpha+1)(0.0012M^2-0.054M+1)$,
  $C_T=0.0105[1-164(\alpha-\alpha_0)^2]\,(1+17/M)\times\{1.15\delta T$ if
  $\delta T<1$; $1+0.15\delta T$ otherwise$\}$, plus pitching-moment fits.
  The second-order engine dynamics constants $k_1,k_2,k_3$ are not
  printed.
* **Design:** NDI in Lie-derivative coordinates ($\varepsilon_V\in\mathbb R^3$,
  $\varepsilon_h\in\mathbb R^4$), observer of Eq. 2; gains from LQR with
  $Q=\mathrm{diag}(10,2,2,5,2,2,2)$, $R=\mathrm{diag}(1,3)$ (MATLAB);
  Lie derivatives by the Symbolic Math Toolbox.
* **Comparators:** baseline NDI ($\Upsilon=0$, no observer, Eq. 31) and
  integral NDI (Eq. 32).
* **References:** 5th-order linear velocity filter driven by a step
  command; altitude reference holding constant dynamic pressure (Eq. 30).
  MATLAB/Simulink; integrator and step not stated.

# Tests and results

One simulated case (§4): cruise at $V_0=15{,}060$ ft/s,
$h_0=110{,}000$ ft, $\alpha_0=1.8$°, $\delta T_0=0.21$,
$\bar q_0=2370$ psf; velocity command 17,060 ft/s for $0\le t<500$ s,
then 15,060 ft/s to 800 s. A seven-component lumped disturbance (sinusoids,
constants, an exponentially damped sinusoid and a step $8S(t-300)$ in the
second component) acts for $t<500$ s and is zero afterwards. Observer
gain $l=\mathrm{diag}(40,40,10,10,15,5,5)$; LQR gains printed for the
proposed/baseline and the integral laws.

Results are graphical only (Figs. 2–6), with no error norms or tables:
the proposed law has the smallest velocity and altitude tracking errors
(the other laws stay within about ±60 ft/s and ±200 ft, read from the
axes); the step at 300 s pushes baseline NDI off zero error and integral
NDI overshoots, mostly in velocity; after 500 s the proposed and baseline
responses coincide (performance recovery); flight-path angle, angle of
attack and pitch rate settle; throttle and elevator fluctuate to cancel
the sinusoids; the observer estimates track the true disturbances.
