# Summary

The paper proposes the Projecting Surface Method (PSM), a mesh-geometry
design procedure for large deployable mesh reflectors (DMRs). It places
the cable-net nodes deliberately off the desired working (RF) surface,
on an iteratively adapted "projecting surface", so that the best-fit
surface of the faceted mesh approaches the desired paraboloid. It
introduces a "direct RMS error" (§3.3, Eqs. 13–14): the area-weighted
normal deviation of the flat facets from the desired working surface,
with exact facet-to-paraboloid distance formulas in the Appendix
(Eqs. A.1–A.11). The PSM (§4, Eqs. 15–16, six steps) wraps any existing
form-finding method: the force density method (FDM), dynamic relaxation
(DRM) or the fixed nodal position method (FNPM). Numerical examples
(§5) on AstroMesh-type front nets of 37, 271 and 817 nodes, centre-feed
(diameter 12 m, focal ratio 0.26) and offset-feed (parent diameter
30 m, focal ratio 0.2, offset 1 m), give direct-RMS reductions of
36.8–73.6% with tension distributions practically unchanged. The paper
has no plasma or propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Nodal-deviation RMS is misleading** (§1, §3.1, argued): the
   nodal-deviation RMS error (Eq. 8) is zero whenever all nodes lie on
   the working surface, whatever the node count, and ranks the facets
   of Fig. 4 (items a and b) in the wrong order. Argued, not proved.
2. **Direct RMS error** (§§3.2–3.3, Eqs. 13–14): the best-fit RMS error
   of Agrawal et al. [19] (Eqs. 10–12) is not a true measure of the
   deviation from the desired surface and rests on a shallow-paraboloid
   approximation. The proposed
   $\delta_{d\text{-}rms}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{S_{mesh}}\sum_i\varphi_i}$ with
   $\varphi=\iint_S\mu^2\,d\tau\,d\upsilon$ uses the exact normal
   distance $\mu(\tau,\upsilon)$ (Eq. A.11, the root of smaller
   magnitude) and is claimed valid for deep and shallow reflectors
   (Conclusion i).
3. **Node-on-surface placement leaves a geometric error** (§5.1, Tables
   1–2, shown numerically): FDM with 37 nodes on the working surface
   yields a best-fit surface with focal-length error
   $\varepsilon_F=1.22$–1.59% and diameter error $\varepsilon_D$ above
   2.1% (Eq. 17), which node-on-surface placement cannot remove.
4. **PSM iteration** (§4, Eqs. 15–16): the projecting surface starts as
   the desired surface and is updated as
   $F^i_{pro}=F^{i-1}_{pro}+\kappa(F^i_{bf}-F_{des})$,
   $D^i_{pro}=D^{i-1}_{pro}+\lambda(D^i_{bf}-D_{des})$, until
   $\varepsilon=\sqrt{(F^i_{bf}-F_{des})^2+(D^i_{bf}-D_{des})^2}$ falls
   below a threshold. No convergence proof; $\kappa$, $\lambda$ are
   "properly selected" with no values given.
5. **Centre-feed reductions** (§5.2, Table 3, shown): PSM cuts the
   direct RMS error by 63.6/52.5/49.2% (FDM/DRM) and 72.8/73.6/71.4%
   (FNPM) for 37/271/817 nodes. The best 817-node case is 0.86 mm
   (PSM with FNPM) against 36.43 mm (FDM/DRM, nodes on the surface), a
   97.6% reduction.
6. **Offset-feed reductions** (§5.3, Table 5, shown): 58.0/44.7/36.8%
   (FDM/DRM) and 47.9/46.3/52.2% (FNPM).
7. **Tensions unchanged** (Tables 4 and 6, shown): e.g. centre-feed,
   271 nodes, FNPM, maximum-to-minimum tension ratio 2.1911 without and
   2.1917 with PSM.
8. **FDM and DRM agree** (§5.2, reported, not explained): identical
   results for centre feed, almost identical for offset feed.

# Methods

* Form-finding methods reviewed (§2): FDM (Schek [21], Linkwitz [22]),
  nodal equilibrium $\sum_j\frac{T_{ij}}{L_{ij}}(x_i-x_j)=f_{xi}$ (Eq. 1),
  force density $q_{ij}=T_{ij}/L_{ij}$ (Eq. 3), linear system
  $C^TQCx=f_x$ once $Q$ is known (Eq. 4); a planar mesh projected
  vertically onto the working surface, vertical forces balanced by
  tension ties [11]. DRM [23]: $m\ddot x+c\dot x+kx=f_x$ (Eq. 5), a
  fictitious mass–damper system relaxed to steady state. FNPM [16–18]:
  pseudo-geodesic mesh on a spherical reference surface of equal height
  and aperture (Eq. 6, $H=H_p$, $R_s^2=(R_s-H)^2+D^2/4$; Fig. 3, five
  steps), vertical projection, then the tension optimisation
  $\min\lVert T-T_{des}\rVert^2$ subject to $MT=T_{tie}$, $T>0$ at fixed
  nodal coordinates (Eq. 7).
* Surface-error measures (§3): nodal deviation
  $\delta_{nd\text{-}rms}=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum(\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2+\Delta z^2)}$
  (Eq. 8); a centroid-based variant from [12] (Eq. 9); best-fit RMS
  (Eqs. 10–12, minimised over $F_{bf}$, $H_{bf}$ by nonlinear
  optimisation); direct RMS (Eqs. 13–14).
* Appendix: local facet frame (A.1–A.7), paraboloid
  $z-H=-\frac{1}{4F}(x^2+y^2)$ (A.8), a quadratic in $\mu$ (A.9–A.10)
  and its smaller-magnitude root (A.11). How the facet integral of
  Eq. 13 is evaluated (quadrature or closed form) is not stated.
* PSM (§4, Fig. 10): initial guess; mesh on the reference surface (the
  plane for FDM/DRM, the sphere for FNPM); projection onto the
  projecting surface; best-fit surface (§3.2); comparison (Eq. 15);
  update (Eq. 16).
* Scope: only the facet-versus-surface geometric error; tie-load
  variation, member-length imperfection, ring distortion and thermal
  strain are neglected (§3). Front net only, boundary nodes fixed (§5).
  $T_{des}=[10\ 10\ \cdots\ 10]$ N (Eq. 18). No code, solver, tolerance
  or iteration count is reported.

# Tests and results

* **Node-on-surface FDM, 37 nodes** (§5.1): Table 1 ($D_{des}=12$ m):
  $F_{des}=6$, 8, 10 m give $F_{bf}=5.9269$, 7.8822, 9.8408 m and
  $\varepsilon_F=1.22\%$, 1.47%, 1.59%. Table 2 ($F_{des}=12$ m):
  $D_{des}=14$, 16, 18 m give $D_{bf}=13.6978$, 15.6577, 17.6187 m and
  $\varepsilon_D=2.16\%$, 2.14%, 2.12%.
* **Centre feed** (§5.2, Table 3, $D_{des}=12$ m,
  $F_{des}/D_{des}=0.26$, direct RMS error in mm):

  | nodes | FDM/DRM, working | FDM/DRM, projecting | FNPM, working | FNPM, projecting |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 37 | 117.84 | 42.84 | 85.28 | 23.19 |
  | 271 | 43.06 | 20.44 | 9.51 | 2.51 |
  | 817 | 36.43 | 18.50 | 3.01 | 0.86 |

  Table 4 (271 nodes, same column order): mean tension 9.9958 / 9.9962
  / 9.7982 / 9.7972 N; maximum-to-minimum tension ratio 1.0048 / 1.0046
  / 2.1911 / 2.1917; mean tie tension 2.8961 / 2.8786 / 3.1516 /
  3.1571 N; tie ratio 2.2620 / 2.1848 / 1.2500 / 1.2493.
* **Offset feed** (§5.3, Table 5, parent $D_p=30$ m, $F_p/D_p=0.2$,
  offset $e_{off}=1$ m, mm):

  | nodes | FDM/DRM, working | FDM/DRM, projecting | FNPM, working | FNPM, projecting |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 37 | 64.34 | 27.04 | 65.27 | 33.99 |
  | 271 | 8.89 | 4.92 | 7.87 | 4.23 |
  | 817 | 3.34 | 2.11 | 2.53 | 1.21 |

  Table 6 (271 nodes): mean tension 9.9999 / 9.9999 / 9.9419 /
  9.9419 N; tension ratio 1.0008 / 1.0008 / 2.0118 / 2.0118; mean tie
  tension 1.6007 / 1.6017 / 1.5971 / 1.5971 N; tie ratio 2.7985 /
  2.8017 / 3.0194 / 3.0194.
* Conclusion (v): 817-node offset feed, 1.21 mm against 3.34 mm, a
  63.8% reduction.

registry counterpart.
