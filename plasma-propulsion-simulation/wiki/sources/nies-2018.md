# Summary

The paper describes a two-step toolchain for day-ahead, battery-aware
payload scheduling of the GOMX-3 3U CubeSat (ESA in-orbit demonstration,
deployed from the ISS on 5 Oct 2015). Step 1 models the satellite
(background load, jobs, a linear battery, attitude, sun) as a network of
priced timed automata and computes cost-optimal schedules with UPPAAL
CORA, using six heuristics against state-space explosion and a "dynamic"
scheme that schedules the horizon interval by interval. Step 2 validates
each candidate schedule on a stochastic kinetic battery model (KiBaM)
with capacity limits, which yields a cumulative depletion risk;
schedules with too high a risk are rejected and regenerated. Three
in-orbit experiments (November 2015, February 2016, March 2016) are
reported with predicted against measured loads and state of charge
against measured voltage. The discussion names battery ageing as a
future model extension. The paper is an extended, revised version of an
IAC 2016 paper.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Kinetic battery model** (§2, attributed). With available charge $a$,
   bound charge $b$, load $I$, diffusion rate $p$ and available-well width
   $c\in[0,1]$:
   $$\dot a(t)=-I(t)+p\Bigl(\frac{b(t)}{1-c}-\frac{a(t)}{c}\Bigr),\qquad \dot b(t)=p\Bigl(\frac{a(t)}{c}-\frac{b(t)}{1-c}\Bigr).$$
   Stated to be the simplest model capturing the rate-capacity and
   recovery effects.
2. **Stochastic state of charge** (§2, attributed). With capacity limits
   $a_{max}$, $b_{max}$ and stochastic load and state of charge, the
   distribution is a triple: joint density inside the limits, density on
   the line $a=a_{max}$, and cumulative depletion probability
   $z\in[0,1]$. An analytical under-approximation after a task exists; its
   derivation is omitted and referred to the authors' ref. [3].
3. **Linear battery in the automata** (§3.3, §4.2). The timed-automata
   model uses a linear single-well battery because UPPAAL CORA cannot
   express the KiBaM nonlinearity; this model is "known to be
   unjustifiably optimistic", hence the validation step. Stated.
4. **Heuristic ablation** (§4.1, table on p. 731, one unspecified
   instance; CORA time unit not printed): all heuristics 2.6, 172452
   states, optimum 262792; all but 2: 80.7, 5474775, 262792; all but 6:
   86.1, 6029126, 243269; all but 3: 8.9, 592233, 258081. Shown:
   heuristics 2 and 6 are the most effective; the lower costs without 3
   or 6 correspond to useless or battery-straining schedules.
5. **In-orbit results** (§5, Table 3).

| Run | Horizon | Initial SoC | Threshold | Min SoC | Depletion risk | Safe Mode entries |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 Nov 2015 | 24 h | 85% | 40% | 40.3% | ≈19.3% | 2 |
| 14 Feb 2016 | 36 h | 90% | 55% | 69.0% | $<10^{-50}$ | 0 |
| 20 Mar 2016 07:00 | 60 h | 90% | 55% | 55.9% | $<10^{-2}$ | 0 |

   Shown: the KiBaM risk estimate flagged the November schedule, which
   entered Safe Mode twice (attributed to a data discrepancy in the
   energy budget).
6. **Against manual scheduling** (§6). The schedules are "of unmatched
   quality" relative to manual scheduling (experiments performed, planning
   mistakes, workload, depletion risk). Stated, without numbers.
7. **Battery ageing** (§6). In laboratory measurements of GOMX-3 cells the
   capacity decreases very slowly for about the first 130 full cycles,
   then the per-cycle degradation increases by as much as a factor of 5.
   Stated, no data shown.

# Methods

* **Spacecraft** (§1): GOMX-3, 3U CubeSat (30 × 10 × 10 cm, 3 kg),
  launched on HTV-5 on 19 Aug 2015. Payloads: 3-axis ADCS (2° precision
  or better; reaction wheels and magnetorquers), ADS-B receiver
  (1090 MHz), FPGA software-defined radio for L-band (1–2 GHz) INMARSAT
  spot-beam monitoring, X-band transmitter (8–12 GHz, up to 100 Mbps), UHF
  link to the Aalborg ground station. Power: 14.8 V Li-ion pack, 2.6 A h,
  NanoPower P31u; 11 P110 solar panels at about 5.5 W orbit average each.
* **Power budget** (Table 1): consumption (mJ/s) and background duty
  cycle per module, e.g. OBC 276/100%; battery heater 3360/0%; UHF 276
  receive/100%, 2630 transmit/0%; ADS-B 552/100%; magnetorquers 584/25%;
  reaction wheels 992/100%; GPS 1436/10.77%; SDR 3863 receive, 2825
  transmit/0%; X-band transmitter 9120/0%.
* **Thresholds** (§3.2, §3.4): Safe Mode at 40% of capacity; the
  scheduling lower bound is 40% (aggressive) or higher, e.g. 55%.
* **Automata** (§3.3, Fig. 4): background load; generic job (window,
  skip, warm-up, attitude, piecewise-constant loads); linear battery
  checked against the lower bound, with a depletion location; attitude
  (worst-case slewing 5 min, combined warm-up raised to 10 min); sun
  (insolation/eclipse).
* **Costs** (§3.4): penalties for skipped jobs and unused window time;
  L-band and X-band skip-cost rates $2\cdot\Delta X$ and $\Delta L$ for a
  1/2 ratio; goal query over an orbit counter reaching 20, with an
  end-of-interval charge requirement.
* **Heuristics** (§4.1): take every job when the battery is almost full;
  deadlock on depletion; one L-band job precedes two X-band jobs; balance
  L-band jobs across INMARSAT satellites (difference at most 2); always
  schedule UHF; upper bound on discharging loads. The horizon is split
  into subintervals scheduled successively with carried-over state
  (greedy, trading optimality).
* **Validation** (§4.2): the schedule as a task sequence is fed to a
  simplified StoKiBaM tool (C++); initial state of charge a truncated 2-D
  Gaussian around the automata initial state; white noise on loads. On
  failure, cost-equal optimal schedules are tried first, then the
  threshold is raised slightly.
* **Implementation** (§4.4): a Python wrapper (GomSpace POWERSIM csv
  windows → automata templates → UPPAAL CORA → trace parsing → StoKiBaM →
  csv and plots); mission times 24–72 h. Supplementary material URL
  printed: https://www.powver.org/gomx3-supplementary-material/.

# Tests and results

* **Fig. 3** (§2), illustrative battery: $a_{max}=b_{max}=5\times10^6$,
  $c=0.5$, $p=0.0003$, initial state of charge uniform over
  $[0.3,0.7]a_{max}\times[0.3,0.7]b_{max}$, tasks (500, U[3000,3600]) and
  (600, U[−3300,−3900]); roughly 75% of the density flows into depletion
  after the first task (units not printed).
* **Ablation, remaining rows** (§4.1): all but 1: 10.2, 700429, 262792;
  all but 4: 3.7, 224517, 262792; all but 5: 2.7, 175191, 262792.
* **Table 2** (§4.3): sample schedule of 17 Nov 2015, accesses 1
  (1863.720 s, scheduled), 2 (1739.781 s, not scheduled), 15
  (1878.493 s, not scheduled).
* **November 2015** (Fig. 7): 24 h; 4 L-band, 1 X-band, 5 UHF passes;
  risk "over 19%"; Safe Mode entered twice.
* **February 2016** (Fig. 8): 36 h; L-band hardware active for one whole
  orbit; 30 min ADCS warm-up for L-band; drift between state of charge and
  voltage from about 3 p.m. on day 1, traced to a mismatch in the net
  power-balance input.
* **March 2016** (Fig. 9): 60 h (20 Mar 07:00 to 22 Mar 19:00); 10 min
  ADCS cool-down after non-nominal-attitude jobs (drag minimisation);
  slight continuous drift after about 18 h, attributed to deliberate
  pessimism (worst-case slewing, underestimated solar infeed).
* State of charge, voltage and load curves are normalised to $[0,1]$; the
  paper notes that voltage and state of charge are "generally not
  comparable".
