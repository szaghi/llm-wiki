# Summary

A policy review of European space integration. §2 traces the EU treaty
history (Paris 1951 to Lisbon 2007, which introduced an explicit EU space
competence) and ESA's origin (ELDO and ESRO; Convention signed 30 May
1975, in force 30 Oct 1980), its mandatory and optional programmes, the
geographical-return principle, and the European Cooperating State and
Associate Member routes to membership. §3 describes the 26 Oct 2016
ESA–EU Joint Statement "Shared Vision and Goals for the Future of Europe
in Space", the European Commission's "Space Strategy for Europe"
(COM(2016) 705, Oct 2016) and ESA's 2 Dec 2016 Ministerial Resolution
"Towards Space 4.0 for a United Space in Europe", with the Space 4.0 and
Space 4.0i concepts. §4 argues for an all-encompassing single European
Space Strategy to reinforce European integration. The paper is
descriptive and normative: no data, models or analysis, and no mention of
propulsion, plasma or numerics.

# Key claims

All claims are stated or quoted from official documents; none is shown.

1. The Lisbon Treaty (signed 2007) introduced, for the first time
   explicitly, an EU space competence (§2.1, citing ref. [11]).
2. ESA programmes are mandatory (General Budget, Science Programme;
   contributions proportional to GNP) or optional (subscription);
   industrial policy rests on the geographical-return principle (Art. IV
   of Annex V of the ESA Convention) (§2.2).
3. Since 2008 the European Cooperating State scheme prepares Central and
   Eastern European states for ESA membership; accession is accompanied by
   an Industrial Incentive Scheme; Slovenia became an Associate Member in
   2016 (§2.2, refs. [14]–[17]).
4. The ESA–EU Joint Statement of 26 Oct 2016 sets three goals to 2030:
   maximise the integration of space into European society and economy;
   foster a globally competitive European space sector; ensure European
   autonomy in accessing and using space in a safe and secure environment
   (§3.2, Fig. 5, quoted from ref. [24]).
5. The Commission's Space Strategy for Europe sets four objectives:
   benefits for society and economy; a competitive and innovative sector;
   autonomy in access to and use of space; Europe as a global actor (§3.3,
   quoted from refs. [29]/[38]).
6. Space 4.0 is presented as a fourth era of space activity (after
   astronomy, the Space 2.0 race and Apollo, and a third, ISS era),
   marked by private actors and commercialisation, by analogy with
   Industry 4.0; ESA's derivative Space 4.0i stands for Innovate, Inform,
   Inspire, Interact (§3.4, refs. [40]–[42]).
7. The authors' thesis: a single all-encompassing European Space Strategy
   can underpin the future of Europe in space and reinforce European
   integration (abstract, §§1, 4, Fig. 8). Argued, not shown.

# Methods

A narrative policy review of treaties, ESA Convention articles, and EU
and ESA official documents and speeches. No models, experiments,
equations or data analysis. Figs. 1–4 map the ESA and EU member states in
1975, 1997, 2007 and 2017; Figs. 5–8 reproduce the European goals, the
Commission and ESA strategy documents, and the proposed single strategy.
