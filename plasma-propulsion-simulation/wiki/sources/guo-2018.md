# Summary

The paper proposes an integrated scheme for a high-resolution observation
spacecraft. A six-strut Stewart-type vibration isolation platform with
magnetic-suspension struts (a permanent magnet between two
electromagnets, PD-type control current) attenuates the high-frequency
disturbance of control moment gyroscopes (CMGs); an adaptive attitude
controller handles the low-frequency vibration of flexible solar arrays
and an unknown payload inertia. Because the payload is assumed comparable
in mass and inertia to the bus (JWST-like), the base-platform motion is
retained and a 6×12 platform transfer matrix is derived (Eqs. 7–9), with
the nonzero channels in closed form. The undamped natural frequencies of
three channels (Eq. 10) size the proportional gain; the derivative gain
is chosen from a Bode plot. The attitude controller (Eqs. 17–23) is a
certainty-equivalence adaptive law with a Lyapunov/LaSalle argument; its
gains follow the rule, attributed to [30], that the controller bandwidth
be well below the platform's natural frequency. Two numerical examples
(unknown inertia; slowly varying inertia) show attitude stability
improving from about $10^{-4}$ to about $10^{-6}$ deg/s with the
platform. The paper has no plasma or propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Transfer matrix with bus motion** (§2.2, Eqs. 8–9): with the bus
   motion retained, the platform mass, stiffness and input matrices are
   12×12 and the transfer matrix $G_{pl}=K_{pl2}(s)(M_{pl}s^2+K_{pl}(s))^{-1}$
   is 6×12; from its last 6×6 block and the platform symmetry only
   $G_{11}$, $G_{15}$, $G_{22}$, $G_{24}$, $G_{33}$, $G_{42}$, $G_{44}$,
   $G_{51}$, $G_{55}$, $G_{66}$ are nonzero. Shown (derivation outline,
   block-matrix inversion).
2. **Reduction** (§2.2): every channel depends on both payload and bus
   mass and inertia; for a payload much smaller than the bus the 6×6
   matrices of earlier work ([30], [37], [38]) are recovered. Stated.
3. **Natural frequencies** (§2.3, Eq. 10, derivative gain neglected):
   $\omega_{n33}=\sqrt{2(m_b+m_p)k_{magnet}/(m_bm_p)}$, with
   $\omega_{n11}$ and $\omega_{n66}$ in closed form (printed slips in
   Recorded as printed); $k_{magnet}=k_0k_{pmag}$,
   $k_0=\mu_0N^2A/x_0^3$.
4. **Example platform** (§2.3, Fig. 2, Table 1): one main resonant peak
   per main-diagonal channel; natural frequencies 1.49 Hz and 4.62 Hz
   within the 1–10 Hz design range; −53 dB at 100 Hz; −7.71 dB near 0 Hz,
   "close to" $M_p/(M_b+M_p)$; coupling channels share the natural
   frequencies of the main-diagonal ones. Shown in a figure.
5. **Magnet equilibrium** (§2.1, Eq. 5): with symmetric bias the magnet
   sits mid-strut, $x_{am}=0$. Shown.
6. **Adaptive attitude law** (§3.3, Eqs. 22–23):
   $T_c=L_2\hat\alpha+f_2(\omega_b)+\hat I_{s3}K_ps$,
   $\dot{\hat\alpha}=-\Gamma L_2s$, $s=\dot e+ke$, with
   $V=\tfrac12s^TI_{s3}s+\tfrac12e_\alpha\Gamma^{-1}e_\alpha$ and
   $\dot V=-s^T\hat I_{s3}K_ps$; global asymptotic stability claimed via
   LaSalle. Proved as a sketch (rigour issues in Recorded as printed).
7. **Initial inertia guess** (§3.3, item 2): the initial estimate should
   be below the true inertia; too large raises the actual bandwidth above
   the reference and may destabilise, too small starts low and tracks
   upward. Stated qualitatively, via the [30] bandwidth rule; no
   counter-example simulation.
8. **Platform benefit** (§3.4, Tables 4, 6): attitude stability improves
   from about $10^{-4}$–$10^{-3}$ deg/s to about $10^{-6}$ deg/s and
   accuracy by about one order of magnitude; the settling time is
   unchanged because the controller bandwidth is far below the platform
   bandwidth. Shown numerically.
9. **Solar-array vibration** (§3.4, Fig. 4e–f): with the adaptive
   controller the modal vibrations converge "one time faster" (about
   twice as fast) than with a PD controller. Shown by figure only; the PD
   gains are not stated.
10. **Varying inertia** (§3.4, Example 2): with increasing payload inertia
    the high-frequency attenuation is better than in Example 1,
    "consistent with the frequency domain analysis". Stated.

# Methods

* Platform dynamics (§2.1): Newton–Euler equations of the payload and
  base platforms (Eqs. 1–2) taken from [30]; strut kinematics Eq. 3(a)–(h);
  magnetic strut force (Eq. 4)
  $F=-\frac{\mu_0N^2A}{4}\left(\frac{(i_0+i_{ax1})^2}{(x_0-x_{am})^2}-\frac{(i_0-i_{ax2})^2}{(x_0+x_{am})^2}\right)$
  with $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}$ N/A²; control current
  $i_{ax}^2=k_{pmag}(l_j-l_{j0})+k_{dmag}\dot l_j$.
* Small-angle linearisation, quasi-linear model (Eq. 6), Laplace transform
  (Eq. 7), transfer matrix (Eq. 8), 12×12 inversion by the block formula.
* Gain design: proportional gain from Eq. 10 (small damping, natural ≈
  undamped frequency, citing the single-axis study [39]); derivative gain
  from a Bode plot.
* Spacecraft model (§3.1): payload, platform, bus, four single-gimbal CMGs
  (pyramid-like, 30°) and two flexible solar arrays with modal equation
  $\ddot\eta_{ak}+2\xi_{ak}\Lambda_{ak}\dot\eta_{ak}+\Lambda_{ak}\eta_{ak}+F_{bak}\dot\omega_b=0$
  (Eq. 13); Newton–Euler plus Kane's method; CMG disturbance from static
  and dynamic rotor imbalance. Full model (Eqs. 11–13) for simulation,
  reduced attitude model (Eqs. 14–17, neglecting translation–rotation
  coupling, platform torques and relative payload motion) for design.
* Controller: error Euler angles $e=\theta_{bs}-\theta_b$ (Eq. 18); linear
  regressor $I_{s3}x=L(x)\alpha$ with
  $\alpha=[I_{xx},I_{yy},I_{zz},I_{yz},I_{xz},I_{xy}]^T$ (Eq. 19).
* Numerics: integration scheme, step and software not stated; plots to
  200 s, zoom windows 190–190.1 s.

# Tests and results

* Platform example (§2.3, Table 1): $N=50$, $A=1\times10^{-1}$ m²,
  $x_0=0.05$ m; payload platform 800 kg, inertia diag(810, 730, 690) kg m²;
  base 1100 kg, inertia [[1112.5, −20, −10], [−20, 912.5, −15],
  [−10, −15, 810]] kg m²; $k_{pmag}=1.2\times10^5$, $k_{dmag}=500$; design
  range 1–10 Hz. Results in claim 4.
* Example 1 (§3.4, Tables 2–4, Fig. 4): unknown payload inertia; manoeuvre
  from [0, 0, 0] to [30, 0, 0] deg. Bus 1000 kg, inertia [[1100, −20, −10],
  [−20, 900, −15], [−10, −15, 800]] kg m²; payload 800 kg, inertia
  diag(810, 730, 690) kg m²; solar array 40.25 kg, natural frequencies
  diag(5, 15, 38) rad/s, damping 0.01; CMG gimbal 6.7 kg, rotor 9 kg,
  static imbalance $[0.8, 1, 0.8]^T\times10^{-5}$ m. Control: $K_p=0.8$,
  $k=2/0.8$, initial inertia estimate 0.8 × true, $\Gamma=5\times10^2E_6$.
* Example 2 (Tables 5–6, Fig. 5): payload inertia changes from
  diag(810, 730, 690) to diag(1620, 1460, 690) kg m² (rate not stated);
  initial estimate equal to the initial true value.
* Accuracy (max − min attitude angle at steady state, deg) and stability
  (max − min angular velocity, deg/s), without / with platform, axes x, y,
