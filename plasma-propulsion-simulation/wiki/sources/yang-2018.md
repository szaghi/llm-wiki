# Summary

The paper writes the in-plane and out-of-plane libration equations of a
rigid (dumbbell) electrodynamic tether (EDT) in an elliptical orbit with
the geomagnetic field given by the International Geomagnetic Reference
Field truncated at degree and order 13, instead of the nontilted dipole of
earlier work (Peláez & Lara 2003, Peláez & Andrés 2005, Iñarrea et al.
2014). Because the Lorentz torque is linear in the field, the generalized
electrodynamic torques split into a nontilted-dipole part and a remainder
from the higher-order terms; the remainder is added as a perturbation to
the classical dipole equations. Starting from unstable periodic solutions
of the dipole equations, the perturbed equations are integrated until the
libration turns into tumbling, and the elapsed number of orbits is called
the "failure time". It is mapped against the electrodynamic parameter,
inclination, eccentricity and perigee altitude (Figs. 7–16), and the trends
are rationalized (Table 2) as a balance between the instability of the
periodic solution and the size of the perturbation. The conclusion is that
the dipole model is adequate for short-term but not long-term libration
prediction. The only plasma-related content is the Lorentz force on a
current-carrying wire: there is no current-collection, ionosphere or
plasma model.

# Key claims

Symbols below are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Torque decomposition** (§2.3, Eqs. 14–17). For a uniform tether
   current $\bar I$ and a field constant along the tether (taken at the
   centre of mass),
   $Q_\theta=\frac{\bar Il^2}{2}\Lambda\cos\phi[\sin\phi(B_x^{13}\cos\theta+B_y^{13}\sin\theta)-B_z^{13}\cos\phi]$,
   $Q_\phi=\frac{\bar Il^2}{2}\Lambda[B_y^{13}\cos\theta-B_x^{13}\sin\theta]$,
   with $\Lambda=(m_1-m_2)/M$; each splits as the sum of a term with the
   nontilted-dipole components (Eq. 16) and a term with the differences
   $B^{13}-B^{1}$ (Eq. 17). Derived (linearity of the Lorentz torque in the
   field).
2. **Nontilted dipole in the orbital frame** (Eq. 7, attributed to
   Williams 2009): $B_x^1=-2\frac{\mu_m}{r^3}\sin\nu\sin i$,
   $B_y^1=\frac{\mu_m}{r^3}\cos\nu\sin i$, $B_z^1=\frac{\mu_m}{r^3}\cos i$.
3. **Electrodynamic parameter** (Eq. 20):
   $\varepsilon=\frac12\frac{\Lambda}{m^*}\frac{\mu_m\bar I}{\mu}$. With
   dipole torques only, the libration equations (Eq. 8, from Iñarrea et al.)
   reduce to the classical Eq. 19 (attributed), which depends on
   $\varepsilon$, $e$, $i$ but not on the altitude; the perturbed system
   (Eq. 21) adds $Q_{\theta2}/(m^*l^2\omega^2\cos^2\phi)$ and
   $Q_{\phi2}/(m^*l^2\omega^2)$. Derived.
4. **Field and torque comparison** (§§3.1–3.2, Figs. 3–4; perigee altitude
   500 km, $e=0.2$, $i=40°$, $\varepsilon=0.5$, 5 orbits). IGRF-13 and
   dipole components and torques are "generally consistent", differing
   "mainly at the peak and valley", small against the overall strength.
   Shown graphically; no norms given.
5. **Onset of tumbling** (§3.2, Figs. 5–6; same parameters, initial state
   $[\theta,\dot\theta,\phi,\dot\phi]=[-0.13508\ \mathrm{rad},0.39475,0.39524\ \mathrm{rad},0.02536]$).
   The perturbed trajectory stays close to the dipole periodic solution
   for 2 orbits, deviates by 5 orbits and tumbles within 10. Shown.
6. **Dependence on $\varepsilon$ and $i$, circular orbits** (Figs. 7–8,
   500 km). Failure time decreases with $\varepsilon$; for
   $\varepsilon\gtrsim0.8$ it is below 10 orbits (Fig. 8). Against
   inclination it has a minimum near $i\approx40°$–$50°$ (Fig. 7). Shown.
7. **Elliptical orbits** (Figs. 9–12, 500 km). The same $\varepsilon$ trend;
   minimum in $i\approx30°$–$60°$ (Fig. 11) and $40°$–$50°$ (Fig. 12); the
   influence of $e$ on these curves is "small" (Figs. 10, 12). Shown.
8. **Dependence on $e$** (Figs. 13–14, 500 km). For $e<0.2$ the failure time
   is almost constant; it then rises to a maximum and drops rapidly. Shown.
9. **Dependence on altitude** (Fig. 15; $\varepsilon=0.5$, $i=40°$, $e=0$
   and 0.2). Failure time increases monotonically with perigee altitude;
   at equal altitude the elliptical case lasts longer. Shown.
10. **Rationalization** (Table 2). $\varepsilon$ raises both instability and
    disturbance, so failure time falls; with $i$ both first rise then fall,
    so failure time first falls then rises; $e$ raises instability and
    lowers disturbance ("offset"); altitude leaves instability unchanged
    and lowers disturbance, so failure time rises. Argued, not proved.
11. **Conclusion** (§4). "For short-term running, it is feasible to use the
    nontilted dipole model …; for long-term running, the nontilted dipole
    model will lead to high error." Stated; "short" and "long" are not
    quantified.

# Methods

* **Model:** dumbbell EDT (rigid, inextensible rod) in the station-keeping
  phase; tether length 1000 m, end masses 100 kg and 10 kg, tether mass
  5 kg (Table 1); reduced mass
  $m^*=\frac{(m_1+m_t/2)(m_2+m_t/2)}{m_1+m_2+m_t}-\frac{m_t}{6}$;
  independent variable the true anomaly. Libration equations Eq. 8 from
  Iñarrea et al.
* **Forcing:** only the Lorentz force on the tether element,
  $d\mathbf F=I(s)\,\mathbf t\times\mathbf B\,ds$ (Eq. 9), through virtual
  work (Eq. 10); current and field uniform along the tether. No
  current-collection (OML) model, no ionosphere; the electric field is
  explicitly out of scope (§1).
* **Geomagnetic field:** IGRF scalar potential expanded in Schmidt
  quasi-normalized associated Legendre functions to degree 13 (Eqs. 1–3),
  rotated from the local north–east–down frame to the inertial and the
  orbital frames (Eqs. 5–6, Appendix A). Earth's rotation is ignored
  (§2.2). The IGRF generation, epoch and coefficients are not stated.
* **Orbit:** orbital elements frozen during the attitude integration
  (end of §2.3), justified by their slow secular change.
* **Numerics:** periodic solutions of Eq. 19 are tabulated (Appendix B,
  Tables B1–B35, 4–5 significant digits), without the method used to
  obtain them; the introduction cites Poincaré continuation and Legendre
  pseudospectral methods. Time integrator, tolerances and step are not
  stated; the criterion that declares tumbling is not quantified.
