# Summary

Bai et al. study self-pulsation of a gas–liquid swirl coaxial injector,
liquid-centered (LCSC) with a 5 mm inner-post recess, in water and air at
atmospheric back-pressure. A quartz outer injector gives high-speed backlit
imaging inside the recess chamber and a Kulite transducer measures the
recess pressure; a 2D axisymmetric-swirl unsteady VOF simulation with the
RNG k–ε model (solver not named) reproduces the baseline case. The proposed
mechanism: the conical liquid sheet moves out to the recess wall and blocks
the annular gas, the confined pressure builds, the sheet is pushed inward
and thins, and the high-speed gas tears it open. Experiment and simulation
agree on frequency (3165 Hz against 3246 Hz) and amplitude (0.13 MPa
against 0.12 MPa) at 160 g/s liquid and 5 g/s gas. The paper fits frequency
linearly against liquid Reynolds number, reports a transition from about
3 kHz to an "ultra-high frequency" 8–9 kHz pulsation as gas flow increases
at large liquid flow, and maps lower and upper self-pulsation boundaries in
the liquid/gas momentum-flux plane, which it claims is a first.

# Key claims

1. **Blocking mechanism** (§3.2, Figs. 5, 7, 8; Conclusion 1). In a
   recessed LCSC injector self-pulsation is caused by the blocking action of
   the conical liquid sheet (cycle as in the Summary). Shown qualitatively
   by simulation contours and imaging; no causal test. The two-stage force
   argument for the ultra-high-frequency case "is based on the qualitative
   analysis" (§3.3.2).
2. **Experiment–simulation agreement** (§3.2, Figs. 8–9). At liquid 160 g/s
   and gas 5.0 g/s the simulation (probe 5) gives 3246 Hz and 0.12 MPa; the
   experiment (3.5 mm upstream of the exit) gives 3165 Hz and about
   0.13 MPa. The simulated peak is "about two times the atmospheric
   pressure", the minimum about 80% of atmospheric. Shown.
3. **Spray frequency equals pressure frequency** (§3.3.1, Fig. 10a;
   Conclusion 2) "in most conditions"; the pressure frequency is adopted as
   the characteristic self-pulsation frequency. Shown.
4. **Linear frequency law** (§3.3.1). At gas 5 g/s (gas Reynolds number
   35700), frequency in Hz $=890+0.089\times$ liquid Reynolds number, for
   liquid Reynolds number between 6800 and 39000; outside that range
   self-pulsation disappears. Empirical fit; the Reynolds-number definitions
   are not given.
5. **Effect of gas Reynolds number** (§3.3.1, Fig. 10b). At 40 and 80 g/s
   liquid the frequency rises slightly with gas flow, then falls, and
   pulsation disappears; at larger liquid flow it stays roughly constant,
   then jumps to an ultra-high-frequency pulsation of 8000–9000 Hz, in the
   gas window 11.67–16.01 g/s at 160 g/s liquid and 12.55–16.97 g/s at
   200 g/s. Shown.
6. **Waveforms** (§3.3.2, Figs. 9, 11). High-frequency pulsation has a
   positive half-wave much larger than the negative one (peak about
   0.21 MPa absolute); the ultra-high-frequency pulsation is nearly
   symmetric (peak about 0.13 MPa), 8332 Hz at 160 g/s liquid and 14.5 g/s
   gas. The annular gas reaching sonic speed in the ultra-high-frequency
   regime is stated, not shown.
7. **Self-pulsation boundaries** (§3.4, Fig. 12; Conclusion 3), in the
   plane of liquid against gas momentum flux. The lower (onset) threshold
   rises with liquid flow ("skewed parabola or exponential-like"), stated as
   consistent with Bazarov and Im et al.; the upper (vanishing) threshold
   falls quickly at small liquid flow and rises slightly at large liquid
   flow; the ultra-high-frequency band has nearly parallel boundaries, with
   no oscillation between the two states. Claimed plotted "for the first
   time". Shown.
8. **Two recess flow patterns** (§3.1, Figs. 5–6). At small liquid flow the
   whole sheet in the recess oscillates; at large liquid flow only the
   downstream sheet does. Stable sprays are solid cones whose angle
   decreases with gas flow. Shown.
9. **Competing mechanisms in the literature** (§1), stated: recess length
   as the most significant parameter and a petal-valve impedance model
   (Bazarov); acoustic resonance between the annular gas passage and the gas
   core (Huang et al.); frequency linear in both Reynolds numbers and
   suppression by ambient pressure (Im et al.); self-pulsation without
   recess with Kelvin–Helmholtz excitation identified by POD (Eberhart et
   al.); strongest pulsation near critical mixing (Kang et al.). The paper
   offers its mechanism for recessed injectors without adjudicating the
   others.

# Methods

* **Experiment** (§2.1, Figs. 1–2, Table 1): pressurized feed of water and
  dried air into ambient conditions; inner-post diameter 4.7 mm and length
  40.05 mm, annular gas gap 0.5 mm (8 and 9 mm diameters), swirl chamber
  10.2 mm, four 2 mm tangential ports, recess 5 mm. Photron SA-Z at 30000
  frames/s (exposure 80.4 μs, 1024 × 688 pixels); Kulite XCQ-080-7BARA
  sampled at 1000 kHz for 2 s; turbine flowmeters 0.5% full scale.
  Imaging and pressure in separate runs. Mass-flow deviation at most 4%
  (liquid) and 8% (gas); no frequency or amplitude uncertainty stated.
* **Conditions** (Table 2): seven series at recess 5 mm, liquid 40–285 g/s,
  gas 0.5–31.7 g/s.
* **Simulation** (§2.2): 2D unsteady axisymmetric-swirl VOF with CSF
  surface tension, ideal-gas air and incompressible water, shared momentum
  and energy equations, RNG k–ε with non-equilibrium wall functions;
  CICSAM interface, PRESTO pressure, PISO coupling, second-order upwind,
  time step 0.1 μs. The four tangential ports are replaced by a wall slit.
  Grid study at the baseline (123232, 193360, 258300 cells):
  intermediate–fine deviation under 0.012%, intermediate–coarse over
  15.4% (quantity not named); intermediate grid used. Only the baseline
  case is simulated.

# Tests and results

* Baseline: simulation 3246 Hz / 0.12 MPa, experiment 3165 Hz / about
  0.13 MPa; both spectra show a second peak near 6.3–6.5 kHz (read from the
  figures, not discussed).
* Fig. 10a (read from the figure, approximate): frequency from about
  1700 Hz at liquid Reynolds number about 7000 to about 4100 Hz at about
  38000.
* Fig. 10b (approximate): jump to 8–9 kHz at gas Reynolds number about
  0.85–0.9 × 10⁵ for 160 and 200 g/s liquid.
* Fig. 12 (approximate): liquid momentum flux up to about 2.6 × 10⁵ and gas
  up to about 1.3 × 10⁵ kg m⁻¹ s⁻².
