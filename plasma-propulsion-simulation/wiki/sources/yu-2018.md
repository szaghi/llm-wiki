# Summary

The paper tests, on a planar air-bearing testbed, an analytical tether
length-rate control law from the authors' earlier work [27]. The law
deploys a sub-satellite along a prescribed in-plane pitch angle
$\theta_e$ while keeping the tether taut. Section 2 writes the in-plane
rigid-rod (massless-tether) equations of a two-body tethered satellite
system (TSS) on a Keplerian orbit and the planar equations of a
tethered simulator, and matches them by "dynamics similarity":
cold-gas thrusters on the simulator produce the missing Coriolis and
gravity-gradient terms $F_\theta$, $F_l$. Section 3 derives the law by
imposing $\theta=\theta_e$, $\theta'=\theta''=0$ in the non-dimensional
pitch equation, with the admissible range of $\theta_e$ for deployment.
Section 5 gives a numerical case (500 km circular orbit) with Floquet
multipliers of modulus 0.1596, then ground experiments (equivalent
orbital rate 0.08 rad/s, tether about 1.6 m). They need gravity
compensation of the testbed tilt and PD control of the simulator
attitude; with both, the pitch angle converges to $-0.2$ rad within
about two cycles. Cold-gas (CO₂) nozzles appear only as testbed
actuators; the paper has no plasma content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **In-plane TSS equations** (Eq. 1, rigid massless tether, mother
   satellite much heavier than the sub-satellite; derivation attributed
   to Williams [26]):
   $$\begin{aligned}L\ddot\theta+2\dot L\dot\theta&=-2\Omega\dot L-\dot\Omega L-3\Omega^2L\sin\theta\cos\theta+Q_\theta/(m_SL),\\ \ddot L-L\dot\theta^2&=2\Omega L\dot\theta+3\Omega^2L\cos^2\theta-\tilde T/m_S.\end{aligned}$$
2. **Non-dimensional form** (Eqs. 2–3), with $\xi=L/L_r$, primes
   $=d/d\nu$, $\kappa=1+e\cos\nu$, $u=\tilde T/(m_S\dot\nu^2L_r)$:
   $$\begin{aligned}&\theta''+2(\theta'+1)\left(\frac{\xi'}{\xi}-\frac{e\sin\nu}{\kappa}\right)+\frac3\kappa\sin\theta\cos\theta=0,\\ &\xi''-\frac{2e\sin\nu}{\kappa}\xi'-\left[(\theta'+1)^2+\frac{3\cos^2\theta-1}{\kappa}\right]\xi=-u.\end{aligned}$$
3. **Ground model and similarity** (Eqs. 4–5): $l\ddot\theta+2\dot l\dot\theta=F_\theta/m_s$,
   $\ddot l-\dot\theta^2l=(F_l-T)/m_s$; matching Eq. 1 (with
   $Q_\theta=0$) requires the thruster forces
   $F_\theta=-m_sl(2\omega\dot l/l+\dot\omega+3\omega^2\sin\theta\cos\theta)$
   and $F_l=m_s\omega l(2\dot\theta+3\omega\cos^2\theta)$, $\omega$ the
   equivalent orbital angular velocity. Shown by term matching.
4. **Equilibrium pitch** (Eqs. 6–7):
   $\theta_e=\tfrac12\arcsin\!\left[\tfrac43\left(e\sin\nu-\kappa\xi'/\xi\right)\right]$,
   $\theta_e'=0$, with length-rate bounds
   $\xi(4e\sin\nu-3)/(4\kappa)\le\xi'\le\xi(4e\sin\nu+3)/(4\kappa)$.
5. **Control law** (Eqs. 8–11):
   $\xi'=\frac\xi\kappa\left(e\sin\nu-\tfrac34\sin2\theta_e\right)$,
   valid for $-\pi/4\le\theta_e\le\pi/4$ (Eq. 9). Deployment
   ($\xi'>0$) needs
   $-\pi+\tfrac12\arcsin(\tfrac43e)<\theta_e<-\tfrac12\arcsin(\tfrac43e)$
   with $e\le3/4$ (Eq. 10); combined (Eq. 11):
   $-\pi/4\le\theta_e<-\tfrac12\arcsin(\tfrac43e)$, $e<3/4$. Derived.
6. **Ground law** (Eq. 14, circular orbit):
   $\dot l=-\tfrac34\omega l\sin2\theta_e$, an exponential deployment for
   $\theta_e<0$.
7. **Stability** (stated, attributed): local and global stability,
   including the out-of-plane roll of a 3-D TSS, are attributed to [27]
   (Floquet theory, cell mapping) and not proved here; only the
   in-plane Floquet multipliers of one case are computed (§5.1).
8. **Numerical case** (§5.1, shown numerically): Floquet multipliers
   $\lambda_{1,2}=-0.1045\pm0.1207i$, $|\lambda_{1,2}|=0.1596<1$, so
   asymptotically stable; the pitch reaches $\theta_e$ after 4–5 swing
   periods (Fig. 9a), the phase trajectory ending at the stable focus
   $(-0.2,\,0)$ (Fig. 9b).
9. **Controlled deployment experiment** (§5.3, Fig. 14, shown
   experimentally, one run, no error bars): the pitch converges to
   $-0.2$ rad after two cycles, the phase trajectory to $(-0.2,\,0)$, and
   the tether deploys along the assigned direction in a taut state.
10. **Testbed artefacts** (Figs. 10–12, shown experimentally): testbed
    tilt and simulator attitude motion each cause significant
    deviations; both gravity compensation (Eq. 12) and PD attitude
    control are needed for a decaying pitch oscillation.

# Methods

* Model: planar two-body TSS (point masses M and S, massless rigid
  tether), Lagrange equations in $(\theta,L)$ (Eq. 1), non-dimensional in
  the true anomaly (Eqs. 2–3).
* Control: kinematic length-rate law (Eq. 8) obtained by imposing the
  pitch equilibrium; stability by linearisation about $\theta_e$ and
  Floquet multipliers (Eq. 13). Numerical integrator not stated.
* Testbed (§4): air-bearing satellite simulator (three air bearings,
  5 μm gap); a liquid-CO₂ cartridge feeds eight nozzles $F_1$–$F_8$
  parallel to the table (Fig. 5) through solenoid valves; on-board
  computer; Dyneema tether on a 30 mm diameter spool driven by a
  brushless DC motor in speed mode (Fig. 7); optical motion capture of
  two markers at 10 Hz. The main computer evaluates the Coriolis forces
  (Eq. 5) and the length rate (Eq. 8) at each sample (Fig. 8). The
  simulator attitude angle $\phi$, defined by the two markers (Fig. 6),
  is held by PD control; gains not given.
* Gravity compensation (Eq. 12): $F_x-m_sg\sin i_x=0$,
  $F_y-m_sg\sin i_y=0$, with $i_x$, $i_y$ the testbed inclinations.
* The experimental pitch rate comes from finite differences of the
  measured angle; the phase portrait shows a fitted curve.

# Tests and results

* **Numerical** (§5.1): circular orbit at 500 km,
  $\Omega\approx1.109\times10^{-3}$ rad/s, $m_S=50$ kg, $L_0=0.1$ km,
  $L_r=10$ km, $\theta_e=-0.2$ rad; Floquet multipliers
  $-0.1045\pm0.1207i$, modulus 0.1596; Fig. 9 shows pitch history,
  phase portrait, trajectory in the orbital frame and tether length.
* **Experimental parameters** (§5.2): $\omega=0.08$ rad/s,
  $\theta_e=-0.2$ rad, tether about 1.6 m; simulator mass falling from
  8.75 kg to 8.25 kg with CO₂ consumption, averaged $m_s=8.5$ kg;
  $g=9.795$ m/s².
* **Case A** (Fig. 10, testbed tilt): fixed tether length, attitude held
  by PD, only the Coriolis forces applied (station keeping): the
  equilibrium shifts away from $\theta=0$ and the amplitude grows, unlike
  the expected on-orbit behaviour [28].
* **Case B** (Fig. 11, attitude motion): gravity compensation only;
  irregular pitch and rigid-body motion (simulator diameter 0.2 m,
  tether 1.6 m).
* **Case C** (Fig. 12): gravity compensation with measured
  $i_x=0.16$ mm/m, $i_y=-0.07$ mm/m, i.e.
  $F_x=0.16\times10^{-3}m_sg$, $F_y=-0.07\times10^{-3}m_sg$, and the
  attitude held at $\pi/4$: a decaying oscillation about the equilibrium.
* **Case D** (Fig. 13): free deployment from the spool under the
  Coriolis forces only; the trajectory "agrees with" the numerical
  studies of [28] (qualitative, no overlay).
* **Case E** (Fig. 14): controlled deployment with Eq. 14; pitch
  converges to $-0.2$ rad after two cycles; length history in Fig. 14d.
  No quantitative error metric.
