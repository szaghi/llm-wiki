# Summary

The paper derives geometric design relations for two "constant swept"
osculating-cone waveriders whose leading edge contains a straight
constant-sweep segment: a delta-winged waverider (straight segment
outboard) and a cuspidal waverider (straight segment near the symmetry
plane, cusped apex). The inlet capture curve (ICC) and flow capture tube
(FCT) are built from straight, power-law and parabolic pieces joined by
tangency, intersection and perpendicularity constraints (Eqs. 3–15). A
delta-winged waverider is designed at Mach 8; a cuspidal (design Mach
3.86) and a general osculating-cone waverider (design Mach 4.4) are then
chosen with the same volumetric efficiency $\eta=V^{2/3}/S_{wet}$ (Fig. 6).
Euler and RANS (SST k–ω) simulations with ANSYS Fluent verify shock
attachment at design conditions (Figs. 9–11), and a matrix of Mach 3.0–10.0
and angles of attack −2° to 10° at 25 km compares lift-to-drag ratios.
The cuspidal waverider beats the general one in L/D at every condition
(maximum +18.5%), the delta-winged one is worse, and only the cuspidal
waverider shows a nonlinear lift rise (vortex lift) above about 6° at
Mach 8.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **General osculating-cone inputs** (§2, Eqs. 1–2, Fig. 1): ICC = central
   straight line $x=H_s$ ($|y|\le L_s$) plus power-law branches
   $x=-a(y-L_s)^q+H_s$; FCT = parabola $x=R_0+A_0y^2$. The method is
   stated (citing [23]) to be an inverse design for airframe-inlet
   integration.
2. **Delta-winged relations** (§2.1, Eqs. 3–10, Figs. 2–3): angle relation
   $\sin\gamma=\cos\alpha\tan\beta\tan\lambda$ (Eq. 6), FCT tangency
   $2A_0L_l=\tan\alpha$ (Eq. 7), ICC tangency (Eq. 8), perpendicularity
   (Eq. 9), intersection at E (Eq. 10), with $\lambda$ the leading-edge
   sweep, $\beta$ the design shock angle, $\alpha$ the inclination of the
   FCT straight segment, $\gamma$ the angle between EF and the FCT; solution
   procedure in Fig. 3.
3. **Cuspidal relations** (§2.2, Eqs. 11–15, Fig. 4): $\tan\lambda=\tan\alpha/\tan\beta$
   (Eq. 13), $2A_0L_s=\tan\alpha$ (Eq. 14), intersection
   $R_0+A_0L_w^2=-a(L_w-L_s)^4+H_s$ (Eq. 15, exponent printed 4).
4. **Novelty** (§1, stated): earlier constant-sweep designs (Rodi, Duan et
   al., Liu et al.) used a circular-arc ICC and straight FCT; power-law and
   parabolic curves are "more flexible".
5. **Iso-volumetric-efficiency matching** (§3.2, Eq. 16, Fig. 6): $\eta$
   rises monotonically with design Mach number (about 0.111 at Mach 4 to
   0.132 at 14, and 0.102 at 3 to 0.122 at 5; approximate readings);
   matching the Mach 8 delta-winged waverider gives design Mach 4.4
   (general) and 3.86 (cuspidal); CFD $\eta$ deviations 4.6% (delta) and
   7.3% (cuspidal) relative to the general one, "within the range for
   acceptance".
6. **Design-point shocks** (§4, Figs. 9–11; 25 km, zero incidence): the
   inviscid shock coincides with the designed shock profile curve at the
   base plane; in viscous runs it is deflected downward by the boundary
   layer. Shown, qualitative.
7. **Ordering** (§5, Fig. 12): $C_L$ and $C_D$ rise and L/D falls with
   incidence; cuspidal > general > delta-winged in L/D at every flight
   Mach number. Shown.
8. **Mach 8 coefficients** (§5, Tables 4–6, viscous, no base drag), L/D at
   −2/0/2/4/6/8/10°: delta-winged 2.9464 / 2.8415 / 2.6506 / 2.4405 /
   2.2386 / 2.0547 / 1.8908; cuspidal 3.4976 / 3.4111 / 3.1639 / 2.8833 /
   2.6205 / 2.3864 / 2.1805; general 2.9641 / 2.9363 / 2.7688 / 2.5627 /
   2.3558 / 2.1613 / 1.9862. $C_L$ spans 0.5902–1.6038 (delta),
   0.4888–1.6989 (cuspidal), 0.5141–1.5357 (general); $C_D$ 0.2003–0.8482,
   0.1398–0.7791, 0.1734–0.7732. $C_L/C_D$ reproduces the L/D columns to
   0.0012 (reader's check).
9. **Increments** (§5, Fig. 13, §7): cuspidal over general decreases with
   incidence and generally grows with Mach number, ">10%" at Mach 8 and 10,
   ">6%" for Mach ≤ 4.4, maximum 18.5% (about 18.4% at Mach 10 and −2° in
   Fig. 13a); general over delta-winged peaks near 7% at Mach 3, about 4°,
   and grows as Mach number falls.
10. **Thickness** (§§6.1–6.2, Figs. 14, 20): tangent of the symmetry-plane
    surface angle (SOLIDWORKS) 0.2471 cuspidal, 0.2626 general, 0.3046
    delta-winged; thinner body, higher L/D (stated qualitative
    explanation).
11. **Pressure and drag** (§6.1, Figs. 15–17, 21): at Mach 8 and zero
    incidence the cuspidal lower surface has lower pressure than the
    general one, hence a weaker shock and lower wave drag; at 10° they are
    close. That drag at high speed is mainly wave drag is stated, not
    shown. The delta-winged lower surface has significantly higher
    pressure, hence higher wave drag and $C_L$.
12. **Vortex lift** (§6.1, Figs. 18–19, 22): the cuspidal $C_L$ is
    nonlinear above about 6°, almost 8% above the linear function at 10°;
    general and delta-winged are almost linear. Fig. 19 (upper-surface
    pressure at 0/4/8/12°) shows a growing low-pressure region attributed
    to vortex/separation; the mechanism is attributed to Miller & Wood and
    Rodi; hypersonic upper-surface separation is said to need further
    study. Shown (coefficients) and stated (mechanism).
13. **Generality** (§5, stated): all three bodies are short and have low
    L/D because the basic cone half-angle is 20°; the comparison is argued
    to hold for higher-L/D waveriders. Not shown.

# Methods

* Osculating-cone inverse design with streamline tracing in local conical
  flows, basic cone half-angle 20°. Design parameters (Tables 1–3): delta
  Mach 8, $L_w=400$ mm, $L_s=48$ mm, $H_s=338$ mm, $\lambda=60°$,
  $\alpha=18.9°$, $R_0=152.8$ mm, $L_l=218.8$ mm, $q=4$; cuspidal Mach
  3.86, $L_s=148$ mm, $\lambda=45°$, $\alpha=26.5°$, $A_0=1.68\times10^{-3}$,
  $a=10^{-8}$; general Mach 4.4, $L_s=48$ mm, $R_0=120$ mm. Units of $A_0$,
  $a$ not stated.
* In-house volumetric-efficiency program, verified in ref. [16].
* ANSYS Fluent, 3-D steady, coupled implicit, second-order upwind; Euler
  for inviscid fields; RANS with SST k–ω; ideal-gas air; half domain.
  Grid, grid independence, boundary conditions, wall thermal condition,
  reference area and validation are not stated (deferred to refs. [16, 37,
  38]).
* Freestream at 25 km: 221.552 K, 2549.22 Pa; Mach 3.0, 3.86, 4.0, 4.4,
  8.0, 10.0; incidence −2 to 10° in 2° steps.
