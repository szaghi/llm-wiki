# Summary

A two-step method to decide from public two-line-element (TLE) history
whether a manoeuvrable low-Earth-orbit satellite is still operational.
Step 1 (§2) detects orbit-maintenance manoeuvres: the TLE-derived
semi-major-axis series is smoothed with RLOESS and cut into segments by
a moving window of size $m$; each segment's reference element is
propagated with SGP4/SDP4 to the next $m$ epochs, and the prediction
errors are compared with their duration-dependent mean and variance
through an anomaly index and a 3σ threshold (Eqs. 1–3). Manoeuvres
within three days of an inclination anomaly are removed as
non-coplanar. Step 2 (§3) collects the intervals and semi-major-axis
decrements between successive detected manoeuvres, and declares the
satellite retired when both the elapsed time and the accumulated
decrement since the last manoeuvre exceed their mean plus three standard
deviations. Applied to RADARSAT, TERRA and METOP-A with TLEs up to 21 Dec
2017 (§4), the method judges RADARSAT retired between 23 Feb and 11 Jun
2013 (public retirement date 9 May 2013) and the other two active. The
paper contains no propulsion modelling.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Criterion** (§1, stated). A manoeuvrable LEO satellite keeps its
   altitude by orbit-maintenance manoeuvres and stops once retired, so
   "still performs orbit-maintenance manoeuvres" serves as the
   operational-status criterion.
2. **Manoeuvre signatures** (§2, attributed to Vallado, ref. [8]).
   Orbit-maintenance manoeuvres are coplanar and change the semi-major
   axis abruptly; non-coplanar manoeuvres change inclination and right
   ascension of the ascending node; fixed-Δv manoeuvres change both.
3. **Anomaly test** (§2, Eqs. 1–3, read on rendered page 90). A segment
   is normal if
   $-3\sigma_{i+j,i}\le\Delta a_{i+j,i}-\mu_{i+j,i}\le3\sigma_{i+j,i}$ for
   $j=1..m$ (Eq. 1). The anomaly index is
   $d_i=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\Delta a_{i+j,i}-\mu_{i+j,i})^2}$ (Eq. 2) and
   the threshold $th_i=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}(3\sigma_{i+j,i})^2}$ (Eq. 3);
   the segment is abnormal if $d_i>th_i$. The prediction duration is
   counted in orbital periods, $n_{i+j,i}=\mathrm{round}(\Delta t_{i+j,i}/T)$.
4. **Retirement rule** (§3, proposed). Retired if the time since the last
   detected manoeuvre exceeds $\mu_{\mathrm{interval}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{interval}}$
   and the semi-major-axis decrement over that time exceeds
   $\mu_{\Delta a}+3\sigma_{\Delta a}$; the retiring time then lies in
   $[t_{\mathrm{last}},\,t_{\mathrm{last}}+\mu_{\mathrm{interval}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{interval}}]$.
   Otherwise active.
5. **Validation** (§4, shown for one case). The RADARSAT interval agrees
   with the publicly reported retirement on 9 May 2013.
6. **Limitation** (§4, stated). Small manoeuvres cannot be detected, an
   "inherent defect" of anomaly-based detection.
7. **Use** (§5, stated). The method gives an interval, not the exact
   retirement time, which suffices to assess compliance with the IADC
   25-year guideline.

# Methods

- **Data**: public TLEs (US Strategic Command); semi-major axis and
  inclination series derived from them.
- **Smoothing**: RLOESS (robust local regression, weighted linear least
  squares, second-degree polynomial; refs. [10, 11]); the span is not
  given.
- **Propagation**: SGP4/SDP4 (ref. [12]).
- **Statistics**: a series of $N$ elements yields $L=N-m$ segments
  (Table 1); all $L\times m$ prediction errors are grouped by prediction
  duration (integer multiples of the orbital period), data-deficient
  groups are dropped, and the mean and variance at arbitrary duration are
  interpolated (scheme not specified).
- **Clustering**: a manoeuvre is a run of adjacent abnormal segments (the
  run length is stated inconsistently, see Recorded as printed), dated
  at the reference epoch of the segment with the largest $d_i$.
- **Inclination filter**: the same procedure on the inclination series;
  semi-major-axis manoeuvres within 3 days of an inclination anomaly are
  removed.
- **Window sizes** (§4, empirical): $m=12$ for TERRA (about 3 TLEs per
  day), $m=10$ for RADARSAT, $m=8$ for METOP-A (about 1 TLE per day).

# Tests and results

Table 2: RADARSAT (SSN 23710, launched 1995-11-04, 98.58°, 790/789 km,
TLEs 1997-01-01 to 2017-12-21); TERRA (SSN 25994, 1999-12-18, 98.20°,
703/701 km, TLEs from 2001-01-01); METOP-A (SSN 29499, 2006-10-19,
98.66°, 822/819 km, TLEs from 2007-01-01).

- **RADARSAT** (Figs. 1–2): 141 orbit-maintenance manoeuvres;
  $\mu_{\mathrm{interval}}=41.9$ days, $\sigma_{\mathrm{interval}}=22.3$ days,
  $\mu_{\Delta a}=0.0524$ km, $\sigma_{\Delta a}=0.0244$ km. More than 4
  years since the last manoeuvre with an accumulated decrement of about
  2.3 km: retired, in the interval 23 Feb – 11 Jun 2013.
- **TERRA** (Fig. 3): 87 manoeuvres and 47 inclination anomalies, 5
  overlapping within 3 days, leaving 82 manoeuvres (16 Jan 2001 – 12 Nov
  2016); $\mu_{\mathrm{interval}}=71.3$ days, $\sigma_{\mathrm{interval}}=46.8$
  days, $\mu_{\Delta a}=0.1413$ km, $\sigma_{\Delta a}=0.0798$ km. 391 days
  without a detected manoeuvre (above the 211.7-day bound) but a
  decrement of only about 0.053 km: active. The authors suggest that
  undetectably small manoeuvres continued.
- **METOP-A** (Figs. 4–5): 34 manoeuvres, 7 removed by inclination
  anomalies, leaving 27; interval mean 146 days, standard deviation 147
  days; decrement mean 0.0586 km, standard deviation 0.037 km. Last
  manoeuvre on 5 Dec 2017, 16 days before the data end, semi-major axis
  "almost unchanged": active.
- Detection accuracy is not validated against operator manoeuvre logs;
  "accurately identify" (§5) rests on the RADARSAT date agreement and on
  visual inspection of the figures.
