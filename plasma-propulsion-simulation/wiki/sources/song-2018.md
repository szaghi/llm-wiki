# Summary

The paper reports direct-connect experiments on lean blowoff of an
ethylene-fuelled model scramjet combustor with a cavity flameholder and
fuel injected upstream of the cavity ("cavity passive fuel injection").
The inflow is Mach 2.52 with total temperature 1600 K, simulating flight
Mach 6. Lean blowoff limits are measured as the minimum injection pressure
against injection distance for two schemes, one 2 mm orifice and four 1 mm
orifices, sized to give the same global equivalence ratio at equal
injection pressure. The limits rise with injection distance and are higher
for the single orifice. Multiple-orifice flames never blow off at constant
fuel supply; single-orifice flames show intermittent local extinction and
reignition near the limit. Below the limit, high-speed luminosity imaging
at 20000 frames/s resolves the blowoff in five steps. FLUENT RANS
simulations (SST k–ω, one-step ethylene chemistry) of the 10 mm cases
explain the difference through the cavity temperature and fuel
distributions.

# Key claims

1. **Lean blowoff limit trend** (§3.1, Fig. 5; Conclusion 1). The limit,
   defined as the minimum injection pressure that keeps combustion
   continuous, increases with injection distance and is higher for the
   single orifice. Read from Fig. 5 (approximate, stable boundary / failed
   point, MPa): single orifice 0.8/0.6 at about 10 mm, 1.4/1.2 at 30 and
   50 mm, 1.6/1.4 at 110 mm, 1.8/1.6 at 180 mm; four orifices 0.4/0.2 at 10
   and 30 mm, 0.6/0.4 at 60 mm, 1.0/0.8 at 180 mm. The right axis maps
   injection pressure linearly to global equivalence ratio (0.2 at 2 MPa),
   so the limits lie at about 0.02–0.18. Shown.
2. **Explanation of the distance trend** (§3.1). A jet-penetration
   correlation implies more penetration and less fuel entrained into the
   shear layer at larger distance; the low single-orifice limit at 10 mm is
   attributed to jet–cavity interaction pumping fuel into the recirculation
   zone, after Wang et al. Stated, not computed.
3. **Flame stability by scheme** (§3.1, Figs. 6–7). Multiple-orifice
   flames are basically steady in the cavity shear layer (near the limit,
   only below the cavity mouth); single-orifice flames are strongly
   unsteady with intermittent local extinction; "no obvious change" with
   distance. Shown.
4. **Wall pressure** (Fig. 8). Combustion raises the lower-wall pressure
   only slightly (peak about 122 kPa near the cavity rear, cold flow about
   110 kPa). At equal injection pressure (0.8 MPa) the multiple-orifice
   case rises more in the cavity and less downstream; at the respective
   lean boundaries the single-orifice case rises more (about 98 kPa at
   300 mm against 74 kPa and 62 kPa cold), read as single-orifice flames
   needing more combustion intensity to persist. Shown.
5. **CFD rationale** (Figs. 9–10). Multiple orifices: cavity hot
   throughout, uniform spanwise, fuel jet closer to the cavity. Single
   orifice: high temperature confined to the cavity rear, fuel concentrated
   in the jet wake. Shown numerically.
6. **Local extinction and reignition** (§3.2, Fig. 11; single orifice,
   0.8 MPa). Over about 1 ms the shear-layer flame weakens (0–0.15 ms), is
   split by local extinction near the trailing edge (0.20 ms), partly blows
   off and shrinks into the cavity (0.25 ms), re-spreads (0.30–0.45 ms),
   breaks again (0.50 ms) and strengthens back to the main stream
   (0.65–1.00 ms). Shown. The mechanism (shear layer displaced by an inflow
   disturbance, flame stretch at the trailing edge, then stronger
   shear-layer/trailing-edge interaction entraining fuel) is stated, not
   measured.
7. **Five-step blowoff** (§3.3, Fig. 12; single orifice, 0.6 MPa). The
   shear-layer flame weakens, is partly extinguished near the trailing edge,
   shrinks into the cavity rear, moves to the front half near the front
   wall, and is extinguished. Multiple-orifice flames show no blowoff at
   constant supply: below their boundary spark ignition fails, and a
   hydrogen-piloted ethylene flame dies when the hydrogen is cut. Shown.
8. **Region migration** (Fig. 13, non-reacting CFD). The shear layer has
   the highest velocity and lowest temperature, the cavity front half the
   highest temperature and lowest velocity; the flame is read as migrating
   toward the "more combustible" region. Interpretation.
9. **Driscoll–Rasmussen correlation** (§1). A modified Damköhler number
   with a non-premixed flame time from shear-layer similarity; as printed,
   its inverse equals the overall equivalence ratio at lean limits and the
   inverse overall equivalence ratio at rich limits. Attributed.

# Methods

* **Facility** (§2): NUDT direct-connect rig with a vitiated-air heater
  (air/O2/alcohol), total temperature 1600 K, stagnation pressure up to
  1.65 MPa, Mach 2.52 nozzle, about 1.0 kg/s; heater 6–7 s, fuel 1.8–2 s;
  uncooled walls.
* **Geometry** (Figs. 2–4): isolator 315 mm, combustor 512 mm, expansion
  270 mm (3°); entrance 50 × 40 mm; lower wall diverging 3.1° behind the
  cavity; cavity depth 16 mm, length 64 mm, aft ramp 45°; quartz windows.
* **Injection** (Fig. 3): four 1 mm orifices at 10, 30, 60, 180 mm, or one
  2 mm orifice at 10, 30, 50, 110, 170 mm upstream of the cavity;
  room-temperature ethylene.
* **Procedure**: injection pressure lowered in 0.2 MPa steps (display
  resolution 0.1 MPa), each point repeated at least 5 times at and below
  the boundary; spark-plug ignition.
* **Diagnostics**: luminosity imaging at 20000 frames/s (shutter
  1/20416 s, f/1.4); wall pressure at 100 Hz, ±0.5%.
* **Numerics**: 3D RANS with SST k–ω in FLUENT (density-based coupled
  solver), finite-rate one-step ethylene–air chemistry without
  turbulence–chemistry interaction; 812000 cells (single orifice) and
  1232000 (four orifices); only the 10 mm cases. Scheme, order, steady or
  unsteady, grid independence and validation against wall pressure are not
  stated.

# Tests and results

* Fig. 5: limits for 2 schemes × 4–5 distances (Key claim 1).
* Figs. 6–7: luminosity sequences 0.05 ms apart.
* Fig. 8: wall pressure for three reacting cases and cold flow, about
  62–122 kPa; inset traces steady over 0.5 s (tap A: about 114, 113, 110
  and 102 kPa).
* Fig. 11: extinction–reignition cycle 0.00–1.15 ms; Fig. 12: blowoff over
  the last 0.60 ms; Fig. 13: non-reacting temperature and velocity fields.
