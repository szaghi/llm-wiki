# Summary

The paper characterizes the mechanical response of SasolWax 0907, a
brittle microcrystalline paraffin wax used as a liquefying hybrid-rocket
fuel, pure (PW00) and loaded with 40 wt% aluminium powder (AL40, average
particle size 100 μm). Tension tests (ASTM D638 Type III) were run at 1, 10
and 100 mm/min extension rates and at 23, 30 and 40 °C; compression tests
(ASTM D695 cylinders) at 1 and 10 mm/min at room temperature only.
Aluminium raises the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) by 10–20% and roughly
doubles the tensile modulus at room temperature; UTS rises 60–70% from 1 to
100 mm/min and drops 65–75% from 23 to 40 °C, where failure becomes
ductile. The paper also gives casting procedures and rejection modes for
aluminized specimens, DSC melting and crystallization onsets, thermal
conductivities (0.46 against 1.36 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹) and a 1D semi-infinite
conduction estimate of heat penetration into the grain. The stated purpose
is input to a future constitutive model and structural analysis of hybrid
grains, by analogy with solid-propellant grain structural-integrity
analysis.

# Key claims

1. **Rate dependence at 23 °C** (§4.1, Table 1, Figs. 6–7). UTS rises and
   strain at UTS falls with extension rate for both fuels: PW00 1.78, 2.27,
   2.97 MPa and AL40 2.02, 2.71, 3.30 MPa at 1, 10, 100 mm/min; tensile
   modulus also rises (PW00 512, 678, 781 MPa; AL40 1025, 1162, 1282 MPa).
   Shown.
2. **Aluminium effect** (§4.1, Table 1). AL40 UTS is 10–20% above PW00 at
   room temperature (reader's computation: +13.5%, +19.4%, +11.1%); maximum
   strain is similar, pure slightly higher. Shown.
3. **Temperature dependence** (§4.1, Table 2, Figs. 8–11). UTS falls and
   strain to failure rises with temperature; at 30 and 40 °C the UTS point
   no longer coincides with failure (ductile failure, minimal necking); rate
   dependence weakens; modulus drops sharply (PW00 at 1 mm/min: 512, 308,
   156 MPa at 23, 30, 40 °C). Shown.
4. **Compression** (§4.2, Table 3, Figs. 12–13). Ultimate compressive
   strength exceeds UTS at the same rate; the compressive modulus is below
   the tensile one; failure by bulging, base cracking, then shear at an
   angle. Shown.
5. **Summary percentages** (§5). UTS +60–70% from 1 to 100 mm/min (reader's
   computation: +67% PW00, +63% AL40) and −65–75% from 23 to 40 °C
   (computed 68–75%). Shown.
6. **DSC** (§3, Fig. 5). Melting onset 39.75 °C (text; Fig. 5 annotation
   39.86 °C, 105.8 J/g, peak 76.59 °C); crystallization onset 89.27 °C;
   supplier congealing range 83–94 °C and melting point 108 °C (§2). The
   wax is too soft to test above the melting onset, which sets 40 °C as the
   maximum test temperature. Shown.
7. **Thermal conductivity** (§3). Pure 0.46 ± 0.03 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, aluminized
   1.36 ± 0.03 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹, about three times higher. Shown.
8. **Heat penetration** (§3). A 1D semi-infinite transient conduction
   estimate with the surface at 120 °C (radiation and convection
   neglected) puts the 30 °C isotherm at 1.37 mm (pure) and 1.69 mm
   (aluminized) after 1 s, less than the theoretical regression per second,
   so penetration is judged not significant. Estimate; diffusivities not
   stated.
9. **Regression rates** (§3, attributed to a thesis from the authors'
   group). Predicted time-averaged 1.63 mm/s (pure) and 1.82 mm/s
   (aluminized); measured about 1.4 and 1.6 mm/s in a lab-scale N2O motor;
   the aluminized motor was not tested at peak performance. Stated.
10. **Thermal propagation in a long burn** (attributed to Santi et al.). No
    measurable radial thermal propagation in an 80 s paraffin motor; the
    authors caution this may not hold for aluminized grains.
11. **Isotropy** (§2). Randomly oriented wax crystal nuclei make the
    material quasi-isotropic; individually cast specimens did not differ
    noticeably from specimens machined from full grains. SasolWax 0907 is
    C50H102, microcrystalline, brittle class. Viscoelastic behaviour from
    −20 °C to +40 °C is stated without citation.
12. **Comparison with Ryu et al.** (§4.2). Pure-wax UTS 2.118 MPa (Ryu,
    5 mm/min) against 2.27 MPa here (10 mm/min); ultimate compressive
    strength 3.503 MPa (Ryu) against 3.34 MPa here, with different specimen
    dimensions. Shown, rate-mismatched.

# Methods

* **Material**: SasolWax 0907 (Sasol Wax GmbH); aluminium powder, average
  100 μm, not handled in an inert environment; 40 wt% loading.
* **Casting** (§2.1–2.2, Figs. 1, 3): pure wax melted at 110 °C, mould
  preheated to 110 °C, slow cooling in the switched-off kiln (about 12 h),
  demoulding with boiling water; aluminized melt stirred outside the kiln
  down to 90 °C before pouring into a 90 °C mould. Rejection modes: layer
  formation and voids (pour too cold), particle settling (too hot),
  localized solidification (poor mixing); 5% of specimens rejected.
* **Specimens and machine**: D638 Type III dog-bones 10 mm thick; D695
  cylinders 12.7 × 25.4 mm; Instron 5500R, 5 kN load cell, 50 mm
  extensometer; heated grips, specimens soaked at least 8 h, surface
  temperature checked by thermal camera.
* **Matrix**: five specimens per case from one wax batch, standard
  deviations reported; some tests outsourced for verification ("came back
  as a match"). Rates are crosshead extension rates, called "strain rate"
  in the paper; engineering stress, strain in %.
* **Other**: DSC (second heat run, rate not stated); TIM Tower
  conductivity; the conduction estimate is described in words only. No
  equations in the paper.
