# Summary

The paper proposes a tightly coupled (TC) strapdown-inertial/celestial
(SINS/CNS) navigation scheme for high-orbit automated transfer vehicles.
The filter takes the star sensor's raw image-point positions, not a
star-sensor attitude, as measurements, and adds a "virtual observation"
from the complete-weightlessness constraint: in free orbital flight the
true specific force is zero, so the accelerometer output observes its
own bias. The paper derives a star-image-point attitude-determination
model (a weighted least-squares variant of Wahba's problem, §2.3), the
J2-gravity error-state dynamics with a flight-mode factor (§3.1,
Appendices A–B), the image-point measurement equation (§3.2.1) and the
virtual accelerometer measurement (§3.2.2), fused by an information
filter that handles the multi-rate data (§3.3). The evaluation is by
simulation only: 100 Monte Carlo trials on one LEO-to-HEO trajectory of
18,000 s (§4). Tight coupling alone halves the attitude error but leaves
position and velocity errors near $1.23\times10^5$ m and 15.4 m/s; the
constraint brings them to about 4057 m and 0.39 m/s (Table 2). The paper
has no plasma or propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Virtual observation** (abstract, §1, §3.2.2, Eqs. 36–38): in free
   flight $f^b=0$, so the accelerometer output
   $\hat f^b=f^b+b_f+\delta f^b$ observes the bias; the virtual
   measurement $z_{virtual}=\hat f^b-f_{Virtual}$ with $f_{Virtual}=0$
   makes $b_f$ observable. Shown by simulation (§4.2.2, Figs. 10b, 11b).
2. **Residual unobservability** (§3.2.2): position and velocity errors
   stay unobservable even with the virtual observation; the constraint
   only slows their divergence through the bias estimate. Stated,
   attributed to [13,15].
3. **Attitude observability** (§3.2.2): attitude misalignment, gyro bias
   and star-sensor installation error are fully observable with CNS
   attitude feedback under regular manoeuvres. Stated, attributed to
   [30–32].
4. **Tight versus loose coupling** (§4.2.1, Table 2, Fig. 9): attitude
   RMSE at 18,000 s falls from 0.2288 arcsec (loose) to 0.1051 arcsec
   (tight), "54.1%"; the attitude variance falls below 0.001° in 380 s
   (tight) against 855 s (loose). Shown by simulation.
5. **Constraint** (§4.2.2, Table 2): the constrained TC scheme reduces
   the position RMSE from 123243.3873 m (TC) to 4057.2367 m (96.71%) and
   the velocity RMSE from 15.4180 m/s to 0.3944 m/s (97.44%), with no
   attitude gain (0.1051 arcsec in both), because the constraint does not
   inform attitude or gyro bias. Shown by simulation.
6. **Continuity** (§1, §4.2.1): TC works with a single observed star,
   while loose-coupling attitude determination needs at least two stars
   per observation. Argued, not separately simulated.
7. **Against adaptive filtering** (§1): raw image points avoid the
   adaptive (windowed IAE/MMAE) estimation of time-varying star-sensor
   attitude noise, which the authors argue fails in dynamic conditions
   and is interrupted by star switching [23]. Argued.
8. **Information filter** (§3.3, Eqs. 41–48): multi-rate sources are
   fused by summing per-source information contributions
   $H^TR^{-1}z$ and $H^TR^{-1}H$. Standard, refs. [13,33].

# Methods

* Star sensor (§2.1, Eq. 1): pinhole model on an $N\times N$ pixel image
  plane with field angle $\gamma$; star-point prediction between epochs
  from the gyro angular increment (Eq. 5); image-point covariance
  propagated from the right ascension, declination and roll covariance
  (Eqs. 6–12).
* Attitude determination (§2.3, Eqs. 13–21): least squares on the
  image-point residuals, linearised in the misalignment and the
  systematic errors (focal length, principal point), with a prior on the
  systematic errors; iterated weighted least-squares solution.
* Dynamics (§3.1, Eqs. 22–24): J2000 inertial frame; attitude,
  velocity and position kinematics with a flight-mode factor ($\alpha=1$
  powered, $\alpha=0$ free flight, set from engine commands); J2
  gravity with $J_2=1.082627\times10^{-3}$, $R_e=6378137.0$ m,
  $\mu=3.986\times10^{14}$ m³/s².
* Error state: 21 states (position, velocity, attitude misalignment,
  accelerometer and gyro biases, star-sensor installation error and
  systematic errors); biases as random constants; discretisation and
  covariance in Appendix B (Eqs. B.7–B.8).
* Measurements: image-point TC equation (Eq. 35) and the virtual
  accelerometer measurement (Eq. 40) with a noise covariance equal to
  the accelerometer bias instability (Eq. 39).
* Fusion: information filter (Eqs. 41–48, Fig. 3). Baselines: loosely
  coupled SINS/CNS (star-sensor attitude as measurement) and
  unconstrained TC.

# Tests and results

* Scenario (§4.1, Figs. 4–6, Table 1): launch from 40°N, 100°E at
  12:00:00 UTC on 16 March 2015; 18,000 s of flight to an altitude of
  $4.0\times10^4$ km; powered phase 0–180 s; three manoeuvres at
  0.25 m/s² (800–1000 s, 5400–5600 s, 17000–17200 s); virtual
  observation at 1.0 Hz at all other times.
* Sensors: IMU at 100 Hz, gyro 0.01°/h, accelerometer 50 μg; star sensor
  from 200 s at 0.25 Hz, field angle 12°, 1024×1024 pixels, limiting
  magnitude 6.5, extraction accuracy 0.01 pixel, principal point and
  focal length 0.1 pixel, installation misalignment 20 arcsec, lever arm
  1.0 m per axis ± 0.1 m.
* Monte Carlo (Table 1): 100 trials perturbing initial position (1 m),
  velocity (0.1 m/s), attitude (0.01°), biases and star-sensor errors.
* Case 1 (§4.2.1, Figs. 7–9), loose versus tight: almost all errors
  within 3σ; no clear position or velocity gain; gyro bias estimated
  faster and better; accelerometer bias not estimated.
* Case 2 (§4.2.2, Figs. 10–11), constrained TC: all errors within 3σ;
  both biases estimated.
* RMSE (Table 2): loose 123268.1936 m, 15.4254 m/s, 0.2288 arcsec;
  tight 123243.3873 m, 15.4180 m/s, 0.1051 arcsec; constrained tight
  4057.2367 m, 0.3944 m/s, 0.1051 arcsec.
* No hardware, flight data or real star catalogue; no sensitivity study
  on trajectory, number of manoeuvres or bias magnitude.
