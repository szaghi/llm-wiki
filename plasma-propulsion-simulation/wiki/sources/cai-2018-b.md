# Summary

A space-situational-awareness paper on initial orbit determination from
angles-only optical observations. Associating short-arc tracklets
circumvents the too-short-arc problem. The proposed "improved IVP"
method draws candidate virtual particles from the intersections of
semi-major-axis and eccentricity constraint curves in the constrained
admissible region, maps them to nonsingular canonical (Poincaré-type)
variables built from Delaunay elements, and minimises the squared
Euclidean distance between the two tracklets' orbits over semi-major axis
and eccentricity with a genetic algorithm, separately in each
orbit-revolution valley. On 308 Mount Stromlo Observatory tracklets of
2013 (LEO, MEO, GEO, GTO) it gives the best association performance of
the three methods compared (with the IVP and BVP methods of Siminski et
al.) and needs no noise-model calibration; its run time lies between
BVP (fastest) and IVP. Constellation tracklets (86 Iridium) cause many
false associations; a second "common ellipse" method compares
inclination, node and argument of perigee by a Gaussian-mixture-weighted
Mahalanobis distance and raises the true negative rate considerably at a
slight cost in true positive rate. The paper has no propulsion or plasma
content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Multi-valley IVP loss** (§2.2, Eqs. 7–8, Fig. 1, shown for one
   example): for two GEO tracklets 3 days apart with
   $a\in[4,7]$ Earth radii the IVP loss has four valleys in the
   (semi-major axis, relative range) plane, each with possibly several
   local minima; adding $e\in[0,0.1]$ leaves one valley.
2. **Noise-model-free loss** (§§3.3–3.4, Eqs. 14–15, stated as a design
   property): $D=(\mathcal O_1-\mathcal O_2)^2$ in the nonsingular
   canonical variables $(\xi_i,\eta_i)$ needs no measurement-noise model
   and hence no calibration.
3. **Revolution valleys** (§3.4, Eqs. 16–17, Fig. 3, shown for two LEO
   tracklets 1 day apart): the valleys of $D$ correspond to integer
   numbers $m$ of complete revolutions between the tracklets, with
   $m_{min}=\mathrm{floor}\big(\frac{|\Delta t|}{2\pi}\sqrt{\mu/a_{max}^3}\big)$
   and $m_{max}=\mathrm{ceil}\big(\frac{|\Delta t|}{2\pi}\sqrt{\mu/a_{min}^3}\big)$;
   each is optimised separately and the global minimum is the smallest
   local one.
4. **Genetic algorithm** (§3.4, stated): chosen over gradient methods
   because one $(a,e)$ pair can give several intersections and the
   angular variables make the loss discontinuous.
5. **TLE comparison** (§3.4.4, Fig. 4b): in one example the GA minimum is
   within 7 km in semi-major axis of the TLE value; consistency is not
   tested (TLEs carry no covariance).
6. **Comparison** (§4.2, Figs. 5–6, shown as curves): on all pairs within
   5 days, improved IVP and IVP perform similarly, improved IVP with a
   slightly higher true negative rate at equal true positive rate; BVP is
   worst, even re-optimised by pattern search. The attribution to the
   two-body Lambert solver on mostly LEO, drag-perturbed objects is
   stated.
7. **Constellation anomaly** (§4.2, Fig. 5b, shown): pairs under 3 days
   apart have lower true negative rates than pairs 3–5 days apart, against
   the expected trend; the cause is the 86 Iridium tracklets (all under 3
   days); the 222 non-constellation tracklets recover the expected trend.
8. **Run time** (§4.3, Fig. 7): mean time per pair is BVP < improved IVP
   < IVP in all five interval groups (1–5 days), growing quickly with the
   interval (LEO's small $a$ makes $\Delta m$ large). That improved IVP is
   fastest overall once the calibration runs of IVP and BVP are counted is
   argued, not measured.
9. **Common ellipse** (§5, Eq. 21, Figs. 8–10, shown): threshold
   $T_c=600$ chosen from 1661 true-association and 7058 false-association
   values of the 86 Iridium tracklets; the true negative rate rises
   considerably across thresholds and the true positive rate falls
   slightly; in a 10-tracklet case only the true pair falls below $T_c$.
   $T_c$ is fitted and evaluated on the same set (not out of sample).

# Methods

* **Admissible region** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–6): attributable vector of right
  ascension, declination and their rates, extended with the station
  location; the admissible region is the set of (range, range-rate) with
  negative two-body energy; the constrained admissible region (DeMars et
  al.) adds bounds on $a$ and $e$. A virtual particle is a point of that
  plane.
* **Baselines** (Siminski et al.): IVP loss minimised by pattern search
  (10 initial points, at most 1000 steps); BVP loss by BFGS (at most 10
  steps, typically 3–4); noise standard deviations
  $4.89\times10^{-6}$ rad (≈1 arcsec) on angles and
  $1.17\times10^{-4}$ rad/hour on rates. All methods use two-body
  dynamics.
* **Candidate virtual particles** (§3.2, Eqs. 9–13): the semi-major-axis
  constraint is quadratic and the eccentricity constraint quartic in
  range-rate (coefficients from DeMars et al.); intersections are
  bracketed by sign changes along sparse range samples and refined by
  bisection; of several intersections the minimum-loss pair is kept.
* **Canonical space** (§3.3, Eq. 14): nonsingular variables from the
  Delaunay set; exact and linearised transformations deferred to the
  conference version (ref. [13]).
* **Optimisation** (§3.4, Eqs. 16–19): per valley, a GA over the 2-gene
  chromosome $(a,e)$ with fitness-proportionate selection, single-point
  crossover and improvement-only mutation; population 20, at most 20
  iterations; Matlab on an Intel Core i7 at 2.7 GHz. Crossover and
  mutation probabilities are not stated.
* **Common ellipse** (§5, Eqs. 20–21, Algorithm 1): an EKF estimates the
  covariance of the improved-IVP state of a tracklet pair; the unscented
  transform maps it to Keplerian elements; the $3\sigma$ bounds on $a$,
  $e$ constrain the admissible region of every other tracklet, which is
  approximated by a Gaussian mixture and mapped to Keplerian space; the
  weighted mean Mahalanobis distance over inclination, node and argument
  of perigee decides association. The EKF initial covariance and the
  mixture size are not stated.

# Tests and results

* **Data** (§4.1, Table 1): Mount Stromlo optical observations,
  1 March–10 May 2013; 308 tracklets of 5–6 observations over 2–3 min:
  LEO 289 (18 single-pass, 271 multi-pass), MEO 6 (4/2), GEO 11 (0/11),
  GTO 2 (0/2); 47278 pairs in total, only pairs under 5 days tested;
  reference associations from the observatory.
* **Association** (Figs. 5–6): curves only, 20 threshold points each; no
  rates are tabulated.
* **Run time** (Fig. 7): values only in the figure.
* **Common ellipse** (Figs. 8–10): 10 Iridium tracklets within one day,
  only one pair (NORAD 24795) from the same satellite; motivating
  example NORAD 24795 vs 24793, 0.89 days and 72.3° of mean anomaly apart.
