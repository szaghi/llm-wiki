# Summary

The paper studies how the axial distribution of heat release in a
kerosene-fuelled RBCC engine with an embedded strut rocket sets the
"matching" among the pre-combustion shock train (isolator), combustion and
thermal choking (combustor and nozzle), from flight Mach 3 to 6. It uses 3D
RANS FLUENT simulations of the full integrated flow path (inlet, isolator,
two-section combustor, nozzle; half model, about 3.3 million cells) with
SST k–ω, a Lagrangian kerosene spray and a global three-step finite-rate
kerosene mechanism, validated against a Mach 3 direct-connect test and
against flight-Mach 4 and 5 freejet tests in a CARDC shock tunnel. The main
claims: the shock train balances the intake–combustion pressure difference
by strengthening as it moves upstream; the main heat release should sit in
the downstream (larger-area) cavity at Mach 3, with thermal choking at the
combustor exit, and move upstream as flight Mach rises; and a "distributed
heat release" strategy (fuel split between injection stations) keeps the
inlet started and raises combustion efficiency. At Mach 6 with the rocket
off, isolator injection keeps self-sustained scramjet combustion going.

# Key claims

1. **Shock-train self-balancing** (§4.1, Fig. 7; unsteady CFD, flight Mach
   5). After ignition combustion grows behind the pylon until 5.7 ms, then
   pushes the pre-combustion shock upstream until 9.7 ms; its angles and
   pre-shock velocity rise, so its pressure ratio rises until it balances
   the combustion pressure, and it stabilizes in the forepart of the
   isolator; thermal choking appears at the frontal-cavity exit. Shown.
2. **Shock strength** (eq. 4), attributed to Emanuel: the normal-shock
   static-pressure ratio from the pre-shock Mach number and the ratio of
   specific heats.
3. **Distributed heat release at Mach 4** (§4.2, Fig. 8). Equivalence
   ratio 0.9 at the first station puts the shock at the isolator entrance,
   the inlet-operation limit; splitting 0.7 + 0.2 moves it back, raises
   total heat release by 18% and moves choking downstream; 0.3 + 0.6 lowers
   efficiency, with heat release 8% below the 0.7 + 0.2 case. Final
   combustion efficiency about 89% against about 85% (read from the figure,
   approximate). Shown.
4. **Mach 3** (§4.2, Fig. 9). Frontal-cavity-heavy injection (0.6 + 0.3)
   reaches 4.5 times the reference pressure and pushes the shock into the
   inlet throat; more air spills through the bleed and capture drops by
   17%. Downstream-heavy injection (0.3 + 0.6) restores the inlet and moves
   choking downstream; thrust coefficient 0.458 against 0.402, stated as
   "+12%". Shown.
5. **Mach 6 scramjet mode** (§4.2, Fig. 10). Splitting fuel between the
   isolator (0.4) and the first station (0.6) raises combustion efficiency
   (about 89% against about 81% for 1.0 at one station, read from the
   figure) and pressure; no clear thermal choking forms, and subsonic and
   supersonic regions coexist. Shown.
6. **Optimal choking location** (Fig. 11, Table 5). At the combustor exit
   (large expansion) at Mach 3, moving upstream as flight Mach rises.
   Thrust coefficients (effective thrust over freestream dynamic pressure
   times capture area, eq. 6): case 0 (Mach 2.5) 0.355; 1-1 0.402; 1-2
   0.458; 2-1 0.433; 2-2 0.460; 2-3 0.437; 4-1 0.297; 4-2 0.316. Shown.
7. **Freejet validation** (§4.3, Figs. 12–14). At Mach 4 ignition at
   2.7 ms, pressure rise to 8.3 ms, stable at 30 ms with peak about 4.3
   times the reference pressure; shock train at the isolator entrance,
   x/h = 22. At Mach 5 peak 3.9 times reference. CFD agrees with measured
   wall pressure in "range and trend" (no error metric); schlieren shows
   external compression shocks unchanged by combustion. Shown.
8. **Rocket-off scramjet combustion at Mach 6** (§4.3, Fig. 15;
   direct-connect test). With 0.4 + 0.6, combustion pressure is 3.8 times
   reference in the combustor forepart with the rocket off; the
   combustor-entrance pressure is only 0.2 times reference below the
   rocket-on case. Shown.
9. **Literature** (§1), attributed: combustion shock forms and moves
   upstream in HyShot II only above equivalence ratio 0.38 (Laurence);
   shock-train strength falls with fuel flux (Fotia); λ-to-X shock shape
   change with mainstream velocity (Tamaki); easier choking in a
   constant-area section (Doolan, printed first author Byrne); gradient
   heat-release model superior to linear (Riggins); upstream injection and
   faster heat release move the thermal throat forward (Wang); direct-connect
   and freejet tests differ in shock-train length and ignition (Thomas).

# Methods

* **Engine** (§2, Figs. 1–2, Table 1): mixed-compression inlet (design
  Mach 5.5, compression angles 8.3° and 9.8°, bleed block), isolator,
  two-section rectangular combustor with two cavities and pylon and wall
  injection, nozzle (design Mach 4.0, initial expansion 20°). Stations in
  x/h: inlet 0–25.1, isolator to 35.3, combustor to 63, nozzle to 89;
  injectors at 28, 42, 50. Section exit areas 1.2H and 1.8H. Strut rocket
  GOX/kerosene, 0.15 kg/s, mixture ratio 1.2, expansion ratio 6.
* **Numerics** (§3.1–3.3): FLUENT 3D RANS, unsteady for the matching
  transient and steady for the heat-release studies; SST k–ω (printed
  constants include low-Reynolds corrections); turbulent Prandtl and
  Schmidt numbers 0.9; Lagrangian spray with non-equilibrium evaporation
  and boiling; laminar finite-rate chemistry. Scheme, order, time step and
  solver type not stated. Half-model structured grid of about 3.3 million
  cells. Rocket as a mass-flow inlet at 1855 K with CEA composition (CO
  0.941, H2 0.057, CO2 0.001, H2O 0.001); rocket combustion not simulated.
  Flight conditions: Mach 3 at 12 km, 4 at 17 km, 5 at 21 km, 6 at 24 km.
* **Mechanism** (Table 2), irreversible Arrhenius, as printed ($A$ in
  cm³/(mol s), activation energy in J/(kg mol)):

  | Reaction | $A$ | temperature exponent | activation energy |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | C10H16 + 5O2 → 10CO + 8H2 | 2.35e+04 | 1 | 1.633e+08 |
  | 2CO + O2 → 2CO2 | 3.48e+08 | 2 | 8.42e+07 |
  | 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O | 3.01e+17 | −1 | 0 |

* **Validation** (§3.3, Fig. 4): Mach 3 direct-connect test (air 4.36 kg/s,
  0.56 MPa, 600 K, equivalence ratio 0.5, rocket on); combustor meshes of
  2.2 and 6.86 million cells differ by under 7%; agreement called "high
  accordance" without a metric. Discrete-phase error accumulation at most
  1.831%.
* **Experiments** (§3.4, Table 3): CARDC combustion-heated shock tunnel,
  nozzle exit 2400 mm, 400 ms test window; freejet at Mach 4 (total
  pressure 0.52 MPa, 845 K, Re 9.92e+06) and Mach 5 (1.35 MPa, 1124 K,
  Re 8.97e+06); 13 inlet, 34 combustor and 6 nozzle taps; schlieren.
  Uncertainty and Mach-6 test conditions not stated.
