# Summary

The reaction-wheel angular-momentum management strategy flown on the
Chinese geostationary meteorological satellite FengYun-4 (FY-4), whose
single-winged solar array produces a solar-radiation torque that
accumulates about 30 N·m·s per day in roll and yaw. The preset wheel
momentum is split into an inertial-frame bias $\mathbf H_0$, sized to
counteract the daily accumulation (Eq. 14), and a null-space bias
$\mathbf M\mathbf h_0$ chosen per wheel configuration (Table 1), so as to
avoid zero crossing and saturation and to narrow the speed range. A
Lyapunov-based null-motion law (Eqs. 20–29) drives the null-space part
of the wheel momentum to the bias in service; a wheel-failure procedure
redistributes the momentum to the remaining wheels (Eqs. 30–36); and a
fixed-pulse-width, fixed-interval bipropellant-thruster dumping law with
thresholds (Eq. 37, Cases A–C) unloads the momentum in a daily 15-minute
housekeeping window. Ten days of flight data (Figs. 6–8) and simulations
of other configurations and of a wheel failure (Figs. 9–12) are the
evidence. The thrusters are chemical; the paper has no electric
propulsion content.

# Key claims

Symbols are the paper's own (see the notation map).

1. **Daily accumulation** (Abstract, §§1, 7, an authors' estimate used as
   design input). The single-wing array causes about 30 N·m·s per day
   (28 N·m·s in midwinter to 34 N·m·s at the spring equinox, from panel
   reflection coefficient, solar intensity at GEO and centre of
   pressure), dumped by bipropellant thrusters in under 15 minutes per
   day.
2. **Momentum design** (§2, Eqs. 4–5).
   $\mathbf h(t)=\mathbf M\mathbf h_0(t)+\mathbf D\mathbf H_0(t)+\mathbf D\mathbf H_D(t)$,
   with $\mathbf D=\mathbf C^T(\mathbf C\mathbf C^T)^{-1}$ the
   pseudo-inverse distribution matrix and $\mathbf M=\mathbf E-\mathbf D\mathbf C$
   the null-motion distribution matrix.
3. **Inertial bias** (§3.1, Eqs. 13–15, derived). The solar-radiation
   accumulation is counteracted if $\sigma_S=\sigma_H+\pi$ ($a>0$) or
   $\sigma_S=\sigma_H$ ($a<0$), best with
   $\sqrt{H_{x0}^2+H_{z0}^2}=aT/2$ (Eq. 14); each wheel then follows
   Eq. 15 with range $\pm\sqrt{d_{i1}^2+d_{i3}^2}\,a\pi/\omega_0$. The pitch
   bias is zero.
4. **Null-space bias** (§3.2, Eqs. 17–19, Table 1).
   $h_{nulli}=(b\mathbf e)_i$ with $\mathbf C\mathbf e=0$; for five
   configurations: type X $[-1,1,-1,1]^T$, $b=20.0$ N·m·s; type K
   $[-1,2,-2,1]^T$, 22.0; type Ψ $[-1,2,-3,2]^T$, 15.0; five wheels
   $[-3,2,2,-5,4]^T$, 9.0; six wheels $[-1,1,-1,1,-1,1]^T$, 18.0. For four
   wheels $b$ is unique; for five and six an "optimal" $b$ is designed
   (the optimisation is not shown). The choice keeps speeds above the
   500 rpm limit set by the bearing oil film.
5. **Null-motion law** (§4, Eqs. 20–25, Lyapunov sketch). With
   $V=\tfrac12(\mathbf M(\mathbf h-\mathbf h_0))^T\mathbf K(\mathbf M(\mathbf h-\mathbf h_0))$,
   $\mathbf K=\mathbf E$, the law
   $\dot{\mathbf h}=\mathbf D\dot{\mathbf H}-\mathbf M\mathbf K_1\mathbf M(\mathbf h-\mathbf h_0)$
   gives $\mathbf M\mathbf h\to\mathbf M\mathbf h_0$ independently of the
   disturbance.
6. **Gains** (Eqs. 26–29). $\mathbf K_1=k_ck_n\mathbf K_2$; the per-wheel
   gain gives 0.002 times the momentum error (0.02 N·m at 10 N·m·s,
   Eq. 27); the null-motion torque is capped at 0.02 N·m (Eq. 28);
   $k_c\in\{0,1\}$ switches null motion off during radiometer-scan
   accelerations, which are compensated by feed-forward.
7. **Wheel failure** (§5, Eqs. 30–36). A failed wheel decays by friction;
   null-motion control on the remaining wheels converges, and the
   synthetic momentum and dumping time are unchanged. One zero crossing
   during recovery is "inevitable but acceptable". With five or more
   wheels the remaining ones continue; with four, a redundant wheel is
   selected by ranking the null-space vectors.
8. **Dumping law** (§6, Eq. 37). Pulse counts
   $P_i=\mathrm{INT}(\Delta h_i/(W_iT_i))$ for the three axes, pulse
   widths 64, 32, 64 ms, intervals 23.04, 46.08, 23.04 s; thresholds
   3 N·m·s (10% of the daily accumulation) and 10 N·m·s (one third).
   Case A: residual at most 3 N·m·s; Case B: additional dumping with a
   corrected torque; Case C: residual at least 10 N·m·s, no further
   dumping and a ground warning (blockage or leakage suspected). The
   thruster torque is corrected on line.
9. **Flight evidence** (§7, Figs. 6–8, ten days). Stable, repeatable daily
   accumulation; no saturation and no zero crossing; dumping within
   15 minutes; automatic dumping undisturbed by a 1–2 N·m increase of the
   thruster torque from a tank-pressure change.

# Methods

- Rigid-body attitude dynamics with $N\ge4$ redundant reaction wheels
  (Eq. 1); small-angle kinematics in the orbit frame (Eq. 2); PID wheel
  torque law with zero commanded attitude (Eq. 3).
- Pseudo-inverse allocation plus null motion (Eq. 4).
- Solar-radiation torque modelled as a constant $a$ in the inertial
  frame over one dumping period $T=24$ h (Eq. 6), rotated to the body
  frame at orbit rate (Eqs. 7–8).
- Hardware (§2): six reaction wheels, 50 N·m·s at 4600 rpm maximum
  momentum, 0.1 N·m maximum torque, mounted symmetrically about the pitch
  axis at 75° (Fig. 2); three pairs of bipropellant thrusters of 10 N
  nominal thrust, about 20 N·m torque. FY-4 has been at 99.5° E since
  17 Dec 2016. The simulation tool is not named.

# Tests and results

- §3.1, Figs. 4–5 (wheels 1, 2, 4, 5, 24 h, radiation torque only):
  maximum wheel speed 1900 rpm without and 1100 rpm with the inertial
  bias (text values).
- Flight, type X (wheels 1, 2, 4, 5): preset three-axis bias
  $[-15,0,0]^T$ N·m·s; per-wheel inertial part
  $[-7.7646,0,7.7646,0]^T$ N·m·s; null-space bias $[-20,20,-20,20]^T$
  N·m·s; expected $\mathbf h_0=[-27.7646,20,-12.2354,20]^T$ N·m·s. Ten
  days of data: momentum phase plane about −17 to +17 N·m·s in roll and
  −11 to +1 N·m·s in yaw (Fig. 6); wheel speeds within about
  1100–2900 rpm in magnitude (Fig. 7); body rates of order
  0.01–0.02 °/s during dumping (Fig. 8). Figure values are approximate
  readings.
- Simulation, type K (wheels 1, 2, 3, 6, Fig. 9): speed range "almost
  4800 rpm/600 rpm", against 2900/1100 rpm for type X; type Ψ (Fig. 10)
  is shown without numbers.
- Simulation, failure: wheel 1 fails at 60000 s through a 0.06 N·m
  friction increase, is isolated in under 1 minute, and wheel 3 is
  brought in to form type K; the attitude fluctuates for about 700 s,
  new speeds are reached in under 1800 s, with one zero crossing
  (Figs. 11–12). The failure case was not tested in orbit.
