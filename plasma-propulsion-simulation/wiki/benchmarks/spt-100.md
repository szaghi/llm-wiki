# SPT-100 as a validation case

The SPT-100 is a stationary plasma thruster (SPT) developed in the former
Soviet Union ([Boyd (2005)](/sources/boyd-2005.md), §3.4; see
[Hall-effect thruster](/definitions/hall-effect-thruster.md)). No source
here defines a single SPT-100 benchmark: each uses the device's geometry
and operating point, or published measurements on it, as a reference.

## The device as the sources give it

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| annular channel diameter | 100 mm | [Boyd (2005)](/sources/boyd-2005.md), §3.4 |
| channel radii | 3.5 and 5.0 cm | [Eckels et al. (2024)](/sources/eckels-2024.md), Table 6; [Saravia et al. (2026)](/sources/saravia-2026.md), Table 1; [Taccogna et al. (2023)](/sources/taccogna-2023.md), Sec. V B |
| channel length | 2.5 cm | [Eckels et al. (2024)](/sources/eckels-2024.md), Table 6; [Saravia et al. (2026)](/sources/saravia-2026.md), Table 1 |
| | "≈3 cm" | [Taccogna et al. (2023)](/sources/taccogna-2023.md), Sec. V B |
| peak radial $B$ | about 0.02 T (printed as "about 200 G") | [Boyd (2005)](/sources/boyd-2005.md), §3.4 |
| | 0.016 T | [Eckels et al. (2024)](/sources/eckels-2024.md), Table 6; [Reza et al. (2022)](/sources/reza-2022.md), Table 3 (16 mT, profile from Panelli et al. 2021) |
| | 0.015 T (LANDMARK case) | [Saravia et al. (2026)](/sources/saravia-2026.md), eq. 15 |
| | 0.017 T (printed as 170 G) | [Miranda et al. (2026)](/sources/miranda-2026.md), §2.1 |
| peak-$B$ location | about 2.5 cm from the anode ("typical for SPT-100") | [Charoy et al. (2020)](/sources/charoy-2020.md), set-up |
| plasma density, ion velocity | about $10^{17}$ m$^{-3}$, about 16 km/s | [Boyd (2005)](/sources/boyd-2005.md), §3.4 |
| plasma density | $10^{18}$ m$^{-3}$ | [Taccogna et al. (2023)](/sources/taccogna-2023.md), Sec. V B |
| | peak $1.04\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ (LANDMARK case, computed) | [Saravia et al. (2026)](/sources/saravia-2026.md), App. A |
| propellant, operating point | xenon; 300 V and 5 mg/s in most runs below | per run |

The figures differ by source: the peak field ranges from 0.015 to about
0.02 T, and the channel length is 2.5 cm or about 3 cm. Boyd's SPT-100
density, about $10^{17}$ m$^{-3}$ (§3.4), is an order of magnitude below
the other sources and below his own Table 1 range for Hall thrusters,
$10^{18}$–$10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ (see [Hall-effect thruster](/definitions/hall-effect-thruster.md)).
The SPT-100 performance Muñoz Tejeda et al. quote from the literature
(below: 1050–1270 s, about 35 % anode efficiency) also sits below Boyd's
typical Hall-thruster range of 1500–2500 s at 50–60 % overall efficiency
(§3.4). Several entries are
model inputs chosen for a study, and Boyd's and Taccogna et al.'s are
approximate; no source claims a measured value against another, so this
is recorded as a spread, not a contradiction. Boyd adds that, with these
values, electrons are magnetized and ions are not, so electrons may be
treated as a fluid while ions and neutrals need a kinetic description.

## Simulations referenced to it

* **2D multi-fluid erosion.** Yim et al.'s model matches the measured
  inner- and outer-wall erosion of the SPT-100 at 160, 310, 600, 800 and
  1000 h, erosion reaching about 5 mm ([Boyd (2005)](/sources/boyd-2005.md),
  §3.4, Fig. 17, attributed). [Taccogna & Garrigues (2019)](/sources/taccogna-2019.md),
  App. 2, report a sputtering threshold of 30–70 eV for BN-SiO$_2$ in the
  SPT-100 (attributed to Garrigues 2003).
* **Wall material and regimes.** Current–voltage curves at 5 mg/s for
  Al$_2$O$_3$, SiC and BN-SiO$_2$ walls (experiment of Gascon et al. 2003,
  1D fluid of Barral et al. 2003), and a 1D hybrid [breathing-mode](/definitions/breathing-mode.md)
  cycle at 280 V and 5 mg/s compared with high-speed imaging (Darnon et
  al. 1999) ([Taccogna & Garrigues (2019)](/sources/taccogna-2019.md),
  §2.6, Figs. 6–7, attributed).
* **Plume.** A polytropic fit of the plume electrons gives an index of
  1.2–1.5 for the SPT-100 (attributed to Giono et al.;
  [Taccogna & Garrigues (2019)](/sources/taccogna-2019.md), §3.6; see
  [polytropic closure](/definitions/polytropic-electron-closure.md)).
* **Heavy-species particle code.** [Gallo et al. (2021)](/sources/gallo-2021.md),
  §4.1, Figs. 5–7, run an SPT-100-like channel (a 0.025 × 0.015 m
  axial–radial box with 34 × 22 nodes, inlet 5 mg/s, $\Delta t=5\times10^{-8}$ s,
  $5\times10^{-4}$ s simulated) with prescribed $\phi$ and $T_e$, and
  compare radially averaged neutral and ion densities with the numerical
  results of Hofer et al. (2007), which were themselves compared with the
  SPT-100 experiment of Keidar et al. (2001). Agreement is "good"; the ion
  peak is slightly narrower than the reference's. No thrust is reported.
* **1D fluid, LANDMARK.** The LANDMARK test case 3 geometry of the
  [1D fluid Hall model](/methods/one-dimensional-fluid-hall-model.md)
  verification is "based on the SPT-100" (300 V, 5 mg/s;
  [Saravia et al. (2026)](/sources/saravia-2026.md), Table 1).
* **Pseudo-2D PIC.** [Reza et al. (2022)](/sources/reza-2022.md), §5,
  Table 3, run an SPT-100-like axial–radial domain after the 2D hybrid
  model of Panelli et al. (2021): 300 V, 5 mg/s, axial length 3.0 cm
  (6 cm in the reference, shortened for speed), radial 1.5 cm,
  $\Delta x=2\times10^{-5}$ m, $\Delta t=2\times10^{-12}$ s, 30 µs (about
  one discharge-current cycle). Compared with 1D axial runs they obtain
  better $T_e$, ionization and $n_i$, but a spurious high anode-sheath drop
  and an over-predicted axial field from the single-region averaging (see
  [reduced-order PIC](/methods/reduced-order-pic.md)).
* **Axial–azimuthal PIC.** [Miranda et al. (2026)](/sources/miranda-2026.md),
  §§2–3, run a 4 cm × 2 cm SPT-100-like slab with XOOPIC at 300 V and real
  mass ratio and permittivity; the runs reach no steady state. The
  [axial–azimuthal PIC benchmark](/benchmarks/axial-azimuthal-pic-benchmark.md)
  is not an SPT-100 model: it places the peak field at 0.75 cm, against
  about 2.5 cm typical for the SPT-100, and its azimuthal length is a
  fraction of the about 18 cm real circumference
  ([Charoy et al. (2020)](/sources/charoy-2020.md)).
* **3D cost.** For an SPT-100 channel with $n=10^{18}$ m$^{-3}$ and
  $T_e\sim10$ eV ($\lambda_D\approx20$ µm), a 3D explicit PIC run would
  need about $10^{10}$ cells, "hundreds of billions of particles" and about
  10 TB of memory ([Taccogna et al. (2023)](/sources/taccogna-2023.md),
  Sec. V B; see [explicit-PIC resolution constraints](/definitions/explicit-pic-resolution-constraints.md)).

## Measurements used for calibration

[Eckels et al. (2024)](/sources/eckels-2024.md) calibrate a coupled
cathode–thruster–plume model on five SPT-100 datasets
(App. C, Tables 8–9; see [Bayesian calibration](/methods/bayesian-calibration.md)):
1. cathode coupling voltage against background pressure (Jorns & Byrne
   2021): 31.12–33.11 V at 300 V;
2. thrust against background pressure at constant $I_d=4.5$ A (Diamant
   et al. 2014): 80.05–83.11 mN at 300 V and 4.764–5.252 mg/s anode flow;
3. plume ion current density against background pressure (Diamant et al.
   2014);
4. centreline axial ion velocity by LIF (Macdonald et al. 2019);
5. thrust and $I_d$ at off-nominal conditions (Sankovic et al. 1993),
   held out for validation.

The calibrated 1D model gets the thrust trend with background pressure
wrong (falling where the data rise), over-predicts $I_d$ by more than
50 %, and keeps errors of about 30 % in thrust and 53 % in $I_d$ on the
held-out dataset (Obs. 1, 4; Table 5).

[Muñoz Tejeda et al. (2025)](/sources/munoz-2025.md) quote SPT-100 xenon
performance from the literature as a reference for their water-fuelled
thruster: 40 mN, 1050 s and 34.5 % anode efficiency at 598 W (201 V,
2.98 A); 64.9 mN, 1270 s and 35.8 % at 1130 W (301 V, 3.77 A).
