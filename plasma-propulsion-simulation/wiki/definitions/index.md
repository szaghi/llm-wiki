# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Anomalous electron cross-field transport](/definitions/anomalous-electron-transport.md) — Electron transport across the magnetic field of a Hall thruster in excess of classical collisional mobility, attributed to azimuthal instabilities and wall effects, resolved self-consistently by kinetic models and closed in fluid and hybrid models by Bohm-type collision frequencies whose fitted coefficients span 0.01 to 7.5 in the convention nu_ano = alpha_B omega_ce / 16.
* [Breathing mode](/definitions/breathing-mode.md) — The low-frequency (about 10–30 kHz) longitudinal oscillation of a Hall-thruster discharge, driven by periodic depletion and refilling of the neutral gas, which sets the run length of whole-device simulations and is the usual target signal for calibrating one-dimensional models.
* [Charge-exchange ions](/definitions/charge-exchange-ions.md) — Slow ions created when a fast ion takes an electron from a slow neutral, which carry little momentum but populate the plume wings and the backflow, erode ion-optics accel grids, and are modelled as DSMC or MCC collisions or as a separate low-energy ion fluid.
* [Cross-field electron mobility and the Hall parameter](/definitions/electron-mobility-and-hall-parameter.md) — The electron mobility tensor of a magnetized drift-diffusion model, whose perpendicular component is the parallel mobility divided by one plus the square of the Hall parameter omega_ce/nu_e; Hall-thruster values of the Hall parameter make the tensor strongly anisotropic and ill-conditioned for numerics.
* [Current-free double layer](/definitions/current-free-double-layer.md) — A localized electrostatic potential drop, a few tens to hundreds of Debye lengths thick, that forms without net current where a helicon plasma expands into a diverging magnetic field and accelerates a supersonic ion beam; measurements and 1D PIC tie it to a depleted-tail electron distribution rather than beam electrons, and its potential drop scales with the electron temperature.
* [Electron drift instability (ECDI)](/definitions/electron-drift-instability.md) — The azimuthal instability driven by the E x B electron drift in Hall thrusters, with millimetre wavelengths and MHz frequencies, cyclotron-resonant in linear theory and close to an ion-acoustic mode in most axial–azimuthal PIC; it carries most of the anomalous electron transport in kinetic simulations.
* [Hall-effect thruster](/definitions/hall-effect-thruster.md) — A gridless electrostatic thruster in which a mainly radial magnetic field across an annular channel impedes the cathode electrons' axial motion, so that an axial electric field accelerates unmagnetized ions; built as the stationary plasma thruster (SPT, ceramic walls, long channel) or the anode-layer thruster (TAL, metallic walls, short channel), and the main simulation target of this bundle.
* [Helicon plasma thruster](/definitions/helicon-plasma-thruster.md) — An electrodeless, neutralizer-free radiofrequency thruster in which a helicon (whistler) wave sustains a dense plasma that expands through a magnetic nozzle, reaching about 10 mN/kW in laboratory tests and up to 17.8% rf efficiency in a privately communicated result, and whose simulation needs electromagnetic fields, neutral depletion and nozzle physics.
* [Ion beam shepherd](/definitions/ion-beam-shepherd.md) — Contactless space-debris removal by a shepherd satellite whose impulse-transfer ion thruster pushes the debris with its plume while a compensation thruster cancels the reaction; the beam force is modelled with a self-similar conical far-plume model, and the concept is the reference against which magnetic drag augmentation is compared.
* [Magnetic nozzle](/definitions/magnetic-nozzle.md) — A diverging applied magnetic field that guides an expanding plasma and converts electron thermal energy into directed ion momentum, its reaction force acting on the field coils or magnets; models range from analytic thrust integrals and 1D kinetic solutions to 2D collisionless fluid models, and plasma detachment from the field lines remains unsettled.
* [Magnetic shielding](/definitions/magnetic-shielding.md) — A Hall-thruster magnetic topology, with field lines grazing the channel walls and a chamfered channel, that keeps the electrons near the walls cold so that the radial electric field and the sheath drop shrink; reported to cut computed wall erosion by three orders of magnitude.
* [Near-wall conductivity](/definitions/near-wall-conductivity.md) — Electron cross-field transport produced by electron collisions with the channel walls and by secondary-electron emission, modelled in fluid codes as an electron–wall collision frequency and resolved in radial PIC; the sources treat it as a contributor to, not the explanation of, anomalous transport.
* [Polytropic electron closure and plume cooling](/definitions/polytropic-electron-closure.md) — Closure of the electron energy equation by a polytropic law, $T_e\propto n_e^{\gamma-1}$, equivalent to a purely convective heat flux; used in plume and magnetic-nozzle models, where measured, kinetic and fitted indices range from near-isothermal to adiabatic and depend on the electron population and direction.
* [Pulsed plasma thruster](/definitions/pulsed-plasma-thruster.md) — An electric thruster that ablates a solid propellant (usually PTFE) in a microsecond discharge and accelerates the products, with low efficiency, dense continuum-to-kinetic plasma, a spark-plug ignition phase and late-time ablation that fluid models miss; laser ablation variants separate ablation from acceleration.
* [Resolution constraints of explicit PIC](/definitions/explicit-pic-resolution-constraints.md) — The cell-size, time-step, collision-probability and particles-per-cell conditions an explicit particle-in-cell simulation of a thruster plasma must meet, with the values the sources state and use and the different criteria behind their particles-per-cell requirements.
* [Rotating spoke](/definitions/rotating-spoke.md) — A large-scale, kHz, centimetre-wavelength azimuthal structure rotating at a fraction of the E x B drift in Hall thrusters and other E x B discharges, classed as a coherent structure of the discharge and studied with 3D PIC and 2D axial–azimuthal hybrid models.
* [Secondary electron emission and sheath regimes](/definitions/secondary-electron-emission.md) — Electron-induced emission from dielectric thruster walls and its effect on the sheath: yield models (linear, Vaughan, Furman–Pivi), the classical sheath drop and its collapse at the space-charge-saturation yield (about 0.983 for xenon), Bohm-criterion and anode-sheath conditions, and the kinetic effects (distribution depletion, sheath oscillations, SEE bursts) that fluid sheath models miss.
* [Thermalized potential](/definitions/thermalized-potential.md) — Morozov's combination of the electrostatic potential and the electron density, constant along each magnetic line when the electron temperature is uniform along the line and parallel mobility is large; used to reduce the 2D electron fluid problem to a problem across field lines.
