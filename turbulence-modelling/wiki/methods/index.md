# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Active model split hybrid RANS/LES](/methods/active-model-split.md) — Haering, Oliver and Moser's hybrid RANS/LES method that splits the modelled stress into a mean part scaled from RANS and an energy-transfer part acting on resolved fluctuations, and adds active forcing where an anisotropic resolution indicator allows; it needs no blending function and removes log-layer mismatch.
* [Boundary-layer-conforming grids for wall-modelled LES](/methods/boundary-layer-conforming-grid.md) — Grids whose local isotropic cell size follows the local boundary-layer thickness, floored by a minimum grid Reynolds number at leading edges and capped by surface curvature; the boundary-layer-thickness estimate they need, their cost, and the evidence that they reduce WMLES error at fewer points than constant-size grids.
* [Detached-eddy simulation and its delayed and zonal forms](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) — The single-model hybrid RANS/LES in which a RANS model's length scale is capped by a grid length so that the model acts as a subgrid model away from walls; the DES switch and its failure on ambiguous grids, the DDES shielding function, IDDES, ZDES modes, grid lengths and the shear-layer-adapted length, calibration with the numerical scheme, and applications.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md) — The DGSEM is a collocated nodal discontinuous Galerkin scheme on tensor-product hexahedra with Legendre–Gauss or Gauss–Lobatto nodes; this page states its weak and strong forms, the SBP property, modal filtering and filtered artificial viscosity, its cost per degree of freedom, how interface dissipation shapes LES on it, element-local filters and mortars, and modal and continuous relatives.
* [Divergence-consistent discretize-first LES](/methods/divergence-consistent-les.md) — Agdestein & Sanderse's framework for learned LES closures: discretize, differentiate the constraint, filter with a face-averaging filter that maps discretely divergence-free fields to divergence-free fields, and close inside the pressure projection; it yields exact closure targets and a stable CNN closure.
* [Dynamic procedure and the Germano identity](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) — The dynamic procedure computes subgrid-model coefficients from the resolved field through the Germano identity between grid- and test-filter stresses, solved by least squares with averaging or clipping; the sources report its forms, regularizations, failures near walls and on coarse grids, and its tensorial extension.
* [Dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model](/methods/dynamic-tensor-coefficient-smagorinsky-model.md) — The dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model (DTCSM) replaces the scalar Smagorinsky coefficient by a traceless-constrained coefficient tensor with four components fitted dynamically, giving a non-Boussinesq stress that dissipates like dynamic Smagorinsky and converges monotonically in wall-modelled LES of the Gaussian bump.
* [Equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) — The wall-stress model of wall-modelled LES that assumes a locally constant-stress, equilibrium inner layer: its ODE and algebraic forms, constants, matching heights, compressible extension, error propagation from LES input and model constants, cost, and the regimes where the sources show it fails.
* [Field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md) — Field inversion infers a spatial correction field of a RANS model from data by solving a PDE-constrained inverse problem; FIML then learns that field from local flow features and embeds the learned map in the solver. The sources compare which model term to correct and show non-unique, over-fitted inversions.
* [Fractional subgrid-scale model](/methods/fractional-sgs-model.md) — The fractional subgrid-scale model closes the divergence of the subgrid stress with a fractional Laplacian of the filtered velocity, derived from a filtered BGK equation; its dynamic form computes the coefficient from a Germano identity posed on forces and fixes the fractional order from filtered DNS of isotropic turbulence.
* [Gradient (Clark) model and the clipping of structural SGS models](/methods/gradient-model.md) — Clark's gradient model approximates the subgrid stress by a truncated Taylor expansion of the filter; it correlates well with the exact stress but under-dissipates and backscatters, so it is clipped or mixed. Optimal clipping is the Frobenius-nearest backscatter-free stress, a parameter-free mixed model.
* [High-order gas-kinetic schemes for turbulence simulation](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md) — A high-order gas-kinetic scheme is a finite-volume Navier–Stokes solver whose interface flux comes from the time-dependent solution of the BGK kinetic equation; this page gives its flux, collision time, reconstruction and two-stage fourth-order time stepping, and its use and cost as a DNS tool from low-Mach to supersonic turbulence.
* [Immersed boundary methods](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) — Immersed boundary methods impose solid walls on grids that do not conform to them, by reconstructing values at boundary or ghost points or by a penalization source; this page covers the variants used for DNS and LES, their coupling with wall models in a lattice Boltzmann solver, and the loss of near-wall stress they cause.
* [Inverse-velocity-transformation wall model](/methods/inverse-velocity-transformation-wall-model.md) — A one-ODE wall-stress model for compressible wall-modelled LES with heat transfer: the incompressible mixing-length solution is mapped to compressible profiles by the inverse total-stress-based velocity transformation, and temperature follows from a matching-anchored Reynolds analogy, with a priori and a posteriori accuracy and cost.
* [Lagrangian relaxation towards equilibrium (LaRTE) wall model](/methods/lagrangian-relaxation-towards-equilibrium-wall-model.md) — A wall-stress model that transports the friction-velocity vector along wall trajectories and relaxes it towards the equilibrium stress with the inertial time of the fluid layer below the matching height, optionally adding a laminar Stokes-layer stress driven by fast pressure-gradient fluctuations.
* [Local-correlation-based transition models](/methods/local-correlation-based-transition-model.md) — RANS laminar–turbulent transition models that evaluate an empirical onset correlation with local variables and switch on the turbulence model through an intermittency; the γ-Reθ model, its calibrations and separation-induced correction, the one-equation γ and algebraic γ-alg-SST members, hysteresis, very-low-Reynolds airfoils and mesh requirements.
* [Low-dissipation low-dispersion schemes with shock sensor](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md) — The LD2 scheme pairs a skew-symmetric energy-preserving central flux, with gradient-extrapolated face values that cut dispersion, and a much-reduced fourth-difference matrix dissipation; its compressible extension LD2C adds extrapolated density and sound speed and a Ducros-weighted shock sensor, and removes the excess dissipation that spoils hybrid RANS/LES.
* [Machine-learning wall models](/methods/machine-learning-wall-model.md) — Wall-stress models for wall-modelled LES learned by supervised regression or multi-agent reinforcement learning: the published models, the SciMARL formulation of its authors, a posteriori behaviour in channel flow up to Re_tau = 10^10 and in a flat-plate boundary layer, the extrapolation argument, log-law preservation as the acceptance test, and cost.
* [Mesh adaptation for turbulent-flow simulation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md) — Changing the mesh (h, p or hp; isotropic or anisotropic) from an error estimate or refinement criterion in a turbulent-flow computation: Hessian and metric criteria for RANS, time-averaged criteria for hybrid RANS/LES, LES modelling-residual and truncation-error estimators for high-order DG, and refinement strategy as a dissipation control.
* [Model-consistent (a posteriori) training of learned closures](/methods/model-consistent-training.md) — Training a learned closure with the solver in the loop, so the loss is evaluated on the model's own solution: field inversion, FIML, adjoint-based integrated training, differentiable-solver unrolling and reinforcement learning, contrasted with open-loop injection of a priori-trained corrections, with consistency arguments, costs and limits.
* [Partially-averaged Navier–Stokes](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md) — Girimaji's bridging hybrid model that solves RANS-form transport equations for the unresolved fractions of kinetic energy and dissipation; its SST form on high-order flux reconstruction, its variable-density BHR form for Rayleigh–Taylor mixing, the choice of resolution parameters, and the criticism of feeding RANS equations fluctuating inputs.
* [Physics-informed neural networks for RANS](/methods/physics-informed-neural-network.md) — A neural network maps the coordinates to the mean velocity, pressure and Reynolds stresses and is trained on the RANS residuals plus boundary data; the RANS system is closed by data rather than by a turbulence model, with errors reported for boundary layers, an airfoil and the periodic hill.
* [Reinforcement-learning turbulence closures](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md) — Closures set or learned online by a policy trained with reinforcement learning, the running LES being the environment and the reward a solver statistic: element-local SGS coefficients in DG-LES, pointwise SGS stress tensors for channel LES trained on mean profiles, and multi-agent wall-stress models with disputed extrapolation.
* [STITCH helicity-condensation model](/methods/stitch-helicity-condensation-model.md) — STITCH replaces explicitly resolved photospheric vortical driving in coronal MHD simulations by one source term in the induction equation that injects condensed magnetic helicity at the base; it is divergence-free, leaves the normal field unchanged, and cuts the cost of energy buildup about fifteenfold.
* [Scale-similarity and mixed subgrid-scale models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) — Scale-similarity and deconvolution models build the subgrid stress from the resolved field itself rather than from an eddy viscosity; the sources give Bardina-type and modified-Leonard forms, agree that they reproduce the stress structure but are under-dissipative or unstable alone, and mix them with an eddy viscosity.
* [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) — The Smagorinsky model closes the deviatoric subgrid stress with an eddy viscosity (C_s Δ)²|S̄|; the sources report its constants, filter-width definitions in element methods and on high-aspect-ratio cells, its interaction with numerical error, and its a priori and a posteriori behaviour.
* [Spalart–Allmaras model and its corrections](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) — The one-equation eddy-viscosity RANS model transporting the working variable ν̃: its equations and constants, the negative, rotation–curvature, helicity and quadratic-constitutive-relation variants, field-inversion and data-assimilation corrections, its role as host of detached-eddy simulation, and evidence from compressors, rotors, airfoils and high-lift flows.
* [Spectral eddy viscosity](/methods/spectral-eddy-viscosity.md) — Kraichnan's spectral eddy viscosity expresses the energy transfer across a cutoff as a wavenumber-dependent viscosity with a low-wavenumber plateau and a cusp near the cutoff; the sources use it to diagnose explicit and implicit LES and to build a modal plateau–cusp model for DG.
* [Spectral vanishing viscosity](/methods/spectral-vanishing-viscosity.md) — Spectral vanishing viscosity concentrates an added viscosity on the highest resolved scales. The sources use two forms: an implicit one that boosts the modified wavenumber of the viscous scheme near the cutoff, and an explicit modal filter on a DG artificial-viscosity flux made entropy stable; both serve as LES models, the second also for shock capturing.
* [Subcell finite-volume shock capturing for DG](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md) — Troubled DG elements are recomputed as (N+1)^3 finite-volume subcells with the same degrees of freedom, either switched by an indicator or blended convexly with the DG operator; the blending factor has also been controlled by reinforcement learning as an implicit subgrid model.
* [Subgrid turbulent dynamo model](/methods/subgrid-turbulent-dynamo-model.md) — A subgrid closure of the induction equation for cosmological MHD: an isotropic mean-field alpha effect whose coefficient is the subgrid turbulent velocity dispersion of a one-equation SGS kinetic-energy model, quenched when the magnetic energy reaches a tunable fraction q of the subgrid turbulent energy.
* [Tensor-basis neural network and the integrity basis](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) — A neural network that predicts the scalar coefficients of Pope's ten-tensor integrity basis from invariants of the mean strain and rotation, so the learned Reynolds-stress anisotropy is objective by construction; random forests, gene-expression programming and sparse regression learn the same expansion, and larger integrity bases supply invariant input features.
* [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md) — Vreman's static eddy-viscosity model builds the subgrid viscosity from an invariant of the resolved velocity-gradient tensor so that it vanishes in many laminar flows; the sources print it in two index orders with constants from 0.08 to 0.17, and use it in aircraft, channel, lattice Boltzmann, DG and shock-interaction LES.
* [WENO and hybrid shock-capturing schemes in scale-resolving simulation](/methods/weno-scheme.md) — WENO schemes capture shocks by nonlinear weighting of candidate stencils; in DNS and LES of compressible turbulence their dissipation is reduced by optimized weights and limiters, by linear weights in smooth flow, or by hybrids that apply WENO only where a shock sensor fires, and in implicit LES it serves as the subgrid dissipation.
* [Wall-modelled large-eddy simulation](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) — LES that resolves the outer-layer eddies and replaces the near-wall viscous region by a wall-stress model: its definition and cost advantage, the grid-dependent error scaling of mean quantities, the interaction with SGS models, non-monotonic grid convergence, grid adaptation, immersed-boundary coupling, and its demonstrations on aircraft and separated flows.
* [k-ω SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) — Menter's two-equation shear-stress-transport eddy-viscosity model, blending a k-ω inner formulation with an outer one through F1 and limiting the eddy viscosity through F2; its equations and constants, its transition, PANS and DES forms and modifications, and evidence on airfoils, fan stages, vortex flows and its transported k.
