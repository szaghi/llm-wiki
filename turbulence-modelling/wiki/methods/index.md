# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Active model split hybrid RANS/LES](/methods/active-model-split.md) — Haering, Oliver and Moser's hybrid RANS/LES method that splits the modelled stress into a mean part scaled from RANS and an energy-transfer part acting on resolved fluctuations, and adds active forcing where an anisotropic resolution indicator allows; it needs no blending function and removes log-layer mismatch.
* [Boundary-layer-conforming grids for wall-modelled LES](/methods/boundary-layer-conforming-grid.md) — Grids whose local isotropic cell size follows the local boundary-layer thickness, floored by a minimum grid Reynolds number at leading edges and capped by surface curvature; the boundary-layer-thickness estimate they need, their cost, and the evidence that they reduce WMLES error at fewer points than constant-size grids.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md) — The DGSEM is a collocated nodal discontinuous Galerkin scheme on tensor-product hexahedra with Legendre–Gauss or Gauss–Lobatto nodes; this page states its weak and strong forms, the SBP property, its cost per degree of freedom and how its interface dissipation shapes LES on it.
* [Divergence-consistent discretize-first LES](/methods/divergence-consistent-les.md) — Agdestein & Sanderse's framework for learned LES closures: discretize, differentiate the constraint, filter with a face-averaging filter that maps discretely divergence-free fields to divergence-free fields, and close inside the pressure projection; it yields exact closure targets and a stable CNN closure.
* [Dynamic procedure and the Germano identity](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) — The dynamic procedure computes subgrid-model coefficients from the resolved field through the Germano identity between grid- and test-filter stresses, solved by least squares with averaging or clipping; the sources report its forms, regularizations, failures near walls and on coarse grids, and its tensorial extension.
* [Dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model](/methods/dynamic-tensor-coefficient-smagorinsky-model.md) — The dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model (DTCSM) replaces the scalar Smagorinsky coefficient by a traceless-constrained coefficient tensor with four components fitted dynamically, giving a non-Boussinesq stress that dissipates like dynamic Smagorinsky and converges monotonically in wall-modelled LES of the Gaussian bump.
* [Equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) — The wall-stress model of wall-modelled LES that assumes a locally constant-stress, equilibrium inner layer: its ODE and algebraic forms, constants, matching heights, compressible extension, error propagation from LES input and model constants, cost, and the regimes where the sources show it fails.
* [Field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md) — Field inversion infers a spatial correction field of a RANS model from data by solving a PDE-constrained inverse problem; FIML then learns that field as a function of local flow features and embeds the learned map in the solver, so the training targets come from the model's own environment.
* [Inverse-velocity-transformation wall model](/methods/inverse-velocity-transformation-wall-model.md) — A one-ODE wall-stress model for compressible wall-modelled LES with heat transfer: the incompressible mixing-length solution is mapped to compressible profiles by the inverse total-stress-based velocity transformation, and temperature follows from a matching-anchored Reynolds analogy, with a priori and a posteriori accuracy and cost.
* [Lagrangian relaxation towards equilibrium (LaRTE) wall model](/methods/lagrangian-relaxation-towards-equilibrium-wall-model.md) — A wall-stress model that transports the friction-velocity vector along wall trajectories and relaxes it towards the equilibrium stress with the inertial time of the fluid layer below the matching height, optionally adding a laminar Stokes-layer stress driven by fast pressure-gradient fluctuations.
* [Local-correlation-based transition models](/methods/local-correlation-based-transition-model.md) — RANS laminar–turbulent transition models that evaluate an empirical onset correlation with local variables and switch on the turbulence model through an intermittency; the γ-Reθ, one-equation γ and algebraic γ-alg-SST members, the hysteresis of transition location and its harmonic-averaging remedy, and mesh requirements.
* [Machine-learning wall models](/methods/machine-learning-wall-model.md) — Wall-stress models for wall-modelled LES learned by supervised regression or multi-agent reinforcement learning: the published models, their a posteriori behaviour in channel flow up to Re_tau = 10^10, the extrapolation argument from activation functions, log-law preservation as the acceptance test, and cost.
* [Model-consistent (a posteriori) training of learned closures](/methods/model-consistent-training.md) — Training a learned closure with the solver in the loop, so the loss is evaluated on the model's own solution: field inversion, FIML, adjoint-based integrated training, differentiable-solver unrolling and reinforcement learning, with their consistency arguments, costs and limits.
* [Partially-averaged Navier–Stokes](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md) — Girimaji's bridging hybrid model that solves RANS-form transport equations for the unresolved fractions f_k and f_ω of the kinetic energy and specific dissipation; its SST form, its use with high-order flux reconstruction, the shift of the optimal f_k with discretization order, and the criticism of feeding RANS equations fluctuating inputs.
* [Reinforcement-learning turbulence closures](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md) — Closure parameters chosen online by a policy trained with reinforcement learning, with the running LES as the environment and a reward computed from solver statistics; element-local SGS coefficients and DG/FV blending in DG-LES, and a multi-agent wall-stress model in WMLES.
* [Scale-similarity and mixed subgrid-scale models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) — Scale-similarity and deconvolution models build the subgrid stress from the resolved field itself rather than from an eddy viscosity; the sources agree that they reproduce the stress structure and allow stable backscatter but are under-dissipative, hence mixed with a Smagorinsky term.
* [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) — The Smagorinsky model closes the deviatoric subgrid stress with an eddy viscosity (C_s Δ)²|S̄|; the sources report its constants, filter-width definitions in element methods, its interaction with numerical error, and its a priori and a posteriori behaviour.
* [Spalart–Allmaras model and its corrections](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) — The one-equation eddy-viscosity RANS model transporting the working variable ν̃, with its negative-ν̃, rotation–curvature, Kato–Launder, strain-based, helicity and quadratic-constitutive-relation variants, and the evidence on corner separation, high-lift and field-inversion corrections.
* [Spectral eddy viscosity](/methods/spectral-eddy-viscosity.md) — Kraichnan's spectral eddy viscosity expresses the energy transfer across a cutoff as a wavenumber-dependent viscosity with a low-wavenumber plateau and a cusp near the cutoff; the sources use it to diagnose explicit and implicit LES and to build a modal plateau–cusp model for DG.
* [Spectral vanishing viscosity](/methods/spectral-vanishing-viscosity.md) — Implicit spectral vanishing viscosity boosts the modified wavenumber of the viscous-term discretization near the mesh cutoff, giving a scale-selective hyperviscous dissipation with one parameter; it serves as an implicit LES model that beats Smagorinsky on the Taylor–Green vortex and pipe flow but misses the spectral plateau.
* [Subcell finite-volume shock capturing for DG](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md) — Troubled DG elements are recomputed as (N+1)^3 finite-volume subcells with the same degrees of freedom, either switched by an indicator or blended convexly with the DG operator; the blending factor has also been controlled by reinforcement learning as an implicit subgrid model.
* [Tensor-basis neural network and the integrity basis](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) — A neural network that predicts the scalar coefficients of Pope's ten-tensor integrity basis from invariants of the mean strain and rotation, so the learned Reynolds-stress anisotropy is objective by construction; random forests, gene-expression programming and sparse regression learn the same expansion.
* [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md) — Vreman's static eddy-viscosity model builds the subgrid viscosity from an invariant of the resolved velocity-gradient tensor so that it vanishes in many laminar flows; the sources give its form and report its use in aircraft LES, burner LES and cavity noise, and its failure to separate on the Gaussian bump.
* [Wall-modelled large-eddy simulation](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) — LES that resolves the outer-layer eddies and replaces the near-wall viscous region by a wall-stress model: its definition and cost advantage, the grid-dependent error scaling of mean quantities, the interaction with SGS models, non-monotonic grid convergence, and its demonstrations on aircraft and separated flows.
* [k-ω SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) — Menter's two-equation shear-stress-transport eddy-viscosity model, blending a k-ω inner formulation with an outer one through F1 and limiting the eddy viscosity through F2; its equations and constants as printed in the sources, its transition and PANS forms, and its unsuitability where k must be the true kinetic energy.
