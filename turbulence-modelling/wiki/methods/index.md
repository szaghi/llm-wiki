# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Active model split hybrid RANS/LES](/methods/active-model-split.md) — Haering, Oliver and Moser's hybrid RANS/LES method that splits the modelled stress into a mean part scaled from RANS and an energy-transfer part acting on resolved fluctuations, and adds active forcing where an anisotropic resolution indicator allows; it needs no blending function and removes log-layer mismatch.
* [Aggregation and blending of data-driven turbulence models](/methods/turbulence-model-aggregation.md) — Several RANS models, each trained for one flow class, are combined with space-dependent convex weights learned from local flow features: externally, by mixing the solutions of separate runs, or internally, by blending the experts' corrections inside one RANS solve; tested on separated flows, a jet, a hump and an airfoil.
* [Anisotropic minimum-dissipation model](/methods/anisotropic-minimum-dissipation-model.md) — The anisotropic minimum-dissipation (AMD) model is an eddy-viscosity subgrid model built from directional grid spacings and switched off where the resolved flow does not transfer energy to the subgrid; the sources give its formula and constant, use it as the solver model of learned wall models, and compare it with dynamic Smagorinsky and learned closures.
* [Artificial-viscosity shock capturing](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md) — Artificial-viscosity shock capturing adds a dissipative term, switched by a shock sensor or built from compression, to stabilize high-order schemes at shocks; this page gives the von Neumann–Richtmyer viscous pressure in modal DG, localized artificial diffusivity, filtered and residual-based forms, and how they compare with order-lowering schemes in compressible turbulence.
* [Autonomous eddy-viscosity model](/methods/autonomous-eddy-viscosity-model.md) — Domaradzki's autonomous LES derives the eddy viscosity at every step from the resolved field itself: in spectral space from the subgrid transfer across a test cutoff, in physical space from the filtered dissipation of a similarity stress, with no postulated functional form and no adjustable constant.
* [Baldwin–Lomax algebraic model and its compressible recalibration](/methods/baldwin-lomax-model.md) — The two-layer algebraic RANS eddy viscosity of Baldwin and Lomax, a mixing length with van Driest damping under a Clauser–Klebanoff outer layer, in its semilocal form, and Chen et al.'s compressible modification by the Griffin–Fu–Moin and van Driest transformations and a two-layer temperature relation, tested on twelve high-speed boundary layers.
* [Boundary-layer-conforming grids for wall-modelled LES](/methods/boundary-layer-conforming-grid.md) — Grids whose local isotropic cell size follows the local boundary-layer thickness, floored by a minimum grid Reynolds number at leading edges and capped by surface curvature; the boundary-layer-thickness estimate they need, their cost, the evidence that they reduce WMLES error at fewer points than constant-size grids, and the opposite thickness-relative constraint of detached-eddy grids.
* [Building-block-flow wall model](/methods/building-block-flow-wall-model.md) — Supervised wall-stress model for wall-modelled LES that classifies the local near-wall state among seven canonical building-block flows and blends per-flow neural-network predictions by the class probabilities, trained on solver-consistent WMLES data, with a confidence score, and tested from laminar plates to the CRM-HL and the NASA Juncture Flow.
* [Continuous eddy simulation](/methods/continuous-eddy-simulation.md) — Heinz's hybrid RANS/LES whose length-scale-equation destruction coefficient varies with the ratio of modelled to total length scale, so that modelled and resolved motion stay balanced under grid and Reynolds-number changes; its derivation, its contrast with PITM, and its SST implementation in a lattice Boltzmann solver.
* [Continuous-Galerkin spectral-element method](/methods/spectral-element-method.md) — The continuous-Galerkin spectral-element method expands the solution in tensor-product Lagrange polynomials on LGL nodes, continuous across elements by direct stiffness summation; this page covers its formulation, the derivative jumps and weak wall-stress imposition that shape LES on it, nodal subgrid coefficients and filter widths, and implicit RANS source terms.
* [Detached-eddy simulation and its delayed and zonal forms](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) — The single-model hybrid RANS/LES in which a RANS model's length scale is capped by a grid length so that the model acts as a subgrid model away from walls; the DES switch and ambiguous grids, DDES shielding, IDDES, ZDES modes including mode 2 (2020), grid lengths, calibration with the numerical scheme, transitional DDES, and applications.
* [Differential Reynolds-stress models](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md) — Second-moment RANS closures that transport the six Reynolds stresses instead of postulating an eddy viscosity: the SSG/LRR-ω model with its F1 blending and constants, its coupling to an intermittency transition model, the ATAAC model, and evidence on turbine and compressor cascades, delta wings, wake vortices and buffet.
* [Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md) — The DGSEM is a collocated nodal discontinuous Galerkin scheme on tensor-product hexahedra; this page states its forms, SBP property, de-aliasing, modal filtering, cost and GPU implementation, how dissipation and penalties shape LES on it, and its modal, hybridized, mixed and continuous relatives in shock–turbulence, RANS and error-estimation work.
* [Divergence-consistent discretize-first LES](/methods/divergence-consistent-les.md) — Agdestein & Sanderse's framework for learned LES closures: discretize, differentiate the constraint, filter with a face-averaging filter that maps discretely divergence-free fields to divergence-free fields, and close inside the pressure projection; it yields exact closure targets and a stable CNN closure.
* [Dynamic procedure and the Germano identity](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) — The dynamic procedure computes subgrid-model coefficients from the resolved field through the Germano identity between grid- and test-filter stresses, solved by least squares with averaging or clipping; the sources report its forms, regularizations, failures near walls and on coarse grids, and its tensorial extension.
* [Dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model](/methods/dynamic-tensor-coefficient-smagorinsky-model.md) — The dynamic tensor-coefficient Smagorinsky model (DTCSM) replaces the scalar Smagorinsky coefficient by a traceless-constrained coefficient tensor with four components fitted dynamically, giving a non-Boussinesq stress that dissipates like dynamic Smagorinsky and converges monotonically in wall-modelled LES of the Gaussian bump.
* [Ensemble Kalman filter calibration of turbulence closures](/methods/ensemble-kalman-closure-calibration.md) — Derivative-free calibration of closure parameters from data with an ensemble of solver runs updated by a Kalman analysis; listed for RANS field inversion and used in LES to calibrate the dissipative coefficient of a gradient-plus-Smagorinsky mixed model against filtered-DNS energy spectra, with shifting and inflation for convergence.
* [Equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) — The wall-stress model of wall-modelled LES that assumes a locally constant-stress, equilibrium inner layer: its ODE and algebraic forms, constants, matching heights, compressible extension, error propagation from LES input and model constants, cost, and the regimes where the sources show it fails.
* [Explicit modal filtering as an LES closure](/methods/explicit-modal-filtering.md) — Explicit filtering of the solution itself, in the modal space of a high-order element or by compact physical-space filters, damps the highest resolved modes in place of a subgrid stress. The sources gate it by a resolution or smoothness sensor, tie its cutoff to local flow characteristics, and compare it with Smagorinsky, entropy filtering, split forms and implicit LES.
* [Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings acoustic analogy](/methods/ffowcs-williams-hawkings-acoustic-analogy.md) — The Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings analogy turns a scale-resolving flow solution into far-field sound through surface (thickness, loading) and volume (quadrupole) integrals; this page gives its convective form, surface and quadrupole choices, retarded-time handling and the accuracy reported for jets, fan stages and a wind turbine.
* [Field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md) — Field inversion infers a spatial correction field of a RANS model from data by solving a PDE-constrained inverse problem; FIML learns that field from local flow features and embeds it in the solver. Sources compare which term to correct, show non-unique and over-fitted inversions, gate corrections out of attached layers, and invert on high-order DG.
* [Flamelet-based tabulated chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md) — Flamelet-based tabulated chemistry stores the thermochemical state of one-dimensional flames in a table indexed by mixture fraction, progress variables and enthalpy; this page gives the manifold construction, the extensions for curvature, wall quenching and exhaust-gas recirculation, the subgrid mixture-fraction variance in LES of supersonic combustion, and assessments against flame-resolved simulations and experiment.
* [Flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md) — Flux reconstruction (FR/CPR) corrects a degree-N discontinuous flux polynomial per element with Riemann-solver interface corrections; its correction functions recover nodal DG or spectral difference. This page covers its formulation, the correction parameter as a filter, nonlinearly stable split-form FR, implicit FR for transitional RANS, its use as implicit LES, explicit SGS models and filter width, and its stabilization.
* [Fractional subgrid-scale model](/methods/fractional-sgs-model.md) — The fractional subgrid-scale model closes the divergence of the subgrid stress with a fractional Laplacian of the filtered velocity, derived from a filtered BGK equation; its dynamic form computes the coefficient from a Germano identity posed on forces and fixes the fractional order from filtered DNS of isotropic turbulence.
* [Gradient (Clark) model and the clipping of structural SGS models](/methods/gradient-model.md) — Clark's gradient model truncates the Taylor expansion of the filter; it correlates well with the exact stress but under-dissipates and backscatters, so it is clipped (optimal clipping is a parameter-free mixed model) or mixed with a calibrated eddy viscosity. Its fourth- and sixth-order truncations run stably in two-dimensional LES.
* [High-order gas-kinetic schemes for turbulence simulation](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md) — A high-order gas-kinetic scheme is a finite-volume Navier–Stokes solver whose interface flux comes from the time-dependent BGK solution; this page gives its flux, collision time, reconstructions, two-stage fourth-order time stepping, curvilinear extension, its use as DNS and implicit LES of compressible wall-bounded flows, and its multi-GPU cost.
* [High-order projection-based upwind (HOPU) method](/methods/high-order-projection-based-upwind-method.md) — HOPU restricts the upwind penalty of a high-order discontinuous convective form to the part of the velocity jump that a facet projection of order l cannot represent, with l chosen facet by facet from a jump indicator; in implicit LES it removes excess near-wall dissipation in channel, hill and transitional airfoil flow without altering transition.
* [Immersed boundary methods](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) — Immersed boundary methods impose walls on non-conforming grids by reconstruction, ghost points, volume penalization or direct forcing; this page covers DNS, LES and RANS variants, stiff penalization by Strang splitting, the Lagrangian weight, wall functions with a modelling height or linearization, volume filtering with wall-slip models for LES, Reynolds averaging of penalized flows, and the near-wall errors reported.
* [Inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md) — How a scale-resolving simulation is fed with turbulence at its inflow or in its initial field: recycling–rescaling and precursor planes, digital-filter and Fourier-mode synthetic turbulence built from prescribed statistics (also from a RANS solution), forcing, wall blowing and suction, geometric tripping, or laminar inflow by choice, with the recovery each needs and the errors each leaves.
* [Inverse-velocity-transformation wall model](/methods/inverse-velocity-transformation-wall-model.md) — A one-ODE wall-stress model for compressible wall-modelled LES with heat transfer: the incompressible mixing-length solution is mapped to compressible profiles by the inverse total-stress-based velocity transformation, and temperature follows from a matching-anchored Reynolds analogy, with a priori and a posteriori accuracy and cost.
* [Jump-penalty stabilization of high-order methods](/methods/jump-penalty-stabilization.md) — Jump-penalty stabilization adds to a high-order element method face terms that penalize the jump of the solution or of its gradient across element interfaces; it acts as localized numerical diffusion and serves as the implicit subgrid dissipation of DG and spectral/hp implicit LES of under-resolved turbulence.
* [Lagrangian relaxation towards equilibrium (LaRTE) wall model](/methods/lagrangian-relaxation-towards-equilibrium-wall-model.md) — A wall-stress model that transports the friction-velocity vector along wall trajectories and relaxes it towards the equilibrium stress with the inertial time of the fluid layer below the matching height, optionally adding a laminar Stokes-layer stress driven by fast pressure-gradient fluctuations.
* [Laminar kinetic energy transition models](/methods/laminar-kinetic-energy-transition-model.md) — RANS transition closures that transport the energy of pre-transitional laminar fluctuations and transfer it to the turbulent kinetic energy at transition; the multi-mode reformulation of Pacciani et al. coupled to SST, the Walters–Cokljat model with correlation-based compressible thresholds of Ghidoni & Noventa, and their validation.
* [Lattice Boltzmann methods for turbulent flow](/methods/lattice-boltzmann-method.md) — Lattice Boltzmann methods advance particle populations on a discrete velocity lattice by local collision and streaming, recovering weakly compressible Navier–Stokes; this page gives the scheme, regularized and central-moment collisions, LES and RANS/hybrid eddy viscosities in the relaxation time, immersed boundaries, a Hall-MHD extension, and cost and accuracy.
* [Local-correlation-based transition models](/methods/local-correlation-based-transition-model.md) — RANS laminar–turbulent transition models that evaluate an empirical onset correlation with local variables and switch on the turbulence model through an intermittency; the γ-Reθ model and its separation correction, the one-equation γ model and its Reynolds-stress coupling, algebraic γ-alg-SST, coupling to delayed DES, hysteresis, low-Reynolds airfoils, cascades and mesh requirements.
* [Low-dissipation low-dispersion schemes with shock sensor](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md) — The LD2 scheme pairs an energy-preserving central flux with much-reduced matrix dissipation, LD2C adds a Ducros-weighted shock sensor; the page also compares an optimized sixth-order upwind reconstruction with a central scheme for energy budgets, and records stencils, filters and reconstruction weights optimized by spectral objectives.
* [Machine-learning wall models](/methods/machine-learning-wall-model.md) — Wall-stress models for wall-modelled LES learned by supervised regression, classification over building-block flows or multi-agent reinforcement learning: the published models, a posteriori behaviour from channel flow up to Re_tau = 10^10 to aircraft configurations, solver-consistent training data, the extrapolation argument, log-law preservation as the acceptance test, and cost.
* [Mesh adaptation for turbulent-flow simulation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md) — Changing the mesh (h, p or hp; isotropic or anisotropic) from an error estimate or refinement criterion in a turbulent-flow computation: Hessian and metric criteria for RANS, Hessians scaled by DG error estimators, time-averaged criteria for hybrid RANS/LES, LES modelling-residual and truncation-error estimators, error transport equations, and refinement as a dissipation control.
* [Method of manufactured solutions](/methods/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md) — The method of manufactured solutions verifies a flow solver by choosing an analytic solution, adding the residual it leaves in the governing equations as a source term, and measuring the observed order of accuracy; this page gives the manufactured solutions used for Euler, RANS and GPU DG codes and what they verify.
* [Model-consistent (a posteriori) training of learned closures](/methods/model-consistent-training.md) — Training a learned closure with the solver in the loop, so the loss is evaluated on the model's own solution: field inversion, FIML, adjoint-based integrated training, differentiable-solver unrolling and reinforcement learning, contrasted with open-loop injection of a priori-trained corrections, with consistency arguments, costs and limits.
* [Multi-resolution independent simulations](/methods/multi-resolution-independent-simulations.md) — MRIS obtains dissipation-range DNS statistics at resolutions too costly for long runs: a long coarse run is refined in time, then in space, for a few Kolmogorov times from several independent snapshots, and ensemble averaged; Yeung et al. (2025) extend it to raise the Reynolds number by viscosity reduction.
* [Neural-network subgrid-scale models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md) — LES closures in which a neural network maps resolved-field features to the subgrid stress, its divergence, a model coefficient or an energy-conserving operator; trained a priori, by reinforcement learning or through the solver. Covers inputs, multi-filter fusion, normalization, invariance, structure preservation, cost against dynamic models and backscatter.
* [Neural-operator surrogates of large-eddy simulation](/methods/neural-operator-les-surrogate.md) — A neural operator (Fourier, implicit Fourier or transformer-based) learns the time advance of the filtered velocity field on the LES grid and replaces the whole LES solver in an autoregressive rollout; trained on filtered DNS or, physics-informed, on the residual of the Smagorinsky-closed filtered equations, and compared with dynamic and static Smagorinsky LES.
* [Partially-averaged Navier–Stokes](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md) — Girimaji's bridging hybrid model that solves RANS-form transport equations for the unresolved fractions of kinetic energy and dissipation; its SST form on high-order flux reconstruction and with a dynamic resolution parameter for transonic buffet, its variable-density BHR form, the choice of resolution parameters, and the criticism of fluctuating inputs.
* [Physics-informed neural networks for RANS](/methods/physics-informed-neural-network.md) — A neural network maps the coordinates to the mean velocity, pressure and Reynolds stresses and is trained on the RANS residuals plus boundary data; the RANS system is closed by data rather than by a turbulence model, with errors reported for boundary layers, an airfoil and the periodic hill.
* [Reinforcement-learning turbulence closures](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md) — Closures set or learned online by a policy trained with reinforcement learning, the running LES being the environment and the reward a solver statistic: element-local SGS coefficients in DG-LES, pointwise SGS stress tensors for channel LES trained on mean profiles, multi-agent wall-stress models with disputed extrapolation and Reynolds-number-independent states; RL also drives offline symbolic-expression search.
* [Residual-based dynamic subgrid-scale model](/methods/residual-based-dynamic-sgs-model.md) — The residual-based dynamic SGS model sets the eddy viscosity from the residual of the inviscid filtered equations, capped by a first-order-upwind bound. One source compares its residual and time-derivative-free variants, element-constant and nodal, with Smagorinsky and Vreman in a continuous spectral-element code on tracer, Burgers, Taylor–Green and density-current tests.
* [STITCH helicity-condensation model](/methods/stitch-helicity-condensation-model.md) — STITCH replaces explicitly resolved photospheric vortical driving in coronal MHD simulations by one source term in the induction equation that injects condensed magnetic helicity at the base; it is divergence-free, leaves the normal field unchanged, and cuts the cost of energy buildup about fifteenfold.
* [Scale-adaptive simulation](/methods/scale-adaptive-simulation.md) — Menter's scale-adaptive simulation: an unsteady RANS approach whose length-scale equation carries the von Kármán length, so that the eddy viscosity falls in resolved unsteady regions; the k-√kL model as printed, its first high-order discontinuous Galerkin implementation, and comparisons with DES-type hybrids reported by other sources.
* [Scale-similarity and mixed subgrid-scale models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) — Scale-similarity and deconvolution models build the subgrid stress, or a subgrid scalar variance, from the resolved field rather than from an eddy viscosity; the sources agree that they reproduce the stress structure but are under-dissipative or unstable alone, and mix them with an eddy viscosity, add numerical dissipation, or derive an eddy viscosity from their flux.
* [Sigma model](/methods/sigma-model.md) — The Sigma model of Nicoud et al. builds a static subgrid eddy viscosity from the singular values of the resolved velocity gradient; the sources use it with constants 1.3–1.35 in jet-noise, spectral-element and finite-volume wall-modelled and learned-closure LES, where it is over-dissipative in coarse isotropic turbulence but accurate in channel spectra.
* [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) — The Smagorinsky model closes the deviatoric subgrid stress with an eddy viscosity (C_s Δ)²|S̄|; the sources report its constants, filter-width definitions in element methods and on high-aspect-ratio cells, its interaction with numerical error, and its a priori and a posteriori behaviour.
* [Spalart–Allmaras model and its corrections](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) — The one-equation eddy-viscosity RANS model transporting the working variable ν̃: its printed forms and constants, negative, rotation–curvature, helicity and quadratic-constitutive-relation variants, positivity and entropy stability at high order, data-driven corrections, its role as host of detached-eddy simulation, and evidence from turbomachinery and airfoils.
* [Spectral difference method](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md) — The spectral difference method is a nodal high-order element scheme that interpolates the solution on N+1 points and the flux on N+2 points per direction, made continuous by Riemann fluxes and differentiated at the solution points; this page covers its formulation, its place in the flux-reconstruction family, and its DNS and shock-capturing uses.
* [Spectral eddy viscosity](/methods/spectral-eddy-viscosity.md) — Kraichnan's spectral eddy viscosity expresses the energy transfer across a cutoff as a wavenumber-dependent viscosity with a low-wavenumber plateau and a cusp near the cutoff; the sources use it to diagnose explicit and implicit LES, to build a modal plateau–cusp model for DG, and to compute it from the LES field itself.
* [Spectral vanishing viscosity](/methods/spectral-vanishing-viscosity.md) — Spectral vanishing viscosity concentrates an added viscosity on the highest resolved scales. The sources use an implicit finite-difference form, an explicit modal filter on a DG artificial flux made entropy stable, and a modal SVV in continuous spectral/hp elements; all serve as LES closures, and one source measures how little of the dissipation the SVV term itself supplies.
* [Subcell finite-volume shock capturing for DG](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md) — Troubled DG elements are recomputed as (N+1)^3 finite-volume subcells with the same degrees of freedom, switched by an indicator or blended convexly with the DG operator (the blend also learned as an implicit subgrid model); indicator-switched modal and entropy filters are the alternative that keeps the polynomial.
* [Subgrid turbulent dynamo model](/methods/subgrid-turbulent-dynamo-model.md) — A subgrid closure of the induction equation for cosmological MHD: an isotropic mean-field alpha effect whose coefficient is the subgrid turbulent velocity dispersion of a one-equation SGS kinetic-energy model, quenched when the magnetic energy reaches a tunable fraction q of the subgrid turbulent energy.
* [Super-resolution subgrid-scale models](/methods/super-resolution-sgs-model.md) — An LES closure that reconstructs a fine-grid velocity field from the coarse LES field with a neural network and computes the subgrid stress by filtering products of the reconstructed sub-cell fluctuations; for very coarse wall-bounded LES an unsupervised CycleGAN first maps the LES field to filtered-DNS quality, with a clipping that keeps moderate backscatter.
* [Symbolic-regression turbulence closures](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md) — Closures whose model terms are explicit algebraic expressions found by a search over expression space (gene-expression programming, sparse and sparse Bayesian regression, deep symbolic regression, PySR), as integrity-basis coefficients, a scalar multiplier of a model term or an LES subgrid stress; covers learned k-ε and SST corrections and the higher-order gradient model.
* [Tensor-basis neural network and the integrity basis](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) — A neural network that predicts the scalar coefficients of Pope's ten-tensor integrity basis from invariants of the mean strain and rotation, so the learned Reynolds-stress anisotropy is objective by construction; other regressors learn the same expansion, larger bases supply invariant features, and an eight-term basis carries the idea to LES subgrid stresses.
* [The k-τ model](/methods/k-tau-model.md) — Two-equation RANS eddy-viscosity model transporting the turbulent kinetic energy and the turbulence time scale 1/ω, whose wall value is zero and whose sources stay bounded; the spectral-element implementation of Tomboulides et al. with its source limiters, low-Reynolds variant, implicit source treatment and validation.
* [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md) — Vreman's static eddy-viscosity model builds the subgrid viscosity from an invariant of the resolved velocity-gradient tensor that vanishes in many laminar flows; the sources print it in two index orders with constants from 0.08 to 0.17, use it in aircraft, channel, lattice Boltzmann, DG and shock-interaction LES, and recommend it with a recalibrated filter width for high-order wall-modelled LES.
* [WALE model](/methods/wale-model.md) — The wall-adapting local eddy-viscosity (WALE) model builds a static subgrid eddy viscosity from an invariant of the squared resolved velocity gradient that vanishes in pure shear; the sources print its formula with constants 0.325 and 0.5, find its dissipation small beside numerical dissipation, and find it the least dissipative of three models in high-order LES.
* [WENO and hybrid shock-capturing schemes in scale-resolving simulation](/methods/weno-scheme.md) — WENO and TENO schemes capture shocks by nonlinear weighting or selection of candidate stencils; in compressible turbulence their dissipation is reduced by optimized weights, adaptive cut-offs, linear weights in smooth flow or hybrids with central and jump reconstructions, they serve as DG limiters, and in implicit LES as the subgrid dissipation.
* [Wall functions for RANS](/methods/rans-wall-function.md) — The near-wall closure of RANS that imposes a law of the wall at the first grid points instead of resolving the viscous sublayer: wall-stress inversion, turbulence-variable boundary values, an explicit power-law wall function for high-order DG, its accuracy and cost up to a first-element height of 320 wall units, and its failure in strong adverse pressure gradient and recirculation.
* [Wall-modelled large-eddy simulation](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) — LES that resolves the outer-layer eddies and replaces the near-wall viscous region by a wall-stress model: its definition and cost advantage, the grid-dependent error scaling of mean quantities, the dominance of SGS-model errors, non-monotonic grid convergence, the insufficiency of wall-mesh criteria, grid adaptation, immersed-boundary coupling and wall-slip models, and demonstrations on aircraft, fan stages and separated flows.
* [Wilcox k-ω model](/methods/k-omega-model.md) — Wilcox's two-equation k-ω eddy-viscosity model and its unblended variants (1998, 2006, Kok's TNT, low-Reynolds damping), with constants, the near-wall singularity of ω and its regularized and k-τ remedies, the log-ω form, and the obstacles of solving it with high-order element discretizations.
* [k-ε models](/methods/k-epsilon-model.md) — Two-equation RANS eddy-viscosity models transporting the turbulent kinetic energy and its dissipation rate: the standard high-Reynolds form and its constants, the realizable variant with a two-layer wall treatment, low-Reynolds and elliptic-relaxation relatives, and evidence on bluff-body wakes, periodic hills and learned non-linear corrections.
* [k-ω SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) — Menter's two-equation shear-stress-transport eddy-viscosity model, blending a k-ω inner formulation with an outer one through F1 and limiting the eddy viscosity through F2; its printed variants, log-ω, transition, PANS, DES and compressibility-corrected forms, high-order discretization, data-driven corrections, and evidence on airfoils, cascades, cars and separated flows.
