# Shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction

A shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction (STBLI) arises when a
[turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) at
supersonic or hypersonic free-stream Mach number meets a shock, either
generated by the wall itself (a **compression ramp** of angle $\phi_r$,
page-local) or by an external body (an **impinging oblique shock**). A
strong enough interaction separates the layer: in the ramps of
[Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md) the Mach 7, 8° case stays
attached while the 24°, 33° and 34° cases separate, with a separation shock
forming inside the incoming layer ahead of the bubble, a separation shear
layer over it, and reattachment on the ramp (§III C–D, §IV B, Fig. 17). The quantities a simulation must predict are the mean
separation length $L_{\mathrm{sep}}$ (page-local: the distance between the
zero crossings of the mean $C_f$), the wall pressure, skin friction and heat
transfer through the interaction, and the low-frequency unsteadiness of the
separation shock, measured by the Strouhal number $St_L=fL_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$
(page-local, $U_e$ the boundary-layer-edge velocity). The case combines the
compressible boundary layer, a separated shear layer and shock motion, and is
expensive because the low-frequency cycle needs long records.

Both sources run a Mach 2.9, 24° ramp: Dang et al. validate against the DNS
of Martín et al. and the experiment of Bookey et al. (§5.1), and Helm &
Martín's R24-M3 DNS is that of Priebe & Martín (their ref. [35]). The
printed incoming conditions are close (edge temperature 108.9 K in Helm &
Martín, Table II; free-stream 108.1 K in Dang et al., Table 8), but neither
paper states that the two set-ups are identical. Helm & Martín's attached
Mach 7 case reproduces, through the DNS of Priebe & Martín, the conditions
of the Bookey et al. experiment (§I).

## Methods the sources use

**LES with a dynamic mixed model.** [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md),
§II, solve the Favre-filtered compressible equations in curvilinear
coordinates with the one-coefficient
[dynamic mixed model](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) of Martín, Piomelli
& Candler for the subgrid stress and heat flux (a dynamic eddy-viscosity term
plus a scale-similar term built with a top-hat test filter, the coefficients
and the subgrid Prandtl number computed by the
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) with spanwise averaging),
Knight et al.'s model for the subgrid turbulent diffusion, and the subgrid
viscous diffusion neglected. Inviscid fluxes use a fourth-order
bandwidth-optimized [WENO scheme](/methods/weno-scheme.md) with absolute and
relative limiters and near-wall filtering of the candidate weights, to reduce
its [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md); viscous
terms are fourth-order central, time stepping low-storage third-order
Runge–Kutta. The filter width is the cube root of the product of the
computational grid spacings. Inflow comes from an auxiliary boundary layer
with recycling–rescaling over a long box, interpolated onto the ramp inlet,
so that the incoming state stays fixed over the long records the
low-frequency cycle needs (§II, Fig. 1).

**DNS on GPUs.** [Dang et al. (2022)](/sources/dang-2022.md) run the
Mach 2.9 ramp with [OpenCFD-SCU](/software/opencfd-scu.md): Steger–Warming
flux-vector splitting, the seventh-order bandwidth-optimized WENO-SYMBO of
Martín et al. for convection (the scheme of the reference DNS), eighth-order
central viscous terms, and turbulence generated by blowing and suction on
the upstream flat plate rather than by recycling (§5.1, §6.1). The wide-span
grid has $\Delta x^+=1.2$, $\Delta y^+=0.41$, $\Delta z^+=1.2$ at the
reference station 35 mm upstream of the corner, "more than 6000 times the
number [of points] used by Martín et al." (§6.1).

**Wall-modelled LES.** [Hoy & Bermejo-Moreno (2022)](/sources/hoy-2022.md)
use the [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md), the
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) of Kawai &
Larsson matched at $0.1\delta_0$ with four cells below it, and digital-filter
inflow; the run details are on
[turbulent boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).

## What the case tests

**Agreement of LES with DNS at a fraction of the cost.**
[Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md), §III, validate the LES on
grids coarser than the DNS by about $4\times4\times2$ (32 times fewer points)
with a time step about 2.8 times larger. On the incoming boundary layers at
Mach 3, 7 and 10 the mean velocity and temperature are within 3% (5% at
Mach 10) and the peak turbulent kinetic energy within 2% of the top-hat
filtered DNS; the unresolved kinetic energy, estimated from filtered against
unfiltered DNS, is 14% to 25% at Mach 7 and 10% to 15% at Mach 3 and 10
(Figs. 2–5). On the separated Mach 3 ramp the LES reproduces the
separation length, the multi-inflection skin friction inside the bubble, the
wall-pressure plateau that excess numerical dissipation suppresses, peak
shear-layer intensities within 6%, and separation spectra peaking at
$St\approx0.01$–0.03 as in the DNS (Figs. 8–10). On the attached Mach 7 ramp
the skin friction differs from DNS by about 3% on the ramp, the heat-transfer
coefficient by 4%, and the peak reverse-flow probability at the corner is 31%
in both (Figs. 12–13). The authors state the LES costs "3% of the DNS
computational cost" (§V).

**The span of the domain.** [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md), §III C, Table III,
Fig. 8, report for the Mach 3 ramp a separation length of $2.93\delta$ in
the narrow-span DNS, $3.05\delta$ in the narrow-span LES ($2\delta_o$ span,
$\delta_o$ the reference thickness) and $3.71\delta$ in the wide-span LES
($4\delta_o$); they consider the effect physical (spanwise periodicity on the
order of $\delta$), note that many STBLI simulations use spans of about two
boundary-layer thicknesses, and state that the effect "has not been closely
addressed in the literature". The distributions collapse when plotted
against $x'/L_{\mathrm{sep}}$ ($x'$ the distance along the wall from the
corner). [Dang et al. (2022)](/sources/dang-2022.md), §6.1, §7, Figs. 23–25,
do not compare spans but run the Mach 2.9 ramp with a span of more than 29 boundary-layer
thicknesses and see a series of Taylor–Görtler vortices behind the shock,
with several small co-rotating vortices beneath one large vortex and
streak spacings that grow strongly in the interaction; this run is shown by
visualizations only, with no statistics.

**Hypersonic separated ramps.** For the two new LES datasets
[Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md), §IV, Table IV, report:

- mean separation lengths $6.2\delta$ (Mach 7, averaged over
  $3150\,\delta/U_e=510\,L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$) and $4.1\delta$ (Mach 9.1,
  $450\,\delta/U_e=110\,L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$);
- separation spectra peaking at $St_L=0.1$ with energy down to $St_L=0.03$,
  reattachment spectra broadband about $St_L\approx1$ (Fig. 15);
- maximum reverse flow about 12% of $U_e$; peak mean temperature in the
  bubble $8.7T_e$ and $12.5T_e$, 50% and 40% above the inviscid post-shock
  values (Fig. 21);
- peak $C_f$ 9.3 and 11.3 times the incoming value, the rms of $C_f$ up to
  about 20 times upstream, peak Stanton number 15.2 and 16.8 times the
  incoming value (Fig. 23);
- streamwise intensity in the separation shear layer twice the upstream
  maximum, anisotropy invariants moving at the corner toward the
  axisymmetric-compression branch typical of mixing layers, and a separation
  shear layer whose extra production is balanced by transport, convection,
  pressure diffusion and a dissipation dominated by its subgrid part
  (Figs. 24–27).

A record of $100\,L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$, about three low-frequency cycles at
$St_L=0.03$, converges the separated mean flow to within 3%: ten overlapping
segments of the Mach 7 signal give mean separation positions within 3% of
the full average (§IV A, Fig. 16).

**Sensitivity to the subgrid model.** Removing the scale-similar terms
(dynamic eddy viscosity only) changes the Mach 3 separation length by less
than 5% and the attached Mach 7 corner reverse-flow probability from 31% to
35%, but lengthens the separated Mach 7 bubble by 30% (§IV F, Fig. 30). The
difference is not in the incoming layer, whose profiles are identical
(Fig. 31), but in the separation shear layer, whose spreading rate falls from
0.194 to 0.173 without the similarity term; less spreading means less
entrainment and a larger bubble (Figs. 32–33). The authors attribute the
sensitivity to the convective Mach number of about 2 of that shear layer
(about 1 in the Mach 3 case, from their earlier work) and conclude that the
conservative energy exchange of the scale-similar term is necessary there
(§V). The sensitivity is shown for one separated hypersonic case, with one
numerical scheme. See [scale-similarity and mixed models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md);
the paper speaks of conservative energy exchange and does not use the term
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md).

**Engineering assumptions.** [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md),
§IV E, Figs. 28–29, test the relations RANS and design practice rely on in
the separated hypersonic flows on cold walls: the Reynolds analogy factor
$2C_h/C_f$ is not near one in the separated region ($C_f/2C_h$ is 0.85
upstream and about 0.7 downstream); the Back & Cuffel power law
$q/q_u=(p/p_u)^{0.85}$, derived for attached interactions, over-predicts the
heat transfer at separation and in the recovering layer; $Pr_t$ is within
13% of one upstream between 0.05 and 0.75 local boundary-layer thicknesses
from the wall, but about 1.4 downstream at Mach 9.1; the strong Reynolds analogies fail on these cold walls and the
modified analogy of Huang et al. holds only in parts of the layers. See
[Morkovin's hypothesis](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md).

**Beyond RANS.** Two sources agree that RANS is not adequate for separated
interactions: [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md), §I, call RANS
"notoriously unreliable" for separated STBLIs, with large errors in
separation length and wall heat transfer, and
[Hoy & Bermejo-Moreno (2022)](/sources/hoy-2022.md), §1, state that RANS
cannot predict the strong separation and low-frequency dynamics, while DNS
and wall-resolved LES are too costly at experimental Reynolds numbers.
[Dang et al. (2022)](/sources/dang-2022.md), §1, name the two obstacles of
DNS of such flows as the conflict between robustness at shocks and low
dissipation, and the cost of long domains, small time steps and the long
records of low-frequency shock motion.

**Fluid–structure coupling.** In the impinging-shock case of
[Hoy & Bermejo-Moreno (2022)](/sources/hoy-2022.md), §4.3, panel flexibility
moves separation about $3\delta_0$ upstream and lengthens it from
$7.8\delta_0$ to $12.6\delta_0$, lowers the peak wall pressure and shifts the
dominant frequencies down.

## Qualifications

- Coordinates: [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md) write $z$
  wall-normal and $y$ spanwise, hat for the Favre filter and a second hat for
  the test filter; this page uses $y$ wall-normal, and the subgrid model is
  described in words. They define $u_\tau=(\tau_w/\rho_e)^{1/2}$ with the
  edge density, where the registry uses the wall density, and a recovery
  factor 0.9 in $T_r$ (the sources cited for $T_r$ on
  [turbulent boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) use
  0.89).
- [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md) give the increase of the
  Mach 7 separation length without the scale-similar term as "as much as a
  32%" in the abstract and as 30% in §IV F and §V; their abstract gives the
  LES time step as three times the DNS one, §V as 2.8 times. Their
  Table III prints the wide-span DNS row (R24-M3w-D) identical in every
  column to the narrow-span row, although the text says the two separation
  lengths differ, and several figure captions misname the cases (e.g.
  "R24-M10-L", "R35-M10-L" for R34-M10-L).
- The Mach 7 and Mach 9.1 separated ramps of
  [Helm & Martín (2022)](/sources/helm-2022.md) have no DNS or experimental
  reference; they are validated only through the Mach 3 and attached Mach 7
  cases. §V calls the 32-fold reduction in grid points "equivalent to 3%
  of the DNS computational cost" and discusses the larger time step and the
  model overhead separately. §III B prints the unresolved energy at the wall
  of the Mach 7 layer both as 25% and as "a maximum of 18%".
- [Dang et al. (2022)](/sources/dang-2022.md) were read in an author
  manuscript. Their validation (mean wall pressure, van Driest velocity
  profile against the DNS of Martín et al. and the experiment of Bookey et
  al.) is on the $16100\times240\times200$ grid; the wide-span run has no
  quantitative comparison, and its averaging time is not stated.
