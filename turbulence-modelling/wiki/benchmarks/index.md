# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [ACAT1 fan stage](/benchmarks/acat1-fan-stage.md) — The ACAT1 fan and outlet-guide-vane stage of the TurboNoiseBB project, run at approach (tip relative Mach 0.57) with hot-wire wake data between rotor and stator: a test of zonal DES and of wall-modelled LES on 95 to 380 million cells for the rotor wakes that drive broadband interaction noise.
* [Ahmed body](/benchmarks/ahmed-body.md) — The Ahmed body, a bluff car-like body with a slanted rear, whose slant angle sets the separation regime: at 35° (massive separation) a test of immersed-boundary wall-modelled LES on lattice Boltzmann grids, at 25° (separation and reattachment) the calibration case of a DDES procedure later applied to a full-scale estate car.
* [Backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md) — Turbulent flow over a sudden expansion of a channel, with fixed separation at the step edge, a free shear layer and reattachment downstream; here the reattachment length as a measure of filter-width choices in wall-resolved LES at Re_H about 5700 and of adaptive meshing for IDDES at Re_H 28,000, and an out-of-geometry test of a learned RANS closure at 5000.
* [Homogeneous isotropic turbulence (decaying and forced)](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md) — Turbulence in a triply periodic box, decaying from a prescribed spectrum or sustained by forcing: a priori database and a posteriori test of subgrid closures, learned ones included (Re_λ 70 to 418, LES of 24³ to 144³ degrees of freedom), a dissipation test of schemes for hybrid RANS/LES, DNS up to supersonic turbulent Mach numbers, and driven MHD turbulence.
* [Imperial Front Wing](/benchmarks/imperial-front-wing.md) — The Imperial Front Wing, a three-element inverted race-car wing in ground effect proposed as an open motorsport benchmark; a spanwise-periodic slice at Re 220,000 and ride height 0.36 chords, computed by stabilized spectral/hp implicit LES, shows laminar separation bubbles and wake-induced transition that lower-fidelity models must reproduce.
* [JAXA Standard Model high-lift configuration](/benchmarks/jaxa-standard-model.md) — The JAXA Standard Model, a half-span civil transport in landing configuration from the Third AIAA High-Lift Prediction Workshop at chord Reynolds number 1.96 million and Mach 0.172, on which wall-modelled LES predicts maximum lift within five lift counts on grids of 32 to 185 million cells.
* [NASA Juncture Flow](/benchmarks/nasa-juncture-flow.md) — The NASA Juncture Flow wing–fuselage model, with smooth-body corner separation at the wing-root trailing edge (crank-chord Reynolds number 2.4 million, Mach 0.189, 5°): wall-modelled LES predicts wall pressure within 5% but mean velocity only within 3–8% on the fuselage and about 100% in the corner, which a learned wall model does not fix.
* [Periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) — The streamwise-periodic channel with a hill constriction, a smooth-wall separation and reattachment test: at hill-height Reynolds number about 10,600 for hybrid RANS/LES and implicit LES on coarse grids, as the parametrized hill family at 5600 for learned RANS corrections, and at 2800 for data assimilation, physics-informed networks and high-order DG implicit LES.
* [Rayleigh–Taylor mixing](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-mixing.md) — Buoyancy-driven mixing of a heavy fluid placed above a light one: a transient flow that passes from linear laminar growth through bubbles, spikes and Kelvin–Helmholtz transition to turbulent mixing, here at Atwood number 0.5 as a test that RANS fails and partially-averaged Navier–Stokes recovers at a fraction of the resolution.
* [Shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md) — A supersonic or hypersonic turbulent boundary layer meeting a shock from a compression ramp or an impinging oblique shock: separation length, low-frequency unsteadiness and wall heating test the subgrid model, the span of the domain and the DNS cost, here from Mach 2.9 to 9.1 by LES, DNS and wall-modelled LES, with a RANS compressibility-correction test at Mach 2.92.
* [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) — The triply periodic vortex flow that transitions from a single-mode analytic field to decaying turbulence: at Re 40,000 against a 5400³ DNS to separate Smagorinsky and implicit LES errors, at Re 1600 to split numerical dissipation and test subgrid models and stabilizations, inviscid to tune spectral vanishing viscosity, in 2D for commutator errors, and for GPU timing and parallel scaling.
* [Turbulent boundary layers: zero and adverse pressure gradient, compressible](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) — Spatially developing turbulent boundary layers on flat walls: zero- and adverse-pressure-gradient layers as wall-model, log-law and physics-informed-network tests, supersonic and hypersonic layers on cooled walls as DNS databases for compressibility transformations and as LES validation, and the incoming layer of shock interactions.
* [Turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) — Pressure-driven flow between two parallel walls, periodic in the wall-parallel directions: here DNS at Re_τ 100 to 395 (gas-kinetic, GPU, real-gas), wall-resolved and implicit LES at 180 to 2000 (learned subgrid models, grid adaptation), a priori data at 395 to 2000, wall-modelled LES at Re_τ 1000 to 10¹⁰, hybrid RANS/LES to 20,000, and compressible channels to bulk Mach 4.
