# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [Backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md) — Turbulent flow over a sudden expansion of a channel, with fixed separation at the step edge, a free shear layer and reattachment downstream; here the reattachment length as a measure of filter-width choices in wall-resolved LES at Re_H about 5700 and of adaptive meshing for IDDES at Re_H 28,000.
* [Homogeneous isotropic turbulence (decaying and forced)](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md) — Turbulence in a triply periodic box, decaying from a prescribed spectrum or sustained by forcing, used as a priori database and a posteriori test of subgrid-scale closures, as a DNS test up to supersonic turbulent Mach numbers, and as driven MHD turbulence for dynamo studies: here Re_λ from 70 to 418, LES of 24³ to 144³ degrees of freedom.
* [JAXA Standard Model high-lift configuration](/benchmarks/jaxa-standard-model.md) — The JAXA Standard Model, a half-span civil transport in landing configuration from the Third AIAA High-Lift Prediction Workshop at chord Reynolds number 1.96 million and Mach 0.172, on which wall-modelled LES predicts maximum lift within five lift counts on grids of 32 to 185 million cells.
* [NASA Juncture Flow](/benchmarks/nasa-juncture-flow.md) — The NASA Juncture Flow wing–fuselage model, with smooth-body corner separation at the wing-root trailing edge at crank-chord Reynolds number 2.4 million, Mach 0.189 and 5° incidence, on which wall-modelled LES predicts wall pressure within 5% but mean velocity only within 3–8% on the fuselage and about 100% in the separated corner.
* [Periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) — The streamwise-periodic channel with a hill constriction, a smooth-wall separation and reattachment test: at hill-height Reynolds number about 10,600 for hybrid RANS/LES and implicit LES on grids 14 to 60 times smaller than wall-resolved LES, and as the parametrized hill family at 5600 for field-inversion RANS corrections.
* [Rayleigh–Taylor mixing](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-mixing.md) — Buoyancy-driven mixing of a heavy fluid placed above a light one: a transient flow that passes from linear laminar growth through bubbles, spikes and Kelvin–Helmholtz transition to turbulent mixing, here at Atwood number 0.5 as a test that RANS fails and partially-averaged Navier–Stokes recovers at a fraction of the resolution.
* [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) — The triply periodic vortex flow started from a single-mode analytic field that transitions to turbulence and decays: at Re 40,000 against a 5400³ DNS to separate Smagorinsky and implicit LES errors, at Re 1600 to measure numerical dissipation and test subgrid models, inviscid to tune spectral vanishing viscosity, and in 2D to compare commutator errors.
* [Turbulent boundary layers: zero and adverse pressure gradient, compressible](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) — Spatially developing turbulent boundary layers on flat walls: the zero-pressure-gradient layer as a wall-model generalization test, adverse-pressure-gradient layers for the extent of the log law, supersonic and hypersonic layers on cooled walls as DNS databases for compressibility transformations, and the Mach 3 shock interaction for WMLES.
* [Turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) — Pressure-driven flow between two parallel walls, periodic in the wall-parallel directions, the canonical wall-bounded test: here DNS at Re_τ 180, wall-resolved LES at 590, a priori data at 395 to 2000, wall-modelled LES at Re_τ 1000 to 10¹⁰, hybrid RANS/LES at 5200 and 20,000, and compressible channels to bulk Mach 4.
