# Results

Theorems, proved properties and barriers.

* [Compressible MHD turbulence](/results/compressible-mhd-turbulence.md) — Compressible MHD turbulence carries Alfvénic, slow, fast and non-wave fluctuations: most power lies off the dispersion branches, subsonic Hall-MHD cascades depend little on Mach number, trans-sonic compressive modes need bulk viscosity, a flare simulation shows Kelvin–Helmholtz-driven Alfvénic turbulence, and a nearly incompressible expansion yields a moment-transport closure for solar-wind turbulence.
* [Cross-scale interaction in plasma turbulence](/results/cross-scale-interaction.md) — In magnetized fusion plasmas, ion-scale and electron-scale micro-turbulence interact across scales; multi-scale gyrokinetic simulation at electron-to-ion temperature ratio 3 shows mutual suppression of trapped-electron and electron-temperature-gradient turbulence, reducing the electron heat flux from 33.63 to 5.66 gyro-Bohm units against an ion-scale-only simulation.
* [Decaying MHD turbulence and its invariants](/results/decaying-mhd-turbulence.md) — Decaying MHD turbulence is a selective decay whose power laws follow from the conserved quantity that constrains it and from a reconnection-controlled decay time (Schekochihin 2022, §12); decaying runs also serve as code tests and as probes of the Hall effect, as in the Orszag–Tang decay with and without the Hall term.
* [Exact laws of (Hall-)MHD turbulence](/results/turbulence-exact-laws.md) — Exact laws relate the energy cascade rate of MHD turbulence to two-point increments under statistical homogeneity; the page gives the reduced-MHD third-order law and the compressible Hall-MHD laws, their general non-stationary form, and DNS evidence that two such laws agree and survive decaying turbulence.
* [Grid-point requirements of LES and DNS](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md) — How the grid-point and time-step counts of DNS, wall-resolved and wall-modelled LES scale with Reynolds number, the explicit WMLES cost model, wall-unit and per-thickness resolution criteria and evidence that meeting them is not sufficient, the resolutions used in practice and found by adaptive grids, measured costs, and why WMLES cost exponents differ.
* [Shock-generated turbulence and turbulent shock jump relations](/results/shock-generated-turbulence.md) — A strong shock crossing a medium with order-one density fluctuations generates turbulence whose velocity dispersion scales with shock speed times a pre-shock Atwood number and whose Reynolds stress lowers post-shock pressure and density; conversely, shocks form inside supersonic turbulence and the supersonic Taylor–Green vortex, where they test shock capturing.
* [Small-scale turbulent dynamo](/results/small-scale-dynamo.md) — The small-scale (fluctuation) dynamo amplifies magnetic fields by turbulent stretching without a mean field; the page gathers its kinematic and saturated stages, Schekochihin's (2022) tearing-limited saturation theory, and simulation evidence on growth rates, saturation levels, resolution, multiphase gas, and the stretching, winding and compression split of the growth rate under explicit bulk viscosity.
* [Tearing-mediated MHD turbulence](/results/tearing-mediated-mhd-turbulence.md) — Below a disruption scale proportional to Rm^-4/7, aligned sheets of MHD turbulence are torn faster than they cascade; the resulting recursive, tearing-mediated cascade has a k_perp^-11/5 envelope and ends at a Kolmogorov-scaled dissipation cutoff (Schekochihin 2022, after Mallet et al. and Loureiro & Boldyrev).
* [Weak wave turbulence (magneto-capillary waves)](/results/weak-wave-turbulence.md) — Weak wave turbulence describes cascades of weakly nonlinear waves through resonant interactions, with Kolmogorov–Zakharov spectra scaling as the square root of the flux for three-wave systems; the page covers weak Alfvén-wave turbulence and its critique, and its magneto-capillary analogue on a ferrofluid surface.
