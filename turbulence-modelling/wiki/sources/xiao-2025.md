# Summary

Xiao et al. (2025) present [CaLES](/software/cales.md), an open-source
GPU solver for LES of incompressible wall-bounded flow built on the DNS
code CaNS 2.3.0. The numerics are CaNS's: fractional-step pressure
correction, Wray's low-storage third-order Runge–Kutta scheme,
second-order central differences on a staggered grid that conserve
kinetic energy discretely in the inviscid limit
([kinetic-energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)),
and a direct FFT/tridiagonal Poisson solver; viscous terms may be
explicit, implicit in all directions or implicit in $y$ only. CaLES adds
the static [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) with van
Driest damping, the Germano–Lilly
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) (planar averaging,
clipping at zero, box test filters with a 2D filter in the first
off-wall layer) and an algebraic log-law
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) fed by the
instantaneous velocity. GPU porting uses OpenACC, CUDA Fortran, cuFFT and
the cuDecomp pencil library; one A100 runs 12–19 times faster than a
32-core CPU node. The motivation is the many fast LES runs that
[model-consistent](/methods/model-consistent-training.md) and
reinforcement-learning training of closures need, and the
[a priori against a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)
gap of closures trained on filtered DNS. Among the open-source solvers
compared, [LESGO](/software/lesgo.md) supports LES without GPUs and
[STREAmS](/software/streams.md) is a compressible GPU DNS code.

Validation: decaying [isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
against Comte-Bellot and Corrsin; wall-resolved
[channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) LES at $Re_\tau\approx544$,
where the static model over-predicts $C_f$ by 9.4% on the coarsest grid
and the dynamic one stays within about 3%; and
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) of channel flow at
$Re_\tau\approx5200$ on 13 grids per aspect ratio (up to $21.7\times10^9$
points, reaching wall-resolved spacing; see
[grid-point requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)) and
of a square duct at $Re_\tau\approx1055$. The main finding is that WMLES
grid convergence is non-monotonic in $C_f$ and the Reynolds stresses for
both subgrid models and both aspect ratios, with a positive $C_f$ error on
the finest grids attributed to the instantaneous wall-model input. The
[law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) is argued to hold as a
wall model beyond equilibrium flows. The [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md)
is named as a model the code can host. The authors state the paper's
purpose is the platform, not an assessment of the models
([LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) remains far cheaper than
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) at these Reynolds
numbers).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $AR=\Delta x/\Delta z$ the grid aspect ratio;
$K_{\mathrm{sf}}$ the secondary-flow kinetic energy of the duct (Eq. 25);
$e_{C_f}=(C_f-C_{f,\mathrm{DNS}})/C_{f,\mathrm{DNS}}$ (Eq. 23) and
$e_\phi$ the outer-layer profile error of Eq. (24), in the registry's
$e_q$ family; $Re_b=2u_b\delta/\nu$ on the full channel height or duct
side.

1. CaLES is a GPU-accelerated incompressible LES solver for wall-bounded flows built on CaNS 2.3.0; of the open-source solvers in Table 1 (STREAmS-2, URANOS, AFiD, LESGO, CaNS, CaLES) only URANOS and LESGO already support LES, and LESGO has no GPU support (§1, Table 1). **Stated**
2. Incompressible solvers such as LESGO are "typically more efficient" than compressible ones such as URANOS for low-Mach flows, mainly because explicit time integration then allows much larger time steps (§1). **Stated**
3. Earlier CaNS benchmarks: 4 V100 GPUs of a DGX-2 are roughly equivalent (0.9 times slower to 1.6 times faster) to 2048 CPU cores, and 3.1 to 5.6 times faster with all 16 GPUs [22] (§1). **Stated**
4. Subgrid models trained on filtered DNS may perform well a priori but fail a posteriori because the subgrid stress of implicitly filtered LES differs from the filtered-Navier–Stokes stress, especially when the grid is much coarser than $\eta$; model-consistent and RL training need hundreds or thousands of LES runs, motivating fast solvers (§1). **Stated**
5. The wall model is the log law, Eq. (10), $U_{\mathrm{LES}}/u_\tau=\kappa^{-1}\ln(u_\tau h_{\mathrm{wm}}/\nu)+B$ with $\kappa=0.41$, $B=5.2$, solved by Newton–Raphson to 0.01% relative tolerance in typically 3–7 iterations (§2.1–2.2). **Stated**
6. In the dynamic procedure the first off-wall layer uses a 2D box test filter (wall-parallel velocity extrapolated linearly to ghost points, then the 3D filter) with $\hat\Delta/\Delta=4^{1/3}$; WMLES channel tests gave more accurate results than with $8^{1/3}$ (§2.2). **Shown** (data not displayed)
7. With a wall model, wall-normal derivatives of the wall-parallel velocity in the first off-wall layer are first-order one-sided (linear extrapolation to ghost points plus central differencing), "crucial" for the strain rate entering the subgrid viscosity (§2.2). **Stated**
8. With viscous terms implicit in all three directions the wall model can be applied only on $y$ walls, since the Fourier directions need homogeneous boundary conditions; implicit-in-$y$ has no such limit (§2.2). **Stated**
9. One GPU against one CPU node (channel, $512\times384\times1440$): explicit viscous terms give speed-ups of 13 (DNS) and 19 (wall-resolved LES); implicit-in-$y$ gives 12 and 17. Printed timings: explicit CPU 19.7/34.5/34.7 against GPU 1.5/1.8/1.8 ns per step per point (DNS/WRLES/WMLES); implicit-$y$ CPU 28.9/46.4/46.5 against GPU 2.4/2.7/2.7 (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
10. The wall model adds negligible cost; the Smagorinsky model with van Driest damping adds about 0.3 ns per step per point (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
11. Time breakdown (static model, explicit): Poisson solver 38%, right-hand side 30%, subgrid model 19%, correction 13%; implicit-$y$: solver 33%, right-hand side 31%, Helmholtz solve 16%, subgrid model 12%, correction 8% (Fig. 3). **Shown**
12. Memory per grid point (Table 2): explicit DNS 136 B, WRLES with the static model 168 B, with the dynamic model 352 B; implicit-$y$ 160/192/376 B; WMLES equals WRLES (§3.2). **Shown**
13. Strong scaling (fixed $512\times384\times11520$) and weak scaling ($512\times384\times(5760N_{\mathrm{nodes}})$) from 2 to 64 nodes; weak-scaling time grows by about 80% over that range; no implicit-$y$ point at 64 nodes for lack of GPU memory (§3.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
14. The fully implicit scheme is not analysed because solving the momentum equations by FFT is inefficient; ADI at $O(N_x)$ cost could be used, though in distributed memory it also needs transposes or three tridiagonal sweeps (§3.2). **Stated**
15. Decaying isotropic turbulence: the static model with $C_s=0.18$ reproduces the Comte-Bellot–Corrsin spectra on $32^3$ and $64^3$ regardless of the filter, except the coarse grid with box filter, which needs $C_s=0.22$ (not shown); the dynamic model agrees reasonably, near-perfectly with the box filter; deviations near the Nyquist limit are attributed to finite-difference errors (§4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
16. Wall-resolved channel ($Re_b=20{,}000$): the static model's $e_{C_f}$ falls from 9.38% (coarsest) to 0.50% (finest); the dynamic model gives −2.25%, −0.69%, −1.49%, −3.22% (Table 3), summarized as "less than approximately 3%" (§4.2). **Shown**
17. The total shear stress is linear in WRLES and WMLES; the dynamic model gives a higher modelled stress and a lower resolved-stress peak than the static one because its eddy viscosity is higher (Figs. 6b, 9b). **Shown**
18. Wall-resolved velocity profiles in each run's own wall units are grid-sensitive (mostly the static model) but show little grid dependence in DNS wall units, so the sensitivity comes from the grid dependence of $C_f$; the resolved shear stress is over-predicted with the static and under-predicted with the dynamic model, matching their $C_f$ errors, and the streamwise normal-stress peak is over-estimated (§4.2, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
19. WMLES channel ($Re_b=250{,}000$, $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.1\delta$): grid convergence is non-monotonic for both subgrid models and both $AR=1$ and 2, most marked in $R_{11}$ and also in $C_f$ and $R_{12}$, consistent with Meyers & Sagaut [49] for wall-resolved LES (§4.3, Fig. 12, Table 4). **Shown**
20. Finest-grid WMLES $e_{C_f}$: 2.43% (static) and 0.44% (dynamic) for $AR=1$; 0.26% and 1.30% for $AR=2$; the sign on the finest grids is always positive, also in unshown WMLES at $Re_b=20{,}000$, "consistent with the fact that mean wall shear stress obtained with WMLES is biased toward larger values when the instantaneous velocity is used as input for the wall model" (§4.3). **Shown** (bias explanation **Stated**, no reference printed)
21. $AR=1$ grids converge better than $AR=2$: smaller variation of the wall-stress and mean-velocity errors and weaker non-monotonicity (§4.3). **Shown**
22. The LES velocity inside the wall-modelled layer "should not be taken seriously"; $e_\phi$ integrates only over $0.1\le y/\delta\le1$ (§4.3, Eq. 24). **Stated**
23. Square-duct WMLES ($Re_b=40{,}000$): $\lvert e_{C_f}\rvert$ within about 3% in all cases (largest −3.08%), supporting the log law as a wall model "in flows with moderate geometrical complexity"; finest-grid $K_{\mathrm{sf}}$ errors about 22% (static) and 25% (dynamic) against the DNS value $3.26\times10^{-5}$; $C_f$ and $K_{\mathrm{sf}}$ converge non-monotonically for both models (§4.4, Table 5). **Shown**
24. In the duct, unlike the channel, the predicted $R_{11}$ peak along the wall bisector rises with refinement and improves; small oscillations near the stress peak on coarse grids are attributed to the absence of near-wall clustering; the shear stresses vary non-monotonically (§4.4, Figs. 14–16). **Shown**
25. The dynamic model shows "somewhat superior performance in grid convergence and turbulence prediction"; one GPU gives about 15 times the speed of a full CPU node; the solver is meant to host other models (Vreman, ODE-based wall models) and models developed by machine learning or RL (§5). **Stated**

The closures in registry notation:
$\tau^d_{ij}=-2\nu_t\bar S_{ij}$ with the isotropic part absorbed into the
pressure (Eqs. 1–3); static model
$\nu_t=(C_s\Delta D(y))^2\lvert\bar S\rvert$ with
$D(y)=1-\exp(-y^+/A^+)$, $A^+=25$, $y^+$ in the run's own wall units
(Eq. 4); dynamic model $\nu_t=C_s^2\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert$ with
$C_s^2=\langle M_{ij}L_{ij}\rangle/\langle M_{ij}M_{ij}\rangle$ clipped at
zero, $L_{ij}=\widehat{\bar u_i\bar u_j}-\hat{\bar u}_i\hat{\bar u}_j$,
$M_{ij}=2\Delta^2\widehat{\lvert\bar S\rvert\bar S_{ij}}-2\hat\Delta^2\lvert\hat{\bar S}\rvert\hat{\bar S}_{ij}$,
$\hat\Delta/\Delta=2$ (Eqs. 5–9). The filter width $\Delta$ and the
static $C_s$ of the channel and duct runs are not printed. $U_{\mathrm{LES}}$
is the instantaneous wall-parallel velocity magnitude at $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$,
interpolated linearly between the first two off-wall points; the wall
model's stress is applied last in the boundary-condition sequence.

# Printed slips

- **Eqs. (11), (17), (18), (21):** the Runge–Kutta coefficients are printed with the subscript $\kappa+1$ ($\alpha_{\kappa+1}$, $\beta_{\kappa+1}$, $\gamma_{\kappa+1}$), where the text and Eqs. (12)–(14), (19)–(20), (22) write $k+1$.
- **Fig. 11 caption:** "Normalization is based on the wall units of the DNS (a,b) and the wall units of each simulation (c,d). Normalization is based on the DNS wall units."
- **§4.3:** the thirteen grids per aspect ratio are "generated through uniform refinement in all three directions", while in Table 4 the $1536\times256\times576$ row keeps $N_y=256$ of the preceding $1024\times256\times384$ row.
- **§4.2:** the dynamic model "consistently exhibits errors of less than approximately 3%", while Table 3 lists −3.22% on its finest grid.
- **Fig. 6 caption:** "(solid)" is assigned to both the total and the viscous shear stress.
- **Abstract against §1 and §5:** GPU acceleration "through OpenACC directives" in the abstract, "a combination of CUDA Fortran and OpenACC directives" in the introduction and conclusion.
