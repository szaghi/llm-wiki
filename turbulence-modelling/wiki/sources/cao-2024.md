# Summary

Cao et al. (2024) extend the
[high-order gas-kinetic scheme](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md)
(HGKS) to general curvilinear meshes given only as discrete grid points
(HGKS-cur). HGKS is a finite-volume Navier–Stokes solver whose interface flux
is the time-dependent solution of the BGK kinetic equation. The BGK equation
is written in computational coordinates with the Jacobian and metric terms.
The metrics come from fourth-order central differences of the grid
coordinates, cast in a symmetric conservative form to satisfy the
[geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md), and
are interpolated to two-point Gauss quadrature points. Fifth-order
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) reconstructs the cell-averaged Jacobian and the
Jacobian-weighted conservative variables in computational space, and a
two-stage fourth-order Lax–Wendroff-type method advances in time. Tests on two
sinusoidally perturbed meshes give roughly fourth- to fifth-order convergence
and preserve a uniform flow to $O(10^{-14})$. A three-dimensional lid-driven
cavity is also checked.

The scheme is then used as [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md), with
no explicit subgrid model: its built-in
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) acts as the
subgrid dissipation. For an isothermal-wall compressible
[channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at bulk $M_b=1.5$ and
$Re_b=3000$, the $128^3$ implicit LES matches a $160^3$ run that the authors
call [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) and the reference DNS
of Coleman et al. (1995) and Morinishi et al. (2004). It matches them in wall
statistics, rms profiles and the van Driest-transformed velocity
([compressible velocity transformations](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)).
A streamwise box halved, or the native BGK Prandtl number $Pr=1$, visibly
degrades the density and temperature fluctuations. On a compressible
[periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) ($M=0.2$, $Re_b=2800$, mesh from
discrete points) the separation and reattachment points and $C_p$ are close to
the DNS of Peller & Manhart. The authors find that the reference
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) with the approximate
deconvolution model overpredicts the Reynolds stresses. They note that the
reference LES statistics are density-weighted while theirs are not. The
constrained-LES reference of the channel is said to perform slightly better
than LES with the [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md), a claim
cited from its original authors.

# Key claims

1. HGKS-cur handles curvilinear meshes given analytically or as discrete grid points without an analytical transformation; the metrics are built by dimension-by-dimension Lagrange interpolation and the GCL is preserved (§1, §2.3). **Derived**
2. The BGK equation in computational space is Eq. 4, with the metric terms of Eq. 5; the semi-discrete finite-volume scheme updates the Jacobian-weighted conservative variables (Eq. 6). **Derived**
3. The reconstructed variables are the cell-averaged Jacobian and the Jacobian-weighted conservative variables, by fifth-order WENO dimension by dimension in computational space (§2.3). **Stated**
4. The two-stage fourth-order time discretization (Eq. 12) "provides a fourth-order time accurate solution" for hyperbolic equations (§2.4; proof cited to Li & Du 2016). **Stated**
5. Advection of $\rho=1+0.2\sin(\pi(x+y+z))$ to $t=2$: $L^1$ orders 4.39, 4.56, 4.39, 4.16 on mesh 1 and 4.61, 4.65, 4.14, 4.03 on mesh 2, from $10^3$ to $160^3$ cells (Tables 1–2, §3.1). **Shown**
6. Free-stream preservation: uniform-flow errors stay at $O(10^{-14})$ ($L^1$ from $6.2\times10^{-15}$ at $10^3$ to $4.6\times10^{-14}$ at $160^3$ on mesh 1), so "the geometric conservation law is well preserved" (Table 3, §3.1). **Shown**
7. Three-dimensional lid-driven cavity at $M=0.15$: steady $Re=1000$ on $33^3$ and unsteady $Re=3200$ on $65^3$ agree with the Chebyshev-collocation solution of Albensoeder & Kuhlmann and the experiment of Prasad & Koseff (Fig. 3, §3.2). **Shown**
8. In implicit LES "the built-in numerical dissipation acts as the subgrid-scale (SGS) dissipation, thus no explicit SGS model is utilized" (§1). **Stated**
9. Channel at $M_b=1.5$, $Re_b=3000$: implicit LES G1 ($128^3$, $\Delta y^+_{\min}=0.50$, $\Delta x^+=21.18$, $\Delta z^+=7.06$) "converges to" G4 ($160^3$, called DNS), and both agree with the reference DNS: $Re_\tau=211$ and 213 against 222 (Coleman) and 218 (Morinishi); non-dimensional wall heat flux $-0.047$, $-0.048$ against $-0.049$, $-0.048$ (§3.3, Table 5). **Shown**
10. Halving the streamwise box ($2\pi\delta$, case G2) "almost does not affect" the wall statistics but visibly changes the rms density and temperature, so the box of Coleman et al. should be kept (§3.3, Figs. 7, 9). **Shown**
11. $Pr=1$ (the native BGK value, case G3) "changes the mean density, temperature and Ma number profiles greatly", raises $Re_\tau$ (221), the wall density (1.476) and the rms density and temperature peaks, and lowers the peak turbulent Mach number; the Prandtl number must be corrected to the target value (§3.3, Table 5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
12. In this channel the peak $M_t$ stays below 0.25, with "no strong shock-lets" (§3.3). **Shown**
13. The van Driest-transformed mean velocity of the implicit LES is "in reasonable agreement" with the reference DNS; constrained LES "also performs very well on coarse grids" (Fig. 8). **Shown**
14. A long enough streamwise box and the target Prandtl number "are essential in compressible wall-bounded turbulence simulations" (§3.3). **Shown**
15. Constrained LES "underestimates the values of turbulence intensities in the inner layer" but "performs slightly better than the explicit LES with Smagorinsky model" (§3.3). **Stated** (cited)
16. Periodic hill ($M=0.2$ volumetric, target $Re_b=2800$): $Re_b$ fluctuates around 2730, slightly below the target, because the mass flux varies over the cross-section (§3.4, Fig. 12). **Shown**
17. Separation/reattachment points (Table 6): DNS 0.21/5.41; ADM-LES 0.21/5.30; H1 ($100^3$) 0.23/5.18; H2 ($200\times100\times100$) 0.20/5.15; H3 ($400\times200\times200$) 0.24/5.52, "close to" the reference LES and DNS (§3.4). **Shown**
18. For $C_p$ "current iLES agrees well with the refereed DNS solution, better than the explicit LES" (Fig. 14). **Shown**
19. "The explicit LES overpredicts the normalized Reynolds stresses, especially for $\langle U'V'\rangle/\langle U_b\rangle^2$ … the explicit LES model may pollute current low-Reynolds number separated turbulent flow", while the implicit LES agrees with DNS (Figs. 15–16). **Shown** (the ADM-LES profiles are density-weighted, the DNS and implicit-LES profiles are not, §3.4)
20. H3 gives better Reynolds stresses, but "the improvement is not so worthwhile" for its cost; "coarse grids is enough for iLES when simulating low-Reynolds number separated turbulent flows" (§3.4). **Shown**
21. The highest $M_t$ reached by high-order finite-difference DNS of supersonic isotropic turbulence is $M_t\le1.2$, while HGKS simulated $M_t=2.0$ (§1, cited). **Stated**
22. Even for a Mach 8 hypersonic flat plate the maximum $M_t$ is below 0.5, which is why high-order finite differences dominate compressible wall-bounded turbulence (§1, cited). **Stated**
23. The cost of the MPI HGKS code "is comparable with the high-order finite difference method"; the two-dimensional domain decomposition scales properly with small communication cost (§1, §3). **Stated**
24. The BGK model corresponds to unit Prandtl number; the target $Pr$ is obtained by modifying the energy flux as in Xu (2001) (§3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. 11, $\hat\xi_y$ (p. 7, checked visually):** "$\widehat\xi_y=\frac12((xz_\eta)_\zeta-(zx_\eta)_\zeta+(zx_\zeta)_\eta-(zx_\zeta)_\eta)$": the last two terms are identical and cancel.
- **Eq. 11, $\hat\xi_x$ and $\hat\xi_z$:** expanding the printed $\tfrac12\big((zy_\eta)_\zeta-(yz_\eta)_\zeta+(zy_\zeta)_\eta-(yz_\zeta)_\eta\big)$ by the product rule leaves $z\,y_{\eta\zeta}-y\,z_{\eta\zeta}$, not the $y_\eta z_\zeta-z_\eta y_\zeta$ of Eq. 5; the ordering $\tfrac12\big((yz_\zeta)_\eta-(yz_\eta)_\zeta+(zy_\eta)_\zeta-(zy_\zeta)_\eta\big)$ does reproduce Eq. 5 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. The version of record may differ.
- **p. 7:** "Eq.(10) for $(\boldsymbol x_\xi)_{ijk}$, $(\boldsymbol x_\eta)_{ijk}$, $(\boldsymbol x_\eta)_{ijk}$": the third should be $(\boldsymbol x_\zeta)_{ijk}$.
- **Eq. 12:** "$\widehat Q^*=Q^n+\dots$": $Q^n$ lacks the hat used elsewhere.
- **Eq. 9 against $f_0$:** subscripts $A_l$, $A_r$ become superscripts $A^l$, $A^r$.
- **Channel mapping (§3.3):** with the printed computational range $\eta\in[-1.5\pi,1.5\pi]$, $y=\tanh(b_g(\eta/1.5\pi-1))/\tanh b_g$ with $b_g=2$ gives $y\approx-1.037$ and $y=0$ at the ends, not $[-1,1]$; it maps $[0,3\pi]$ onto $[-1,1]$. The hill mapping has the same mismatch *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Bulk quantities (§3.3):** "$U_b=\int_{-H}^HU(y)dy$, $\rho_b=\int_{-H}^H\rho(y)dy$", with no $1/(2H)$.
- **Hill coefficients (§3.4):** $C_f=\tau_w/(\langle\rho_b\rangle\langle U_b\rangle^2)$ and $C_p$ are printed without the factor $\tfrac12$ (checked visually).
- **Wall-unit spacings against $Re_\tau$:** $\Delta x^+=4\pi Re_\tau/128=21.18$ implies $Re_\tau\approx216$ (and so do $\Delta z^+=7.06$ and G4's $\Delta x^+=16.92$), while Table 5 gives 211 and 213 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **§3.3:** "the large Prandtl number enlarges … the friction non-dimensional heat flux $B_q$", while Table 5 gives $-0.045$ for G3 against $-0.047$ for G1, a smaller magnitude.
- **References:** [26] and [55] are the same work (Chen et al. 2017, J. Turbul. 18) printed twice; [24] author "N. Adams, A."; [56] second author "N. C. Gary"; [45] third author "J. R. William".
