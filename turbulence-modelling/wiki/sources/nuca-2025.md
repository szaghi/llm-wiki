# Summary

Nuca et al. (2025) remove the iteration from three common
[equilibrium wall models](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) of
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md). Spalding's and
Reichardt's [laws of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) are
inverted for $\tau_w$ by Newton iteration, which the authors report to
diverge sporadically in complex flows: the sampled velocity fluctuates
strongly, and the model is applied where it is not valid, as in transition
regions. The equilibrium ODE model (EqODE: the thin-layer momentum balance
with a van Driest-damped mixing-length eddy viscosity) does not diverge,
but needs a local 1-D mesh or quadrature and a fixed-point iteration.

The explicit approximants write $u^+$ as a function of the local Reynolds
number $Re_y=U_{\mathrm{LES}}h_{\mathrm{wm}}/\nu$, known from the sampled
data, so $\tau_w=\rho(U_{\mathrm{LES}}/u^+)^2$ follows directly. The base
is the explicit "model 2" of Cai & Sagaut (2021), which blends
$\sqrt{Re_y}$ with a Lambert-$W$ log-law branch, with its two blending
parameters re-fitted; a sum of Gaussian bumps in $\log_{10}Re_y$ corrects
the buffer layer. Coefficients come from global optimization of the
pointwise relative error. Global one-bump models have coefficients linear
in the law's constants ($\kappa$ and $B$, $C$ or $A^+$) with error below
1%; fixed-constant three-bump models, for "high-Re" ($\kappa=0.387$,
$B=4.21$) and "classical" constants, are claimed to stay below 0.05%.

The a priori test (in the sense of
[a priori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md))
feeds a stored LES velocity signal of a
[channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at $Re_\tau\approx1000$
to the models. The a posteriori run is a WMLES of the Aérospatiale
A-airfoil near stall in [OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md)
(libWallModelledLES, [sigma](/methods/sigma-model.md) SGS model). Used as
initial guesses, the explicit models cut the maximum iteration counts
(Newton 10–11 to 3; EqODE 100, the cap, to 6); EqODE wall-modelling cost
drops from 11.9% to 1.9% of run time. Machine-learning wall models,
including the [building-block-flow model](/methods/building-block-flow-wall-model.md),
and WMLES of the [NASA CRM high-lift](/benchmarks/nasa-crm-high-lift.md)
configuration are cited as context only.

# Key claims

1. In WMLES of complex flows the Newton solver for Spalding's law "would often sporadically diverge", particularly when $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ exceeds the first off-wall node, because of large velocity fluctuations or use of the model where it is not valid, such as transition regions (§I). **Stated** (authors' and users' experience)
2. On the A-airfoil near stall, with the previous-step stress as initial guess, Spalding's Newton count rises near the transition region and reaches 5 at selected points; these are "danger zones" for divergence (§II, Fig. 2). **Shown**
3. Robustness is not an issue for channel or zero-pressure-gradient boundary layers in the fully turbulent regime; much of the wall-model literature tests only such flows, which probe accuracy but not robustness (§II). **Stated**
4. EqODE has no divergence problem, but needs a local mesh or quadrature whose node distribution is not $y^+$-independent; adaptivity adds overhead and porting to accelerators is "involved for a non-expert" (§I). **Stated**
5. Without van Driest damping ($\nu_t/\nu=\kappa y^+$) EqODE integrates to $u^+=\kappa^{-1}\ln(1+\kappa y^+)+C$, the log part of Reichardt's law; with damping no analytical relation between $B$ and $A^+$ exists, so $B$ is evaluated pointwise at $y^+\gg30$ (§III, Eqs. 1, 8). **Derived**
6. $y^+\to\infty$ in Reichardt's law gives $C=B-\ln\kappa/\kappa$ (§III, Eq. 6). **Derived**
7. Spalding, Reichardt, EqODE and Cai–Sagaut laws coincide in the linear and log regions for matching constants, so only a buffer-layer perturbation needs fitting (§IV, Eq. 9). **Stated**
8. The target is 1% relative error in $u^+$: the models themselves are not accurate in the buffer layer, and Meneveau's explicit approximation of an ODE model also has about 1% maximum error (§IV). **Stated**
9. The buffer-layer perturbation has "one to several bumps" between $Re_y=1$ and $10^5$ for all three models (§IV, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. Global one-bump models, with coefficients linear in the law's constants, reach relative error below 1% over the whole $Re_y$ range for all three models, including an extrapolation in $\kappa$ above 0.4 for Reichardt (§V.A, Figs. 4–6, Table IV). **Shown**. With a minus sign in the exponent of Eq. (10) (see Printed slips), the Table IV Spalding model gives maximum errors of 0.82% ($\kappa=0.4$, $B=5.5$) and 0.63% ($\kappa=0.387$, $B=4.21$) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
11. Results are not very sensitive to the optimization grid: $n=5$ gives similar accuracy (§V.A). **Stated** (not shown)
12. Fixed-constant three-bump models (11 coefficients) reach errors "an order of magnitude" below the global ones, "does not exceed 0.05% for all three models" (§V.B, §VII, Fig. 7, Tables V–VI). **Shown** in Fig. 7. The coefficients of Tables V–VI, as printed to four decimals, are not reproduced by a computation from the printed tables: Eqs. (2), (3), (5), (6) and (10), with the minus sign restored, give maximum errors of about 1.5% (Table V) and 1.8% (Table VI) for Spalding and 0.5% and 1.0% for Reichardt *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
13. Classical constants: Spalding and Reichardt $\kappa=0.4$, $B=5.5$, $C=7.8$; EqODE $\kappa=0.41$, $A^+=17$, "essentially a standard choice" (Kawai & Larsson 2012; Xu & Bermejo-Moreno 2024), while Cabot used $A^+=19$ (§V.B). **Stated**
14. A priori channel test ($Re_\tau\approx1000$, velocity averaged over a cube of side $\delta/30$ at $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.05\delta$): the explicit $\tau_w$ error is below 0.07% for the high-Re models and below 0.7% for the global ones (§VI). **Shown**
15. Channel iteration counts (mean/max, relative tolerance $10^{-4}$; Table VII): Reichardt 1.95/3 (previous step), 1.79/2 (high-Re), 1.91/2 (global); Spalding 1.95/3, 1.52/2, 1.95/2; EqODE 3.27/6, 1.03/2, 3.9/4 (§VI). **Shown**
16. For the algebraic models the initial guess barely matters on average because Newton converges quadratically; EqODE is more sensitive, and the high-Re guess reduces its count "by more than a factor of 3" (§VI). **Shown**
17. On the airfoil the explicit Spalding $\tau_w$ deviates from the iterated one by at most 0.06%, over a wider $Re_y$ range than in the channel; "the results for the other two models are similar" (§VI, Fig. 8). **Shown** (Spalding), **Stated** (others)
18. Airfoil iteration counts over one $c/U_\infty$ (mean/max; Table VIII): Reichardt 2.88/11 (previous step), 2.93/3 (high-Re); Spalding 2.95/10, 2.95/3; EqODE 1.37/100 (the cap: convergence failed at some steps), 1.00/6. In the laminar region $x/c<0.08$ the previous-step guess is better because the flow is steady there (§VI, Fig. 9). **Shown**
19. Wall-modelling cost on the airfoil: about 2% of run time with the iterative algebraic models, 1.8% with explicit Spalding, 1.9% with explicit EqODE, 11.9% with the integrated EqODE (§VI). **Shown**
20. All explicit models perform the same operations with different coefficients, so their cost is identical (§VI). **Stated**
21. Used directly, the explicit models make the stress computation "unconditionally stable"; used as initial guesses, they stabilize the iteration count where the velocity fluctuates strongly or the model disagrees with the physics (Abstract, §VII). **Stated** (no iteration by construction), **Shown** (initial guess)
22. The approach should apply to any wall model coupling the linear and log laws in one dimension, and to thermal boundary layers (Abstract, §VII). **Stated**
23. One refinement of the Iacono–Boyd initial guess gives an explicit Lambert $W$ with at most 0.02% error; two give negligible error (App. A, Fig. 10). **Shown**
24. Cai & Sagaut (2021) and Meneveau (2020) are the two prior explicit wall models; Meneveau approximates an ODE model with a different eddy viscosity and also treats roughness and pressure gradients, so the works "complement each other" (§I). **Stated**

The explicit law, translated (Eqs. 5, 9, 10, 24):
$$u^+(Re_y)\simeq\big(e^{-Re_y/s_{\mathrm{CS}}}\big)^{p_{\mathrm{CS}}}\sqrt{Re_y}+\frac{\big(1-e^{-Re_y/s_{\mathrm{CS}}}\big)^{p_{\mathrm{CS}}}}{\kappa}W\!\big(\kappa e^{\kappa B}Re_y\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{N_b}\xi_i\,e^{-\sigma_i(\log_{10}Re_y-\mu_i)^2},$$
with the minus sign in the bump exponent restored (see Printed slips),
$W$ the Lambert function, and $(p_{\mathrm{CS}},s_{\mathrm{CS}},\mu_i,\sigma_i,\xi_i)$
fitted: as linear functions of $(\kappa,B)$, $(\kappa,C)$ or $(\kappa,A^+)$
for $N_b=1$ (Table IV), as fixed numbers for $N_b=3$ (Tables V–VI). The
re-fitted $p_{\mathrm{CS}}\in[1.22,1.29]$, $s_{\mathrm{CS}}\in[76,378]$
(Table III bounds) are not Cai & Sagaut's values. EqODE (Eq. 4):
$u^+=\int_0^{y^+}d\zeta/(1+\nu_t^+(\zeta))$,
$\nu_t^+=\kappa y^+(1-e^{-y^+/A^+})^2$.

# Printed slips

- Eq. (10) prints the bump as $\xi_ie^{\sigma_i(\log_{10}(Re_y)-\mu_i)^2}$ without a minus sign, while every printed $\sigma_i$ is positive and the text requires the bumps to decay for large $Re_y$; as printed, the corrections grow without bound.
- Fig. 7: the caption says "fixed high-Reynolds-number (top) and classical (bottom) parameters", while the panel labels read "Classical" (top) and "High Reynolds number" (bottom).
- §V.A: "for κ, we consider values from 0.38 to 0.41", while Table II gives $\kappa_{\max}=0.42$ (Spalding, EqODE) and 0.4 (Reichardt).
- §V: the $N_b=1$ regression is said to have "a total of 10 coefficients", "close to" the 11 of the $N_b=3$ models; Table IV prints 15 numbers per model (10 slopes and 5 intercepts).
- Eqs. (1) and (8) state the same pointwise relation for $B$ twice, with $\log$ and $\ln$.
- The channel test uses "a relative tolerance of $10^{-4}$", the airfoil EqODE "the set relative tolerance of $10^{-3}$"; whether the difference is intentional is not stated.
- The high-Re constants give $C=B-\ln\kappa/\kappa\approx6.66$, outside the Reichardt grid $C\in[6.8,7.7]$ of Table II, so the global Reichardt model of the channel test is an extrapolation in $C$, which the paper does not state *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- "Aerospatialle" (§II); "cord" for chord (§VI); ref. 25 prints the article number "41703"; ref. 11 prints pages "15–00418–23".
