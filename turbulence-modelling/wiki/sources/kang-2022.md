# Summary

Kang & Masud (2022) present the variational multiscale immersed boundary
(VMIB) method, a finite-element [immersed boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md)
that imposes the no-slip condition weakly on a triangulated surface
immersed in a fixed, non-body-fitted background mesh, for incompressible
turbulent flow. Starting from a mixed weak form with a Lagrange multiplier
on the immersed surface, the velocity is split into resolved and
unresolved parts in the manner of the [variational multiscale method](/methods/variational-multiscale-method.md),
and the unresolved velocity in the band of cut elements is expanded in an
interfacial bubble that peaks on the cut facet. Keeping the
generalized-$\alpha$ time discretization and the inertia inside this
fine-scale problem yields closed-form, element-wise stabilization tensors
that depend on fluid properties, the Navier–Stokes operator, the time
integrator and the cut-cell geometry; an analytical Lagrange multiplier
(the traction plus a stabilization term), which removes it as an
unknown; and the interfacial unresolved velocity
$\boldsymbol u^{<}=-(\boldsymbol u^{>}-\boldsymbol g)$, used as an
a posteriori estimator of the boundary-condition error. The resulting
Nitsche-like terms carry no user-defined penalty, which the authors set
against CutFEM and immersogeometric analysis. A ghost-penalty-type term
is added, and turbulence is modelled by the authors' residual-based VMS
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) model with bubble functions,
with no explicit filter or eddy viscosity.

Tests: the [circular-cylinder wake](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md)
at $Re_D=3900$ on 1.5 million linear hexahedra, a sphere at $Re_D=10^4$
on 7.3 million tetrahedra, and a 1:10 high-speed train. The paper reports
quadratic Newton convergence, $-5/3$ wake spectra, bounded stabilization
norms that grow on small cut facets, and boundary-condition errors within
3% (cylinder, sphere) and 1% (train) of the inflow velocity. The
paper does not analyse [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)
or run [isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md);
it cites the latter only through earlier VMS work.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol u^{>}$, $\boldsymbol u^{<}$ the resolved
(coarse) and unresolved (fine) velocity of the VMS split (the source's
$\hat v$, $\tilde v$); $\boldsymbol g$ the prescribed wall velocity;
$\Gamma_{\mathrm I}$ the immersed surface, $\Gamma^e_{\mathrm I}$ and
$\Omega^e_f$ the cut facet and fluid part of a cut element; $b^e_{\mathrm I}$
the interfacial bubble; $\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathrm{LM}}$ the Lagrange
multiplier; $\tau_{\mathrm I}$, $\tau_1$, $\tau_2$ the interfacial
stabilization tensors and $\tilde{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\mathrm I}$ the
interfacial operator tensor of Eq. (27) (none is an SGS stress);
$\tau_{\mathrm M}$, $\tau_{\mathrm C}$ the interior momentum and
continuity stabilization; $\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm M}$, $r_{\mathrm C}$
the resolved momentum and continuity residuals; $\alpha_m$, $\alpha_f$,
$\gamma_{\mathrm G}$ the generalized-$\alpha$ parameters; $e^h_v$ the
element-wise boundary-condition error.

1. VMIB derives the Lagrange multiplier that weakly enforces Dirichlet conditions on an immersed boundary, $\boldsymbol\lambda_{\mathrm{LM}}=-(-p\boldsymbol I+\boldsymbol\sigma)\cdot\boldsymbol n+\tau_{\mathrm I}(\boldsymbol u^{>}+\tau_1\boldsymbol u^{<}_n+\tau_2\,\partial_t\boldsymbol u^{<}|_n-\boldsymbol g)$ at $n+\alpha_f$, so it need not be solved for (§3.2.2, Eq. 36). **Derived**
2. Substituting it into the fine-scale model gives $\boldsymbol u^{<}_{n+1}=-(\boldsymbol u^{>}-\boldsymbol g)$ on $\Gamma_{\mathrm I}$ (Eq. 37), the error of the Dirichlet enforcement, used as an a posteriori estimator $e^h_v=\|\boldsymbol u^{<,h}\|$ (Eq. 64). **Derived**
3. $\tau_1$, $\tau_2$, $\tau_{\mathrm I}$ (Eqs. 32–34) emerge from the derivation and depend on material parameters, the Navier–Stokes operators, the generalized-$\alpha$ parameters and the cut geometry through $\Omega^e_f$ and $\Gamma^e_{\mathrm I}$; the formulation has no user-defined parameters (§3.2.1, §3.3). **Derived**
4. Lemma 1: as $\Delta t\to0$, $\boldsymbol u^{<}_{n+1}\to\boldsymbol u^{<}_n$ (Eqs. 28–29). **Proved**
5. Lemma 2: as $\Delta t\to0$ the stabilization tensor of the last term of Eq. (41) is bounded by the positive-definite tensor $\lim_{\Delta t\to0}\tau_{\mathrm I}(\boldsymbol I-\tau_1)=\mathrm{meas}(\Gamma^e_{\mathrm I})\,(\int_{\Gamma^e_{\mathrm I}}b^e_{\mathrm I}\,d\Gamma)^{-2}\,\tilde{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\mathrm I}$ (Eqs. 43–44); positive definiteness of $\tilde{\boldsymbol\tau}_{\mathrm I}$ is cited from earlier work. **Proved** (relies on a cited property)
6. Fine-scale models without the inertial term can lose stability at very small $\Delta t$ (citing Hsu et al.); keeping the inertia, the stabilization effects "do not vanish" as $\Delta t\to0$ (§3.2.1). **Derived**
7. $\|\tau_{\mathrm I}\|$ grows on small cut facets because $\int_{\Gamma^e_{\mathrm I}}$ appears in the denominator of Eq. (34), which improves the conditioning of the tangent matrix (§3.3). **Stated**
8. The four interface terms of Eq. (41) supply consistency, adjoint consistency, mass conservation and stabilization; the formulation is variationally consistent (§3.3). **Derived**
9. The VMIB interface terms have the structure of CutFEM for Stokes and of immersogeometric analysis, which need free parameters chosen by guidelines, whereas VMIB derives them, and its stabilization is time-dependent through the fine-scale history (§3.3, Table 1). **Stated**
10. The ghost-penalty-type term (Eq. 48), obtained from level-set advection stabilization, is "form identical" to full-gradient stabilization (§3.3). **Stated**
11. The method works on linear hexahedral and tetrahedral meshes (§5, Conclusions). **Shown**
12. Newton–Raphson converges quadratically with consistent linearization, at most three iterations per step (Tables 3–4). **Shown**
13. Cylinder at $Re_D=3900$: mean centreline velocity, mean velocity profiles, fluctuations and Reynolds shear stress at $x/D=6,7,10$ show "good correlation" with the LES of Kravchenko & Moin, the experiments and the authors' body-fitted residual-based LES (§5.1, Figs. 7–12). **Shown**
14. Cylinder wake spectra show the $-5/3$ decay (§5.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
15. Cylinder: $\|\tau_{\mathrm I}\|$ ranges from 1 to 1000, larger on sliver elements, which "seems to help" weak enforcement (Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
16. Cylinder: $e^h_v$ peaks within 3% of the inflow velocity, larger where the local velocity is larger and on sliver elements (Fig. 16). **Shown**
17. Sphere at $Re_D=10^4$: $C_D=0.410$, $-C_{pb}=0.288$, $\theta_{\mathrm{sep}}=86.0°$ at $\Delta t=0.01$ and $0.405$, $0.305$, $86.3°$ at $\Delta t=0.0025$, against Constantinescu et al. LES 0.393, 0.229, 85.0°, Rodríguez et al. DNS 0.402, 0.272, 84.7° and Rodríguez et al. LES 0.402, 0.293, 90.0° (§5.2, Table 5). **Shown**
18. Sphere: the time-history fine-scale model and the simplified one (history term dropped) "produce identical results", and different $\Delta t$ give "nearly same" responses (§5.2, Figs. 19–24). **Shown**
19. Sphere: the Q-criterion wake shows about nine vortex rings along the perimeter, as in the DNS (Fig. 18a). **Shown**
20. Sphere: centreline velocity fluctuations are slightly overestimated, attributed to slight slip at the surface from weak no-slip enforcement; near-wake turbulent kinetic energy is slightly above the DNS and matches about four diameters downstream (§5.2, Figs. 21–24). **Shown**
21. Sphere: $\|\tau_{\mathrm I}\|$ is "inversely proportional to the spatial dimension of the cut facet", bounded, mostly between 0.5 and 10; the boundary-condition error stays within 3% (Figs. 27, 29). **Shown**
22. Train ($Re=7.2\times10^5$ on the car width): wake spectra approach $-5/3$, presented as the residual-based LES capturing turbulence "even with relatively crude spatial discretization"; the boundary-condition error is at most 1% of the inflow velocity, highest at the nose (§5.3, Figs. 33, 35). **Shown**
23. Surface triangulation takes "only a few seconds on a single processor"; fictitious elements are removed (§4). **Stated**
24. Runs used 384–432 CPU processors (Stampede2), GMRES with additive Schwarz preconditioning at tolerance $10^{-12}$ (§5). **Stated**

In the residual-based VMS model (§3.4, Eqs. 49–59), after Calderer &
Masud and Masud & Calderer, $\boldsymbol u^{<}=-\tau_{\mathrm M}\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm M}+\mathcal F(\boldsymbol u^{<},\partial_t\boldsymbol u^{<})|_n$
and $p^{<}=-\tau_{\mathrm C}r_{\mathrm C}$, with bubble-function
stabilization tensors and a fine-scale history term; the term
$(\nabla\boldsymbol w,\rho\,\boldsymbol u^{<}\otimes\boldsymbol u^{<})$ is
kept for turbulence. In the present implementation the unresolved
velocity and pressure are uncoupled, so the level-2 term of the operator
matrix is neglected.
