# Summary

Helm & Martín (2022) produce and document
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) datasets of two separated
hypersonic [shock/turbulent boundary layer interactions](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
over compression ramps: a Mach 7.2 layer at $Re_\theta\approx3300$ over a
33° ramp (R33-M7-L, air) and a Mach 9.1 layer at $Re_\theta\approx8000$
over a 34° ramp (R34-M10-L, nitrogen), both on cold walls. The Favre-filtered
compressible equations are closed with the one-coefficient dynamic mixed
model of Martín, Piomelli & Candler (2000), a dynamic
[Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) eddy viscosity plus a
[scale-similar term](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) for both the SGS
stress and the SGS heat flux, with coefficients from the
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md); inviscid fluxes use a
fourth-order bandwidth-optimized [WENO scheme](/methods/weno-scheme.md)
whose [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) is
reduced by limiters. Inflow comes from an auxiliary recycling–rescaling
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).

The method is validated against tophat-filtered
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) of boundary layers at
Mach 3, 7 and 10, a separated Mach 3, 24° ramp and an attached Mach 7, 8°
ramp, on grids with 32 times fewer points; the van Driest
[transformed](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
profiles agree within 3%. A spanwise-domain-width effect on the Mach 3
separation length is reported. The hypersonic datasets document mean
fields, wall loads, Reynolds stresses, anisotropy invariants, TKE budgets
and low-frequency unsteadiness, and assess the Reynolds analogy factor, the
QP85 heat-transfer law, constant $Pr_t$ and the strong Reynolds analogies
([Morkovin's hypothesis](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md)), which fail
in the separated and recovering regions. An a posteriori ablation
([a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md))
removes the scale-similar terms: the Mach 3 and attached Mach 7 results
barely change, while the separated Mach 7 bubble grows by 30% (abstract:
32%) and the shear-layer spreading rate drops by about 10%. The authors
attribute this to the convective Mach number of about 2 of the separation
shear layer and to the conservative scale exchange of the scale-similar
term (the paper does not use the word
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md)).

# Key claims

1. The LES method (dynamic mixed model, Knight et al. SGS turbulent diffusion, bandwidth-optimized WENO) reproduces DNS of compressible boundary layers at Mach 3, 7 and 10: mean velocity and temperature within 3% (Mach 3 and 7) and 5% (Mach 10); van Driest-transformed profiles within 3% (Sec. III B, Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
2. Peak TKE of the LES boundary layers differs from tophat-filtered DNS by less than 2% (Sec. III B, Fig. 4). **Shown**
3. The unresolved (SGS) TKE of the Mach 7 boundary layer, estimated from filtered against unfiltered DNS, is 14–25% (25% at the wall and above $y/\delta=0.7$, about 14% elsewhere; below $y/\delta=0.2$ a minimum of 7%, then a maximum of 18% at the wall; see Printed slips); at Mach 3 and 10 it is 10–15% with a near-wall minimum not below 5% (Sec. III B, Fig. 5). **Shown**
4. A tophat filter applied to the DNS in space, and in time through Taylor's hypothesis, truncates the spectrum as the LES does; spectra are essentially unaffected for $f\delta/U_e\lesssim0.4$, so tophat-filtered DNS is an acceptable LES reference (Sec. III B, Fig. 6). **Shown**
5. For the separated Mach 3, 24° ramp the separation length depends on the spanwise domain width in both LES and DNS (DNS 2.93$\delta$ narrow; LES 3.05$\delta$ narrow, 3.71$\delta$ wide); the authors consider the effect physical and state that it "has not been closely addressed in the literature"; plotted against $x'/L_{\mathrm{sep}}$ all solutions collapse (Sec. III C, Table III, Fig. 8). **Shown**
6. The LES captures the multi-inflection skin friction inside the bubble and the wall-pressure plateau that excess numerical dissipation suppresses (Sec. III C, Fig. 8). **Shown**
7. Peak turbulence intensities in the Mach 3 separation shear layer are within 6% of filtered DNS; separation spectra peak at $St\approx0.01$–$0.03$ in LES and DNS, reattachment spectra are broadband at $St=O(0.1)$ (Sec. III C, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
8. For the attached Mach 7, 8° ramp, LES skin friction differs from DNS by about 3% on the ramp, the heat-transfer coefficient by at most 4%, wall pressure is indistinguishable, and the peak reverse-flow probability at the corner is 31% in both (Sec. III D, Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
9. Peak Favre-averaged stresses of the LES differ from filtered DNS by $0.2u_\tau$ (streamwise and shear), $0.1u_\tau$ (wall-normal) and less than $0.03u_\tau$ (spanwise) (Sec. III D, Fig. 14). **Shown**
10. A duration of $100L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$, about three low-frequency cycles at $St_L=0.03$, converges the separated mean flow within 3%: ten overlapping segments of the Mach 7 separation signal give a mean separation position within 3% of the full average, standard deviation 1.7% (Sec. IV A, Fig. 16). **Shown**
11. Separation spectra of both hypersonic cases peak at $St_L=0.1$ with energy down to $St_L=0.03$; reattachment spectra are broadband around $St_L\approx1$ (Sec. IV A, Fig. 15). **Shown**
12. Mean separation lengths are 6.2$\delta$ (R33-M7-L, averaged over $3150\,\delta/U_e=510\,L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$) and 4.1$\delta$ (R34-M10-L, $450\,\delta/U_e=110\,L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$) (Sec. IV A, Table IV). **Shown**
13. Maximum reverse flow is about 12% of $U_e$ in both cases; the maximum mean temperature in the bubble is $8.7T_e$ (Mach 7) and $12.5T_e$ (Mach 10), 50% and 40% above the inviscid post-shock values $5.7T_e$ and $8.7T_e$; peak mean density $6.5\rho_e$ and $10.0\rho_e$ against inviscid $5.0\rho_e$ and $5.3\rho_e$ (Sec. IV C, Fig. 21). **Shown**
14. Peak $C_f$ is 9.3 (Mach 7) and 11.3 (Mach 10) times the incoming level and its rms peaks at about 20 times upstream; peak Stanton number $C_h$ is 15.2 and 16.8 times the incoming value and 2.2 and 2.0 times the outlet value; downstream wall pressure is 3% below the inviscid oblique-shock level (Sec. IV C, Fig. 23). **Shown**
15. Streamwise turbulence intensity in the separation shear layer is amplified to twice the upstream maximum in both flows (Sec. IV D, Fig. 24). **Shown**
16. Anisotropy invariants of $b_{ij}$: the incoming layers follow typical boundary-layer trajectories; at the corner the invariants move towards the axisymmetric-compression branch typical of mixing layers and towards isotropy (Sec. IV D, Fig. 26). **Shown**
17. TKE budgets: in the separation shear layer the increased production is balanced by transport, convection, pressure diffusion and dissipation, the increase of dissipation being mostly SGS; pressure work and pressure dilatation are small; downstream production at Mach 10 is twice that at Mach 7; the upstream production peak is at $y^+=14$ (Mach 7) and 17 (Mach 10) (Sec. IV D, Fig. 27). **Shown**
18. The inverse Reynolds analogy factor $C_f/(2C_h)$ is 0.85 upstream and about 0.7 downstream; the assumption of a factor near 1 fails in the separated region; with inviscid post-shock $U_e$ and $T_r$ downstream it overshoots slightly and decays towards 1 (Sec. IV E, Fig. 28a). **Shown**
19. The QP85 law of Back & Cuffel overpredicts heat transfer at separation and in the recovering layer (error growing to the exit) and misses the steep rise past the corner; it was derived for attached interactions (Sec. IV E, Fig. 28b; Sec. V). **Shown**
20. Turbulent Prandtl number: upstream $Pr_t$ is within 13% of 1 for $0.05<y'/\delta_x<0.75$, 0.75 at the edge, and fails below 0.05; downstream $Pr_t\approx1.0$ for $y'/\delta_x>0.1$ at Mach 7 and about 1.4 for $y'/\delta_x=0.3$–$0.5$ at Mach 10 (Sec. IV E, Fig. 29a). **Shown**
21. The SRA velocity–temperature correlation $-R_{u''T''}$ falls from 0.85 at $y'/\delta_x=0.05$ to 0.6 at the edge upstream, changes sign at the cold wall, and is about 0.5 in the Mach 10 downstream layer (Sec. IV E, Fig. 29b). **Shown**
22. The SRA magnitude relation is 0.65 upstream and 0.4 downstream (Mach 7), 0.6 and 0.35 (Mach 10), with a minimum of about 0.3 near the wall, so SRA $\approx1$ fails on these cold walls; Huang et al.'s modified SRA is about 1.2 upstream for $y'/\delta_x=0.1$–$1.0$ in both cases, 1.2 downstream only for 0.2–0.5 at Mach 7, and fails for the Mach 10 recovering layer (Sec. IV E, Fig. 29c–d). **Shown**
23. Removing the scale-similar terms (dynamic eddy viscosity only, DEV) changes the Mach 3 separation length by less than 5% and the attached Mach 7 corner reverse-flow probability from 31% to 35%, but increases the separated Mach 7 separation length by 30% (Sec. IV F, Fig. 30; Sec. V); the abstract says "as much as a 32%". **Shown**
24. The DEV/DMM difference lies not in the incoming boundary layer (identical van Driest profiles and stresses) but in the separation shear layer: spreading rate 0.194 (DMM) against 0.173 (DEV), "nearly a 10% decrease"; lower spreading means lower entrainment and a larger bubble; the TKE budgets scale with the local shear-layer thickness, so DEV produces proportionally less turbulence activity (Sec. IV F, Figs. 31–33). **Shown**
25. The Mach 7 separation shear layer has a convective Mach number of 2 (Mach 3 case: 1), taken from Helm et al. (2021); the conservative energy exchange of the scale-similar term is "necessary for the simulation of this condition" (Sec. V). **Stated** (convective Mach number cited); the conclusion is **Shown** for one case.
26. The LES uses 32 times fewer grid points than the DNS, "equivalent to 3% of the DNS computational cost"; the average time step of the Mach 3 separated LES is 2.8 times the DNS one; the a priori study of Martín et al. suggests that 25–30% SGS TKE might be tolerable (Sec. V). **Shown** (point count, time step) / **Stated** (cost equivalence)
27. The SGS viscous diffusion is neglected because it is typically an order of magnitude smaller than the SGS heat flux and available models carry an uncertainty of the order of the term itself (Sec. II). **Stated**
28. RANS methods are "notoriously unreliable" for separated shock/boundary-layer interactions, with large errors in separation length and wall heat transfer (Sec. I). **Stated**

# Printed slips

1. Separation-length increase without the scale-similar terms: "as much as a 32%" (abstract) against "increased by 30%" (Sec. IV F) and "significant increase of 30%" (Sec. V).
2. Time step: "increase of three times the DNS" (abstract) against "2.8 times larger" (Sec. V).
3. Sec. III, p. 6: "the tophat filter of Eq. (11)"; the tophat filter is Eq. (12), Eq. (11) being the SGS diffusion model.
4. Sec. III B: "The much shorter rescaling length of the DNS (29δ compared to 7δ for the LES)": the DNS value printed is the longer one, and Table II gives $L_x/\delta_o=8.3$ for M3n-D against 15.0 and 30.7 for M3n-L and M3w-L.
5. Sec. III D: "As with the bounday layer solutions of Sec. III A and the supersonic STBLI solution of Sec. III B": the boundary layers are in Sec. III B and the supersonic interaction in Sec. III C.
6. Table III: row R24-M3w-D is identical to R24-M3n-D in every column (21.0 M points, $L_{\mathrm{sep}}/\delta=2.93$, 1058, 361), although the text states that the separation length "differs significantly between the wide-domain and narrow-domain solutions", seen "in both the LES and DNS", and Table II gives the wide box 13.4 M against 7.3 M points.
7. Sec. IV A: "corresponds to a time scale of 33Ue/Lsep" and "a duration of 100Ue/Lsep", with the ratio inverted; elsewhere $100L_{\mathrm{sep}}/U_e$.
8. Sec. IV C text: "mean separation (x'/Lsep = 0.65)"; the captions of Figs. 22, 25, 26 and 27 give $-0.65$.
9. Case names in captions: Fig. 17 and Figs. 23, 28 "R24-M10-L"; Figs. 22 and 25 "R35-M10-L"; Fig. 19 "R33-M-L"; Fig. 7 "R24-M3-L"; Fig. 9 legend "R24-Mw-D". The Fig. 25 caption reads "The top row is R33-M7-L, and the top row is R34-M10-L. The top row is R33-M7-L, and the bottom row is R35-M10-L."
10. Sec. IV E: "It was noted in Fig. 26(c) that the Ch dips slightly at separation but the same feature does not occur in the wall pressure shown in Fig. 26(a)"; Fig. 26 holds the Lumley triangles, the wall distributions are Figs. 23(c) and 23(a).
11. Sec. IV E: the SRA magnitude relation under discussion is Eq. (19), but the text says "the relation of Eq. (18) was originally derived for an adiabatic boundary layer".
12. Eq. (9) prefixes the scale-similar term with $\rho$, Eq. (10) with $\bar\rho$.
13. Eq. (10) prints the temperature gradient as $\partial\hat T/\partial\xi$ without the index $j$.
14. Eq. (3): the last entry of the SGS flux mixes $\mathcal Q_x$, $\mathcal J_x$ with $\mathcal D_j$, and includes $\mathcal D_j$ although Sec. II states that it is neglected.
15. Sec. II prints the strain rate as "$\hat S_{ij}=\tfrac12(\partial\hat u_i/\partial x_i+\partial\hat u_j/\partial x_i$", with $x_i$ for $x_j$ in the first derivative and no closing parenthesis.
16. Sec. III A prints the recovery temperature as $T_r=[1+0.9(\gamma-1)M_e^2/2]$, without the factor $T_e$.
17. Sec. II: $\hat k=\hat\mu(2.5c_v-1.5)$ subtracts a pure number from a specific heat, consistent only if $c_v$ is nondimensional, which the paper does not say *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
18. Sec. II calls $\hat\mu$ the "kinematic viscosity" while it is the dynamic viscosity of $\hat\sigma_{ij}$ and Eqs. (13)–(15).
19. Sec. IV D prints the third invariant as $III=b_{ij}b_{jk}b_{kl}/3$; the last index should close on $i$.
20. Eq. (16): $\mathcal C=-\partial k\tilde u_j/\partial x_i$ (index mismatch); the text calls $\Pi$ "pressure strain", the Fig. 27 legend "P. Dilatation", and Eq. (16) defines the pressure–dilatation correlation.
21. Eq. (12): the sum runs from $i-n/2+1$ to $i+n/2+1$, one index beyond the symmetric stencil whose end points are $f_{i\pm n/2}$, and the summand is written $f_i$ with the summation index equal to the central index.
22. Sec. III B: "essentially unaffected for wavelengths below $f\delta/U_e\lesssim0.4$", a frequency called a wavelength.
23. Sec. V: "Maximum skin friction and heat transfer fluctuations were also found to occur on the ramp just downstream of separation", while Sec. IV C places the $C_f$ rms peak "downstream of reattachment".
24. Sec. I: "lrge eddy simulation"; Sec. III C: "Preibe and Martín [35]".
25. Ref. [5] (Gnoffo et al., 43rd AIAA Aerospace Sciences Meeting) is printed with year 2011.
26. Unresolved TKE at the wall of the Mach 7 layer: Sec. III B gives "25% at the wall" and, in the next sentence, "a maximum of 18% at the wall"; Sec. V gives "approximately 25% energy in the SGS at the wall and 12% in the majority of the boundary layer above z/δ = 0.2" (wall-normal $y$ here), against "about 14%" in Sec. III B.
