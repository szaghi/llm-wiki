# Summary

Yu et al. (2023) study how turbulence recovers downstream of an impinging
oblique [shock/boundary-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md).
They reuse the [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) database
of [Yu et al. (2022)](/sources/yu-2022-e.md): $M_\infty=2.28$, shock angle
33.2°, mild separation, computed with [STREAmS](/software/streams.md). The
convection scheme is sixth-order [kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md),
switched to fifth-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) by the Ducros
[sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md). Inflow is a digital filter with the
generalized Reynolds analogy ([inflow generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)).
The separation bubble leaves a mixing layer "hidden" in the downstream
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md), with a velocity
defect, an inflection point and strong outer large-scale motions.

The contribution is a decomposition into a canonical portion and a
mixing-layer-induced residue. The canonical portion is built from
canonical statistics: a composite [law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
with a cosine wake, inverted through the [van Driest transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md),
plus the reference-station stresses and spectra. The decomposition applies
to the mean velocity, the shear stress and the spanwise spectra. The
Renard–Deck [skin-friction decomposition](/definitions/skin-friction-decomposition.md)
in compressible form shows that the large mixing-layer Reynolds-shear-stress
term is mostly balanced by a negative spatial-growth term. The mixing layer
therefore adds under 15 % of the skin friction (about 11 % beyond ten
thicknesses). The mixing-layer energy peaks at $y\approx0.5\delta_r$,
$\lambda_z\approx\delta_r$. Its footprints reach the wall in $u$ and $w$
but not in $v$. Its anisotropy sits on the axisymmetric-expansion line of
the Lumley triangle, like a free mixing layer at low convective Mach number
(compared with spatial mixing layers, not the
[temporal mixing layer](/benchmarks/temporal-mixing-layer.md) benchmark).
Near-wall recovery is judged by the wall-normal and shear stresses, the
"detached" variables of the [attached-eddy](/definitions/attached-eddy-hypothesis.md)
picture. The mean velocity recovers about 13 thicknesses after
impingement and near-wall turbulence after 10, while the outer stresses
stay elevated at the domain end. A self-similar integral model of the
mixing-layer energy reproduces the decay and predicts that full recovery
needs more than 50 thicknesses. [Pressure dilatation](/definitions/pressure-dilatation.md)
is neglected in the budget, by citation.

# Key claims

The source's overbar (Reynolds average) is $\langle\cdot\rangle$, its tilde
(Favre average) $\langle\cdot\rangle_\rho$, $u_i''$ the Favre fluctuation;
its $R_{u_iu_j}=\langle\rho u_i''u_j''\rangle/(\rho_\infty U_\infty^2)$ is
the density-weighted Reynolds stress; its $K$ is $k$; $\delta_r$ is the
99 % thickness at the reference station $x_r=15\delta_{in}$, with
$x^*=(x-x_{imp})/\delta_r$, $y^*=y/\delta_r$. Page-local symbols:
$\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm c}$, $\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm m}=\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm c}-\langle u\rangle_\rho$
the canonical and mixing-layer mean velocities; $\tau^{\mathrm{sh}}$,
$\tau^{\mathrm{sh}}_v$, $\tau^{\mathrm{sh}}_t$ the total, viscous and
turbulent shear stress (subscripts c, m for the portions); $C_V$, $C_T$,
$C_P$, $C_G$ the Renard–Deck terms; $\phi_{ij}$ the pressure–strain (the source's $\Pi_{ij}$); $M_c$ the
convective Mach number; $L_{\mathrm{sep}}$ the separation length;
$b_{\mathrm{wk}}$ the wake coefficient of Eq. (4.1).

1. The DNS is of an oblique shock (wedge 8°, shock angle 33.2°, imposed by the inviscid Rankine–Hugoniot jump at the top boundary) impinging at $x_{imp}=30\delta_{in}$ on a boundary layer at $M_\infty=2.28$, $T_\infty=155.8$ K, $Re_{in}=\rho_\infty U_\infty\delta_{in}/\mu_\infty=12\,000$, with mild separation (§2). **Stated**
2. $L_{\mathrm{int}}\approx3.27\delta_r$ and $L_{\mathrm{sep}}\approx1.65\delta_r$, consistent with Volpiani et al. (2018, figure 12) (§2). **Shown** (reported, "we reported", from the authors' earlier study). For the same database and the same reference station ($x=15\delta_{in}$, identical Table 1), [Yu et al. (2022)](/sources/yu-2022-e.md), §2.2, prints separation at $x^*\approx-2.60$, reattachment at $-0.73$ and $L^*_{sep}\approx1.87$, with the same $L^*_{int}\approx3.27$: recorded as an open contradiction on the [shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md) page.
3. Reference-station statistics: $C_f=3.104\times10^{-3}$, $\delta^*/\delta_r=0.334$, $\theta/\delta_r=0.0902$, $Re_\theta=982$, $Re_\tau=201$, shape factor 3.70 (Table 1). **Shown**
4. The Reynolds-stress fields in the interaction agree reasonably with Bernardini et al. (2016), differences attributed to the Reynolds number; normal-stress peaks rise from the wall to $y^*\approx0.3$ through the interaction, near-wall small-scale structures reform from $x^*\approx4.0$, and the shear stress peaks at $x^*\approx1.0$ (§2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
5. Immediately downstream ($x_1$) the log-law intercept of the van Driest velocity is much lower than 5.0 and the wake higher, with an inflection point at $y/\delta\approx0.3$; at $x_4$ (13.3$\delta_r$ after impingement) the intercept is back to 5.0 and the mixing-layer residue vanishes visually (§3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
6. For $y^+\lesssim30$ the wall-normal and shear stresses $R_{22}^+$, $-R_{12}^+$ collapse to the reference station downstream of $x_3^*=10$; the authors classify them as "detached" variables of the attached-eddy model, while $R_{11}^+$ and $R_{33}^+$ are wall-attached: $R_{11}^+$ collapses below $y^+\approx40$ downstream of $x_2$ but above the reference value, and $R_{33}^+$ decreases continuously without converging before $x_5$ (§3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
7. The anomalous near-wall $R_{11}^+$, $R_{33}^+$ are conjectured to balance a rising Reynolds number (which intensifies wall-attached fluctuations) against decaying outer large-scale motions (whose footprints enhance them); near-wall recovery is therefore judged by $R_{22}^+$ or $-R_{12}^+$, giving $x_3^*=10.0$ (§3). **Stated** (supported in §5.1)
8. The decomposition $\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm m}=\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm c}-\langle u\rangle_\rho$ with the canonical profile (4.1) reproduces the profiles at $x_r$, $x_4$, $x_5$; $\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm m}$ peaks at $y=0.2\delta$, decreases monotonically to the edge, the mixing layer is as thick as the boundary layer, and at $x_4$, $x_5$ it contributes less than 4 % of the mean velocity (§4.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
9. The maximum of $-\mathrm d\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm m}/\mathrm dy$ (cubic fit in $y/\delta=0.3$–0.9) lies at $y/\delta\approx0.65$, weakly dependent on $x$; normalized by their maxima the profiles are roughly self-similar but asymmetric because of the wall (§4.1, Fig. 7d). **Shown**
10. The mixing-layer Reynolds shear stress $\tau^{\mathrm{sh}}_{t,\mathrm m}$ decreases monotonically downstream with maxima at $y/\delta\approx0.5$, below the maximum of the mixing-layer mean shear (§4.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
11. In the Renard–Deck decomposition $(C_V+C_T+C_G)/C_f>0.997$ outside the interaction zone, so the zero-pressure-gradient form is valid there; $C_P$ matters only inside the interaction (§4.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
12. Upstream $C_V$ and $C_T$ each give about 50 % of $C_f$ and $C_G$ less than 10 %; downstream $C_V$ is about 40 % with $C_{Vc}\approx C_V$, $C_T$ exceeds $C_f$ while $C_{Tc}$ stays about 50 %, the excess $C_{Tm}$ is mostly balanced by a negative $C_G$, and $(C_{Vm}+C_{Tm}+C_G)/C_f$ is below 15 %, about 11 % from $x^*\approx10.0$ (§4.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. The mixing-layer-induced large-scale motions, though strong in the outer region, make a "trivial" contribution to skin friction (§4.2, §7). **Shown**
14. $0\le C_G/C_f\le1$ holds under non-negative production and non-increasing total shear stress; the latter fails downstream of the interaction (Fig. 8), so $C_G<0$ is possible, meaning loss or entrainment of mean kinetic energy in the convective frame (§4.2). **Stated** ("it can be proved"; no proof or citation for the bound is printed, the energetic reading of the formula is attributed to Renard & Deck 2016)
15. Downstream $\delta$ grows rapidly while the displacement and kinetic-energy thicknesses stay almost constant, so mean kinetic energy is redistributed wall-normally by mean convection without significant dissipation (§4.2, Eq. 4.10, Fig. 11). **Shown**
16. Canonical spanwise spectra at the reference station peak at $y^+\approx12$, $\lambda_z^+\approx100$ for $u$ (streaks) and at $y^+\approx50$ ($v$) and $y^+\approx20$ ($w$) with $\lambda_z^+\approx150$ (quasi-streamwise vortices) (§5.1, Fig. 12a–c). **Shown**
17. At $x_3$ the $u$ spectrum has two peaks (near-wall streaks; outer large-scale motions at $y^*\approx0.5$, $\lambda_z^*\approx1.0$); the $v$ and $w$ spectra have a single outer peak at the same location and scale (§5.1, Fig. 12d–f). **Shown**
18. In the mixing-layer-induced spectra the inner small-scale $u$ peak is replaced by a small negative region (as in Hu & Zheng 2018); the outer peak leaves footprints at the wall ("superposition"), none obvious in $v$, stronger and wider in $w$ than in $u$ (§5.1, Fig. 12g–i). **Shown**
19. Spectra at $x_1$, $x_2$, $x_4$, $x_5$ (not shown) have outer peaks at about the same height and scale, partly reflecting self-similarity of the mixing-layer motions (§5.1). **Stated**
20. Mixing-layer Reynolds stresses peak at $y=0.5\delta$ at $x_1$, the peak moving lower downstream; the ratios of the stresses to $2k$ are almost constant for $y/\delta\approx0.3$–1.0, at 0.49, 0.23, 0.28 and 0.15 for the $11$, $22$, $33$ and $-12$ components (§5.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. The invariants of $b_{ij}$ for $y/\delta\approx0.3$–1.0 lie about on the axisymmetric-expansion line of the Lumley triangle, consistent with Pirozzoli et al. (2010), Fang et al. (2020) and the mixing-layer state (Pope 2000) (§5.1, App. A, Fig. 19). **Shown**
22. With $M_c=\Delta u/(a_u+a_l)$ ($\Delta u$ the maximum of $\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm m}$, sound speeds at the wall and the edge), $M_c$ falls from about 0.32 to 0; the ratios $R_{22,\mathrm m}/R_{11,\mathrm m}$ and $-R_{12,\mathrm m}/R_{11,\mathrm m}$ match free mixing-layer data, with no compressibility effect at this $M_c$ (§5.1, Eq. 5.1, Fig. 14). **Shown**
23. In the mixing-layer TKE budget production and dissipation dominate, with dissipation over production about $-0.8$ for $y/\delta=0.2$–1.0; advection carries TKE inward and downstream from $y/\delta\gtrsim0.5$, turbulent diffusion from $0.2\lesssim y/\delta\lesssim0.7$ to inner and outer regions; both matter at $x_1$–$x_3$ and decay downstream; pressure dilatation and mass diffusion are neglected at $M_\infty=2.28$ by citation (§5.2, Eq. 5.2, Fig. 15). **Shown** / **Stated** (neglect)
24. Split production $-R_{12,\alpha}\partial_y\langle u\rangle_\beta$ ($\alpha,\beta\in\{\mathrm c,\mathrm m\}$): at $x_1$ the mm and cm parts dominate; downstream mm decreases and cc increases; the part due to the mixing-layer shear stress persists far downstream in the outer region (§5.2, Eq. 5.3, Fig. 16). **Shown**
25. Pressure–strain: $\phi_{11}<0$ and $\phi_{22},\phi_{33}>0$ above $y/\delta\approx0.1$, with $\phi_{22}\approx\phi_{33}\approx-0.5\phi_{11}$, so the axisymmetric-expansion state is maintained dynamically (§5.2, Fig. 17d). **Shown**
26. A reduced integral model (Eqs. 6.1–6.8), started from the DNS at $x^*=2.0$ with self-similar profiles, approximately replicates the DNS decay of the mixing-layer velocity and TKE amplitudes; the mean shear decays rapidly, the TKE exponentially, and full TKE recovery may need more than 50 boundary-layer thicknesses (§6, Fig. 18; §7). **Shown** (within the DNS range; extrapolation beyond)
27. The recovery corresponds to the second and third stages of Ding & Smits (2021) for pipe flow behind a square bar; unlike the pipe, no Reynolds-stress oscillation appears, speculatively because the pipe wall restricts the mixing-layer growth (§6). **Stated**
28. The canonical/mixing-layer decomposition "has not been proposed or applied to study OSBLI flows yet" (§1). **Stated**

Method details: the canonical profile (4.1) is the reference buffer layer
for $y^+<30$, $2.55\ln y^++5$ up to $y/\delta=0.3$, plus
$2.55b_{\mathrm{wk}}(\cos0.3\pi-\cos\pi y/\delta)$ to the edge, with
$b_{\mathrm{wk}}=0.0005Re_\tau-0.411$ fitted at $x_r$ and $x_5$
(recovery postulated at $x_5$). The Renard–Deck terms (Eqs. 4.4–4.9, after
Fan, Li & Pirozzoli 2019) are $C_V$, $C_T$ the integrals of the viscous and
turbulent shear stress times $\partial_y\langle u\rangle_\rho$, $C_P$ the
pressure-gradient term and $C_G$ the mean advection and spatial growth,
all over $\tfrac12\rho_\delta u_\delta^3$; the c parts use the canonical
stress and velocity, the m parts are the remainders (cross terms
included).

# Printed slips

- **Table 1:** "friction Reynolds number $Re_{\tau r}=\rho_wu_\tau\theta_r/\mu_w$" = 201, the length printed being the momentum thickness. *(compiler check, not in the sources)* With $\theta_r/\delta_r=0.0902$, a $\theta$-based value of 201 corresponds to a $\delta_r$-based value of about 2230, against the paper's comparisons at "$Re_\tau\approx200$" (Figs. 5–6). [Yu et al. (2022)](/sources/yu-2022-e.md) (Eq. 2.1, Table 1) print the same table for the same station with $Re_\tau=\rho_wu_\tau\delta_0/\mu_w=201$, $\delta_0$ the reference thickness.
- **§1:** Pirozzoli & Grasso (2006) is cited for "the incoming Mach number $M_\infty=2.9$ and the shock angle of 33.2°", while the reference title reads "…interaction at M = 2.25" and §2 cites the same paper for the present parameters (2.28, 33.2°).
- **Eq. (3.1):** the upper limit of the van Driest integral is printed $\bar u$ while the text and figures use the Favre mean $\tilde u^+_{VD}$.
- **Typos:** "proceeding investigations" (§3, p. 8); "measuring aera" (§1).
- **References:** Jammalamadaka, Li & Jaberi (2014), Phys. Fluids 26 (5), printed with pages "87–108", the page range printed for Piponniau et al. (2009), J. Fluid Mech. 629; Babinsky & Harvey (2011), a book, printed with "vol. 32".
