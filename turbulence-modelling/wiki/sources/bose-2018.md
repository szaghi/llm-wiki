# Summary

Bose & Park (2018) review [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
in the narrow sense: the LES equations with a formal subgrid model are
solved everywhere, and only the wall (or a virtual wall) boundary condition
is changed to represent the unresolved inner layer;
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md), in which RANS is solved
near the wall, is excluded. The motivation is the
[grid-count scaling](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md) of Choi &
Moin (2012): $N_{\mathrm{DNS}}\sim Re_L^{37/14}$, $N_{\mathrm{wr}}\sim Re_L^{13/7}$,
$N_{\mathrm{wm}}\sim Re_L$.

Section 2 classifies wall models: wall-stress models from the algebraic
[law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) through the
thin-boundary-layer equations, in their
[equilibrium](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) (constant-stress,
mixing-length) and [non-equilibrium](/methods/nonequilibrium-wall-model.md)
forms with the reduced-$\kappa$ corrections of Wang & Moin, Kawai & Larsson
and Park & Moin; [pressure-gradient-scaled](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md)
and integral wall models; separation; the
[log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md); virtual-wall
conditions; the dynamic slip-wall model built on the
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md); control-based wall
models; and thermal wall models with the strong Reynolds analogy
([Morkovin's hypothesis](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md)). The authors'
own von Kármán integral analysis bounds the pressure-gradient part of the
modelled wall stress by the Clauser parameter times $h_{\mathrm{wm}}/\delta^*$,
so the matching height needed for equilibrium validity may depend on the
pressure gradient rather than being a fixed fraction of $\delta$.

Section 3 compares the cell counts of airfoil WMLES with Chapman's
estimates: most calculations need about $20^3$ points per $\delta^3$, a
Choi–Moin prefactor 3–5 times larger; the full non-equilibrium model costs
about twice the baseline LES. Section 4 surveys applications: very high
Reynolds numbers and [channels](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md),
roughness and transition in [boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md),
inner–outer reconstruction of near-wall fluctuations, the
[NACA 4412](/benchmarks/naca-4412-airfoil.md) and Aerospatiale-A near
stall, the [periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md), the
[wall-mounted hump](/benchmarks/nasa-wall-mounted-hump.md), the 30P/30N
airfoil, the NASA Common Research Model,
[shock/boundary-layer interactions](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md),
scramjet combustion, jet and trailing-edge noise and aero-optics. Section 5
lists the open problems: under-resolved laminar and transitional layers,
choosing $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ without knowing $\delta$, wall-stress alignment
in 3-D flows, mild-adverse-pressure-gradient separation, multiphysics needs,
and confidence without validation data.

# Key claims

The matching height is $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (the source's $y^*$), the Clauser
pressure-gradient parameter $\beta_{RC}$ (the source's $\Pi$; page-local
name as on the [adverse-pressure-gradient page](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md)).

1. Near-wall eddies are of spanwise size $O(10^2)$ wall units near the edge of the buffer layer and grow linearly with wall distance; as $Re_\tau$ grows they shrink relative to $\delta$, which makes wall resolution prohibitive (§1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
2. Grid points per differential wall area are $dN_{\mathrm{wr}}=\frac{n_y}{\Delta x_w^+\Delta z_w^+}\frac{\tau_w\,dx\,dz}{\rho\nu^2}$ for wall-resolved and $dN_{\mathrm{wm}}=\frac{n_xn_yn_z\,dx\,dz}{\delta^2}$ for wall-modelled LES, $n_i$ points per $\delta$ in direction $i$; integrated, $N_{\mathrm{DNS}}\sim Re_L^{37/14}$, $N_{\mathrm{wr}}\sim Re_L^{13/7}$, $N_{\mathrm{wm}}\sim Re_L$ (Choi & Moin 2012) (§1, Eqs. 1–2). **Stated**
3. With $\Delta/\delta\sim0.05$ the wall gradient cannot be computed by differencing and the inner fluctuation peak is subgrid; larger near-wall cells also reduce viscous stiffness, which helps explicit compressible solvers (§1). **Stated**
4. Keeping only the right-hand side of the thin-boundary-layer equations (Eq. 4) gives the equilibrium stress-balance model, a constant-total-stress layer; with the mixing-length eddy viscosity $\mu_t=\rho\kappa u_\tau y\,D(y)$, $D$ a damping function, it reproduces the linear sublayer and the log law (Wang & Moin 2002) (§2.1, Eq. 6). **Stated**
5. The ensemble-of-eddies reading of the inner-layer solution needs cells larger than a minimal channel, $\Delta x^+\gtrsim300$, $\Delta z^+\gtrsim100$; experiments (Nakayama et al. 2004) suggest this holds for $\Delta^+\gtrsim1800$ (§2.1). **Stated**
6. Data show a constant-stress layer for $y^+\lesssim100$ that then departs linearly in $y/\delta$, with $0.95<\tau/\tau_w<1$ for $y/\delta\lesssim0.03$ at high enough Reynolds number (§2.1, Fig. 3, fit $\tau/\tau_w=1-0.6y/\delta$ from Spalart 1988). **Stated**
7. Integrating the thin-boundary-layer equations with the inviscid outer momentum equation gives $\tau_w/\rho$ as the stress at $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ plus unsteady, pressure-gradient, convective and transpiration integrals (Eq. 7); the pressure-gradient term, normalized by $\tau_w$, is bounded by $\beta_{RC}\,h_{\mathrm{wm}}/\delta^*$ (Eq. 8). Hence pressure-gradient effects on the modelled stress can be made negligible by controlling $h_{\mathrm{wm}}/\delta^*$, and the $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ needed for equilibrium validity may depend on $\beta_{RC}$, unlike recommendations such as $h_{\mathrm{wm}}\approx0.1$–$0.2\delta$ (Larsson et al. 2016); the argument fails near separation, where $\tau_w\to0$ (§2.1, Eqs. 7–8). **Derived**
8. When the convective terms are kept, the unsteady inner-layer solution resolves part of the Reynolds stress, so its eddy viscosity must be reduced to avoid overpredicting skin friction (Wang & Moin 2002, Eq. 9); Kawai & Larsson (2013) found Eq. 9 potentially inaccurate at high Reynolds number and proposed a linear blending with an $O(1)$ coefficient; Park & Moin (2014) found that coefficient flow- and mesh-dependent and proposed the dynamic correction of Eq. 10 (§2.1). **Stated**
9. Hickel et al. (2012) argued that including the pressure gradient without the convective and unsteady terms is inconsistent with the outer momentum equation (the Bernoulli limit); the mixed scaling of Duprat et al. (2011) may still improve the wall stress (§2.1). **Stated**
10. Separation ($u_\tau\to0$) is not handled reliably by presumed profiles or eddy-viscosity closures, whose $\mu_t\to0$ as $u_\tau\to0$; earlier studies imposed no-slip in regions known to separate or at corners; the treatment of separation points "remains a theoretical challenge" (§2.1). **Stated**
11. Log-layer mismatch: matching at the first cell shifts the profile (Fig. 4); for a log profile an $n$th-order scheme gives at $y=d\,\Delta y$ the discrete shear $\frac{d\langle u\rangle}{dy}\big(1+c_nn!/d^n\big)$, whose remainder is large for $d=1/2$ or 1 and does not vanish as $n$ grows (Wu & Meyers 2013); Kawai & Larsson (2012) found the Reynolds shear stress converged for $\Delta y/h_{\mathrm{wm}}\lesssim0.3$ (the "third grid point") but advocate $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ as a fraction of $\delta$ (§2.1, Eq. 13). **Stated**
12. An algebraic model gives $\langle\tau_w\rangle\propto\langle U_{\mathrm{LES}}^2\rangle$ where the law of the wall gives $\propto\langle U_{\mathrm{LES}}\rangle^2$, so the modelled stress is too large and filtering the input relieves the mismatch (Bou-Zeid et al. 2004); Yang et al. (2017) attribute the mismatch instead to an unphysically high correlation between wall stress and first-cell LES data, which unifies the filtering and off-wall-matching remedies but does not explain negative mismatch (§2.1). **Stated**
13. Imposing unfiltered boundary conditions on filtered fields is valid only when the near-wall filter width is small against the gradient length (Ghosal & Moin 1995), which WMLES violates (§2.3). **Stated**
14. The slip-wall Robin condition $\bar u_i-C\Delta_w\,\partial\bar u_i/\partial n=0$ follows from a differential filter whose regularization length vanishes at the boundary; it needs no assumption on the velocity profile, stress alignment or equilibrium, and has no empirical coefficient when the slip length $C\Delta_w$ is computed dynamically; its accuracy is limited by the robustness of the dynamic procedure, which is sensitive to the SGS model and the numerics (§2.3, Eqs. 15–16). **Stated**
15. The slip wall admits transpiration and so a finite wall Reynolds stress, which has been shown to reduce the overprediction of the near-wall streamwise intensity; Yang et al. (2016) showed its wall stress can be equivalent to an equilibrium wall-stress model and functionally similar to virtual-wall conditions (§2.3). **Stated**
16. Control-based wall models (Templeton et al. 2008) reproduce channel flow up to $Re_\tau=20\,000$; the computed streamwise stress correlates best with the LES fluctuations at the second wall-normal point, and with $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ at the wall-adjacent cell, minimizing the deviation from the target profile necessarily overpredicts near-wall intensities (§2.4). **Stated**
17. The strong Reynolds analogy $C_h=C_f/2$ ($C_h$ the Stanton number) holds experimentally within less than 30% for $M<10$, weakly dependent on $Re_\theta=10^3$–$10^5$, confirmed by DNS with wall heating; it fails near separation, where skin friction vanishes but heat transfer does not, and law-of-the-wall errors in adverse pressure gradient are amplified in temperature and heat flux (§2.5). **Stated**
18. In high-speed WMLES the subgrid viscous-work term must be kept in the LES total-energy equation, so that the outer LES gives the wall model a correct thermal condition; it is sometimes neglected (§2.5). **Stated**
19. Published airfoil WMLES cell counts (scaled to span/chord aspect ratio 4) suggest that most calculations need outer-layer resolution $n_xn_yn_z\gtrsim20^3$, which raises the Choi–Moin prefactor by a factor of 3–5; the scatter is partly due to keeping $\delta^3/(\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)$ fixed when $\delta=\delta(x)$ (§3, Fig. 5). **Shown** (compilation of published cell counts)
20. Wall-model cost is often small against the flow solution: the full non-equilibrium model costs about a factor of two over the baseline LES, and load imbalance at large processor counts can matter more (§3). **Stated**
21. The claim that laminar regions dominate cost is overly pessimistic: it presumes isotropic resolution, and cell aspect ratios $O(10)$ would cut the prefactor by orders of magnitude; Slotnick et al.'s (2014) petascale estimate for $L_z/c=1$, $Re_c\sim10^6$ is inconsistent with the cost of recent WMLES; relaxing the laminar-region resolution presupposes that the LES captures transition (§3). **Stated**
22. WMLES has reached $Re_\tau\sim10^6$–$10^8$ in channels and $10^4\lesssim Re_\theta\lesssim10^{12}$ in boundary layers; whether it captures the outer peak of the streamwise fluctuations is unclear, as that peak "is likely also unresolved" with the Choi–Moin resolution $\delta/\Delta x_i\sim10$ (§4.1). **Stated**
23. Transition with equilibrium-type wall models requires suppressing the turbulent wall stress upstream of transition; the sensor of Bodart & Larsson (2012) switches the model off below a threshold of the fluctuation intensity over $u_\tau$, with well predicted transition locations (Fig. 6a); the slip-wall model becomes laminar through the dynamic procedure ($C\Delta_w\to0$) (§4.1). **Stated**
24. With wall-parallel resolution $\Delta x_t/\delta\lesssim0.05$, WMLES recovers wall-pressure fluctuations within a few percent of DNS, but wall-shear-stress fluctuations remain underpredicted, consistent with the wall-normal pressure gradient being zero in the model (Park & Moin 2016b) (§4.2). **Stated**
25. NACA 4412 at $Re_c=1.6\times10^6$ near stall: equilibrium, non-equilibrium and dynamic slip-wall models all reproduce the trailing-edge pressure plateau; for the Aerospatiale-A at $Re_c=2.1\times10^6$ algebraic, equilibrium and Kawai–Larsson models differ in the trailing-edge separation, showing the sensitivity of mild-adverse-pressure-gradient separation to the wall model (§4.3, Fig. 7a). **Stated**
26. Periodic hill ($Re_h=10\,595$): slip, equilibrium and non-equilibrium models agree favourably with DNS, the non-equilibrium one slightly better near separation, the differences possibly due more to the outer LES numerics; wall-mounted hump (inflow $Re_\tau\approx2000$): the full non-equilibrium model outperforms equilibrium models in the bubble and recovery (Avdis et al. 2009, Park 2017) (§4.3). **Stated**
27. 30P/30N ($Re_c=9\times10^6$, equilibrium model): the maximum lift is captured but the stall angle is about 23° against about 21° in experiment; NASA Common Research Model ($Re_c=5\times10^6$, aspect ratio 9, $M=0.85$, 4°, Werner–Wengle model): shock location, $C_p$, lift and drag agree with experiment on a grid with $\delta/\Delta\sim10$ (§4.3). **Stated**
28. Supersonic duct WMLES with an equilibrium model overpredicts the secondary corner flow and corner shear stress, suggesting that the locally 1-D equilibrium approximation is inadequate for secondary flows; a compression ramp shows a delayed initial pressure rise (§4.4). **Stated**
29. Scaling incompressible closures to variable inertia works for small turbulent Mach number ($M_t\lesssim0.2$, Lele 1994); thermal predictions may fail where the strong Reynolds analogy fails, with significant stagnation-temperature variation (§4.4). **Stated**
30. Wall modelling is needed to predict the viscous heating that stabilizes combustion in the HIFiRE scramjet; for a $M=0.9$, $Re_j=10^6$ jet the wall-modelled tripped nozzle boundary layer gives far-field OASPL within 2 dB against about 5 dB for a quasi-laminar layer; NACA 0012 trailing-edge noise ($Re_c=1.5\times10^6$) is within 3 dB but used $\Delta y^+\lesssim10$ (§4.5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (9):** printed
  $\kappa_{wm}=\nu_{SGS}(y^*)/\{y^{*+}[1-\exp(-y^{*+}/A^+)]^2\}$.
  *(compiler check, not in the sources)* The numerator is a kinematic
  viscosity and the denominator is dimensionless, while $\kappa$ is
  dimensionless; the ratio $\nu_{SGS}/\nu$ makes it consistent.
- **Inviscid outer relation (§2.1, before Eq. 7):** printed
  "$\frac1\rho\frac{dp}{dx}=U_\infty\frac{dU_\infty}{dx}$". *(compiler
  check, not in the sources)* The steady inviscid momentum equation gives
  $\frac1\rho\frac{dp}{dx}=-U_\infty\frac{dU_\infty}{dx}$; integrating Eq.
  (4) from the wall to $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ with continuity and the minus sign
  reproduces Eq. (7) as printed, so the slip is confined to the inline
  relation. The paper does not define $\Pi$ explicitly, so the sign of the
  middle expression of Eq. (8) relative to $\Pi$ is not fixed by the paper.
- **Fig. 5:** the caption reads "lines denote Chapman's cost estimates
  using $n_xn_yn_z=10^3$ (green) and $20^3$ (blue)", while the in-figure
  labels name the blue curve "Cost $n_xn_yn_z=10^3$" and the green one
  "Cost $n_xn_yn_z=20^3$".
- **References:** "Balaras E, Benocci C, Piomelli P. 1996" (elsewhere
  "Piomelli U"); "Piomelli U, Balaras AE. 2002" (elsewhere "Balaras E");
  "Duprat C, Balarac G, Mtais O, Congedo PM, Brugire O. 2011", with the
  accented letters dropped.
