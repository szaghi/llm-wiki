# Summary

Lv & Ekaterinaris (2023) review high-order discontinuous schemes —
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md)
(including hybridized and embedded DG), [flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md)
and the [spectral difference method](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md) —
and the algorithms that carry them to multiphase, multicomponent and
reacting flows. Turbulence enters only at the edges; the bundle uses the
review for its numerics. The core section treats bound preservation
(positivity of density and pressure, a minimum entropy, mass fractions in
$[0,1]$) by sequential rescaling towards the element mean, which the
authors distinguish from limiters; a survey of 21 DG limiters (minmod,
moment, vertex-based, [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)/HWENO);
[artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
and its [sensors](/definitions/shock-sensor.md); a posteriori
[subcell finite-volume](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md)
correction; and a nonlinear filter equivalent to a high-order dissipative
filter ([explicit modal filtering](/methods/explicit-modal-filtering.md)).
For [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) the authors warn that
limiters and artificial viscosity can kill small scales
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)) and that
under-resolved turbulence can be mistaken for shocks; they hold limiting
"too intrusive" and report a shift to artificial viscosity or filtered
forms such as [spectral vanishing viscosity](/methods/spectral-vanishing-viscosity.md),
citing [implicit-LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) studies of the
[NASA CRM](/benchmarks/nasa-crm-high-lift.md).

A second theme is [pressure equilibrium](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md)
with variable thermodynamic properties: fully conservative schemes
oscillate at interfaces, and the remedies — $\gamma$-advection, a pressure
evolution equation, double flux (oscillation-free but not energy
conserving, hybridized at shocks), and the conservative $L^2$-projected
equation of state — are weighed. The review further covers level-set
interface capturing, Lagrangian and ALE tracking under the
[geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md),
[mesh adaptation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md) (with a passing mention of
[Rayleigh–Taylor](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-mixing.md) mixing), and
Euler–Lagrange particles on curved elements, with illustrations from the
authors' work including DG LES of a bluff-body flame with the
[thickened flame model](/methods/thickened-flame-model.md) and of a jet
flame with [flamelet/progress-variable chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md).
The evidence is almost entirely cited; the illustrations are qualitative.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $s=\log(p/\rho^\gamma)$ the entropy and $s_{\min}$ its
local minimum; $\mathscr L_1$, $\mathscr L_2$, $\mathscr L_3$ the rescaling
operators and $\varepsilon_1$, $\varepsilon_2$, $\varepsilon_3$ their
factors; $\{U\}_e$ the element mean (registry §11); $R_e$ the element
residual; $g_L$, $g_R$ the flux-reconstruction correction functions; the
reference element is $\xi\in[0,1]$.

1. Second-order finite-volume solvers struggle to converge and stay robust where RANS averaging fails (separated, large-scale vortical flows), attributed to the "inherent dissipation or dispersion error characteristics of second-order schemes" (§1, citing [13]). **Stated**
2. High-order discontinuous schemes differ from classical high-order FD/FV in their finite-element framework with compact stencils and localized operators on complex geometry (§1). **Stated**
3. FV/FD algorithms for complex fluids rely on point- or cell-wise operators, inconsistent with the multiple internal degrees of freedom and interface jumps of discontinuous schemes (§1). **Stated**
4. Nodal DG with equispaced nodes is unsuitable at very high order because the mass-matrix conditioning deteriorates; well-chosen nodes avoid it (§3.1, citing [48], [5]). **Stated**
5. HDG/EDG couple only face (hybrid) variables globally, reducing memory and cost for steady and implicit problems; EDG's continuity at face nodes reduces the unknowns further (§3.4, Eq. 28, Fig. 2). **Stated**
6. A robust scheme must enforce $\rho>0$, $p>0$, $s>s_{\min}$, $Y\in[0,1]$ at every quadrature or flux point of the residual (Eq. 29); high-order discrete forms do not satisfy this automatically (§4.1). **Stated**
7. Bound preservation is (i) rescaling of the element polynomial towards $\{U\}_e$ where a point value violates a constraint, $\mathscr L=\mathscr L_3\mathscr L_2\mathscr L_1$ (Eqs. 30–35), and (ii) time marching under a CFL condition that keeps the mean admissible (Eq. 36), which needs an entropy-stable Riemann flux (§4.1, citing [71, 72, 76]). **Stated** (summary of results proved in the cited works)
8. The sufficient conditions of [72, 75] were limited to 2-D simplex elements; Lv & Ihme's entropy-bounded DG [76] covers multi-dimensional, arbitrary and curved elements, with "rather conservative" CFL bounds, so in practice a larger CFL is ramped down by trial (§4.1). **Stated**
9. Bound-preserving schemes prevent non-physical states and blow-up but not oscillations; shock capturing is still needed (§4.1). **Stated**
10. Moment limiters are shown only up to $N=2$; vertex-based limiters suit linear representations, Park & Kim's [91] reaches $N=3$ on unstructured meshes, and Li et al.'s p-weighted WENO limiter [96] $N=5$ (§4.2, Table 1). **Stated**
11. Multiphase and multicomponent applications adopt the low-complexity limiters; minmod suppresses spurious interface velocity but over-diffuses the interface [103], and WENO limiters applied blindly produce pressure oscillations with variable thermodynamics [32, 106] (§4.2). **Stated**
12. Johnson's artificial viscosity scales as $h^2|R_e|$, but a large residual does not always mark a discontinuity; Persson & Peraire's sensor-based localized viscosity scales as $h/N$ and gives subcell shock resolution (§4.2, [110, 113]). **Stated**
13. In LES the dissipation of limiters and artificial viscosity can smear smooth extrema and kill small scales, and under-resolved turbulence can be mistaken for shocks; in Wang & Rahmani's limiter-assisted FR LES of the NASA CRM [142] "only 0.3% of elements have the limiter activated" (§4.2). **Stated**
14. Limiting remains "too intrusive for high-order solutions" even with a good sensor, so artificial viscosity or filtered forms [143] have become more popular for LES with discontinuous schemes [144–147] (§4.2). **Stated**
15. "Since LES typically requires very long temporal integration, the parameter-tuning can be easily done during the course of LES run" (§4.2). **Stated** (opinion)
16. The a posteriori subcell correction (MOOD [136], extended to DG [137–139]) removes the ad hoc modification of troubled elements, but troubled-cell detection, with problem-dependent parameters, remains (§4.2). **Stated**
17. The subcell-resolution approach of [140] is equivalent to a high-order dissipative filter in the finite-element context (§4.2). **Stated**
18. With variable thermodynamic properties fully conservative schemes produce spurious pressure oscillations at interfaces and contacts: numerically triggered thermal non-equilibrium changes internal energy and properties and breaks mechanical equilibrium (§4.3). **Stated** (qualitative mechanism)
19. Double flux is oscillation-free but relaxes energy conservation where thermal properties vary, acceptable at low Mach but giving wrong shock speeds at supersonic conditions; Lv & Ihme's sensor-based hybrid flux [119] reverts to the conservative flux at shocks and predicts correct detonation speeds (§4.3). **Stated**
20. Double flux relies on an element-wise constant thermal property, "a rather intrusive treatment in conflict with the core feature of discontinuous schemes–multiple internal degrees of freedom" (§4.3). **Stated**
21. The conservative $L^2$-projection of the equation of state of Franchina et al. [157], with artificial viscosity, keeps pressure oscillations within "0.1%~1% relatively"; Johnson & Kercher [158] extended it to thermally perfect gases (§4.3). **Stated**
22. Zalesak's disk: DG with $N=3$ without re-initialization preserves the interface topology better than WENO5 with re-initialization, more so on coarse meshes (§4.4, Fig. 5). **Shown** (qualitative)
23. Most level-set capabilities of discontinuous schemes (Table 2) were developed for prescribed or incompressible flow, except [101, 173]; non-PDE re-initialization is promising but lacks benchmark comparison (§4.4). **Stated**
24. High-order DG on moving meshes needs an ALE form, and a space–time formulation satisfies the geometric conservation law automatically when all space–time integrals are exact (§4.5, citing [190, 191]). **Stated**
25. A delta-function point-particle source causes spurious oscillations in the DG carrier phase, cured by Ching & Ihme's finite-size anisotropic polynomial kernel [201] (§4.7). **Stated**
26. Bluff-body premixed-flame DG LES ($N=3$, 10,000 hexahedra): "the reaction zone is captured by only one or two elements", whereas second-order schemes need "more than ten cells"; mean velocities agree with experiment (§6.3, Fig. 14, from [220]). **Shown** (the second-order comparison is stated)
27. DME/air jet-flame DG LES (12,000 elements, $N=2$, flamelet/progress-variable model): mean temperature, mixture fraction and CO in "excellent agreement" with measurements; the near-nozzle discrepancy is attributed to the neglect of heat transfer in the flamelet model (§6.3, Figs. 15–16, from [221]). **Shown**
28. Cellular H$_2$/O$_2$/Ar detonation with $N=2$ and 14 elements per induction length reproduces the triple-point structure, cell size and geometry in "excellent quantitative agreement" with experiment and earlier computations (§6.1, Fig. 11, [119]). **Shown** (from cited work)
29. Stabilization techniques struggle to generalize to high order, unstructured meshes and 3-D; artificial viscosity "continues to be problematic due to the dependence on problem-specific parameters"; it is unclear whether interface capturing or tracking is preferable; each physical effect adds algorithmic components (§7). **Stated**
