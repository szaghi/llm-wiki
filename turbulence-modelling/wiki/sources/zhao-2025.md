# Summary

Zhao et al. (2025) build LESnets, a physics-informed neural operator that
learns the coarse-grid time advance of three-dimensional incompressible
turbulence; it is one of the [neural-operator surrogates of LES](/methods/neural-operator-les-surrogate.md).
The network maps one filtered velocity field to the next ten frames and is
trained only on the residual of the [filtered Navier–Stokes equations](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md)
closed by the [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md). No labelled
time series enter the loss. The residual is evaluated pseudo-spectrally on the
periodic grid and the time derivative by central differences across the output
frames. Two backbones are used, the Fourier neural operator (LESnets) and the
implicit Fourier neural operator (LESnets-I). Unlike the original
physics-informed neural operator, and unlike the residual losses of
[physics-informed neural networks](/methods/physics-informed-neural-network.md),
the initial field is an input, not a loss term, so the model can be rolled out
autoregressively.

Two test cases are used: decaying [isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
at $Re_\lambda\approx60$ ([DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
$128^3$ filtered to $32^3$) and a [temporally developing mixing layer](/benchmarks/temporal-mixing-layer.md).
Over 250 learning steps of rollout ([a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md))
LESnets track Smagorinsky LES closely, while the data-driven Fourier neural
operator, best in test loss, diverges in decaying turbulence after about two
turnover times. LESnets underpredict and data-driven models overpredict the
r.m.s. quantities. A hybrid loss learns $C_s$ during training from one
11-frame trajectory, by a gradient step on the coefficient, not by the
[ensemble Kalman calibration](/methods/ensemble-kalman-closure-calibration.md)
that motivates it. It recovers $C_s\approx0.1$ from Smagorinsky-LES data and
$C_s\approx0.19$ from filtered DNS, and both give comparable rollouts. Inference
is about 30 (isotropic turbulence) and 40 (mixing layer) times faster than LES
on the same grid, GPU against CPU and with training excluded. The network
replaces the solver, not the closure, so its accuracy is bounded by the
Smagorinsky LES it emulates, in contrast with [neural-network SGS models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $w_{\mathrm{data}}$ and $w_C$ are the weights of the data
loss and of the coefficient loss (the source's $\gamma$ and $\gamma'$);
$n_L$, $d_v$ and $k_{\max}$ are the number of Fourier layers, the channel width
and the number of retained Fourier modes; $T_E$ is the large-eddy turnover
time.

1. LESnets encode the filtered equations (Eqs. 3–4) with the Smagorinsky closure (Eq. 5) into FNO and IFNO and train with the PDE loss only, without labelled data; the authors state that, to their knowledge, no earlier study used the LES equations as the PDE constraint of a physics-informed neural operator for 3D turbulence (§2.5, §6). **Stated**
2. The initial field is a known input, not a learned quantity; training uses the pair $\bar u(t)\mapsto\bar u(t+1),\dots,\bar u(t+10)$ of one time node, and prediction feeds the last output back, so one initial field per case suffices for training and prediction (§2.5). **Stated** (design)
3. The original PINO with an initial-condition loss (Eq. A1, weight 0.1 to 10) diverges in iterative a posteriori prediction of decaying isotropic turbulence; for weights 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5 it fails at the first iteration, and oscillations are reduced but not removed at 2 and 5 (Appendix A, Fig. A1). **Shown**
4. The original PINO of Li et al. on 2D turbulence has 6% relative $L_2$ error at $T=0.125$ but almost 74% at $T=0.5$ (§2.4). **Stated** (cited)
5. Decaying-turbulence minimum losses (Table 2): FNO data 0.019680 / test 0.029363; IFNO 0.021713 / 0.031973; LESnets PDE 0.062677 / test 0.059160; LESnets-I 0.030308 / 0.057906. The PDE loss reaches order $10^2$ early in training while the data loss stays below 1 (§3.1). **Shown**
6. Lower test loss does not mean a better rollout: FNO, best in test loss, deviates in r.m.s. velocity and vorticity at the 10th iteration ($t\approx2T_E$) and fails on the kinetic energy at $t\approx T_E$, while LESnets stay stable and close to filtered DNS over $5T_E$ (§3.1, Figs. 6–7; §5.4). **Shown**
7. Data-driven IFNO overestimates r.m.s. velocity, r.m.s. vorticity, kinetic energy and dissipation in decaying turbulence; LESnets underestimate them and stay close to Smagorinsky LES (§3.1, Figs. 6–8). **Shown**
8. The implicit backbone improves data-driven training but gives "a negligible enhancement" for LESnets; implicit models converge faster in training (§3.1, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
9. LESnets reproduce the second- and fourth-order structure functions of Smagorinsky LES for $r/\Delta=2$ to 8 and PDFs of normalized velocity increments "nearly identical" to filtered DNS; the FNO tails lengthen at $t\approx4T_E$ (§3.1, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
10. In the temporal mixing layer FNO does not diverge; data-driven models predict the momentum thickness better than LES; LESnets are comparable to Smagorinsky LES; the test error of LESnets exceeds that of the data-driven models (§3.2, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
11. Learning $C_s$ from one Smagorinsky-LES trajectory (generated with $C_s^2=0.01$): $w_C=0.5$, 5, 25, 50, 100 give $C_s=0.1797$, 0.1123, 0.0998, 0.0998, 0.0982; from filtered-DNS data 0.1983, 0.1892, 0.1866, 0.1859, 0.1856 (Table 4, learning rate $10^{-5}$). **Shown**
12. At $w_C=50$, learning rates $10^{-3}$, $10^{-4}$, $10^{-5}$, $10^{-6}$ give $C_s=0.0987$, 0.0989, 0.0988, 0.0661 (Smagorinsky data) and 0.1868, 0.1868, 0.1859, 0.0680 (filtered DNS) (Table 4). **Shown**
13. Although the coefficient learned from filtered DNS ($\approx0.19$) differs from 0.1, the rollouts are comparable to the Smagorinsky-data case; the influence of $w_C$ is "relatively minor"; best overall $w_C=50$, best learning rate $10^{-5}$ (Smagorinsky data) and $10^{-6}$ (filtered DNS) (§4.1–4.2, Figs. 19–23). **Shown**
14. The learned coefficient is not always unique when only one 11-frame instance is added, but different learned values give similar results (§4.2). **Shown**
15. Decaying-turbulence cost (Table 5; NVIDIA A100 40 GB for the networks, Intel Xeon Gold 6148 cluster for LES): training 0.39 / 2.17 / 0.41 / 2.20 s per epoch (FNO / IFNO / LESnets / LESnets-I); parameters 1061.7, 622.1, 1061.7, 622.1 (column unit $\times10^6$, as printed); GPU memory 38.2, 37.7, 38.0, 37.6 GB; inference for 250 steps (5000 DNS steps), averaged over 100 cases: LES 45.62 s, FNO 1.51, IFNO 11.80, LESnets 1.51, LESnets-I 11.80 s; "LESnets is 30 times faster than LES simulations" (§5.1). **Shown**
16. Mixing-layer cost (Table 6; A100 80 GB): training 0.69 / 3.95 / 0.70 / 3.96 s per epoch; memory 53.5 / 77.4 / 53.5 / 77.4 GB; inference LES 126.79 s, FNO 3.18, IFNO 18.32, LESnets 3.18, LESnets-I 18.32 s; LESnets cost "1/40 of the cost of the LES" (§5.1). **Shown**
17. Counting training time, LESnets are less efficient than traditional LES; the efficiency pays off for many inference tasks with a trained model (§5.1). **Stated**
18. Sensitivity (decaying turbulence, Table 7, Fig. 24): prediction is "significantly weakened" for $n_L<6$; $d_v<80$ could impair capacity; $k_{\max}\le8$ may be insufficient; $n_L=6$, $d_v=80$, $k_{\max}=12$ give converged results. **Shown**
19. With 2000 initial fields the test $L_2$ loss drops to about 0.059 but a posteriori r.m.s. velocity and spectra are too high; converged results need 3000 fields, comparable to 5000 (§5.3, Fig. 25). **Shown**
20. The combined loss $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{PDE}}+w_{\mathrm{data}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}$, $w_{\mathrm{data}}$ from 0.01 to 100, moves the results between FNO and LESnets; $w_{\mathrm{data}}=0.1$ is closest to the truth (§5.4, Figs. 26–27). **Shown** (see the printed slip on the direction)
21. LESnets accuracy is bounded by the underlying SGS model and neural-operator architecture; non-uniform grids and complex boundaries need other discretizations of the loss (§6). **Stated**

The PDE loss (Eq. 17), in registry notation with the forcing set to zero:
$$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{PDE}}=\big\|\partial_i\bar u_i\big\|^2+\big\|\partial_t\bar u_i+\bar u_j\partial_j\bar u_i+\partial_i\bar p-\nu\partial_j\partial_j\bar u_i+\partial_j\tau^d_{ij}\big\|^2,\qquad \tau^d_{ij}=-2(C_s\Delta)^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij},$$
norms over the output frames and the periodic domain; the coefficient loss
$\mathcal L_{C}$ (Eq. 25) is the same momentum residual evaluated on the one
added trajectory, and it updates only $C_s$ (second Adam optimizer), with
$\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{PDE}}+w_C\mathcal L_C$ (Eq. 26).

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (5), p. 3:** "$\bar S_{ij}=\frac12(\partial\bar u_i/\bar x_j+\partial\bar u_j/\bar x_i)$": the denominators lack $\partial$ and put a bar on $x$.
- **§3.2, p. 21:** "where Q is defined by Eq. (19)"; $Q$ is defined in Eq. (18), Eq. (19) is the initial energy spectrum.
- **Eqs. (17), (25) and Algorithm 1 line 17** write "$-\nabla\tau$" ("$-ik\hat\tau$") for the SGS term, which Eq. (4) writes as the divergence $-\partial\tau_{ij}/\partial x_j$.
- **Algorithm 1 line 19, p. 8:** "$\mathcal L_2=\partial_tu+\mathcal F^{-1}(\nu k^2\hat u)-\mathcal F^{-1}(\hat R)+\mathcal F^{-1}(\hat p)$" adds the scalar pressure, defined on line 18 as $\hat p=-\frac1{k^2}ik\cdot\hat R$, to a vector residual instead of its gradient; Algorithm 2 repeats the form.
- **Eq. (17) against Algorithm 1 line 20:** the squared $L^2$ norms of Eq. (17) become "$\sum_{n=2}^{T-1}\|\mathcal L_1+\mathcal L_2\|$", unsquared and adding the scalar continuity residual to the vector momentum residual.
- **Algorithm 1 line 10:** superscript $N$ for $N_1$; line 6 prints the learning rate as "$1e^{-3}$".
- **§5.4, p. 29:** "As $\gamma$ decreases from $\gamma=100$ to $\gamma=0.01$, the loss value shifts from that of LESnets to that of FNO", while the same section says the results "transition from FNO to LESnets when $\gamma$ decreases from $\gamma=100$ to $\gamma=0.01$"; the two sentences state opposite directions.
