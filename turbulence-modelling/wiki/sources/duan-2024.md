# Summary

Duan & Wang (2024) address a robustness problem of high-order
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md): high-order schemes
embed little [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md),
so severely under-resolved [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)
needs stabilization, and stabilizing by solution limiting gives results that
depend on how much limiting is applied (channel at $Re_\tau=5200$, Fig. 2).
The authors instead add an explicit eddy-viscosity model, the
[Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md), [WALE](/methods/wale-model.md)
or [Vreman](/methods/vreman-model.md) model, in their
[flux-reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md) (FR/CPR) solver
hpMusic, with an element-averaged $\nu_t$ and zero common interface flux of
the SGS stress. The coefficients are kept and the filter width of
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) is recalibrated: d1
$\Delta=V^{1/3}$, d3 $\Delta=V^{1/3}/(N+1)$, and the new intermediate d2
$\Delta=V^{1/3}/\sqrt{N+1}$, $V$ the element volume and $N$ the polynomial
degree.

On the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) the SGS
models dissipate far more than the Riemann solver, WALE least, Vreman
next, Smagorinsky most; with the viscous case at $Re=1600$ implicit LES
nearly reproduces the DNS enstrophy. In decaying
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
(Comte-Bellot–Corrsin) d3 adds very little dissipation, d1 too much, d2 a
balance. In wall-resolved [channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
at $Re_\tau=180$ implicit LES agrees best with DNS and Smagorinsky fails at
every width. In wall-modelled channel flow at $Re_\tau=5200$ (and 544), with
an [equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) fed from the
second element off the wall, implicit LES is unstable; Vreman is
consistent from $N=2$ to 5 (d1 too dissipative, d3 too little, d2 best),
while WALE is best with d1 at $N=2$–4 and with d2 at $N=5$, where its trend
with the width reverses. The recommendation is Vreman with d2 for
high-order WMLES. Stabilization alternatives (limiting, filtering,
over-integration, [jump penalties](/methods/jump-penalty-stabilization.md)
and other dissipative fluxes, kinetic-energy- and entropy-preserving
schemes) are listed in the introduction only. Evidence is numerical, by
comparison of profiles and spectra with DNS and experiment; no error norms
are tabulated.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $V$ the element volume; d1, d2, d3 the three
filter-width choices of Eqs. (28)–(30); $C_w$, $C_v$ the WALE and Vreman
coefficients.

1. Most high-order LES so far are wall-resolved implicit LES; high-order implicit LES in severely under-resolved LES (WMLES) "often runs into robustness and accuracy issues due to the low dissipation embedded in these methods" (Abstract, §1). **Stated**
2. In a comparison of central finite differences, upwind-biased finite differences, filtered compact differences and DG on a broadband Burgers spectrum (ref. [10]), filtered compact differences and DG resolve best and central differences worst, with the largest high-wavenumber energy pile-up; all high-order schemes show some pile-up (§1, Fig. 1). **Stated** (figure reproduced from [10])
3. In WMLES of channel flow at $Re_\tau=5200$ with a fourth-order discontinuous method, too little damping of the small scales (solution limiting) over-predicts the wall friction and under-predicts the velocity, too much does the reverse; the right amount is hard to fix for complex configurations (§1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
4. Many studies find SGS models detrimental in high-order wall-resolved LES because of excessive damping; the models introduce "physically un-correlated dissipation" (§1, refs. [50, 51]). **Stated**
5. Projecting the solution onto the piecewise-polynomial space is a spatial filter, so FR/CPR without an SGS model is implicit LES; an SGS model adds extra dissipative fluxes (§3). **Stated**
6. The eddy viscosity is made element-wise constant, the quadrature-weighted element average $\nu_t=V^{-1}\sum_q w_q\nu_{t,q}$, to make the model less oscillatory (§3, Eq. 26). **Stated** (implementation choice)
7. The common interface flux of the SGS stress is set to zero at all element interfaces "to guarantee the conservation and minimize data communication", justified by the poor correlation of modelled with physical SGS stresses, whose role is then only to dissipate energy (§3, Eq. 27). **Stated**
8. Three filter widths: d1 $\Delta=V^{1/3}$, d3 $\Delta=V^{1/3}/(N+1)$, and the new d2 $\Delta=V^{1/3}/\sqrt{N+1}$; d3 is expected to under-dissipate because high-order methods are much less dissipative than the second-order methods on which the SGS models were calibrated (§3, Eqs. 28–30). **Stated** (contribution: d2)
9. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex on $16^3$ elements at $N=4$: all three models at every width heavily dissipate the eddies compared with implicit LES; the SGS dissipation is much larger than that of the Riemann solver; d2 and d3 are clearly less dissipative than d1 (§5.1, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
10. Dissipation ranking: WALE least, then Vreman, then Smagorinsky (inviscid and viscous Taylor–Green vortex; §5.1, Figs. 5–7; Conclusions). **Shown**
11. Viscous Taylor–Green vortex at $Re=1600$, $64^3$ elements, $N=4$: the implicit-LES enstrophy is almost on top of the DNS; the SGS models degrade the enstrophy significantly with d1 and d2, much less with d3 (§5.2 lead-in, Fig. 7). **Shown**
12. Decaying isotropic turbulence on $32^3$ cells: lower $N$ shows more high-wavenumber pile-up than the experiment, attributed to aliasing ("we believe"); at $N=4,5$ the low-wavenumber spectra agree with the experiment; implicit-LES spectra differ most from the measurements at high wavenumbers (§5.2, Fig. 8). **Shown** / **Stated** (aliasing)
13. In isotropic turbulence WALE and Vreman give similar spectra, Vreman slightly more damping; d3 adds very little dissipation (spectrum close to implicit LES), d1 too much (resolved wavenumber range considerably reduced), d2 a good balance (§5.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
14. Channel flow at $Re_\tau=180$, wall-resolved, $N=3$: implicit LES agrees very well with DNS; Smagorinsky performs very poorly at all three widths because of its large near-wall eddy viscosity; WALE and Vreman match DNS for $y^+<10$, perform poorly in the buffer and log regions with d1, and agree well over the whole layer with d2 and d3, WALE slightly better (§5.3, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
15. At $Re_\tau=180$ "the smaller the eddy viscosity is, the better the agreement is with DNS because of the fine mesh resolution" (§5.3). **Shown**
16. Wall-modelled channel at $Re_\tau=5200$ on $60\times16\times40$ elements: implicit LES was not stable (§5.4). **Shown** (outcome stated, no figure)
17. In the $Re_\tau=5200$ WMLES the spread among models and widths is larger in the mean velocity than in the Reynolds shear stress at every $N$, so the comparison rests on the mean velocity (§5.4, Figs. 11–14). **Shown**
18. At $N=2$, reducing the width from d1 to d3 shifts the mean velocity downward (less eddy viscosity), like the trend with a solution limiter; WALE still lacks dissipation with d1, its best result; Vreman is too dissipative with d1, too little with d3, best with d2 (§5.4, Fig. 11). **Shown**
19. At $N=3$ and 4 the WALE profiles agree better with DNS at all widths, best with d1; at $N=5$ the best WALE result is d2, and reducing the eddy viscosity from d1 to d3 shifts the profile upward, reversing the trend of $N=2$–4 (§5.4, Figs. 12–14). **Shown**
20. With Vreman the results are consistent from $N=2$ to 5: d1 always too dissipative, d3 too little, d2 best (§5.4). **Shown**
21. WMLES at $Re_\tau=544$, same mesh, $N=3$: same trend, d2 best for Vreman, d1 best for WALE (§5.4, Fig. 15). **Shown**
22. Recommendation: Vreman with d2 for high-order WMLES; WALE is inconsistent across polynomial degrees and "appears to have insufficient dissipation" even with d1 (§6). **Shown** (drawn from claims 18–21)
23. Implicit LES performs better than explicit SGS models for wall-resolved, well-resolved turbulence, confirming earlier studies (§6). **Shown**
24. The wall-model exchange location is the interface between the first and second elements off the wall, with data from the second element, because the scales of the first element are always under-resolved; a near-wall element aspect ratio of one is desirable (§4, Fig. 4). **Stated**

The models as printed, in registry notation (kinematic, per unit density):
$\tau_{ij}=-2\nu_t\bar S_{ij}$ with the full (not deviatoric) strain rate
(Eq. 8); Smagorinsky $\nu_t=(C_s\Delta)^2|\bar S|$, with 0.16 quoted as
Lilly's isotropic-turbulence estimate (Eqs. 9–10); WALE
$\nu_t=(C_w\Delta)^2(\mathcal S_{ij}\mathcal S_{ij})^{3/2}/[(\bar S_{ij}\bar S_{ij})^{5/2}+(\mathcal S_{ij}\mathcal S_{ij})^{5/4}]$
with $\mathcal S_{ij}$ the traceless symmetric part of the squared velocity
gradient (as on the [WALE model](/methods/wale-model.md) page),
$C_w$ "often estimated to be 0.325" (Eqs. 11–14); Vreman
$\nu_t=(C_v\Delta)^2\sqrt{B_\beta/(\alpha_{ij}\alpha_{ij})}$, $\alpha_{ij}=\partial_i\bar u_j$,
$\beta_{ij}=\alpha_{mi}\alpha_{mj}$ (as on the [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md)
page), with an isotropic filter width, $C_v\approx\sqrt{2.5}\,C_s$ (Eq. 15). The
coefficient values actually used in the runs are not stated. The SGS
pressure work, heat flux and turbulent and viscous diffusion are dropped
"for the low Mach number flows considered" (§2.1). The specific wall
function is not printed (refs. [24, 63]).

# Printed slips

- **§5.3:** "The Reynolds number based on $\delta$ and the friction velocity $u_\tau$ is $Re_\tau=\frac{u_\tau L}{\nu}=180$": the formula uses an $L$ not defined for the channel.
- **Fig. 10** ($Re_\tau=180$): legend entries read "P2 …" while the text and Fig. 9 give $P=3$.
- **Conclusions:** "isentropic turbulence" (twice) for the isotropic-turbulence case of §5.2.
- **§5.1:** "Smagorinsy model"; **§1:** "to main simulation stability".
- **§1:** "kinetic energy preserving (KEP) and entropy preserving (KEEP) schemes", the acronym KEEP printed for entropy preserving.
- **Ref. [1]:** DOI printed "10.1175/1520-0493(1963)0912.3.CO;2", with the angle-bracketed segment missing.
- **§2.1:** "$\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}+\tau^{SGS}_{ij}$" printed as an equation, meaning an assignment.
- The values of $c_s$, $c_w$, $c_v$ used in the runs are not stated (only the quoted 0.16, 0.325 and $c_v\approx\sqrt{2.5}\,c_s$).
