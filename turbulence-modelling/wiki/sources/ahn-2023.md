# Summary

Ahn et al. (2023) simulate over-expanded, screeching twin square jets
(aspect ratio 1) from a twin converging–diverging nozzle tested at the
University of Cincinnati, at nozzle pressure ratio 3.0 and temperature
ratio 1.0 ($M_j=1.36$, $Re=8.85\times10^5$ on an equivalent diameter). The
paper is jet aeroacoustics; its turbulence-modelling content is the
simulation approach and the nozzle boundary layer.

The approach is [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) (MILES): no
subgrid-scale model, second-order central convective fluxes on the
unstructured finite-volume solver EDGE, four-stage Runge–Kutta, and a
modified Jameson second/fourth-difference
[artificial dissipation](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
switched by a pressure sensor and a Ducros dilatation/vorticity factor,
applied once per time step at the end of the first stage. The authors use
no [wall model](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md): the first cell inside the
nozzle sits at $y^+=10$–$20$ and does not resolve the viscous sublayer.

Instead of synthetic [inflow turbulence](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)
(the inlet is laminar), a geometric step of height $0.02h_n$ in
the converging section trips the nozzle boundary layer, after Langenais et
al. (2019). With tripping the exit turbulence intensity reaches about 0.06,
and the length of the supersonic region, the axial velocity decay and the
turbulent kinetic energy of the initial shear layers move closer to PIV;
without it the [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) overpredicts the
kinetic energy near the nozzle lip. Both runs overpredict shock-cell
strength. Axial-velocity spectra follow a $-5/3$ slope over about one
decade before a drop the authors assign to the grid and the
[resolving power of the scheme](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md).

On the tripped run, cross-correlations and spectral proper orthogonal
decomposition show a screech tone at $St\approx0.37$, a symmetrical
flapping coupling of the two jets, and a strong upstream mode confined
inside the jets. Far-field noise from a permeable
[Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings](/methods/ffowcs-williams-hawkings-acoustic-analogy.md)
surface agrees with the microphones at $67.2h_n$.

# Key claims

Positions are written $x/h_n$, with $x$ streamwise (the source's
$z/h$).

1. The simulations are implicit LES (MILES): "no explicit sub-grid-scale (SGS) model is implemented … the physical dissipation originated by small-scale eddies is implicitly considered by taking the advantage of truncation errors" (§III, p. 066105-4). **Stated**
2. Convective terms use a second-order central difference on an unstructured finite-volume grid (EDGE), time integration the explicit four-stage Runge–Kutta, viscous fluxes a fourth-order central difference, viscosity Sutherland's law (§III). **Stated**
3. A modified Jameson artificial dissipation (Eqs. 1–6), with coefficients $\kappa^{(2)}$, $\kappa^{(4)}$ built from a pressure sensor and the Ducros factor $\Phi=(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u)^2/((\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u)^2+|\boldsymbol\omega|^2)$, is applied "at the end of every first stage of the Runge–Kutta method" (§III). **Stated**
4. The first wall cell inside the nozzle is $h_n/320$, giving $y^+$ of 10 to 20; no wall-layer model is used; "The grid resolution is insufficient to resolve the viscous sub-layer but allows us to convect forced velocity fluctuations" (§III). **Stated**
5. Tripping is a step of height $0.02h_n$ in the middle of the converging section, placed there to avoid the shocks a step in the diverging section would generate; Langenais et al. used $0.01D$ and obtained an exit turbulence intensity of about 0.04 (§III, Fig. 3). **Stated**
6. Inlets impose total pressure $3.0P_{\mathrm{atm}}$, total temperature $1.0T_{\mathrm{atm}}$ and zero turbulence intensity; walls are adiabatic no-slip in a weak formulation; the far field is a characteristic condition (§III). **Stated**
7. Tripping raises the velocity fluctuations near the nozzle walls, streamwise more than transverse; the total turbulence intensity at the exit "reaches around 0.06 for LES-WT" (§IV A, Fig. 5). **Shown**
8. Vortical structures shed by the step convect along the converging section without significant loss of strength, then are stretched and weakened by the acceleration through the throat and the diverging section (§IV A, Fig. 4). **Shown**
9. Both LES slightly overpredict the shock-cell strength and the length of the supersonic region against PIV; the tripped run is closer to PIV in that length and in the axial velocity decay (§IV B 1, Figs. 6–8). **Shown**
10. Both LES give much higher turbulent kinetic energy than PIV at $x/h_n=1$ and 2 in the initial shear layers; tripping reduces the overestimate and reproduces the measured pattern in the inner shear layers (§IV B 2, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
11. Axial-velocity spectra in the lateral shear layer follow the $-5/3$ slope "for about one order of magnitude", which the authors take as evidence that the grid resolves a large range of inertial-range scales; the rapid drop at high $St$ marks the resolution limit of grid and scheme (§IV C 1, Fig. 12). **Shown**
12. Pressure fluctuations at the twin-nozzle centre vary strongly in amplitude in time without tripping and are more uniform with it; wavelet analysis captures $St\approx0.37$ (screech) consistently in the tripped run (§IV C 1, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
13. The near-field overall sound pressure level is about 5 dB higher upstream in the flat-side plane than in the C–D-side plane; standing waves appear between the jets and in the inter-nozzle cavity (§IV C 1, Fig. 15). **Shown**
14. Two-point space–time correlations (40 probes, reference at the fourth shock cell) show upstream-propagating waves at about the ambient sound speed in the lateral and inner shear layers of the flat-side plane, and downstream convection for $x/h_n>6$ (§IV C 2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
15. The twin square jets couple continuously in a symmetrical flapping mode in the flat-side plane, opposite to the anti-symmetric, intermittent coupling found for aspect-ratio-2 twin rectangular jets at the same condition (ref. 42) (§IV C 2, Fig. 17). **Shown**
16. SPOD of 500 snapshots at $\Delta t=7.5\times10^{-6}$ s: the leading mode at the fundamental is anti-symmetric about each jet centreline and symmetric about the symmetry plane; the harmonics concentrate in the shear layers (§IV C 3, Fig. 18). **Shown**
17. In pressure SPOD the second harmonic radiates at about 30° to the upstream direction in the flat-side plane and the third over wider angles; the second harmonic sets the near-field directivity at $6<x/h_n<8$ (§IV C 3, Fig. 19). **Shown**
18. A spatial Fourier decomposition of the SPOD mode separates upstream and downstream parts; the upstream mode is strong and confined inside the jets, "contrary" to the accepted external-mode view of the screech feedback loop and in line with ref. 32; its wavelength follows the shock-cell length (§IV C 3, Figs. 20–21). **Shown**
19. The finest Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings surface cell, $\approx0.024h_n$, resolves up to $St\approx3.5$ at about 11 cells per wavelength; data are saved every 100 steps ($5\times10^{-6}$ s), 8000 samples are used, and the surface is open (§IV D). **Stated**
20. The far-field overall sound pressure level at $67.2h_n$ agrees with the microphones, with its maximum near 140° and a local hump near 90° in the flat-side plane in both prediction and experiment (§IV D, Fig. 22). **Shown**
21. Screech frequency: Tam's model 7863 Hz ($St$ 0.364), experiment 8172 Hz (0.378), tripped LES 7928 Hz (0.367) (Table II). **Shown**
22. At 90° in the flat-side plane the predicted spectrum carries only the second-harmonic tone, as in the aspect-ratio-2 LES of ref. 42; the fundamental is weak near 90° and stronger at other angles, the opposite of the second harmonic (§IV D, Figs. 23–25). **Shown**
23. Time step $0.0012h_n/u_j$; statistics after $300h_n/u_j$; 960 processors (§III, §IV C 1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Table II header cites "Tam's model$^{57}$"; ref. 57 is Zhou et al. (2022), while the text cites Tam's model as ref. 16.
- §IV D: the convection velocity $0.79u_j$ is "based on our previous study.$^{11}$", but ref. 11 is Goparaju & Gaitonde (2018).
- Eq. (7) prints "$D_{eq}u_c/_j$", with a symbol missing after the slash, and uses $h_j$, $b_j$ without defining them.
- Eq. (2) prints $r_{01}=(|u_{01}n_{01}+c_{01}|)S_{01}$, with $c_{01}$ inside the absolute value.
- Eq. (6) defines $\Theta_j$ with the dilatation written $\nabla\cdot u_0$, mixing indices; Eq. (5) uses $\Theta_0$, $\Theta_1$.
- Fig. 12 caption: "lateral shear layer ($r=0.5D$)"; no $D$ is defined for this nozzle.
- §IV C 1: reflected waves come "from the nozzle surface located at around z/h = 2.75", while the cavity zone is described as $z/h<0$.
- "over-expended" (§II), "jest shear layers" (§IV C 1).
