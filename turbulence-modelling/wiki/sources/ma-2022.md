# Summary

Ma et al. (2022) improve the weighted compact nonlinear scheme (WCNS) of
Deng and Zhang (2000), a high-order finite-difference shock-capturing scheme
built from three parts: an eighth-order hybrid cell-edge/cell-node flux
difference, a numerical flux (Roe) from left and right interface states,
and a weighted nonlinear interpolation of those states. Earlier studies
found the original scheme too dissipative for
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md), acting as a strongly
dissipative SGS model; the authors attribute this to the explicit
fifth-order interpolation, which dominates the scheme's
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md).

They replace it with a nonlinear tri-diagonal compact interpolation: on the
same five-point stencil the linear compact interpolation is seventh order;
it is split into four fourth-order compact sub-interpolations combined with
Z-type nonlinear weights (as in the [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) family)
whose smoothness indicators include a third-derivative term, giving a
solution-dependent tri-diagonal system. Near a discontinuity the idealized
weights decouple that system into two independent systems on either side,
which is the shock-capturing mechanism, with no switch between compact and
explicit interpolation families and no separate
[shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md). The new scheme (WCNS-T)
resolves a larger fraction of wavenumbers than the original (WCNS-E), in
line with the resolution-enhanced schemes of the
[low-dissipation low-dispersion](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md)
page; [artificial diffusivity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
is cited among the other nonlinear compact approaches (§1). Grid metrics use
the same scheme as the fluxes to satisfy the
[geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md).

ILES with both schemes (no SGS model, the same equations in laminar,
transitional and turbulent regions, the subject of
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) for compressible flows) is run
on the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md), the
[circular cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md) at $Re=3900$,
the T106A [low-pressure turbine cascade](/benchmarks/turbine-cascade.md)
at $Re\approx60\,000$ and the transonic M219 open cavity. WCNS-T gives a
better dissipation-rate history, near-wake profiles and wake spectra,
suction-side pressure and separation, and cavity recirculation and pressure
spectra; on T106A even the linear WCNS-E beats the nonlinear WCNS-E near the
leading edge. The authors conclude that WCNS-T "is not over-dissipative
anymore for ILES"; the turbulent comparisons are qualitative.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\hat u_{j+1/2}$ the interpolated interface value (the
hat is not a test filter); $\hat u^{k}_{j+1/2}$ the candidate
sub-interpolations; $\mathsf w_k$, $a_k$, $d_k$, $\mathrm{IS}_k$ the
nonlinear, unnormalized and linear weights and the smoothness indicators
(registry §11; the source's $\omega_k$, $\alpha_k$, $C_k$, $\beta_k$);
$\alpha_{\mathrm W}$ the free parameter of the flux difference (the source's
$\alpha$); $K$ the volume-averaged kinetic energy.

1. The ILES performance of the original WCNS is poor because its numerical dissipation imitates a dissipative SGS model (citing Ishiko & Shimada, Matsukawa); the explicit interpolation dominates its dissipation and resolution (§1, §2.2). **Stated**
2. The flux difference Eq. (9) is sixth order in general and eighth order for $\alpha_{\mathrm W}=256/175$, the value used (§2.1, Eq. 9). **Stated** *(compiler check, not in the sources: with exact half-point fluxes, Eq. (9) differentiates $x^m$ exactly up to $m=8$ for $\alpha_{\mathrm W}=256/175$ and up to $m=6$ for $\alpha_{\mathrm W}=1$.)*
3. On the five-point stencil of the explicit fifth-order interpolation, the tri-diagonal compact interpolation reaches seventh order at most, given by Eq. (16) (§2.2). **Stated** *(compiler check, not in the sources: Eq. (16) is exact for monomials up to degree 6.)*
4. Eq. (16) is a convex combination of four fourth-order compact sub-interpolations (Eq. 18); the nonlinear relation Eq. (20) reduces to Eq. (16) at the linear weights $d_k=(1,18,30,7)/56$ (§2.2, Eqs. 17–21). **Derived**
5. The nonlinear weights are of Z type, $a_k=d_k\big(1+|\mathrm{IS}_0+3\mathrm{IS}_1-3\mathrm{IS}_2-\mathrm{IS}_3|/(\mathrm{IS}_k+10^{-6})\big)$, with indicators $\mathrm{IS}_k=(hf^k_j)^2+(h^2s^k_j)^2+(h^3t^k_j)^2$ of first-, second- and third-derivative approximations on $\{x_{j-3+k},\dots,x_{j+k}\}$ (§2.2, Eqs. 21–25). **Stated**
6. Applying Eq. (20) at all interfaces gives a solution-dependent tri-diagonal system $A\hat u=Bu$ (Eq. 26): one solve per variable, per grid line, per direction (§2.2). **Stated**
7. For a sharp discontinuity in $[x_j,x_{j+1}]$, with each weight either zero (stencil crossing the discontinuity) or proportional to its linear weight, the row of $\hat u_{j+1/2}$ has zero super-diagonal and the row of $\hat u_{j+3/2}$ zero sub-diagonal, so the system splits into two independent tri-diagonal systems, one on each side (Eqs. 27–29); "compact interpolations are operated within each sub-domain", which provides shock capturing (§2.2). **Derived** (under the idealized weights; the paper calls it "a theoretical analysis")
8. The nonlinear compact interpolation needs no switch between two interpolation families, unlike hybrid explicit–compact interpolations, and is easier to implement (§2.2, §4). **Stated**
9. Fourier and approximate-dispersion-relation analyses give WCNS-T lower dispersion and dissipation errors; resolving ratios are 30.2% / 49.3% (Fourier) and 28.6% / 39.7% (ADR) for WCNS-E / WCNS-T, resolvable wavenumbers about 1.6 and 1.4 times larger (§2.3, Fig. 1, Table 1). **Shown**
10. Shock–entropy wave interaction at $t=1.8$: WCNS-T on 200 points is comparable with WCNS-E on 300 points, "which agrees with the ADR analysis" (§2.3.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
11. Isentropic vortex: each scheme reaches its design order (WCNS-E 4.98, WCNS-T 7.01 at $N=2802$); WCNS-T costs more on the same grid (1.26e+04 against 1.83e+03 CPU time at $N=2802$) but less at equal error (§2.3.2, Table 2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
12. Taylor–Green ILES: WCNS-T agrees "quite good" with DNS for $-dK/dt$, in the peak height and in the onset of instability; the velocity spectra show an inertial range with a $-5/3$ slope (§3.1, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
13. Cylinder at $Re=3900$: the WCNS-T mean streamwise velocity is U-shaped close to the cylinder; the computations "confirm" Lysenko et al.'s statement that larger numerical dissipation moves the shear-layer transition upstream (§3.2, Fig. 7). **Shown** (the V shape further downstream is **Stated**, not shown)
14. Cylinder: WCNS-T centreline velocity and surface $C_p$ agree with earlier simulations and experiments; transverse-velocity spectra at $x/D=3$ and 5 match the references much better than WCNS-E, "indicating that inherent dissipation is not over-dissipative by the WCNS-T" (§3.2, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
15. T106A: the WCNS-T wall $C_p$ is "reasonably better" than WCNS-E against experiment (Stieger) and DNS (Michelassi et al.), with differences left on the front half of the suction side (§3.3, Fig. 11). **Shown**
16. T106A: the early leading-edge separation is not captured by the nonlinear WCNS-E, while the linear WCNS-E ($\mathsf w_k=d_k$) gives qualitatively correct behaviour (§3.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
17. T106A: both schemes capture transition; the WCNS-T vortical structures are finer, especially far downstream (§3.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
18. Cavity: both schemes show the recirculation with two sub-centres; the over-dissipative WCNS-E resolves a weaker recirculation; a coarse grid or excess dissipation makes the mixing layer more diffusive and deflected earlier (§3.4, Figs. 14–16). **Shown** / mechanism **Stated**
19. Cavity: band-integrated SPL predictions are "generally in line with the accuracy attained for the mean flow" (§3.4, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
20. WCNS with the nonlinear compact interpolation "is not over-dissipative anymore for ILES" (§4). **Stated** (no error norms are given for the ILES cases)
21. CRWENO (Ghosh & Baeder) improves small-scale resolution, but "the excessive upwind causes too much dissipation compared with the excellent dispersion behaviour" (§1). **Stated**
22. Most nonlinear compact schemes have been run on simple geometries; LES of industrial-type configurations with compact schemes is "rare in literature" (§1). **Stated**
23. The same equations, with no model, are used in laminar, transitional and turbulent regions; grid metrics are computed with the flux-derivative scheme to satisfy the geometric conservation law (§2). **Stated**
