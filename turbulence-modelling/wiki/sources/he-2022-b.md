# Summary

He et al. (2022) build a data-driven correction of the
[Spalart–Allmaras](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) model with the
quadratic constitutive relation (SA-QCR) and make it explainable. The test
flow is the Bachalo–Johnson axisymmetric transonic bump, a
[shock–boundary-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
with shock-induced separation, at $Re_c=2.763\times10^6$ and six Mach
numbers from 0.6 to 0.925. Following the four-level classification of
[model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md) of
Duraisamy et al. (2019), the correction targets the transport-equation
level (L3), argued to be better conditioned than a correction of the
[constitutive relation](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) (L2): a
random forest maps nine bounded local features to a normalized
eddy-viscosity discrepancy, $\nu_t^{\mathrm{ML}}=\nu_t+\Delta\nu_t$, with
the low-fidelity $\nu_t$ from a frozen-mean-flow solve. The route follows
Wu et al. (2018), and the introduction reviews
[field inversion](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md),
[tensor-basis networks](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) and
[symbolic regression](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md). Only the
offline phase is studied ([a priori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)):
the corrected model is never run in the solver.

Two explainability families are demonstrated. Intrinsic methods reduce
complexity: a hyperparameter search gives a Pareto front of
cross-validated accuracy against complexity, and feature selection drops
redundant features; these studies use [SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)-QCR
results as surrogate high-fidelity data. Post-hoc methods (SHAP values and
SHAP interaction values) explain a forest trained on the wall-resolved LES
of Uzun & Malik (2019), whose eddy viscosity is extracted by a
least-squares fit of the QCR2013V relation. The authors read the SHAP
output as the forest re-discovering the scaling of the eddy-viscosity
source with $\nu_t$ and the shear-against-rotation effect of
[Reynolds-stress transport](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md), and
suggesting a new dependence on the ratio of pressure-gradient to
kinetic-energy-gradient magnitudes, which needs verification. The
shielding function of [DDES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) is
considered as a wall-proximity feature and rejected as "mostly binary".
Evidence is numerical on one geometry.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_9$ the normalized input features
(the source's $q_1,\dots,q_9$; Table 2): $\zeta_2$ streamwise
pressure-gradient magnitude, $\zeta_3$ ratio of the pressure-gradient
magnitude to itself plus $|\frac12\rho\nabla U^2|$ ("pressure normal
stress to normal shear stress"), $\zeta_4$ normalized Q-criterion
$(\|\Omega\|^2-\|S\|^2)/(\|\Omega\|^2+\|S\|^2)$, $\zeta_5$, $\zeta_6$
strain/rotation ratio and Richardson-number features, $\zeta_7$ flow
misalignment, $\zeta_8$ the SA function $f_w$, $\zeta_9=(\nu_t/\nu)/(500+|\nu_t/\nu|)$;
$\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_i$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{i,j}$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{i,i}$
the SHAP value, interaction value and pure effect of feature $i$;
$N_{\mathrm{tree}}$ the number of trees and $m_f=N/M$ the feature fraction
per tree; $R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}$ the 5-fold cross-validated score;
$\epsilon_{L1}$, $\epsilon_{L2}$ relative errors (Eqs. 10–11).

1. RANS model uncertainty is classified in four levels (after Duraisamy et al. 2019): L1 ensemble averaging, L2 constitutive relation, L3 functional form of the transport equations, L4 model parameters (§1). **Stated**
2. L3 modification is "better conditioned and more robust than the L2 modification" (citing Wu et al. 2019), hence the SA-QCR correction acts on the eddy viscosity, $\Delta\nu_t$ (§2.1). **Stated**
3. Tuning SA coefficients within calibrated ranges "does not appear useful for complex academia flows or industrial flows" (citing He et al. 2020, 2021) (§2.1). **Stated**
4. SU2 (v7.1.0, JST scheme) SA and SST wall pressures at $M_\infty=0.875$ "almost overlap" with CFL3D; grid independence is reached at or above the medium RANS grid ($181\times81$ nodes) (§2.4, Fig. 4). **Shown**
5. SST-QCR predicts the wall pressure well at all six Mach numbers, especially for $M_\infty\ge0.85$; SA-QCR delays the shock and the shock-induced separation at $M_\infty\ge0.85$ and deviates most from LES (§2.5, Fig. 5). **Shown**
6. Both SA-QCR and SST-QCR underpredict the Reynolds shear stress and eddy viscosity downstream of the shock at $M_\infty=0.875$, SST-QCR slightly less; SST-QCR data therefore tune the ML structure (§4), LES data train the final model (§5) (§2.5, Fig. 6). **Shown**
7. LES minus SA-QCR eddy viscosity: SA-QCR overpredicts $\nu_t$ in the separation bubble and underpredicts it in the pre-shock boundary layer, shear layer, reattached boundary layer and at the shock front; the shock-front discrepancy is attributed to shock buffet, "not an effect of turbulence and thus is conceptually impossible to be modeled by steady RANS" (§2.7, Fig. 7). **Shown** (interpretation **Stated**)
8. The QCR-based extraction of $\nu_t$ from LES with denominator limiter $10^3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ keeps near-wall regions free of limiter effects while removing non-physical high $\nu_t/\nu$ above the shear layer; the QCR2013V extraction is "less sensitive to the limiters and thus more reliable than the Boussinesq-based method" (§2.7, Appendix, Fig. 19). **Shown**
9. Random-forest construction time complexity is printed "$(TNP\log_2P)$" (Eq. 12, citing Louppe 2014); explainability improves by reducing $N_{\mathrm{tree}}$, $m_f$ (hyperparameters, physics-free) and the number of features (feature selection, physics-based) (§3.1). **Stated**
10. Over $0<N_{\mathrm{tree}}\le300$, $1/9\le m_f\le9/9$: $R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}$ rises rapidly for $N_{\mathrm{tree}}\le20$ and $m_f\le4/9$; $5/9\le m_f\le7/9$ scores high for large enough $N_{\mathrm{tree}}$; $m_f\ge8/9$ lowers the score (§4.1, Fig. 9a). **Shown**
11. Best-accuracy set $N_{\mathrm{tree}}=130$, $m_f=6/9$: $R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}=0.91011$, complexity $N_{\mathrm{tree}}m_f=86.67$; cost-effective set $N_{\mathrm{tree}}=20$, $m_f=5/9$: $R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}=0.90682$, complexity 11.11; complexity "about 1/8", $R^2_{\mathrm{CV}}$ loss "only 0.004" (§4.1, Fig. 9b). **Shown**
12. A complex forest "is not necessarily predictive"; accuracy and complexity form a Pareto front (§4.1, §6). **Shown**
13. In feature group A, $\zeta_2$ and $\zeta_3$ are most significant; removing $\zeta_1$ increases accuracy; $\zeta_1$ and $\zeta_2$ identify the same regions, $\zeta_2$ more informatively — $\zeta_1$ is dropped (§4.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
14. Group B features $\zeta_4$, $\zeta_5$, $\zeta_6$ carry the same physics and give almost the same accuracy; only $\zeta_4$ is kept "due to its better dimensionless form" (§4.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
15. Table 4 (interpolation $M_\infty=0.9$ / extrapolation $0.925$): best-accuracy, full features ($M=9$, $N=6$, $N_{\mathrm{tree}}=130$): $R^2=0.98682/0.97632$, $\epsilon_{L1}=7.0/10.8\%$, $\epsilon_{L2}=11.0/14.7\%$; best-accuracy, selected ($6,4,130$): $0.98601/0.97551$, $7.2/10.9\%$, $11.3/14.9\%$; cost-effective, full ($9,5,20$): $0.98610/0.97393$, $7.4/11.6\%$, $11.3/15.4\%$; cost-effective, selected ($6,3,20$): $0.98325/0.97057$, $7.9/11.9\%$, $12.4/16.3\%$ (§4.3, Table 4). **Shown**
16. $\epsilon_{L2}$ for extrapolation is "3%–4%" higher than for interpolation, "similar to that reported in subsonic separated flows (Xiao et al., 2020)"; the cost-effective set costs 0.3–1.4% more $\epsilon_{L2}$; the selected features cost 0.2–1.1% more and cut complexity to 2/3 (§4.3). **Shown**
17. The offline-corrected SA-QCR viscosity ratio matches the SST-QCR target at all sections in both scenarios, and the four forest settings predict "effectively the same viscosity ratio": "A simple and thus more explainable ML model is sufficient" (§4.3, Fig. 12). **Shown** (offline only)
18. SHAP global ranking (LES-trained forest, best-accuracy hyperparameters, selected features): $\zeta_9\gg\zeta_4>\zeta_8>\zeta_3>\zeta_2>\zeta_7$; the dominance of the viscosity ratio, also seen by Wang et al. (2017), is explained by the SA source terms scaling with $\nu_t$ or $\nu_t^2$ (§5.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. Monotonic SHAP relations: $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_9\propto\zeta_9$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_4\propto-\zeta_4$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_8\propto\zeta_8$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_3\propto\zeta_3$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_7\propto\zeta_7$ (weak); $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_2$ is non-monotonic in $\zeta_2$, implying interactions (§5.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
20. $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_8\propto\zeta_8$ (eddy viscosity raised near the log-law region, lowered elsewhere) "indicates that the equilibrium turbulence assumption used by the original SA model fails in the transonic bump flow" (§5.1). **Stated** (interpretation of shown SHAP data)
21. $\zeta_4>0$ (rotation-dominated) in the pre-shock boundary layer and shear layer with $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_4<0$; $\zeta_4<0$ in the reattached boundary layer and bubble with $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_4>0$; $\zeta_4$ cannot distinguish the shear layer from the bubble (§5.1, Fig. 14). **Shown**
22. Pure effects are generally stronger than interactions; ranking by $|\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{i,i}|$ almost equals ranking by $|\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_i|$, except that $\zeta_8$ drops one place — it "serves better as a flow region classifier rather than a regressor" (§5.2, Fig. 15a). **Shown**
23. Pronounced interactions: $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{9,4}$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{8,4}$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{9,3}$, $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{8,3}$ and those of $\zeta_2$. $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{9,4}$ counteracts $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{4,4}$ for $\zeta_9<0.8$ ($\nu_t/\nu<2000$) and enhances it otherwise; $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{9,3}$ counteracts $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{3,3}$ for $\zeta_9<0.5$ or $\zeta_9>0.8$ and enhances it between; $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{8,4}$ counteracts $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{4,4}$ for $\zeta_8<0.1$ or $\zeta_8>1.6$ and enhances it between; $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{8,3}$ counteracts $\phi^{\mathrm{SH}}_{3,3}$ except for $0.04<\zeta_8<0.1$ (§5.2, Figs 15b, 17). **Shown**
24. Pure effects (Fig. 16): $\zeta_8$ reduces $\nu_t$ in free shear flow and the viscous sublayer ($0<\zeta_8<0.3$) and increases it in the log-law region ($0.3<\zeta_8<0.7$) and outer boundary layer ($0.7<\zeta_8<2.0$); $\zeta_2$ matters only at $|\zeta_2|\to1$, $\zeta_7$ only at $|\zeta_7|\to0$ (§5.2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
25. The model (1) re-discovered the scaling of the $\nu_t$ source term known from dimensional analysis, (2) re-discovered the shear-against-rotation effect of the Reynolds-stress transport equations, and (3) suggests a correlation of the eddy-viscosity source with the $\zeta_3$ ratio, which "has not attracted much attention" and needs verification (abstract, §5.3, §6). **Stated** (from SHAP evidence)
26. SHAP is data-dependent, describes how and not why the model responds, and cannot find physics outside the prescribed features; feature search "should be regarded as the focus of future research" (§5.3). **Stated**
27. The cost-effective set has 1.4% higher $L2$-type error and 87.5% lower complexity than the best-accuracy set; feature selection costs 1.1% $L2$ error for 33.3% lower complexity (§6). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
28. The methods "are transferable to a general ML turbulence model" (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **§2.1, after Eq. (2):** "$S^*_{ij}=S_{ij}-\mathrm{tr}(S_{ij})$ is the traceless strain rate tensor". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: as printed the trace is $-2\,\mathrm{tr}(S)$; the paper's own Eq. (6), whose numerator and denominator carry $-\frac13\overline{u_k'u_k'}S_{kk}$ and $-\frac13(S_{kk})^2$, follows from minimizing $J$ with $S^*_{ij}=S_{ij}-\frac13\mathrm{tr}(S)\delta_{ij}$.)*
- **§5.2, bullet on $q_3$:** "increases the eddy viscosity ($\phi_{3,3}<0$) when the pressure normal stress surpasses the normal shear stress ($q_3>0.5$)", against $\phi_{3,3}\propto q_3$ stated just above and $\phi_3>0$ for large $q_3$ (§5.1).
- **Eq. (9):** "$R^2=1-\|y_t-y_p\|_F/\|y_t-\bar y_t\|_F$", a ratio of unsquared norms.
- **Table 3 against §5.2:** Table 3 maps $0.4\le f_w<0.8$ to the outer boundary layer, $f_w=1.0$ to the log layer and $1.0<f_w\le2.0$ to the near-wall boundary layer under adverse pressure gradient; §5.2 gives the log-law region as "$0.3<q_8<0.7$" and the outer boundary layer as "$0.7<q_8<2.0$", and its interaction bullet maps $q_8>1.6$ to the near-wall boundary layer under adverse pressure gradient.
- **§4.3, "3%–4% higher".** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the Table 4 differences of $\epsilon_{L2}$ are 3.7, 3.6, 4.1 and 3.9 points.)*
- **§6, "87.5% lower" complexity.** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $11.11/86.67=0.128$, i.e. 87.2% lower; §4.1 calls the ratio "about 1/8".)* The "1.4% higher" $L2$ error of §6 is the largest of the 0.3–1.4% range of §4.3.
- **Eq. (12):** printed "$(TNP\log_2P)$" without an order symbol; the text then calls it "$P\log_2P$".
