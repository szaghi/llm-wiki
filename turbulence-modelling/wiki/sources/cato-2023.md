# Summary

Cato et al. (2023) study the field-inversion step of
[field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md):
which space-dependent correction of the
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) (SA-neg variant)
best fits a dense DNS mean-velocity field, and which one is easiest to learn
afterwards. Six correctors are optimized one at a time: a momentum forcing
$\boldsymbol f_u$; the divergence of a trace-free symmetric tensor
$\boldsymbol\Psi$, a conservative forcing; a production multiplier $\beta$
(Singh & Duraisamy); an additive source $f_{\tilde\nu}$ and a proportional
source $g_{\tilde\nu}\tilde\nu$ in the SA equation; and a multiplier
$(1+\varphi)$ of the eddy viscosity. The cost is the $L^2$ velocity misfit. The
gradients come from continuous adjoint expressions combined with the
transposed Newton Jacobian, and the optimizer is L-BFGS on a FreeFEM++
finite-element discretization.

On a converging–diverging channel ($Re=12\,600$), the
[periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) ($Re_b=2800$) and a square
cylinder ($Re=22\,000$), the momentum correctors fit best, and $g_{\tilde\nu}$
does nearly as well although it stays within the
[Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md), even where the
stress–strain alignment indicator is low. The multiplier $\varphi$ is the poorest
and fails on the square cylinder. A good velocity match does not give the right
Reynolds stresses: the trace-free tensor is non-unique, and the eddy viscosity
obtained with $g_{\tilde\nu}$ overshoots strongly in the channel, which the
authors call over-learning. The mean flow can be right with wrong stresses
"as long as the Reynolds force vector is correctly represented". These
findings bear on the non-uniqueness side of
[model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md), and on the
consistency argument for inverting inside the solver
([model-consistent training](/methods/model-consistent-training.md),
[a priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)).
Regularized objectives are proposed but not tested.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm m}$, $p_{\mathrm m}$ are the mean velocity and pressure of the (baseline or corrected) RANS model (the source's tilde, registry $(\cdot)_{\mathrm m}$); $\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle$ the DNS mean; $\boldsymbol f_u$, $\boldsymbol\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_1&\psi_2\\\psi_2&-\psi_1\end{pmatrix}$, $\beta$, $f_{\tilde\nu}$, $g_{\tilde\nu}$, $\varphi$ the six correctors; $c_{RS}$ the stress–strain alignment indicator. The source's production multiplier $1+\beta_{\mathrm{src}}$ is the registry's $\beta$ (prior $\beta=1$).

1. Six space-dependent ("non-parametric") correctors, $\boldsymbol f_u$, $\boldsymbol\Psi$, $\beta$, $f_{\tilde\nu}$, $g_{\tilde\nu}$, $\varphi$, are compared with the same meshes, numerics and cost functional, each optimized alone (§II B, Eq. 5). **Stated**
2. $\beta$, $f_{\tilde\nu}$, $g_{\tilde\nu}$ and $\varphi$ keep the solution within the Boussinesq hypothesis, "only the length-scale of the turbulence is corrected, not the structure of the Reynolds-stresses"; $\boldsymbol f_u$ and $\boldsymbol\Psi$ are not so constrained (§II B). **Stated**
3. $\boldsymbol\Psi$ gives a conservative forcing $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\Psi$, and $(\psi_1,\psi_2)$ is defined only up to $(\psi_1+\partial_x\Phi,\psi_2+\partial_y\Phi)$ with $\partial_{xx}\Phi+\partial_{yy}\Phi=0$, which gives the same force; this freedom could be used to impose realizability (§II B). **Derived**
4. Matching the Reynolds-stress force does not imply matching the Reynolds stresses: with only mean velocity in the cost there is no reason for the reconstructed and DNS stresses to agree (§II B, Eq. 9); shown later on the square cylinder (Figs. 16–17). **Derived**/**Shown**
5. The adjoint gradients (Eq. 12) locate the controls: $g_{\tilde\nu}$ (and $\beta$) act where $\tilde\nu$ (the production) overlaps the adjoint $\tilde\nu^\dagger$; $f_{\tilde\nu}$ acts "more upstream", where $\tilde\nu^\dagger$ peaks, possibly in a "non-physical upstream region, where Reynolds stresses are weak"; gradients involving derivatives ($\boldsymbol\Psi$, $\varphi$) "might be noisier" (§II C). **Derived**
6. The adjoint gradients pass a first-order finite-difference test (App. B, Eq. B2, Fig. 20). **Shown**
7. Converging–diverging channel: with $\boldsymbol f_u$ and $\boldsymbol\Psi$ "the cost functional decreased almost three orders of magnitude" (§III B, Fig. 3). **Shown**
8. Channel errors (Table I), skin friction $E_{C_f}$ and velocity $E_u$: RANS 0.39, $4.9\cdot10^{-2}$; $\boldsymbol f_u$ 0.059, $3.1\cdot10^{-3}$; $\boldsymbol\Psi$ 0.057, $2.9\cdot10^{-3}$; $\beta$ 0.18, $8.4\cdot10^{-3}$; $f_{\tilde\nu}$ 0.19, $8.1\cdot10^{-3}$; $g_{\tilde\nu}$ 0.12, $4.6\cdot10^{-3}$; $\varphi$ 0.23, $1.2\cdot10^{-2}$ (§III B). **Shown**
9. In the channel the $\varphi$ correction "rapidly interrupts the L-BFGS iterations"; the $\varphi$ problem can be ill-conditioned, in agreement with Dow & Wang [40], visible as small perturbations in the separated region; its gradient norm stays on a "high plateau" while the others fall by three to four orders of magnitude (§III B, Fig. 5f; App. B, Fig. 21). **Shown**
10. In the channel $g_{\tilde\nu}$, $f_{\tilde\nu}$ and $\beta$ recover the mean velocity "very accurately", but "at the expense of large eddy-viscosity corrections, which very strongly overshoot the reference values", "a classical over-learning situation" (§III B, Fig. 6). **Shown**
11. In the channel the $\varphi$ result gives $(1+\varphi)\nu_t$ "pretty much in agreement" with the DNS-estimated $\nu_t$ (Eq. 18), noisy where the flow separates; $\varphi$ "reaches such a good compromise without penalizing term" there (§III B, Fig. 6f). **Shown**
12. Regularized costs are proposed to keep the control physical, a penalty $l_2\int g_{\tilde\nu}^2$ (Eq. 19) or proximity to a prior RANS eddy viscosity (Eq. 20); they are not tested (§III B). **Stated**
13. Channel: $c_{RS}$, restricted to $k>0.1k_{\max}$, falls below 0.8 near the wall behind the bump in the reverse-flow region, yet the $g_{\tilde\nu}$ assimilation reconstructs that region well (§III C, Figs. 5e, 7). **Shown**
14. Periodic hill ($Re_b=2800$): $c_{RS}<0.8$ "in much of the domain below $y/h=1$"; $f_{\tilde\nu}$ and $g_{\tilde\nu}$ nevertheless recover the DNS velocity and skin friction, so "missing the proper stress alignment does not play a large role for predicting the mean flow" (§§IV A–B, Figs. 8–10). **Shown**
15. Periodic-hill errors (Table II), $E_{C_f}$ and $E_u$: RANS 0.38, $7.1\cdot10^{-2}$; $\boldsymbol f_u$ 0.17, $3.8\cdot10^{-3}$; $\boldsymbol\Psi$ 0.26, $3.7\cdot10^{-3}$; $\beta$ 0.27, $3.2\cdot10^{-2}$; $f_{\tilde\nu}$ 0.23, $7.8\cdot10^{-3}$; $g_{\tilde\nu}$ 0.20, $7.5\cdot10^{-3}$; $\varphi$ 0.30, $4.3\cdot10^{-2}$; $\beta$ and $\varphi$ "were not as effective" (§IV B). **Shown**
16. In both the channel and the hill, $g_{\tilde\nu}$ lowers the cost faster than $f_{\tilde\nu}$ (§IV B). **Shown**
17. Square cylinder ($Re=22\,000$): $c_{RS}>0.8$ only in "a few areas"; $\boldsymbol f_u$ and $\boldsymbol\Psi$ reproduce the DNS ($\boldsymbol\Psi$ "slightly noisier"); $g_{\tilde\nu}$ "performed almost as well as the source terms in the momentum equations"; $\beta$ and $f_{\tilde\nu}$ improve the result partially, $f_{\tilde\nu}$ degrading the shear layer above the cylinder; $\varphi$ "was not able to correct the field" (§§V A–B, Figs. 12–15). **Shown**
18. Square cylinder: the baseline SA Reynolds stresses are "very weak compared to the DNS"; assimilation raises them but no corrector matches the DNS stresses, not even $\boldsymbol\Psi$ with its large drop of the cost; the stresses near the corners are wrong for $\boldsymbol\Psi$ and $g_{\tilde\nu}$; "the mean-flow prediction can be accurate even though the Reynolds stresses are wrong" (§V C, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
19. Square cylinder: $R_{12}$ improves near the cylinder with $\beta$, $f_{\tilde\nu}$ and $g_{\tilde\nu}$, and $g_{\tilde\nu}$ "manages to capture a realistic stress tensor especially in the wake region"; its Reynolds-force field approximates the DNS better in the recirculation region (§V C, Figs. 17–19). **Shown**
20. Square cylinder: $\boldsymbol\Psi$ gives wrong stresses but a reasonable streamwise Reynolds force, so "even with the wrong stresses, it is possible to have accurate mean fields, as long as the Reynolds force vector is correctly represented"; $\boldsymbol f_u$ captures the streamwise but not the transverse component (§V C, Figs. 18–19). **Shown**
21. For later learning, $\boldsymbol f_u$ and $\boldsymbol\Psi$ are dimensional vectors or tensors, costlier and with "non trivial (especially in 3D)" rotational invariance; $\beta$ and $\varphi$ are dimensionless scalars; $f_{\tilde\nu}$ and $g_{\tilde\nu}$ are scalars "not naturally dimensionless" (§VI, Table III). **Stated**

23. Correcting the SA equation itself "improves numerical robustness regardless of the test case" compared with the eddy-viscosity multiplier $\varphi$; for skin friction the best correctors are $\boldsymbol f_u$, $\boldsymbol\Psi$ and $g_{\tilde\nu}$; only dense velocity data were treated (§VII). **Shown**/**Stated**
24. The $L^2$ velocity cost favours the bulk over small near-wall regions, which is why $u^+$ profiles scaled with each assimilated $u_\tau$ reveal differences that scaling with the DNS $u_\tau$ hides (§III B, Fig. 4b–c). **Shown**
25. The DNS-based eddy viscosity $\nu_t=\mathbf R:\nabla_s\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle/(\nabla_s\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle:\nabla_s\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle)$ (Eq. 18, with the source's $\mathbf R$ of Eq. 9 and $\nabla_s=\nabla+\nabla^T$) "remains an approximation": injected in RANS it "does not guarantee a perfect mean field", but is "a good approximation for attached flows" [41] (§III B). **Stated**
