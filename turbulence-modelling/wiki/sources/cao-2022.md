# Summary

Cao et al. (2022) extend their
[high-order gas-kinetic scheme](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md)
(HGKS) to large-scale MPI computation and present it as a
[direct numerical simulation](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
tool from nearly incompressible to supersonic turbulence. HGKS is a
finite-volume scheme whose interface flux comes from the time-dependent
integral solution of the BGK kinetic equation, with fifth-order WENO
reconstruction (linear weights for smooth low-speed flows, WENO-Z on
characteristic variables for compressible turbulence) and a two-stage
fourth-order Lax–Wendroff-type time discretization. The parallel code splits
the domain in two directions and exchanges data with eight neighbours.

On the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at $Re=1600$,
$M_0=0.1$, on $128^3$ to $1024^3$ cells, the kinetic energy and its decay
rate converge to a finite-difference reference with refinement. The authors
quantify the [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) as
the decay rate of the kinetic energy minus the solenoidal, bulk-viscous and
pressure-dilatation contributions, and find that it decreases with
refinement. They read the result as support for
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) with high-order schemes, although
the same section reports a large peak-dissipation discrepancy on the two
coarsest grids. HGKS costs about 1.4 times the core hours of the reference
scheme (about 1.2 after normalising processor speed).

In [turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at
$Re_\tau=180$, $M=0.1$, on $96^3$ and $128^3$ stretched grids with
constant-mass-flux forcing, mean velocity, Reynolds shear stress, rms
profiles and near-wall asymptotics are compared with spectral DNS and with
lattice-Boltzmann and DUGKS results, using the
[log law](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) with $\kappa=0.40$, $B=5.5$ to set
the forcing. In decaying compressible
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md) at
$Re_\lambda=72$ and $M_t=0.8$–$1.6$ on $512^3$ cells, HGKS runs without
special treatment; the paper offers the time histories of density rms,
kinetic energy, derivative skewness and flatness, solenoidal and
dilatational dissipation and pressure-dilatation as benchmark data, and
shows ribbon-shaped shocklets next to block-like expansion regions.

# Key claims

1. A parallel HGKS code uses two-dimensional domain decomposition (split in $y$ and $z$, not in $x$) with MPI; each sub-domain communicates only with its eight neighbours (§2.2, Fig. 1). **Stated**
2. The two-stage time discretization (Eq. 6) "provides a fourth-order time accurate solution" for hyperbolic equations (§2.1; proof in cited work). **Stated**
3. For smooth low-speed flows (TGV, channel) HGKS uses the simplified smooth second-order gas-kinetic flux and WENO with linear weights, "to avoid the numerical dissipation from artificially created interface discontinuity"; for compressible isotropic turbulence it uses WENO-Z with characteristic-variable reconstruction (Remark 1). **Stated**
4. For discontinuous flows the collision time includes an artificial term $C|p_l-p_r|/(p_l+p_r)\,\Delta t$, $C=1$, to "enlarge the kinetic scale physics in the discontinuous region" (§3). **Stated**
5. TGV at $Re=1600$, $M_0=0.1$: the kinetic energy agrees with the $512^3$ BB13 DRP finite-difference reference except on $128^3$; the peak dissipation differs markedly on $128^3$ and $256^3$ and agrees with refinement (Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
6. The $1024^3$ case is "the finest resolution for the Taylor-Green vortex problem", provided as benchmark result (§3.1). **Stated**
7. The scheme's inherent bulk viscosity is $\mu_b=\frac{2K_{\mathrm{int}}}{3(K_{\mathrm{int}}+3)}\mu$ with $K_{\mathrm{int}}=2$ internal degrees of freedom for a diatomic gas (§3.1, cited). **Stated**
8. In the nearly incompressible TGV the solenoidal dissipation $\varepsilon_1$ matches the enstrophy-based dissipation and the bulk and pressure-dilatation terms are negligible; $\varepsilon_3$ is of the same order as in the reference (Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. The numerical dissipation $\varepsilon_{num}=\varepsilon(E_k)-(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3)$ decreases with grid refinement; on coarse grids $\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3$ is significantly lower than $\varepsilon(E_k)$ (Fig. 5). **Shown**
10. Because $\varepsilon(E_k)$ is "well predicted at all grid levels", "the filtering due to the coarse mesh correctly mimics the physical dissipation from the unresolved scale dynamics", which "validates the usage of high-order numerical methods for the implicit large-eddy simulations (iLES)" (§3.1). **Stated**. The same section reports "a large discrepancy" in the peak of $\varepsilon(E_k)$ on $128^3$ and $256^3$ (claim 5).
11. Timing on TianHe-II (Xeon E5-2692, 2.2 GHz), 30 steps averaged: $256^3$ at 121.01, 34.08, 9.09 s/step on 4, 16, 64 cores; $512^3$ at 69.00, 17.29, 4.66 s/step on 64, 256, 1024 cores; "our MPI code scales properly" (Table 1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
12. TGV total costs: TG1 $128^3$, 16 cores, 13.3 h, 213 core hours; TG2 $256^3$, 256 cores, 13.5 h, 3456; TG3 $512^3$, 1024 cores, 66 h, 67584; TG4 $1024^3$, 1024 cores, 730 h, 747062. BB13 on NASA Pleiades (Xeon E5-2670, 2.6 GHz): $256^3$, 64 cores, 40 h, 2560; $512^3$, 368 cores, 130 h, 47840 (Tables 3–4). **Shown**
13. HGKS total cost is "around 1.4 times higher" than the finite-difference method and "around 1.2 times higher" after accounting for processor speed, which the authors call "comparable and affordable" (§3.2). **Shown** (from Tables 3–4; different machines and time steps)
14. Channel at $Re_\tau=180$: G1 ($96^3$) gives $Re_\tau=176.03$, $Re_c=3297.78$, $Re_b=5720.09$, $C_f=7.58\times10^{-3}$; G2 ($128^3$) 179.21, 3319.22, 5730.11, $7.82\times10^{-3}$; G2 is "much closer" to Dean's correlation $C_f=0.073Re_b^{-0.25}=8.39\times10^{-3}$ (Table 6). **Shown**
15. After 350 $\delta/U_c$ of averaging the total-stress residual is below 2.6% (G1) and 1.2% (G2); transition from the perturbed laminar state takes over 500 $\delta/U_c$ (Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
16. HGKS mean velocity agrees reasonably with spectral, LBM and DUGKS results; its Reynolds shear stress is "clearly better than DUGKS and LBM results, especially in the near-wall region" (Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
17. For rms profiles DUGKS is better for the streamwise component near the wall, and LBM (near the centreline) or DUGKS (near the wall) for the other two; the authors nonetheless conclude "HGKS outweighs LBM and DUGKS" (Fig. 12, §3.3). **Shown** (comparative judgement)
18. The near-wall limiting coefficients of the rms velocities and of $-\langle u'v'\rangle^+$ (scaled by $y^+$, $y^{+2}$, $y^+$, $y^{+3}$) for G1 agree with the spectral results (Table 7). **Shown** (reference values not tabulated)
19. HGKS results are obtained on a coarse grid with a large domain, whereas LBM needs equidistant grids with $\Delta x^+\approx0.3$ everywhere to resolve the viscous layer (§3.3). **Stated**
20. Compressible decaying isotropic turbulence at $Re_\lambda=72$, $M_t=0.8,1.0,1.2,1.6$, $512^3$ cells on 256 cores, is simulated "up to the supersonic regime" with WENO-Z and "no extra special treatment" (§3.4, Table 8). **Shown**
21. Resolution criterion from the authors' earlier work: $k_{max}\eta_0\ge2.71$ and $\Delta t_{ini}/\tau_{to}\le5.58\times10^{-3}$ suffice for HGKS (§3.4). **Stated**
22. The Kolmogorov length exceeds the mean free path, and each cell holds more than one mean free path ($h/\eta_0=0.819$; $h/\lambda_0=2.551$ to 1.275); this "provides the intuitive evidence for controversial issue that smallest eddies in turbulence may still within the framework of continuum mechanics assumption" (§3.4, Table 8). **Shown**/**Stated**
23. With increasing $M_t$: $\rho_{rms}/M_t^2$ decreases monotonically; skewness and flatness peaks depart strongly from the low-Mach thermal regime; the peak total dissipation increases; the solenoidal part decreases and the dilatational part increases (Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
24. The pressure-dilatation changes sign during the evolution and then stays small and positive, as in Sarkar et al. for subsonic turbulence; early in supersonic decay it can be of the order of the total dissipation (Fig. 14). **Shown**
25. Shocklets (regions of dilatation below $-3$ times its rms) appear as narrow long ribbons; strong-expansion regions (above 2 times the rms) are localized blocks close to them (Fig. 15). **Shown**
26. Compact finite-difference schemes are limited to $M_t\le0.8$ and "fail to capture strong shocklets"; spectral and LBM methods "fail to capture shocklets and suffer from numerical instability" with shocks; hybrid compact/WENO schemes have a critical $M_t$ threshold (§1, §3.4). **Stated**
27. The paper calls itself "the first successful DNS application of HGKS for turbulent flow from nearly incompressible to supersonic one" and its channel flow "the first simulation of turbulent channel flow using HGKS" (§3.3, §4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Sign in Eq. 11.** The energy balance is printed $\frac{d\langle K\rangle}{dt}=\varepsilon+\langle p\theta\rangle$ with $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_s+\varepsilon_d$, $\varepsilon_s=\langle\mu\omega_i\omega_i\rangle$, $\varepsilon_d=\tfrac43\langle\mu\theta^2\rangle$ non-negative by definition, while the text describes the decay of $\langle K\rangle$.
- **$\kappa_{max}$ (§3.4)** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. The paper prints $\kappa_{max}=\sqrt2\kappa_0N/3$ "with $\kappa_0=8$ as Eq. (9)". With $h=2\pi/512$ and the Table 8 value $h/\eta_0=0.819$, $\sqrt2\cdot512/3=241.4$ gives $k_{\max}\eta_0=3.617$, matching the printed 3.613; the printed factor $\kappa_0=8$ would give 28.9.
- **Figure references.** §3.4 says the histories of $\rho_{rms}/M_t^2$, $K/K_0$, $S_u$ and $F_u$ "are presented in Fig.14"; they are in Fig. 13. §3.3 says the mesh and initial velocity "for G1 are given in Fig.7"; the Fig. 7 caption says "for case G2".
- **Wall shear stress (p. 15).** $\tau_{wall}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial y}\big|_{wall}$ is printed without the viscosity.
- **Speedup (§2.2).** Printed $S_n=T_n/T_{n_{ref}}$, while the text gives the ideal speedup as $n/n_{ref}$, which holds only for $T_{n_{ref}}/T_n$.
- **"Hypersonic" (§1).** The test cases are described as "from nearly incompressible flow to the hypersonic one"; the abstract, §3.4 and the conclusion say supersonic (maximum $M_t=1.6$).
- **Wording.** "the diatonic gas" (diatomic); "Re_λ = 72 to and the turbulent Mach number form" (§3.4).
