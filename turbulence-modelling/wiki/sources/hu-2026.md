# Summary

Hu & Yang (2026) review machine-learning closures for the Reynolds-averaged
and filtered Navier–Stokes equations (literature 2015–2026), explicitly as a
curated, not systematic, review with no new computations. They organize the
field around one thesis, which they label "the interpretive synthesis
proposed by this review, not an established conclusion of the field":
closures are trained on a pointwise, offline objective but must work coupled
to a solver, across scales and in a statistically stationary way. They trace
the mismatch to three properties of turbulence (multi-scale chaos,
non-locality, no scale separation) and read one signature of it in each
"pillar": ill-conditioning of the RANS mean-momentum operator under injected
Reynolds stresses (Poroseva et al. and Wu et al. as cited by the review); the
[a priori / a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)
gap of [neural-network subgrid models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md);
and the spectral bias of [PINNs](/methods/physics-informed-neural-network.md).

ML-RANS methods are grouped into corrective
([field inversion](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md)), direct
anisotropy ([TBNN](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md)), symbolic
([symbolic regression](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md)) and Bayesian
or aggregation ([model aggregation](/methods/turbulence-model-aggregation.md);
[model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md)) families,
rated in Tables I–III. The authors' remedy across pillars is to align training
with deployment: corrective targets, the Reynolds force vector, CFD-driven and
solver-in-the-loop training ([model-consistent training](/methods/model-consistent-training.md)),
dissipativity constraints, differentiable solvers. Data-driven wall models are
the [building-block-flow model](/methods/building-block-flow-wall-model.md) and
reinforcement-learning models ([machine-learning wall models](/methods/machine-learning-wall-model.md)).
The review restates the limits of the [Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
and of the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) with respect to
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md), and links the
"resolution-handoff bottleneck" of solver–network coupling to the grey area of
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md). Neural operators, GNNs,
transformers, diffusion and foundation models are found to learn no closure
term; for generative models the authors propose protocols E1–E3. They
conclude that the field is "short of training objectives, evaluation
protocols, and physical constraints" rather than of architectures.

# Key claims

All claims are statements of the review; works it summarizes are attributed
"as cited by" the review. Page-local symbols: $\mathsf S^{\mathrm n}_{ij}$, $\mathsf W^{\mathrm n}_{ij}$
the mean strain and rotation normalized by the strain norm (the source's
$\hat S_{ij}$, $\hat W_{ij}$; not the registry-§11 $\mathsf s_{ij}$,
$\mathsf w_{ij}$ of resolved LES fields, nor the $k/\varepsilon$-scaled
$\mathsf S_{ij}$, $\mathsf W_{ij}$); $I_m$ the invariants of the integrity basis;
$\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{NS}}$, $r_\omega$, $\mathcal E_r$ the Navier–Stokes
residual of a generated sample, its enstrophy-weighted r.m.s. and its
dimensionless form; $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ref}}$ a reference dissipation;
$\lambda_{\mathrm{Ly}}$ a Lyapunov exponent; $\lambda_{\mathrm{PDE}}$,
$\mu_{\mathrm{BC}}$ the PINN loss weights.

1. One obstacle recurs across ML-RANS, ML-LES and PINNs: the training objective is pointwise and offline while deployment is solver-coupled, multi-scale and stationary; the single-root attribution is "the interpretive synthesis proposed by this review, not an established conclusion of the field" (§I, Fig. 2, §VIII). **Stated**
2. Three intrinsic properties make turbulence an unfavourable target for standard ML "largely independently of how much data are available or how expressive the network is": multi-scale chaos (no stationary $(\boldsymbol x,t)$ target beyond a finite predictability time), non-locality, and no scale separation (no spectral gap; the subgrid term depends on $\Delta$ and exchanges energy both ways) (§II E). **Stated**
3. Pope's representation $b_{ij}=\sum_{n=1}^{10}g^{(n)}(I_1,\dots,I_5)T^{(n)}_{ij}$ is an algebraic identity; used as a local closure $b_{ij}=\mathcal G(\langle S_{ij}\rangle,\langle\Omega_{ij}\rangle)$ it imposes local equilibrium, and in strongly non-equilibrium flow "no local algebraic function of the strain and rotation rates can be exact, however expressive the function", TBNNs included (§II A, Eq. 3). **Stated**
4. DNS Reynolds stresses with relative errors below 0.5% injected into the RANS mean-momentum equations can give mean-velocity errors up to 35% (Poroseva et al., as cited); Wu et al. explained this by a local condition number of the mean-momentum operator (as cited) (§II D, §III B). **Stated**
5. Corrective methods that learn scalar multipliers of eddy viscosity or production project the learned object on a one-dimensional subspace compatible with the solver, which the authors give as the reason for their better robustness than direct anisotropy prediction (§III B). **Stated**
6. Learning the Reynolds force vector (the divergence of the Reynolds stress) avoids a differentiation inside the solver and amplifies high-wavenumber errors less (Cruz et al., as cited); CFD-driven training and online RANS avoid ill-conditioning because the loss is evaluated on the coupled solution; transport-equation corrections are smoother and better conditioned (§III B). **Stated**
7. Larger feature sets do not necessarily generalize better (Wu et al., as cited); "a small set of physically meaningful features—typically on the order of ten to a few tens—is often sufficient", an empirical observation (§III B). **Stated**
8. Table II rates five ML-RANS families; a posteriori robustness: corrective High, direct Low–medium, symbolic Medium–high, CFD-driven High, Bayesian High; interpretability: Medium, Low, High, Medium, Medium–high; the ratings are a "reader's guide rather than a rigorous benchmark" (Table II). **Stated**
9. In solver-coupled out-of-distribution settings physics-constrained methods "tend to outperform generic black-box approaches", with a margin depending on configuration, data and metric; in data-rich a priori tests an unconstrained network can match a constrained one, and strict realizability can trade a priori accuracy for a posteriori robustness (§III C, §VII B). **Stated**
10. The constant-coefficient [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) model is purely dissipative and cannot represent backscatter; the dynamic model can through a locally negative eddy viscosity, but averaging and clipping suppress some or all of it; a closure minimizing a pointwise loss on filtered DNS tends to smooth out backscatter events (§II C–D). **Stated**
11. Learned subgrid models depend on the grid or filter width because turbulence lacks scale separation; routes: $\Delta$ as an input, scale and symmetry invariances, transfer learning; "a closure that is demonstrably scale-invariant and grid-independent across the range of $\Delta$ encountered in engineering LES has not been established" (§IV A). **Stated**
12. Models with a priori correlation coefficients often above 0.9 routinely fail a posteriori through instability from local energy injection, systematic over- or under-dissipation, or drift out of the training distribution (§IV A). **Stated**
13. Three structural reasons for the gap: pointwise losses are ill-aligned with spectra and dissipation and average out intermittent backscatter; the subgrid stress is a small signal, so a small relative $L_2$ error on it can be a large error on its divergence ("the LES counterpart of RANS ill-conditioning"); the a posteriori state is partly generated by the closure (§IV A). **Stated**
14. Sirignano et al. (as cited) find a network trained purely a priori "unusable a posteriori" while adjoint-based embedded training works; model-consistent training (Duraisamy, as cited) is the key determinant of robust a posteriori performance (§IV A). **Stated**
15. Beck et al.'s learned closure is unstable when applied directly, and stable forms including a data-informed adaptive eddy viscosity are built; Yuan et al.'s deconvolutional network outperforms implicit LES, dynamic Smagorinsky and dynamic mixed models a posteriori (Table III: correlation above 99%, relative error below 15%, cost about 1.3 times the dynamic mixed model) (§IV A, Table III; both as cited). **Stated**
16. SciMARL wall models treat discretization points as cooperating agents and are reported to generalize to extreme Reynolds numbers and unseen geometries; the building-block-flow model uses a classifier and a predictor trained on canonical flows, which the authors note assumes the complex-flow wall stress is a convex combination of canonical-flow wall stresses (§IV C; as cited). **Stated**
17. Vanilla PINNs, a single MLP minimizing $\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{PDE}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{PDE}}+\mu_{\mathrm{BC}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{BC/IC}}$, cannot recover fully developed turbulence "without additional ingredients", because of NTK spectral bias, the representational bandwidth limit of smooth finite-width MLPs ("too viscous" fields) and chaotic unpredictability; the verdict is "an author perspective, not a settled consensus" (§V A, Eq. 6, Table IV). **Stated**
18. To the authors' knowledge no PINN variant has shown statistically faithful forward integration of high-Reynolds-number 3D wall-bounded turbulence without substantial external data (§V A–B). **Stated**
19. Learned discretizations, solver-in-the-loop corrections and end-to-end differentiable CFD are ordered by increasing integration; tighter coupling gives stronger a posteriori performance at higher training cost (§V C 1–3). **Stated**
20. The "resolution-handoff bottleneck" asks where in scale and space the solver hands the dynamics to the learned component, linked to the DES grey area; learning the RANS/LES switching coefficients of an explicit-algebraic-stress DES (Liu et al., as cited) is a first step, and non-zonal learned crossover placement is open (§V C 4). **Stated**
21. None of the emerging-paradigm studies of Table VI (FNO, DeepONet, MeshGraphNets, Transolver, GNOT, UPT, CoNFiLD, GraphCast, Poseidon, …) learns or replaces a closure term of the averaged or filtered equations (§VI, Table VI). **Stated**
22. FNO assumes periodicity and structured data and, like DeepONet, has no native uncertainty quantification; spectral agreement with filtered DNS in wall-bounded turbulence is reported only in a few follow-ups (channel up to $Re_\tau=590$) (§VI A). **Stated**
23. GNN message passing suffers "under-reaching", related to the CFL condition by Tesán et al. (as cited) and read by the authors as architectural locality clashing with non-local physics (§VI B). **Stated**
24. Diffusion-model evaluations mostly show statistical consistency, dynamical plausibility only qualitatively, and rarely PDE-residual consistency; protocols E1 (one-step solver-forwarding error, in $L^2$ and in a spectral norm weighted by $w(k)\propto k^{\alpha_w}$, $\alpha_w\in[1,2]$, or $k^{5/3}$), E2 ($r_\omega=[\langle\lvert\omega\rvert^2\lvert\boldsymbol r_{\mathrm{NS}}\rvert^2\rangle/\langle\lvert\omega\rvert^2\rangle]^{1/2}$, $\mathcal E_r=u_{\mathrm{rms}}r_\omega/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ref}}=2\nu\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle$) and E3 (trajectory divergence against $e^{\lambda_{\mathrm{Ly}}t}$) are proposed and not applied (§VI D, Table V). **Stated**
25. "Weather is not CFD": weather foundation models rest on a periodic wall-free domain, decades of reanalysis data and affordable resolutions, none of which holds in engineering CFD; no CFD foundation model exists "in the sense that GraphCast is a weather foundation model" (§VI E). **Stated**
26. Mandler & Weigand (as cited) found that tailoring an eddy-viscosity augmentation to one phenomenon reduces generalization and that systematic data mismatch dominates the error; the authors accept this within the variable-coefficient eddy-viscosity class and name three loopholes: anisotropy and transport closures leave the class, the learned object can change, and the result covers offline a priori training only (§VII A). **Stated**
27. "A modest, well-chosen dataset consumed by model-consistent or solver-in-the-loop training can outperform a far larger one used for naive a priori regression" (§VII D). **Stated**
28. Benchmarks ranking models by mean-field $L_2$ error alone "would reward exactly the objective–deployment mismatch"; they should score spectral and dissipation fidelity, a posteriori stationarity and coupled stability (§VII E). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* in E2 the residual has units of
acceleration and the enstrophy weights cancel, so $r_\omega$ is in
m s$^{-2}$ and $u_{\mathrm{rms}}r_\omega$ in m$^2$ s$^{-3}$, the units of
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ref}}$; $\mathcal E_r$ is dimensionless as stated.

# Printed slips

- **Table order:** the table printed on p. 091302-18 is "TABLE VI" and precedes "TABLE V" (p. 091302-21).
- **Table II caption:** refers to "Tables 1 and 3" where the paper's tables are I and III.
- **Table III:** the row "Channel LES NN71" describes ref. 71 as "A pri. and a post. in isotropic turbulence".
- **Table II, CFD-driven/online row:** inference cost "Medium/low", while the caption says these methods "incur medium training but low inference cost".
- **Fig. 2 caption against the text:** "an ~0.5% Reynolds-stress error can yield an ~35% mean-velocity error"; the text writes "relative errors below 0.5%" and "up to 35%".
- **Table V, row E1:** gives only $w(k)\propto k^{5/3}$, while §VI D also allows $w(k)\propto k^\alpha$, $\alpha\in[1,2]$.
- **Table I:** TBNN's target is written "$a_{ij}$", Eq. (3) and the text use $b_{ij}$.
- **§VII D:** "The value of additional high-fidelity data are conditional".
