# Summary

Hu et al. (2024) ask what grid convergence means for
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md), which by construction
runs on grids coarse in viscous units. An order-of-magnitude argument
built on the overlap layer and the
[attached-eddy hypothesis](/definitions/attached-eddy-hypothesis.md)
says that quantities with an $O(\delta)$ length scale can converge on
grids with $\delta/\Delta\sim O(10)$, that is $\Delta/\delta_\nu\sim
O(Re_\tau/10)$, without reaching the wall-resolved limit
([grid-point requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)).
The proposition: once the LES is resolved at the matching height
$h_{\mathrm{wm}}$, the wall-model input and therefore the wall stress stop
changing with refinement; since attached eddies scale with wall distance,
convergence sets in at $\Delta_i=C_{\mathrm H} h_{\mathrm{wm},i}$ with a
possibly common constant. By analogy with explicitly filtered
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md), a fixed $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$
then yields a grid-independent solution whose remaining error is
wall-modelling error.

The test uses [CharLES](/software/charles.md) (second-order central
finite volume, RK3, [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md)) with an
algebraic [law-of-the-wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md) on a
[channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at $Re_\tau=1000$,
Spalart's three-dimensional
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) under a
rotating free stream ($Re_l=767$), and a channel at $Re_\tau=934$ with a
sudden spanwise pressure gradient (DNS references,
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)). Wall stress, mean
velocity and mean flow angle converge on coarser grids for larger
$h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ and collapse better against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$,
with onset near $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}\approx0.3$–0.4, which the authors
compare with the [log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md)
criterion. In the 3-D layers the converged wall stress depends on
$h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ because the model assumes unidirectional flow. The
streamwise rms intensity converges more slowly and does not follow
$h_{\mathrm{wm}}$: a spectral split shows the attached-eddy energy
converged and the small-scale-eddy energy not, and resolving 80% of it
needs $\Delta^+\approx50$ at $Re_\tau=1000$. The paper also surveys the
wall-model families ([non-equilibrium](/methods/nonequilibrium-wall-model.md),
[LaRTE](/methods/lagrangian-relaxation-towards-equilibrium-wall-model.md),
[slip-wall](/methods/slip-wall-model.md)) and prior convergence studies
([NASA hump](/benchmarks/nasa-wall-mounted-hump.md), [Gaussian bump](/benchmarks/gaussian-bump.md),
aircraft such as the [CRM high-lift](/benchmarks/nasa-crm-high-lift.md)).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\Delta$ the isotropic grid spacing (the implicit
filter width); $C_{\mathrm H}=\Delta_i/h_{\mathrm{wm},i}$ the convergence ratio
(the source's $C$); $e_q$ the relative error of a quantity $q$ (registry
§11, defined here by Eqs. 7–10, 12); $\gamma_{\mathrm f}=\arctan(\langle w\rangle/\langle u\rangle)$
the mean flow angle (the source's $\gamma$); $\alpha_{\mathrm{ae}}$,
$\beta_{\mathrm{ae}}$, $y^+_{\mathrm{ae}}$ the bounds of the attached-eddy
region in the spectral split (the source's $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$);
$\lambda_x$ streamwise wavelength; $T^*=Tu_\tau/\delta$; $\Pi_z$ the ratio
of spanwise to streamwise pressure gradient (the source's $\Pi$).

1. In implicitly filtered LES numerical and modelling errors are "fundamentally inseparable", because refinement reduces both; explicitly filtered LES with a fixed filter width larger than the grid spacing "is known to produce grid-independent LES solution" (§1). **Stated**
2. Ideally, WMLES grid convergence should be shown on grids with $\Delta^+>O(100)$ and $\Delta/\delta=O(0.1)$, for $Re_\tau=O(10^4)$ (§2, Fig. 1). **Stated**
3. A quantity with length scale $\ell/\delta\sim O(1)$, resolved by $\ell/\Delta\sim O(10)$ cells, can be converged on WMLES grids, which have $\Delta/\delta_\nu=(\Delta/\delta)Re_\tau\sim O(Re_\tau/10)$ (§2, Eqs. 1–4). **Derived** (order of magnitude)
4. From the integral lengths of Sillero et al. and Xu et al.: the mean velocity converges on typical WMLES grids, Reynolds stresses converge more slowly (possibly at $y/\delta>0.2$), and wall-pressure fluctuations may need a spacing of about $0.02\delta$ (§2). **Stated** (inference from cited data)
5. Proposition: WMLES with wall-stress models converges at a coarser grid when the wall-modelled region extends further from the wall, at $\Delta_i=C_{\mathrm H} h_{\mathrm{wm},i}$ with a ratio that "could potentially be the same" across matching heights (§3, Fig. 2). **Stated** (hypothesis, then tested)
6. Many WMLES calculations adopt $C_{\mathrm H}=0.2\sim0.3$ ("about three LES grid cells in the wall-modeled region"); Kawai & Larsson (2012) suggested $C_{\mathrm H}<1$ to remove the log-layer mismatch (§3). **Stated**
7. The converged state can depend on $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$: equilibrium models assume unidirectional flow in the wall-modelled region, so their error grows with $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ in 3-D boundary layers; and, as the filter width in explicitly filtered LES, $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ fixes the range of modelled scales, so the error of the converged WMLES is attributable to wall modelling alone (§3). **Stated**
8. Channel, $Re_\tau=1000$: $e_{\tau_w}$ plateaus from $\Delta/\delta=0.08$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$ and from about $\Delta/\delta=0.02$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.1\delta$; against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ the curves collapse better, with onset at $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}\approx0.4$ (§6.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
9. Rotating-free-stream 3-D layer: the converged wall-stress magnitude is more sensitive to $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ than in the channel and more accurate for smaller $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$; convergence starts at $\Delta=0.05\delta$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.15\delta$ and $0.02\delta$ for $0.05\delta$, with onset at $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}\approx0.3$ (§6.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
10. Transient 3-D channel: the time-integrated wall-stress error (Eq. 8) converges near $\Delta=0.08\delta$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$, while for $0.1\delta$ it still decreases there; collapse is better against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (§6.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
11. All three flows share $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}<0.4$ as the requirement for wall-stress convergence, "comparable to the condition to avoid the log-layer mismatch problem [14]" (§6.1). **Shown**
12. The mean velocity gradient from the mean momentum balance (Eq. 11) is well converged above the matching location ($y^+=200$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$) but grows sharply toward the wall, each refinement adding near-wall information until wall-resolved resolution; convergence "will hardly be achieved below the wall model matching height" (§6.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. Channel mean velocity: $e_u$ (Eq. 9) plateaus on a coarser grid for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$ than for $0.1\delta$, with better collapse against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ for $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}<0.6$ (§6.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
14. Channel streamwise rms: its error is an order of magnitude larger than the mean-velocity error on the same grid, unconverged where the mean flow has converged; the $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ scaling improves the collapse only marginally (§6.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
15. 3-D layer flow angle: $e_{\gamma_{\mathrm f}}$ converges from $\Delta/\delta\approx0.1$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.15\delta$ and at $\Delta/\delta<0.02$ for $0.05\delta$, with better collapse against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$; the mean velocity magnitude is converged except on the coarsest grid, with errors "all around 0.5% of the maximum velocity magnitude" (§6.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
16. 3-D layer: the flow stays laminar on the coarsest grid with $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.15\delta$; $e_{\gamma_{\mathrm f}}=0.01$ is about 0.2°, "approximately 0.3% error in $U$", while the rms error exceeds 2%; the matching location "has no control over convergence of the Reynolds stress" (§6.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. Transient 3-D channel flow angle (averaged over $T^*=0.21$, 0.405, 0.615, 0.825, 1.02): convergence starts near $\Delta=0.03\delta$ for $h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$ and $0.015\delta$ for $0.1\delta$, with better collapse under $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (§6.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
18. Transient 3-D channel rms: the error still decreases on the two finest grids; curves collapse better against $\Delta/\delta$ than against $\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (§6.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
19. Spectral split (Eq. 13, after Hu, Yang & Zheng 2020): the attached-eddy energy of cases 3–5 ($h_{\mathrm{wm}}=0.2\delta$) collapses over $100<y^+<350$; the small-scale-eddy energy is not converged, grows with refinement, and in case 5 is nearly uniform across the log layer (§6.2, Fig. 13). **Shown**
20. The divergent convergence of mean and Reynolds-stress statistics is due to small-scale eddies, which do not scale with wall distance and contribute non-negligibly to the Reynolds stresses, so the attached-eddy proposition applies less to fluctuating quantities (§6.2). **Derived** (interpretation of Fig. 13)
21. From DNS spectra, with at least four cells per scale, spacings of 80 to 30 viscous units capture 70 to 90% of the small-scale-eddy energy; only case 5 ($\Delta^+\approx30$) resolves 90% in the outer region; cases 4 and 5 resolve 80% at $y^+>200$, a condition that "appears to be adequate" for convergence of the small-scale energy ($\Delta^+\approx50$), and "WMLES starts to approach WRLES upon fulfilling this condition" (§6.2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
22. Shrinking $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ with the grid makes WMLES converge toward wall-resolved LES and DNS (the wall model degenerating to the linear no-slip flux), at extreme cost; fixing $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ gives convergence at much coarser resolution but may keep an $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$-dependent wall-model error, "reminiscent of RANS or explicitly filtered LES" (§7). **Stated**
23. To converge WMLES on a target grid, select $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ "somewhat larger than the target grid spacing", preferring wall models of low sensitivity to the matching location (§7). **Stated**
24. Separated boundary layers may require revising the proposition; grid topology and anisotropy are left for future work (§7). **Stated**

The wall model (Eq. 5) is $u^+=y^++a_1(y^+)^2$ for $y^+<23$ and
$u^+=\kappa^{-1}\ln y^++B$ otherwise, $\kappa=0.41$, $B=5.2$, with $a_1$
"calculated to ensure $C^1$ continuity at $y^+=23$" (value not printed);
it sets the wall-stress magnitude, aligned with the LES velocity at
$h_{\mathrm{wm}}$. The errors are $e_{\tau_w}=|\tau_w-\tau_{w,\mathrm{DNS}}|/\tau_{w,\mathrm{DNS}}$
(Eq. 7; time-integrated in Eq. 8) and, after Lozano-Durán & Bae,
$e_u=[(\delta-h_{\mathrm{wm}})^{-1}\int_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}^\delta(\langle u\rangle-\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm{DNS}})^2\mathrm dy]^{1/2}/\max|\langle u\rangle_{\mathrm{DNS}}|$
(Eq. 9; the same form for the rms and for $\gamma_{\mathrm f}$). The
gradient of Eq. (11) is, in registry form,
$\mathrm d\langle u\rangle/\mathrm dy=[\rho R_{12}+\tau_w(1-y/\delta)]/(\mu+\mu_t)$.
The attached-eddy region of Eq. (13) is
$\alpha_{\mathrm{ae}}y<\lambda_x<\beta_{\mathrm{ae}}\delta$, $y^+>y^+_{\mathrm{ae}}$,
with $\alpha_{\mathrm{ae}}=5.7$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ae}}=2$,
$y^+_{\mathrm{ae}}=100$. Grids are isotropic; in the channel
$\Delta/h_{\mathrm{wm}}=2$, 0.667, 0.4, 0.222, 0.154, 0.118 for both
matching heights *(compiler check, not in the sources, from the
$\Delta^+$ of Table 1 with $\delta^+=1000$)*.
