# Summary

Liu et al. (2022) address a resolution problem of cosmological
magnetohydrodynamic simulations of galaxy formation: they cannot resolve the
[small-scale turbulent dynamo](/results/small-scale-dynamo.md). The authors
recall that a turbulent dynamo needs a dissipation scale at least about 30
times smaller than the injection scale. In these codes the dissipation comes
from truncation error, i.e. from
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) of an
[implicit-LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)-type ideal-MHD solver. The
requirement is then a resolution of about 30 pc for a 1 kpc thick disc and
about 3 pc for a 100 pc disc. Under-resolved runs show slow or no
amplification, and the remedy the authors describe, a strong initial seed field, needs
fine tuning.

The paper's contribution is a
[subgrid turbulent dynamo model](/methods/subgrid-turbulent-dynamo-model.md)
in the [RAMSES](/software/ramses.md) adaptive-mesh code. It is a mean-field
closure of the induction equation. The grid-resolved field is the mean field
(the overbar of an implicit [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md)
filter), and the turbulent electromotive force is truncated to an isotropic
$\alpha$ effect. The coefficient is not taken from the subgrid kinetic
helicity. It is the subgrid turbulent velocity dispersion $\sigma_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ of an
existing one-equation SGS turbulent-kinetic-energy model, times a quenching
factor that vanishes once the magnetic energy reaches a fraction $q$ of the
subgrid turbulent energy. $q$ is the only free parameter. It is chosen as
$10^{-3}$ by a low-resolution scan and is stated to depend on resolution.
The dynamo acts only in star-forming gas.

In a zoom-in simulation of a Milky Way-like halo with 55 pc finest cells,
started from a $10^{-20}$ G seed, the field reaches about 100 μG in the
galaxy and about 1 μG in the halo at redshift 4. The galaxy field is
slightly below equipartition with the subgrid turbulence, and the halo is
filled by outflows. At redshift 0.2 the field is about 10 μG in a mostly
toroidal disc field and about 10 nG in the halo. The halo field follows the
ideal-MHD compression scaling $B\propto\rho^{2/3}$, normalized to the
equipartition field of the dense interstellar medium. Mock Faraday-depth
maps are compared qualitatively with quasar data and with AURIGA
simulations. The evidence is one halo, one fiducial run and three
low-resolution runs, with no convergence study.

# Key claims

1. A turbulent dynamo needs the dissipation scale to be smaller than the injection scale by a factor of at least 30, i.e. a flow Reynolds number above a critical value estimated numerically at about 30–60 (§1, citing Brandenburg & Subramanian 2005). **Stated**
2. With numerical dissipation from truncation error, a 1 kpc disc thickness implies a resolution requirement of at least 30 pc, and a 100 pc thin disc implies 3 pc (§1). **Derived** (order of magnitude)
3. Under-resolved MHD galaxy and cluster simulations (Dolag et al. 2002; Dubois et al. 2010; Vazza et al. 2014; Rieder & Teyssier 2017b; Martin-Alvarez et al. 2018) give an unrealistically slow dynamo, which forces a strong, fine-tuned initial seed (§1). **Stated**
4. The mean-field induction equation is $\partial_t\bar{\boldsymbol B}=\nabla\times(\bar{\boldsymbol u}\times\bar{\boldsymbol B}+\boldsymbol{\mathcal E})$ with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}=\overline{\boldsymbol u^<\times\boldsymbol B^<}$. It is closed by the first term of the two-scale expansion and isotropy, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal E}=\alpha_{\mathcal E}\bar{\boldsymbol B}$ (Eqs. 7–12). **Derived** (background)
5. The new closure is $\alpha_{\mathcal E}=\sigma_{\mathrm{sgs}}\max[1-E_{\mathrm{mag}}/(q\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}),0]$, with $\sigma_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ from the subgrid turbulent kinetic energy density $\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\tfrac32\bar\rho\sigma_{\mathrm{sgs}}^2$ (printed $K_T$) and $E_{\mathrm{mag}}=\bar B^2/8\pi$. Once $E_{\mathrm{mag}}$ reaches the fraction $q$ of $\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$, $\alpha_{\mathcal E}$ (printed $\alpha_T$) is strictly zero (Eq. 14, §2.2). **Stated** (model definition; the contribution)
6. The model has one free parameter, the quenching parameter $q$, determined "by experimenting different values"; the fiducial value is $q=10^{-3}$ (§2.2, §4.2, §7). **Shown** (Fig. 1)
7. $q$ should be low, so that the subgrid dynamo only augments the marginally resolved dynamo and the resolved flow brings the field to higher strength. Larger $q$ would give "unrealistically large field strengths close to equipartition everywhere" (§2.2, §4.2). **Stated**
8. $q$ is likely resolution dependent: many $\alpha$-dynamo models suggest $q$ inversely proportional to $Rm$, and $q$ "must be adjusted when changing the resolution" (§4.2, §7). **Stated** (not tested)
9. Saturated ratios of magnetic to turbulent kinetic energy range from 0.1% to 50% (§2.2, citing Schober et al. 2015). **Stated**
10. The subgrid dynamo is restricted to star-forming gas, $n_H>10^{-2}$ cm$^{-3}$ (the threshold of turbulent star formation and self-shielding). Halo and intergalactic fields therefore come only from outflow pollution or the primordial seed (§2.2, §6). **Stated** (model choice)
11. In the low-resolution $q$ scan at redshift 0, all gas within the virial radius is magnetized above the subgrid saturation value $q$. Resolved motions amplify the field by a further 3 to 4 orders of magnitude, "but not much more". With $q=10^{-3}$ the ratio of magnetic to turbulent kinetic energy reaches one or more (§4.2, Fig. 1). **Shown**
12. Low-density gas with $q=10^{-3}$ is "significantly above equipartition with the turbulent energy", because thermal energy dominates there and the field comes from outflows (§4.2). **Shown**
13. The subgrid dynamo e-folding time is $h/\sigma_{\mathrm{sgs}}$, i.e. 3 Myr for 100 pc and 30 km/s (§5.1.1). **Derived**
14. Before collapse the seed follows the frozen-in scaling $P_{\mathrm{mag}}=P_{\mathrm{ini}}(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{ini}})^{4/3}$. After the subgrid dynamo saturates, the upper envelope of the $P_{\mathrm{mag}}$–$\rho$ diagram follows $P_{\mathrm{mag}}=P_{\mathrm{gal}}(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{gal}})^{4/3}$ with $P_{\mathrm{gal}}\simeq K_T$ in the dense interstellar medium (§5.1.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
15. The upper-envelope scaling at redshift 0.2 is almost two orders of magnitude below that at redshift 4: fields are stronger in the past (§5.1.1). **Shown**
16. At redshift 4 the field is about 100 μG in the galaxy and about 1 μG in the halo, where it comes from metal-enriched outflows (Fig. 3). In the central region ($n=100$ cm$^{-3}$, $T=100$ K, $\beta_p=10^{-2}$) the magnetic pressure is about $10^{-9}$ barye, the 1D velocity dispersion about 30 km/s and the turbulent energy about $2\times10^{-9}$ erg cm$^{-3}$; the field is "slightly below equipartition (close to 0.5)" (§5.1.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
17. Faraday depth at redshift 4 is below 0.1 rad m$^{-2}$ in filaments, above 1 rad m$^{-2}$ in the inner halo and above 1000 rad m$^{-2}$ in places in the galaxy. There is no large-scale signal; the in-beam dispersion is about one order of magnitude larger than the mean (strong depolarization by a small-scale turbulent field) (§5.1.3, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
18. The simulated Faraday depths are systematically lower than in the 49-quasar sample of Kim et al. (2016), with the caveat of one halo against a range of redshifts and masses (§5.1.3, §6). **Shown**
19. At redshift 0.2 the field is about 10 μG in the disc, 100 μG in the nuclear region and tens of nG in the circumgalactic medium. In the central disc ($T\approx100$ K, $n\approx10$ cm$^{-3}$, subgrid dispersion about 10 km/s) the peak field is about 20 μG, $\beta_p\simeq10^{-2}$, and the peak magnetic pressure of about $2\times10^{-11}$ barye is in rough equipartition with the subgrid turbulent pressure. The subgrid dynamo is "totally ineffective" at late times (§5.2, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
20. The face-on Faraday depth is about 20 rad m$^{-2}$ in the disc and about $10^{-2}$ rad m$^{-2}$ in the halo, in good agreement with Pakmor et al. (2018); about 0.1 rad m$^{-2}$ in the circumgalactic medium (§5.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
21. The disc field is dominated by a toroidal component (about 10 μG) with the same orientation across the disc and one reversal at $r=0.5$ kpc. The radial and vertical components flip several times, with standard deviation exceeding the mean. There are no reversals at large radii, "in disagreement with the findings of Pakmor et al. (2018)" (§5.2, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
22. The authors attribute the dominant toroidal field and the missing reversals to absent resolved turbulence in the barely resolved (55 pc) thin disc (§5.2, §6). **Stated** (hypothesis)
23. The field is a factor 2 to 4 weaker than in Pakmor et al. (2020), "possibly a consequence of our less turbulent halo gas" (§6). **Shown**/**Stated**
24. Because $\alpha_{\mathcal E}$ is a positive scalar, the subgrid dynamo amplifies the initial field parallel to itself. The initial orientation is preserved except where resolved turbulence randomizes it, so the initial topology and coherence length matter (§6). **Derived** (qualitative)
25. Earlier simulations with the unmodified induction equation required $\Delta x\simeq10$ pc for a fast turbulent dynamo in a dwarf galaxy (§6, Rieder & Teyssier 2016, 2017b). **Stated**
26. By redshift 0 the whole region within ten virial radii is polluted by winds with fields of about 1 nG, and no pocket of pristine $10^{-20}$ G gas remains, in contrast with the volume-non-filling winds of Bertone et al. (2006) (§6). **Shown**

The model drops the turbulent-diffusivity term $\beta_{ijk}$ of the two-scale
expansion (Eq. 10 to Eq. 11), so it has no turbulent magnetic diffusion; the
authors call the closure "very simple (probably simplistic)" (§6). The SGS
turbulent-kinetic-energy model that supplies $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ is used but not printed:
no equation or constants appear (§2.3, referring to Schmidt et al. 2006,
Semenov et al. 2016, Kretschmer & Teyssier 2020).

# Printed slips

- **Fig. 2 caption:** "using quenching parameter $q=10^3$"; the text (§2.2, §4.2) gives the fiducial $q=10^{-3}$.
- **Eq. 13:** printed $\alpha=-\frac{\tau_{\rm corr}}{3}\overline{\mathbf u\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf u)}$ without primes, while the text says it uses "the average subgrid kinetic helicity" and Eq. 7 marks the unresolved fluctuations with primes.
- **Eqs. 11–12:** Eq. 11 prints $\mathcal E_i$ without the overbar of Eqs. 8, 9 and 12, and the sentence before Eq. 12 says "Eq. 9 becomes" where Eq. 11 is the one that becomes Eq. 12.
- **Units:** §2.2 writes $E_{\rm mag}=\overline B^2/8\pi$ (Gaussian), while the MHD system (Eqs. 2–3) writes the magnetic energy and pressure as $\tfrac12B^2$ (rationalized units). The meaning of $B$ in the quenching ratio $E_{\rm mag}/(qK_T)$ depends on which convention is meant.
- **Fig. 11 caption:** "Magnetic field topology at $z=2$". The figure is discussed in §5.2, the section on the redshift-0.2 snapshot.
- **Resolution threshold quoted twice:** §1 says the resolved dwarf-galaxy dynamo needs about 30 pc, "roughly what was observed" in Rieder & Teyssier (2016, 2017b); §6 says it "has to be better than $\Delta x\simeq10$ pc" for the same references.
- **Missing table:** §2 announces "Finally, we list our various parameters used in our galaxy formation model", but no parameter table follows.
- **Abstract:** "consistent will the ideal MHD dilution factor".
- **Reference list:** "Kretschmer M., Teyssier R., 2019, MNRAS, 492, 1385" and "Kretschmer M., Teyssier R., 2020, MNRAS, 492, 1385" print the same volume and page under two years. "Schmidt W., Federrath C., 2011a, A&A, 528, 106" and "2011b, A&A, 528, A106" print the same volume and article twice.
