# Summary

Gao et al. (2025) bring [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
into a high-order computational-aeroacoustics finite-difference code,
where wall-resolved LES or the group's own high-order
[DDES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) (with the
[spectral difference method](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md)),
which still need $y^+\approx1$, are too costly at high Reynolds number
([grid-point requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)).
The first contribution is a [slip-wall condition](/methods/slip-wall-model.md):
the compressible [equilibrium wall-model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md)
ODEs, with a van Driest-damped mixing-length eddy viscosity and
$Pr_t=0.9$ ([turbulent Prandtl number](/definitions/turbulent-prandtl-number.md)),
are solved on a 100-point 1-D virtual mesh fed by the LES velocity at the
second off-wall grid point; the constant total stress gives the velocity
gradient at that point, and a linear extrapolation to the wall gives an
explicit slip velocity, imposed tangentially with zero normal velocity.
The LES uses the [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md), the seven-point
fourth-order DRP scheme of Tam & Webb with the Bogey–Bailly selective
filter ([low-dissipation low-dispersion schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md))
and four-step Adams–Bashforth time stepping. The second contribution is a
low-noise [synthetic inflow](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md):
steady, spanwise-periodic outer-layer modes (a time-independent
simplification of Sandham's Fourier modes) injected as source terms in the
Tam & Dong radiation boundary condition.

Validation: a Mach 0.3 [channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
at $Re_\tau\approx2000$ on four grids, where the mean velocity follows the
[log law](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) for sample heights
$y^+=55$–220 without [log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md),
Reynolds stresses match DNS for $y/\delta>0.2$, and wall-pressure spectra
agree within 3–6 dB at 20–30 points per $\delta$; the same resolution
holds for $Re_\tau=576$–5100, and the spectra agree better with DNS than
those of an [OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md) WMLES of Fan et al. A Mach 0.15
flat-plate [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
becomes turbulent after $20\delta_0$ and matches Spalart's DNS for
$y>0.2\delta$, but its wall-pressure spectrum is 8.6 dB above Goody's
model and its rms wall pressure 3.8 times Viazzo's correlation, which the
authors attribute to fluctuations passed through the sample point and to
grid coarseness. The introduction surveys
[non-equilibrium](/methods/nonequilibrium-wall-model.md),
[machine-learning](/methods/machine-learning-wall-model.md) and
[immersed-boundary](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) wall models;
the conclusions contrast direct far-field noise from the solver with the
[Ffowcs Williams–Hawkings analogy](/methods/ffowcs-williams-hawkings-acoustic-analogy.md).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $U_{\mathrm{wm}}$, $T_{\mathrm{wm}}$ the wall-model
velocity and temperature; $\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ the wall-model eddy
viscosity (registry §11); $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ and $U_{\mathrm{LES}}$ the
sample height and the wall-parallel LES velocity there (registry §5; the
source's $\delta_p$, $u_\parallel$); $u_{\mathrm{slip}}$ the imposed wall
slip velocity (registry §11 $\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{slip}}$); $\omega_{\mathrm a}$
the angular frequency of the wall-pressure spectrum $\phi_{pp}$;
$R_T$ the time-scale ratio of Goody's model; $V_g$ the acoustic group
velocity of the radiation condition.

1. Grid requirements scale as $N_{\mathrm{DNS}}\sim Re^{9/4}$, $N_{\mathrm{WRLES}}\sim Re^{13/7}$ and $N_{\mathrm{WMLES}}\sim Re$, citing Choi & Moin (ref. 1) (§I). **Stated**
2. The slip condition prescribes the tangential velocity at the wall with zero normal penetration; the slip velocity comes from a linearized near-wall velocity profile at the sample point (Abstract, §II.B, Fig. 2, Eqs. 13–18). **Derived**
3. The wall-model input is sampled at the second grid point to mitigate numerical and SGS errors at the first off-wall point, following Kawai & Larsson (ref. 8) (§II.B.1). **Stated**
4. The wall-model ODEs are solved on a 1-D virtual mesh of 100 points with hyperbolic-tangent stretching 2.0 (after Bala, ref. 42) by first-order backward differences in finite-volume form, giving a tridiagonal system solved iteratively for $\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ and $U_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (§II.B.1). **Stated**
5. With zero eddy viscosity at the wall, integration of Eq. (8) gives the constant total stress $(\mu+\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}})\,dU_{\mathrm{wm}}/dy|_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}=\tau_w$, and with $dU_{\mathrm{wm}}/dy|_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}=(U_{\mathrm{LES}}-u_{\mathrm{slip}})/h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ the explicit slip velocity of Eq. (18) (§II.B.2). **Derived**
6. The Vreman model, the authors state, reduces numerical dissipation relative to Smagorinsky and is as accurate as the dynamic Smagorinsky model without spatial averaging or filtering (§II.A). **Stated**
7. Space: seven-point fourth-order DRP scheme (Tam & Webb); time: four-step fourth-order Adams–Bashforth with Courant number below 0.15; Bogey–Bailly selective damping filter (§II.A). **Stated**
8. Channel at Mach 0.3, $Re_\tau\approx2000$, domain $2\pi\delta\times2\delta\times\pi\delta$, four grids C101015, C202030, C203030, C404060 (Table II); filter amplitude 0.15 every six steps for C404060 and 0.2 every three steps otherwise (§III.A.1). **Shown**
9. The mean velocity agrees with the Hoyas & Jiménez DNS at all resolutions and follows the log law for first-point placements $y^+=55$–220, "effectively circumventing the log-layer mismatch (LLM) phenomenon that typically plagues conventional detached eddy simulation (DES) and WMLES approaches" (§III.A.1, Fig. 5). **Shown** (mean profiles)
10. Reynolds stresses deviate from DNS for $y/\delta<0.1$ in all cases; C202030 and C203030 agree with DNS for $y/\delta>0.2$; C101015 over-predicts the stresses beyond $y/\delta>0.2$ (§III.A.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
11. Wall-pressure spectrum: C203030 agrees with Farabee & Casarella over $10\le St\le300$ ($St=\omega_{\mathrm a}\delta/u_\tau$); C101015 over-predicts, with a premature high-frequency roll-off; C202030 and C203030 balance accuracy and cost best, $(\delta/\Delta x,\delta/\Delta y,\delta/\Delta z)=(20,20\text{–}30,30)$, with errors within 3–6 dB at low-to-mid frequency (§III.A.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
12. Spectra by Welch's method, 50% overlap, $\Delta St=2.89$, 20–30 independent blocks; one-sided spectra rescaled by $4\pi$ to compare with the two-sided Farabee–Casarella spectra (§III.A.1). **Stated**
13. The recommended resolution reproduces the log-law mean velocity and the Reynolds stresses for $y/\delta>0.2$ across $576\le Re_\tau\le5100$ (Table III, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
14. Predicted wall-pressure spectra are elevated at low-to-mid frequency and decay sharply beyond $St=500$ at all $Re_\tau$; discrepancies are within about 4 dB over $St=10$–400 at $Re_\tau=576$ and 2100, up to 5 dB at $Re_\tau=1050$ (attributed to the coarse wall-normal grid) (§III.A.2, Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
15. The low-frequency excess is attributed to velocity fluctuations at the sample point reaching the wall through the wall function, the high-frequency deficit to grid coarseness (§III.A.2, §IV). **Stated** (interpretation)
16. Against the OpenFOAM WMLES of Fan et al. (ref. 48) at $Re_\tau=1000$ (grid $127\times101\times127$), the present method on $127\times41\times95$ agrees better with the DNS spectra, which the authors read as a limitation of low-order schemes (§III.A.2, Fig. 10). **Shown** (against a cited result)
17. The synthetic inflow injects steady divergence-free fluctuations (Eq. 22) as source terms in the Tam & Dong radiation condition (Eq. 21); time invariance minimizes unsteady effects and spurious noise (§III.B, Appendix). **Stated**
18. Flat plate at Mach 0.15, $\delta_0=0.004$ m, $Re_{\delta_0}=1.4\times10^4$, inlet profile $(y/\delta_0)^{1/6}$: the flow is fully turbulent after $20\delta_0$ and the boundary-layer thickness reaches $1.2\delta_0$ at $35\delta_0$ (§III.B.2, Fig. 13, Table VI). **Shown**
19. At $x=35\delta_0$ the mean velocity agrees with Spalart's DNS and the LES of Wang & Wang in the outer region, slightly under-predicted near the wall; turbulence intensities and Reynolds stress agree with the DNS for $y>0.2\delta$ (Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
20. Near the inlet the wall-pressure spectra are dominated by low frequencies; downstream the low-frequency part decays and mid-to-high frequencies grow; the spectra are self-similar by $x=20\delta$ and $30\delta$ (Fig. 17). **Shown**
21. Against Goody's model at $x=35\delta_0$ the WMLES spectrum is over-predicted; a vertical shift of 8.6 dB aligns it with Goody up to $St=600$; the mid-frequency decay is $\omega_{\mathrm a}^{-0.7}$ and the high-frequency decay steeper than $\omega_{\mathrm a}^{-5}$ (Fig. 18). **Shown**
22. Viazzo's correlation (Eq. 25) "suggests an ideal $p_{rms}$ value of 10.29 Pa"; the simulation gives about 15 Pa near the inflow (background noise), rising to 39.58 Pa at $x=35\delta_0$, about 3.8 times the expected value (§III.B.2, Fig. 19). **Shown**
23. Limitations: up to 8.6 dB error remains in the boundary layer; the condition adds low-frequency acoustic contributions; the slip condition does not account for natural transition (§III.B.2). **Stated**
24. Future work: boundary layers over Gaussian bumps and cavities; direct far-field noise from the CAA solver without FW–H; a non-equilibrium extension with pressure-gradient source terms in the wall-model equations (§IV). **Stated**

The model in registry notation. The LES equations (Eqs. 4–7) are written
without filter symbols, with total stress $2(\mu+\mu_t)S^d_{ij}$ and heat
flux $-(k_{\mathrm{th}}+k_{\mathrm{th},t})\partial_jT$, $Pr=0.72$. The
wall model (Eqs. 8–10) is
$$\frac{d}{dy}\Big[(\mu+\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}})\frac{dU_{\mathrm{wm}}}{dy}\Big]=0,\qquad \frac{d}{dy}\Big[(\mu+\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}})U_{\mathrm{wm}}\frac{dU_{\mathrm{wm}}}{dy}+c_p\Big(\frac{\mu}{Pr}+\frac{\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}}}{Pr_t}\Big)\frac{dT_{\mathrm{wm}}}{dy}\Big]=0,$$
$$\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\kappa\rho y\sqrt{\tau_w/\rho}\,\big[1-\exp(-y^+/A^+)\big]^2,\qquad\kappa=0.41,\ A^+=17,$$
with Sutherland's law for $\mu$, $\tau_w=\mu u_1/y_1$ at the first
virtual cell centre (Eq. 12), and the slip velocity (Eq. 18)
$$u_{\mathrm{slip}}=U_{\mathrm{LES}}-h_{\mathrm{wm}}\frac{\tau_w}{(\mu+\mu^w_{\mathrm{sgs}})|_{y=h_{\mathrm{wm}}}}.$$
The reference log law of the figures is $u^+=\ln(y^+)/0.41+5.2$. The
synthetic modes (Eq. 22, Table IV) are three outer modes
$u'=\sum_jc_{1j}[\tfrac{y}{y_{p,j}}e^{-y/y_{p,j}}+\alpha_1y^2e^{-\theta_jy^2}]\cos(\beta_jz+\phi_j)$
and similar $v'$, $w'$, with $c_{1j}=0.24U_0$, $c_{2j}=-0.1U_0$,
$y_{p,2}=0.1\delta$, $\beta_2=2\pi/(7\Delta z\times1.33)$, no $x$ or $t$
dependence. Goody's spectrum (Eq. 24) is
$\phi_{pp}U_\infty/(\tau_w^2\delta)=3.0(\omega_{\mathrm a}\delta/U_\infty)^2/\{[(\omega_{\mathrm a}\delta/U_\infty)^{0.75}+0.5]^{3.7}+[1.1R_T^{-0.57}\omega_{\mathrm a}\delta/U_\infty]^7\}$
($R_T$ not printed for the case), and Viazzo's correlation (Eq. 25) is
$\langle p'^2\rangle/\tau_w^2=3+2\ln(Re_\tau/250)$.
