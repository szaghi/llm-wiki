# Summary

Yu et al. (2023b) run [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
with [STREAmS](/software/streams.md) of a Mach 5 cold-wall
[turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
impinged by oblique shocks from $14^\circ$, $10^\circ$ and $6^\circ$
wedges (strong, mild and incipient separation), a case of
[shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md).
The convective scheme is the sixth-order
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
central scheme switched to fifth-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) by
a modified Ducros [shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md); the inflow
is a digital-filter [synthetic turbulence](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md),
validated through the van Driest [velocity transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md).
Large-scale streaks amplified in the interaction are attributed to the
instability of the separated shear layer rather than to Görtler-type
circulations; the peak wall heat flux follows a power law of the peak
wall pressure with exponent about 0.8, and the streamwise heat flux a law
with exponent 0.6.

The DNS then serves for [a priori tests](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md):
the best-fit linear eddy viscosity of the
[Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) fails in
the interaction zone, where strain and stress are misaligned, and a
strain–rotation commutator term of the
[explicit algebraic](/methods/explicit-algebraic-reynolds-stress-model.md)
type repairs it; the [$k$-$\varepsilon$](/methods/k-epsilon-model.md)
estimate $\mu_t=C_\mu\bar\rho k^2/\varepsilon$ with $C_\mu=0.09$
overestimates the eddy viscosity downstream, which a wall-pressure-dependent
$C_\mu(x)$ fixes. For the turbulent heat flux, a rotation-based nonlinear
term beats gradient diffusion. In the [$k$-$\omega$ SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)
$\beta^*$ is replaced by $C_\mu(x)$ and the
[turbulent Prandtl number](/definitions/turbulent-prandtl-number.md) is
lowered in pressure gradients; RANS of the Schülein Mach 5 interactions
improves skin friction and the Stanton-number peak (error from about 50%
to under 10%), and RANS of the Holden Mach 8 compression ramps the
separation extent.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ the inlet boundary-layer
thickness and $\delta_r$ that at the reference station
$x_r=30\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ (registry §11); $x_\delta=(x-x_{\mathrm{imp}})/\delta_r$,
$y_\delta=y/\delta_r$ (the paper's $x^*$, $y^*$); $L_{\mathrm{sep}}$ the
separation length and $L_{\mathrm{int}}$ the interaction length (registry
§11), both over $\delta_r$; $q_w$ the wall heat flux, $q_{w,r}$ its
reference value; $C_h$ the Stanton number; $\Gamma_r$ the flow-reversal
probability; $G_T$ the Görtler number; $\mu_{t2}$ the coefficient of the
nonlinear stress term; $C_{\mu,1}$ the pressure-sensitivity coefficient;
$\kappa_{h,t}$, $\kappa_{h,t2}$, $\kappa_{h,tS}$, $\kappa_{h,tA}$ the
turbulent conductivities of the heat-flux models; $\lambda_z$ the spanwise
wavelength. Averages: $\langle\cdot\rangle$ Reynolds, $\langle\cdot\rangle_\rho$
Favre; $R_{ij}=\langle\rho u_i''u_j''\rangle$ (positive trace).

1. DNS of $M_\infty=5$ oblique-shock interaction at $Re_\infty=\rho_\infty U_\infty\delta_{\mathrm{in}}/\mu_\infty=3.3\times10^4$, $T_w=0.75T_r$, wedges $14^\circ$/$10^\circ$/$6^\circ$ (shock angles $23.3^\circ$/$19.4^\circ$/$15.9^\circ$), impingement at $50\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$, domain $100\times10\times6.5\,\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ on $3600\times360\times256$ points, $\Delta x^+\approx4.16$, $\Delta z^+\approx3.81$, $\Delta y^+_w=0.3$ (§II, Table I). **Stated**
2. At $x_r$: $Re_{99}\approx48\,800$, $Re_{\delta^*}\approx19\,400$, $Re_\theta\approx2087$; the van Driest velocity follows the linear ($y^+\lesssim5$) and log ($y^+=30$–60) laws and the normal stresses collapse with the Pirozzoli & Bernardini (2011) $M=2$ DNS (§II, Eqs. 7–9, Fig. 1). **Shown**
3. $L_{\mathrm{sep}}=6.75$, 2.46, 1.47 and $L_{\mathrm{int}}=6.74$, 3.18, 2.26 for S14, S10, S06 (§III B, Table I). **Shown**
4. Mean wall pressure agrees with Volpiani et al. (2020) except a shorter shoulder at $x_\delta\approx-4$ in S14, and $C_f$ is slightly higher, both attributed to the lower Reynolds number; separation lengths are about the same (§III B, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
5. In S14 the large-scale low-speed streaks in the separation bubble come without strong large-scale cross-flow circulations; the authors conclude they stem from the instability of the shear layer, not from Görtler-type circulations (§III A, Fig. 2). **Shown** (interpretation of visualizations)
6. In S06 the low-speed streaks do not form larger-scale motions, and their relation with quasi-streamwise vortices resembles canonical wall turbulence (§III A, Fig. 2e–f). **Shown**
7. $q_{w,\max}/q_{w,r}=(\langle p_w\rangle_{\max}/p_\infty)^n$ with the data between $n=0.85$ and 0.75; $n=0.8$ predicts the maximum within 15% (§III B, Eq. 11, Fig. 5a). **Shown**
8. $q_w(x)/q_{w,r}=C_m(\langle p_w\rangle(x)/p_\infty)^m$ with $m=0.6$ is more universal than the compression-ramp values of Priebe & Martín and Helm & Martín; $C_m\approx1.0$ downstream of separation and $\approx2.0$ at the heat-flux peaks; cold-wall oblique-shock interactions only (§III B, Eq. 12, Fig. 5b). **Shown**
9. $\Gamma_r$ at the wall never exceeds 0.8 even in S14, while a small near-wall region at $x_\delta\approx-2$ has $\Gamma_r\approx1.0$ (§III C, Fig. 7). **Shown**
10. The critical Görtler number $G_T=0.58$ and curvature parameter $\delta/R\approx0.11$ are exceeded over a large region only in S14 (§III C, Eq. 13, Fig. 8). **Shown**
11. The maximum of $u''_{\mathrm{rms}}$ rises with the shear layer and collapses with the sonic line, while $v''_{\mathrm{rms}}$ and $\langle\rho u''v''\rangle$ peak near $x_\delta=0$, supporting the non-circulation origin of the streaks (§III D, Figs. 9–11). **Shown**
12. Turbulence has not recovered to the canonical state at the outlet even in S06, attributed to the slow decay of the enhanced large-scale motions (§III D). **Shown**
13. The turbulent heat flux $\langle v''T'\rangle$ is trivial below the sonic line in the interaction zone (§III D, Fig. 12). **Shown**
14. Integrated spanwise spectra peak at $\lambda_z/\delta_r\approx1$ upstream and downstream, slightly wider in the S14 interaction zone; positive large-scale $E_{uv}$ near $x_\delta\approx-2$ to $-1$ is conjectured to come from shock-induced correlated $u$ and $v$ fluctuations; temperature and heat-flux spectra peak at $\approx2\delta_r$ (§III E, Figs. 13–16). **Shown**
15. The velocity scale should switch from $u_\tau$ to one tied to the outer mean shear, and the length scale from $y$ to $\delta$, downstream of the interaction (§III E). **Stated**
16. A priori, the best-fit linear eddy viscosity (Eqs. 17–18) reconstructs $-R_{12}$ well upstream and downstream but severely overpredicts it in the interaction zone, especially along the rising shear layer $x_\delta=-6$ to $-3$, because $\langle S\rangle^d_{ij}$ and $R_{ij}$ are misaligned (§IV, Fig. 17a). **Shown**
17. Adding $\mu_{t2}(\langle S\rangle^d_{ik}\langle\Omega\rangle_{kj}-\langle\Omega\rangle_{ik}\langle S\rangle^d_{kj})$ (Eq. 19) collapses the reconstruction onto the DNS, justified by the traceless stress having two independent components in a 2-D mean flow (§IV, Fig. 17b). **Shown**
18. $\mu_t=C_\mu\bar\rho k^2/\varepsilon$ with $C_\mu=0.09$ matches the DNS eddy viscosity upstream but severely overestimates it downstream (at $y_\delta=0.5$, S14), as the paper says Morgan et al. (2013) also found (§IV, Eq. 20, Fig. 18a). **Shown**
19. $C_\mu(x)=0.09/[1+C_{\mu,1}(\langle p_w\rangle(x)/\langle p_w\rangle_r-1)]$ with $C_{\mu,1}=1$ (data fit) predicts the downstream eddy viscosity well, and the reconstructed $-R_{12}$ matches DNS except where separation begins (§IV, Eq. 21, Figs. 18b, 19). **Shown**
20. Linear gradient diffusion $\langle u_i'T'\rangle=-\kappa_{h,t}\partial_i\langle T\rangle$ (Eqs. 22–23) fails only in the interaction zone; the velocity-gradient term $-\kappa_{h,t2}\partial_k\langle T\rangle\partial_k\langle u_i\rangle$ (Eq. 24) worsens it, the strain term $-\kappa_{h,tS}\partial_k\langle T\rangle\langle S\rangle^d_{ik}$ (Eq. 25) helps slightly, the rotation term $-\kappa_{h,tA}\partial_k\langle T\rangle\langle\Omega\rangle_{ik}$ (Eq. 26) works much better (§IV, Fig. 20). **Shown**
21. In SST $\beta^*$ plays the role of $C_\mu$; the refinement replaces $\beta^*$ by Eq. (21) and sets $Pr_t=0.9-0.5\tanh(10|\nabla p\,\delta/U_\infty|)$, because $Pr_t$ is below unity in the non-equilibrium interaction zone (§V). **Stated** (the $Pr_t$ formula is given without derivation)
22. RANS of the Schülein $M=5$ interactions ($14^\circ$, $10^\circ$): the refined SST improves $C_f$ and particularly $C_h$, whose peak error falls from about 50% to less than 10% (§V, Fig. 21). **Shown**
23. The original SST underpredicts $k$ and the Reynolds shear stress against the S14 DNS; the revised model is better downstream but still inaccurate inside the interaction, where a nonlinear term would be needed unless only wall quantities matter (§V, Fig. 22). **Shown**
24. Holden et al. $M=8$ ramps ($27^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $33^\circ$): the original SST overpredicts separation at $27^\circ$ and $30^\circ$ and underpredicts it at $33^\circ$; the refined SST gives the separation onset and plateau at $33^\circ$ and no separation at the smallest angle, as in experiment (§V, Figs. 23–24). **Shown**
25. Lowering $C_\mu$ reduces the modelled dissipation and raises $k$ and the shear stress; the $\omega$ equation ties the dissipation of mean and turbulent kinetic energy, while the separated shear layer raises production without instantly raising turbulent dissipation, so $\omega$ is overpredicted downstream; $C_\mu(x)$ relieves this, and RANS constants such as $C_{\mu,1}$ can differ from DNS-fitted values (§V). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Table I: the S10 separation length is printed "2, 46"; §III B gives 2.46.
- §V: the ramps are $27^\circ$, $30^\circ$, $33^\circ$ (text, Figs. 23–24), but the refined model "shows no region of flow separation in the case with a 22° ramp angle".
- Eq. (28): the cross-diffusion coefficient is printed $\sigma_{w2}$, against $\sigma_{\omega2}$ in Eqs. (30)–(31) and the constant list, and the product is printed $\partial_jk\,\partial_i\omega$ with mismatched indices (Eq. 31: $\partial_ik\,\partial_i\omega$).
- The text before Eq. (30) names the blending function "$F_l$", Eq. (30) $F_1$; Eq. (32) writes $\mu_T$, Eqs. (27)–(29) $\mu_t$; $a_1$ of Eq. (32) has no printed value.
- Eq. (8): $u^+_{vd}=\frac1{u_\tau}\int_0^{\tilde u^+}\sqrt{\bar\rho/\bar\rho_w}\,du$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with the dimensionless upper limit $\tilde u^+$ the integration variable is dimensionless, and the prefactor $1/u_\tau$ then gives the result the dimension of an inverse velocity.)*
- Fig. 19 caption: "Reynolds shear stress $R_{12}$ reconstructed by Eq. (20)"; the text substitutes Eq. (20) with $C_\mu(x)$ of Eq. (21) into Eq. (17).
- Abstract: "the turbulent kinetic equation"; Eq. (17) "$\delta ij$"; "Görlter" (§III A), "Piasquariello" (§III C), "Staton number" (§V).
- Eq. (9) prints $\overline{\rho u_iu_j}/\tau_w$ without fluctuation primes; Eqs. (22)–(26) use Reynolds fluctuations $u_i'T'$ while the stresses use Favre fluctuations, unexplained.
