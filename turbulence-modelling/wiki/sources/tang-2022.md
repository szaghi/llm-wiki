# Summary

Tang et al. (2022) assess the flamelet/progress-variable (FPV) model of
Pierce & Moin ([flamelet-based tabulated chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md))
for supersonic hydrogen combustion. The turbulence is computed with an
in-house [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) solver. Away from
walls it runs [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) with Yoshizawa's
one-equation subgrid kinetic-energy model, recast as a transport equation
for $\nu_t$. In a thin near-wall layer it runs the
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md). A
wall-distance blending function $F$ merges the two into one equation for
$\tilde\nu$. The RANS layer covers about 10–20% of the boundary-layer
thickness. Inviscid fluxes use fifth-order Jiang–Shu
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md).

The FPV table is read with $\tilde Z$, $\widetilde{Z''^2}$ and $\tilde C$,
using a $\beta$-PDF in mixture fraction and a $\delta$-PDF in progress
variable. The paper compares two closures of the subgrid mixture-fraction
variance $\widetilde{Z''^2}$. The first is an algebraic
[scale-similarity](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) reconstruction with a
test filter $\hat\Delta=2\Delta$. The second is a transport equation with a
modelled scalar dissipation.

Two experiments are simulated: the DLR strut-injection scramjet and the
NUDT cavity-stabilized combustor. The scale-similarity variance is locally
high but confined to regions of large mixture-fraction gradient. In the DLR
case it raises the heat release near the injector and shortens the flame
lift-off. In the NUDT case it over-intensifies combustion in the jet wake
and induces boundary-layer separation upstream of the cavity. The
transport-equation variance has lower peaks spread over a wider region. It
agrees better with the DLR temperature profiles at $x=125$ mm and with the
NUDT wall pressure. A Takeno flame index shows that diffusion burning
dominates both cases, which the authors take as support for the FPV
premise. They recommend the transport equation when resources allow. All
comparisons with experiment are visual; no error norms are given.

# Key claims

1. The hybrid model uses LES with Yoshizawa's one-equation SGS kinetic-energy model in the core and the Spalart–Allmaras model in a thin near-wall layer, blended into one transport equation for $\tilde\nu$ by a blending function $F$ (§2.1, Eqs. 1–9). **Stated** (after Wang et al. 2015 and Sánchez-Rocha & Menon)
2. Substituting $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\nu_t^2/(C_\mu^2\Delta^2)$ into the SGS $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ equation gives an eddy-viscosity transport equation (Eq. 3) with extra terms $P_\Delta$ from a spatially varying $\Delta$ (Eq. 4) (§2.1). **Derived** (as stated, after Wang et al. 2015)
3. The RANS region is "quite thin (≈ (0.1∼0.2)$\delta_{inf}$)"; its height is usually 10–20% of the boundary-layer thickness, and LES covers the main regions of interest (§2.1). **Stated**
4. Constants: $C_\mu=0.02075$, $C_d=1.0$, $Pr_t=0.9$ (SGS model); $C_{f1}=2.0$, $C_{f2}=0.2$ (blending); $\Gamma(F)=F^{20}$ "for the sake of fast transition" (§2.1, Eqs. 1, 8, 9). **Stated**
5. Numerics: fifth-order WENO (Jiang–Shu) for inviscid fluxes, second-order central differences for viscous fluxes, second-order implicit dual time stepping with LU-SGS inner iterations, CFL 0.5; MPI multi-zone with three ghost layers (§2.1). **Stated**
6. FPV: the thermochemical state $\phi(Z,C)$ is filtered with a presumed $\beta$-PDF in $Z$ and a $\delta$-PDF in $C$ (fast chemistry), and the table is read with $\tilde Z$, $\widetilde{Z''^2}$, $\tilde C$ (§2.2, Eqs. 10–11). **Stated**
7. Scale-similarity variance with test filter $\hat\Delta=2\Delta$, evaluated with a discrete seven-point formula (Eqs. 15–16). **Stated**
8. Transport equation for $\widetilde{Z''^2}$ with a modelled scalar dissipation, $Sc_t=0.5$, $c_q=40.0$ (Eq. 17). **Stated**
9. DLR cold flow: wall and centreline pressures agree reasonably with experiment, except the peaks at $x\approx130$ mm (lower wall) and $x\approx110$ mm (centreline), also missed by earlier RANS (§3.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. DLR reacting: the scale-similarity variance peaks near 0.01 but is mostly below 0.001; the transport-equation variance spreads over a larger interval, below 0.002 (§3.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
11. A higher variance gives "a bit higher heat release" in the flamelet database under the same conditions (§3.1). **Stated** (database inspection not shown)
12. DLR: scale similarity predicts higher heat release near the jet exit ($x=80$–90 mm), a shorter flame lift-off and an upstream-shifted flame core; the transport equation gives a longer lift-off (§3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. DLR: the transport-equation closure agrees better with the measured temperature at $x=125$ mm and with the velocity (§3.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
14. The Takeno flame index (Eq. 18), positive for premixed and negative for non-premixed burning, shows diffusion combustion dominating the whole reactive zone in both cases, with premixed burning confined to a small downstream area (§3.1, Fig. 8; §3.2, Fig. 15). **Shown**
15. NUDT cold flow: lower-wall pressure agrees with measurement; small differences near inlet and outlet are attributed to locally relaxed grids and measurement discrepancies (§3.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
16. NUDT reacting: the scale-similarity variance is higher in almost the whole jet wake and in the separated boundary layer upstream, attributed to the transverse injection confining the fuel with high mixture-fraction gradients (§3.2, Figs. 12–13). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
17. NUDT: the over-predicted variance gives more intense combustion, a thermal disturbance propagating upstream through subsonic boundary-layer zones, and separation ahead of the cavity (§3.2, Fig. 14). **Shown** (mechanism **Stated**)
18. NUDT wall pressure: both closures are acceptable; the transport equation is better, though it slightly over-predicts the pressure near the cavity back edge (§3.2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
19. The two closures differ by "almost order of magnitude" in variance at some mixture fractions (§4, conclusion 2). **Shown** (Figs. 5, 13)
20. The transport equation for $\widetilde{Z''^2}$ is recommended when computing resources allow (Abstract; §4, conclusion 3). **Stated**
21. FPV is better than the steady flamelet model (§1). **Stated** (cited)

The blended equation (Eq. 6) and blending (Eqs. 7–9), translated:

$$\frac{D\tilde\nu}{Dt}=P_v+\partial_j\big[(\nu+\sigma_{v1}\tilde\nu)\partial_j\tilde\nu\big]+\sigma_{v2}(\partial_j\tilde\nu)^2-D_v+(1-F)P_\Delta,$$

$P_v=F\,c_{b1}\tilde S\tilde\nu+(1-F)\tfrac12C_\mu^2\Delta^2\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{ij}$,
$D_v=F\,c_{w1}f_w\tilde\nu^2/d^2+(1-F)\frac{C_d}{2C_\mu}\frac{\tilde\nu^2}{\Delta^2}$,
$\sigma_{v1}=F/\sigma+(1-F)\sigma_k$,
$\sigma_{v2}=Fc_{b2}/\sigma+(1-F)(\nu/\tilde\nu+\sigma_k)$, $\sigma_k=1/Pr_t$;
$F=1$ for $y\le y_{\mathrm{RANS}}$, $F=0$ for $y\ge y_{\mathrm{LES}}$, a tanh
ramp between (Eq. 8); $f_{v1}=\chi^3/(\chi^3+\Gamma(F)c_{v1}^3)$; the scalar
eddy viscosity is $\mu_t^{\mathrm{bl}}=F\mu_t^{\mathrm{RANS}}+(1-F)\mu_t^{\mathrm{LES}}$ (Eq. 14).
The SA $\tilde S$ is built with $|S|$, the strain-rate magnitude (Eq. 2).
The scale-similarity variance (Eq. 15) is
$\widetilde{Z''^2}=c(\widehat{\tilde Z^2}-\hat{\tilde Z}^2)$; the dissipation of
Eq. (17) is printed
$\bar\rho\chi_Z=\frac{\mu_t^{\mathrm{bl}}}{Sc_t}\frac{c_q}{\Delta^{1/3}}\bar\rho\widetilde{Z''^2}$.

# Printed slips

- Eq. (17): the printed scalar dissipation $\frac{\mu_t}{Sc_t}\frac{c_q}{\Delta^{1/3}}\bar\rho\widetilde{Z''^2}$ has units kg$^2$ m$^{-13/3}$ s$^{-1}$, against kg m$^{-3}$ s$^{-1}$ for the other terms of the equation *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (3): substituting $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\nu_t^2/(C_\mu^2\Delta^2)$ at constant $\Delta$ into Eq. (1) with $P_k=2\rho\nu_tS_{ij}S_{ij}$ gives a production $C_\mu^2\Delta^2S_{ij}S_{ij}$, while Eq. (3) prints $\frac12C_\mu^2\Delta^2\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{ij}$; the destruction term agrees with the substitution *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. $\Omega_{ij}$ is not defined in the paper.
- Eqs. (12)–(13): the turbulent fluxes $\tilde Z^{Hybrid}=\frac{\mu_t^{Hybrid}}{Pr_t}\nabla\tilde Z$ and $\tilde C^{Hybrid}$ enter the divergence with a plus sign, next to $-\bar\rho\bar D\nabla\tilde Z$; they use $Pr_t$ where Eq. (17) uses $Sc_t$.
- Eq. (15) carries a coefficient $c$ whose value is never given; Eq. (16) has none.
- §2.1: "the one-equation Yoshizawa SGS model [20]": ref. [20] is Spalart & Allmaras; Yoshizawa is [18].
- §1: "Wang [13]": ref. [13] is Han, Scholtissek & Hasse.
- Fig. 7 caption: "the scalar similarity method".
- Garbled references: [19] lists a fifth author "F. Flow" and the journal "Int. J. Heat Mass Transf."; [25] venue "in: Medium: X, Size, 1996"; [26] title begins "Flame,"; [30] (a Stanford thesis) lists four authors; [32] "Cited in 81"; [33] has no journal name.
