# Summary

Tang et al. (2024) study wall heat transfer in a hypersonic
[shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md):
a $30^\circ$ compression ramp at $M_\infty=5$, $Re_\theta=4652$
($Re_\tau=700$), over an isothermal cold wall with $T_w/T_r=0.5$. The
spatially developing [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
runs on $1.63\times10^8$ points with the finite-difference code
OpenCFD-SC (the CPU parent of [OpenCFD-SCU](/software/opencfd-scu.md)).
The inviscid fluxes switch between a seventh-order low-dissipation upwind
scheme, seventh-order and fifth-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) under
a modified Jameson [shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md). The
laminar inflow is tripped by wall blowing and suction
([inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)).
The incoming [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) is
checked against the van Driest
[velocity transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
and the [log law](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md), the Walz and
generalized Reynolds-analogy temperature–velocity relations and
[Morkovin-scaled](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md) fluctuations.

The interaction is at incipient separation and has no pressure plateau.
Two contributions follow. The first is a correlation of the peak heat-flux
ratio, Eq. (3.7). It assumes that compressible boundary-layer heat-transfer
theory with the Eckert reference temperature still holds behind the shock,
and it fixes the post-shock layer thickness by oblique-shock geometry. It
lowers the maximum relative error on a compiled experiment, DNS and LES
data set from 72 % (the $q_{\max}/q_u=(p_{\max}/p_u)^{0.85}$ law) to 35 %.
The second is a Reynolds/Favre-averaged heat-transport equation, Eq.
(3.12). Its generation terms are mean and turbulent compression heating
and mean and turbulent shear dissipation. The DNS budgets explain the
local heat-flux minimum at separation, the sharp rise inside the
recirculation and the wide peak after reattachment. Downstream the
Reynolds-analogy factor $C_f/(2C_h)$ settles at 0.5 instead of the
boundary-layer value 0.86. No velocity profile inside the interaction is
compared with an [adverse-pressure-gradient wall law](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md).

# Key claims

Notation: $C_h$ is the Stanton number $q_w/[\rho_eU_e(H_r-H_w)]$ (the
source's $St$), $q_w$ the wall heat flux, subscripts $u$ and $d$ the
undisturbed and post-shock states, $\beta_{\mathrm s}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm d}$
the shock and deflection angles (page-local).

1. The $q_{\max}/q_u=(p_{\max}/p_u)^{0.85}$ correlation (Markarian 1968) clearly overpredicts the heat-flux ratio of recent studies, especially at hypersonic conditions; the maximum relative error exceeds 70 % (§3.1, Figs. 16, 19: ±72 %). **Shown**
2. The new correlation (3.7), with the oblique-shock relations (3.4) and (3.8), reduces the relative error from 72 % to 35 % over the same compiled data set (§3.1, Fig. 19; Conclusion (1)). **Shown**
3. The predicted heat-flux ratio is approximately the 0.85 power of the pressure ratio for hot walls ($T_w/T_0=1.2$–1.8) and the 0.8 power for cold walls ($T_w/T_0=0.2$–0.8), for sample points at $M_u=3$–15 (§3.1, Fig. 18). **Shown** (evaluation of the authors' formula)
4. The three bracketed factors of (3.7) express heat-transfer enhancement by boundary-layer compression through the Mach-number decrease, the wall-temperature effect before and behind the shock, and the enhancement of the fluid's heat-transport capacity by the density increase; hence "the sharp increase of the heat flux in the STBLI is caused by the compression of boundary layers and the density increase in the mainstream" (§3.1, p. A66-21). **Derived**
5. The correlation is "not sensitive enough to the wall temperature for some cases", because the coupling of wall temperature with the interaction is not in the derivation (§3.1). **Stated**
6. The correlation reproduces the streamwise heat-flux distributions of Coleman & Stollery (1972) ($M=9.2$, $T_w/T_r=0.3$), Delery & Coet (1991) ($M=5.0$, $T_w/T_r=0.7$) and Schülein (2006) ($M=5.0$, $T_w/T_r=0.8$, $10^\circ$ and $14^\circ$ shock generators); for $14^\circ$ the peak is reasonable but the separation region deviates (§3.1, Fig. 20). **Shown**
7. For the present DNS the relative deviation of the peak heat-flux ratio is 23 % for the present theory and 34 % for Coleman & Stollery (1972); both agree with the DNS before reattachment and are higher downstream (§4.1, Fig. 22). **Shown**
8. Across the interaction the wall heat flux rises to six times the incoming value, with a local minimum before the jump at the mean separation point and a wide peak downstream of reattachment (§4.1, Fig. 21a). **Shown**
9. The Reynolds analogy fails in the interaction: $C_f/(2C_h)$ departs from its boundary-layer value 0.86 (Qu et al. 2001), changes drastically in the recirculation region and becomes constant at 0.5 downstream instead of recovering; Helm & Martín (2022) report the same and it is "not well understood" (§4.1, Fig. 21b). **Shown**
10. Separation and reattachment lie at $x_S=-8.66$ mm and $x_R=2.25$ mm ($x_S/\delta=-0.82$, $x_R/\delta=0.21$); with no wall-pressure plateau and no clear reattachment shock the interaction is incipient or small separation (§2.4, Figs. 14, 15a). **Shown**
11. The wall-pressure ratio agrees with inviscid oblique-shock theory, 13 (§2.4, Fig. 15a). **Shown**
12. The interaction length $L_{\mathrm{int}}$ is 14 mm from the DNS against 18 mm from Souverein's correlation $L^*=1.3\,Se^{*3}$ with the non-adiabatic $Se^*$ of Jaunet et al. (2014); the printed "relative deviation of 20 %" is judged acceptable (§2.4, Fig. 15b). **Shown**
13. Grid convergence is reached on grid M3 ($2660\times204\times300=1.63\times10^8$; $\Delta y^+=0.33$ at the reference station and 0.59 at most, $\Delta x^+=6.6$–17.5, $\Delta z^+=6.6$, wall-normal grid Reynolds number 7) for $C_f$, $C_h$, wall pressure, $u_{\mathrm{rms}}$ and turbulent heat flux; wall-normal refinement moves reattachment upstream and raises the downstream $C_f$ and $C_h$ up to M3, pressure is insensitive, and spanwise refinement (M5, $\Delta z^+=4.4$) raises $C_h$ slightly only after the peak (§2.2, Table 2, Figs. 5–9). **Shown**
14. The wall gradients ($C_f$, $C_h$) are more grid-sensitive than the profiles of turbulence statistics (§2.2, p. A66-11). **Shown**
15. The incoming boundary layer at $x=-60$ mm is fully developed: $C_f$ rises from $x>-400$ mm, peaks near $-250$ mm and follows the White & Majdalani turbulent correlation after $-150$ mm (§2.3, Fig. 10). **Shown**
16. The van Driest velocity follows $u^+=y^+$ for $y^+<4$ and the log law at $y^+=30$–200 with $\kappa^{-1}=2.44$, $B=5.5$; the deviation from Duan et al. (2010) reaches 3 % in the wake, attributed to Reynolds number and the turbulence-generation method (§2.3, Fig. 11a). **Shown**
17. The temperature–velocity relation agrees with the Walz relation, with a slight mid-layer deviation attributed to the cold wall violating $Pr_t=1$; the generalized relation of Zhang et al. (2014), Eq. (2.8), agrees well (§2.3, Fig. 11b). **Shown**
18. Morkovin-scaled $u_{\mathrm{rms}}$ collapses to incompressible data for $y/\delta<0.4$ and is slightly larger beyond relative to Spalart (1988), as Pirozzoli et al. (2004) also observed; it is closer to the higher-Reynolds-number data of Sillero et al. (2013); the edge deviation from Huang et al. (2022) is 5 % (§2.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
19. Outside the interaction, heat is generated mainly by mean dissipation in the viscous sublayer, with compression heating near zero, and is carried to the wall by conduction in the sublayer and by turbulence in the buffer layer; after reattachment ($x/\delta=6$) the dissipation heating moves closer to the wall and grows by nearly one order of magnitude (§4.2, Fig. 25). **Shown**
20. In the interaction the dissipation heating is dominated by its turbulent part $\Phi_{\mathrm t}$, with two near-wall heating cores tied to shear-layer detachment and reattachment and attributed to Kelvin–Helmholtz instability; compression heating is mainly mean ($H_{p,\mathrm m}$) and is lifted off the wall by the recirculation bubble; the fluctuating compression heating changes sign around the theoretical shock, indicating shock unsteadiness (§4.2, Fig. 23). **Shown**
21. At separation ($x/\delta=-0.82$) turbulent dissipation heating peaks near $y^+=25$ and exceeds the mean dissipation at all heights; more heat is carried outward by turbulence and balanced by convection, so less reaches the wall, which explains the local heat-flux minimum (§4.2, Fig. 26a). **Shown**
22. At the recirculation centre ($x/\delta=-0.05$) turbulent transport carries heat from turbulent dissipation in the shedding shear layer and from outer-layer compression to the wall; the turbulent input balances the viscous output in the extreme near-wall region, which explains the sharp rise of wall heat flux (§4.2, Fig. 26b). **Shown**
23. Near reattachment ($x/\delta=0.21$) compression heat from the local oblique impingement and compression heat convected from upstream is carried wallward by turbulence; downstream, mean shear and dissipation rise and near-wall compression continues, giving a wide heat-flux peak a little after reattachment (§4.2, Fig. 26c). **Shown**
24. The generation and transport of compression heating "reveal the underlying mechanism of the strong correlation between the peak heat flux ratios and the pressure ratios" and "provide theoretical support for the canonical Q-P correlation" (§4.2, Abstract, Conclusion (2)). **Stated** (interpretation of the budgets)
25. Near-wall heat-flux lines show an adiabatic line in the conduction and turbulent-flux fields: conduction carries heat to the main stream above it and to the wall below it; the turbulent heat flux points upstream in most areas and wallward in the extreme near-wall region; mean convection in the recirculation carries heat downstream at its outer edge and upstream at its bottom (§4.2, Fig. 24). **Shown**
26. Near-wall streamwise vortices (Q criterion) are enhanced through the shock, lift up and spawn spanwise vortices typical of free shear layers; the instantaneous shock shows pits where streamwise vortices pass and bulges of boundary-layer scale (§4.2, Fig. 27). **Shown**
27. Compression heating is reversible and dissipation heating irreversible; turbulent shear dissipation stays considerable even where fluctuations are small, because of the small spatial scales (§3.2, p. A66-24). **Stated**
28. The wall-heat-flux decomposition of Sun et al. (2021), used by Tong et al. (2022b), starts from the total-energy equation; the authors say it couples kinetic-energy transport and transformation, gives "cumbersome and unclear terms" and "hides the energy dissipation", and work from the enthalpy transport equation instead (§1, p. A66-4). **Stated**

The correlation (3.7), with $\omega_\mu=0.78$ the viscosity–temperature
exponent, $r=Pr^{1/3}$ the recovery factor and $T_0$ the total temperature
(page-local), reads as printed

$$\frac{q_{w,d}}{q_{w,u}}=\left(\frac{\sin(\beta_{\mathrm s}-\theta_{\mathrm d})}{\sin\beta_{\mathrm s}}\right)^{-0.25}\left(\frac{\left(1+\frac{T_w}{T_0}\right)+0.2M_d^2\left(0.44r+\frac{T_w}{T_0}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{T_w}{T_0}\right)+0.2M_u^2\left(0.44r+\frac{T_w}{T_0}\right)}\cdot\frac{1+0.2M_d^2}{1+0.2M_u^2}\right)^{0.25\omega_\mu-0.75}\left(\frac{p_d}{p_u}\right)^{0.75},$$

with $\sin(\beta_{\mathrm s}-\theta_{\mathrm d})/\sin\beta_{\mathrm s}=\delta_d/\delta_u$
(Eq. 3.3) and $M_d$ from Eq. (3.8). The heat-transport equation (3.12),
non-dimensional, in registry averaging ($\langle\cdot\rangle$ Reynolds,
$\langle\cdot\rangle_\rho$ and $''$ Favre), reads

$$\partial_j\big(\langle\rho\rangle\langle u_j\rangle_\rho\langle T\rangle_\rho\big)+\partial_j\big(\langle\rho\rangle\langle u_j''T''\rangle_\rho\big)-\frac{1}{Pr\,Re}\partial_j\big(\langle\mu\rangle\partial_j\langle T\rangle\big)=\underbrace{(\gamma-1)M^2\Big(\langle u_j\rangle\partial_j\langle p\rangle+\langle u_j'\partial_jp'\rangle\Big)}_{H_{p,\mathrm m}+H_{p,\mathrm t}}+\underbrace{\frac{(\gamma-1)M^2}{Re}\Big(2\langle\mu\rangle\langle S^d_{ij}\rangle\langle S^d_{ij}\rangle+2\langle\mu\rangle\langle S^{d\prime}_{ij}S^{d\prime}_{ij}\rangle\Big)}_{\Phi_{\mathrm m}+\Phi_{\mathrm t}},$$

with correlations of the property fluctuations $\mu'$, $\lambda'$ neglected
and the flow statistically steady. At the wall the convective and turbulent
fluxes vanish, so the wall heat flux equals the conduction (§3.2).
