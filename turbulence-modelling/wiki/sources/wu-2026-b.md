# Summary

Wu et al. (2026) propose SATES-II, a new variant of
[self-adaptive turbulence eddy simulation](/methods/self-adaptive-turbulence-eddy-simulation.md),
and AIDDES, an adaptive IDDES
([detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)). In this
paper SATES is "developed based on the SST $k$-$\omega$ turbulence model"
([$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)) and multiplies the SST
eddy viscosity, shear-stress limiter included, by the resolution control
function $F_r$ of $L_c=C_x\Delta$, $L_i$ and $L_k$. The earlier variants fix
$C_x=0.61$ (SATES-SM, calibrated on the [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md))
or weight it by the [σ model](/methods/sigma-model.md) (SATES-σ, weak in
attached boundary layers). The authors read $C_x$ as a ratio of subgrid and
RANS time scales that should not depend on the grid, against the dynamic
coefficient of Lu et al. (2025) that grows with $\Delta$, and build a
two-layer coefficient: a [WALE](/methods/wale-model.md)-type wall-treatment
coefficient in the inner layer, blended through an $F_2$-like function with a
σ-model coefficient outside. AIDDES puts the same $C_x$ into the IDDES LES
length. The claim is a general strategy for
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md).

The numerics are those of [PHengLEI](/software/phenglei.md)-HyOrder: a
sixth-order [WCNS](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md) with
multi-order-candidate weights and a TENO-type smooth scale separation
([WENO/TENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)), and a Roe flux whose dissipation is
scaled by a total-variation indicator combined with the Ducros sensor
([shock sensors](/definitions/shock-sensor.md);
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)). On a flat
plate the wall treatment restores RANS skin friction; on the
[supersonic axisymmetric base flow](/benchmarks/supersonic-axisymmetric-base-flow.md)
of Herrin & Dutton the recirculation-length error drops from +20.5%
(SATES-SM) and +36.1% (IDDES) to +6.8% (SATES-II and AIDDES) at similar
cost; on the [30P30N](/benchmarks/30p30n-high-lift-airfoil.md) at
$\alpha=21°$ SATES-SM separates early on the slat, IDDES gives a quasi-2D slat
wake, and SATES-II and AIDDES improve the velocity profiles.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $F_{\mathrm{cor}}=\omega/\max(\omega,\lvert S\rvert F_2/a_1)$
the SST limiter factor; $\beta_F=0.002$, $n_F=2$ the $F_r$ constants;
$C_x^\sigma$, $C_x^{\mathrm{EWT}}$, $C_{x,\mathrm{II}}$ the σ-model,
wall-treatment and blended coefficients, $C_{x,\mathrm{base}}=0.61$;
$C_m$, $D_m$ a generic subgrid constant and frequency operator; $C_{\mathrm{WT}}$
the wall-treatment constant; $\mathcal S^d_{ij}$ the WALE tensor;
$f_{\mathrm{bl}}$, $C_1$ the blending function and its constant;
$\tau_{\mathrm{RANS}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{SGS}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{SATES}}$,
$L_{\mathrm{SGS}}$ the time and length scales of Eq. (10);
$\phi_{\mathrm R}=\phi_1+\phi_2-\phi_1\phi_2$ the Roe dissipation scale;
$R$ the base radius.

1. SATES modifies only the eddy viscosity of SST: $\mu_t=F_r\rho kF_{\mathrm{cor}}/\omega$, $F_r=\min\{1,[(1-e^{-\beta_FL_c/L_k})/(1-e^{-\beta_FL_i/L_k})]^{n_F}\}$, $L_c=C_x(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$, $L_i=k^{3/2}/(\beta^*k\omega)$, $L_k=\nu^{3/4}/(\beta^*k\omega)^{1/4}$ (§2.2, Eqs. 1–6). **Stated**
2. SATES-SM uses $C_x=\sqrt{0.3}\,C_s/C_\mu$, which with $C_s=0.1$ gives 0.61 (Eq. 7, Table 1). **Stated**
3. The dynamic coefficient of Lu et al. (2025), $C_x=C_m^2D_m\Delta/\sqrt k$ from $\mu_t^{\mathrm{SGS}}=\rho(C_m\Delta)^2D_m$, "exhibits an evident dependence on the local grid scale", an "undesirable grid-dependence" (§2.2, Eqs. 8–9, Table 1). **Derived**
4. Equating the SATES eddy viscosity with the RANS and with the subgrid one gives $C_x=C_m\sqrt{\tau_{\mathrm{SGS}}/\tau_{\mathrm{RANS}}}$: $C_x$ "can be regarded as a blending of the RANS and LES time scales" (§2.2, Eq. 10, following Johansen et al.). **Derived** (as printed)
5. Applied to the σ model, $C_x^\sigma=\frac{C_\sigma}{\beta^*}\sqrt{\mathcal D_\sigma/\omega}\,F_{\mathrm{cor}}^{-1/2}$, $C_\sigma=1.5$, $\mathcal D_\sigma=\varsigma_3(\varsigma_1-\varsigma_2)(\varsigma_2-\varsigma_3)/\varsigma_1^2$, floored at 0.15 as in the previous SATES-σ (§2.2, Eqs. 11–12). **Derived**
6. The wall-treatment coefficient is $C_x^{\mathrm{EWT}}=C_{x,\mathrm{base}}(C_{\mathrm{WT}}/C_s)^2\frac{(\mathcal S^d_{ij}\mathcal S^d_{ij})^{3/2}}{[(\mathcal S^d_{ij}\mathcal S^d_{ij})^{5/4}+(S_{ij}S_{ij})^{5/2}]\lvert S\rvert}$ with $C_{\mathrm{WT}}=0.325$ (range 0.325–0.5) (§2.2, Eqs. 13–14). **Stated**
7. SATES-II: $C_{x,\mathrm{II}}=C_x^\sigma(1-f_{\mathrm{bl}})+C_x^{\mathrm{EWT}}f_{\mathrm{bl}}$, $f_{\mathrm{bl}}=\tanh[(\arg_1)^4]$, $\arg_1=C_1\max\big(\frac{2\sqrt k}{\beta^*\omega d},\frac{500\nu}{\omega d^2}\big)$, $C_1=0.67$; $f_{\mathrm{bl}}=1$ gives wall treatment in the inner layer, 0 the σ branch outside (§2.2, Eqs. 15–16). **Stated** (definition)
8. In SATES-II "the grid effect is confined solely in the filter length scale, $L_c=C_x\Delta$", which the authors call physically more appropriate because a ratio of time scales "should be independent of numerical discretization" (§2.2). **Stated**
9. AIDDES replaces the IDDES LES length (SST form of Gritskevich et al., $C_{\mathrm{DES}}=0.78F_1+0.61(1-F_1)$) by $C_{x,\mathrm{II}}(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$; for IDDES and AIDDES a resolution indicator $F_r=l_{\mathrm{IDDES}}/L_i$ is defined (§2.3, Eqs. 17–21). **Stated**
10. The adaptive $C_x$ is a general improvement strategy for hybrid RANS/LES, shown here for SATES and IDDES; PANS and PITM are left open (§1, §2.3, §5). **Shown** (two methods only)
11. Flat plate ($M=0.2$, $Re=5\times10^6$ per unit length, $137\times97\times97$): the wall treatment improves the SATES-II skin friction over SATES-SM, and AIDDES does not harm the IDDES wall modelling (§2.4, Fig. 1). **Shown**
12. MOC6-STA reaches nearly the error levels and convergence of MOC6, the design sixth order at a non-critical point and at a first-order critical point of $x^ke^x$, with errors below WCNS-CU4 (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
13. MOC6 reduces dispersion and dissipation errors at resolved scales and supplies more dissipation at high wavenumbers (ADR analysis) (§3.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
14. On the 2D Riemann problem configuration 3 ($400\times400$) the adaptive dissipation coefficient stays close to one in most regions and decreases near shear layers (§3.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
15. Base-flow grid study (A1, A2 with $5.07\times10^6$ cells; B1, B2 with $9.5\times10^6$): SATES-II profiles show "negligible discrepancies" in the recirculation region; grid B2 is used thereafter (§4.1.1, Figs. 6–7, Table 2). **Shown**
16. In the attached boundary layer upstream of the base SATES-SM gives $F_r$ "about 0.2 − 0.4"; SATES-II gives $F_r\approx0.9$–1.0 in the inner layer and low $F_r$ in the separated shear layer and recirculation (§4.1.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
17. IDDES keeps $F_r\approx1$ in the attached layer, but its $F_r$ "decays more slowly and remains at moderate values" in the separated region; AIDDES keeps the shielding and adapts rapidly in the shear layer (§4.1.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
18. Recirculation length $L_r/R$: SATES-SM 3.17 (+20.5%), SATES-II 2.81 (+6.8%), IDDES 3.58 (+36.1%), AIDDES 2.81 (+6.8%), experiment 2.63 (Table 3). **Shown**
19. Wall-clock hours on 640 cores: SATES-SM 50.28, SATES-II 51.59, IDDES 45.30, AIDDES 46.54, "comparable computing resources" (Table 3, §4.1.3). **Shown**
20. Resolved turbulent kinetic energy in the base-flow shear layer: IDDES deviates most, SATES-SM less, SATES-II is most accurate, AIDDES comparable to SATES-II; remaining AIDDES deviations "may originate from the lack of a realistic turbulent boundary layer at inlet" (§4.1.3, Fig. 11). **Shown** / **Stated**
21. 30P30N ($Re=9\times10^6$, $M=0.2$, $\alpha=21°$): $C_p$ agrees with experiment on both grids, and the fine grid gives better $C_f$ at the main-element leading edge by resolving a faster transition (§4.2.1, Fig. 12). **Shown**
22. 30P30N: SATES-SM separates prematurely on the slat upper surface; SATES-II has $F_r\to1$ near walls; IDDES suppresses the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability of the slat wake, giving a quasi-2D wake; AIDDES gives stronger 3D fluctuations (§4.2.2, Fig. 13). **Shown**
23. 30P30N velocity profiles: SATES-II improves the near-wall profiles at $x/c=0.85$–0.975 and the flap boundary layer; IDDES deviates much less than SATES-SM thanks to its shielding but under-resolves the wake turbulence; AIDDES improves the downstream prediction under varying adverse pressure gradient (§4.2.3, Fig. 14). **Shown**
24. Open issues: PANS and PITM, other baseline RANS models (time scales to be reinterpreted), and strongly non-equilibrium flows such as strong shock/boundary-layer interaction "where the time-scale separation may become less well-defined" (§5). **Stated**

The WCNS interpolation (background, Ma et al. 2024; Bai et al. 2024) weights
three third-order candidates and one sixth-order optimized linear candidate,
$Q_{j+1/2}=\sum_{k=0}^3\mathsf w_kQ^k_{j+1/2}$,
$\mathsf w_k=a_k/\sum_la_l$, $a_k=d_k/(\mathrm{IS}_k+10^{-40})$,
$d_k=1/16,10/16,5/16,100$; the STA variant replaces $\mathsf w_k$ by
$d_k\bar\delta^{\mathrm T}_k/\sum_ld_l\bar\delta^{\mathrm T}_l$ with the
smoothed cut-off $\bar\delta^{\mathrm T}_k=\tanh((\chi^{\mathrm T}_k/C_T)^n)$
for $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k<10C_T$ and 1 otherwise (Eqs. 22–25). The Roe flux is
$\frac12[\hat E(Q^L)+\hat E(Q^R)-\phi_{\mathrm R}\lvert\tilde A\rvert(Q^R-Q^L)]$
with $\phi_1=\lvert(Q^R-Q^L)/\min(Q^R,Q^L)\rvert+\epsilon_1$ and the Ducros
factor $\phi_2$ (Eqs. 27–28).

*(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* substituting the printed Eq. (8),

gives $\rho C_m^2D_m\Delta/(C_\mu\sqrt k)$, while Eq. (9) ends with

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (13) text:** "$g^2_{ij}=g_{ij}g_{ij}$, which is defined as square of local velocity gradient": with the repeated indices summed this is a scalar, while $s^d_{ij}=\frac12(g^2_{ij}+g^2_{ji})-\frac13\delta_{ij}g^2_{kk}$ uses $g^2_{ij}$ with free indices.
- **Eq. (28):** $\phi_2=(\nabla\cdot u)^2/((\nabla\cdot u)^2+\nabla\times u+\epsilon_2)$ adds the vector $\nabla\times u$ to scalars.
- **§3.2:** MOC6-STA attains the designed sixth order "at both" test points, then "showing the same first-order convergence rate as all other high-order schemes".
- **Table 1 against Eq. (11):** Table 1 prints the previous SATES-σ coefficient as $C_x=\max(C_{x,\mathrm{base}}F_\sigma,0.15)$, $F_\sigma=(C_\sigma/C_s)^2D_\sigma/\lvert S\rvert$, and the "Present SATES-II" row as $C_x=(C_m/\beta_1^*)\sqrt{D_m/\omega}$ (no $F_{\mathrm{cor}}$); Eq. (11) prints $C_x^\sigma=\frac{C_\sigma}{\beta_1^*}\sqrt{D_\sigma/\omega}\frac1{\sqrt{F_{\mathrm{cor}}}}$ and calls it "the SATES-σ model … calibrated as follows".
- **§3.2, Eq. (24):** the candidate index $n$ is also the exponent in $\tanh((\chi_n/C_T)^n)$; "$\bar\delta_k=0$" uses $k$ for the same index; $\gamma_n$ is not defined.
- **Eq. (27) against the text:** the coefficient is $\phi$ in Eq. (27) and "$\phi_k$" ("of kth's variable") before Eq. (28).
- **§1:** $F_r$ is printed without the $\min(1,\cdot)$ of Eq. (5).
- **Fig. 5 and axes:** the base radius is $R$ in the text and $R_0$ in Fig. 5 ($x/R_0$ and $r/R$ mixed).
- **Table 2:** the column "Points" (325, 406) is not explained.
- **Typos:** "normal stain rate" (§2.2), "An unified" (§2.2 heading), "fours streamwise positions", "Reynold number" (§4.2 heading), "origin IDDES".
