# Summary

Seta & Federrath (2022) study the
[small-scale (turbulent) dynamo](/results/small-scale-dynamo.md) in a
non-isothermal, two-phase medium representative of the interstellar medium.
Almost all earlier turbulent-dynamo simulations assume an isothermal gas.
The authors solve compressible MHD with explicit viscosity and resistivity
($Re=Rm=2000$, $Pm=1$), a constant heating rate and the Koyama & Inutsuka
cooling function. The domain is a triply periodic 200 pc box at $512^3$,
computed with a modified FLASH code (HLL3R Riemann solver). They argue that
the explicit diffusion dominates the
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md), whose
effective Reynolds number they estimate at about 8000 (see
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)). The setup is a forced
box (compare forced
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)),
driven at the largest scales to an rms velocity of about 10 km s$^{-1}$,
either purely solenoidally (Sol) or purely compressively (Comp), from a
$10^{-10}$ G seed. Thermal instability splits the gas into a cold phase
($T<10^3$ K, a few per cent of the volume, $M_t\approx5$) and a warm phase
($T\ge10^3$ K, $M_t\approx1$).

The kinematic growth rate of $E_{\mathrm{mag}}/E_{\mathrm{kin}}$ is the same
in both phases ($0.37\,t_0^{-1}$ Sol, $0.18\,t_0^{-1}$ Comp). In isothermal
runs the growth rate falls with Mach number. The saturation level is lower
in the cold phase. Both the growth rate and the saturation level lie below
the isothermal empirical model of Federrath et al. (2011) at matched Mach
number, which the authors attribute to energy exchange between the phases.
They explain the equal growth rates with a term-by-term budget of the
compressible, magnetized vorticity equation. The turbulent term always
amplifies vorticity. The baroclinic term amplifies it in the warm phase,
and a viscous log-density-gradient term destroys it in the cold phase. The
net result is equal rms vorticity in both phases. The lower cold-phase
saturation goes with a stronger rms Lorentz force there. The paper contains
no closure or LES content; the evidence is two production runs and one
reduced-resolution test.

# Key claims

1. The simulations are of non-isothermal turbulent-dynamo MHD with heating and cooling in a two-phase medium, whereas "almost all studies of the turbulent dynamo assume turbulence in an isothermal gas (except Gent et al. 2021 …)" (§1). **Stated**
2. With $N_x=512$ grid points the numerical Reynolds numbers are "approximately equal to $2n_g^{4/3}$", about 8000 (Appendix C of McKee et al. 2020). Choosing $Re=Rm=2000$ "ensures that the explicit diffusion is always significantly higher and at larger scales than the numerical diffusion" (§2.4). **Stated**
3. The cold phase fills 3–4% of the volume according to the text (the Fig. 7 legend prints $\langle F\rangle=0.045\pm0.001$ Sol and $0.030\pm0.005$ Comp); the warm phase fills $0.96\pm0.01$ (Sol) and $0.97\pm0.01$ (Comp) (§3.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
4. The cold phase is supersonic, $\langle M_t\rangle=4.8\pm0.3$ (Sol) and $5.4\pm0.4$ (Comp); the warm phase is transonic, $1.2\pm0.1$ (Sol) and $1.1\pm0.1$ (Comp). The rms velocity is about 10 km s$^{-1}$ in both phases (§3.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
5. The density PDF of the whole medium is double-humped. In each phase, away from the transition, $s_\rho=\ln(\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{mean}})$ is fitted well by a lognormal: Sol cold $s_0=1.85\pm0.01$, $\sigma_{LN}=1\pm0.01$; Sol warm $s_0=-0.85\pm0.01$, $\sigma_{LN}=0.54\pm0.01$; Comp cold $s_0=2.13\pm0.03$, $\sigma_{LN}=1.6\pm0.02$; Comp warm $s_0=-2.10\pm0.01$, $\sigma_{LN}=1.2\pm0.01$. Kinematic and saturated PDFs coincide within one sigma, so the growing field does not change the density distribution (§3.1, Eq. 7, Fig. 4). **Shown**
6. The $T$–$\rho$ relation follows no simple thermodynamic law. The cold-phase trend is close to isobaric ($T\propto\rho^{-0.92}$ Sol, $\rho^{-0.76}$ Comp), and the warm-phase trend is flatter ($\rho^{-0.37}$ Sol, $\rho^{-0.11}$ Comp) (§3.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
7. The $B$–$\rho$ relation follows none of the simple compression laws ($B\propto\rho^0,\rho^{1/2},\rho^{2/3},\rho^1$). Fitted slopes are Sol $\rho^{0.22}$ (cold) and $\rho^{0.27}$ (warm); Comp $\rho^{0.71}$ (cold) and $\rho^{0.51}$ (warm), with large spread. "The magnetic field strength is not only controlled by the density of the medium" (§3.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
8. The field is non-Gaussian (spatially intermittent) in both phases, and its kurtosis decreases at saturation (e.g. Sol cold $23\pm3\to8\pm2$; Comp warm $60\pm10\to32\pm6$). The standard deviation of $B_x/B_{\mathrm{rms}}$ is about twice (Sol) and four times (Comp) higher in the cold phase (§3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
9. The kinematic growth rate is the same in both phases: $\Gamma_E=0.37\pm0.02\,t_0^{-1}$ (Sol) and $0.18\pm0.01\,t_0^{-1}$ (Comp). This "clearly does not agree with the isothermal models", where the growth rate changes with Mach number (§4.1, Fig. 8, Table 1). **Shown**
10. The saturation level $R_{\mathrm{sat}}=E_{\mathrm{mag}}/E_{\mathrm{kin}}$ is lower in the cold phase: Sol $0.019\pm0.002$ (cold), $0.13\pm0.02$ (warm), $0.08\pm0.01$ (whole); Comp $0.0024\pm0.0007$, $0.021\pm0.005$, $0.006\pm0.002$. The trend with Mach number matches isothermal runs (decrease for $M_t\gtrsim1$) (§4.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
11. The empirical isothermal model of Federrath et al. (2011) gives, at $M_t=1$, $\Gamma_{E,\mathrm{iso}}\approx0.78$ (Sol) and $0.30$ (Comp) $t_0^{-1}$, and at $M_t=5$, $0.53$ and $0.24$; $R_{\mathrm{sat,iso}}\approx0.24$ and $0.03$ ($M_t=1$), $0.03$ and $0.006$ ($M_t=5$). Both the growth rate and $R_{\mathrm{sat}}$ of the non-isothermal runs are lower (relative differences $0.43$, $1.11$, $0.33$, $0.66$ for the growth rate and $0.58$, $0.85$, $1.50$, $0.43$ for $R_{\mathrm{sat}}$). The Sol/Comp growth-rate ratio of about 2 is preserved (§4.1, Table 1). **Shown** (model values **Stated**)
12. The lower growth rate and $R_{\mathrm{sat}}$ against isothermal runs are "probably due to significant and continuous energy exchange between the two phases"; the magnetic energy present in one phase need not be generated there (§4.1, §5). **Stated** (interpretation)
13. $\omega_{\mathrm{rms}}$ is lower for Comp than for Sol (kinematic, warm: $0.76\pm0.03$ against $0.49\pm0.03$ in $10^{-13}$ s$^{-1}$), lower in saturation than in the kinematic stage, and about equal between the phases in each run. The authors take this as "probably" the reason why the growth rate is equal in both phases (§4.2, Fig. 9). **Shown** (causal link **Stated**)
14. Vorticity budget (Eq. 9 projected on $\boldsymbol\omega$, normalized by $\omega_{\mathrm{rms}}$): the turbulent term is always dominant and positive. The baroclinic term is dominant and positive in the warm phase, slightly exceeding the turbulent term for Sol ($0.97$ against $0.65$, kinematic). The viscous log-density-gradient term is dominant and negative in the cold phase ($-2.0\pm0.3$ Sol, $-1.1\pm0.3$ Comp, kinematic, in $10^{-27}$ s$^{-2}$). The dissipation and Lorentz terms are subdominant (§4.2, Fig. 10, Table 2). **Shown**
15. Initial vorticity seed: for Sol the driving term contributes about $10^{-29}$ s$^{-2}$. For Comp it is negligible (about $10^{-35}$ s$^{-2}$), and the seed comes from the baroclinic and log-density-gradient terms (about $10^{-28}$ s$^{-2}$ for $t/t_0\lesssim1$) (§4.2). **Shown**
16. The baroclinic term is weaker in the cold phase "primarily because of compression which aligns density and pressure gradients", and the log-density-gradient term is stronger there "because of higher density and density gradients" (§4.2). **Stated** (interpretation of the shown budgets)
17. The dissipation term is always small, "probably because of a well resolved physical velocity diffusion" (§4.2). **Stated**
18. Split of the turbulent term (Eq. 10): vortex stretching dominates in the warm phase (Sol kinematic $0.89\pm0.08$), and vortex compression dominates in the cold phase (Sol kinematic $-0.42\pm0.05$, Comp $-0.6\pm0.2$). Advection is significant only in the Comp cold phase ($0.3\pm0.2$) (§4.2, Fig. 11, Table 3). **Shown** (the sign reading of the compression term is discussed under Printed slips)
19. The rms Lorentz force is stronger in the cold phase (Sol $(0.13\pm0.02)\times10^{-30}$ against $(0.25\pm0.03)\times10^{-31}$; Comp $(1.0\pm0.5)\times10^{-31}$ against $(0.6\pm0.2)\times10^{-32}$, printed unit "dyne"). The cold-phase field therefore stops growing earlier and saturates lower (§4.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
20. The field-line curvature $\kappa_{B,\mathrm{rms}}L/2$ is higher in the cold phase (Sol kinematic $19\pm0.3$ against $18\pm0.2$; Comp $15.1\pm0.8$ against $13.8\pm0.3$), decreases at saturation, and is higher for Sol than for Comp (App. C, Eq. C1, Fig. C1). **Shown**
21. Equilibrium against non-equilibrium cooling (subcycling factors 0.5 and 5.0) at $252^3$ (Sol): growth rate, saturation level, and density and temperature PDFs are practically unchanged, so equilibrium cooling is adopted for efficiency (App. A, Figs. A1, A2). **Shown**
22. Velocity is practically uncorrelated with density (slopes $\rho^{-0.02}$ to $\rho^{-0.08}$), and the $u$ PDFs are Gaussian. The local Mach number correlates positively with density in the cold phase ($\rho^{0.42}$ Sol, $\rho^{0.35}$ Comp), unlike the negative correlation of Federrath & Banerjee (2015) for a polytropic $\gamma=5/3$ gas (App. B, Figs. B1, B2). **Shown**
23. "The non-isothermal turbulent dynamo is significantly different from its isothermal counterpart", which motivates multiphase dynamo studies (Abstract, §5). **Stated** (conclusion drawn from claims 9–12)

# Printed slips

- **Growth-rate symbol (§4.1, p. 8):** "the Sol ($\Gamma\approx0.37\,t_0^{-1}$) and Comp ($\Gamma\approx0.18\,t_0^{-1}$) runs". Everywhere else the growth rate is $\gamma$ (Fig. 8, Table 1), and $\Gamma$ is the heating rate of Eq. 5.
- **Sign reading of the compression term:** §4.2 states that the sign of $\langle\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_X\rangle/\omega_{\rm rms}$ "indicates growth (positive) or decay (negative)". It then says the compression term is dominant in the cold phase "(though it is negative, implying growth of vorticity, see Table 3)", repeated in §5 and in the Fig. 11 caption. Eq. 10 prints the underbrace labels under the terms without their signs, so the sign carried by the plotted compression term is not stated. As printed, the sign rule and the reading of the negative term contradict each other.
- **Table 3 against Table 2** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*: in the Sol cold kinematic row, stretching + advection + compression $=0.08-0.07-0.42=-0.41$, while the turbulent term of Table 2 is $1.9\pm0.3$; with the signs of Eq. 10 applied ($0.08+0.07+0.42=0.57$) the parts still do not sum to the whole. The paper does not explain the difference.
- **Cold volume fraction:** "around 3 – 4%" (§3.2), "≲ 4%" (§5) and "3 – 4 %" (Fig. 7 caption), while the Fig. 7(a) legend prints $\langle F\rangle=0.045\pm0.001$ for Sol.
- **Lorentz-force unit (Fig. 12):** labelled "[dyne]". *(Compiler check, not in the sources)*: with the current density as printed after Eq. 9, $\boldsymbol j=(c/4\pi)\nabla\times\boldsymbol B$, $\boldsymbol j\times\boldsymbol B/c=(\nabla\times\boldsymbol B)\times\boldsymbol B/(4\pi)$ has units G$^2$ cm$^{-1}$ = dyn cm$^{-3}$, a force per unit volume.
- **Fig. B2:** both lower panels are labelled "(c)", while the caption refers to "(c, d)".
- **Typography:** "This is the probably reason" (Fig. 9 caption), "perpendicular to to" (Fig. 5 caption), "magneta"; the data-availability e-mail domain differs by one letter from the page-1 address.
