# Summary

Yan et al. (2024) build a [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
method for incompressible flow over bluff bodies from three parts: a
diffused-interface (direct-forcing) [immersed boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md)
with Lagrangian markers, a three-point smoothed delta kernel and an
explicit diagonal approximation of the marker system; explicit algebraic
wall functions; and the sixth-order
[compact finite-difference](/methods/compact-finite-difference-scheme.md)
framework [Xcompact3d](/software/xcompact3d.md), in which the subgrid
dissipation is the implicit
[spectral vanishing viscosity](/methods/spectral-vanishing-viscosity.md)
(iSVV) of Lamballais, Dairay and coworkers built into the second-derivative
scheme ([implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md),
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)). The wall
model replaces the no-slip target velocity of each marker by a
[slip](/methods/slip-wall-model.md) target: the tangential velocity probed
about three grid spacings off the wall is extrapolated to the wall with a
gradient obtained by integrating a thin-boundary-layer equation that keeps
only the pressure gradient, with a van Driest mixing length
([law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)). The wall stress comes
explicitly from the Werner–Wengle power law under zero or favourable
pressure gradient ([equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md))
or from the adverse-pressure-gradient power law of Wilhelm et al.
([wall law in adverse pressure gradient](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md)),
with a separation test that falls back to no-slip. The authors call the
combination an explicit [non-equilibrium wall model](/methods/nonequilibrium-wall-model.md)
and present it as the first use of explicit non-equilibrium wall functions
with a diffused interface, and the first coupling of a wall-modelled
diffused-interface IBM with high-order compact schemes (Table I).

The validation covers the [circular cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md)
at $Re_D=3.9\times10^3$ and $1.4\times10^5$, the
[square cylinder](/benchmarks/square-cylinder-flow.md) at
$Re_D=2.2\times10^4$, and a large-span flat roof, stationary and in forced
vibration at 50 Hz. Bulk coefficients, wake profiles, $C_p$ and $C_f$ agree
with DNS, LES and experiments within acknowledged discrepancies (the
recirculation lengths, the favourable-gradient wall stress, the
square-cylinder lift fluctuation). The sixth-order iSVV run is more
accurate and cheaper than a second-order central scheme with the
[Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) on the same mesh, and an
equilibrium variant without the pressure gradient fails on the cylinder
wake. Evidence is numerical comparison with published data.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $s$, $y$ the wall-tangential and wall-normal
directions (the source's $\xi$, $\eta$), $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ the probe distance
(the source's $\eta_P$); $U^B$, $U^P$, $U^{BR}$ the marker (body), probe and
slip-target velocities, $u^P_s=U^P_s-U^B_s$ the probe velocity relative to
the wall; $A_{\mathrm{pl}}$, $m_{\mathrm{pl}}$
the power-law constants (registry §11) and $y_c^+$ the junction height;
$a_{\mathrm P}$, $b_{\mathrm P}$, $c_{\mathrm P}$ the
constants of the adverse-pressure-gradient law; $\Delta p_s^+$ the
inner-scaled tangential pressure gradient (registry §11); $u_D$ the
separation indicator; $\nu_0/\nu$ the iSVV viscosity ratio at the cut-off.

1. Existing wall-modelled diffused-interface IBMs either solve the thin-boundary-layer equation numerically (non-equilibrium) or use equilibrium wall functions; explicit non-equilibrium wall functions had been applied only to sharp-interface IBM (refs. 45, 46); this study applies them to the diffused interface (§I, Table I). **Stated**
2. Wall-modelled IBM with high-order compact finite differences had been restricted to sharp-interface approaches (refs. 62–66); this study couples a wall-modelled diffused-interface IBM with sixth-order compact schemes (§I, Table I). **Stated**
3. The wall model replaces the tangential component of the no-slip target velocity $U^B_s$ by a slip target $U^{BR}_s=U^P_s-(\partial U_s/\partial y)_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}h_{\mathrm{wm}}$, keeping the normal (non-penetration) component (Eqs. 21, 32). **Derived**
4. The probing distance $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ is about three Eulerian mesh spacings, chosen so that the probing markers are outside the region of influence of the IB (§II.C). **Stated**
5. The velocity gradient at the probe is $(\partial U_s/\partial y)_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}=(\tau_w+h_{\mathrm{wm}}\,\partial p/\partial s)/(\rho(\nu+\nu_t))$, from integrating the thin-boundary-layer equation with only the pressure-gradient source (Eqs. 22–23). **Derived**
6. The wall stress is explicit: the Werner–Wengle power law with $y_c^+=11.81$, $m_{\mathrm{pl}}=1/7$, $A_{\mathrm{pl}}=8.3$ (Eqs. 26–27); the adverse-pressure-gradient power law with $a_{\mathrm P}=7.5789$, $b_{\mathrm P}=-1.4489$, $c_{\mathrm P}=191.1799$ (Eqs. 28–30); separation is flagged by $u_D\le0$, and then no-slip is kept with $u_\tau=\sqrt{u^P_s\nu/y}$ (Eqs. 29, 31) *(compiler check, not in the sources: continuity of $u^+=y^+$ and $u^+=A_{\mathrm{pl}}(y^+)^{m_{\mathrm{pl}}}$ gives $y_c^+=8.3^{7/6}=11.81$)*. **Derived**
7. If a strong favourable pressure gradient makes the gradient of Eq. (23) negative, $U^{BR}_s$ is reset to $U^P_s$ (uniform profile); under adverse gradient the lower limit of $U^{BR}_s$ is $U^B_s$ (no-slip), for numerical stability (§II.C, last paragraph). **Stated**
8. The power-law wall function cannot itself provide $(\partial U_s/\partial y)_{h_{\mathrm{wm}}}$ because its velocity gradient is discontinuous at $y_c^+$; the hybrid of wall function and thin-boundary-layer equation is needed for the diffused-interface IBM (§II.C). **Stated**
9. The explicit approximation of the IBM correction (Eq. 18, following ref. 32) avoids inverting an $N_L\times N_L$ matrix, with an $O(h^2)$ error (Eqs. 15–18). **Derived**
10. Circular cylinder at $Re_D=3.9\times10^3$ (sixth order, iSVV $\nu_0/\nu=15$): $\langle C_D\rangle=1.02$, $C_{L,\mathrm{rms}}=0.113$, $L_r/D=1.42$, $-\langle C_{pb}\rangle=0.93$, $St=0.229$; second-order central scheme with Smagorinsky on the same mesh: 1.17, 0.017, 2.42, 0.59, 0.223 (Table II). **Shown**
11. The sixth-order iSVV solver costs 15.04 h wall clock per $10^5$ time steps against 18.16 h for second order with Smagorinsky (96 cores, AMD EPYC 7713, ASPIRE 2A, NSCC Singapore), because iSVV avoids computing subgrid terms explicitly (§III.A). **Shown**
12. The strong scaling of Xcompact3d is preserved with the wall-modelled diffused-interface IBM (Fig. 4, about 10 to about 1000 CPUs; Conclusion: "even with more than $10^3$ cores"). **Shown**
13. Velocity spectra of $u$ and $v$ (the latter captioned "spanwise") in the wake follow the $-5/3$ line and show the shedding peak and its second harmonic (Fig. 5). **Shown**
14. The equilibrium variant (Werner–Wengle only, the pressure-gradient term of Eq. 23 omitted) fails to capture the complexity of the cylinder wake; the non-equilibrium variant captures separation, shear-layer transition, shedding and the multiscale wake by giving much lower $U^{BR}_s$ near separation (Fig. 7). **Shown**
15. Circular cylinder at $Re_D=1.4\times10^5$ ($\nu_0/\nu=300$): $\langle C_D\rangle=1.01$, $C_{L,\mathrm{rms}}=0.24$, $L_r/D=0.98$, $-\langle C_{pb}\rangle=1.04$, $St=0.22$; LES references give $L_r/D=0.42$–0.62, experiments 0.44 (Table III). The discrepancies in recirculation length and downstream pressure are attributed to flow sensitivity, eddy-viscosity models and the simplifications of the boundary-layer equation (§III.A). **Shown**
16. At $Re_D=1.4\times10^5$ the wall stress in the favourable-pressure-gradient upstream region is noticeably lower than the reference, attributed to Werner–Wengle not accounting for favourable gradients; solving the boundary-layer equation by finite differences in post-processing ("Present (FDM)") gives a closer $C_f$ (Fig. 9b). **Shown**
17. Neglecting favourable pressure gradients in the velocity reconstruction is acceptable, because the target velocity is reset to the probe velocity under strong favourable gradients (§III.A). **Stated**
18. Square cylinder at $Re_D=2.2\times10^4$ ($\nu_0/\nu=80$): $\langle C_D\rangle=2.46$, $C_{L,\mathrm{rms}}=1.06$, $L_r/D=0.81$, $St=0.144$, against the DNS of Trias et al. 2.18, 1.71, 1.04, 0.132 (Table IV); $C_{L,\mathrm{rms}}$ and $L_r/D$ are "slightly lower" than the references. **Shown**
19. The wall model misses the two small leading- and trailing-edge recirculation bubbles found by wall-resolving LES, because it assumes a boundary layer with constant pressure gradient between probe and wall, and because of the coarse mesh (§III.B). **Stated**
20. Square-cylinder cost: 13.2 CPU hours (cores times wall clock) per shedding cycle, with 4.95 wall-clock hours per $10^5$ steps, against about 1058 h (body-fitted wall-resolving LES, ref. 102, inferred) and about 676 h (IBM wall-resolving LES, ref. 103, inferred) (Table V). **Shown**
21. Flat roof ($H=0.05$ m, $L=0.3$ m, $U_0=6$ m/s, $Re_H\approx2\times10^4$, laminar inflow): the mean suction is strongest at the leading edge and decays downstream as in wind-tunnel and LES references; the $C_{p,\mathrm{rms}}$ magnitudes are consistent but the distributions differ (Fig. 15). **Shown**
22. Forced vibration in the basic asymmetric mode (Eq. 34, $A_m/H=1/20$, 50 Hz) breaks the leading-edge vortex faster and gives two $C_{p,\mathrm{rms}}$ peaks upstream and one downstream; the magnitudes are smaller than those of ref. 105 (Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
23. The roof case costs about 398 CPU hours per $5\times10^4$ time steps (§III.C). **Shown**
24. The reproduction of the $-5/3$ range by iSVV had been validated on the Taylor–Green vortex (ref. 74) and on wind-turbine wakes (ref. 70) (§III.A). **Stated**

The wall model in registry notation (§II.C, Eqs. 22–31). The
boundary-layer equation $\partial_y[(\nu+\nu_t)\partial_yU_s]=\rho^{-1}\partial p/\partial s$
(the unsteady and convective terms of Eq. 22 dropped) with
$\nu_t/\nu=\kappa y^+(1-e^{-y^+/19})^2$, $\kappa=0.41$, gives the probe
gradient of claim 5. The Werner–Wengle law $u^+=y^+$ ($y^+\le y_c^+$),
$u^+=A_{\mathrm{pl}}(y^+)^{m_{\mathrm{pl}}}$ above, with $u^+=u^P_s/u_\tau$,
inverts explicitly to $u_\tau=(\nu/y)^{m_{\mathrm{pl}}/(m_{\mathrm{pl}}+1)}(u^P_s/A_{\mathrm{pl}})^{1/(m_{\mathrm{pl}}+1)}$;
under adverse gradient

$$u^+=A_{\mathrm{pl}}(y^+)^{m_{\mathrm{pl}}}+a_{\mathrm P}\sqrt{y^+\Delta p_s^+}+b_{\mathrm P}(\Delta p_s^+)^{1/3}\ln\big[c_{\mathrm P}(y^+)^3\Delta p_s^+\big],\qquad \Delta p_s^+=\frac{\nu}{\rho u_\tau^3}\frac{\partial p}{\partial s},$$

and the same inversion is applied to
$u_D=u^P_s-a_{\mathrm P}\sqrt{(y/\rho)\,\partial p/\partial s}-b_{\mathrm P}((\nu/\rho)\,\partial p/\partial s)^{1/3}\ln(c_{\mathrm P}y^3\rho^{-1}\nu^{-2}\,\partial p/\partial s)$
when $u_D>0$. The iSVV replaces the sixth-order compact second derivative
by a 3–9-point compact scheme whose coefficients (Eq. B8) are fixed by
sixth-order accuracy and by the modified wavenumbers at $k_c=\pi/h$ and
$\tfrac23k_c$, so that $(\nu+\nu_{\mathrm{SVV}})k^2$ matches the kernel
$\nu_{\mathrm{SVV}}=\nu_0\exp[-((k_c-k)/(0.3k_c-k))^2]$ for $k\ge0.3k_c$ (zero
below); $\nu_0/\nu$ is the single parameter, preset with the Pao-like
procedure of Dairay et al., and is 15, 300, 80 and 120 in the four cases.
The convection is skew-symmetric, time integration third-order
Adams–Bashforth with a fractional step, and the Poisson equation is solved
directly by FFT on staggered grids (Eqs. 1–7). The comparison closure is
printed $\nu_t=C_s\Delta^2\sqrt{2\bar S_{ij}\bar S_{ij}}$ with $C_s=0.12$
"as in Ref. 60" and $\Delta=(h_xh_yh_z)^{1/3}$ (Eqs. B9–B11; see the
Notation map).

# Printed slips

- **§II.C, after Eq. (32):** "a hybrid approach combining wall function and TBLE, where velocity gradient is derived as Eq. (32)"; Eq. (32) is the target velocity, the gradient is Eq. (23).
- **§II.C, after Eq. (32):** "$U^{BR}$ … which finally influences the $f_i^{n+1/2}$ term in Eq. (3)"; $f_i^{n+1/2}$ appears in Eq. (4).
- **Fig. 2 caption and §II.C:** the velocity gradient $(\partial U_\xi/\partial\eta)_{\eta=\eta_P}$ is called the "pressure gradient" ("the pressure gradient $(\partial U_\xi/\partial\eta)_{\eta=\eta_P}$ for velocity correction can be negative").
- **Eq. (7):** the first convective term is printed $\partial(\tilde u_i^n\tilde u_j^n)/\partial x_i$, Eq. (2) has $\partial(\tilde u_i\tilde u_j)/\partial x_j$.
- **Eq. (35):** $P^B=P^P-\frac{\partial P}{\partial\eta}\eta_P=P^P+\frac{\partial U^B_\eta}{\partial t}\eta_P$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with $P$ the pressure of $C_p=(P^B-P^{ref})/(0.5\rho U_0^2)$, Eq. (33), $\partial P/\partial\eta$ has the dimensions of a density times an acceleration and $\partial U^B_\eta/\partial t$ those of an acceleration; the paper does not say that $P$ is kinematic.)*
- **Eq. (A1):** the sixth-order mid-point interpolation is printed $\tfrac{3}{10}g^I_{i-1/2}+g^I_{i+1/2}+\tfrac{3}{10}g^I_{i+3/2}=\tfrac34\,\tfrac{g_{i+1}+g_i}{2}+\tfrac1{20}\,\tfrac{g_{i+2}+g_{i-1}}{2}$ (article p. 15, read visually). *(Compiler check, not in the sources: for a constant $g\equiv1$ the left side is $8/5$ and the printed right side $4/5$, so the printed right side is half of what consistency for a constant requires.)*
- **Figs. 5–6 captions:** $v$ is called the "spanwise" velocity ("mean spanwise velocity $\overline v$", "variance of spanwise velocity $\overline{v'v'}$"), while the text calls $v$ the transverse velocity and $z$ is the spanwise direction (§III.A).
- **Minor:** Table II defines $St=f_sD/U$ with $U_0$ the free-stream velocity elsewhere; "six-order" throughout; refs. 58 and 61 printed in "Numer. Methods Fluids"; ref. 104 printed "J. Wind Eng. 1990, 35." (kept as printed).
