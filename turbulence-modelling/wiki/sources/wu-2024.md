# Summary

Wu et al. (2024) target stall prediction of multi-element high-lift
airfoils at flight Reynolds numbers with
[self-adaptive turbulence eddy simulation](/methods/self-adaptive-turbulence-eddy-simulation.md)
(SATES), the unified URANS–LES–DNS method of Han and coworkers formerly
called very-large eddy simulation, one of the
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) families. SATES keeps
the BSL $k$-$\omega$ equations ([$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md))
and multiplies the eddy viscosity by the resolution control function $F_r$.
In the earlier version, here SATES-SM, the cutoff coefficient $C_x=0.61$
is calibrated to a [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) LES mode
with $C_s=0.1$, which the authors say puts too much eddy viscosity in
boundary layers. The contribution, SATES-σ, multiplies $C_x$ by an
"enhancing factor", the ratio of the [sigma-model](/methods/sigma-model.md)
to the Smagorinsky viscosity ($C_\sigma=3.0$), with a floor
$C_x\ge0.15$. Because the sigma operator vanishes in pure shear, the
eddy viscosity drops in laminar and initial-shear regions, which the
authors credit with capturing laminar–turbulent transition.

Convection uses the fourth-order
[weighted compact nonlinear scheme](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md)
WCNS-E-4 with linear weights in the CARDC structured solver
[PHengLEI](/software/phenglei.md), against a MUSCL alternative. The tests
are the [turbulent channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at
$Re_\tau=395$, the [square cylinder](/benchmarks/square-cylinder-flow.md)
at $Re_D=2.2\times10^4$, the supercritical
[circular cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md) at
$Re_D=3\times10^6$ (SATES-σ, SATES-SM and SST-IDDES,
[DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)), and the
[30P30N airfoil](/benchmarks/30p30n-high-lift-airfoil.md) at
$Re_C=9\times10^6$, $M=0.2$, from 8° to 24°. On the cylinder SATES-σ
with WCNS gives the supercritical topology ($C_D=0.55$), SATES-SM a
subcritical one ($C_D=1.39$), and MUSCL's
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) prevents the
delayed separation. On the airfoil SATES-σ matches the measured $C_p$
better than SATES-SM and IDDES, captures a
[laminar separation bubble](/definitions/laminar-separation-bubble.md) at
8°, and keeps the total-lift error below 6.5% through stall. Evidence is
numerical comparison with experiments and simulations.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $F_r$, $L_i$, $L_c$, $L_k$, $C_x$, $\beta_F$ the SATES
resolution function, lengths, cutoff coefficient and constant (registry
§11; the source's $\beta^*=0.002$); $C_{x,\mathrm{base}}=0.61$ and
$F_\sigma$ the base coefficient and the enhancing factor of SATES-σ;
$\varsigma_1\ge\varsigma_2\ge\varsigma_3$, $C_\sigma$, $\mathcal D_\sigma$
the singular values, constant and operator of the sigma model (registry
§11); $a_{\mathrm c}$, $b_{\mathrm c}$ the WCNS flux-difference
coefficients; $C$ the stowed chord.

1. SATES modifies only the eddy viscosity of the BSL $k$-$\omega$ model, $\mu_t=F_r\rho k/\omega$; the $k$ and $\omega$ equations are those of BSL (§2.1, Eqs. 1–4). **Stated**
2. $F_r$ "roughly resembles" the ratio of modelled to total turbulent kinetic energy, obtained by integrating an energy spectrum over length between $L_k$ and $L_c$ (numerator) and $L_k$ and $L_i$ (denominator) (§2.1, Eq. 5). **Derived** (heuristic, from [27])
3. Three modes: URANS when $L_c$ is coarse against $L_i$ ($F_r=1$), DNS when $L_c$ is small enough, quasi-LES or LES in between, adapting with the local length scales (§2.1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
4. Han and coworkers' SATES "behaves significantly different from the original VLES method defined by Pope [36] regarding to the ratio of the resolved turbulent energy to the total turbulent energy", hence the renaming (§2.1). **Stated**
5. In SATES-SM, $C_x=\sqrt{0.3}\,C_s/\beta^*$ with $C_s=0.1$, $\beta^*=0.09$, so $C_x=0.61$, calibrated to match the Smagorinsky LES model when $L_c=L_i$ (§2.2, Eq. 7) *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\sqrt{0.3}\times0.1/0.09=0.609$)*. **Stated**
6. The Smagorinsky-type LES mode is "questionable for near-wall treatment because too much sub-grid-scale (SGS) eddy viscosity is introduced into the boundary layer" (§1). **Stated**
7. SATES-σ: $C_x=\max(C_{x,\mathrm{base}}F_\sigma,0.15)$, $C_{x,\mathrm{base}}=0.61$, $F_\sigma=(C_\sigma/C_s)^2\mathcal D_\sigma/|\bar S|$, $C_\sigma=3.0$, $C_s=0.1$ (§2.2, Eqs. 8–11, Table 1). **Stated** (contribution)
8. The floor 0.15 prevents underestimating $C_x$ on coarse grids, where unresolved turbulent boundary layers "would be identified as pure shear flows with vanished turbulent viscosity" (§2.2). **Stated**
9. WCNS-E-4: fourth-order flux difference with $a_{\mathrm c}=9/8$, $b_{\mathrm c}=-1/24$ (Eq. 14), fifth-order linear interpolation (Eq. 15), Roe flux, fourth-order central viscous terms; linear weights reduce the numerical dissipation at low Mach number (§2.3) *(compiler check, not in the sources: $a_{\mathrm c}+3b_{\mathrm c}=1$, and the interpolation weights sum to $128/128$)*. **Stated**
10. Channel: the mean velocity agrees with DNS, the small shift on case 1 disappears on case 2; $F_r=1$ near the wall (URANS mode) and decreases away from it; the ordering of the normal stresses (streamwise > spanwise > wall-normal) is captured, with a considerable resolved shear stress (§3.1a, Figs. 2–4). **Shown**
11. Square cylinder: mean velocities and resolved normal stresses at $x/D=0$–3 agree with DNS [43] and experiment [44] on both grids (§3.1b, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
12. Supercritical cylinder (Table 3), $St$, $C_{pb}$, $L_r/D$, $C_D$: IDDES (WCNS) 0.29, $-0.68$, 0.63, 0.62; SATES-SM (WCNS) 0.20, $-1.69$, 0.33, 1.39; SATES-σ (WCNS) 0.24, $-0.34$, 0.79, 0.55; SATES-σ (MUSCL) 0.19, $-1.32$, 0.61, 1.26; references DES-TS7 [47] 0.35, $-0.53$, 1.0, 0.41; DES-TS10 [47] 0.33, $-0.64$, 1.0, 0.51; RCS-LES [48] 0.311, $-0.696$, 0.527, —; LES [49] at $10^6$ 0.35, $-0.32$, 1.04, 0.31; experiment [50] at $10^6$ 0.22, $-0.33$, —, 0.24 (§3.2). **Shown**
13. SATES-SM predicts "the incorrect flow topology of the subcritical regime" at $Re_D=3\times10^6$; SATES-σ predicts a turbulent separation that suppresses alternate shedding, the supercritical topology (§3.2, Figs. 8–10). **Shown**
14. SATES-σ gives low eddy viscosity in the initial (pure-shear) boundary layer at the leading edge, rising downstream; $C_x$ rises from the floor 0.15 to above 1.0 in the boundary layer before separation (§3.2, Figs. 8, 11). **Shown**
15. SATES-σ reproduces transition on the cylinder: low turbulent kinetic energy and rms $C_p$ on the front half, a sharp peak, then decay; it gives the best mean $C_f$ against Achenbach's experiment [54] (§3.2, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
16. Near the cylinder wall $F_r\approx1$ in SATES-SM (fully turbulent boundary layer), while in SATES-σ $F_r$ rises from a low value to 1 (§3.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
17. WCNS is "more accurate and necessary": MUSCL fails to delay separation (subcritical-like $C_p$, higher drag), damps the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability, shortens the recirculation and suppresses moderate frequencies in the wake spectrum (§3.2, Figs. 13–17; §4). **Shown**
18. Results at supercritical $Re_D$ are sensitive to inflow perturbation, turbulence model and scheme, and experiments [53, 54] and simulations [47, 49] "do not have good convergence"; the criterion used is the correct tendency of the flow topology (§3.2). **Stated**
19. 30P30N at 21°: SATES-σ gives grid-converged $C_p$ (coarse against fine) in good agreement with experiment [56], better than SATES-SM and IDDES, especially at the suction peaks of the main element and flap (§3.3, Fig. 22). **Shown**
20. SATES-σ also agrees with the measured $C_p$ at 8°, 16°, 23°; at 8° a plunge at $x/c\approx0.15$ indicates a large recirculation bubble (transition captured), weakening with incidence (§3.3, Fig. 23). **Shown**
21. Velocity profiles at six stations ($x/C=0.45$ to 1.1125): SATES-σ agrees better than SATES-SM near the wall, especially on the flap under strong adverse pressure gradient; discrepancies remain in the outer layer (§3.3, Fig. 25). **Shown**
22. The total-lift error of SATES-σ is "always less than 6.5%, even at post-stall conditions"; slat lift agrees, flap lift is over-predicted, attributed to free-air simulation against wind-tunnel blockage (§3.3, Fig. 26). **Shown**
23. RANS [57, 58] and transitional and turbulent DDES [25] did not show an appropriate drop of lift after stall; the thick RANS layer of DDES gives too much eddy viscosity and reduces boundary-layer unsteadiness (§1, §3.3). **Stated** (cited)
24. Because SATES-σ works close to LES mode, transition "may not be captured accurately if the computational grid resolution is insufficient", unlike DDES, which is closer to URANS (§4). **Stated**

The model in registry notation (Eqs. 4–11):
$\mu_t=F_r\rho k/\omega$,
$F_r=\min\{1,[(1-e^{-\beta_FL_c/L_k})/(1-e^{-\beta_FL_i/L_k})]^2\}$,
$L_c=C_x\Delta$, $L_i=k^{3/2}/(\beta^*k\omega)$,
$L_k=\nu^{3/4}/(\beta^*k\omega)^{1/4}$, $\Delta=(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$,
$\beta_F=0.002$, $\beta^*=0.09$; SATES-σ sets
$C_x=\max(C_{x,\mathrm{base}}F_\sigma,0.15)$ with
$F_\sigma=(C_\sigma/C_s)^2\mathcal D_\sigma/|\bar S|$,
$\mathcal D_\sigma=\varsigma_3(\varsigma_1-\varsigma_2)(\varsigma_2-\varsigma_3)/\varsigma_1^2$,
$|\bar S|=\sqrt{2\bar S_{ij}\bar S_{ji}}$. $F_\sigma$ is described as the
ratio of the sigma-model and Smagorinsky subgrid viscosities. $C_\sigma=3.0$
enters only through the ratio $(C_\sigma/C_s)^2$; the sigma-model page
records other sources' $C_\sigma$ of 1.3–1.35 for the model used as an
LES closure. The BSL constants other than $\beta^*$ are not printed. Time
integration is second-order backward differentiation with LU-SGS and
Newton-like sub-iterations, started from steady RANS fields; the
comparator MUSCL scheme uses a second-order flux difference and a
limited interpolation (Eqs. 16–17).

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (12):** printed $\partial U/\partial t+\partial E/\partial t=0$, while Eqs. (13)–(14) approximate the spatial derivative $E'_j$ of the flux.
- **Time normalizations:** the time steps are printed as $\Delta t\,D/U_\infty$ for the square and circular cylinders and also for the 30P30N airfoil (§3.3), and the cylinder flow-through times as "$tU_\infty/C$" with the chord $C$ (§3.1b, §3.2); the channel uses $\Delta tU_b/\delta$.
- **Grid count:** §3.3 prints 12.8 M (coarse) and 26.4 M (fine) cells for the 30P30N; §4 says "a relatively coarse grid containing approximately 24 million cells".
- **MUSCL order:** §2.4 "Third-order MUSCL interpolation" with a second-order flux difference; §3.2 "the SATES-σ model with the second-order MUSCL scheme" and "The third-order MUSCL and high-order WCNS-E-4 schemes were compared"; §4 "second-order MUSCL scheme".
- **Table 3** is titled at $Re_D=3\times10^6$ but lists the LES [49] and the experiment [50] at $Re_D=1\times10^6$.
- **§3.2** cites "[46,47,58]" for numerical methods applied to circular cylinders; [58] is printed as Rumsey & Gatski, "Recent turbulence model advances applied to multielement airfoil computations".
- **Square-cylinder profiles** are normalized by $U_0$, the set-up uses $U_\infty$ (§3.1b, Fig. 5).
- **Typesetting:** the Nomenclature lists $\mu_t$ twice; heading "2.3. 2.3 Weighted Compact Nonlinear Schemes"; "suing" for "using" (§2.3).
- **Ref. [54]** (Achenbach) is printed "J. Fluid Mech 34 (4) (2006)" (recorded as printed).
