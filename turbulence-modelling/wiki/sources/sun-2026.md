# Summary

Sun et al. (2026) build a higher-order-in-time
[variational multiscale](/methods/variational-multiscale-method.md) (VMS)
formulation for LES of incompressible flow, which the authors say has been
integrated in time almost only with second-order implicit schemes
(generalized-$\alpha$). Following the Rothe method (time first, space
after) they discretize first with a half-explicit Runge–Kutta (HERK)
scheme of Brasey and Hairer: convection and viscous terms are explicit,
and incompressibility, with the pressure as Lagrange multiplier, is
enforced at every stage. Each stage is then a linear steady Darcy-type
saddle-point problem, so the VMS analysis needs no linearization: the
fine-scale velocity is an $L^2$ projection of the stage momentum residual,
reduced by a bubble argument to an algebraic model with a dimensionless
parameter independent of the mesh size (default $1/2$). Because the
incompressible equations form an index-2 DAE with linear algebraic
variable and constraint, the classical Butcher conditions suffice up to
fourth order, so forward Euler, Heun, SSP-RK3 and classical RK4 tableaux
serve. A fully discrete Fourier
([eigensolution](/methods/eigensolution-analysis.md)) analysis of 1-D
advection–diffusion on $C^1$ quadratic B-splines compares the Rothe
variant with the method-of-lines variant and the IMEX scheme of Le and
Moin. The stage matrix factorizes into one constant matrix for all stages
and steps.

Tests use [isogeometric](/methods/isogeometric-analysis.md) NURBS
discretizations: a [manufactured solution](/methods/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md)
gives the spatial orders $N+1$ in $L^2$ and the temporal orders 1–4; on the
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at $Re=1600$
against a [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) reference,
HERK(3,3)-VMS removes a spurious second peak of the dissipation rate that
VMS with generalized-$\alpha$ produces, its resolved dissipation follows the
DNS while the model dissipation stays small
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)), and it is
less sensitive to the time step; an open cavity gives the critical
Reynolds number 4128 against 4130. The advective (not skew-symmetric) form
of convection is used; split forms for
[kinetic-energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
are named as alternatives only. The statement that in VMS the numerical
method "defines the model" ties the approach to
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) and
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) generally.

# Key claims

$\tau^*$) and $\tau_\diamond$ the classical mesh-dependent parameter of
Eq. (5.5); $a^*=a\Delta t/\Delta x$ and $\kappa^*_{\mathrm d}=\kappa_{\mathrm d}\Delta t/\Delta x^2$
the Courant number and the nondimensional diffusivity of the 1-D model;
$\xi^h$, $\omega^h$ its algorithmic damping and frequency; $\epsilon_E=-dE/dt$

1. In the VMS framework "the numerical method is not just a vehicle for solving the equations, but in fact defines the model" (§1). **Stated**
2. VMS has been discretized predominantly by fully implicit schemes, generalized-$\alpha$ being the most popular; segregated RK schemes suffer order reduction and are restricted to inf-sup stable elements with local-projection stabilization, and "only a five-stage segregated RK scheme has been numerically observed to achieve third-order convergence" (§1). **Stated**
3. Residual-based modelling terms "inevitably alter the index-2 DAE structure" assumed by conventional RK schemes; the Rothe method removes this transient coupling (§1). **Stated**
4. HERK convergence (Proposition 1, from Brasey and Hairer): for an index-2 DAE with consistent initial data, local error $O(\Delta t^{p+1})$, $b_s\neq0$ and $a_{i\,i-1}\neq0$, the differential variable converges with order $p$, and the algebraic variable recovered from the hidden constraint as well (§3.1). **Stated** (proof cited)
5. For general index-2 DAEs a unique three-stage HERK of order three exists and no four-stage HERK attains order four, but when $\mathcal F$ is linear in $\boldsymbol z$ and $\mathcal G$ is linear the extra order conditions vanish or reduce to the Butcher conditions at least to fourth order; incompressible Navier–Stokes on fixed meshes is this case (§3.1–3.2). **Stated**
6. HERK reduces the implicit work from $m+n$ unknowns (fully implicit) to the $m$ of the constraint (§3.1). **Stated**
7. Each HERK stage gives a linear Darcy-type operator with the permeability replaced by $\rho/\Delta t_n$ and gradient and divergence scaled by $\alpha^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ii}$ (§4, Eq. 4.4). **Derived**
8. With no fine-scale pressure and fine scales vanishing on the Dirichlet boundary, the coarse-scale problem with $\boldsymbol u^{<}_i=-(\Delta t_n/\rho)\mathcal P'(\mathcal R_i)$ is "an exact formulation for the coarse-scale problem", with no perturbation-series truncation (§4.1, Eq. 4.10). **Derived**
9. With an element-bubble fine-scale space and the residual replaced by its element average, $\tau_{\mathrm H}=(b_e,1)^2/(|\Omega_e|(b_e,b_e))\in(0,1)$, and the model $\boldsymbol u^{<}_i=-\tau_{\mathrm H}\Delta t_n\mathcal R_i/\rho$ "does not depend on any spatial discretization parameter" (§4.2, Eq. 4.12). **Derived**
10. In the norm $|||\boldsymbol W|||_i^2=(1-\tau_{\mathrm H})(\rho/\Delta t_n)\|\boldsymbol w\|^2+\tau_{\mathrm H}(\Delta t_n/\rho)(\alpha^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ii})^2\|\nabla q\|^2$ the formulation is "unconditionally stable for any continuous velocity-pressure interpolation"; $\tau_{\mathrm H}=1/2$ is balanced and the limits 0 and 1 degenerate (§4.2, Eq. 4.18). **Derived**
11. Both the Rothe variant RK($s,p$)-VMS and the method-of-lines variant VMS-RK($s,p$) have temporal order $p$; RK($s,p$)-VMS has leading spatial errors $O(\Delta x^4)$ in $\xi^h$ and $O(\Delta x^6)$ in $\omega^h$ plus mixed $\Delta x^2\Delta t$, $\Delta x^2\Delta t^2$, $\Delta x^4\Delta t$ terms; VMS-RK($s,p$) has mixed terms in $\tau_\diamond$, so its spatial accuracy drops to third (dissipation) and fourth (dispersion) order when $\Delta x\ll\Delta t$ in advection-dominated conditions (§5.2.2, Tables 2–3). **Derived**
12. Decreasing $\tau_{\mathrm H}$ expands the stability region along $a^*$ for orders above one; RK($s,p$)-VMS extends further along $a^*$ (convection-dominated), VMS-RK($s,p$) is "more amenable to diffusion-dominated regimes" (§5.2.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
13. At high wavenumbers and large $a^*$ RK($s,p$)-VMS has smaller dissipation and dispersion errors than VMS-RK($s,p$) (§5.2.2, Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
14. Against the Le–Moin IMEX scheme (Wray RK3 with stage-wise Crank–Nicolson), the implicit viscous treatment enlarges stability at large $\kappa^*_{\mathrm d}$, both explicit variants being unstable for $\kappa^*_{\mathrm d}\gtrsim0.6$; RK(3,3)-VMS is more stable over a range of small $\kappa^*_{\mathrm d}$, and the IMEX scheme is only $O(\Delta t^2)$ (§5.3, Figs. 4–5). **Shown / Derived**
15. In Kolmogorov units the diffusive limit is $\Delta t/\tau_\eta\lesssim(\Delta x/\eta)^2$ and the convective one $\Delta t/\tau_\eta\lesssim(a_L/a_{\max})Re^{-1/4}\Delta x/\eta$, so at high $Re$ the convective limit controls and implicit viscous treatment does not significantly enlarge the time step (§5.3, Remark 4). **Stated**
16. The stage matrix scales as $\mathbf A/\Delta t_n$, $\alpha^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ii}\mathbf B$, $(\alpha^{\mathrm{RK}}_{ii})^2\Delta t_n\mathbf D$, and with a diagonal rescaling "the same constant matrix problem is solved at every time step and every RK stage" (§6.1–6.2, Eq. 6.8). **Derived**
17. Manufactured solution: $C^1$ quadratic and $C^2$ cubic NURBS give third and fourth order in $L^2$ and second and third in $H^1$ for velocity and pressure; HERK(1,1) to (4,4) reach their temporal orders for velocity, pressure and its time derivative (§7.1, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
18. On $200\times200\times3$ quadratic NURBS (HERK(3,3)-VMS, $\Delta t=2.5\times10^{-4}$, 10 steps): GMRES 35.43 iterations and 0.354 s, FGMRES 6.00 and 2.09 s, MINRES 264.23 and 1.71 s; GMRES is the most efficient (§7.1, Table 5). **Shown**
19. HERK(3,3)-VMS solves three linear systems per step against two Newton iterations of VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5), yet spends significantly less time in linear solves, with assembly and preconditioner set-up "almost negligible" and about 20% less matrix storage (45% with the Kronecker structure) (§7.1, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
20. Taylor–Green vortex, $Re=1600$: VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5) shows "an anomalous double-peaked structure in the dissipation rate, with the secondary peak occurring at $t\approx11$" on all meshes; HERK(3,3)-VMS does not and closely matches DNS on $128^3$ elements (§7.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
21. For VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5), reducing $\Delta t$ from $5\times10^{-2}$ to $10^{-2}$ improves agreement with DNS, but $10^{-3}$ makes the dissipation deviate, attributed to small-time-step instabilities; HERK(3,3)-VMS is mildly sensitive to $\Delta t$ (above $10^{-2}$ it is stability-limited) (§7.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
22. For VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5) the resolved dissipation $\epsilon_{\mathcal Z}$ is only a fraction of $\epsilon_E$, the secondary peak comes from the model dissipation, and $\epsilon_{\mathcal Z}$ does not approach DNS as $\Delta t$ decreases; for HERK(3,3)-VMS $\epsilon_{\mathcal Z}$ follows DNS and the model dissipation is small, mainly near the peak (§7.2, Fig. 12). **Shown**
23. Spectra at $t=9$: on the finest mesh HERK(3,3)-VMS agrees with DNS "up to nearly 80% of the resolved wavenumbers"; on the coarsest it accumulates energy at wavenumbers 20–40; VMS-generalized-$\alpha$ is over-damped at high wavenumbers and deviates as $\Delta t$ decreases (§7.2, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
24. $\tau_{\mathrm H}=0.2$, 0.5, 0.8 give nearly indistinguishable spectra and 0.1 and 0.9 "failed to converge"; a fine-scale pressure $p^{<}=-c_p\rho(\Delta x^2/\Delta t)\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u^{>}$ is indistinguishable for $c_p=0$, 0.2, 2 and over-dissipative at high wavenumbers for $c_p=20$; no fine-scale pressure is adopted (§7.2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
25. Open cavity, $Re=5000$: HERK(2,2)- and HERK(3,3)-VMS at $\Delta t=2.5\times10^{-4}$ match the refined reference to the third decimal ($St=1.692$, 1.695 against 1.692); VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5) is non-monotone in $\Delta t$ and at the smallest step overestimates $u_f$ by about 3% at probes S1 and S2 (§7.3, Table 7). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: the printed Table 7 values give $2.362/2.294=1.030$ at S1 and $4.049/3.941=1.027$ at S2)*
26. From $u_f=\theta\sqrt{1/Re_c-1/Re}$ by regression of $u_f^2$ on $1/Re$: HERK(3,3)-VMS gives $Re_c=4128$, $\theta=4.20$; VMS-generalized-$\alpha$(0.5) $Re_c=4110$, $\theta=4.23$; the theoretical values are 4130 and 4.18 (§7.3, Fig. 20). **Shown**
27. GMRES iterations for HERK(3,3)-VMS are insensitive to $Re$ (108.13, 108.20, 109.93 at $Re=2500$, 5000, 10000) because the matrix does not depend on $Re$ (§7.3, Table 8). **Shown**
28. Explicit convective and viscous terms make the stability limit more restrictive than for fully implicit or IMEX schemes; low-cost RK strategies that avoid stage pressure solves are left to future work (§8). **Stated**
