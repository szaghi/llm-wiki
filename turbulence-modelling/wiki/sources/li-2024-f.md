# Summary

Li et al. (2024b) apply improved delayed
[detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) based on
the [Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md)
(SA-IDDES) to the [NASA CRM high-lift configuration](/benchmarks/nasa-crm-high-lift.md).
The case is the HLPW-4 landing configuration (flaps $40^\circ/37^\circ$) at
$M=0.2$, $Re_c=5.49\times10^6$ and $7.05^\circ$ incidence. The authors
compare it with steady SA-RANS. The [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md)
choice is argued from two premises. Seamless methods are needed because
zonal ones are "intractable" for this geometry. IDDES avoids the
grid-induced separation and [log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md)
of the original DES. The closure is written in
[Boussinesq](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) form. The solver is the
in-house unstructured cell-centred finite-volume code HUNS3D: second-order
Roe flux with low-Mach preconditioning and dual time stepping, run on the
HLPW-4 ANSA level-C grid of $276\times10^6$ cells.

The time-averaged surface pressure of both methods agrees with the
QinetiQ wind-tunnel data. SA-RANS underestimates the flap leading-edge
pressure behind the inboard flap bracket because it overpredicts the
separation there. The drag is 0.1868 in the experiment, 0.1867 with
SA-RANS and 0.1843 with SA-IDDES, which has the lower friction drag. The
authors compare vorticity magnitude, Q-criterion and Rortex on the IDDES
field over a threshold sweep. Vorticity and $Q$ mark shear layers in the
boundary layer as vortices, while Rortex at $R=0.5$ removes this shear
contamination and keeps weak vortices. IDDES resolves the nacelle-strake
vortex, hairpin-like vortex forests on the inboard wing and in slat-bracket
wakes, the tip vortices, a horseshoe vortex at the lower wing–fuselage
junction, and Kelvin–Helmholtz wake vortices. RANS captures only the strong
tip vortices. The authors explain RANS's larger flap separation by a
flap-bracket tip vortex that does not break down. The vortex discussion cites
slat-vortex elongation work on the [30P30N airfoil](/benchmarks/30p30n-high-lift-airfoil.md).
The evidence is one grid, one incidence and qualitative visualization,
with no time-step or averaging-window information.

# Key claims

1. DES, DDES and IDDES are seamless hybrid RANS/LES methods, and for the geometric complexity of high-lift transport configurations a zonal method is "intractable to use" (§I). **Stated**
2. Earlier hybrid RANS/LES studies of the CRM-HL (refs. 26–29) focused on surface data and verification and did not investigate the vortex system; this paper sets out to (§I). **Stated**
3. IDDES is adopted because it "can avoid grid-induced separation (GIS) and logarithmic layers mismatched (LLM) issues of the original DES method" (§II.B.2, citing Shur et al. 2008). **Stated**
4. SA-IDDES replaces $d$ in the destruction term of the SA equation by $l_{\mathrm{IDDES}}=\tilde f_d(1+f_e)\,d+(1-\tilde f_d)\,C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta$, with $\Delta=\min[\max(C_wd,\,C_w\Delta_{\max},\,\Delta_{wn}),\Delta_{\max}]$ and $C_w=0.15$ "recommended in Ref. 15" (§II.B.2, Eq. 4). **Stated**
5. The case is the HLPW-4 landing configuration (inboard flap $40^\circ$, outboard $37^\circ$) at $M=0.2$, $Re_c=5.49\times10^6$, incidence $7.05^\circ$, the highest-priority HLPW-4 case, close to the nominal approach incidence of $8^\circ$ (§III.A). **Stated**
6. Grid: HLPW-4 "103-ANSA-Unstructured" level C of five, $276\times10^6$ cells for the half model, first-layer $y^+\le1$, refined near the high-lift wing and in the nacelle wake; far field at $1000c$; 6000 MPI partitions (§III.B). **Stated**
7. Low-Mach preconditioning (Weiss & Smith) is applied to the second-order Roe scheme because the $M=0.2$ flow, especially in the slat cove and flap cabin, is nearly incompressible and stiff for a compressible solver (§III.C, Table I). **Stated**
8. The time-averaged $C_p$ of SA-RANS and SA-IDDES is basically consistent and both agree well with the incidence-corrected wind-tunnel data (§III.D.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. At $\eta=0.33$ (inboard flap bracket) SA-RANS significantly underestimates $C_p$ on the flap leading edge, attributed to an overpredicted separation near the bracket (§III.D.1, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
10. At $\eta=0.42$ SA-IDDES deviates from the experiment slightly more than SA-RANS; the other slices are almost identical (§III.D.1). **Shown**
11. $C_D$: experiment 0.1868; SA-RANS 0.1867 (pressure 0.1701, friction 0.0165); SA-IDDES 0.1843 (pressure 0.1691, friction 0.0151): the lower IDDES friction gives a slightly lower $C_D$ (§III.D.2). **Shown**
12. A DES drag below the RANS one also appears in Probst et al. (ref. 29), whose DDES gave $C_D=0.1793$ (§III.D.2). **Stated**
13. SA-IDDES gives a lower friction magnitude than SA-RANS but predicts the separation better, judged against oil flow (§III.D.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. SA-IDDES skin-friction patterns at the wing root and tip, shaped by the pylon, nacelle strake and slat brackets, agree with the oil flow, and separation at the nacelle tail, wing tip and outboard flap is captured (§III.D.2, Figs. 6c–f). **Shown**
15. Vorticity and Q-criterion are both affected by shear and mark spurious structures in strong-shear regions without vortices; Rortex defines a vortex by the rigid rotation of the fluid element and "theoretically" performs better (§III.D.3, refs. 42–45). **Stated**
16. On a threshold sweep (0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 5.0, 10.0, scaled by the free-stream speed of sound and the mean aerodynamic chord) the vorticity method at 0.05 is "seriously affected by numerical contamination" and almost unusable, and at 0.2 and 0.5 shows structures at poorer resolution than the other two methods (§III.D.3, Fig. 7). **Shown**
17. Vorticity and $Q$ iso-surfaces surround the walls in the boundary layer; raising the $Q$ threshold reduces the contamination at the cost of filtering weak vortices and may not remove it under strong shear (§III.D.3, Fig. 7). **Shown**
18. Rortex is shear-contaminated at $R=0.05$ and 0.2, but at $R=0.5$ the contamination is "well eliminated" while weak vortices are kept; $R=0.5$ is used for Figs. 7–13 (§III.D.3). **Shown**
19. SA-RANS captures only strongly swirling tip vortices; SA-IDDES captures fine vortices of wall turbulence, free shear turbulence and wakes (§III.D.3, Figs. 8–11). **Shown**
20. Because SA-RANS misses the "rushing effect" of upstream vortices on the flap-bracket tip vortex, that vortex persists without breakdown and rolls the flap upper-surface flow into separation, which explains the larger RANS separation near the brackets (§III.D.3). **Stated** (interpretation of Figs. 8–9)
21. The nacelle-strake tip vortex keeps its form over almost the entire upper surface at $7.05^\circ$, whereas at $19.57^\circ$ it breaks down at the leading edge (ref. 29); its roll-up accelerates the upper-surface flow, the purpose of the strake (§III.D.3). **Shown** ($7.05^\circ$) / **Stated** ($19.57^\circ$)
22. A hairpin-like vortex forest forms on the rear half of the wing-root upper surface: slat-shed vortices stretched by the acceleration in the slat gap become longitudinal vortices at the main-wing leading edge and evolve by bending, pairing and lifting into hairpins, which Rortex lines confirm (§III.D.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
23. Hairpin-like vortices also form in slat-bracket wakes near the tip; the wake near the flap tip is rolled into the flap-tip vortex, lifting one leg of the hairpin (§III.D.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
24. Rortex lines, integral curves of the Rortex vector $\boldsymbol r$, give the rotation axis and the core independently of any threshold, an advantage over scalar criteria (§III.D.3). **Stated**
25. A horseshoe vortex forms at the wing–fuselage junction on the lower surface only, because the upper surface has no backflow to form the other branch (§III.D.3, Figs. 11, 14). **Shown** (visualization), mechanism **Stated**
26. Free shear layers shed from the nacelle and flap trailing edges generate Kelvin–Helmholtz wake vortices, called "a prominent source of airframe noise in the cruising stage" (§III.D.3). **Stated**
27. For this case IDDES "basically satisfies the engineering simulation requirements of aerodynamic load and complex vortex system" of the landing configuration (§IV). **Stated** (one case, one grid)

The vortex criteria compared (Eqs. 5–7), in registry symbols:
$|\boldsymbol\omega|$; $Q=\tfrac12(\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{ij}-S_{ij}S_{ij})$, as
printed with tensor norms $\tfrac12(\|\boldsymbol\Omega\|^2-\|\boldsymbol S\|^2)$;
and the Rortex magnitude $R$, the rigid-rotation strength about the unit
axis $\boldsymbol r$ (page-local).
