# Summary

Abe (2021) asks how the filter width $\Delta$ of
[large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) should be
built from the grid spacings when the cells are strongly anisotropic. The
cube root of the cell volume, $(\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)^{1/3}$, is
compared with the largest spacing, $\max(\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z)$. To
separate the aspect-ratio effect from near-wall damping, the study looks at
the free shear layer behind a
[backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md) (expansion ratio
1.5, $Re_\tau=290$ in the driver channel, $Re_H\approx5700$). There the
wall-normal spacing is very small although no wall is near: on the coarse
grid $\Delta x/\Delta y\sim100$.

Three wall-resolved LES on a fine, a medium and a coarse grid use the
author's hybrid LES/RANS model, which behaves as pure LES with the
"stabilized mixed model" (SMM) here. The fine run matches the Kasagi–Matsunaga
experiment ($X_R/H=6.54$ against 6.51). It is top-hat filtered in $x$ and
$z$ to the coarse resolution and gives reference SGS stresses for an
[a priori test](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) of five
models: the [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md), its
[dynamic](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) form, two
[scale-similarity models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md) (Bardina-type
and modified-Leonard) and the SMM. The comparison uses an equivalent eddy
viscosity, which keeps both forward transfer and
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md) of the reference stress.

With the cube-root width the Smagorinsky eddy viscosity is far too small in
the shear layer. The dynamic model, the similarity models and the SMM, whose
width reduces to the largest spacing on these cells, agree well. Swapping
the widths reverses the result, both a priori and in a coarse-grid
a posteriori run, which lengthens the recirculation bubble. A no-model
([implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)) coarse run lengthens it
further. The conclusion is that a spacing much smaller than the others must
be left out of $\Delta$. The [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md)
switching function enters only as context; the SMM is set beside optimal
clipping on the [gradient-model](/methods/gradient-model.md) page.

# Key claims

1. On a high-aspect-ratio grid the cube root of the cell volume, $\Delta=(\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)^{1/3}$, is "not always appropriate" for the filter width; "the effect of a grid width in one direction much smaller than in other directions must be excluded in determining the filter width" (Abstract; §V). **Shown**
2. In the a priori test the Smagorinsky model with the cube-root width gives an eddy viscosity "much smaller than the reference data", "clearly suppressed by the extremely narrow grid width in the y-direction" in the shear layer at $y/H\sim0$ (§IV C, Fig. 9(a)). **Shown**
3. The dynamic Smagorinsky model gives a "generally reasonable" $\nu_t$ with some overestimation downstream. Because it computes the product $C\Delta^2$ at once, it "does not depend explicitly on the filter-width definition" (§II A; §IV C, Fig. 9(b)). **Shown** (prediction) / **Stated** (reason)
4. The scale-similarity models BRD and mLND give $\nu_t$ "in good agreement with the reference data" (§IV C, Fig. 9(c),(d)). **Shown**
5. The SMM predicts $\nu_t$ well although its eddy-viscosity part is an algebraic one-equation form; its different filter-width definition is proposed as the cause of the difference from the Smagorinsky model (§IV C, Fig. 9(e)). **Shown**
6. Swapping filter widths reverses the result: mSMM (SMM with the cube root) is much smaller than the SMM and suppressed in the shear layer, and mSM (Smagorinsky with $\max(\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z)$) has "a reasonable distribution even in the shear layer" (§IV D, Fig. 10). **Shown**
7. A posteriori, the coarse run with mSMM gives $X_R/H=7.43$ (+14.1%) and $X_S/H=1.82$, against 7.19 (+10.4%) and 1.75 with the SMM and 6.51 and 1.70 in the experiment. mSMM shows "considerable suppression of the SGS values in the shear-layer region", which "does not disappear even if further tuning is performed" and is "an essential feature of SGS models using the cube root of a grid-cell volume" (§IV D, Tables II–III, Fig. 11). **Shown** (numbers) / **Stated** (independence of tuning)
8. Grid convergence of the LES with the SMM: $X_R/H=6.54$ (+0.5%), 6.67 (+2.5%), 7.19 (+10.4%) and $X_S/H=1.72$, 1.95, 1.75 on the fine, medium and coarse grids, against 6.51 and 1.70 (§IV A, Table II). **Shown**
9. Without SGS model on the coarse grid, $X_R/H=7.60$ (+16.7%) and $X_S/H=1.50$, a much longer bubble than with the SMM; "as the grid resolution becomes coarser, the performance of the ILES suddenly decreases", so a high-quality SGS model is preferable on coarse grids (App. B, Table IV, Fig. 14; §I citing Abe 2019). **Shown**
10. On the fine grid the SGS kinetic energy and SGS shear stress are very small against the total statistics, so the fine-grid resolved field "can be approximately regarded as DNS data" and serves as the a priori reference (§IV B, Fig. 5; App. B). **Shown**
11. Statistics from 20 snapshots (resolved field only, spanwise ensemble of 128 samples per snapshot on the fine grid) show "no significant conflict" with long-time averages, with imperfect convergence (wavy shear stress) (§IV B, Fig. 6). **Shown**
12. The coarse run with the SMM gives SGS kinetic energy and SGS shear stress that "generally correspond well" to the filtered reference, with overprediction at $x/H=1$; the instantaneous $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ looks "a little too diffusive" (§IV B, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
13. For algebraic $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ estimates, the Smagorinsky model with the cube root is considerably suppressed in the shear layer, mSM is reasonable, and BRD and mLND agree well, mLND best in instantaneous fields; this is attributed to the high correlation of mLND with the true SGS stress (§IV E, Figs. 12–13). **Shown** / attribution **Stated**
14. Equating the Smagorinsky and one-equation eddy viscosities gives $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\big[(C_sf_S)^2/(C_{\mathrm{sgs}}f_{\mathrm{sgs}})\big]^2\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert^2$, printed as $0.08\,(f_S^4/f_{SGS}^2)\Delta^2S^2$ with $S^2=S_{ij}S_{ij}$, $C_S=0.1$, $C_{SGS}=0.05$ (§IV E, Eqs. 18–19). **Derived**. In registry form the constant is $C_s^4/C_{\mathrm{sgs}}^2=0.04$ multiplying $\lvert\bar S\rvert^2$, the same as the printed $0.08$ multiplying $S_{ij}S_{ij}$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
15. Filtering also in $y$ (three-point trapezoidal filter, medium to coarse data) gives "almost the same values" of SGS energy and eddy viscosity in the shear layer as $x$–$z$ filtering (App. C, Fig. 15). **Shown**
16. For $\Delta x=\Delta z\gg\Delta y$ the SMM width $\sqrt{\max(\Delta x\Delta y,\Delta y\Delta z,\Delta z\Delta x)}$ equals $\max(\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z)$ (§II C). **Derived**
17. The extra anisotropic term of the SMM "yields no undesirable extra energy transfer between the GS and SGS components" (§II C, from Abe 2013, 2014). **Stated**
18. A pure scale-similarity model "often causes numerical instability", and $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ from BRD did not always work a posteriori on coarse grids (Abe 2013), hence the transport equation for $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ in the SMM. Inagaki & Kobayashi (2020) report that a local-equilibrium algebraic $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ can work on coarse grids and resembles mSM (§V). **Stated**
19. Implicit LES "does not always account for the detailed dynamics of small-scale turbulent structures" (§I). **Stated**
20. The hybrid switching function "guarantee[s] a full LES under a sufficiently fine grid-resolution condition"; it indicates LES throughout $x/H<12$ on the fine and medium grids, and almost everywhere except near the upper wall on the coarse grid (§III; App. A). **Stated** (cited to Abe 2014b; distributions not plotted)
21. Earlier checks (Abe 2013, 2014, 2020) showed that the convection blending and the first-order time / second-order upwind discretization of the $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ equation had "no important effect on the final conclusions" (§III). **Stated**

## Models as printed, in registry notation

All models write the deviatoric SGS stress $\tau^d_{ij}$ (Eq. 2: $\tau_{ij}=\overline{u_iu_j}-\bar u_i\bar u_j$, positive trace, incompressible). Test filter $\hat{\ }$ is top-hat with twice the grid width.

- Smagorinsky (Eqs. 3–4): $\tau^d_{ij}=-2\nu_t\bar S_{ij}$, $\nu_t=(C_sf_S\Delta)^2\lvert\bar S\rvert$, $f_S=1-\exp(-y^+/A^+)$, $C_s=0.1$, $A^+=25$, $\Delta$ the cube root. §IV E uses instead $f_S=\sqrt{1-\exp[-(y^+/A^+)^3]}$ (Eq. 20).
- Dynamic Smagorinsky (Eq. 5): $\nu_t=C\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert$ with $C$ from Germano's identity and Lilly's least squares.
- BRD (Eqs. 7–8): $\tau^d_{ij}=C_B\big[(\bar u_i-\hat{\bar u}_i)(\bar u_j-\hat{\bar u}_j)\big]^d$, $C_B=2$.
- mLND (Eqs. 10–11): $\tau^d_{ij}=C_L\big(\widehat{\bar u_i\bar u_j}-\hat{\bar u}_i\hat{\bar u}_j\big)^d=C_LL^d_{ij}$, $C_L=0.5$.
- SMM (Eqs. 12–16): $\tau^d_{ij}=-2\nu_t\bar S_{ij}+2k_{\mathrm{sgs}}b^{\mathrm{EAT}}_{ij}$, with $b^{\mathrm{EAT}}_{ij}$ the Bardina stress (with $C_B=1$) minus its eddy-viscosity projection, normalized by its trace, minus $\tfrac13\delta_{ij}$; $\nu_t=C_{\mathrm{sgs}}f_{\mathrm{sgs}}\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{sgs}}}\,\Delta$; a transport equation for $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgs}}=C_\varepsilon k_{\mathrm{sgs}}^{3/2}/\Delta+2\nu k_{\mathrm{sgs}}/y^2$; $C_{\mathrm{sgs}}=0.05$, $A_0=30$, $C_l=4$, $C_\varepsilon=0.835$, $\sigma_k=0.5$; $\Delta=\sqrt{\max(\Delta x\Delta y,\Delta y\Delta z,\Delta z\Delta x)}$, unlike the cube root of Abe (2013).
- Equivalent eddy viscosity (Eq. 17): $\nu_t=-\tau^d_{ij}\bar S_{ij}/\lvert\bar S\rvert^2=\Pi/\lvert\bar S\rvert^2$ of a given stress; used in the eddy-viscosity form it reproduces exactly the resolved–subgrid transfer of that stress, forward and backward.

# Printed slips

- **Case label (§III, p. 115120-5; Fig. 8 caption).** "Even in the coarse-grid case (BFC)" and "(b) real calculation (BFC)"; the coarse case is BSC everywhere else (Table I).
- **Bulk velocity (§III).** $U_b$ is defined as "the mean velocity at the center of the driver channel", a centreline velocity, yet it is called the bulk velocity and $Re_H=U_bH/\nu$ the "bulk Reynolds number".
- **Two damping functions named $f_S$.** Eq. 4 prints $f_S=1-\exp(-y^+/A)$; Eq. 20 prints $f_S=\sqrt{1-\exp\{-(y^+/A)^3\}}$. The paper states the change in §IV E.
- **Cosmetic.** "We intentionally adopts uniform grid spacing" (§III); "The authors have no conflicts" for a single-author paper; ref. [34] ends with an unmatched parenthesis "2020)."
