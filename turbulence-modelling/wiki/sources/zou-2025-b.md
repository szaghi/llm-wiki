# Summary

Zou et al. (2025) build a moving-mesh fluid–structure interaction solver for
the transverse vortex-induced vibration (VIV) of an elastically mounted
rigid circular cylinder inside their Runge–Kutta discontinuous Galerkin
framework (the FSMESH module of the FSLAB software). The mesh translates
rigidly with the cylinder in an earth-fixed frame, and the compressible
Navier–Stokes equations are rewritten with a convective flux shifted by the
mesh velocity, $\tilde{\boldsymbol F}^c=\boldsymbol F^c-\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\boldsymbol U$.
The discretization is a modal DG method
(complete $P^N$ bases on quadrilaterals, not the nodal form of the
[DG spectral element method](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md)) with an interior-penalty
viscous flux, a Lax–Friedrichs flux whose wave speed is shifted by
$\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\cdot\boldsymbol n$, and a positivity-preserving
limiter combined with an unstructured [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)
limiter; [artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
is set aside as parameter-dependent and over-dissipative. Block-based
quad-tree [mesh adaptation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md) is driven by a
normalized-vorticity threshold, and a cell-based nodal correction moves
nodes onto the curved wall. Fluid and oscillator are advanced together by
the third-order TVD Runge–Kutta scheme of Gottlieb, Shu & Tadmor (their ref. 46). The mesh motion is a rigid
translation; the paper does not discuss the
[geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md).

Verification: compressible Couette flow gives the design orders of IP(2),
IP(3) and IP(4); the laminar fixed [cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md)
at $Re_D=150$, $M=0.2$ gives $C_D$, lift and drag amplitudes and $St$ close
to Zhang et al. and Müller. Six VIV runs ($Re_D=150$, $m^*=2$, $c^*=0$,
$U_R=3$–8, $M=0.085$) reproduce the lock-in amplitude curve of Ahn &
Kallinderis and Borazjani et al. The authors call these runs "direct
numerical simulations"; they are two-dimensional laminar computations
without a turbulence model (the term as printed is recorded on the
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) page). Synthetic jets at $U_R=4$,
$C_\mu=3.0$, fully suppress the vibration at actuation frequency $f^*=5$ and
progressively less at $f^*=50$, 75 and 90, which the authors attribute to
the jet energy leaving as acoustic waves, seen in density contours.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol U=[\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E]^T$ the conserved
vector, $\boldsymbol F^c$, $\boldsymbol F^v$ the convective and viscous fluxes;
$\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}=(u_{\mathrm{mesh}},v_{\mathrm{mesh}})$ the mesh
velocity (the source's $\mathbf V_{mesh}$); $\partial^\circ/\partial^\circ t$ the
time derivative following a mesh point; $c$ the sound speed; $\alpha_{\mathrm{LF}}$
the Lax–Friedrichs wave-speed bound (the source's $\alpha$); IP($n$) the
interior-penalty DG scheme of order $n$; $A_{\min}$, $A_{\max}$ the
refinement levels; $\omega^*_z=\omega_zd/(2|\boldsymbol u_\infty|)$ the
normalized vorticity with thresholds $\omega_{\min}$, $\omega_{\max}$;
$m$, $c_{\mathrm s}$, $k_{\mathrm s}$ the cylinder mass, damping and stiffness
(the source's $m$, $c$, $k$); $Y=y_{\mathrm c}/d$ the displacement;
$t^*=tu_\infty/d$; $m^*=m/(\rho_\infty d^2)$, $c^*=c_{\mathrm s}/(2\sqrt{k_{\mathrm s}m})$,
$U_R=u_\infty/(f_nd)$ the mass ratio, damping ratio and reduced velocity;
$f^*=f_{\mathrm{sj}}/f_v$ and $C_\mu=2U_{\max}^2\delta_{\mathrm o}/(u_\infty^2d)$
the actuation frequency and momentum coefficient with orifice length
$\delta_{\mathrm o}=\pi d/32$.

1. The compressible Navier–Stokes equations on a rigidly translating mesh, in the earth-fixed frame, read $\partial^\circ\boldsymbol U/\partial^\circ t+\nabla\cdot(\tilde{\boldsymbol F}^c-\boldsymbol F^v)=-\boldsymbol U\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}$ with $\tilde{\boldsymbol F}^c=(\boldsymbol F^c_x-u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\boldsymbol U,\ \boldsymbol F^c_y-v_{\mathrm{mesh}}\boldsymbol U)$ (§II A, Eqs. 8–10). **Derived**
2. The Jacobian of the moving-mesh convective flux is the fixed-mesh one minus $(\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\cdot\boldsymbol n)\mathsf I$, shares its right eigenvectors, and its eigenvalues are $\tilde\lambda=\lambda-(\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\cdot\boldsymbol n)$ (§II C, Eqs. 16–17). **Derived** (the eigenvector statement is "it can be shown", not shown)
3. The Lax–Friedrichs bound on a moving mesh is $\alpha_{\mathrm{LF}}=\max[(|\boldsymbol u\cdot\boldsymbol n-\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\cdot\boldsymbol n|+c)^+,(|\boldsymbol u\cdot\boldsymbol n-\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{mesh}}\cdot\boldsymbol n|+c)^-]$ (Eq. 18). **Derived**
4. Among the DG viscous discretizations (BR1, BR2, IPDG, LDG, CDG, DDG), IPDG "demonstrates superior computational efficiency and implementation simplicity", hence its choice (§I). **Stated** (no comparison shown)
5. There is "no universally optimal method for selecting" the penalty coefficient; larger values strengthen inter-element continuity and too large values make the problem ill-conditioned; the paper uses $\sigma_{\mathrm{IP}}=4$ with a penalty scaling as $\mu N^2/h$ on the jump of the conserved vector (§II C, Eq. 19). **Stated**
6. Artificial-viscosity shock capturing is parameter-dependent and over-dissipative, reconstruction-based capturing is hard in complex and three-dimensional geometry because of non-compact stencils, and limiters are more flexible; the paper uses the positivity-preserving limiter of Liu et al. (2016) with the unstructured WENO limiter of Zhu et al. (2013) (§II B). **Stated**
7. Adaptation uses a block-based quad-tree with $N_{\mathrm{seg}}^2$ cells per block, guard cells for inter-block exchange, MPI parallelism and levels between $A_{\min}$ and $A_{\max}$ (§II D, Fig. 1). **Stated**
8. The refinement criterion refines where $|\omega^*_z|>\omega_{\max}$ and coarsens where $|\omega^*_z|<\omega_{\min}$; in the cylinder case blocks reach $A_{\max}=5$ where $|\omega^*_z|>0.25$ (§II D; §III B, Fig. 9). **Stated / Shown**
9. The cell-based nodal correction, which also repositions nodes of individual cells along block edges, approximates a curved wall better than the block-based correction of Kong et al. (§II D, Fig. 2, circle of radius $R=0.05$). **Shown** (qualitative figure, no error measure)
10. Ghost-node moving-wall conditions: adiabatic no-slip $\boldsymbol u_R=-\boldsymbol u_L+2\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{wall}}$ with $p$, $T$, $e$, $\rho$ mirrored; isothermal no-slip with $T_R=T_{\mathrm{wall}}$, $\rho_R$ from the Tammann equation of state and $\rho_L\boldsymbol u_L+\rho_R\boldsymbol u_R=(\rho_L+\rho_R)\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{wall}}$; jet orifice $\boldsymbol u_R=\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{wall}}+\boldsymbol u_{\mathrm{sj}}$ (§II E, Eqs. 20–24). **Stated**
11. Fluid and structure are advanced together as one state $\boldsymbol Z=[\boldsymbol U,y_{\mathrm c},\dot y_{\mathrm c}]^T$ by the third-order TVD Runge–Kutta scheme, the cylinder acceleration being recomputed at every stage from the stage lift (Eqs. 29–30). **Stated**
12. Couette flow, density $L_2$ error on $2\times2$, $4\times4$, $8\times8$ grids: IP(2) $5.18\times10^{-3}$, $1.28\times10^{-3}$, $3.2\times10^{-4}$ (orders 2.0168, 2.00); IP(3) $6.8\times10^{-5}$, $8.7\times10^{-6}$, $1.02\times10^{-6}$ (orders 2.9664, 3.0924); IP(4) $1.64\times10^{-6}$, $8.52\times10^{-8}$, $4.35\times10^{-9}$ (orders 4.2667, 4.2918) (Table I). **Shown**
13. Fixed cylinder, $Re_D=150$, $M=0.2$: mean $C_D$ 1.33 / 1.3503 / 1.3547, drag amplitude 0.0497 / 0.0518 / 0.0512, lift amplitude 1.016 / 1.04 / 1.0456, $St$ 0.187 / 0.1845 / 0.1847 for IP(2) / IP(3) / IP(4), against Zhang et al. (1.348, 0.0519, 1.048, 0.184) and Müller (1.34, 0.05228, 1.0406, 0.183) (Table II). **Shown**
14. IP(2) shows "significant flow field distortion", evident from its higher Strouhal number and low-resolution vortex structures, while the IP(3) and IP(4) vorticity fields differ negligibly (§III B, Fig. 8). **Shown**
15. Adaptation "achieves substantial computational efficiency gains while preserving solution accuracy" (§III B; Conclusion 1). **Stated** (no cost or timing figures given)
16. Six transverse VIV cases ($Re_D=150$, $m^*=2$, $c^*=0$, $U_R=3.0$–8.0, $M=0.085$, IP(3)) agree "excellently" with Ahn & Kallinderis (2006) and Borazjani et al. (2008) for the maximum amplitude against $U_R$, with lock-in between $U_R\approx4$ and 7 (§III C, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. The authors call the six VIV runs "direct numerical simulations (DNS)" (§III C); they are two-dimensional laminar computations at $Re_D=150$ without a turbulence model. **Stated**
18. $M=0.085<0.2$ is said to satisfy "the incompressibility condition" (§III C). **Stated**
19. Synthetic jets at $f^*=5$ fully suppress the vibration: $Y$ falls from 0.562 to 0.1497 ($t^*=57.7611$), 0.0346 ($t^*=65.9610$) and 0.0001 ($t^*=100$) (§III C, Table III). **Shown**
20. At $f^*=50$ suppression is slower but near-complete: $Y=0.0113$ at $t^*=138.5217$ (Table III). **Shown**
21. At $f^*=75$ and 90 suppression is incomplete: $Y=0.2149$ ($t^*=145.0911$) and 0.3153 ($t^*=147.2236$), the decay rate "approaching zero" (Table III, Fig. 12c,d). **Shown**
22. Higher actuation frequency weakens suppression because "the energy of the SJs is more in the form of acoustic waves": shorter acoustic wavelengths appear in the density contours as $f^*$ rises (§III C, Figs. 15–17; Conclusion 4). **Shown** (qualitatively, from contours; no energy budget computed)
23. This frequency trend is "consistent with the conclusions drawn by Wang et al. in their 2016 study" (ref. 63) (§III C). **Stated**
24. The moving-mesh method is limited to single bodies; an overset-grid extension is planned (§IV). **Stated**
