# Summary

Chen et al. (2024) modify the algebraic [Baldwin–Lomax model](/methods/baldwin-lomax-model.md)
for compressible zero-pressure-gradient [turbulent boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).
The model is a zero-equation RANS closure of the whole boundary layer through a
[Boussinesq](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) eddy viscosity. The title's
"wall model" is this RANS closure, not an LES wall-stress model of
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md). The baseline is the semilocal
variant ("BL-local") used in hypersonic codes. It predicts the mean velocity well
for adiabatic layers but degrades with wall heat transfer, and its temperature
deviates from DNS. The authors set themselves three rules: leave the
incompressible model unchanged, use only established relations without fitting
new coefficients, and keep the change simple.

The authors make three changes. (1) They show that the BL-local inner eddy viscosity
is equivalent to a mixing-length relation in the Trettel–Larsson
[velocity transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md),
and replace it by the total-stress-based Griffin–Fu–Moin transformation. (2) From
the van Driest defect scaling, they weight the outer vorticity function by
$\sqrt{\langle\rho\rangle}$ and divide the outer eddy viscosity by it. (3) DNS shows
that the turbulent Prandtl number is non-monotonic and singular near the
temperature peak of cold-wall layers, a point taken up under
[Morkovin's hypothesis and Reynolds analogies](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md).
The energy equation with constant $Pr_t=0.9$ is therefore solved only above
$y^*=100$. Below that height the temperature follows the quadratic
temperature–velocity relation, anchored at the matching point as in the
[inverse-velocity-transformation wall model](/methods/inverse-velocity-transformation-wall-model.md)
of Griffin et al. (2023). The model's [law-of-the-wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
ingredients are the Johnson–King inner eddy viscosity and the Clauser defect law.

The scalings are diagnosed from [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
data first. The model is then tested
[a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) by a
boundary-layer marching solver against 12 published DNS datasets
($M_\infty$ 2–13.64, $T_w/T_r$ 0.18–1.9) and one incompressible dataset. The
mean velocity errors drop from 1.2% to 0.7% (inner coordinate) and from 2.5% to
1.3% (outer coordinate). The temperature errors drop from 1.4% to 0.4% and from
8.8% to 3.4%. For highly cooled walls, modifying the eddy viscosity alone is not
enough.

# Key claims

1. The BL-local inner eddy viscosity (mixing length with $y^*$ in the van Driest damping) is equivalent to the Trettel–Larsson (TL) transformation: in semilocal form the mixing-length relation reads $-\langle\rho\rangle^+\langle u''v''\rangle^+_\rho=\big|\partial U^+_{TL}/\partial y^*\big|^2\kappa^2y^{*2}\big[1-\exp(-y^*/A^+)\big]^2$ (§3.1, Eqs. 3.1–3.2). **Derived**
2. The semilocal eddy viscosity $\mu^*_{t,TL}=\mu_t/\langle\mu\rangle_\rho$ diagnosed from DNS collapses on $\kappa y^*[1-\exp(-y^*/17)]^2$ within and below the buffer layer but falls below it in the log region, most for diabatic cases (§3.1, Fig. 3a). **Shown**
3. With the Griffin–Fu–Moin (GFM) transformation, $\mu^*_{t,GFM}$ scatters less than $\mu^*_{t,TL}$ at $y^*\lesssim70$ and follows Eq. (3.4) more closely in the log region (§3.1, Fig. 3b). **Shown**
4. BL-local underestimates the outer eddy viscosity in the hypersonic cold-wall case M8Tw048R20: the matching point is $y^*_{m\mu}=67$ ($0.09\delta_{99}$), so $67<y^*\lesssim300$ is not governed by the log scaling; for the incompressible Schlatter–Örlü case at $Re_\theta=2540$, $y^+_{m\mu}=152$ ($0.18\delta_{99}$) (§2.3, Fig. 2a,c). **Shown**
5. The Cebeci–Smith outer scaling $\nu_{t,\max}=\alpha_2u_\tau\delta_{99}$ ($\alpha_2=0.06$–$0.075$) is less robust than $\nu_{t,\max}=\alphaU_e\delta^*_k$ once compressible cases are included; $\alpha_2$ diagnosed from DNS varies by more than a factor two among diabatic cases (§3.2, Eq. 3.8, Fig. 4a,b). **Shown**
6. For the incompressible high-$Re$ boundary layer (Monkewitz, Chauhan & Nagib 2007) the outer maximum of $y\,\partial U^+/\partial y$ is 5.55 at $\eta_o=0.155$, so $\nu_{t,\max}/(\alphay_{\max}F_{\max})=1/(0.155\times5.55)$, a constant that justifies the Baldwin–Lomax replacement $U_e\delta^*_k\to C_{cp}y_{\max}F_{\max}$, "namely $C_{cp}$, though not exactly the same value" (§3.2, Eq. 3.10). **Derived** The constant evaluates to $\approx1.16$, against $C_{cp}=1.6$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
7. $\alpha_{VD}=\nu_{t,\max}/(U_e\delta^*_{VD})$ diagnosed from DNS varies about as little as $\alpha$, with a decreasing trend in $Re_{\delta^*_{VD}}$; diabatic cases depart no more than adiabatic ones (§3.2, Fig. 4c). **Shown**
8. A mixed displacement thickness (GFM-based integrand below $0.15\delta_{99}$) differs from $\delta^*_{VD}$ by less than 2.5% (supersonic) and 4.5% (hypersonic) (§3.2). **Shown**
9. The van Driest diagnostic function $y\,\partial U^+_{VD}/\partial y=\sqrt{\langle\rho\rangle^+}\,y\,\partial U^+/\partial y$ collapses the outer peaks better than the untransformed one, whose peaks sit near $0.7\delta_{99}$ but vary widely in magnitude for cold walls (§3.2, Eq. 3.12, Fig. 5d,e). **Shown**
10. The modified outer eddy viscosity $\mu_{t,o}=\sqrt{\langle\rho\rangle}\,\alphaC_{cp}y_{VD,\max}F_{VD,\max}F_{\mathrm{Kleb}}$ gives $\mu_{t,o}\sim\langle\rho\rangle^{1/2}F_{\mathrm{Kleb}}$ and $\nu_{t,o}\sim\langle\rho\rangle^{-1/2}F_{\mathrm{Kleb}}$, a compromise between modelling $\nu_{t,\max}$ and $\mu_{t,\max}$, and reduces to the original model at constant density (§3.2, Eq. 3.14). **Derived**
11. $y_{VD,\max}/\delta_{99}$ lies within 0.6–0.8 for most cases, closer to the edge than $y_{\max}/\delta_{99}$ (§3.2, Fig. 5f). **Shown**
12. The DNS turbulent Prandtl number (Eq. 3.15) is 0.7–1.1 above the buffer layer (about 0.85 in the log region, from the literature), but near the temperature peak of cold-wall layers $\partial\langle T\rangle_\rho/\partial y$ and $\langle v''T''\rangle_\rho$ change sign, so $Pr_t$ becomes negative or singular; between the singular point and the wall $Pr_t<0.5$ for the three ZDC cases; a singularity also appears in the adiabatic M2p5Tw10R17 at $y^*\approx2$ (§3.3, Fig. 6a). **Shown**
13. With BL-local on M8Tw048R20, $\langle T\rangle_\rho$ at $y^*\gtrsim100$ is insensitive to $Pr_t$, while below it decreases with decreasing $Pr_t$; $Pr_t=0.8$ best captures the peak; M6Tw025R11 needs $Pr_t=0.6$ (not shown); the Subbareddy–Candler $Pr_t=1-0.25y/\delta_{99}$ behaves like $Pr_t=1$ (§3.3, Fig. 6b). **Shown**
14. Constant $Pr_t=0.9$ over-predicts the peak temperature of cold-wall cases (§3.3). **Shown**
15. Two-layer temperature: the energy equation with constant $Pr_t$ above $y_{mT}$, the quadratic temperature–velocity relation (Eq. 3.17) anchored at the matching values below; $y^*_{mT}=100$; results change little for $y^*_{mT}$ from 50 to 150, a higher value being closer to DNS near the peak and a lower one more robust at the first streamwise stations (§3.3, App. A, Fig. 15). **Shown**
16. For M8Tw048R20 the final model (BL-GFM-VD-TV) raises the matching point to $y^*_{m\mu}=242$ ($0.24\delta_{99}$) from 67, and its a posteriori $Pr_t$ below $y^*_{mT}$ varies like the DNS one, with a singular point at $y^*\approx8$ (§4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
17. For M6Tw025R11, $y^*_{m\mu}$ rises from 80 to 224; both models under-predict $u_\tau$; the final model deviates more in inner scaling but follows DNS more closely in outer scaling; it is the only case where the inner-coordinate velocity error is not improved (§4, Figs. 8, 13). **Shown**
18. For the adiabatic PB-M4Tw10R21 case the log scaling of Eq. (3.6) holds up to $y^*_{m\mu}=210$; the temperature–velocity relation helps little because the $Pr_t$ singularity, if any, lies in the viscous layer, where molecular conduction dominates (§4, Fig. 9). **Shown**
19. For heated and moderately cooled walls ($T_w/T_r>0.5$) BL-GFM-VD already improves the temperature; for highly cooled walls ($T_w/T_r\le0.5$) modifying only $\mu_t$ is insufficient and the temperature–velocity relation is required (§4, Fig. 11). **Shown**
20. BL-local errors: $\epsilon_{lg,U}<1.0\%$ and $\epsilon_{n,U}<1.6\%$ for adiabatic cases 1–4; $\epsilon_{n,U}>3\%$ for the four hypersonic cases; $\epsilon_{n,T}>10\%$ for the five hypersonic diabatic cases and $>20\%$ for heated case 12 (§4, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. Mean errors over all cases, BL-local → BL-GFM-VD-TV: $\epsilon_{lg,U}$ 1.2% → 0.7%, $\epsilon_{n,U}$ 2.5% → 1.3%, $\epsilon_{lg,T}$ 1.4% → 0.4%, $\epsilon_{n,T}$ 8.8% → 3.4% (§4, Fig. 13; abstract). **Shown**
22. $\epsilon_{lg,T}$ of BL-GFM-VD lies between BL-local and BL-GFM-VD-TV in every case: the temperature improvement comes jointly from the velocity transformations and the temperature–velocity relation (§4, Fig. 13b). **Shown**
23. The final model improves the shape factor $H$ and, in most ZDC cases, $u_\tau$ and $C_f$; not all wall quantities improve at the sampled stations, notably for M6Tw025R11 (§4, Table 2). **Shown**
24. The boundary-layer marching solver returns the mean flow at a target $x$ within minutes on a desktop computer; Newton convergence is first order for turbulent flows because $\mu_t$ and the eddy conductivity are non-smooth (max and intersection operations) (§2.2). **Stated**
25. The solver agrees with the full Navier–Stokes US3D results of Hendrickson et al. (2023) for M6Tw025R11 and with the authors' second-order shock-capturing RANS on a $300\times163$ mesh for M14Tw018R24 (App. A, Fig. 14). **Shown**
26. Transformation errors over all cases: GFM 2.2%, HLPP 2.4%; BL-HLPP-VD-TV gives $\epsilon_{lg,U}=0.6\%$, $\epsilon_{lg,T}=0.4\%$, $\epsilon_{n,U}=1.3\%$, $\epsilon_{n,T}=3.5\%$ (App. B, Fig. 16). **Shown**
27. The modifications are restricted to attached thin-layer flows ($|\omega_i|=|\partial U/\partial y|$), so the model is less general than the baseline; pressure gradients and separation are future work; the gain over BL-local is expected to be smaller in channels and pipes, where TL is accurate and $Pr_t$ has no near-wall singularity (§5.1). **Stated**

The baseline two-layer eddy viscosity (Eqs. 2.6–2.9), with
$U=\langle u\rangle_\rho$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mu_{t,i}&=\langle\rho\rangle\,l_{\mathrm{mix}}^2\lvert\omega_i\rvert,\qquad l_{\mathrm{mix}}=\kappa y\Big[1-\exp\Big(-\frac{y^*}{A^+}\Big)\Big],\qquad A^+=\frac{26}{\sqrt{\lvert\tau^+\rvert}},\\
\mu_{t,o}&=\langle\rho\rangle\,\alphaC_{cp}\,y_{\max}F_{\max}F_{\mathrm{Kleb}},\qquad F_{\max}=\frac{1}{\kappa}\max_y\big(l_{\mathrm{mix}}\lvert\omega_i\rvert\big),\qquad F_{\mathrm{Kleb}}=\Big[1+5.5\Big(C_{\mathrm{Kleb}}\frac{y}{y_{\max}}\Big)^6\Big]^{-1},
\end{aligned}$$
$\mu_t=\mu_{t,i}$ below the intersection height $y_{m\mu}$ and $\mu_{t,o}$ above,
with $\kappa=0.40$, $\alpha=0.0168$, $C_{cp}=1.6$, $C_{\mathrm{Kleb}}=0.3$ and
$\kappa_{\mathrm{th},t}=c_p\mu_t/Pr_t$, $Pr_t=0.9$.

# Printed slips

- **Eqs. 3.13–3.14 (p. A7-16):** "the denominator $\sqrt{\rho_w}$ has been moved out of this function to combine with other factors in $\mu_{t,o}$", but Eq. 3.14 divides by the local $\sqrt{\bar\rho}$, which the next sentence calls "a compensation to the amplified vorticity function"; the step from $\sqrt{\rho_w}$ to $\sqrt{\bar\rho}$ is not derived.
- **p. A7-10:** "the region $67<y^*\lesssim300$ ($0.09<y_{m\mu}/\delta_{99}\lesssim0.27$)", where $y/\delta_{99}$ is meant.
- **Fig. 5(d,f):** axis labels "$y/\partial_{99}$" for $y/\delta_{99}$.
- **Case name:** "Ma8Tw048R20" (p. A7-19) and "ZDC-Ma8Tw048R20" (Fig. 7 caption) against "M8Tw048R20" elsewhere.
- **p. A7-6:** "$Re_\tau=\delta_{99}/\delta_v$" with $\delta_v$ for the defined $\delta_\nu$.
