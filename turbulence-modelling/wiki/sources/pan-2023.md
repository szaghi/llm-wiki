# Summary

Pan & Zhang (2023) compare the original
[detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) (DES,
1997) and its delayed form (DDES, 2006), both built on the
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md), on the
incompressible flow in a six-blade, backward-swept, low-specific-speed
centrifugal impeller (Pedersen et al. 2003 configuration) at 25% of the
design flow rate, where alternate rotating stall appears. The reference is
an [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) with the
[dynamic](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md)
[Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md)–Lilly model. All three run in
ANSYS-Fluent with default parameters, on the same 12.33-million-cell
structured grid (maximum $y^+\approx1.2$), with a multiple-reference-frame
treatment of rotation and one degree of rotation per time step.

The LES is validated loosely: its head is within 3.75% of the measurement,
and its velocity profiles against PIV show a "remarkable discrepancy" that
the authors assign to the different experimental rig. The paper also calls
the LES "the implicit filtered LES approach of ANSYS-Fluent", citing a work
on [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md), next to the explicit dynamic
model. DES and DDES are compared with this LES on the mid-span plane.

Both capture alternate stall. DDES streamlines are closer to the LES vortex
pattern, while the DES $Q$ field agrees better with LES; DES reproduces the
leading-edge low-pressure region better; mean radial velocities of both
differ by about 10% from LES in peak-to-valley amplitude; DES is closer to
LES for the r.m.s. fluctuations, which DDES underpredicts. The authors
conclude that neither approach is better for every quantity. The abstract
states the opposite ranking for the fluctuations (see Printed slips). The
paper develops no model; it is an engineering assessment of
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) defaults. Its
introduction cites the modulated [gradient model](/methods/gradient-model.md)
and the [anisotropic minimum-dissipation model](/methods/anisotropic-minimum-dissipation-model.md)
as "novel sub-grid-scale stress models based on pressure gradient".

# Key claims

1. At 25% of the design flow rate the impeller shows alternate stall: strong separated vortices fill alternate passages (stalled) while the others are not stalled; LES, DES and DDES all predict it (§IV.A, Fig. 9). **Shown**
2. LES grid study: impeller head 2.64, 2.60, 2.55, 2.51, 2.49, 2.48 m for 6.49 to 13.49 million cells, against 2.40 m measured; the 12.33-million grid is chosen, "about 3.75%" off the experiment (§III.A, Table II). **Shown**
3. The maximum $y^+$ of the LES is "around 1.2", at the leading and trailing edges; DES and DDES are "about the same level" (§III.B, Fig. 5). **Shown**
4. The inlet static pressure becomes statistically steady from about the fifth revolution; averaging from the eleventh is "reasonable" (§III.B, Fig. 4). **Shown**
5. The modelled fraction $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}/(k_{\mathrm{res}}+k_{\mathrm{sgs}})$ of the LES peaks in the separated shear layer at the blade leading edges and is "rather small" in the through-flow parts of the passages (§III.B, Eq. 23, Fig. 6; $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ is not defined in the paper). **Shown**
6. Pan & Zhang write, citing Pope (2000), that "the modeled turbulent kinetic energy could reach as high as 80% of the overall fluctuating kinetic energy" (§III.B, ref. 38). **Stated** (as Pan & Zhang print it; [Jakhar et al. (2026)](/sources/jakhar-2026.md), p. 4, recall from Pope the rule that LES should resolve at least 80% of the kinetic energy; both attributions are set side by side on [large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md))
7. LES velocity profiles at $\bar r=0.20$ and $0.90$ follow the distribution of the PIV of Pedersen et al. but show "remarkable discrepancy", which the authors attribute to the experimental rig (impeller in a cylindrical tank, pre-swirl) (§III.C, Fig. 8). **Shown**
8. Instantaneous streamlines: LES and DDES vortices are "almost identical in size and location at all moments"; the DES vortex of the stalled passage differs in duration, size and location, and DES hardly captures the small pressure-side vortex of the non-stalled passage (§IV.A, Fig. 9). **Shown**
9. Instantaneous normalized $Q$: DES is "in very good agreement" with LES; DDES "generally overpredicts the magnitude" and "lacks in the resolution of small-scale structures" (§IV.A, Fig. 10). **Shown**
10. Mean static pressure: the leading-edge low-pressure region on the suction side is comparably large for LES and DES and notably smaller for DDES (§IV.B, Figs. 4, 11). **Shown**
11. Mean radial velocity at the passage inlet: DES "is prone to overpredict the reversed flow" near the suction surface, DDES "normally underpredicts"; their peak-to-valley differences are "about 10% different from the LES data" (§IV.B, Fig. 12). **Shown**
12. At mid-passage ($\bar r=0.64$) of stalled passages the LES radial-velocity profile shows a transitional deflection that DES reproduces with different location and amplitude and DDES hardly captures; in non-stalled passages both overpredict the peak and shift it by up to 15° toward the pressure surface (§IV.B, Fig. 12). **Shown**
13. Near the outlet, reversed flow forms only on the pressure surface of stalled passages; there DES and DDES broadly agree; in non-stalled passages the three approaches are "drastically inconsistent", DDES closest to LES (§IV.B, Fig. 12). **Shown**
14. Circumferential velocity: DES and DDES follow the LES trends, with a larger peak-to-valley difference near the outlet; the axial velocity is small and "the variation trend could not even be reasonably compared" (§IV.B, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
15. R.m.s. radial velocity (one revolution of sampling): in stalled passages the LES fluctuation is about 7% of the blade tip speed at the inlet and 10% at the outlet; DES is generally close to LES, DDES underpredicts, especially near inlet and outlet (§IV.C, Eq. 26, Fig. 15). **Shown**
16. The passage-to-passage differences of the r.m.s. profiles are attributed to limited sampling and small magnitudes (§IV.C). **Stated**
17. Of the time-averaged velocity products ("momentum transport terms", means of full products, not Reynolds stresses), the radial–circumferential one dominates and resembles the mean radial velocity; the two products with the axial component are small (§IV.D, Figs. 16–18). **Shown**
18. For the radial–circumferential product in stalled passages DES often overpredicts and DDES underpredicts; "The DES outperforms DDES in the upstream part of the blade passages, while DDES is better in the middle and downstream part" (§IV.D, Fig. 17). **Shown**
19. The vortex patterns of DDES are closer to LES than those of DES (§V). **Shown** (via Fig. 9)
20. Fluctuations at passage inlet and outlet are "more accurately predicted by DES than DDES, which underpredicts"; "DDES is obviously incapable in predicting the strong separated flow in the central passage especially close to the suction surface" (§V). **Shown**
21. "It is infeasible to arrive at a universal conclusion that one approach outperforms the other for all quantities"; the performance depends on the local separation pattern (§V). **Stated**
22. Abstract: "DES performs better in the pressure distribution, while DDES is superior in resolving the transient evolution of velocity field, especially the fluctuation quantities and turbulent statistical quantities." **Stated** (contradicted by claims 15 and 20 of the same paper)
23. Cited findings: dynamic Smagorinsky beats constant-coefficient Smagorinsky for rotating stall in a centrifugal pump (Zhou et al. 2017); DDES is inferior even to RANS for weakly separated near-design compressor-rotor flow (Kim et al. 2015); SA-based DES beats SST-based DES for massively separated double-suction pump flow (Zhou et al. 2012) (§I, refs. 4, 19, 24). **Stated**

The DES and DDES length scales as printed (Eqs. 7–11), in registry form
with $l_{\mathrm{RANS}}=d$ and $\Delta_{\max}=\max(\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z)$:

$$l_{\mathrm{DES}}=\min(d,\,C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta_{\max}),\qquad l_{\mathrm{DDES}}=d-f_d\max(0,\,d-C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta_{\max}),\qquad f_d=1-\tanh\big[(8r_d)^3\big],\qquad r_d=\frac{\tilde\nu}{\sqrt{\partial_ju_i\,\partial_ju_i}\;\kappa^2d^2},$$

with $C_{\mathrm{DES}}=0.65$ and the SA constants printed as $c_{b1}=0.1355$,
$\sigma=0.6667$, $c_{b2}=0.622$, $\kappa=0.41$, $c_{w1}=2.5093$,
$c_{w2}=0.3$, $c_{w3}=2.0$, $c_{v1}=7.1$ ("default values"). The $r_d$ of
Eq. (11) carries $\tilde\nu$ alone in its numerator.

# Printed slips

- Abstract against §IV.C and §V: the abstract says DDES "is superior in resolving … the fluctuation quantities"; §IV.C says "DES gives prediction generally close to that of LES … while DDES normally gives underprediction", and §V says the fluctuation "is more accurately predicted by DES than DDES".
- §IV.D against §V: §IV.D gives the middle part of the (stalled) passages to DDES ("DDES is better in the middle and downstream part"); §V says "In the non-stalled passages, DES performed slightly better in the upstream and middle parts".
- §V calls DES and DDES "well validated against available experimental data"; only the LES is compared with experiment (§III.C, Fig. 8).
- Eq. (4) prints $f_{v1}=\chi^3/(\chi^3+c_{v1}^6)$.
- $\tilde S$ is called "the magnitude of modified vorticity", but Eqs. (4)–(6) build it on the strain-rate magnitude $(2\bar S_{ij}\bar S_{ij})^{1/2}$.
- §II: "42 cells are placed in the spanwise direction with 10 layers of cells in the boundary layer region and 12 layers of cells of fixed size in the central region"; the stated layers do not add up to 42 ($2\times10+12=32$ with a boundary layer on each wall, $10+12=22$ with one) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- "Pedersen" (p. 3, ref. 2) against "Pederson" (pp. 5–6, ref. 32); ref. 32 title "article image velocimetry"; "gird scale" (p. 4); "greatly than 0.47" (p. 5).
- $T$ is both the blade thickness (Table I) and the revolution period (Fig. 9).
