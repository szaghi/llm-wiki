# Summary

Mani & Dorgan (2023), an MIT academic and a Boeing engineer, review how
CFD is used in aerospace design, what limits it and where investment is
needed. The review prints no equations; its quantitative statements are
reproduced figures of earlier work or statements of industrial practice,
mostly at Boeing. It traces the history from panel and potential methods
to the structured multi-block RANS codes (CFL3D, OVERFLOW, Wind-US) and
Boeing's unstructured BCFD (finite volume) and GGNS (finite element), which
share the EPIC anisotropic adaptation tool; describes the industrial
process from CAD clean-up to post-processing; and defines verification and
validation, with NASA's Turbulence Modeling Resource as a non-intrusive
verification tool on the [flat-plate boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).

The technical sections cover output-based [mesh adaptation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md)
and its open problems for unsteady and chaotic flows; the dominance of
second-order finite volumes, the absence of a run-time benefit from
third-order finite volumes, and the promise of higher-order finite
elements (a continuous Galerkin [variational multiscale](/methods/variational-multiscale-method.md)
discretization among them); and reduced-dissipation, [kinetic-energy-](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
and [entropy-preserving](/properties/entropy-conservation.md) fluxes for
scale-resolving simulation ([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)).
In turbulence modelling, [Spalart–Allmaras](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md)
with its RC and QCR variants, [SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) and
Smith's $k$-$l$ model are the industrial workhorses and fail for large
separation; the macroscopic forcing method is named as a closure not based
on the [Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md).
[DDES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) is used "in almost all
circumstances" for unsteady simulation at Boeing, but modelled-stress
depletion persists, and a serpentine-inlet study shows that raising the
shielding constant restores the boundary layer at the price of damping the
resolved eddies. [Wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
(with [CharLES](/software/charles.md) and Alya) is presented as the
promising route to stall prediction on the [NASA CRM high-lift](/benchmarks/nasa-crm-high-lift.md)
and [JAXA Standard Model](/benchmarks/jaxa-standard-model.md)
configurations, still lacking mesh-independent outputs within wind-tunnel
uncertainty and an automatic choice of the exchange location. The
closing section lists the challenges of certification by analysis.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $r_d$ the argument and $f_d$ the shielding function of
DDES (registry §11); $h_N=N_{\mathrm{pts}}^{-1/2}$ the grid measure of the convergence
plots ($N_{\mathrm{pts}}$ the number of grid points; the source's $h$ and
$N$); QoI a quantity of interest; MSD modelled-stress depletion; MFM the
macroscopic forcing method; CbA certification by analysis.

1. Industry calls RANS codes "Navier–Stokes codes" loosely, and the review keeps this usage (§1). **Stated**
2. The X3D RANS solver (McDonnell Douglas, early 1980s) used the Cebeci–Smith closure, ran an 80 000-node grid in a day on a VAX-11/780, and served the F/A-18A and AV-8B inlets (§1). **Stated**
3. The structured multi-block solvers of the late 1980s were limited by the lack of robust turbulence models and of residual convergence, by grid-quality demands and by long solution times (§1). **Stated**
4. GGNS is a second-order finite-element RANS solver focused on iterative convergence and shares the EPIC adaptation with the finite-volume BCFD (§1). **Stated**
5. A RANS solution on a full aircraft at low incidence takes typically eight hours; GPUs could reduce this by an order of magnitude, as FUN3D and charLES show (§3). **Stated**
6. Finite-volume codes rarely converge the residual to machine zero on industrial problems, so convergence is judged from output histories; finite-element codes with strong linear solvers aim at residual convergence on every problem (§3). **Stated**
7. Verification ("model implementation accurately represents the developer's conceptual description") and validation ("degree to which a model is an accurate representation of the real world from the perspective of the intended uses") follow Lee et al. (2016) (§4.1). **Stated**
8. The NASA Turbulence Modeling Resource enables verification without code sharing: CFL3D and FUN3D converge to the same $C_f$ at $x=0.97$ for SA and SA-QCR2000 on the zero-pressure-gradient flat plate at $M=0.2$, $Re_L=5\times10^6$, on grids from $35\times25$ to $545\times385$ (§4.1.1, Fig. 5, from Rumsey 2021). **Shown** (reproduced)
9. Validating a new code takes years and several engineers, which keeps industry from replacing codes; Alya WMLES predicts pre- and post-stall lift on the HiLiftPW-3 configuration but would still need this effort (§4.1.2, Fig. 6, from Lehmkuhl et al. 2019). **Shown** (reproduced) / **Stated**
10. Shrink-wrap meshing has been "generally insufficient for design needs in the aerospace industry"; adapted grids must be projected onto the CAD geometry, not onto the initial surface mesh (§4.2). **Stated**
11. Feature-based (Hessian) adaptation refines features that do not affect the output; output-based (adjoint) adaptation resolves only those that do (Fig. 7: RAE airfoil, $M=0.725$, $\alpha=2.466^\circ$; 22 150 nodes lift-adapted, 18 698 drag-adapted, 37 681 Hessian-adapted; from Venditti & Darmofal 2003) (§4.3). **Shown** (reproduced)
12. Michal et al. (2021): four solvers, four mesh-mechanics methods and four error estimates on the 2-D high-lift CRM section give nearly identical results once mesh-converged, with different trajectories, output-error methods reaching a prescribed error on coarser meshes (§4.3, Fig. 8, $\alpha=8^\circ$). **Shown** (reproduced)
13. Unsteady output-based adaptation meets memory limits: 50 million points × 5 variables = 250 million values, 2 GB per time step in double precision, stored for the backward adjoint sweep (§4.3). **Derived** *(compiler check, not in the sources: $2.5\times10^8\times8$ B $=2.0\times10^9$ B)*
14. For chaotic turbulent flows adjoint sensitivities "blow up as the backward sweep approaches the initial conditions"; general techniques are needed before unsteady output-based adaptation is useful (§4.3). **Stated**
15. Second-order finite volumes with implicit time stepping dominate industry; codes claiming higher order usually raise only the face-interpolation order, while the face-flux quadrature must also improve (§4.4). **Stated**
16. Third-order finite volumes have shown no net run-time benefit (unpublished industrial studies; Darmofal & Haimes 2005), while the First Workshop on Higher-Order Methods reported an order-of-magnitude efficiency gain of higher-order finite elements over second-order finite volumes at equal error (§4.4, citing [wang-2013](/sources/wang-2013.md)). **Stated**
17. Ursachi et al. (2020): adaptive continuous Galerkin VMS with discontinuous subscales at third and fourth order needs an order of magnitude less time than second-order methods for equal accuracy (§4.4, Fig. 4). **Stated** / **Shown** (reproduced)
18. Finite-volume codes linearize the second-order residual to first order in the implicit update, which generally prevents full residual convergence; GGNS uses an exact Jacobian with ILU-preconditioned GMRES and converges Newton-like to machine level (§4.4). **Stated**
19. Scale-resolving simulation benefits from reduced inviscid-flux dissipation; Goc et al. (2021) used charLES with a kinetic-energy- and entropy-preserving scheme and predicted the maximum lift of the High-Lift Prediction Workshop configuration (§4.4). **Stated**
20. At Boeing the RANS workhorses are SA, SA-RC, SA-QCR and SST; Lockheed Martin's Falcon code uses Smith's $k$-$l$ model; all work for attached and mildly separated flow but can fail for large separation at high incidence, and the many SA modifications "rarely have ... provided general improvements" (§4.5.1). **Stated**
21. The macroscopic forcing method (Mani & Park 2021) determines the closure operators of mean-field mixing, is not based on Boussinesq, isotropy or scale separation, has no tuning constants, unifies laminar and turbulent macroscopic modelling, and is not yet ready for industry (§4.5.1). **Stated**
22. Modelled-stress depletion arises when the RANS-to-LES switch occurs near the wall on a grid too coarse for the near-wall eddies, giving too little stress and grid-induced premature separation; the DDES shielding function strongly reduced it (§4.5.2). **Stated**
23. Serpentine inlet (Lakebrink & Mani 2018): with DDES the eddy viscosity decays substantially inside the attached boundary layer upstream of separation compared with RANS on the same grid; raising the shielding constant from 8 to 16 (from $(8r_d)^3$ to $(16r_d)^3$) restored the boundary layer but damped all detached eddies and substantially delayed separation (§4.5.2, Fig. 11). **Shown** (reproduced)
24. On nearly isotropic surface meshes DDES selects the LES branch in the boundary layer and the solution deteriorates; the same holds for IDDES (§4.5.2, citing Mani & Winkler 2016, Ashton 2017, Shur et al. 2008, Ashton et al. 2016). **Stated**
25. Menter (2016) built a robust shielding function for two-equation hybrid models whose blending details are proprietary to Ansys; a robust public-domain shielding function is needed (§4.5.2). **Stated**
26. Hybrid RANS/LES is too expensive for database generation and has struggled with smooth-body separation and reattachment (§4.5.2). **Stated**
27. HiLiftPW-3 RANS results indicate RANS "may not be adequate" near and after stall, some models and codes missing stall dramatically; WMLES (Goc et al. 2020–2022) predicted the abrupt lift break on the JSM but overpredicted lift at lower incidence (§4.5.2, Fig. 12). **Shown** (reproduced)
28. For industrial use WMLES must demonstrate mesh-independent QoI within wind-tunnel uncertainty and automate (or remove) the choice of the exchange location $h_{\mathrm{wm}}$ (§4.5.2). **Stated**
29. Certification by analysis by 2040 requires validation on real configurations, multidisciplinary validation data, standard uncertainty quantification and robust adaptation (§4.3, §5). **Stated**
30. A code's long-term survival will depend on its use of accelerator (GPU) hardware (§5). **Stated**
