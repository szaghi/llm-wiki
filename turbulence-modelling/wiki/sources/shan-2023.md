# Summary

Shan et al. (2023) address the overprediction of lift by RANS closures for
separated flow over wind-turbine airfoils near and beyond stall, where no
DNS or LES data exist. In a first stage, data assimilation by parameter
calibration, the nine coefficients of the
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) are optimized
per flow case against measured surface $C_p$ with teaching–learning-based
optimization (population 20, excursion bounded at $\pm50\%$). The
calibrated "DA model" reproduces pressure and lift at its own condition
(lift error from 16–28% down to 0.2–7.2%) but does not generalize along a
lift curve. In a second stage a fully connected network maps six invariant
mean-flow features to the SA working variable $\tilde\nu$ (obtained by
inverting $\nu_t=f_{v1}\tilde\nu$) and, trained on a mixture of baseline
SA fields (attached flow) and DA fields (separated flow), replaces the SA
equation in iterative coupling with the solver. The authors frame the
approach against [field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md)
(learning a spatial correction factor) and list
[ensemble Kalman](/methods/ensemble-kalman-closure-calibration.md) and
adjoint inversion as the data-assimilation route not taken; they cite
[tensor-basis networks](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) and
[gene-expression programming](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md) as
nonlinear alternatives. The network is trained on fields and selected by
a priori scores, then judged a posteriori in the solver
([a priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)).

On S809 (trained at four incidences, $Re_c=2\times10^6$) the network keeps
SA accuracy in attached flow and DA accuracy in separated flow, separates
earlier and with a larger separated region than SA; trained on S809 and
S805 it improves the lift curves at $Re_c=0.5$–$3\times10^6$ and on the
unseen S814. The mean relative lift error beyond stall falls by over 60%
(text; over 57% in the abstract). The Discussion concedes that only the
coefficient layer of the four-layer uncertainty taxonomy of
[model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md) is
addressed; the linear [Boussinesq closure](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
and the SA functional forms remain. Evidence is numerical comparison with
the Somers fixed-transition experiments; the paper does not state a
transition treatment for the RANS runs.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_6$ the network inputs (the
source's $q_1,\dots,q_6$); $\|\Omega\|_F$, $\|S\|_F$ the Frobenius norms of
the rotation and strain tensors (the source's $\|R\|$, $\|S\|$, equal to
$|\Omega|/\sqrt2$, $|S|/\sqrt2$); $l_{\mathrm{mix}}$, $u_{\mathrm{mix}}$ the
mixing length and mixing velocity; $\eta_{\mathrm{DA}}$ the ratio of DA to
SA training samples; $\lambda_{\mathrm{LR}}$ the LeakyReLU slope;
$\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$ the loss weights; $e_R$ the mean relative error of
a lift curve (Eq. 14); DA1–DA4 the four calibrated SA coefficient sets.

1. SA with standard coefficients overpredicts lift above stall: $C_L=1.278$ against 0.996 measured for S809 at $Re_c=2\times10^6$, $\alpha=12.2^\circ$ (28.3%) (Table 3). **Shown**
2. Calibrating the nine coefficients $\boldsymbol\theta=(c_{t3},c_{t4},c_{b1},c_{b2},\sigma,c_{v1},\kappa,c_{w2},c_{w3})$ against measured $C_p$ (objective Eq. 3, the mean square $C_p$ error) reduces the lift error at the calibration condition to 6.3%, 0.7%, 7.2%, 0.2% for DA1–DA4, against 28.3%, 19.1%, 16.2%, 24.8% for SA (Table 3, §III.B). **Shown**
3. For DA2 the $C_p$ error of the 10th generation is 90% below that of the initial SA model, and the optimization converges at the 15th generation (Fig. 7, §III.B). **Shown**
4. The DA model enlarges the eddy viscosity in the wake and the separation vortex relative to SA, lowering lift (Figs. 9–10, §III.B). **Shown**
5. A DA model calibrated at one condition does not generalize: over the S809 lift curve, $e_R=0.1225$ (DA1) against 0.1200 (SA) at $Re_c=10^6$, and 0.0835 against 0.1341 at $Re_c=2\times10^6$, with attached-flow accuracy lost (Fig. 11, §III.C). **Shown**
6. The calibrated sets differ strongly across cases, e.g. $c_{v1}=3.550$–7.295 and $\kappa=0.396$–0.615 (Table B1). **Shown** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: several values sit on the stated $\pm50\%$ bound, e.g. $c_{b2}=0.311=0.5\times0.622$ in DA1, DA3, DA4, $c_{v1}=3.550=0.5\times7.1$ in DA1, DA4, $\kappa=0.615=1.5\times0.41$ in DA4; the paper does not comment.)*
7. A network mapping invariant mean-flow features to the eddy viscosity can replace the SA model entirely during the simulation, "different from many existing studies" that keep a traditional model and learn a correction factor (Abstract, §I, §V). **Stated** (design) / **Shown** (operation)
8. Iterative coupling (the network evaluated at every solver iteration, acting as an algebraic eddy-viscosity model) converges better than frozen coupling for separated flow at high Reynolds number (§II.A, citing Liu et al.). **Stated**
9. Near the wall $\tilde\nu\sim\nu_t^{1/4}$, so predicting $\tilde\nu$ instead of $\nu_t$ compresses the range the network must fit; $\tilde\nu$ is the unique positive real root of a quartic (Eq. 10, §II.C.2). **Derived**
10. Bayesian hyperparameter optimization selects 8 hidden layers of widths 265, 362 (six times), 469, learning rate 0.00013 and $\lambda_2=0$ (iteration 84; Fig. 12, §III.D). **Shown**
11. The sample ratio controls accuracy: the lift-curve $e_R$ is minimal at $\eta_{\mathrm{DA}}=1.62$; as $\eta_{\mathrm{DA}}\to0$ the error approaches that of SA (0.1341), for $\eta_{\mathrm{DA}}>2.74$ that of DA1 (0.0835) (Fig. 13, §III.E). **Shown**
12. Case 1 (S809, $Re_c=2\times10^6$): network lift errors 2.9%, 1.2%, 5.5%, 7.4%, 6.3%, 6.3% at $\alpha=8.2$, 10.2, 12.2, 13.2, 14.2, 15.2$^\circ$, against 4.3%, 18.2%, 28.4%, 23.4%, 19.1%, 16.6% for SA (Table 5). **Shown**
13. The network reproduces SA pressure in attached flow (training $\alpha=8.2^\circ$) and DA pressure in separated flow (12.2$^\circ$, 14.2$^\circ$), and improves pressure at incidences outside the training set (Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
14. Relative to SA the network separates earlier with a larger separated region and larger near-wall eddy-viscosity maxima; at $\alpha=13.2^\circ$ the flow separates at $x/c=0.5$ (Figs. 18–20). **Shown**
15. The network's skin friction agrees with DA on the upper surface but tends to SA between $x/c=0.2$ and 0.5 on the lower surface (Fig. 18b). **Shown**
16. Case 2: trained on S809 ($Re_c=2\times10^6$) and S805 ($Re_c=10^6$), four incidences each, the network improves lift for S805 and S809 at $Re_c=0.5$–$3\times10^6$ and for the unseen, thicker S814 (Figs. 21–23). **Shown**
17. For S814 at higher $Re_c$ the stall point is predicted but not the steep post-stall lift drop; the error stays below that of SA (§III.F). **Shown**
18. The mean relative lift error in the stall zone ($\alpha>8^\circ$) falls by over 60% for all three airfoils (§III.F, Fig. 24); the abstract says "over 57%". **Shown**
19. Better lift gives a more accurate stall point in the drag polar (Fig. 25) and earlier upper-surface separation on S805 (Fig. 29). **Shown**
20. Only coefficient uncertainty within SA is reduced; the uncertainties of ensemble averaging, of the Reynolds-stress representation (linear eddy viscosity) and of the functional forms remain (four-layer taxonomy of Duraisamy et al.; §IV). **Stated**
21. For one flow type (airfoils), generalization is achieved with a small amount of data (§V). **Shown** (within the tested range)

The network (Eqs. 4–12): fully connected, LeakyReLU hidden activations
with slope $\lambda_{\mathrm{LR}}$, ReLU output "for enforcing positivity of
eddy viscosity"; inputs $\zeta_1=u_{\mathrm{mix}}=l_{\mathrm{mix}}\|\Omega\|_F$,
$\zeta_2=\|\Omega\|_F$, $\zeta_3=\|S\|_F$,
$\zeta_4=d^2\|\Omega\|_F(1-\tanh d)$, $\zeta_5=d^2\|S\|_F(1-\tanh d)$,
$\zeta_6=|\nabla\langle p\rangle|$; $l_{\mathrm{mix}}$ (Eq. 8) uses
$\kappa=0.41$, $A^+=26$ and a flat-plate thickness correlation
$\delta=0.37x/Re_x^{1/5}$ along the chord (Eq. 9); min–max scaling
(Eq. 11); loss $\mathcal L=\lambda_1N^{-1}\sum(\hat y_i-y_i)^2+\lambda_2\|\boldsymbol W\|$
with $\lambda_1=1$ (Eq. 12); AdamW, batch 64, PyTorch. Training samples
are selected by physics-assisted recursive selection (5–30% of each field).
The solver is an in-house second-order cell-centred finite-volume RANS
code (Roe flux, implicit Gauss–Seidel, convergence accelerated by online
dimension reduction); the S809 grid has 34 205 elements, 400 surface
points and $y^+\approx1$.
