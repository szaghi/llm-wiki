# Summary

Xu et al. (2026) address the spurious pressure oscillations that
fully conservative schemes produce in transcritical (real-fluid) flows,
where density and heat capacity vary sharply across the pseudo-boiling
line. Evolving primitive variables, with a pressure equation, avoids the
oscillations but loses total-energy conservation and mispredicts shock
speed and strength. The paper keeps the primitive update where the flow is
smooth and returns to the conservative form at shocks (the trade-off of
[pressure-equilibrium preservation](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md)).

The contribution is the Central Differential flux with High-Order
Dissipation (CDHD) for the quasi-linear primitive Euler system in
$\boldsymbol V=(\rho,u,p)$. Its central part multiplies a two-point
difference of WENO-5 interface states by the primitive-system matrix at the
cell average. Its dissipation is built from Dumbser–Osher–Toro path-integral
fluctuations evaluated on the high-order interface jump. A PVRS
pressure-ratio sensor flags shock cells, which are updated with a
conservative finite-volume WENO-5 and a real-gas Roe flux (the hybrid of
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) shock capturing). The Peng–Robinson
equation of state closes the system for nitrogen. A formal Taylor analysis
gives a second-order central term and a dissipation of order $h^4$, so the
scheme is second order in smooth regions. The added
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) is shown to be
needed: without it the central primitive update blows up.

The evidence is inviscid and one- or two-dimensional: periodic advection
of a cold transcritical nitrogen slab, a grid-convergence study, an
ablation, a transcritical shock tube and a 2-D droplet advection. The
paper has no turbulence, viscous, LES or DNS content. It cites the kinetic-energy- and
pressure-equilibrium-preserving conservative scheme of Bernades et al.
(2023) as one that is "not easily extended to general transcritical flows"
(see [kinetic-energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
and [entropy conservation](/properties/entropy-conservation.md)), without
testing it.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol V=(\rho,u,p)^T$ the primitive and
$\boldsymbol W=(\rho,\rho u,E)^T$ the conservative variables; $B(\boldsymbol V)$
the quasi-linear primitive-system matrix, $\partial_t\boldsymbol V+B\,\partial_x\boldsymbol V=0$;
$c$ the speed of sound; $\boldsymbol V_L$, $\boldsymbol V_R$ the WENO-5
interface states and $d\boldsymbol V=\boldsymbol V_R-\boldsymbol V_L$;
$\boldsymbol H^\pm$ the path-integral fluctuations; $\mathcal L_v$ the
primitive spatial operator; $e_E=\lvert(E(t)-E(0))/E(0)\rvert$ the relative
error of the integrated total energy; $h$ the uniform cell size.

1. Conventional conservative schemes applied to transcritical flows "often generate severe unphysical pressure oscillations" that can destabilize the simulation; similar oscillations occur in ideal gases at material interfaces and in multicomponent flows (§1). **Stated**
2. Non-conservative formulations (double flux, pressure evolution) "come at the cost of energy conservation error, which can lead to an incorrect prediction of shock wave locations and strengths" (§1). **Stated**
3. The low-Mach approximation "can introduce non-negligible errors when the flow Mach number exceeds 0.1" (§1). **Stated**
4. CDHD splits the primitive spatial operator as $\mathcal L_v=\mathcal L_v^{\mathrm{central}}+\mathcal L_v^{\mathrm{diss}}$, the central part providing accuracy and the dissipative part stability (§3.5, Eq. 20). **Derived** (definition of the method)
5. The central term, a two-point difference of WENO-5 interface states, has truncation error $O(h^2)$ "even though the individual interface states are fifth-order accurate"; a higher-order central derivative can be substituted without changing the rest of the formulation (§3.5, §4.2, Eqs. 28–34). **Derived**
6. Two independent $O(h^2)$ mechanisms limit the central term: the two-midpoint difference, with error $\frac{h^2}{24}\partial_x^3\boldsymbol V$ (Eq. 29), and the evaluation of $B$ at the cell average instead of the point value (Eq. 32); reconstruction errors contribute only $O(h^4)$ (Eq. 31) (§4.2). **Derived**
7. In smooth regions the WENO-5 interface jump is $d\boldsymbol V=O(h^5)$ (Eqs. 26–27, 40). **Derived** (assumes fifth-order WENO-5 interface values)
8. The fluctuations satisfy $\boldsymbol H^\pm=B^\pm(\boldsymbol V_L)\,d\boldsymbol V+\frac12DB^\pm(\boldsymbol V_L)[d\boldsymbol V]\,d\boldsymbol V+O(\lVert d\boldsymbol V\rVert^3)$, so $\lVert\boldsymbol H^\pm\rVert\le C_1\lVert d\boldsymbol V\rVert+C_2\lVert d\boldsymbol V\rVert^2$ (Eqs. 37–39), assuming $B\in C^2$ and a smooth eigenstructure with no eigenvalue sign change along the path (§4.3). **Derived**
9. Hence $\boldsymbol H^\pm=O(h^5)$ and $\mathcal L_v^{\mathrm{diss}}=O(h^4)$: the dissipation "is high-order small and does not limit accuracy", and $\mathcal L_v$ is $O(h^2)$ in smooth regions (Eq. 41, §4.3). **Derived**
10. With piecewise-constant states ($d\boldsymbol V=O(1)$) the same path integral is a first-order Riemann solver; in CDHD it is "a targeted stabilizer" that "behaves similarly to a local Lax-Friedrichs scheme" scaled by the high-order jump (§4.4). **Stated**
11. The dissipation "acts as a gentle upwind stabilization proportional to the spectral radius $\lvert u\rvert+a$" ($a$ the speed of sound) (§3.5). **Stated**
12. WENO-5 is applied directly to the primitive variables "to avoid characteristic projections through potentially ill-conditioned real-gas eigenstructures near the critical point" (§3). **Stated**
13. 1-D transcritical advection (512 points, $t=0.1$ s, ten traversals): CDHD agrees with the analytical solution in $\rho$, $u$, $p$ and $T$, while the first-order Dumbser–Osher–Toro scheme keeps $u$ and $p$ uniform but smears $\rho$ and $T$ (§5.1.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
14. The relative total-energy error of CDHD reaches "approximately $3.95\times10^{-4}$ at $t=0.1$ s"; that of the first-order scheme grows "to about 10 %", i.e. "over two orders of magnitude" larger (§5.1.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
15. Abstract: "Compared to traditional primitive-variable approaches, CDHD reduces the energy conservation error in two order of magnitude"; the only primitive-variable comparator tested is the first-order Dumbser–Osher–Toro scheme. **Shown** (against that comparator only)
16. Grid convergence ($N_x=32,\dots,1024$, Table 1, Fig. 4): the two coarsest grids are pre-asymptotic; pairwise orders for $\rho$ are 1.0843, 1.6281, 1.4969, 1.8654, 2.1740 (identical for $\rho u$) and for $E$ 1.1490, 1.6447, 1.5292, 1.8993, 2.2075; the text gives least-squares rates over the three finest meshes of $(2.17, 2.17, 2.21)$ (§5.1.3; see Printed slips). **Shown**
17. The order can be raised by "replacing the central gradient with higher-order central or split-flux differencing" (§5.1.3). **Stated** (not demonstrated)
18. Ablation: with the central term alone, after 600 steps at CFL 0.8 the 1-D advection develops oscillations in $\rho$ and $T$ that "grow rapidly in time" and "blow up the simulation quickly", while $u$ and $p$ stay nearly uniform (§5.1.4, Fig. 5). **Shown**
19. "A small amount of high-order, characteristic-based dissipation is essential for stabilizing the primitive variable update in transcritical flows" (§5.1.4). **Shown** (one test case)
20. Transcritical shock tube: the hybrid resolves shock, contact and expansion "while maintaining sharp interfaces without spurious oscillations", with no limiter or filter (§5.2, Fig. 6). **Shown** (no exact or reference solution plotted)
21. 2-D droplet advection ($151\times151$, one period, CFL 0.5): the droplet keeps its circular shape "with minimal numerical diffusion" and pressure and velocity stay uniform; the scheme "successfully maintains pressure equilibrium" (§5.3, Fig. 7). **Shown** (contour plots only)
22. The conservative form is needed at shocks: "compared with primitive form, the conservative form can maintain an accurate prediction of the speed and strength of shock waves", and a conservative scheme is "essential to ensure that the Rankine-Hugoniot jump conditions are satisfied" (§3, §3.4). **Stated** (cited)
23. The Peng–Robinson equation of state "has been shown to reliably predict the thermodynamic properties" of hydrocarbons and other fluids in transcritical and supercritical regions (§2.2). **Stated** (cited)
24. The pressure-equilibrium-preserving fully conservative schemes of Fujiwara et al., Terashima et al. and Bernades et al. "are not easily extended to general transcritical flows"; the DG $L^2$-projection approach of Ching et al. "can still result in small deviations from pressure equilibrium" (§1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **§5.1.3 against Table 1:** "least-squares fits over the three finest meshes yield convergence rates of $r=(2.17,2.17,2.21)$ for $(\rho,\rho u,E)$" — these are the finest-pair rates of Table 1 (2.1740, 2.1740, 2.2075). *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with grids halving, the least-squares slope over three points is the mean of the two last pairwise orders, $(1.8654+2.1740)/2=2.02$ for $\rho$ and $\rho u$, $(1.8993+2.2075)/2=2.05$ for $E$.)*
- **§3, notation:** "$B(\mathbf V)=\partial\mathbf F(\mathbf V)/\partial\mathbf V$ is the Jacobian of the inviscid flux written in primitive variables". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the second row of $\partial\boldsymbol F/\partial\boldsymbol V$ is $(u^2,2\rho u,1)$, not the $(0,u,1/\rho)$ of Eq. 3; the printed matrix is the quasi-linear primitive matrix $(\partial\boldsymbol W/\partial\boldsymbol V)^{-1}\partial\boldsymbol F/\partial\boldsymbol V$.)*
- **$\pm$ convention:** §3 and §3.2 label $v^-_{i+1/2}$ the left-biased value from the stencil centred on $i$; §3.4 writes "left ($\mathbf V_L=\mathbf V^+$) and right ($\mathbf V_R=\mathbf V^-$) states" and §4.1 "$V_L=V_i^+$: Right-biased reconstruction from the left cell $i$" — the opposite, cell-indexed labelling. Eq. (21) follows §3.2.
- **Eq. (33), third line:** the exact midpoint difference is printed already divided by $\Delta x$ inside a bracket that is divided by $\Delta x$ again.
- **Eq. (17):** the second wave speed is printed "$s^R_{i+\frac12}z=$" (stray $z$). §3: "$V^-_{1+1/2}$ is the value reconstructed in the left side of cell $i+1/2$".
- **Speed of sound:** written $a$ (Eqs. 3, 6, 10, 13–17) and $c$ (§2.2 text, Eqs. 35, 41), while $a$ is also the Peng–Robinson attraction parameter (Eq. 5, acknowledged in a footnote).
- **Eq. (42):** the formula uses $T_l$, $T_r$; the text defines "$T_L=200$ K and $T_R=100$ K".
- **Citations (§1):** Ching et al. "develop a fully conservative discontinuous Galerkin method for supercritical, real-fluid flows [41]", while [41] is titled "A note on reducing spurious pressure oscillations in fully conservative discontinuous Galerkin simulations of multicomponent flows"; [44] (Spiegel et al., over-integration) is cited for $L^2$ projection of primitive variables; "NASA polynomials[18]" cites Ma, Lv & Ihme (2017); "Pasquale[28]" uses the first name of Lapenna.
- **Typos:** "Wisdom line" (§3, for Widom); "two order of magnitude" (abstract); "Eric Ching et at."; "the the corresponding flux vector" (§3.1); Eq. (9) "$\mathbf W i^{n+1}$".
- **Duplicate reference:** [20] and [40] are the same work (Ching, Johnson & Kercher 2025), [20] with an arXiv identifier.
