# Summary

Han et al. (2022) apply WENO-K, the group's fifth-order
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) reconstruction whose three-cell
sub-stencil interpolants come from Gauss–Kriging interpolation with a
locally optimized hyper-parameter $\theta_{\mathrm K}$, to URANS of a
hovering helicopter rotor, with the aim of keeping the tip-vortex wake as
long as possible. WENO-K reduces to WENO-JS at $\theta_{\mathrm K}=0$ and
keeps its nonlinear weights. Primitive variables are reconstructed at cell
faces and fed to a Roe solver with Harten's entropy fix in a structured
cell-centred finite-volume code; viscous fluxes are second-order central,
the closure is the one-equation
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) (no variant
stated), and time integration is dual-time stepping with LU-SGS.

The rotor sits on a dynamic overset grid: a static Cartesian background
with a uniform orthogonal wake region and two rotating body-fitted blade
grids. The infrastructure contributions are a multi-start donor search,
three-layer fringes so that the five-point stencil keeps its width at
overset boundaries, and an arbitrary partition of the 18,057,216-cell
system into 156 blocks.

The test is the two-bladed
[Caradonna–Tung rotor](/benchmarks/caradonna-tung-rotor.md) (rectangular
untwisted [NACA 0012](/benchmarks/naca-0012-airfoil.md) blades, aspect
ratio 6, collective $8°$) at tip Mach numbers 0.439 and 0.877. Against the
measured thrust coefficient, WENO-K errs by 0.9% and 3.0%, WENO-JS by 1.5%
and 5.1%, third-order MUSCL by 3.5% and 8.2%, at 4.1% more wall-clock time
per revolution than WENO-JS. WENO-K keeps the tip vortex to a wake age of
$900°$ and $1000°$ (WENO-JS $630°$, $720°$; MUSCL $150°$, $250°$), which
the authors attribute to lower
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md). The
paper's turbulence content is limited to the SA closure; scale-resolving
rotor computations such as
[DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) appear only among its
references.

# Key claims

1. WENO-K, an adaptively optimized WENO scheme with Gauss–Kriging reconstruction, reduces dissipation in smooth regions while keeping high resolution at discontinuities (Abstract, §1, §3.2.2). **Stated** (derived in the group's 2020 paper; shown here only through the rotor results)
2. WENO-K degenerates to WENO-JS when $\theta_{\mathrm K}=0$ (§3.2.2, after Eq. 9). **Derived** (the $\theta_{\mathrm K}$-free coefficients of Eq. 8 are the third-order sub-stencil coefficients)
3. Each WENO-K sub-stencil interpolant is fourth order against third order for WENO-JS, and the nonlinear combination of the three stencils is "higher than fifth order" (§3.2.2, Eqs. 7–8). **Stated**
4. The optimized hyper-parameter is $\theta_{\mathrm K}=\frac65\frac{\bar q_{i-1}-3\bar q_i+3\bar q_{i+1}-\bar q_{i+2}}{\bar q_{i-1}-15\bar q_i+15\bar q_{i+1}-\bar q_{i+2}}$, obtained "after a series of complex error analysis" (Eq. 9). **Stated**
5. A troubled-cell indicator $a_{\mathrm{cz}}=(\bar q_{i-1}-\bar q_{i+2})/(\bar q_{i+1}-\bar q_i)$ with a "comfort zone" $-6.5<a_{\mathrm{cz}}<5$ sets $\theta_{\mathrm K}=\frac65\bigl(1-\frac{12}{15+a_{\mathrm{cz}}}\bigr)$ inside and $\theta_{\mathrm K}=0$ (WENO-JS) outside; the range was set by "a large number of tests" (Table 4, §3.2.2). **Stated**
6. WENO-K has dispersion comparable to WENO-JS and clearly lower dissipation over a substantial wavenumber range (Fig. 12, reproduced from the 2020 paper). **Shown** (in the earlier paper)
7. A finite-volume method "can only reach second-order accuracy especially for multi-dimensional flow field around complex boundaries", but on a nearly uniform orthogonal grid dimension-by-dimension WENO gives an "approximate high-order FVM" (§3.2.2). **Stated**
8. Three-layer fringes give "no accuracy reduction for fifth-order reconstruction" at hole and artificial outer boundaries (§2.2, Fig. 5). **Stated** (not separately verified)
9. The multi-start distance-decreasing donor search is efficient and avoids search failure (§2.3, Fig. 6). **Stated** (no timing given)
10. The grid (14,745,600 background cells and $2\times1{,}655{,}808$ blade cells, 18,057,216 in all) is partitioned into 128 background blocks of 115,200 cells and 28 blade blocks of 118,272 cells, run on 156 sub-processors and one main processor (Table 2, §2.4, §4). **Shown**
11. Grid convergence with MUSCL, subsonic case: thrust coefficient 0.00410, 0.00431, 0.00443, 0.00447 on 5, 10, 18 and 36 million cells; levels 3 and 4 differ by less than 1%, so the 18-million-cell grid is used (Table 3, Fig. 11, §2.5). **Shown**
12. Wall-clock time per revolution on 157 processors of TianHe-1(A): MUSCL 12.5 h, WENO-JS 14.6 h (+16.8%), WENO-K 15.2 h (+21.6% over MUSCL, +4.1% over WENO-JS); per pseudo-time step 5.25, 6.30, 6.59 s; per physical step 105, 126, 132 s (Table 5, §4). **Shown**
13. Subsonic case (tip Mach 0.439): measured thrust coefficient 0.00459; MUSCL 0.00443 (3.5%), WENO-JS 0.00452 (1.5%), WENO-K 0.00455 (0.9%) (Table 6). **Shown**
14. Subsonic case: the tip vortex is captured to wake age $900°$ with WENO-K, $630°$ with WENO-JS and $150°$ with MUSCL (Q-criterion iso-surface 0.05) (§4.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
15. Subsonic case: vortex pairing appears at $930°$ with WENO-K and $700°$ with WENO-JS; a published 100-million-cell simulation gives $950°$ (§4.1, Fig. 15). **Shown**
16. Subsonic pressure coefficients at $r/R=0.50$, 0.68, 0.80, 0.96 agree with experiment for all three schemes; the WENO suction peaks are closer, WENO-K better than WENO-JS at 0.80 and 0.96 (Fig. 16). **Shown**
17. Transonic case (tip Mach 0.877): measured thrust coefficient 0.00473; MUSCL 0.00512 (8.2%), WENO-JS 0.00497 (5.1%), WENO-K 0.00487 (3.0%) (Table 7). **Shown**
18. Transonic case: tip vortex captured to $1000°$ (WENO-K), $720°$ (WENO-JS), $250°$ (MUSCL) (iso-surface 0.25) (§4.2, Fig. 17). **Shown**
19. Transonic case: no vortex pairing within the visible revolution, attributed to the faster descent of the stronger tip vortices (§4.2, Fig. 19). **Shown** / **Stated**
20. A tip shock and unsteady separation make tip-vortex strength and position vary with azimuth; the authors state that the smooth, regular tip-vortex iso-surfaces of quasi-steady or coarse-grid simulations are "not actually consistent with the reality" (§4.2, Figs. 20–22). **Shown** / **Stated** (no study cited)
21. For shock capture WENO-K is only slightly better than WENO-JS, its advantage being lower dissipation in smooth regions (§4.2, Fig. 23). **Shown**
22. WENO-K matches the measured transonic tip-vortex trajectory best, circumferentially after $200°$ and axially after $180°$ (Fig. 24). **Shown**
23. Abstract: WENO-K thrust error at most 3%, about 40% more accurate than WENO-JS at 4.1% extra cost, tip vortex resolved about $270°$ further (Abstract). **Shown** *(compiler check, not in the sources: consistent with Tables 6–7 and §§4.1–4.2, errors 1.5→0.9 and 5.1→3.0, a reduction of 40% and 41%; ages $900-630=270°$, $1000-720=280°$)*
24. WENO-K is applied to URANS of vortex-dominated rotor flows "for the first time" (Conclusion 1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Before Eq. (8) the sub-stencils are printed $S_k=(i-k,\,i-k+1,\,i-k+2)$, so $S_0=(i,i+1,i+2)$; Eq. (8) builds $q^{(0)}$ from $\bar q_{i-2},\bar q_{i-1},\bar q_i$ and $q^{(2)}$ from $\bar q_i,\bar q_{i+1},\bar q_{i+2}$, and Eq. (12) follows Eq. (8).
- Eq. (12) prints the second term of $\mathrm{IS}_1$ as $\frac14(\bar q_{i-2}-4\bar q_i+3\bar q_{i+1})^2$, which uses $\bar q_{i-2}$, a cell outside the stencil $\{i-1,i,i+1\}$ of $q^{(1)}$.
- Eq. (12) prints the second term of $\mathrm{IS}_2$ as $\frac14(\bar q_i-4\bar q_{i+1}+3\bar q_{i+2})^2$, the one-sided difference at cell $i+2$, while that of $\mathrm{IS}_0$, $\frac14(\bar q_{i-2}-4\bar q_{i-1}+3\bar q_i)^2$, is the one-sided difference at cell $i$, the reconstruction cell; the text calls the indicators "the same as that of the WENO-JS method" *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (6), the "third-order MUSCL" with the van Albada limiter, gives in smooth regions ($s=1$) $Q_{L,i+1/2}=Q_i+\frac3{16}\Delta_-+\frac5{16}\Delta_+$, while the paper's own third-order three-cell interpolant ($q^{(1)}$ of Eq. 8 at $\theta_{\mathrm K}=0$) is $Q_i+\frac16\Delta_-+\frac13\Delta_+$; as printed Eq. (6) is not that third-order interpolant *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Table 2 prints 3,311,616 under "grid cells/block" for the unpartitioned blade grids $176\times56\times168\times2$; $176\times56\times168=1{,}655{,}808$ per block, and 3,311,616 is the two-block total *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Table 5: 360 physical steps per revolution times the printed physical-step times (105, 126, 132 s) gives 10.5, 12.6 and 13.2 h, against the printed revolution times 12.5, 14.6 and 15.2 h, each 2.0 h larger; the paper does not explain the difference *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- $\theta$ is both the Kriging hyper-parameter and the blade pitch angle; $\alpha$ both the unnormalized weights and the modified wavenumber (Fig. 12 text); $H$ both the flux tensor and the total enthalpy; $q$ the velocity vector, the reconstructed scalar and the Q-criterion value.
- "forth-order" (§3.2.2); refs. 37–38 print "Vanleer B" and "Albada GDV".
- Ref. 3 prints "Chin J Aeronaut 2020;167(02):91–107", while refs. 4 and 9 give the same journal in 2020 as volume 33.
