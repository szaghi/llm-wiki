# Summary

Gan et al. (2022) ask how much of the fluctuation power in driven
three-dimensional [compressible MHD turbulence](/results/compressible-mhd-turbulence.md)
consists of fast magnetosonic waves, that is, fluctuations that satisfy the
linear fast-mode dispersion relation at finite frequency. Three isothermal
ideal-MHD runs at $512^3$ with ATHENA++ have a strong guide field
($\boldsymbol B_0$ along $x$, $v_A=8$, $\beta_p=0.4$) and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
forcing at $|\boldsymbol k|\le2$. Run A has incompressible velocity driving,
run B adds magnetic driving in an elongated box, and run C has highly
compressible velocity driving. The turbulent Mach number is 0.12–0.18. No
explicit viscosity or subgrid model is mentioned.

Two decompositions are compared. The spatial-only method (Cho & Lazarian
2003) projects each velocity snapshot onto the linear Alfvén, fast and slow
polarizations, so all power is assigned to a mode. The space–time method
Fourier transforms in space and time and assigns power to a mode only
within ±10% (also ±3%, ±40%) of that mode's frequency; the rest is
"non-wave". With the injection range and the zero-frequency $k_\parallel=0$
component excluded, the non-wave fraction is 0.73–0.79. Alfvén waves carry
0.20–0.24. The fast fraction is $10^{-4}$ or less with incompressible driving
and 0.024 with compressible driving, against 0.137 from spatial projection.

Structure functions of the separated components give fast modes
scale-independent anisotropy ($l_\parallel\propto\lambda$). With spatial
projection and incompressible driving, the total, Alfvén and slow parts
follow $l_\parallel\propto\lambda^{2/3}$, the
[critical-balance](/properties/critical-balance.md) scaling. Under the
space–time filter the Alfvén and slow components of run B scale with
exponent 1. The authors conclude that spatial decomposition can be "too
optimistic" about fast and compressible modes. The mostly low-frequency
non-wave component should enter particle-transport and energization models.
The set-up is a driven periodic box (compare forced
[homogeneous turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)),
made anisotropic by the guide field.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $f_c$ the compressibility parameter of the velocity
driving, $|\boldsymbol A_\parallel|=|\boldsymbol A_\perp|f_c/(1-f_c)$ for
the forcing amplitudes parallel and perpendicular to $\boldsymbol B_0$;
$\omega$ the angular frequency of the space–time transform (not the
specific dissipation rate); $k_\parallel\simeq k_x$,
$k_\perp\simeq(k_y^2+k_z^2)^{1/2}$; $l_\parallel$, $\lambda$ the parallel
and perpendicular structure-function separations (the source's
$L_\parallel$, $L_\perp$; the paper does not say whether they are taken
along $\boldsymbol B_0$ or a local field) and $m_a$ (registry §11) the exponent of
$l_\parallel\propto\lambda^{m_a}$; A/F/S the Alfvén, fast and slow fractions.

1. The spatial-only decomposition always assigns all power to one of the three linear eigenmodes, but its physical meaning is "a bit unclear" in strong turbulence where nonlinear interactions matter (§2.2). **Stated**
2. The 4D FFT is a 3D spatial FFT at each stored time followed by a temporal FFT; outputs are stored at about 100 frames per $\tau_A$ for about 10 $\tau_A$ (§2.2). **Stated**
3. Power is assigned to a mode within ±10% of its theoretical $\omega(\boldsymbol k)$; the tolerance absorbs spectral leakage (reduced by a Hanning window) and nonlinear broadening; ±3% and ±40% "do not change qualitatively" the conclusions (§2.2, §3, Fig. 3). **Shown**
4. The 4D spectrum of run B shows power cascading to higher $k$ at finite frequency, on the three dispersion surfaces and in large amounts between them; the vertical feature at $|\boldsymbol k|\le2$ is the driving (§3, Fig. 1). **Shown**
5. Slow modes cascade further in $k$ than Alfvén and fast modes (§3, Fig. 1a,c). **Shown**
6. Most of the power lies on none of the three branches ("non-wave"), concentrated at low $\omega$; "by inference" most of the $k_\perp$ cascade power is non-wave (§3, Fig. 1b,d). **Shown**
7. Spatial-only with the injection range included, A/F/S = 0.760/0.223/0.017 (run A), 0.628/0.028/0.344 (B), 0.400/0.206/0.394 (C) (§3, Fig. 2). **Shown**
8. Excluding $|\boldsymbol k|\le2$ (about 70% of the run-B power) and $k_\parallel=0,\omega=0$ (about 3%), spatial-only gives 0.237/0.003/0.760 (A), 0.425/0.007/0.568 (B), 0.210/0.137/0.653 (C); slow modes take the largest fraction, "probably" because the injection range is excluded (§3, Fig. 2). **Shown**
9. With the same exclusions the 4D FFT at ±10% gives non-wave/A/F/S = 0.792/0.204/$1\times10^{-4}$/0.003 (A), 0.762/0.225/$8\times10^{-7}$/0.013 (B), 0.729/0.238/0.024/0.009 (C) (§3, Fig. 2). **Shown**
10. At ±40%: 0.727/0.218/0.005/0.050 (A), 0.590/0.289/$7\times10^{-6}$/0.121 (B), 0.535/0.283/0.080/0.102 (C); at ±3%: 0.795/0.204/$1\times10^{-4}$/$8\times10^{-4}$ (A), 0.773/0.224/$2\times10^{-7}$/0.003 (B), 0.749/0.236/0.011/0.002 (C); the trends are unchanged from ±3% to ±40% (§3, Fig. 3). **Shown**
11. The total wave fraction with the 4D FFT is "less than ∼25%" (§3). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
12. Run C's fast fraction drops from about 14% (spatial-only) to about 2.4% (4D FFT); the abstract rounds to "∼2%" (§3, Abstract). **Shown**
13. Run B, with magnetic driving, has the largest Alfvén fraction under both methods and a small but finite slow fraction (§3). **Shown**
14. Spatial-only structure functions of run B: fast modes tend to be isotropic, Alfvén and slow modes elongated, consistent with Goldreich & Sridhar (1995) and Cho & Lazarian (2002, 2003) (§3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
15. 4D-FFT structure functions of run B (3% width): the fast mode is more isotropic, non-wave, Alfvén and slow more anisotropic; the slow component "might be too noisy to be accurate" (§3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
16. Fitted $m_a$: run B spatial-only total/A/F/S = 2/3, 2/3, 1, 2/3; run B 4D FFT non-wave/A/F/S = 2/3, 1, 1, 1; run C spatial-only total/A/F/S = 5/6, 2/3, 4/5, 2/3; run C 4D FFT non-wave/A/F/S = 5/6, 2/3, 1, 1 (§3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
17. Spatial-only with incompressible driving reproduces $l_\parallel\propto\lambda^{2/3}$ (total, Alfvén, slow) and $l_\parallel\propto\lambda$ (fast) of Cho & Lazarian (2003) (§3). **Shown**
18. With highly compressible driving the spatial-only exponents of the total (5/6) and fast (4/5) parts differ slightly from the incompressible case (2/3, 1) (§3). **Shown**
19. The 4D FFT gives the same or slightly steeper exponents than spatial-only for the wave components; the fast exponent is 1 in runs B and C; total and non-wave exponents agree (2/3 in B, 5/6 in C) because the non-wave part dominates the power (§3). **Shown**
20. After the exclusions the non-wave component carries about 75–80% of the power, mostly at low frequency, like the "1/f noise" of Dmitruk & Matthaeus (2007, 2009); resolving it needs longer times and larger domains (§4). **Shown**/**Stated**
21. The dominant non-wave component is consistent with the 80%–20% two-dimensional–slab decomposition of the solar wind (Bieber et al. 1996) and with nearly incompressible MHD theory (Zank & Matthaeus 1992, 1993; Zank et al. 2017, 2020) (§4). **Stated**
22. The spatial decomposition "might be too optimistic" about the fraction of fast and, in general, compressible modes; the non-wave component should be included in particle-transport and energization models (§4). **Stated**
23. The spatial-only results (Alfvén dominance, fast fraction growing with compressible driving, injection range included) are consistent with Makwana & Yan (2020), who found fast-mode power up to about 30% of the total under highly compressible driving (§1, §3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Wave fraction (§3)** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. "Less than ∼25%": from the printed ±10% fractions the wave fraction is $1-0.792=0.208$ (A), $1-0.762=0.238$ (B) and $1-0.729=0.271$ (C).
- **Non-wave fraction (§4).** "About 75–80% of the total power", while run C at ±10% prints 0.729.
- **Fast fraction with compressible driving (§3, p. 4).** The text cites "the increasing fraction of Fast modes with compressible driving", but with the injection range included the printed fast fraction is 0.223 for run A (incompressible driving) and 0.206 for run C (compressible driving); the increase holds only from run B (0.028) to run C.
- **Units of $v_A$ and $\beta$ (§2.1)** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. $v_A=8$ with $B_0=8$ and $\rho_0=1$ implies $v_A=B_0/\sqrt{\rho_0}$, while $\beta=8\pi p/B^2$ is the Gaussian definition; with $v_A=B_0/\sqrt{4\pi\rho_0}$ it would be 2.26.
- **Fig. 1 axis.** The frequency axis is labelled "omega/B0", not explained.
