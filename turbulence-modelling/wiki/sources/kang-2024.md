# Summary

Kang & Lee (2024) introduce an in-house compressible
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) solver. It uses
sixth-order tridiagonal compact finite differences on curvilinear grids
and third-order Runge–Kutta. An eighth-order compact low-pass filter with
$\alpha_{\mathrm{cf}}=0.47$ is applied at every final stage, a physical-space relative
of [explicit modal filtering](/methods/explicit-modal-filtering.md). Where
a modified Ducros [sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) flags a shock, the
filter drops to second order. Kawai–Lele localized
[artificial diffusivity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
of viscosity and conductivity is added. The authors apply the solver to the
[shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
over the Mach 2.9, $24^\circ$ ramp of Bookey et al. ($Re_\theta=2400$). A
laminar Blasius inflow is tripped by a counter-flow body force
([inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)).
The incoming [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) is
validated by the van Driest [velocity](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
and [log law](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) and by
[Morkovin-scaled](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md) intensities.
Separation and reattachment match the experiment, as does a velocity
profile $4\delta_r$ downstream of the corner.

Turbulent kinetic energy is amplified in two hotspots: by 3.39 near the
wall behind separation and by 3.24 in the free shear layer just before
reattachment. Using a streamline coordinate frame, the authors argue that
the second hotspot is not driven by the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability of the
shear layer. They attribute it to a "secondary shock wave", the mean of
intermittent shocklets spawned above boundary-layer bulges, whose adverse
pressure gradient decelerates the flow
([shock-generated turbulence](/results/shock-generated-turbulence.md)).
They split the production in the streamline frame and find that shear
dominates deceleration overall. In the budget they analyse only
production and turbulent transport, and they list the
[pressure dilatation](/definitions/pressure-dilatation.md) term without
analysing it. Turbulent transport carries the energy to a local
pressure-minimum line, onto which the line of maximum energy converges.

# Key claims

Notation: $\delta_r$ is the boundary-layer thickness at the reference
station $x_0$ ($x/\delta_r\approx-5.4$, the source's $\delta_0=6.7$ mm);
$(x_{\mathrm s},y_{\mathrm s})$ is the streamline frame and a subscript s marks
quantities in it (the source's $x^*$, $y^*$); $\mathrm{AF}_q$ is the
amplification factor of $q$, its wall-normal maximum at $x$ over that at
$x_0$ (page-local, the source's $\varphi_q$).

1. A new in-house high-order compressible DNS solver (sixth-order compact differences, RK3, eighth-order compact filter, adaptive shock filter, localized artificial diffusivity) is introduced and validated against experiment and other DNS on the Mach 2.9, $24^\circ$ ramp (§II.B, §III). **Shown**
2. The reference station matches the experiment of Bookey et al.: $M_\infty=2.9$, $\delta_r=6.7$ mm, $\theta=0.43$ mm, $Re_\theta=2400$, $C_f=0.00227$ (experiment 0.00225) (Table I). **Shown**
3. The van Driest velocity at $x_0$ follows $u^+=y^+$ in the viscous sublayer and $u^+=5.0+2.44\log y^+$ in the log region (as printed) (§III, Eq. 16, Fig. 4b). **Shown**
4. Density-scaled turbulence intensities agree with incompressible DNS (Wu & Moin) and compressible DNS (Tong et al., $Re_\theta=2300$, $M=2.9$); the streamwise peak is at $y^+\approx12$ (§III, Fig. 5). **Shown**
5. Spanwise autocorrelations at $y^+=50$ and $y/\delta_r=0.5$ fall to zero within $0.5\delta_r$, and spectra at $y^+=50$ follow $k^{-5/3}$ with no spurious length scales from the trip (§III, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
6. Separation and reattachment lie at $x_s/\delta_r=-3.13$ and $x_r/\delta_r=1.50$, "in perfect agreement with the experimental data" (§III, Fig. 9). **Shown**
7. The ramp-aligned mean velocity $4\delta_r$ downstream of the corner agrees with the experiment; the authors present this as a validation of the field downstream of the bubble that earlier DNS lacked (§III, Eq. 19, Fig. 11). **Shown**
8. The wall-pressure spectrum peaks at $St\approx1$ at the reference station (probe 0), at $St\approx0.02$ in the intermittent region (probe 1, within the experimental $0.02\pm0.01$), is raised at all frequencies in the bubble (probe 2) and has a second peak at $St\approx0.55$ at reattachment (probe 3) (§III, Fig. 12). **Shown**
9. There are two distinct hotspots of turbulent kinetic energy, near the wall behind separation and in the free shear layer near reattachment; the streamwise normal stress dominates $k$ (§IV.A, Fig. 13). **Shown**
10. $\mathrm{AF}_k=3.39$ at $x/\delta_r=-2.38$ (first hotspot) and 3.24 at $x/\delta_r=1.36$, slightly before reattachment (second hotspot) (§IV.A, Eq. 20, Fig. 14, Table IV). **Shown**
11. Streamline-frame amplification factors for the first and second hotspot: streamwise normal stress 2.76 and 2.22, normal-to-streamline stress 5.63 and 6.00, spanwise 4.68 and 4.64, $k$ 3.39 and 3.24 (Table IV). **Shown**
12. Compared with Shi & Yan (LES of the same flow: $\mathrm{AF}_k$ 3.34 and about 3.1, hotspots at $x/\delta_r=-1.75$ and 0.53), the present hotspots lie farther upstream and downstream, which the authors attribute to different separation lengths (§IV.A). **Stated**
13. The initial amplification starts in the inner region in the streamwise component ($y_\perp/\delta_r=0.09$ at $x/\delta_r=-2.79$, against about 0.32 for the other components), where the shear layer has not detached, so amplification beyond separation "cannot be solely attributed" to the free shear layer (§IV.A–B, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
14. The spanwise vorticity of the free shear layer weakens after the initial detachment and does not grow near or after the second hotspot (§IV.B, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
15. The streamline-frame shear stress peaks downstream of the first hotspot and re-amplifies downstream of reattachment, so the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability "does not appear to be the primary driver" of the second hotspot (§IV.B, Fig. 18). **Shown** (interpretation)
16. Behind the main shock, branches of secondary shocks form λ shapes that appear and vanish intermittently and move fast (shocklets), at speeds of the order of the convective velocity of the large structures in the detached shear layer (§IV.C, Fig. 19). **Shown**
17. Rounded compression waves originate from large-scale bulges at the boundary-layer edge, where free-stream fluid penetrates between the bulges (§IV.C, Figs. 19e–h, 20). **Shown**
18. The pressure spectrum at the second hotspot has a mid-frequency peak at $St\approx0.35$, below the reattachment $St\approx0.55$; a probe in the shocklet region (probe 4) peaks at $St\approx0.02$ and 0.24, which the authors read as the second hotspot's susceptibility to the shocklets (§IV.C, Fig. 21). **Shown** (interpretation)
19. The mean density gradient reveals a concentrated secondary shock, the mean of the shocklets, above the free shear layer near reattachment; its adverse pressure gradient is weaker and broader than that of the main shock and decelerates the mean flow there (§IV.C, Figs. 22–24). **Shown**
20. The production computed in the Cartesian and the streamline frame gives "comparable results"; in the streamline frame the shear part $P_{\mathrm{sh}}$ contributes more than the deceleration part $P_{x_{\mathrm s}}$, and $P_{y_{\mathrm s}}$ is stated to be zero (§IV.D, Eq. 23, Figs. 25–26). **Shown** ($P_{y_{\mathrm s}}=0$ **Stated**)
21. Near separation $P_{\mathrm{sh}}$ has two strong regions, at $x/\delta_r=-2.92$ and $-2.36$, against a single Cartesian peak: boundary-layer roll-up raises $P_{\mathrm{sh}}$ first, $P_{x_{\mathrm s}}$ rises near $-2.64$ by the deceleration of the main shock, and $P_{\mathrm{sh}}$ re-amplifies as the shear layer detaches (§IV.D, Fig. 26a,c). **Shown**
22. Above the bubble a negative $P_{x_{\mathrm s}}$ zone from a positive mean velocity gradient reduces $k$ (as in Shi & Yan); near reattachment the deceleration by the secondary shock raises $P_{x_{\mathrm s}}$ and starts the second amplification, and slightly downstream $P_{\mathrm{sh}}$ peaks, attributed to the shocklets above the bulges (§IV.D, Fig. 26b,d). **Shown** (interpretation)
23. Strong production lies above the line of maximum $k$, so the location of maximum $k$ is not the most productive point; turbulent transport carries $k$ from the production region to the core of the free shear layer (§IV.D, Figs. 25, 27). **Shown**
24. Downstream of the main shock the line of maximum $k$ coincides "precisely" with a local wall-normal pressure minimum, formed between the raised pressure above the free shear layer and the hot high-pressure zone near the wall, which acts as a sink for transported $k$ (§IV.D, Figs. 28–29; also reported by Yu et al.). **Shown** (interpretation)
25. Overall, shear, not flow deceleration, dominates the production of $k$ in the interaction (Abstract, §V). **Shown** (from the decomposition of Eq. 23)

The streamline-frame production split (Eq. 23), in registry averaging with
Favre-averaged stresses and spanwise terms omitted:

$$P_k=\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle u_{\mathrm s}''u_{\mathrm s}''\rangle_\rho\,\partial_{x_{\mathrm s}}\langle u_{\mathrm s}\rangle_\rho}_{P_{x_{\mathrm s}}}\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle v_{\mathrm s}''v_{\mathrm s}''\rangle_\rho\,\partial_{y_{\mathrm s}}\langle v_{\mathrm s}\rangle_\rho}_{P_{y_{\mathrm s}}}\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle u_{\mathrm s}''v_{\mathrm s}''\rangle_\rho\big(\partial_{y_{\mathrm s}}\langle u_{\mathrm s}\rangle_\rho+\partial_{x_{\mathrm s}}\langle v_{\mathrm s}\rangle_\rho\big)}_{P_{\mathrm{sh}}}.$$

Side by side with [Shi & Yan (2023)](/sources/shi-2023.md), as each
prints it. Kang & Lee (§IV.A) attribute $\mathrm{AF}_k=2.7$ to Fang et al.
and 3.34 to Pirozzoli & Grasso "for the same flow conditions"; Shi & Yan
(§IV.C) set their 3.34 beside 2.97 (Fang et al.) and 2.7 (Pirozzoli and
Grasso). Shi & Yan conclude that deceleration by the separation shock
dominates production near separation and shear dominates in the separated
region. Kang & Lee conclude that shear initiates the first amplification
and dominates production overall.

- Eq. (8) prints the compact first derivative with the second right-hand-side term $b\,(\phi_{i+2}-\phi_{i-2})/(6h)$ and the coefficients $\alpha=1/3$, $a=14/9$, $b=1/9$ (§II.B, p. 3). *(Compiler check, not in the sources: for $\phi=x$, $h=1$, the left side is $5/3$ and the printed right side $14/9+2/27=44/27$; with the denominator $4h$ printed for the same coefficients by Yan et al. - Fig. 18 caption "Contour plot of $\langle u''v''\rangle/u_\tau^2$", while the colour bar and §IV.B give the streamline-frame $-\langle u^{*\prime\prime}v^{*\prime\prime}\rangle/u_\tau^2$; §IV.B prints once "$\langle u^{*\prime\prime}u^{*\prime\prime\prime}\rangle$".
- §IV.D: the dissipation term "accounts for the convection of the turbulent kinetic energy to thermal internal energy".
- §I names the impinging-shock angle of Pirozzoli & Grasso and Fang et al. "33.2" and §IV.A "33°".
