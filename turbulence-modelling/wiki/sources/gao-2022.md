# Summary

Gao et al. (2022) run the split-form
[DGSEM](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md) of
[FLEXI](/software/flexi.md) as an
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md): no subgrid model, dissipation
from the interface Riemann solver alone, and
[split-form volume fluxes](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) for
stability. They apply it to the oblique
[shock reflection on a turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md).
Shocks are captured by the
[DG/FV subcell scheme](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md)
of Sonntag & Munz. Their main methodological point concerns the
[shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md). The Jameson pressure
indicator, evaluated on the instantaneous field, flags large parts of the
under-resolved turbulent boundary layer. Ducros-type indicators either
miss the shocks inside the layer or flag too much, and they also flag
expansion fans. The authors therefore evaluate the indicator on a filtered
field: here the spanwise-averaged pressure, which keeps the subcells on
the shocks, including inside the boundary layer, and keeps the
[shock-capturing dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) out of
the turbulence. A positivity-preserving limiter stabilizes the frequent
DG/FV switching.

The scheme is validated first on Katzer's laminar interaction (Mach 2):
DG with $N=4$ reaches the converged separation length with fewer degrees
of freedom than second-order finite volumes. The turbulent case runs at
Mach 2 with $Re_{\theta_0}=1628$ and $8^\circ$ deflection, $N=6$ and about
93 million degrees of freedom. Its inflow uses the reformulated synthetic
eddy method with anisotropic linear forcing
([inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)).
The undisturbed [turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
matches DNS after the [van Driest transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
and [Morkovin's density scaling](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md).
Separation and reattachment agree with a reference
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md). Wall-pressure spectra show
low-frequency unsteadiness at $St\approx0.03$. Spanwise-periodic DNS and
LES fall below the experimental interaction-length scaling of Souverein et
al., which the authors attribute to missing sidewalls. They fit a new law
for periodic computations and expect
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) to cut the cost of such
studies.

# Key claims

1. The Jameson indicator (Eqs. 12–13) evaluated on the instantaneous pressure marks many cells inside the turbulent boundary layer as troubled, so it cannot be used for oblique shock/boundary-layer interaction without modification (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
2. Because dilatation and vorticity have similar magnitudes in the interaction region, the Ducros indicator either detects almost no shock inside the boundary layer (large threshold) or marks too many cells (small threshold); most authors take the large threshold and refine the mesh in the interaction region (§1, §3.2). **Stated**
3. The Ducros indicator and Pirozzoli's regularized form also flag expansion fans, whose squared dilatation can approach that of weak shocks; the signed dilatation $-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u$ (Eq. 16) excludes expansions, as in Larsson et al. and Lusher & Sandham (§3.2). **Stated**
4. The vorticity-scaled variant (Eq. 17, factor $a_\omega$) can improve shock capturing but is "not easy to be generalized for different cases" (§3.2). **Stated**
5. Proposed approach: filter the flow field to remove turbulent structures before evaluating a jump-detecting indicator; spatial filters are preferred, because temporal filters prevent real-time indicator updates and a moving-average filter made sharp shocks and stability hard to reconcile (§3.2). **Stated**
6. The Jameson indicator evaluated on the globally spanwise-averaged pressure captures all shocks, including inside the boundary layer, with a negligible number of finite-volume subcells near the interaction (Fig. 7 against Fig. 2). **Shown**
7. The global spanwise filter applies only to quasi-2-D configurations (compression ramps, oblique shock reflection); element-local spanwise averaging can serve some 3-D configurations; complex 3-D geometries are left to future work (§3.2). **Stated**
8. The filtering idea applies to any jump-detecting indicator, for instance the Persson indicator (§3.2). **Stated**
9. The positivity-preserving limiter of Zhang & Shu is needed to stabilize the frequent DG/FV switching when unsteady shocks inside the boundary layer are captured, and "can maintain the stability of DG methods without losing high-order accuracy" (§3.2). **Stated**
10. Laminar interaction (Katzer): the finite-volume resolution of Katzer (45,500 degrees of freedom) is not grid-converged; refinements by factors 2–6 are required (§4.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
11. At equal degrees of freedom the DG ($N=4$) solution is closer to the converged solution than the second-order finite-volume one (§4.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
12. Both DG/FV with 182,000 and FV with 409,500 degrees of freedom predict a separation length 5.87% larger than Katzer and about 8.25% larger than FV with 45,500; the DNS of Sansica et al. gives about 8.8% larger than Katzer (§4.2, Fig. 13). **Shown**
13. At about 355 degrees of freedom per core, FV (409,500) costs $1152\times9=10{,}368$ CPU-minutes and DG/FV (182,000) $512\times15=7680$, printed as "around 35% less" (§4.2; see Printed slips). **Shown**
14. Undisturbed boundary layer on 16,384 cores (about 5687 degrees of freedom per core): performance index about 1.55 µs, "about 94.0% larger" than the FLEXI baseline of Krais et al., owing to synthetic-eddy load imbalance, linear forcing (5–10% loss) and statistics collection (§5.2, Eq. 19). **Shown**
15. The undisturbed LES boundary layer agrees with the DNS of Wenzel et al. in van Driest-transformed mean velocity and Morkovin density-scaled Reynolds stresses at $11\delta_r$, one $\delta_r$ behind the forcing zone; the transformed $C_f$ quickly approaches the DNS and the correlation $C_{fi}=0.024Re_{\theta i}^{-0.25}$ (§5.2, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
16. The interaction with hybrid DG/FV and the filtered indicator costs about 1.84 µs per degree of freedom and stage, 18.7% more than the shock-free layer, within the 15–20% overhead of the hybrid scheme reported by Krais et al. (§5.3.1). **Shown**
17. Mean separation at $16.7\delta_r$ against $17.6\delta_r$ in the reference LES of Morgan et al.; reattachment at $19.5\delta_r$ against $19.3\delta_r$ (§5.3.1, Fig. 22). **Shown**
18. The separation location of the oblique interaction is highly sensitive to resolution and flow conditions, the reattachment location is not (§5.3.1). **Stated**
19. A skin-friction plateau appears in the separated region; since Morgan et al. found it only at high enough resolution, the authors take it as evidence of the DG method's accuracy on a much coarser grid than the reference LES (§5.3.1, Fig. 22). **Shown**
20. Mean interaction length $L_{\mathrm{int}}=x_{\mathrm{imp}}-x_0=20\delta_r-16.2\delta_r=3.8\delta_r$, from the time- and spanwise-averaged wall pressure (§5.3.1, Fig. 23). **Shown**
21. Wall-pressure spectra: the reformulated synthetic eddy method introduces no extra low-frequency content; low-frequency unsteadiness at $St=fL_{\mathrm{int}}/U_\infty\approx0.03$ near the mean reflected-shock foot, consistent with the experiment of Threadgill & Bruce (§5.3.2, Fig. 24). **Shown**
22. Recycling–rescaling inflow (Lund et al.) introduces extra frequencies that contaminate separation-induced unsteadiness, and synthetic-eddy methods recover faster than digital-filter methods (§1). **Stated** (cited)
23. Present $L_{\mathrm{sc}}=2.92$, $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}=1.39$: $L_{\mathrm{sc}}$ lies 16.4% below the Souverein law $L_{\mathrm{sc}}=1.3\,Se_{\mathrm{sc}}^{3.0}$; the experiment of Threadgill & Bruce ($L_{\mathrm{sc}}=3.22$, $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}=1.39$) lies 7.8% below (§5.3.3). **Shown**
24. From Table 3, a smaller spanwise extent might give a larger $L_{\mathrm{sc}}$ and a higher Reynolds number gives a larger $L_{\mathrm{sc}}$; both effects are too small to matter for the scaling law (§5.3.3). **Shown**
25. Spanwise-periodic DNS and LES fail to match the experimental scaling law, especially for $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}\in(1.3,1.8)$; the underestimate of $L_{\mathrm{sc}}$ is attributed to the missing sidewall effects, consistent with Bermejo-Moreno et al. and Wang et al.: "modeling sidewall effects is compulsory" to match experimental interaction sizes and Strouhal numbers (§5.3.3, Fig. 26). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
26. A least-squares fit to 23 DNS/LES data points gives $L_{\mathrm{sc}}=0.95\,Se_{\mathrm{sc}}^{3.15}$ for spanwise-periodic computations, with an inflow 10–20 $\delta_r$ upstream of the interaction recommended for $\delta_0^*$; it is claimed to apply to compression ramps too (§5.3.3, Fig. 26). **Shown** (fit) / **Stated** (applicability)
27. To the authors' knowledge this is the first high-order DGSEM implicit LES of oblique shock/turbulent boundary-layer interaction (§1). **Stated**
28. Costs: undisturbed layer about 46,173 CPU hours, interaction about 196,608 CPU hours, on up to 16,384 cores (§5.2, §5.3.2). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- **CPU-time saving, §4.2:** "a total CPU time of 7680 min, around 35% less than the 'FV 409500DoFs' case" (10,368 min). *(compiler check, not in the sources)* $7680/10368=0.741$, 25.9% less; 35% is the excess of the finite-volume cost over the DG one ($10368/7680=1.35$).
- **Table 3 "Present" row against the text:** Table 3 lists $Re_{\theta_r}=1864$ and $L_{\mathrm{sc}}=2.33$ for the present LES; Table 1 and §5.1 give $Re_{\theta_r}=2188$ and §5.3.3 gives $L_{\mathrm{sc}}=2.92$. *(compiler check, not in the sources)* Eq. 20 with the printed $L_{\mathrm{int}}=3.8\delta_r$, $\delta_r=0.507$ mm, $\delta_0^*=0.1139$ mm, $\beta_s=37.2^\circ$, $\varphi_s=8^\circ$ gives 2.917, and $1-2.92/(1.3\times1.39^3)=0.164$ reproduces the printed 16.4%: the text value is consistent, Table 3's 2.33 is not. Which value entered the fit of claim 26 is not stated.
- **Factor $k_{Se}$ in $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}$:** the paper prints $k_{Se}=2.5$ for $Re_{\theta_0}\le10^4$ (the present $Re_{\theta_0}=1628$). *(compiler check, not in the sources)* With the printed $\Delta p_s=p_3-p_1$, $p_3/p_1=2.30$, $\gamma=1.4$ and $M=2$, $\Delta p_s/q_0=1.30/(\tfrac12\gamma M^2)=0.464$, hence $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}=1.16$ with $k_{Se}=2.5$ and $1.39$ with $k_{Se}=3$: the printed $Se_{\mathrm{sc}}=1.39$ corresponds to $k_{Se}=3$.
- **Strouhal resolution, §5.3.2:** "$400\delta_r/U_\infty$ … leading to a Strouhal number St resolution of 0.0087". *(compiler check, not in the sources)* $L_{\mathrm{int}}/(400\delta_r)=3.8/400=0.0095$.
- **Symbol clash:** $x_r$ is both the reference position $3\delta_r$ upstream of $x_{\mathrm{imp}}$ (§5.1) and the mean reattachment location (§5.3.1, Fig. 24 caption).
- **Typos and references:** "Ducors" (§3.2), "Pirozzol et al." (Table 3, §5.3.3), "spawise-averaged" (§5.3.3), "vorticies" (§3.2); ref. 23 prints its first author as "dB MDL" (de Laage de Meux in the text); ref. 16 prints "J Fluid Mech. 2017;823(617):014602-014619"; ref. 62 (Hadjadj) prints a single author.
