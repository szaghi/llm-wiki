# Summary

Yu et al. (2022) ask how turbulence amplification in an impinging
oblique [shock/boundary-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
is linked to the large-scale motions that appear in the interaction zone.
A [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) with
[STREAmS](/software/streams.md) (sixth-order
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
central convection switched to fifth-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)
by the Ducros [sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md)) computes a Mach-2.28
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
($Re_\tau=201$ at the reference station; digital-filter
[inflow](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md) with the generalized
Reynolds analogy for temperature) hit by a shock from an 8° wedge, with
incipient separation; the incoming layer is validated in van Driest
[transformed](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
variables.

The contribution is a transport equation for the density-weighted
spanwise two-point velocity correlation of compressible flow (after Arun
et al. 2021), split into convection, production, turbulent, pressure and
viscous diffusion, pressure-strain, dissipation, inter-scale transfer and
mass diffusion, and its Fourier transform in $z$: a scale-by-scale budget
of the Reynolds-stress and turbulent-kinetic-energy spectra. The authors
reject the spectral formulation of Dupuy et al. (2018) for this flow,
because it relies on [Morkovin's](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md)
insignificance of density fluctuations, which may be large near the shock.
Across the interaction the most energetic motions move from near-wall
streaks at $\lambda_z^+\approx100$ to large scales $\lambda_z\approx0.9\delta_r$
at $y\approx0.4\delta_r$, which persist downstream. At large scales the
production is due only to mean-flow deceleration; at small scales
deceleration and shear act together, so the "deceleration first, then
shear" sequence of the integrated budget is an integral effect. The
[pressure dilatation](/definitions/pressure-dilatation.md) and mass
diffusion matter only near the shocks. Inter-scale transfer is forward
overall; the inverse transfer upstream ([backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md)
in the spectral sense, not a subgrid flux) is small against production,
and the spanwise-uniform unsteadiness inside the boundary layer is fed
mainly by inter-scale transfer. The shock-capturing
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) makes the
budget near the shocks unreliable.

# Key claims

The source's overbar (Reynolds average) is $\langle\cdot\rangle$, its tilde
(Favre average) $\langle\cdot\rangle_\rho$, $u_i''$ the Favre fluctuation;
its $K=\langle\rho u_i''u_i''\rangle/2$ is $\langle\rho\rangle k$; its
reference thickness $\delta_0$ (at $x_0=15\delta_{in}$) is $\delta_r$, with
$x^*=(x-x_{imp})/\delta_r$, $y^*=y/\delta_r$; its pressure-dilatation term
$\Pi_K$ is written $\Pi^{\mathrm t}_{\mathrm{pd}}$ as on
[pressure dilatation](/definitions/pressure-dilatation.md) (distinct from
the registry's subgrid flux $\Pi$); $Q_{ij}$ is the source's two-point correlation and
$\hat Q_{ij}$ its spanwise spectrum (the hat is not a test filter).

1. A transport equation for the spanwise two-point correlation $Q_{ij}(\boldsymbol x,\boldsymbol r)=\tfrac12\langle(\rho(\boldsymbol x)+\rho(\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol r))u_i''(\boldsymbol x)u_j''(\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol r)\rangle$, $\boldsymbol r=(0,0,r_z)$, is derived for compressible flow, $\partial_tQ_{ij}=C_{ij}+P_{ij}+D_{ij}+\phi_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ij}+T_{ij}+B_{ij}$ ($\phi_{ij}$ the two-point pressure–strain, the source's $\Pi_{ij}$) (§3.1, Eqs. 3.2, 3.5–3.15); it reduces to the turbulent-kinetic-energy equation at $r_z=0$ with $i=j$ contracted, and to the incompressible two-point equation for unit density. **Derived**
2. The inter-scale transfer $T_{ij}=T^{xy}_{ij}+T^z_{ij}$ tends to zero as $r_z\to0$, so it only moves energy between scales ("we can easily prove", proof not given) (§3.1). **Stated**
3. The Fourier transform in $z$ gives the budget of the Reynolds-stress spectra (Eqs. 3.16–3.27); contracting $i,j$ gives the spectral budget of $\hat Q_{kk}$ (§3.2). **Derived**
4. The DNS ($M_\infty=2.28$, $Re_{in}=12\,000$, wedge 8°, shock angle 33.2°, impingement at $30\delta_{in}$, domain $60\times12\times6.5\,\delta_{in}$, grid $2000\times320\times240$, $\Delta x^+\approx5.4$, $\Delta z^+\approx4.9$, $\Delta y^+_w\approx0.7$, $\Delta y^+\approx7.0$ in the outer uniform region) reproduces the van Driest velocity and Reynolds stresses of Pirozzoli & Bernardini (2011b) at $M=2.0$, $Re_\tau\approx200$ (Fig. 2), and their wall pressure of 2011a (Fig. 3b) (§2). **Shown**
5. Separation at $x^*\approx-2.60$, reattachment at $x^*\approx-0.73$, extrapolated reflected-shock origin $x^*\approx-3.27$; interaction length $L^*_{int}\approx3.27$, separation length $L^*_{sep}\approx1.87$, consistent with Volpiani et al. (2018) (§2.2, Fig. 3a). **Shown** Yu et al. (2023), §2, report $L_{\mathrm{sep}}\approx1.65\delta_r$ for the same database: see the open contradiction on [shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md).
6. $R_{11}$ peaks at $x^*\approx-1.5$, at $y^*\approx0.4$ after the shock; a secondary near-wall peak at $x^*\approx3.5$ marks streak regeneration; $R_{22}$ and $R_{33}$ peak at $x^*\approx0$ and $-1.5$; $R_{12}$ is amplified downstream of the impinging shock (§4, Fig. 5). **Shown**
7. In the turbulent-kinetic-energy budget (Eq. 4.1) the pressure dilatation $\Pi^{\mathrm t}_{\mathrm{pd}}$ and the mass-diffusion term $B_K$ (genuine compressibility effects) matter only near the strong impinging shock; at $x^*=-3.28$ the budget resembles canonical compressible wall turbulence, except for a non-zero convection term due to upstream-travelling sonic waves (§4, Fig. 6). **Shown**
8. Splitting the production $\langle\rho\rangle P_k$ (Eq. 4.2) confirms Humble et al. (2007) and Fang et al. (2020): the deceleration part $P^x_{11}$ rises first, already upstream of the interaction, equals the shear part $P^y_{11}$ at $x^*=-3.28$ and exceeds it above $y^*=0.2$ within the interaction; downstream the production is again dominated by $P^y_{11}$; $P_{22}$ is much smaller (§4, Fig. 6b–i). **Shown**
9. Upstream the most energetic spanwise scale is $\lambda_z^*\approx0.5$, i.e. $\lambda_z^+\approx100$ (near-wall streaks); across the interaction the spectra widen in $\lambda_z^*$ and $y^*$ and extend beyond $\lambda_z^*=1.0$ (§5, Fig. 7a). **Shown**
10. The excited large scales come mainly from the wall-parallel components $\hat Q_{11}$ and $\hat Q_{33}$; $\hat Q_{22}$ contributes less (§5, Fig. 7b–d). **Shown**
11. The amplification rate $R_{Q11}$ (Eq. 5.1) is triggered first, even upstream of the reflected-shock origin, overshoots to 3.5 at $x^*\approx-1.0$, $y^*\approx0.4$, then decays to about 1.5 with the maximum staying at $y^*\approx0.4$; the dominant scale moves from $\lambda_z^+\approx100$ to $\lambda_z^*\approx0.9$ (§5, Fig. 8). **Shown**
12. $R_{Q22}$ and $R_{Q33}$ peak at $x^*\approx-0.5$ and $-1.5$; $R_{Q33}$ overshoots to 5.5 then returns to 4.5; $R_{Q22}$ rises monotonically to a plateau of 4.5; downstream maxima at $y^*=0.3$, $\lambda_z^*\approx0.45$ ($\hat Q_{22}$) and $y^*=0.4$, $\lambda_z^*\approx0.9$ ($\hat Q_{33}$) (§5, Fig. 8). **Shown**
13. Large-scale production ($\lambda_z^*\approx1.0$) inside the interaction is due to $\hat P^x_{11}$ (mean-flow deceleration): it appears at $x^*\approx-3.0$ and becomes negligible at $x^*\approx-1.0$, with a small negative region across the impinging shock (acceleration); $\hat P^y_{11}$ appears only at $x^*\approx-1.0$ (§6.1, Fig. 10a,b). **Shown**
14. At small scale ($\lambda_z^+\approx100$) $\hat P^x_{11}$ and $\hat P^y_{11}$ are equally significant, both rise from $x^*\approx-3.5$ and peak at $x^*\approx-2.0$; downstream $\hat P^x_{11}\to0$ while $\hat P^y_{11}$ persists; a second near-wall peak arises downstream (§6.1, Fig. 10c,d). **Shown**
15. Hence the "deceleration first, shear later" sequence of the integrated budget (Fang et al. 2020) is an integral effect, not a scale-by-scale phenomenon; the authors attribute the difference to the low Reynolds number: shear production dominates only when $\partial_x\tilde u$ is at least a decade smaller than $\partial_y\tilde u$, because $\langle u''^2\rangle_\rho$ is about 8–10 times the Reynolds shear stress (§6.1). **Derived** (order-of-magnitude argument)
16. Large-scale streaks are attributed to the mean adverse pressure gradient, by analogy with incompressible adverse-pressure-gradient boundary layers and supersonic adverse-pressure-gradient and concave-wall flows (§6.1). **Stated** (argued by analogy)
17. The pressure-dilatation spectrum is negligible except near the impinging and reflected shocks, with a small negative region near the wall at $x^*\approx-1.5$ (§6.2, Fig. 11a). **Shown**
18. Pressure-strain redistributes energy from $u''$ and $v''$ to $w''$ near the wall and from $u''$ to $v''$ and $w''$ higher up; with $\hat P_{22}$ small and $\hat P_{33}\equiv0$, the large-scale cross-stream circulations at the end of the interaction are fed by inter-component transfer from $u''$ (§6.2, Fig. 11b–d). **Shown**
19. The authors surmise that the cross-stream circulations come from instability or transient growth of the large-scale streaks, by analogy with the self-sustaining cycle, "partially substantiated" (§§5, 6.2, 8). **Stated**
20. Spatial transport (mass diffusion, convection and diffusion) balances the amplified production within the interaction, carrying energy downstream and towards the wall; the mass-diffusion part is much weaker than convection and diffusion (§6.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
21. Inter-scale transfer $\hat T_{kk}$ is forward overall. Upstream it moves energy mainly from $\lambda_z^+\approx100$ to $\lambda_z^+\approx20$, with inverse transfer to $\lambda_z^*\approx1.0$ as in channel flow (Lee & Moser 2015; Cho et al. 2018); at $x^*=-1.0$ only forward transfer from $\lambda_z^*\approx1.0$ remains; the inverse transfer contributes little to the genesis of large scales next to production (§6.4, Fig. 13). **Shown**
22. Dissipation is significant only near the wall and at small scales (§6.4, Fig. 13b). **Shown**
23. Spanwise-uniform oscillations ($k_z=0$) split into "shock unsteadiness" ($y^*\gtrsim1.0$, reflected shock) and "inner unsteadiness" ($y^*\lesssim1.0$, separation breathing and possible quasi-two-dimensional Kelvin–Helmholtz shedding); their spectral maximum is a decade below that of finite-scale motions; they amplify from $x^*\approx-3$ to a maximum at $x^*\approx-1.0$, $y^*\approx0.4$ (§7, Fig. 14a). **Shown**
24. The inner unsteadiness is fed primarily by positive inter-scale transfer from finite-scale motions, stronger than production even downstream, interpreted as a global instability excited by small-scale disturbances (§7, Fig. 14). **Shown** (transfer) / **Stated** (interpretation)
25. The shock-unsteadiness budget near the shocks is unreliable, because the numerical dissipation added for shock stability reduces the accuracy, "especially in spectral analysis" (§7). **Stated**
26. The scale-by-scale transfer "may benefit the improvement of subgrid modelling for large-eddy simulations", left to future work (§8). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eqs. (3.23)–(3.24):** the spectral pressure-strain and dissipation terms are printed with single-primed fluctuations $u_i'$, $u_j'$, while their physical-space forms (3.11)–(3.12) and all other spectral terms use the Favre fluctuation $u_i''$.
- **Normalization of $\hat Q_{ij}$ and §6:** Eq. (3.16) defines $\hat Q_{ij}=\mathrm{Re}[\widehat{\rho u_i''}\,\widehat{u_j''}^\star+\widehat{u_i''}\,\widehat{\rho u_j''}^\star]$, and Eqs. (6.2)–(6.4) print the production split with a factor $-\tfrac12$ ("similar to (4.2)"). *(compiler check, not in the sources)* At $r_z=0$ Eq. (3.2) gives $Q_{ij}=\langle\rho u_i''u_j''\rangle$, while (3.16), a sum of two cross-spectra without the $\tfrac12$ of (3.2), integrates to twice that; contracting (3.18) gives $\hat P_{kk}=-2\hat Q_{ik}\partial_k\tilde u_i$ (for symmetric $\hat Q_{ij}$), against the $-\tfrac12$ printed in (6.2)–(6.4). The paper does not state the normalization of the spectra of §6.
- **Term names:** pressure dilatation written $\Pi_K$ in Eq. (4.1) and $\Pi_k$ in the text (pp. A2-15, A2-16); $\hat P_{KK}$ (p. A2-20) against $\hat P_{kk}$ elsewhere.
- **§4:** the budget is shown "at seven streamwise stations", while Fig. 6(d–i) lists six ($x^*=-3.28$, $-2.62$, $-1.97$, $-1.32$, $-0.66$, $0.0$).
- **p. A2-21:** "a second peak in the near-wall region arises at $x\approx1.0$", without the asterisk used for every other location.
- **§2.2:** "$L^*_{int}=x^*_i\approx3.27$" with $x^*_i\approx-3.27$.
- **References:** Loginov et al. (2006), third author printed "Zheltovdov"; Jammalamadaka et al. (2014) printed "Phys. Fluids 26 (5), 87–108"; "curvation" for curvature (pp. A2-2, A2-4).
