# Summary

Li & Fu (2024) build hybrid finite-volume reconstructions for inviscid
compressible two-phase flow, governed by the quasi-conservative five-equation
model of Allaire et al. (two partial densities, mixture momentum and energy,
non-conservative volume-fraction advection) with stiffened-gas closure. The
building blocks are:

- **TENOA:** five-, six- and seven-point TENO schemes whose cut-off adapts as
  $C_T=10^{-\lfloor\beta_{\mathrm A}\rfloor}$ between $10^{-10}$ (smooth,
  high-wavenumber regions) and $10^{-5}$ (discontinuities), driven by a
  first-order undivided-difference discontinuity measure
  ([WENO and TENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md));
- **simplified THINC:** a hyperbolic-tangent interface reconstruction whose
  face values reduce to closed forms, claimed cheaper than earlier THINC
  formulae and symmetry-preserving;
- **TENOA-THINC switch:** THINC is used for the partial densities and volume
  fraction in interfacial cells ($10^{-3}<\alpha_1<1-10^{-3}$) and, elsewhere,
  where a parameter-free indicator built from the TENOA cut-off flags of the
  first two stencils (after Takagi et al. 2022) detects a discontinuity;
- **hybrid HLLC-LM/HLLC Riemann solver:** the low-Mach HLLC is used only near
  shocks, by a pressure-ratio switch extended to tolerate negative liquid
  pressures, to cure the 2D carbuncle;
- primitive-variable reconstruction in interfacial cells and characteristic
  variables elsewhere; a relaxed a posteriori MOOD loop recomputes cells with
  NaN or non-admissible states, and their neighbours, with first-order
  Godunov.

Time integration is SSP-RK3, and the face flux uses one quadrature point
(second order), accepted because the target is low
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md), not formal
order. The tests are water–air interface advection (1D and 2D), a liquid–gas
shock tube, a 1 GPa shock on a water–air interface, an underwater-explosion
Riemann problem, the Mach 1.22 air–helium shock–bubble interaction against
Haas & Sturtevant, a Mach 2.4 shock on a water cylinder against Sembian et
al., a Mach 6 shock on two water cylinders and a Mach 10 bubble collapse.
THINC keeps interfaces sharp (2D droplet error 0.0074 against 0.0188 for
TENO5A after ten periods) and reduces MOOD troubled cells; higher-order TENOA
resolves more Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-ups. These are laminar inviscid
interactions: the Rayleigh–Taylor and Kelvin–Helmholtz features of the
Mach 6 case are shock-capturing tests, not the turbulence of
[Rayleigh–Taylor mixing](/benchmarks/rayleigh-taylor-mixing.md) or
[shock-generated turbulence](/results/shock-generated-turbulence.md), and the
paper runs no LES; TENO appears as an [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)
model only through a cited work. The code is the open-source solver MFC.

# Key claims

Notation: $\alpha_k$, $\rho_k$ volume fraction and density of phase $k$;
$\gamma^{\mathrm T}_k$ TENO scale-separation measure, $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k$ its
normalized value, $\delta^{\mathrm T}_k$ cut-off flag ($k$ here the stencil
index); $\beta_{\mathrm A}$ adaptation exponent; $\beta_{\mathrm{TH}}$ THINC
steepness ($\beta_{\mathrm A}$ page-local, the others registry §11; Notation map).

1. The five-equation model of Allaire et al. is preferred to Kapila's, whose $K\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u$ term "can introduce numerical instabilities in scenarios involving strong compression and expansion near material interfaces" and matters less in the extreme compressible problems targeted (§2.1). **Stated**
2. The single-point face flux "can only attain second-order accuracy", accepted because the focus is low-dissipation reconstruction (§3.1). **Stated**
3. TENOA adapts the cut-off as $C_T=10^{-\lfloor\beta_{\mathrm A}\rfloor}$, $\beta_{\mathrm A}=10-5(1-g(m))$, $g(m)=(1-m)^4(1+4m)$: $C_T\to10^{-5}$ at discontinuities ($m\approx1$) and $C_T\approx10^{-10}$ for high-wavenumber fluctuations ($m\approx0$) (§3.2.1, Eq. 47). **Derived**
4. The high-order undivided difference of the scale separation responds to both discontinuities and high-wavenumber fluctuations, the low-order one better detects genuine discontinuities (§3.2.1, citing [30]). **Stated**
5. Every TENO candidate stencil contains at least one upwind point, which gives robustness "even up to the ten-point eighth-order version" (§3.2.1, citing [33]). **Stated**
6. The simplified THINC face values $q^R_{i-1/2}=q_+-q_-(A-CB)$ and $q^L_{i+1/2}=q_++q_-(A-C/B)$, with $A=1/\tanh\beta_{\mathrm{TH}}$, $C=1/\sinh\beta_{\mathrm{TH}}$, follow from conservation of the tanh profile, cost less than the formulae of [51] and [20], and are symmetry-preserving in the sense of [73] (§3.2.2, Eqs. 50–55). **Derived**
7. THINC steepness $\beta_{\mathrm{TH}}=1.5$ "performs well for all cases"; multidimensional weighting by the interface-normal components from Youngs' algorithm (§3.2.2). **Stated**
8. The THINC/TENOA switch (Eqs. 59–61) is parameter-free and uses only $\delta^{\mathrm T}_1$, $\delta^{\mathrm T}_2$ of the TENOA scheme (§3.2.3). **Stated**
9. THINC is active only in flagged cells, "a minority within the entire computational domain", so the hybrids cost little more than TENOA, unlike BVD schemes that compute both candidates everywhere (§3.2.3). **Stated** (no timings printed)
10. The HLLC-LM solver of Fleischmann et al. still oscillates away from shocks with the five-equation model; restricting it to shock regions by the switch of Eqs. 73–76 suppresses the carbuncle without that defect (§3.3, Fig. 16). **Shown**
11. Characteristic-variable reconstruction in interfacial regions causes spurious oscillations for strong shocks on large-density-ratio gas–liquid interfaces; primitive variables there and characteristic variables elsewhere suppress them (§3.4, Fig. 15). **Shown**
12. 1D water–air advection ($N_x=100$, ten periods): the three hybrid schemes keep the interface width, TENO5A-MOOD diffuses it, velocity and pressure errors are negligible (§4.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
13. 2D droplet advection error after ten periods: TENO5A 0.0188, TENO5A-THINC 0.0074, TENO6A-THINC and TENO7A-THINC 0.0073; after two periods 0.0138 against 0.0070 (§4.1, Table 4). **Shown**
14. Volume fractions stay in $[0,1]$ "thanks to the MOOD strategy" (§4.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
15. Liquid–gas shock tube: the THINC hybrids separate the close shock and contact better at $N_x=100$; higher-order TENOA adds little (§4.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
16. 1 GPa shock on a water–air interface ($N_x=200$): all schemes violate the physical admissibility criteria and fall back to first order, yet the hybrids still resolve the interface better (§4.3, Fig. 6). **Shown**
17. Gas–liquid Riemann problem: the hybrids are less diffusive at the interface and sharper at the shock; higher-order TENOA resolves the rarefaction better (§4.4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
18. Air–helium shock–bubble interaction (Mach 1.22, $1600\times200$): negligible troubled cells; thinner interfaces; large-scale structures agree with the Haas–Sturtevant experiment; higher-order TENOA resolves more Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-ups (§4.5, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
19. Mach 2.4 shock on a water cylinder ($1200\times800$): numerical schlieren reproduce the incident, reflected and transmitted shocks, the focused expansion with negative pressures, the "horse-shoe" pattern and the counter-rotating vortex pair of the Sembian et al. shadowgraphs (§4.6, Figs. 12–13). **Shown** (qualitative)
20. Mach 6 shock on two water cylinders ($960\times800$): THINC regions coincide with shocks and contacts, and the hybrids capture Rayleigh–Taylor and Kelvin–Helmholtz features more sharply (§4.7, Figs. 17–20). **Shown**
21. The fraction of MOOD troubled cells is "significantly reduced" when THINC replaces non-TVD TENOA near interfaces (§4.7, Fig. 21). **Shown**
22. Mach 10 bubble collapse ($1200\times600$): no scheme exceeds a troubled-cell frequency of 0.04%; the flow features agree with level-set simulations of ALPACA (§4.8, Figs. 23–25). **Shown**
23. The carbuncle is a multidimensional phenomenon absent in 1D, where the pure HLLC is used (§3.3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. 42** normalizes with $\sum_{l=0}^{l=2}\gamma^{\mathrm T}_l$ (three stencils), while the $K$-th order scheme has stencils $k=0,\dots,K-3$ (Eqs. 33, 48, 49): four for $K=6$, five for $K=7$.
- **§3.2.1** defines $\lfloor\beta_{\mathrm A}\rfloor$ as "the maximum integer smaller than $\beta$".
- **§4.1:** the 2D case "proceeds with a smaller constant time step $\Delta t=5\times10^{-6}$ s", but the 1D case used $2\times10^{-6}$ s. With the water state of Tables 2–3 ($\gamma=6.12$, $p^\infty=3.43\times10^8$ Pa, $\rho=1000$ kg/m³, $p=101325$ Pa, $u=100$ m/s) and $\Delta x=0.01$ m, $(c+|u|)\Delta t/\Delta x$ in $x$ is about 0.77 for the 2D step (0.31 for the 1D one), above the "CFL number less than 0.5" stated in §4 for constant-step runs *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **MOOD** is expanded "multidimensional optimal order detection" (abstract, §1) and "multi-order optimum detection" (§3.5).
- **Eq. 62** prints a non-bold $U_L$; "In contract" for "In contrast" (§3.2.3).
