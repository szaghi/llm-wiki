# Summary

An et al. (2025) apply
[Bayesian calibration](/methods/bayesian-closure-calibration.md) to seven
coefficients of the [Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md)
($C_{b1}$, $C_{b2}$, $\sigma$, $C_{w2}$, $C_{w3}$, $C_{v1}$, $\kappa$) and ask
how the outcome depends on the discretization: a second-order finite-volume
code (NASA CFL3D: MUSCL, Roe flux, approximate factorization) against the
authors' in-house third-order
[discontinuous Galerkin](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md)
RANS solver (Roe flux, $p$-multigrid-preconditioned GMRES, an HWENO limiter
with a modal [troubled-cell indicator](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) on
$\rho\tilde\nu$, see [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md), and a positivity limiter
on $\tilde\nu$). The workflow: uniform priors; Latin-hypercube sampling (72
training and 36 validation runs); a second-order non-intrusive
polynomial-chaos surrogate of wall $C_p$ or $C_f$ at each station; Sobol
indices from its coefficients; 100 000 MCMC samples with the embedded
model-error approach of Sargsyan et al.; posterior predictions with a
decomposition of the variance into model error, posterior uncertainty and
surrogate error ([model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md));
and maximum a posteriori (MAP) coefficients.

The calibration flows are the Driver–Seegmiller
[backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md) ($Re_H=36\,000$,
experiment) and the [Gaussian bump](/benchmarks/gaussian-bump.md)
($Re_L=10^6$, DNS); calibrating each on $C_p$ and on $C_f$ under each scheme
gives eight MAP sets. $\kappa$ dominates the variance everywhere,
insensitive to the scheme; the Sobol index of $C_{v1}$, which the authors
tie to the Reynolds shear stress, rises "approximately 120%" under DG;
$C_{b2}$, $\sigma$, $C_{w3}$ become sensitive in the curved bump flow, more
so under DG. The authors relate the sensitivities to physical mechanisms as
hypotheses, citing [skin-friction decompositions](/definitions/skin-friction-decomposition.md)
and [pressure-gradient boundary layers](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md).
A weighted combination gives one "modified SA model tailored for DG
schemes" ($C_{b2}=0.592$, $\sigma=0.806$, $C_{w3}=2.641$, $C_{v1}=10.3$,
$\kappa=0.39$, $C_{b1}=0.1186$ in separation and 0.1382 in boundary
layers), tested on the bump at $Re_L=2\times10^6$ (26.44% error reduction in
the crest transition region), the HiFi-TURB-DLR rounded step (about 25%
overall $C_f$ error reduction; a [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
without separation) and the [NASA wall-mounted hump](/benchmarks/nasa-wall-mounted-hump.md)
(limited $C_f$ gains). DG on grids 0.2–0.5 the size of the finite-volume
grids matches or beats them, except near the step corner and in separation,
where the authors hypothesize that the lower
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) of DG exposes
model error. The eddy viscosity enters through the
[Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md); the
introduction cites field-inversion and symbolic-regression corrections
([field inversion](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md)) without
content here. All computations are 2D steady RANS.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol\theta$ the uncertain coefficients (the
source's $\boldsymbol\lambda$, registry §7); $\boldsymbol z$, $\varphi_k$,
$a_k$ the polynomial-chaos germ, basis and coefficients, and
$\boldsymbol z_{\mathrm e}$, $a_j$ the embedded-error germ and magnitudes;
$N_{\mathrm{PC}}$ the number of expansion terms (the source's $N_p$);
$\mathcal S_i$ the first-order Sobol index; $s^2_{\mathrm{emb}}$,
$s^2_{pp}$, $s^2_s$, $s^2_p$ model-error, posterior-uncertainty,
surrogate-error and total posterior variances; $s_{\mathrm m}$,
$s_{\mathrm e}$ the discrepancy and measurement standard deviations and
$s_y$ that of the output (the symbols of
[Bayesian calibration](/methods/bayesian-closure-calibration.md)); $\boldsymbol\delta$ the
model discrepancy (registry §7); $S^{\mathrm{tc}}_k$ the troubled-cell
indicator of Eq. (A.1).

1. The SA coefficients $C_{b1}$, $C_{b2}$, $\sigma$, $C_{w2}$, $C_{w3}$, $C_{v1}$, $\kappa$ are selected as "more sensitive to complex flow structures"; the first three are said to be fixed from free shear flow, the others from the zero-pressure-gradient flat-plate boundary layer (§3.1, Table 1, citing [32, 33]). **Stated**
2. Priors are uniform: $\sigma\in[0.6,1]$, $C_{w2}$, $C_{w3}$, $C_{v1}$, $\kappa$ at $\pm50\%$ and $C_{b1}$, $C_{b2}$ at $\pm25\%$ of the original value, "to ensure that non-physical solutions do not occur" (§3.1, Table 1). **Stated**
3. With 7 parameters and expansion order 2, Eq. (17), $N_{\mathrm{PC}}=(n+p)!/(n!\,p!)$, gives 36 terms; an oversampling ratio 2 gives 72 training samples, plus 36 validation samples, drawn by Latin hypercube (§3.1). **Stated**
4. On the step the DG solution on a grid "only 0.21 times the size of the FVM grid" ($249\times81$ against $436\times225$) gives results "comparable to or even better than FVM": the finite volumes are better for $C_p$ before the step and in the separation zone, DG after reattachment, especially in $C_f$ (§3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
5. Both schemes give a low-pressure region "more extensive than in the experiment" and overestimate $C_f$ before the step and in the separation zone (DG slightly more), and both predict a separation-zone size in good agreement with the experiment (§3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
6. The authors hypothesize that the higher accuracy and lower dissipation of the high-order scheme "may amplify" errors of the SA model in the separated shear layer, outside its calibration (§3.1). **Stated** (hypothesis)
7. The DG prior samples have smaller variance against the experiment for $0<x/H<3$ and are "significantly better" for $C_p$ for $x/H>3$; DG shows higher sensitivity to parameter variations (§3.1, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
8. A discontinuity of the DG solution near the step corner is compared with NASA TMR results, where CFL3D is continuous and FUN3D "similarly exhibits discontinuities"; the authors attribute it to the geometric corner and call for more robust limiters (§3.1). **Shown** / **Stated**
9. Regardless of scheme or calibration quantity, $\kappa$, $C_{b1}$, $C_{v1}$ contribute most to the variance on the step (§3.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
10. Under finite volumes $C_{v1}$ becomes the second most influential parameter for $C_f$ from reattachment on, while the Sobol index of $C_{b1}$ "generally decreases to a very small value"; two hypotheses: $C_p$ depends on velocity and $C_f$ on velocity gradients, and $C_f$ depends on the Reynolds shear stress to which $C_{v1}$ is tied (§3.1, Fig. 9, citing Atzori et al. [35] and Renard & Deck [36]). **Shown** (indices) / **Stated** (hypotheses)
11. Under DG, $\kappa$ "remains the dominant contributor", largely unaffected by the scheme; the contribution of $C_{b1}$ falls (particularly for $C_p$); that of $C_{v1}$ is "approximately 120% higher than in the FVM scheme", mainly at reattachment and in the recovery region (§3.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
12. The surrogate's relative error is below 20% and its maximum absolute error does not exceed 0.03 for $C_p$ in both schemes; for $C_f$ it is below 10% in most regions but approaches or exceeds 20% at $x/H\approx0$ and 7, reaching 80% at $x/H\approx0$, attributed to $C_f\approx0$ there (§3.1, Eq. 28, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. Posterior (100 000 MCMC samples, means with 90% intervals): calibrated on $C_p$ the DG posterior mean is worse than the finite volumes only near $x/H\approx0$; calibrated on $C_f$ DG gives more accurate posteriors with wider intervals covering the data, while the finite volumes predict too high $C_f$ at the tail of the recovery region (§3.1, Fig. 11). **Shown**
14. The decomposition $s^2_p=s^2_{\mathrm{emb}}+s^2_{pp}+s^2_s$ shows model error "considerable across all positions, especially in the separation region", posterior uncertainty concentrated near separation, and surrogate error the smallest; for $C_f$ calibration all DG components are "significantly smaller" than the finite-volume ones except in parts of the separation zone (§3.1, Eqs. 29–30, Fig. 12). **Shown**
15. The posterior PDFs of the 3–4 most sensitive parameters are essentially unimodal; $C_{b1}$ is flat under finite volumes and unimodal under DG; $C_{w3}$ under finite volumes with $C_p$ has negligible sensitivity, no consistent trend and two minor secondary peaks, so its inference "fails to yield reliable or significant results" (§3.1, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
16. Gaussian bump ($M=0.2$, $Re_L=10^6$): DG on $\approx201\times81$ points ($y_1^+\approx5$) beats the finite volumes on $401\times161$ ($y_1^+\approx1$) at the crest and in the small separation, "despite … some errors in the inlet conditions" (DG inflow from a precursor flat plate to $Re_\theta\approx530$) (§3.2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
17. On the bump $C_{b2}$, $\sigma$, $C_{w3}$, insensitive on the step, show "a substantial increase in sensitivity", higher under DG; the authors speculate they are tied to first-order derivatives whose computation changes under streamline curvature and under high-order discretization (§3.2, Fig. 17). **Shown** (indices) / **Stated** (attribution)
18. The sensitivity of $C_{v1}$ on the bump decreases in the favourable-pressure-gradient region (attributed to relaminarization weakening the Reynolds shear stress), rises sharply in the adverse gradient and separation, drops at reattachment and rises again (§3.2, Fig. 17). **Shown** / **Stated** (attribution)
19. Bump posterior: the DG $C_p$ at the crest is "almost completely overlapping with DNS", the DG $C_f$ in the separation zone shows "nearly no discrepancy from DNS"; the $C_f$ intervals are much wider than the $C_p$ ones in both flows (§3.2, Fig. 18). **Shown**
20. Table 3 lists eight MAP sets (2 flows × 2 schemes × $C_p$/$C_f$) with an attribution of each parameter's influencing factors and positions (§3.3, Table 3). **Shown**
21. Selection rules for the final set: $C_{b2}$, $\sigma$, $C_{w3}$, $C_{v1}$ and $\kappa$ follow the high-order results ($C_{b2}$ smaller, $\sigma$ and $C_{w3}$ larger; $\kappa$ "consistently deviating below normative values"), $C_{b1}$ prioritises the finite volumes, $C_{w2}$ (negligible sensitivity) is unchanged; the offsets are weighted by baseline error and sensitivity, then "validated and fine-tuned" in the two calibration cases (§3.3, Table 4). **Stated** (weights not given)
22. Final coefficients (Table 4): $C_{b2}=0.592$, $\sigma=0.806$, $C_{w3}=2.641$, $C_{v1}=10.3$, $\kappa=0.39$, $C_{b1}=0.1186$ (separation) and 0.1382 (boundary layer), against the originals 0.622, 0.667, 2, 7.1, 0.41, 0.1355; the switching between the two $C_{b1}$ values is not specified (§3.3, Table 4). **Stated**
23. Bump at $Re_L=2\times10^6$: the baseline SA "nearly eliminate[s]" the massive separation behind the bump; the MAP set gives limited improvement in the separation zone but "near-perfect alignment with DNS" in the crest transition region, "yielding 26.44% error reduction versus baseline" (§4.1, Figs. 20–21). **Shown**
24. HiFi-TURB-DLR rounded step (no separation): baseline finite volumes and DG are close; the modified model's $C_f$ near the pressure-gradient transition is "markedly closer" to DNS, "approximately 25% overall error reduction" (§4.2, Figs. 23–24). **Shown**
25. NASA wall-mounted hump: baseline DG $C_f$ is better than the finite volumes; the modified model gives "limited improvements" overall, but DG with the MAP set improves $C_p$ at the crest transitions and in the separation zone (§4.3, Figs. 26–27). **Shown**
26. "A modified SA model tailored for DG schemes is proposed", and the high-order DG scheme "demonstrates superior accuracy relative to the FVM scheme" for the baseline SA model on the step and the bump (§5). **Stated** (summary)
27. DG RANS with SA needs limiting: the weak solution for $\tilde\nu$ has discontinuous first derivatives at the turbulent/non-turbulent edge, oscillatory high-order solutions produce negative $\tilde\nu$ and the solver diverges; an HWENO limiter with a $\rho\tilde\nu$-based modal troubled-cell indicator and a positivity limiter are used (Appendix, Eqs. A.1–A.2, citing [12, 41, 42]). **Stated**
28. Future work: $k$-$\sqrt kL$, SST and Reynolds-stress models in the DG solver, 3D capability, and Bayesian calibration of multi-equation models (§5). **Stated**

The calibration in registry notation (Eqs. 19–26): $p(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbb D)\propto p(\mathbb D\mid\boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)$
with data $y^{(i)}=f(\boldsymbol\theta,x^{(i)})+\delta(x^{(i)})+\epsilon_i$,
$\delta\sim\mathcal N(0,s_{\mathrm m}^2)$, $\epsilon_i\sim\mathcal N(0,s_{\mathrm e}^2)$;
in the embedded form $\boldsymbol\theta_{\mathrm{emb}}=\boldsymbol\theta+\mathrm{diag}(a_1,\dots,a_J)\boldsymbol z_{\mathrm e}$
the likelihood is Gaussian with mean $\mu_i$ and variance
$s^2_{\mathrm{emb},i}+s_{\mathrm e}^2$, with $a_j$ the calibrated
embedded-error magnitudes (which coefficients carry embedded error is not
stated). Sobol indices
$\mathcal S_i\approx\sum_ja^2_{k_j(i)}\lVert\varphi_{k_j(i)}\rVert^2/s_y^2$
(Eq. 18). The positivity limiter (Eq. A.2) rescales the polynomial about the
cell mean so that $\tilde\nu$ at the quadrature points stays non-negative.
Eq. (10) prints the Spalart–Shur rotation–curvature extension (SARC,
origin ref. [6]) as the prevalent engineering variant: the production is
multiplied by $f_{r1}=(1+c_{r1})\,\frac{2r^*}{r^*+1}\,[1-c_{r3}\arctan(c_{r2}\tilde r)]-c_{r1}$
with $c_{r1}=1$, $c_{r2}=12$, $c_{r3}=0.6$ (§2.1). The paper does not state
that SARC is used in any of its computations, all of which are labelled
SA.
