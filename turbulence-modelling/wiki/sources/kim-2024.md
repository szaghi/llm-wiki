# Summary

Kim et al. (2024) build pointwise fully connected
[neural-network subgrid-scale models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md) for
LES of the [flow over a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md).
This flow combines boundary-layer development, separation, shear-layer roll-up and
a turbulent wake. The network maps the resolved strain rate (6 components) or the
resolved velocity gradient (9 components) at one point to the six components of
the [subgrid-scale stress](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md). Training data
are box-filtered [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) at $Re_D=3900$,
computed with an [immersed boundary method](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md)
and a second-order central scheme that conserves kinetic energy and has no
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md), see
[kinetic-energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md).

The models with grid-filtered inputs follow Park & Choi (2021). A second
dataset is obtained by test filtering the same DNS in the span. Fed by
concatenation into one network, the grid- and test-filtered inputs fail. The
paper's new "fusion" architecture passes them through two separate branches and
subtracts the branch outputs before the output layer. The subtraction is
motivated by the Germano identity of the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md).
[A priori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md), every network
beats the dynamic Smagorinsky model on the mean SGS shear stress, dissipation and
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md). The exception is the
concatenated models, which give the wrong sign in the near shear layers.
A posteriori, at the training grid, the grid-filtered and fusion models beat the
dynamic [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md). On untrained coarser
and finer grids, and at $Re_D=5000$ and $10\,000$, only the fusion models keep
their accuracy. An appendix repeats the gain on [channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
at $Re_\tau=178$. A network step costs 2–2.5 times a dynamic-Smagorinsky step.
A coarse-grid fusion LES is still cheaper than a fine-grid dynamic-Smagorinsky
LES of similar accuracy.

The introduction recalls the [similarity](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md),
[gradient](/methods/gradient-model.md) and [eddy-viscosity](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
families. The paper estimates the finite-difference error of the inputs, a
question of [discretization-consistent closure](/definitions/discretization-consistent-closure.md).
It concludes that its a priori-trained models do not remove the a priori/a
posteriori inconsistency, and that [model-consistent](/methods/model-consistent-training.md)
or [reinforcement-learning](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md) training
needs targets that are unknown for complex geometries.

# Key claims

1. Network SGS models (G-SR, G-VG, T-SR-FU, T-VG-FU) trained on filtered DNS at $Re_D=3900$ beat the dynamic Smagorinsky model (DSM) a posteriori at the training resolution: $L_r/D=1.51$, 1.56, 1.59, 1.56 against DSM 1.32 and DNS 1.56; $-\langle C_{pb}\rangle=0.90$, 0.88, 0.88, 0.86 against DSM 0.94 and DNS 0.87 (Table 3, §4.2.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
2. Concatenating grid- and test-filtered inputs in one network without fusion (T-SR-nFU, T-VG-nFU) degrades the predictions, $L_r/D=0.79$ and 1.28 against DNS 1.56; T-SR-nFU is worse than no SGS model ($L_r/D=1.25$) (Table 3, §4.2.1). **Shown**
3. A priori, the non-fused models give an SGS shear stress with negative and positive peaks in the lower and upper shear layers at $x/D=1.06$, opposite to the filtered DNS, and a wrong-signed SGS dissipation; this explains the early shear-layer transition of their LES through weak SGS dissipation (§4.1, Fig. 6; §4.2.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
4. A priori, all network architectures predict the mean SGS shear stress, dissipation and backscatter better than DSM at $x/D=1.54$ and 2.02; velocity-gradient inputs do better than strain-rate inputs a priori; DSM backscatter is zero (§4.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
5. A better a priori prediction does not guarantee a better a posteriori performance (citing Park et al. 2006; Gamahara & Hattori 2017; Beck et al. 2019; Park & Choi 2021); one cause is error accumulation corrupting the resolved scales (citing Duraisamy 2021) (§4.1). **Stated**
6. Robustness test: adding Gaussian noise with the standard deviation of the training inputs to $\bar S_{ij}$ and $\hat{\bar S}_{ij}$ (Eq. 4.1) changes $\tau_{xx}$, $\tau_{yy}$, $\tau_{xy}$ more for G-SR and T-SR-nFU than for T-SR-FU, which is the most robust (§4.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
7. On an untrained coarser grid (LES3900c, $353\times213\times64$), G-SR and G-VG degrade ($L_r/D=1.16$, 1.07) while T-SR-FU and T-VG-FU stay accurate (1.55, 1.49; DNS 1.56); DSM gives 0.80 and no model 0.74 (Table 3, §4.2.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
8. On an untrained finer grid (LES3900f, $545\times301\times64$), DSM agrees well with DNS ($L_r/D=1.45$), T-SR-FU and T-VG-FU stay good (1.62, 1.57), G-SR and G-VG are worse (1.22, 1.39) (Table 3, §4.2.2). **Shown**
9. Building several filtered datasets of different filter sizes and training a fused network gives successful network-based LES on grids that differ from the training grid, consistent with Park & Choi (2021) (§4.2.2). **Shown**
10. Discretization-error estimate after Sirignano et al. (2020): the averaged relative error $\langle|\delta(\partial_j\bar u_i)|\rangle/\langle|\partial_j\bar u_i|_{\mathrm{DNS}}\rangle$ is 0.5690, 0.5455, 0.5320, 0.5136, 0.4986 for LES3900ccc, cc, c, base and f; the authors call the finite-difference errors "not so significant" and write that the network models "may not suffer" from them (Table 4, Eqs. 4.2–4.3, p. A6-21). **Shown** (values); the conclusion is the authors' wording
11. Fusion models trained at $Re_D=3900$ predict $Re_D=5000$ (grid $545\times301\times64$) in good agreement with DNS: $L_r/D=1.45$ (T-SR-FU), 1.47 (T-VG-FU), DSM 1.35, DNS 1.39 (Table 3, §4.2.3, Fig. 12). **Shown**
12. At $Re_D=10\,000$ ($1025\times649\times64$) T-SR-FU gives $L_r/D=0.85$, $-C_{pb}=1.17$, $C_D=1.10$ against the experiment of Dong et al. (2006), 0.82, 1.13, 1.14; T-VG-FU gives $L_r/D=0.70$, DSM 0.62, no model 0.51 (Table 3, §4.2.3, Fig. 13). **Shown**
13. In the shear-layer transition regime ($1000<Re_D<200\,000$) the Strouhal number, base-pressure coefficient and recirculation length decrease and the Reynolds stress increases with $Re_D$ (citing Williamson 1996), trends visible in Table 3 (§4.2.3). **Stated**
14. The second-order central energy-conserving scheme has no numerical dissipation; LES without SGS model can be stable but inaccurate, and becomes unstable on very coarse grids (§2.1). **Stated**
15. The central scheme produces spurious oscillations near the front of the cylinder; a hybrid QUICK ($x/D<-0.25$) plus central scheme removes them without changing the wake at this Reynolds number; at much higher Reynolds numbers a hybrid scheme may be needed (§4.2.1, Fig. 9 inset). **Shown**
16. More hidden layers or nodes (3 and 256) do not improve the non-fused models; G-SR needs at least 2 layers of 128 nodes; 2 layers of 64 nodes before fusion suffice for T-SR-FU (§3.2, App. B, Fig. 16). **Shown**
17. Training-data size: $N_t=13$, 25, 50 snapshots and $N_s=2$, 4, 8 spanwise planes give very small differences in the a priori mean SGS shear stress; a smaller extraction box ($-1\le x/D\le4$, $-1\le y/D\le1$) is too small for a posteriori accuracy, a larger one ($-1\le x/D\le8$, $-2\le y/D\le2$) changes little (App. A, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
18. Spanwise resolutions $N_z=48$, 64, 80 give similar T-SR-FU results ($N_z=64$ sufficient); DSM at G-64 deviates non-negligibly from the filtered DNS (App. C, Fig. 17). **Shown**
19. On still coarser grids (Gcc-64, Gccc-64) LES with T-SR-FU, DSM and no model all diverge; T-SR-FU runs when the SGS stresses are clipped to zero wherever backscatter occurs, Gcc-64 then resembling Gc-64 while Gccc-64 is not good (App. C). **Shown**
20. The LES5000 and LES10000 grids are marginal for second-order statistics: coarser grids give worse predictions (App. C, Fig. 18). **Shown**
21. Channel flow at $Re_\tau=178$: T-SR-FU (inputs in wall units, with fusion) beats G-SR and DSM on an untrained coarser grid ($12\times49\times12$) and matches a G-SR trained on two filtered-DNS datasets ($16\times49\times16$, $8\times49\times8$) as in Park & Choi (2021) (App. D, Fig. 19). **Shown**
22. Cost (18 cores, Intel i9-9980X, averaged over 10 steps): at LES3900 the SGS-stress evaluation costs about 10 times (G-SR) and 6.5 times (T-SR-FU) that of DSM, a full time step about 2.5 and 2 times (Table 6, App. E). **Shown**
23. LES3900c with T-SR-FU (2.113 s per step) gives results very similar to LES3900f with DSM (2.986 s per step), with about half the grid points, so network models need not raise the overall cost (App. E). **Shown**
24. Inputs and outputs are normalized by $U$ and $D$, because wall units are improper for a wake and mean/rms normalization needs a priori knowledge at untrained Reynolds numbers (§3.3). **Stated**
25. The present models do not overcome the a priori/a posteriori inconsistency; adjoint-based (Sirignano et al. 2020) and reinforcement-learning (Kim et al. 2022) approaches need targets that are unknown a priori for complex geometries (§5). **Stated**

The fusion network (Eq. 3.3): two branches of two hidden layers of 64 nodes
(batch normalization, ReLU) take the grid-filtered inputs $\bar q_i$ and the
test-filtered inputs $\hat{\bar q}_i$; their outputs are subtracted and mapped
linearly to the six stress components,
$$h^{(4)}_k=h^{(3)}_{1,k}-h^{(3)}_{2,k}\ (k=1,\dots,64),\qquad \tau_{l}=\sum_{k=1}^{64}W^{(4)(5)}_{kl}h^{(4)}_k+b^{(5)}_l\ (l=1,\dots,6),$$
trained on the mean-squared error against the filtered DNS (Eq. 3.2) by
stochastic gradient descent (learning rate 0.01) with early stopping. Backscatter
is diagnosed as $\Pi^-=\tfrac12(\Pi-|\Pi|)$ with $\Pi=-\tau_{ij}\bar S_{ij}$
(§4.1).

# Printed slips

- **Table 4 caption:** "LES390ccc (Gccc-64)" for LES3900ccc, as printed in the table body.
- **Appendix D:** "The training data are obtained for the grids of 16 × 97 × 16 by filtering the DNS data", against "training data (16 × 49 × 16)" a few lines later and "The training data (fDNS) have the grids of 16 × 49 × 16" in the Fig. 19 caption.
- **Fusion architecture:** described as "two and one hidden layers before and after fusion" (§3.2, Fig. 5b caption, App. B), while Eq. (3.3) has after fusion only the subtraction layer $h^{(4)}$, with no weights or activation, followed by the linear output layer.
- Typos: "Inter(R) Core(TM) i9-9980X" (App. E); "applied to a a posteriori test" (§4.2.1); "may not suffer form" (§4.2.2).
