# Summary

Ims & Wang (2023) develop and compare three per-cell error indicators for
h-[mesh adaptation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md) at fixed polynomial
degree $N$ of [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) (no subgrid
model) with the [flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md)
(FR/CPR) method of the [hpMusic](/software/hpmusic.md) solver. They argue
that output-adjoint adaptation, the most efficient approach for steady
RANS, is "simply impractical" for LES (reverse-time adjoints, adaptation
at several times, chaos pervading the mean quantities), and that h-adaptation
alone has more potential than p-adaptation alone. The indicators are: the
unsteady residual evaluated one degree higher, the difference between the
FR/CPR residual of the solution projected to degree $N+1$ and the
projection of the degree-$N$ residual, an unsteady extension of the
Gao–Wang steady residual criterion; the Persson–Peraire modal smoothness
indicator, a [shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) repurposed
("spectral decay indicator" of Naddei et al.); and a variant of the
Toosi–Larsson small-scale-energy indicator recast in the computational
coordinates of curved high-order cells, a directional high-pass
differential filter of width $1/(N+1)$ in reference space. The top 20% of
cells, plus those forced by a one-level size constraint and a clumping
constraint, are split isotropically following the parent curvature, with
mortar faces at hanging nodes.

On the T106c ($N=2$, $Re_{is}=80\,000$, against an hpMusic DNS) and T106a
($N=1$, $Re_{is}=60\,000$, against an $N=4$ reference) low-pressure
[turbine cascades](/benchmarks/turbine-cascade.md), all three indicators
improve the solution. The smoothness indicator concentrates on the
boundary layers of both surfaces and converges slowest, on T106a no faster
than random refinement; the small-scale-energy indicator (wake-focused) and
the residual indicator (intermediate) converge nearly in lockstep, the
former slightly ahead. Refinement follows the edge of the suction-side
[separation bubble](/definitions/laminar-separation-bubble.md) into the
wake. The evidence is visual (entropy snapshots, marking maps, the $R_{12}$
field and kinetic-energy spectra at a wake point): no error norm, cell
count or cost is reported. The authors recommend the small-scale-energy
indicator when vortex activity matters most and the residual indicator as
easier to implement and "may actually be the more truthful estimate of the
physics errors".

# Key claims

The polynomial degree is $N$ (the source's $p$); the three indicators are
written $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$,
$\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ (the source's ${}^{\mathbf{unStd}}\mathcal E$,
${}^{\mathbf{smth}}\mathcal E$, ${}^{\mathbf{T.L.err}}\mathcal E$), the
projection onto degree $N$ is $\Pi_N$, the spatial residual operator
$\mathrm{Res}_N$, the pointwise error quantity $\epsilon_{i,j}$ (page-local)
and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the time (or space–time) average.

1. For unsteady LES, output-adjoint adaptation is "simply impractical": the adjoint must be solved in reverse time, the mesh should be adapted at different times, and chaos makes instantaneous quantities irregular and pervades the means (§1). **Stated**
2. For steady RANS, output-adjoint adaptation "appear[s] to be the most efficient" for mesh-independent force coefficients, saving "orders of magnitude in CPU time" in fixed-grid comparisons (§1, citing others). **Stated**
3. h-adaptation alone has more potential than p-adaptation alone, which may fail on shocks, shear layers and contact discontinuities; hp is "the ultimate approach" (§1). **Stated** (not tested)
4. The LES is implicit ("we apply no subgrid scale model"): FR/CPR with BR2 gradients, an accuracy-preserving limiter in severely under-resolved regions, SSP-RK3 or the A-stable BDF2OPT (§2). **Stated**
5. The residual indicator is $\epsilon\equiv\widetilde{\mathrm{Res}}_{N+1}(\{Q_N\})=-(\mathrm{Res}_{N+1}(\{Q_N\})-\mathrm{Res}_N(\{Q_N\}))$, obtained by subtracting the vanishing unsteady residual $\widetilde{\mathrm{Res}}_N(\{Q_N\})=0$ (Eqs. 8–10), computed as $\mathrm{Res}_{N+1}(\Pi_{N+1}[\{Q_N\}])-\Pi_{N+1}[\mathrm{Res}_N(\{Q_N\})]$ (Eq. 12). **Derived**
6. The alternative ODE-error-control route gives the error generation rate with $\mathrm{Res}_{N+1}(\{Q_{N+1}\})$ in place of $\mathrm{Res}_{N+1}(\{Q_N\})$ (Eq. 11); the computable quantity is the hybrid of Eq. (12) (§3.1). **Derived**
7. The absolute value in the cell average (Eq. 13) keeps per-cell errors positive, so the domain means used to normalize $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ (Eq. 14) are non-zero (§3.1). **Stated**
8. $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ is the Persson–Peraire smoothness indicator, the relative squared difference between $Q_N$ and $\Pi_N[\Pi_{N-1}[Q_N]]$ averaged over $\rho$, momentum and $E$ (Eqs. 15–17), called the "spectral decay indicator" by Naddei et al., who judged it worth exploring for unsteady turbulent simulation (§3.2). **Stated**
9. Division by zero in Eq. (15) inflates values near stagnation points or at solver divergence; the cell integration of Eq. (17) smooths the infinities (§3.2). **Stated**
10. Transforming the Toosi–Larsson directional second derivative to a curved cell's reference coordinate gives $\mathcal D^2_{\hat\xi}(\boldsymbol u)=s_\xi^{-2}\big(-\partial_\xi s_\xi\,\mathcal D_{\hat\xi}(\boldsymbol u)+\partial^2_\xi\boldsymbol u\big)$, $s_\xi=\lVert\partial\boldsymbol x/\partial\xi\rVert$ (Eqs. 19–20); the authors drop this route and define the filter directly in reference space, $\boldsymbol u^*_\xi=-\tfrac{h_\xi^2}{4}\partial^2_\xi\boldsymbol u$, $A_\xi=\sqrt{\langle\boldsymbol u^*_\xi\cdot\boldsymbol u^*_\xi\rangle}$, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}_i=\sqrt{\max_\xi\langle\mathcal E_{\xi;i}\rangle}$ (Eqs. 21–22), because the polynomials live there and the scale term is near-constant unless the mesh is unevenly curved (§3.3). **Derived**
11. With $h_\xi=1/(N+1)$ in reference space the physical filter width self-adjusts to the local cell size and curvature (§3.3). **Stated**
12. At mortar faces in 3-D, the geometry vectors must be taken from the coarse side if its polynomial is linear; otherwise taking them from the fine side is "possible and advisable", else the calculation is unstable through under-integration of the coarse side (§4). **Stated** (no data shown)
13. On T106c ($N=2$) all three indicators improve the vortex resolution and refine roughly the same places, near the blade surface and in the active wake (§5.1, Figs. 5–8). **Shown**
14. Each indicator gets the same budget (top 20% plus constraint extras); differences in marked area reflect a preference for physically larger cells (§5.1, Figs. 9–12). **Shown**
15. $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ favours small boundary-layer cells on both surfaces, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ large wake cells (refining once even at the domain exit), $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ lies between (§5.1). **Shown**
16. No indicator refines much at the leading edge, possibly because the laminar flow is already resolved there (§5.1). **Shown** (interpretation **Stated**)
17. On T106c, judged on $R_{12}$ against the DNS, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ lags at both refinements; $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ and $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ converge "nearly in lockstep", $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ slightly better near the most downstream point and the trailing upper surface (§5.1, Fig. 13). **Shown** (visual)
18. In the kinetic-energy spectrum at a wake monitor point, the high-frequency part rises toward the DNS at each refinement while the low-frequency peaks are aligned already on the base mesh; $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ is slowest, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ slightly ahead of $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ (§5.1, Figs. 14–16). **Shown** (visual)
19. T106a (coarser start mesh, $N=1$, lower $Re$) is "less challenging for the flow solver but more challenging for the error indicators", with unresolved laminar flow; the ranking is that of T106c (§5.2, Figs. 18–30). **Shown**
20. Random refinement on T106a with the same budget and constraints: hanging-node boundaries at random places do not disturb the flow, and random refinement improves the resolution "at a pace almost equal to" $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ (§5.2.1, Figs. 31–34). **Shown**
21. Random refinement moves the low-frequency spectral peaks toward the reference faster than $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ and $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$, read as those indicators de-emphasizing laminar convergence; some randomness may help a feature-focused indicator (§5.2.1). **Shown** (interpretation **Stated**)
22. The faster indicators are the more vortex-sensitive ones ($\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ most, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ second), and vortex sensitivity is argued to drive convergence (§5.3). **Stated**
23. $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ is "literally the instantaneous flaw in the simulated flow field", a map of where the Navier–Stokes equations are not followed; $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ maps motion too intricate to resolve; the sharp gradients of $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ are only "a warning that poor physics may be occurring" (§5.3). **Stated**
24. $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{smth}}$ needs only a per-cell projection, $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ the projection plus the degree-$(N+1)$ residual, "freely available" in an hp-aware solver; $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ is hard to make efficient and coordinate-generic across MPI, threads and SIMD (§5.3, §6). **Stated** (no timings)
25. $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{TL}}$ and $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{res}}$ are "two capable error indicators for LES simulations with the FR/CPR formulation", the former better when vortex activity matters most, the latter easier and possibly "the more truthful estimate of the physics errors" (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **§3.1, after Eq. (12):** "It is the unsteady residual from (10) with characteristics similar to the differential error generation rate from equation (12)"; the error generation rate is defined in Eq. (11).
- **Eq. (22) against Eq. (21c):** the first bullet prints "$\epsilon_{\xi;i,j}=A_\xi^2=\vec u^*_{\xi;i,j}\cdot\vec u^*_{\xi;i,j}$", a pointwise product, while Eq. (21c) defines $A_\xi^2$ as the averaged $\langle\vec u^*_\xi\cdot\vec u^*_\xi\rangle$; the averages are applied again in the next two bullets.
- **$\tilde\epsilon$** denotes both the error generation rate (Eq. 11) and the three-way average (Eq. 16).
- **§5.2 against Fig. 21:** "Figs. 19, 20, and 21 show the corresponding wake after triple h-refinement"; the captions of Figs. 19 and 20 say "after the third refinement", that of Fig. 21 "after the second refinement".
- **Round counts:** §5.1 describes refining "firstly ... secondly ... thirdly (as reference only)", the Fig. 9 caption "refinement round #1, #2, #3", and §5.2 says T106a used "three rounds" "same as for T106c".
- **Names and typos:** "Toosi and Larson" (§1, §3.3) against "S. Toosi, J. Larsson" in refs. [23], [37]; "spacial" (§3.1, §5.3); "handing node" (§4); "gleamed" (§5.1); "The later" (§5.3); "Pn+1" (§5.3) against $p+1$ elsewhere.
