# Summary

Mays et al. (2023) assess the scale-resolving hybrid (SRH) model, a
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) of the hybrid
temporal-LES type (credited to Manceau (2018) in the abstract and to
Duffal, De Laage de Meux and Manceau (2019) in the introduction), on the
[flow past a smooth circular cylinder](/benchmarks/circular-cylinder-wake.md)
through the drag crisis, $Re_D=2.5$, $3.8$, $5.3$ and $7.2\times10^5$. The
model multiplies the dissipation of the modelled turbulent kinetic energy
by a factor $\psi_H\ge1$ obtained by formally applying a temporal filter;
$\psi_H$ depends on a theoretical modelled-to-total energy ratio $r_K$
computed from a filtered CFL number built on cell size, time step and the
time-mean total kinetic energy $k_{\mathrm{tot}}=\langle k_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle+k_{\mathrm{res}}$,
so that, unlike [DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md), it responds to
the resolved content. A shielding function of the DDES form, but with a much
thinner shielded zone, enforces URANS near the wall. The base model is the
three-equation Lag elliptic-blending [$k$-$\varepsilon$ model](/methods/k-epsilon-model.md)
of Lardeau and Billard (2016), built by analogy with a
[Reynolds-stress model](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md), in Simcenter
STAR-CCM+ 2022.1 (incompressible segregated finite volume, "All $y^+$"
[wall treatment](/methods/rans-wall-function.md)), with a hybrid
upwind/bounded-central convective scheme blended by the shielding function
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)).

On meshes of 7.65–16.64 million cells, about five times fewer than the
reference [WALE](/methods/wale-model.md) LES of Rodríguez et al. (2015),
SRH reproduces the mean drag through the crisis, the Strouhal number, the
recirculation length $L_r$ and the one-bubble asymmetric state at
$2.5\times10^5$; URANS with the same base model keeps the shear layers
stable through excess eddy viscosity and misses the crisis. On the coarsest
grid an in-house WALE LES over-predicts the drag ($C_D=1.247$) while SRH
gives 0.743 (reference 0.833). The sizes of the
[laminar separation bubbles](/definitions/laminar-separation-bubble.md),
the mean lift and the force fluctuations are less well captured. The
authors check the two criteria of [Heinz (2020)](/sources/heinz-2020.md):
the resolved fraction grows under refinement at roughly constant total
energy, and the predicted $r_K$ matches the realised energy ratio in
turbulent regions; the realised ratio flags under-resolved regions a
posteriori and $r_K$ from a RANS initialisation gives an a priori
resolution measure (a resolution-adequacy use of
[a priori and a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)).
Known weaknesses are cited: [log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md)
in channel flow and a spurious reduction of the reduced-stress function in
the free stream. The related hybrid families [PANS](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md)
and RANS/[VMS](/methods/variational-multiscale-method.md)-LES, and earlier
[Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) cylinder LES, are cited in
the review of prior work.

# Key claims

The SRH factor, energy ratio, calibration constant, filtered CFL number and
shielding function are written $\psi_H$, $r_K$, $\beta_\psi$,
$\mathrm{CFL}_H$, $f_s$ (page-local); the instantaneous modelled energy and
dissipation are $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgs}}$, the
time-averaged modelled, resolved and total energies $\langle k_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle$,
$k_{\mathrm{res}}$, $k_{\mathrm{tot}}$; the reduced-stress function of the
Lag EB model is $\varphi_{\mathrm L}$ and its elliptic-blending variable
$\alpha_{\mathrm{EB}}$ (page-local).

1. The SRH model modifies the URANS $k$ equation by a multiplicative factor $\psi_H$ on the dissipation term, as DDES alters the destruction term; the major difference is how the factor is determined and its sensitivity to the resolved energy (§1, §2.1.2, Eq. 9). **Stated**
2. $\psi_H=1-f_s+f_s\,r_K^{-1}\big(\tfrac43-\tfrac13r_K^{3/4}\big)k_{\mathrm{sgs}}/k_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is derived by formally applying a temporal filter to the Navier–Stokes equations and is always $>1$ (§2.1.2, Eq. 10); the derivation is cited to Duffal et al. (2019) and Manceau (2018), not given. **Stated**
3. $r_K=1-\max\big(0,1-\beta_\psi\mathrm{CFL}_H^{2/3}\big)$ with $\mathrm{CFL}_H=\varepsilon\max(\Delta t\,U_s,\Delta)/k_{\mathrm{tot}}^{3/2}$, $U_s=\lVert\langle\boldsymbol u\rangle\rVert+\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{tot}}}$, $\Delta$ the cube root of the cell volume, and $\beta_\psi=0.69$ calibrated on decaying isotropic turbulence and assumed constant for all flows (§2.1.2, Eqs. 11–12). **Stated**
4. The presence of the time-mean resolved energy in $\psi_H$ (through $k_{\mathrm{tot}}$) is a key feature identified by Heinz (2020) and a major difference from DES formulations (§2.1.2). **Stated**
5. The shielding function $f_s=1-\tanh\big[(45\,\nu^{3/4}/(\varepsilon^{1/4}d))^8\big]$ has the DDES form, but its shielded zone is much thinner; the DDES shielded zone "is generally much broader, producing a RANS-like solution" (§2.1.2, Eq. 13; §3.2.2, referring to Mays et al. 2021). **Shown** (Figs. 5, 10a, and by reference)
6. The shield front ($f_s=0.01$) lies within the boundary layer, is nearly grid-invariant and is consistent around the cylinder after about 90°; the shielding was calibrated to fall away at $y^+=100$ in channel flow (§3.1.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
7. The grey area (between the $r_K=0.99$ and $r_K=0.2$ isolines, 80% resolution being taken as LES) is about $0.05D$ wide on grid A and intersects the free shear layer; its inner edge is set by the shielding and is grid-invariant, its outer edge moves closer to the wall on grid C (§3.1.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
8. Grid A resolves over 70% of the turbulent kinetic energy ($k_{\mathrm{res}}/k_{\mathrm{tot}}$) in most of the domain; refinement raises the resolved fraction while the total energy declines slightly (§3.1.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
9. At $Re_D=2.5\times10^5$ on coarse grid A, SRH gives $C_D=0.743$ and WALE LES 1.247 (reference LES 0.833); on grid C, SRH gives 0.791 and WALE 0.812 (Table 2). **Shown**
10. SRH attenuates the force fluctuations relative to LES, e.g. $C_{L,\mathrm{rms}}=0.238$, 0.311, 0.339 on grids A, B, C against 0.486 for the reference, owing to the near-wall URANS region (§3.1, Table 2). **Shown**
11. Cost: SRH on grid A takes about 39% of WALE on grid C and 51% of WALE on grid B (4695 against 12150 and 9270 CPU hours; Table 3), on 2×20-core Intel Xeon Gold 6248 nodes (§3.1). **Shown**
12. URANS with the Lag EB $k$-$\varepsilon$ model misses the drag crisis: $C_D=0.436$, 0.354, 0.204, 0.156 at the four Reynolds numbers against reference 0.833, 0.481, 0.296, 0.213, and $L_r/D=1.107$–1.339 against 0.559–0.727 (Tables 4, 5, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. The URANS failure is attributed to over-predicted eddy viscosity in the shear layers, which keeps them stable, consistent with Palkin et al. (2016) for linear eddy-viscosity URANS (§3.2.1). **Stated**
14. URANS nevertheless partly captures the laminar separation bubble (slight inflection near separation, non-zero mean lift) (§3.2.1). **Shown**
15. SRH mean drag agrees strongly with the reference LES and the experiments across the range; an accurate back pressure and $C_p$ profile suffice for accurate drag because pressure drag dominates (§3.2.2, Table 4, Fig. 1). **Shown**
16. SRH captures the one-bubble asymmetric state at $2.5\times10^5$ (asymmetric $C_p$, upward-deflected recirculation bubble, mean lift one order of magnitude larger than at the other Reynolds numbers) and the return to symmetry in the super-critical regime (§3.2.2, Figs. 6, 8). **Shown**
17. Agreement on the $C_p$ plateau marking the laminar separation bubble is weaker at $2.5$ and $3.8\times10^5$, because of small variations in the separation and reattachment points (§3.2.2). **Shown**
18. Strouhal numbers: SRH 0.24, 0.28, 0.35, 0.43 against reference 0.25, 0.23, 0.38, 0.45, with similar lift spectra (Table 5, Fig. 7). **Shown**
19. $\psi_H$ has local maxima in the wake and the shear layers and equals 1 in non-turbulent and very-near-wall regions; its rapid growth away from the wall mitigates grey-area effects (§3.2.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
20. In turbulent regions the predicted $r_K$ approximately matches the realised energy ratio; weakly turbulent or irrotational regions diverge because the inertial-range spectrum assumed by $r_K$ fails there (§3.2.2, Fig. 10c). **Shown**
21. Maximum SRH eddy-viscosity ratio $\nu_t/\nu=9.4$, 9.8, 14.5, 18.3 at the four Reynolds numbers (Table 6); URANS values are "typically on the order of ten times greater at least" (§3.2.2). **Shown** (the URANS figure **Stated**)
22. The SRH/Lag EB interaction produces a spurious reduction of $\varphi_{\mathrm L}$ in the free stream, giving very poor results in high-Reynolds-number channel flow; with insufficient shielding this can produce substantial log-layer mismatch (§3.2.2). **Stated**
23. Refining from the coarser to the finer mesh at each Reynolds number brings the results closer to the reference, but the difference is not substantial (§3.2.2, Table 4). **Shown**
24. All meshes are about five times smaller than the reference LES meshes (7.65–16.64 against 38.4–89.4 million cells, Table 3); by visual inspection of the reference's mesh figures at $Re_D=5.3\times10^5$ the authors estimate a reference prism-cell circumferential length of about $0.001D$ (§3.2.2). **Stated** (estimate)
25. The realised modelled-to-total ratio highlights under-resolved regions a posteriori (a patch with about 40% modelled contribution at $7.2\times10^5$, Fig. 13); $r_K$ from a RANS initialisation gives a simple a priori measure (§3.2.2). **Shown** and **Stated**
26. Near-LES quality is reached on meshes five times coarser than the reference LES, but the near-wall resolution must stay high because under-resolution is fatal to the prediction of transition and separation (§4). **Shown**
27. SRH is expected to allow much larger time steps than LES, left to future work (§4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (2):** the momentum equation is printed with "$+\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_i}$" and the viscous term with the denominator "$\partial x_i x_j$".
- **Eq. (3):** printed "$T_{ij}=2\nu_t\overline S_{ij}-\frac13k\delta_{ij}$". *(compiler check, not in the sources)* With $\overline S_{ii}=0$ from Eq. (1), the printed form has trace $-k$; a stress $-R_{ij}$ with the registry's $k=\tfrac12R_{ii}$ has trace $-2k$, which requires the coefficient $\tfrac23$.
- **Production of $k$:** §2.1.1 prints "$P_k=\nu_tS^2$", Eq. (9) "$2\mu_tS^2$" with $\mu_t=\rho\nu_t$; the modulus $S$ is not defined.
- **Attribution of the model:** the abstract says the SRH model was "proposed by Manceau (2018)", the introduction and §2.1.2 "proposed by Duffal et al. (2019)".
- **Time step, §3.1:** "the Grid A time-step is half that of Grid C's", while Table 1 prints $\Delta t\,U_\infty/D=1.0\times10^{-3}$ (A) and $7.5\times10^{-4}$ (C). *(compiler check, not in the sources)* The printed A step is 1.33 times the C step.
- **Table 1:** $\Delta r_1/D=1.0\times10^{-6}$ for mesh D, against $3.0$, $2.0$, $1.5\times10^{-5}$ for meshes A–C (recorded as printed).
- **Table 6:** the fourth Reynolds number is printed $7.8\times10^5$; Tables 4, 5, Fig. 13 and §3.2.2 give $7.2\times10^5$.
- **Eq. (14) against §2.2:** the blending parameter $\sigma_{HU}$ of the second-order-upwind/bounded-central scheme is "here equal to the SRH shielding function given in Eq. (13)", described as giving an "upwinding scheme in the enforced RANS zones while transitioning to a centred scheme in LES-like zones". *(compiler check, not in the sources)* Eq. (13) gives $f_s\to0$ as $d\to0$ (the RANS zone, where Eq. (10) gives $\psi_H=1$) and $f_s\to1$ away from the wall; with $\sigma_{HU}=f_s$ multiplying the upwind flux in Eq. (14), the printed scheme is centred in the RANS zone and upwind in the LES-like zone, the reverse of the description.
- **Eq. (15) against §2.2:** the bounded branch prints "$\dot m(\sigma\phi_{BCD}+(1-\sigma)\phi_{SOU})$" with $\sigma=0.01$, described as "introducing some upwinding"; as printed the upwind weight is $1-\sigma=0.99$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **§3.1.1:** "calibrated to fall away at $y = 100$ in channel flow", later "$y^+ = 100$".
- **Fig. 1 caption:** lists Vaz et al. (2007) among the "experimental data"; the cited Vaz et al. (2007) is titled "Viscous flow computations on smooth cylinders a detailed numerical study with validation".
- **Fig. 3 caption:** "(left) SRH model and (right) WALE on grid C", while the text compares "the SRH model on the coarse grid A and the LES on the most refined grid C".
- **Notation and labels:** the reduced-stress function is printed both $\phi$ (Eq. 4, text) and $\varphi$ (Eqs. 7, 16–18), $\phi$ also being the generic transported quantity of Eqs. (14)–(15); $r_K$ and $r_k$; "$\psi$" for $\psi_H$ (Fig. 9 caption); "WALES" for WALE (Table 3, text); $L_r$ in "(m)" in Table 5 although the set-up is dimensionless ($D=1$, $U_\infty=1$).
