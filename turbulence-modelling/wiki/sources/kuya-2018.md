# Summary

Kuya et al. (2018) build a second-order, non-dissipative central
discretization of the compressible Euler equations, written as two-point
interface fluxes, that is [kinetic-energy preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
in the incompressible limit and [entropy conserving](/properties/entropy-conservation.md)
for shock-free flow, and use it to explain why earlier kinetic-energy-preserving
schemes do not conserve entropy. Mass and momentum convection are written
in divergence, quadratic-split or cubic-split form. The total-energy flux
is split into kinetic-energy, internal-energy and pressure-diffusion fluxes,
and the discretization is called consistent when it satisfies three
"Analytical Relations" discretely: the kinetic-energy equation follows from
the mass and momentum equations; internal and kinetic energy are convected
by the same velocity; and the pressure diffusion $\partial(u_jp)/\partial x_j$
is the sum of the pressure work of the kinetic-energy equation and the
pressure dilatation of the internal-energy equation. Once the mass and
momentum fluxes are chosen, the relations fix the three energy fluxes. Two
consistent families result: DQ (divergence mass, quadratic-split momentum),
identical to one of Kok's schemes, and the new QC (quadratic-split mass,
cubic-split momentum); the proposed scheme is QC$_C$, later called KEEP.

An entropy analysis from the Gibbs relation shows that any inconsistent
kinetic-energy, internal-energy, pressure-diffusion or enthalpy flux puts a
spurious source in the implicitly solved internal-energy equation and so a
spurious entropy change; the enthalpy fluxes of Jameson and Pirozzoli treat
the pressure diffusion as $\partial(\rho u_j\,p/\rho)/\partial x_j$ and
violate the third relation. On the inviscid compressible
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) ($64^3$, $M_0=0.05$
and 0.4) and inviscid decaying
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
($32^3$, initial turbulent Mach number 0.07 and 0.3), with classical
fourth-order Runge–Kutta at CFL $\approx0.1$, every DQ-type combination
blows up, and only QC$_C$ keeps the kinetic energy at low Mach number, the
total entropy, and bounded rms density and temperature fluctuations. The
schemes are non-dissipative central schemes, and QC$_C$ is stable on these
shock-free tests without artificial dissipation
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)). The authors cite
kinetic-energy-preserving schemes as in use in
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) solvers; the
[pressure-equilibrium](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md)
property is not treated here.

# Key claims

At the face $m+\tfrac12$ in direction $j$, $\{\!\{\phi\}\!\}$ is the mean of
the states at $m$ and $m+1$, $u_{j,m}$ the value at point $m$, and $h$ the
grid spacing (the source's $f|_{(m)}$ and $\Delta x_j$). The specific
kinetic energy $\tfrac12u_iu_i$ is written in full (the source's $k$, not
the turbulent kinetic energy).

1. Analytically, kinetic-energy conservation in the incompressible limit follows from mass and momentum conservation, because the kinetic-energy equation is not independent of them; this is not guaranteed discretely (§1.1). **Stated**
2. With periodic boundaries the total entropy is preserved when the mass and internal-energy equations hold, so an incorrect exchange between kinetic and internal energy through the pressure-diffusion term of the total-energy equation produces spurious entropy production or reduction (§1.2, Eqs. 6–7). **Derived**
3. The kinetic-energy-preserving schemes of Jameson (2008) and Pirozzoli (2010) conserve kinetic energy globally, but the kinetic energy implied by their mass and momentum fluxes differs from that in their total-energy flux: their kinetic-energy fluxes (Eqs. 34–35) are inconsistent with Relation 1 (§2.3.2). **Derived**
4. Of the listed mass and momentum fluxes, only two combinations give a consistent kinetic-energy flux: divergence mass (Eq. 14) with quadratic-split momentum (Eq. 20), flux DQK (Eq. 32), and quadratic-split mass (Eq. 15) with cubic-split momentum (Eq. 21), the new flux QCK (Eq. 33); the other four combinations of Table 1 do not (§2.3.1). **Derived**
5. DQK coincides with the flux of Kok (2009) and of Subbareddy & Candler (2009), necessarily, because the skew-symmetric momentum form equals the quadratic-split form when discrete mass conservation holds (§2.3.1). **Derived**
6. Feiereisen's quadratic-split pairing of $\rho$ with $u_j$ is required for kinetic-energy conservation; the Blaisdell et al. pairing of $\rho$ with $u_1$ is not considered (§2.2.2, after Pirozzoli 2010 and Honein & Moin 2004). **Stated**
7. A divergence-form internal-energy flux (DI, Eq. 39) is inconsistent with Relation 2 when used with DQK or QCK; the consistent internal-energy fluxes are DQI (Eq. 40) and QCI (Eq. 41), with general-equation-of-state forms (Eqs. 42–43) (§2.4.1). **Derived**
8. A divergence-form pressure-diffusion flux (DP, Eq. 46) violates the discrete product rule (Relation 3); the consistent flux CP, $\tfrac12(u_{j,m+1}p_m+u_{j,m}p_{m+1})$ (Eq. 50), follows from discretizing the pressure work as $u_{j,m}(p_{m+1}-p_{m-1})/(2h)$ and the pressure dilatation as $p_m(u_{j,m+1}-u_{j,m-1})/(2h)$; Kok (2009) showed the same flux (§2.4.2, Eqs. 47–50). **Derived**
9. With an inconsistent kinetic-energy flux the entropy equation acquires the term $T^{-1}\partial\Delta(k)_j/\partial x_j$ (Eq. 62), $\Delta(k)_j$ the difference between the inconsistent and the consistent flux; for constant temperature (very low Mach number) the total entropy is still preserved (Eq. 63), but larger temperature variations cause spurious entropy changes (§4.2). **Derived**
10. An inconsistent internal-energy flux gives the same situation (Eq. 65) (§4.3). **Derived**
11. With the DP flux the pressure dilatation at point $m$ is implicitly evaluated as an average over $m+1$ and $m-1$ (Eq. 66), which induces internal-energy and entropy errors (§4.4). **Derived**
12. The enthalpy fluxes DQH and QCH split into the consistent DQI or QCI internal-energy flux plus a pressure-diffusion flux (Eqs. 68, 70) that writes $\partial(u_jp)/\partial x_j$ as $\partial(\rho u_j\,p/\rho)/\partial x_j$ (Eq. 71); density variations thus enter the pressure diffusion and violate Relation 3; "it is apparently very challenging to formulate consistent enthalpy numerical fluxes" (§4.5). **Derived**
13. The proposed scheme stores and integrates in time only $\rho$, $\rho u_i$ and $E$; no pressure needs to be stored (§3). **Stated**
14. The formulations are second order but extendable to higher order after Pirozzoli (2010) and to curvilinear coordinates (Pirozzoli 2011), and, written as numerical fluxes, apply to finite-volume methods (§1.2, §6). **Stated**
15. Taylor–Green vortex, $M_0=0.05$, DQ type: DQ$_C$, DQ$_K$, DQ$_I$ and DQ$_P$ all become unstable by $M_0t=28$, DQ$_C$ the most stable of the four; DQ$_H$ and DQ$_{KH}$ (enthalpy fluxes) run longer with kinetic-energy conservation, but all DQ runs show entropy changes and growing density and temperature fluctuations (§5.2, Fig. 1). **Shown**
16. Taylor–Green vortex, $M_0=0.05$, QC type: all QC schemes preserve the total kinetic energy; only QC$_C$ preserves the total entropy and has rms density and temperature fluctuations converging to roughly constant values (§5.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
17. Taylor–Green vortex, $M_0=0.4$: all DQ-type schemes become unstable by $M_0t=20$; QC$_C$ and QC$_K$ are stable at least to $M_0t=160$, but QC$_K$ shows growing kinetic energy and thermodynamic fluctuations with entropy reduction; QC$_H$ and QC$_{KH}$ lose kinetic energy around $M_0t\simeq40$–60, then gain it and diverge; QC$_C$ loses kinetic energy around $M_0t\simeq20$, attributed to pressure work, then stays almost constant, with a "negligibly small" long-run entropy reduction attributed to the non-conservative pressure-dilatation term (§5.2, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
18. The pressure-dilatation dissipation rate $\frac{1}{\rho_0\mathcal V}\int p\,\partial_ju_j\,d\mathcal V$ (Eq. 74, $\mathcal V$ the domain volume) of QC$_C$ rises suddenly at $M_0t\simeq20$; that of QC$_H$ rises from $M_0t\simeq50$ until blow-up, consistent with the kinetic-energy histories (§5.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
19. The density PDF of QC$_C$ changes between $M_0t=20$ and 40 and stays similar afterwards; that of QC$_H$ spreads continuously, and the QC$_H$ run becomes unstable around $M_0t\simeq120$ (§5.2, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
20. Isotropic turbulence, $M_{t0}=0.07$: all DQ-type runs become unstable by 40 eddy-turnover times (DQ$_C$ the most stable); QC$_C$, QC$_K$, QC$_H$ and QC$_{KH}$ run at least to 180, but all except QC$_C$ show growing thermodynamic fluctuations and entropy reduction (§5.3, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
21. Isotropic turbulence, $M_{t0}=0.3$: all DQ-type schemes are unstable; only QC$_C$ gives stable solutions with entropy conservation and converging rms density and temperature fluctuations (§5.3, Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
22. Both DQ$_C$ and QC$_C$ are consistent, yet only QC$_C$ is stable and entropy conserving; the authors suggest this "could partly be explained by the reduction of aliasing errors" of cubic-split forms (Kennedy & Gruber 2008), and say further investigation is required (§5.3). **Stated** (conjecture)
23. CFL $\approx1$, 0.1 and 0.01 are compared: 0.1 and 0.01 overlap, so 0.1 is time-converged; at CFL $\approx1$ QC$_C$ produces entropy; third-order SSP Runge–Kutta (Gottlieb & Shu) gives similar stability to classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta but produces entropy, so the latter is used throughout (§5.1, App. C, Figs. 12–17). **Shown**
24. Isotropic turbulence $M_{t0}=0.07$ with QC$_C$ at CFL $\approx1$: the total kinetic energy stays constant ($\approx1$ of its initial value) and the normalized rms density and temperature fluctuations converge to about 0.32, in "fairly good agreement" with the de-aliased spectral simulation of Honein & Moin (2004), whose kinetic energy and rms temperature (and pressure) reach nearly constant values of 1 and 0.35; the CFL $\approx1$ runs are those the paper itself shows not to be time-converged (App. C, Fig. 16; §5.3). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- **§5.3:** "The rate of the total kinetic energy reduction at $M_{t0}=0.3$
  shown in Fig. 11(a) is steeper than that at $M_{t0}=0.05$ shown in Fig.
  9(a)"; Fig. 9 is at $M_{t0}=0.07$ (its caption and §5.1), and 0.05 is the
  Taylor–Green $M_0$.
- **§5.1:** the eddy-turnover time is printed $\tau=2\sqrt3/(k_0M_{t0})$,
  while the most energetic wavenumber is defined as $\kappa_0$ (Eq. 73) and
  $k$ denotes the kinetic energy.
- **Eq. (24):** the left-hand side prints $\partial\rho u_1u_j/\partial x$
  without the subscript $j$, and the last term prints
  $\rho(m+1)-\rho(m-1)$ without the evaluation bar used elsewhere; App. B
  prints the momentum term $\partial\rho u_iu_j/\partial x$ likewise.
- **App. B:** the kinetic-energy, internal-energy and pressure-diffusion
  terms are printed with $\partial/\partial x_i$ where the summed index is
  $j$ ($\partial\rho ku_j/\partial x_i$, $\partial\rho eu_j/\partial x_i$,
  Eq. B.1 $\partial u_jp/\partial x_i$).
- **App. A:** the total enthalpy is printed $H_0$ in Eqs. (A.2)–(A.3) and
  $H_o$ in its definition.
- **Fig. 5 caption:** "Pressure-dilation based dissipation rate" against
  "pressure-dilatation" in the text. **App. C:** "shown in Figs. 14 in 15".
- **Kinetic-energy axis label** of Figs. 1–4(a) and 8–17(a): corrected by
  the authors' 2019 corrigendum to $\rho k/(\rho_0k_0)$ (see Version read).
