# Summary

Böttler et al. (2022) build a flamelet/progress-variable (FPV) model of
[flamelet-based tabulated chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md)
for laminar premixed hydrogen–air flames, $0.7\le\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\le1.4$,
that must capture differential diffusion, flame stretch and forced
ignition by a hot spot. There is no turbulence in any test; the paper
enters this bundle through its tabulated-chemistry method; the authors
leave its extension to [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md), which
needs turbulence–chemistry closures, to future work (§4).

Two departures from standard FPV coupling are the contribution. First,
the flow solver transports the major species H$_2$, O$_2$, H$_2$O with
mixture-averaged diffusion (tabulated Lewis numbers and source terms) and
the enthalpy $h_t$, instead of composite control variables; the table is
accessed through an approximate Bilger mixture fraction computed from the
transported species only and through $Y_c=Y_{\mathrm{H_2O}}-Y_{\mathrm{H_2}}-Y_{\mathrm{O_2}}$,
and the enthalpy flux of the non-transported species is a tabulated closure.
Second, the manifold is generated by the group's composition space model,
which joins unstretched premixed flamelets continuously to constant-pressure
homogeneous ignition at high fresh-gas temperature, with diluted flamelets
for low enthalpy; it is parametrized as $\psi(Z,H_{\mathrm n},c)$, and a
source-term patch lets a hot fresh mixture ignite.

Implemented in [OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md) (low-Mach, second order),
the model is compared with resolved detailed-chemistry computations for
planar, cylindrical and spherical expanding flames, an igniting counterflow
and the minimum ignition energy. Flame structures, propagation speeds and
ignition energies agree; deviations in propagation speed trace to an
underpredicted gas velocity linked to a small shift in $Z$, largest for the
lean case. The resolved references are laminar computations, not
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) of turbulence. Flamelet
modelling of thermodiffusively unstable hydrogen–air flames by the same
group is [Böttler et al. (2023)](/sources/bottler-2023.md).

# Key claims

1. Standard FPV control variables ($Z$, $Y_c$) are composite variables of several reactive species; their transport equations under differential diffusion need extra modelling assumptions (§2.1). **Stated**
2. The exact transport equation of the Bilger mixture fraction contains the diffusive fluxes of all species and needs either all species fields or a closure based on assumptions about the flame structure; simplified versions without source terms can decouple the local composition from the mixture fraction (§2.1). **Stated**
3. The flow solver transports only H$_2$, O$_2$ and H$_2$O (Eq. 1) with mixture-averaged fluxes (Eq. 2) plus the enthalpy (Eq. 3); these carry most of the H and O element mass and indicate reaction progress (§2.1). **Stated**
4. The mean-molecular-mass gradient term of Eq. (2) is dropped as negligible, and Soret diffusion is neglected following Liang et al., although the authors also cite that it can matter for very lean hydrogen–air flames (§2.1, §2.3). **Stated**
5. The approximate Bilger mixture fraction from the transported species only recovers the characteristics of the original, introducing "no significant modeling error" (§2.1). **Stated** (no figure or number)
6. $Y_c=Y_{\mathrm{H_2O}}-Y_{\mathrm{H_2}}-Y_{\mathrm{O_2}}$ is suitable over a large range of $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}$, with slight non-monotonicity in the post-oxidation zone of very rich flames (§2.1). **Stated**
7. The composition space model (Eqs. 5–7) describes premixed flame structures and homogeneous constant-pressure ignition with one set of equations; ignition is the limit $g_c\to0$ of vanishing progress-variable gradient, in which all diffusive terms vanish (§2.2). **Stated** (background)
8. Flamelets are computed for $0.35\le\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\le2.0$ and fresh-gas temperatures 300–3500 K, with diluted flamelets extending the lower enthalpy bound; neighbouring flamelet solutions do not intersect (§2.2, Fig. 2). **Shown** (for $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.7$)
9. Plasma chemistry above 3000 K is neglected because plasma species recombine quickly (§2.2). **Stated**
10. The manifold is parametrized as $\psi(Z,H_{\mathrm n},c)$ (Eqs. 10–13); the lower enthalpy bound is extrapolated to a constant so that the bounds do not depend on the progress variable, allowing a two-step lookup (§2.2). **Stated**
11. A source-term patch (Eq. 14, after Sun et al.) imposes positive source terms of the transported species at $c=0$ for $H_{\mathrm n}\ge H_{\mathrm{n,crit}}$, with $c_{\mathrm{init}}=10^{-9}$; without it an ignitable mixture with zero fresh-gas source terms would not ignite (§2.2). **Stated**
12. Coupling flamelet manifolds to compressible solvers needs more refined modelling; this model is coupled to a low-Mach solver (§2.2, Fig. 3). **Stated**
13. The FPV model reduces the cost "by an order of magnitude" in these configurations, though it only halves the number of transport equations, because table interpolation is cheaper than evaluating a detailed mechanism (§3). **Stated** (no timings)
14. The model predicts the laminar burning velocity for $0.7\le\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}\le1.4$ (§3.1). **Stated** (shown in the supplementary material, not read)
15. Planar flame at $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.7$: very good agreement with detailed chemistry for all quantities, including the variation of $Z$ and $h_t$ across the flame from differential diffusion; minor deviations in $Y_{\mathrm{OH}}$ on the burned side (§3.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
16. Cylindrical expanding flames: ignition onset is captured, peak velocities slightly underestimated; the offset in the propagation speed $dR_f/dt=u_g+s_d$ comes from the underpredicted gas velocity $u_g$, while $s_d$ agrees very well; the propagation speed deviates by less than 1% for the rich flame and $u_g$ by up to 7% for the lean one (§3.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
17. The offset is linked to a shift of the FPV temperature profile towards leaner mixture: a small deviation in $Z$ changes the density and the unburned-side velocity (§3.2). **Shown** (detailed analysis in the supplementary material)
18. Spherical expanding flames ($E_{\mathrm{ign}}=0.15$ mJ): ignition onset and the position of the peak propagation speed are slightly overestimated; the maximum propagation-speed deviation is about 8% (lean) and below 2% otherwise, close to the cylindrical case despite the larger stretch (§3.3, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
19. Igniting counterflow ($a_g=4U_{\mathrm{in}}/L=1000$ s$^{-1}$): FPV gives slightly slower radial propagation and an axial position slightly closer to the centre; at $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.7$ and 1 ms it gives slightly higher $Z$ on the burned side and slightly higher $Y_{\mathrm{OH}}$ near the stagnation plane (§3.4, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
20. The minimum ignition energy rises with $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}$; FPV reproduces the trend for cylindrical and spherical flames, slightly overestimating it, with good agreement at $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}=1.0$ and 1.4 and growing deviations towards the lean case; the counterflow value is almost that of the spherical flame (not shown) (§3.5, Fig. 11). **Shown**
21. The Conclusions state that the model "accurately captures the minimum ignition energies (MIEs, deviations below 3 %)" (§4). **Stated** (no number in the body)
22. Effective Lewis numbers from the asymptotic theory of Matalon et al.: about 0.5 at $\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.7$, 0.95 at 1.0 and 1.4 at 1.4 (§1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
23. The method transfers to other fuels with other tabulated flames and transported species, at more cost for complex fuels; LES needs turbulence–chemistry closures (§4). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §4 states that agreement is shown by comparing "the ignition delay times to detailed chemistry reference calculations", while §3 reports no ignition delay times; the "deviations below 3 %" of the minimum ignition energy appear only in §4, §3.5 giving no number.
- Eq. (9) closes the enthalpy flux over $\forall k\notin\{\mathrm{H_2},\mathrm{O_2},\mathrm{H_2O}\}$ (on the page image; text extraction renders the symbol as $\in$).
- Eq. (2) calls $\alpha=\lambda/c_p$ the "thermal diffusivity"; $\lambda/c_p$ is a density times a diffusivity, consistently with $Le_k=\alpha/(\rho D_{m,k})$ and the flux form *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Curvature is printed $\kappa$ in Eqs. (5)–(7) and defined as $\kappa_c=-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol n_c$.
- §2.1 "the the model"; §2.2 "the lower boundary of the table is extrapolated to there is a constant minimum enthalpy level"; §3.5 "is can be used"; Fig. 10 caption "counterflow flame flame".
