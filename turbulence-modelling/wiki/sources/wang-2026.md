# Summary

Wang et al. (2026) continue the authors' study of
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)
([Cao et al. (2022b)](/sources/cao-2022-c.md)) across grid resolutions.
Implicit LES and three explicit eddy-viscosity closures, the static
[Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) ($C_s=0.1$), the
[Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md) (prefactor $c=2.5C_s^2=0.025$) and
the dynamic Smagorinsky model ([dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md),
domain-averaged coefficient, top-hat test filter of width $2\Delta$), run in
the same fourth-order [high-order gas-kinetic scheme](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md)
(fifth-order WENO-Z reconstruction, two-stage fourth-order time stepping),
where the eddy viscosity enters through the collision time,
$\tau_{\mathrm{col}}+\tau_{\mathrm{col},t}=(\mu+\mu_t)/p$. The case is the
weakly compressible [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md)
($M=0.1$, $Re=1600$) on $64^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$, against a $512^3$
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) from the same framework.

Two analyses follow. A volume-integrated kinetic-energy budget gives the
total subgrid dissipation as the residual of the energy decay and the physical
dissipation (deviatoric-strain, bulk-viscous and pressure-dilatation terms),
split into modelled and numerical parts
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)): with
coarsening the modelled dissipation grows in absolute terms but its share
falls, so the numerical part dominates on under-resolved grids; Smagorinsky
and Vreman are nearly identical and more dissipative than the dynamic model.
Then PDFs of the resolved velocity gradients are compared with box-filtered
(Gaussian-filtered in an appendix) and unfiltered DNS through the
Jensen–Shannon divergence and the Wasserstein-1 distance, scoring the LES
solutions themselves ([a priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)).
Implicit LES gives the smallest or near-smallest values on $64^3$ and
$128^3$; on $256^3$ the ranking depends on the reference, the metric and the
time. The abstract states that implicit LES "provides consistently robust
agreement with DNS across the tested grid resolutions"; Tables I–II print, on
$256^3$, smaller time-averaged values for Smagorinsky and Vreman against
filtered DNS and the smallest Mean JSD for Smagorinsky against unfiltered
DNS. The authors restrict their conclusions to this scheme, this flow and
uncalibrated coefficients.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\boldsymbol c$, $f$, $g$ the particle velocity,
distribution function and Maxwellian of the BGK model (as on the HGKS page);
$c$ the Vreman prefactor (as on the Vreman page); $C_{\mathrm d}$ the dynamic coefficient
(multiplying $\Delta^2$); $M_{ij}$ the dynamic model tensor; $E_{\mathrm{kin}}$
the kinetic energy per unit mass, $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the domain average,
$(\cdot)_T$ the time average over $t\in[1,16]$; $\varepsilon_\mu$,
$\varepsilon_b$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pd}}$ the deviatoric-strain,
bulk-viscous and pressure-dilatation parts of the physical dissipation
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{phy}}$; $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgs}}=\varepsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}_{\mathrm{sgs}}+\varepsilon^{\mathrm{num}}_{\mathrm{sgs}}$
the total, modelled and numerical subgrid dissipation; JSD, $W_1$ the
Jensen–Shannon divergence and Wasserstein-1 distance; "Mean" their average
over $\partial_x\bar u$, $\partial_y\bar v$, $\partial_z\bar w$.

1. On $64^3$ all models give similar total subgrid dissipation; with refinement the level falls and differences grow; the dynamic model stays close to implicit LES while Smagorinsky and Vreman are noticeably higher (§III B, Fig. 1). **Shown**
2. On $128^3$ at $t=8$ Smagorinsky and Vreman give much larger modelled dissipation than the dynamic model, with nearly identical distributions; the Smagorinsky dissipation field follows the vortex life cycle (localized at $t=4$, spread at 8, more homogeneous at 12 and 16) (§III B, Figs. 2–4). **Shown**
3. Modelled dissipation rises, peaks near transition and decays for all models and grids; its peak falls with refinement, most for the dynamic model (§III B, Fig. 5). **Shown**
4. $(\langle\varepsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle)_T$ decreases with refinement for all three closures (Vreman ≈ Smagorinsky > dynamic), while the ratio $(\langle\varepsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle)_T/(\langle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle)_T$ increases: numerical dissipation dominates on coarse grids, and on the fine grid modelled dissipation is most of the total for Vreman and Smagorinsky while the dynamic model keeps a lower fraction (§III B, Fig. 6). **Shown**
5. "The tested SGS models do not exhibit consistent, scale-independent behavior across grid resolutions", the dynamic model included (§III B). **Shown**
6. Global dissipation alone cannot tell whether explicit closures improve or distort resolved structures; velocity-gradient statistics are a more stringent test because subgrid dissipation depends on velocity gradients (§III B, §IV A). **Stated**
7. Box- and Gaussian-filtered DNS give nearly identical $\partial_x\bar u$ contours and PDFs (similar transfer functions at low wavenumbers); filtering and differentiation commute for homogeneous filters on a periodic domain, and discretely $\partial_x\bar u$ and $\overline{\partial_xu}$ differ minimally; the procedure is filter first, then differentiate (§IV A, Fig. 7). **Derived** / **Shown**
8. At $t=10$ on $128^3$ all PDFs of $\partial_z\bar w$ collapse on filtered DNS for $\lvert\partial_z\bar w\rvert\lesssim0.5$; between 0.5 and 2 implicit LES is closest, the dynamic model next; in the tail Smagorinsky and Vreman under-predict strong-gradient events (§IV B, Fig. 8). **Shown**
9. $128^3$ against filtered DNS, time-averaged Mean JSD: dynamic $7.90\times10^{-4}$, implicit $8.01\times10^{-4}$, Smagorinsky $9.95\times10^{-4}$, Vreman $9.39\times10^{-4}$; Mean $W_1$: implicit and dynamic $1.32\times10^{-2}$, Smagorinsky $1.98\times10^{-2}$, Vreman $1.80\times10^{-2}$ (§IV B, Fig. 9, Table I). **Shown**
10. $128^3$ against unfiltered DNS: implicit LES smallest (Mean JSD $1.49\times10^{-3}$, Mean $W_1$ $3.12\times10^{-2}$), then dynamic ($1.73\times10^{-3}$, $3.72\times10^{-2}$), Vreman ($2.32\times10^{-3}$, $4.52\times10^{-2}$), Smagorinsky ($2.37\times10^{-3}$, $4.72\times10^{-2}$) (§IV B, Fig. 10, Table II). **Shown**
11. Discrepancies are small for $t\lesssim5$, grow during transition ($t\approx5$–8), are largest for Smagorinsky and Vreman around the dissipation peak ($t\approx8$–10) and decrease in decay (§IV B, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
12. $64^3$: implicit LES gives the smallest time-averaged metrics against both references (Table I: implicit $4.16\times10^{-3}$ / $2.07\times10^{-2}$, dynamic $4.20\times10^{-3}$ / $2.21\times10^{-2}$, Smagorinsky $5.17\times10^{-3}$ / $3.38\times10^{-2}$, Vreman $4.77\times10^{-3}$ / $3.06\times10^{-2}$; Table II: $9.13\times10^{-3}$ / $9.30\times10^{-2}$, $1.01\times10^{-2}$ / $1.01\times10^{-1}$, $1.31\times10^{-2}$ / $1.21\times10^{-1}$, $1.21\times10^{-2}$ / $1.17\times10^{-1}$); the advantage is "most pronounced under strongly under-resolved conditions", and the Smagorinsky and Vreman contributions are "excessive for the present coarse-grid calculation" (§IV B). **Shown**
13. $256^3$ against filtered DNS: Smagorinsky and Vreman give smaller discrepancies mainly in late decay ($t=14$–16) and smaller time averages (Table I: implicit $2.39\times10^{-4}$ / $9.09\times10^{-3}$, dynamic $2.43\times10^{-4}$ / $7.76\times10^{-3}$, Smagorinsky $2.01\times10^{-4}$ / $7.18\times10^{-3}$, Vreman $1.95\times10^{-4}$ / $6.94\times10^{-3}$), read as "a modest regularizing benefit" (§IV B, Fig. 9). **Shown**
14. $256^3$ against unfiltered DNS: implicit and dynamic are best at $t=4$, 8, 12, Smagorinsky and Vreman at $t=16$; time-averaged Mean JSD smallest for Smagorinsky ($3.02\times10^{-4}$; implicit $3.25\times10^{-4}$, Vreman $3.53\times10^{-4}$, dynamic $3.89\times10^{-4}$), Mean $W_1$ smallest for implicit LES ($9.61\times10^{-3}$; dynamic $1.23\times10^{-2}$, Smagorinsky $1.29\times10^{-2}$, Vreman $1.45\times10^{-2}$) (§IV B, Fig. 10, Table II). **Shown**
15. "iLES provides the best and most consistent reproduction of the DNS-based velocity-gradient statistics across the tested grid resolutions"; the explicit closures "show no systematic advantage over iLES" (§IV B, §V). **Stated** (synthesis of Tables I–II)
16. Off-diagonal gradients at $t=5$ on $128^3$ against filtered DNS: implicit LES smallest (Mean JSD $1.375\times10^{-4}$, Mean $W_1$ $3.228\times10^{-3}$), then dynamic ($1.516\times10^{-4}$, $3.275\times10^{-3}$), Smagorinsky ($1.867\times10^{-4}$, $5.337\times10^{-3}$), Vreman ($1.893\times10^{-4}$, $4.627\times10^{-3}$) (App. B, Table III). **Shown**
17. A Gaussian-filter reference gives trends and rankings "fully consistent" with the box filter (App. C, Fig. 12). **Shown**
18. Spectra at $t=8$ on $128^3$ agree with filtered DNS at low and intermediate wavenumbers; at high wavenumbers implicit LES is closest, the dynamic model removes slightly more energy, Smagorinsky and Vreman more (App. A, Fig. 11). **Shown**
19. The BGK model has the inherent bulk viscosity $\mu_b=4\mu/15$ (citing Xu) (§III A). **Stated**
20. The extended BGK equation $\partial_tf+\boldsymbol c\cdot\nabla f=(g-f)/(\tau_{\mathrm{col}}+\tau_{\mathrm{col},t})$ recovers an eddy-viscosity model through the Chapman–Enskog expansion (citing Chen et al. 2003) (§II A, Eqs. 11–12). **Stated**
21. Fourth-order central differences in post-processing have truncation errors "expected to be smaller than the discretization errors" (§II C). **Stated**
22. Conclusions are restricted to this scheme, the TGV, these grids and uncalibrated published coefficients; scale-similarity, gradient and dynamic mixed models were not tested and "may exhibit different behavior" (§V). **Stated**

The budget (Eqs. 18–29): $\varepsilon(\langle E_{\mathrm{kin}}\rangle)=-d\langle E_{\mathrm{kin}}\rangle/dt$;
$\langle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{phy}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\rho\Omega}\int\big(2\mu S^d_{ij}S^d_{ij}+\mu_bS_{kk}^2-pS_{kk}\big)d\Omega$;
$\langle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle=\varepsilon(\langle E_{\mathrm{kin}}\rangle)-\langle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{phy}}\rangle$;
$\langle\varepsilon^{\mathrm{mod}}_{\mathrm{sgs}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{\rho_0\Omega}\int\tau^{\mathrm{mod}}_{ij}\bar S_{ij}\,d\Omega$,
with $\Omega$ here the domain volume. The metrics (Eqs. 33–34) are
$\mathrm{JSD}(P\Vert Q)=\frac12\int P\ln\frac{2P}{P+Q}ds+\frac12\int Q\ln\frac{2Q}{P+Q}ds$
and $W_1=\int\lvert F_P-F_Q\rvert ds$ on 100 equal bins over the common range
of all samples plus 2% padding.

*(Compiler check, not in the sources:)* $2.5\times0.1^2=0.025$, the printed
prefactor $c$.

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (28):** "the modeled SGS stress" is printed as $\tau^{\mathrm{mod}}_{ij}=\bar\rho(\overline{U_iU_j}-\bar U_i\bar U_j)$, the definition of the subgrid stress; the model expression used in Eq. (29) is not printed.
- **Eqs. (18), (25) against Eq. (29):** the first two divide by $\rho\Omega$ with the field $\rho$ outside the integral, Eq. (29) by $\rho_0\Omega$.
- **App. C, Eq. (32):** the Gaussian width $\sigma$ is not printed.
- **§II A against §II C:** "fifth-order WENO-Z reconstruction" and "a fifth-order WENO reconstruction".
