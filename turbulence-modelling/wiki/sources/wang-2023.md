# Summary

Wang & Roy (2023) implement error transport equations (ETE) for a modal
discontinuous Galerkin discretization (tensor-product Legendre basis,
Gauss–Legendre quadrature; see the
[DGSEM](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md) page for
the family) of the steady 2-D Euler equations. The discretization error is
the difference between the discrete solution and the projection of the
exact solution on the DG space. Inserting the projected exact solution in
the DG weak form defines the truncation error, and subtracting the two
gives a nonlinear ETE whose source is the negative truncation error. Its
linearization about the discrete solution gives the linearized ETE. The
ETE solution estimates the discretization error and corrects the solution.
The contributions over the authors' conference work are three: the
truncation error is estimated from a higher-order reconstruction of the
discrete solution; the nonlinear ETE is implemented for DG; and Tyson et
al.'s relinearization becomes an "ETE iterative correction" for both forms.

On a sinusoidal [manufactured solution](/methods/method-of-manufactured-solutions.md)
and on Ringleb flow, for second- to fourth-order primal solves, the
estimates reach the expected orders: round-off with the exact truncation
error (nonlinear ETE), order $2p$ (linearized ETE), and order $r$ with
estimated truncation error after iterative correction. The ETE costs little
next to the primal solve. On a supersonic expansion fan and a Kármán–Trefftz
airfoil, both with singularities, the primal order falls to one, the
truncation-error estimates fall below first order, and the corrected error
barely improves. The corrected lift of the airfoil is nevertheless second
order. The authors place truncation-error estimation at the heart of
gradient-, adjoint- and $\tau$-estimation-driven
[mesh adaptation](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md), and propose
discontinuity-aligned grids or embedded-grid truncation-error estimates for
non-smooth flows.

# Key claims

1. The DG discretization uses tensor-product Legendre bases (orthogonal for a constant mapping Jacobian), Gauss–Legendre quadrature on a square reference element, and backward Euler marching to steady state (§2.1). **Stated**
2. The nonlinear ETE (Eq. 12) follows exactly by subtracting the DG weak form at the projected exact solution (Eq. 11) from the discrete equations (Eq. 3); the truncation error is the local source of discretization error (§3.1). **Derived**
3. Linearizing Eq. 12 about the discrete solution gives the linearized ETE (Eq. 13), with the physical-flux, numerical-flux and source Jacobians (§3.1). **Derived**
4. In the nonlinear ETE the projected exact solution is approximated by the corrected solution $\boldsymbol q_h-\boldsymbol e_h^{\mathrm{est}}$; the corrected error $\boldsymbol e_h-\boldsymbol e_h^{\mathrm{est}}$ is both the error of the corrected solution and the error of the estimate (Eq. 14). **Derived**
5. The ETE estimate does not characterize the projection error $I^h\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}}-\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}}$ (end of §3.1). **Stated**
6. For smooth solutions and smoothly varying metrics, with triplet $(p,q,r)$, the corrected error is $O(h^{\min(p+q,r)})$ for the nonlinear ETE (Eq. 15) and $O(h^{\min(2p,p+q,r)})$ for the linearized ETE (Eq. 16), the derivation cited to Banks et al. [13] (§3.1.1). **Stated**
7. $q=p$ is kept throughout, because $q\ne p$ needs a new full linearization; the paper chooses $p<r\le2p$ (§3.1.1). **Stated**
8. The linearized ETE is cheaper than the nonlinear one; both are solved by implicit pseudo-time marching because the linearized system is ill-conditioned (§3.1). **Stated**, partly **Shown** (Table 1)
9. Without iterative correction, estimates with estimated truncation error do not reach the expected order (sinusoidal manufactured solution, second-order primal, first 10 correction steps) (§4.1.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
10. Manufactured solution: the nonlinear ETE with the exact truncation error gives corrected error at round-off; the linearized ETE with the exact truncation error shows order $2p$; after iterative correction both reach order $r$ with estimated truncation error; this holds for second-, third- and fourth-order primal solves (§4.1.1, Figs. 3–5). **Shown**. With the exact truncation error and $q=p$, Eq. 12 is satisfied exactly by the true discretization error, so the round-off result follows by construction *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
11. For $p=4$ the triplet $(4,4,6)$ replaces $(4,4,8)$, which is strongly affected by round-off (§4.1.1). **Shown**
12. Unlike a finite-volume ETE study [26], where round-off affected the linearized ETE earlier than the nonlinear one, here both give similar corrected errors at round-off; the authors infer that the finite-volume linearization is the more ill-conditioned (§4.1.1). **Shown** (DG side), **Stated** (inference)
13. The truncation-error estimate after the final correction step converges slightly below order $r$, without harming the discretization-error estimate (§4.1.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. Cost (Table 1, hardware not stated): $64\times64$, $(3,3,6)$: primal 1707.90 s, linearized ETE 35.35 s, nonlinear ETE 50.69 s, with 10 correction steps 368.77 s and 488.41 s; $32\times32$, $(2,2,\infty)$: primal 19.52 s, linearized 0.82 s, nonlinear 1.68 s. The linearized ETE takes "approximately 2/3" of the nonlinear time; for enough degrees of freedom the whole estimation costs less than the primal solve (§4.1.1). **Shown**. The Table 1 ratios of linearized to nonlinear ETE time range from 0.48 ($32\times32$, $(3,3,\infty)$: 3.28/6.82 s) to 0.77 ($64\times64$, $(2,2,4)$ with 10 steps: 112.25/145.22 s) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
15. The nonlinear ETE converges much faster than the primal problem, possibly because the zero initial guess is already close (§4.1.1). **Stated**
16. Ringleb flow behaves like the manufactured case; for $p=4$ the linearized-ETE error with the exact truncation error is near round-off on the coarsest grids; curved walls (fourth-order edge fit) cause no problem, and "the order of the boundary representation only needs to be higher than the order of the primal solve" (§4.1.2, Figs. 8–10). **Shown** / **Stated** (rule "based on experience")
17. An output functional from the corrected solution has the order of the global error norm when integrated to order $\ge r$; the method of Hartmann et al. [7] is equivalent to the linearized ETE of Tyson et al. [10] (§4.1.2). **Stated**
18. On non-smooth problems the primal order drops to one, so only $p=2$ is used (§4.2.1). **Shown**
19. Expansion fan: the nonlinear ETE with exact truncation error again reaches round-off; the linearized ETE with exact truncation error lowers the error without raising its order; with estimated truncation error, $(2,2,3)$ and $(2,2,4)$ give nearly identical errors only slightly below the base error. With the corner singularity in the domain the orders fall to one; with it outside, all are slightly above one (§4.2.1, Fig. 13). **Shown**
20. The linearized ETE is not higher-order on the expansion fan even with exact truncation error, attributed to linearization error from non-smoothness (§4.2.1). **Shown** (failure), **Stated** (cause)
21. Truncation-error estimates on the expansion fan converge slightly below first order after iterative correction (§4.2.1, Fig. 14). **Shown**
22. The discretization error is largest at the corner singularity and propagates along the edge of the fan ($64\times64$ mesh) (§4.2.1, Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. Kármán–Trefftz airfoil: base error first order; nonlinear ETE with exact truncation error exact to round-off; linearized ETE with exact truncation error about second order; with estimated truncation error the corrected error is almost the base error; the truncation-error estimate is nearly zeroth order, with no difference between $r=3$ and $4$ (§4.2.2, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
24. Kármán–Trefftz airfoil: the lift coefficient of the corrected solution is second order, that of the primal solution below second order, because the corrected error grows symmetrically near the trailing edge and falls near the leading edge ($128\times64$ mesh); the extra cost of the ETE is nearly negligible on finer grids (§4.2.2, Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
25. For non-smooth problems accuracy is limited by truncation-error estimation, not by the ETE; remedies are discontinuity-aligned grids [28, 29] and embedded-grid truncation-error estimation [30, 31]; the nonlinear ETE is preferable when linearization error is comparable to the truncation-error-estimate error (§5). **Stated**

The two equations, translated. With $B_h(\cdot,\boldsymbol v_h)$ the DG
semilinear form, $\tau_h(I^h\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}},\boldsymbol v_h)=B_h(I^h\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}},\boldsymbol v_h)$
the truncation error and $\boldsymbol e_h=\boldsymbol q_h-I^h\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}}$,
the steady nonlinear ETE is
$$B_h(\boldsymbol q_h,\boldsymbol v_h)-B_h(\boldsymbol q_h-\boldsymbol e_h^{\mathrm{est}},\boldsymbol v_h)=-\tau_h(I^h\boldsymbol q^{\mathrm{ex}},\boldsymbol v_h)\quad\forall\boldsymbol v_h,$$
and the linearized ETE replaces the left side by
$B_h'(\boldsymbol q_h)[\boldsymbol e_h^{\mathrm{est}},\boldsymbol v_h]$ (Eqs. 12–13).
The sinusoidal manufactured solution (Eqs. 18–21, Table 2) sets each primitive
variable to $\phi_0+\phi_x\,\mathrm{trig}(a_{\phi x}\pi x)+\phi_y\,\mathrm{trig}(a_{\phi y}\pi y)$,
with a mixed subsonic–supersonic field.
