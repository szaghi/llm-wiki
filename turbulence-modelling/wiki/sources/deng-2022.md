# Summary

Deng et al. (2022) address the conflict in scale-resolving simulation of
compressible flow between low dissipation for broadband turbulence and
robustness at shocks. Upwind [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)-type schemes
are too dissipative, hybrid central/upwind schemes depend on a
[shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md), and central schemes with
[artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
must not swamp the small scales. The authors extend their
$P_nT_m$-BVD finite-volume schemes, which choose between a degree-$n$
upwind-biased polynomial and THINC hyperbolic-tangent reconstructions of
$m$ steepness levels by minimizing the total boundary variation (BVD),
with an adjustable-dissipation (AD) stage. Wherever both sides of a face
still carry the polynomial, the left and right face values are replaced
by convex combinations weighted by $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}$:
$\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=1$ recovers the $(n+1)$-order upwind scheme,
$\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.5$ makes the face values equal, so the upwind
term of the Riemann flux vanishes and the scheme becomes the
non-dissipative $(n+2)$-order central interpolation. A single parameter
thus spans [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)
from a DNS/explicit-LES central scheme to an
[implicit-LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) dissipative one; the
authors relate it to [low-dissipation, low-dispersion](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md)
schemes with controllable dissipation.

Evidence is numerical: approximate-dispersion-relation analysis, grid
convergence at order $n+2$, one-dimensional and two-dimensional Euler
tests (strong Lax problem with a Mach 198 shock, shock–density-wave and
shock–vortex interactions, double Mach reflection), a viscous shock
tube, under-expanded planar jets, and implicit LES of
[decaying compressible isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
with eddy shocklets ($M_t=0.6$ initially, $64^3$ and $128^3$) compared
with the [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) of Johnsen
et al. and with ninth-order WENO-M. The main claim is that
$P_nT_m$-BVD-AD of degree $n$ matches $P_{n+2}T_m$-BVD at lower cost on
a more compact stencil. The paper mentions explicit and implicit
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) and does not study
[shock-generated turbulence](/results/shock-generated-turbulence.md)
beyond its inviscid shock–vortex and shock–entropy tests.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $n$ the degree of the upwind-biased reconstruction
polynomial $P_n$ (not the element degree $N$), $m$ the number of THINC
steepness levels $\beta_{\mathrm{TH},k}$; $q^L_{i+1/2}$, $q^R_{i+1/2}$
the face values from cells $i$ and $i+1$; $TBV_i$ the total boundary
variation of cell $i$; $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}\in[0.5,1]$ the
dissipation-adjusting parameter; $Re_{\lambda,0}$, $\tau_0$ the initial
Taylor-scale Reynolds number and eddy time of the isotropic test.

1. The $P_nT_m$-BVD-AD dissipation is adjustable between the $(n+1)$-order upwind-biased and the non-dissipative $(n+2)$-order central scheme through $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}$ alone (Abstract; §3.2, Remark 1). **Derived**
2. With $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.5$ the scheme recovers $(n+2)$-order non-dissipative central interpolation for smooth solutions over all wavenumbers (Abstract; Remark 2; §4.1). **Shown**
3. In the approximate-dispersion-relation test the ninth-order linear upwind scheme keeps inherent dissipation, and ninth-order WENO-M is worse than the same-order linear scheme at high wavenumbers because nonlinear adaptation treats high-wavenumber waves as discontinuities (§4.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
4. The eighth- and tenth-order schemes retrieve the non-dissipative central schemes; the sixth-order $P_4T_2$ keeps "very small numerical dissipation in high wavenumber region" (§4.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
5. Dispersion is almost that of the corresponding central schemes and better than very-high-order WENO-M, especially at high wavenumbers (§4.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
6. Sine-wave advection: $P_4T_2$, $P_6T_3$, $P_8T_3$-BVD-AD reach $L_1$ orders 5.99, 7.99, 10.00 at 160 cells, errors "exactly same as those calculated by n+2th order central schemes" (§4.2, Table 1). **Shown**
7. Against the authors' $P_nT_m$-BVD the errors are "significantly reduced" and the order one higher (§4.2; comparison data in the earlier paper). **Shown**
8. For the profile with critical points the $L_1$ orders at 160 cells are 5.99, 7.90, 9.73 (§4.3, Table 2). **Shown**
9. Complex-wave advection is essentially oscillation-free, with the square wave resolved within four cells (§4.4, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. In the Sod problem the contact is resolved within two cells, and the three orders coincide there because THINC is selected (§4.5, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. In the Lax problem the scheme is less oscillatory and less diffusive than the central-upwind scheme of Cong & Chen (§4.6, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. Strong Lax problem (pressure ratio $10^5$, Mach 198 shock): $(n+1)$-order WENO-M overshoots and oscillates more with increasing order, whereas $(n+2)$-order $P_nT_m$-BVD-AD suppresses the overshoot; strong shocks are captured robustly although the scheme is central in smooth regions (§4.7, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. In the high-wavenumber shock–density-wave interaction ninth-order WENO-M misses wave peaks that the proposed schemes capture (§4.8, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
14. Shock–vortex interaction (Mach 1.5 shock, vortex Mach 0.9): vortex splitting resolved at $h=1/100$, results at $h=1/200$ "converge to the reference solution"; a Mach 5 case is also resolved (§4.12, Figs. 14–16). **Shown**
15. Viscous shock tube ($Re=1000$): the primary vortex agrees with the DNS of Daru & Tenaud, whereas high-order WENO-M resembles their diffusive result (§4.13, Fig. 17). **Shown**
16. Supersonic planar jets (2D and 3D, coarser meshes than the reference): vortices and vortex–shock interactions better resolved than with $P_nT_m$-BVD, earlier shear-layer roll-up, and $P_4T_2$-BVD-AD comparable to $P_6T_3$-BVD (§4.14, Figs. 18–22). **Shown**
17. Implicit LES of decaying compressible isotropic turbulence ($M_t=0.6$, $Re_{\lambda,0}=100$) on $64^3$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.75$: every order gives a velocity spectrum at $t/\tau_0=4$ more accurate than ninth-order WENO-M, $P_8T_3$-BVD-AD close to the $256^3$ DNS (§4.15, Fig. 23). **Shown**
18. Lowering $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}$ progressively improves the $64^3$ spectrum; $P_4T_2$-BVD-AD at $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.65$ is comparable to $P_6T_3$-BVD ($\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=1$) (§4.15, Fig. 24). **Shown**
19. On $128^3$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.75$ and $0.5$ both converge to the DNS spectrum (§4.15, Fig. 25). **Shown**
20. Adjustable numerical dissipation "is believed to work" for under-resolved turbulence in implicit LES in place of an explicit SGS model; $\lambda_{\mathrm{AD}}=0.5$ is for DNS or explicit LES (Remark 2). **Stated**
21. Designing robust shock capturing with adjustable dissipation within WENO is "not a trivial work", while BVD does it with one parameter (Remark 4). **Stated**
22. Hybrid schemes rely on a shock indicator, and no universally best indicator exists (citing the indicator comparison of Li & Qiu, 2010); adaptive central-upwind WENO schemes may lack robustness at discontinuities (§1). **Stated**
23. Earlier work found a second-order central scheme preserving turbulent energy and spectrum better than seventh-order WENO in explicit LES and DNS of compressible turbulence (§1). **Stated**
24. Future work: adaptive dissipation for implicit LES, and extension to finite-difference, compact and positivity-preserving (MOOD-type) schemes (§5). **Stated**
