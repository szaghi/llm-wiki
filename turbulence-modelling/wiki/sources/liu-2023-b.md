# Summary

Liu & Yuan (2023) carry the a posteriori subcell finite-volume limiter of
Dumbser et al. (2014), designed for DG, over to one-dimensional
[flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md). The FR scheme uses
VCJH correction functions with the parameter that recovers collocation
nodal DG ([DGSEM](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md))
on Gauss–Legendre points; FR also recovers the
[spectral difference method](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md),
whose stability proof the authors cite. The authors state that this is the
first FR simulation of gaseous detonations (reactive Euler equations,
one-step Arrhenius chemistry).

The unlimited FR candidate at $t^{n+1}$ is checked for positivity
(physical admissibility) and by a relaxed discrete maximum principle
(numerical admissibility) on cell means for $N=2,3$ or on subcell means for
$N\ge4$. Troubled cells and their two neighbours are recomputed from $t^n$
on $N+1$ equidistant subcells, with a first-order Godunov or a
second-order MUSCL–minmod scheme
([subcell finite-volume shock capturing](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md)),
and mapped back by the exact inverse of the projection. The modification
puts a KXRCF interface-jump [indicator](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) in
front of the maximum-principle test: cells under a lower threshold are
accepted, cells over an upper one are marked, and only the cells in
between are tested. With the maximum principle alone, the FR–subcell data
transfer at a shock produces Runge-type perturbations. The shock cell then
flips between FR and FV, and the peak-pressure history of a steady
detonation does not converge.

The tests are linear advection (third order recovered), Sod, a double
rarefaction, the Woodward–Colella blast waves and four overdriven ZND
detonations, from stable to chaotic. With the pre-detection every variant
converges on the stable detonation and marks 5–7 cells per step, against
14–85 without it. At equal degrees of freedom, the second-degree TVD
variant is reported to resolve detonations without the post-front
oscillations of fifth-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md). The authors
criticize local [artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
for FR for its extra gradient reconstruction and problem-dependent
parameters. All tests are 1-D and inviscid. The paper has no turbulence
content and enters the bundle only through its shock-capturing numerics.

# Key claims

The source's polynomial degree $K$ is the registry $N$ (scheme order
$N+1$; "FR$K$" is FR of degree $N=K$); its VCJH parameter $\eta$ is the
registry $c_{\mathrm{FR}}$ of [flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md),
with $\eta=0$ the value $c_{\mathrm{DG}}=0$; its element Jacobian $J_i$ is
$\mathcal J$; its cell size $h$ agrees. Page-local: $\{u\}_i^n$ the cell
mean (the source's $\bar u_i^n$; element-average notation of registry §11); $I^{\mathrm{KXRCF}}_i$ the normalized jump (the source's $I_i^*$);
$c_{\mathrm{sm}}$, $c_{\mathrm{dc}}$ its smooth and discontinuity thresholds
(the source's $C_S$, $C_D$); $\delta_0$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{DMP}}$ the
relaxation constants of the maximum principle.

1. To the authors' knowledge this is the first application of an FR method to detonation simulation (§1). **Stated**
2. The FR scheme is the VCJH family with $c_{\mathrm{FR}}=c_{\mathrm{DG}}=0$ (collocation nodal DG) on Gauss–Legendre solution points, chosen after Witherden & Vincent [47] to minimize aliasing from the nonlinear flux collocation (§2.2). **Stated**
3. VCJH correction functions give energy-stable FR schemes "having $(2K-1)$th order of super-accuracy with $K+1$ solution points" for 1-D linear advection (§2.2, citing [15, 20]). **Stated**
4. With $N+1$ subcells the projection $P$ (Eqs. 23–24) is a non-singular square matrix and the reconstruction is $P^{-1}$, so Eq. (25) holds exactly and conservation between subcell means and the FR cell mean is kept (§3.1). **Stated**
5. A strict maximum principle ($\delta_i=0$ in Eq. 26) misjudges constant regions through round-off and makes the scheme dissipative and at most second-order accurate [39]; the relaxation $\delta_i=\max(\delta_0,\epsilon_{\mathrm{DMP}}(\max-\min))$ (Eq. 27) with $\delta_0=10^{-4}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{DMP}}=10^{-3}$ (as in Dumbser et al. [40]) admits small oscillations but keeps high order at smooth extrema (§3.2). **Stated**
6. The cell-mean maximum principle suffices for $N=2$ and 3 but "will miss out spurious oscillations within a cell and does not work well for P4 above", hence the subcell test (Eqs. 28–29) for $N\ge4$ (§3.2). **Stated** (not shown separately)
7. With the maximum principle alone, the FR–subcell data transfer (Eqs. 24–25) near a shock produces a Runge phenomenon [50] with perturbations above the steady-state tolerance; the shock cell switches between FR and FV every few steps and the maximum-pressure history cannot converge to $10^{-4}$ (§3.2). **Stated** (mechanism); the effect **Shown** in Fig. 6b
8. The KXRCF boundary jump $I_i$ (Eq. 30) is $O(h^{N+1})$ in smooth regions and $O(1)$ near discontinuities [44]; normalized, $I^{\mathrm{KXRCF}}_i=I_i/(h^{(N+1)/2}\{u\}_i^n)$ (Eq. 32) tends to zero in smooth regions and to infinity near discontinuities as $h\to0$ (§3.3). **Stated** (cited)
9. The thresholds $c_{\mathrm{sm}}=10^{-3}$ and $c_{\mathrm{dc}}=1.0$ are used in all examples (§3.3). **Stated**
10. The left and right neighbours of a troubled cell are also marked (§3.4, Algorithm 1). **Stated**
11. On linear advection FR2-TVD attains third order in $L^2$ and $L^\infty$, e.g. $L^2$ error $3.47388\times10^{-10}$ at 1280 elements, orders 2.999–3.003 (Table 1); FR2-God gives identical results because no cell is flagged (§5.1). **Shown**
12. Sod (100 cells, $t=0.2$, CFL 0.2): no spurious oscillations; FR2-TVD resolves the contact better than FR2-God; with pressure as detection variable the contact is flagged only intermittently (not between $t=0.07$ and 0.14), and density "may be a more suitable detect variable" (§5.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
13. Double rarefaction (100 cells, $t=0.15$): no oscillations and agreement with the exact solution; troubled cells follow the rarefaction heads and the low-density centre, then disappear (§5.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
14. Woodward–Colella blast waves (400 cells, $t=0.038$, reference second-order TVD on 10 000 cells): both FR2 variants capture the structures without oscillations, FR2-TVD with higher resolution at contacts and extrema (§5.4, Fig. 5). **Shown**
15. Stable detonation $f=1.8$ ($A=145.69$): with the pre-detection FR2–FR5 with Godunov or TVD subcells converge to steady state; without it the Godunov-subcell schemes fail to converge "as a result of improper tracing of the shock front" (§5.5, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
16. Without the pre-detection the TVD-subcell schemes converge but mark many more cells; time-averaged troubled cells per step ($t=0$–120, 20 cells per half-reaction length) are 5.15/6.13/6.16/6.39 (God) and 5.28/5.81/6.47/7.12 (TVD) for FR2–FR5 with it, and 84.98/45.52/17.57/17.43 (God) and 43.79/60.26/14.47/15.09 (TVD) without it (Table 2). **Shown**
17. Reference steady values: von Neumann pressure 75.58 [62] and exact ZND post-shock pressure 75.79 [63] (§5.5, Fig. 6 caption). **Stated**
18. At equal degrees of freedom (20 cells per half-reaction length for FR2, 60 for WENO) FR2-TVD shock resolution is "close to" WENO-JS and WENO-Z and FR2-God more diffusive; WENO-JS and WENO-Z produce non-physical oscillations near the end of the reaction zone and behind it that the FR schemes do not (§5.5, Fig. 9). **Shown**
19. The pre-detection clusters troubled cells around the detonation front; without it more cells are marked to its left (§5.5, Fig. 10). **Shown**
20. Slightly unstable $f=1.6$ ($A=230.75$, $t=80$): FR-God results drift in phase and peaks from the reference after $t=40$; FR2–FR5-TVD agree with each other and the reference, and FR2-TVD matches it better than finite-difference WENO-JS5 and WENO-Z5 at equal degrees of freedom (§5.6, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
21. The "lipped" peak-pressure curves arise because the pressure right behind the shock can drop below that further behind ([64]) (§5.6). **Stated**
22. Multimode $f=1.4$ ($A=411.98$, $t=100$): two distinct peak values per period; all FR schemes agree with the reference at 160 cells per half-reaction length except FR2-God; TVD subcells perform better; FR2-TVD is best at equal degrees of freedom, FR2-God below WENO-JS (§5.7, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
23. In the $f=1.4$ case the initial front emits spurious left-moving compression waves that are flagged twice and develop into a chain of shocks (§5.7, Fig. 16). **Shown**
24. Chaotic $f=1.3$ ($A=583.71$, $t=100$): four WENO variants and FR2/FR3-TVD diverge from each other near the front over time, "verifying the chaotic property"; FR2-God differs most because of its higher dissipation (§5.8, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
25. The pre-detection reduces cost (smooth and strongly discontinuous cells skip the maximum-principle test) and avoids the frequent FR/FV switching that makes the solution "hard to converge to steady state" (§6). **Shown** (Table 2, Figs. 6–7, 10)
26. "The FR2-TVD scheme has higher resolution than the WENO-Z5 scheme under same degree of freedoms" (§6, abstract); §5.5 words the stable case as "close to". **Shown**
27. Time integration is third-order SSP Runge–Kutta [57] with CFL $=1/(2N+1)$; in the convergence test $\Delta t=\mathrm{CFL}\,\Delta x^{(N+1)/3}$ (§5, §5.1). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (17):** the sum runs over $k$ but the term is printed $\hat f_j^\delta\,\frac{dl_k}{dr}(r_j)$, while Eq. (10) writes $\hat f^{\delta D}=\sum_j\hat f_j^\delta l_j$.
- **§5.7 and Fig. 15:** the text says "The density profiles at t = 80 … are shown in Fig. 15"; the caption reads "Comparison of pressure profiles … with f = 1.4 at t = 100".
- **Eq. (30):** the jumps carry the superscript $n$, while Algorithm 1 applies the pre-detection to the candidate solution at $t^{n+1}$.
- **§1:** "Andrew et al. [4]", while ref. [4] is Henrick, Aslam & Powers.
- **References:** [19] (Godunov & Bohachevsky) and [51] (Godunov) print the same journal, volume, issue and pages (Mat. Sb. 47(3), 271–306, 1959) with different titles and author lists; [64] prints "Karagozian" twice in its author list, the year 1970 and no pages; [11] prints "Grabill, J." and [13] "Crabill, J.".
- **§4.1:** $E$ is called "the specific total energy" while Eq. (35) is an energy per unit volume.
- **Typos:** "cell interface vales" (§3.3), "shaper detonation front" (§5.5), "since we us the pressure" (§5.4).
- *(compiler check, not in the sources)* From Eqs. (44), (46)–(48) and (52) with $\gamma=1.2$, $q_0=50$, $E_a=50$, taking the $u_+$ root and a midpoint quadrature of Eq. (52), the compiler's computation gives $A=145.69$, 411.98 and 583.70 for $f=1.8$, 1.4 and 1.3 (printed 145.69, 411.98, 583.71) and $A=231.16$ for $f=1.6$, where 230.75 is printed.
