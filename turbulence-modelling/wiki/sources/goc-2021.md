# Summary

Goc et al. (2021) report a systematic [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
campaign on the [JAXA Standard Model](/benchmarks/jaxa-standard-model.md), a
high-lift civil-transport half model in landing configuration studied by the
Third AIAA High-Lift Prediction Workshop, whose steady RANS submissions (mostly
[Spalart–Allmaras](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) or
[$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)) scatter widely near maximum
lift. Two independent low-dissipation codes are used:
[CharLES](/software/charles.md), a compressible finite-volume solver on Voronoi
grids with entropy- and [kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
fluxes, and Alya, an incompressible Galerkin finite-element solver with an
energy-conserving convective form. The authors stress that
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) is detrimental
for coarse LES; the SGS stress is closed explicitly, with the
[Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md) in free air and the
[dynamic Smagorinsky model](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) in the tunnel cases,
so this is explicit-model LES on low-dissipation numerics, not
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md). The wall condition is the
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md): a constant-stress
layer with a mixing-length eddy viscosity, matched to the LES velocity.

At a baseline resolution sized for about ten cells across the boundary layer
at the main-element trailing edge, both codes capture the lift curve, the
maximum lift and the stall onset; refinement improves lift, but in free air a
drag shift against corrected tunnel data and the post-stall pitching-moment
kickback persist. Simulating the wind tunnel, its 70 mm offset and a
flow-through nacelle removes the drag shift relative to uncorrected data and
recovers the inboard separation and the kickback. The fine in-tunnel grid
over-predicts lift at low angles because a flap separation bubble collapses
under refinement.

The paper argues that earlier [grid-point estimates](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)
(Spalart 1997; Choi & Moin 2012; Slotnick et al. 2014) are too pessimistic for integrated forces: the
wall-modelled grid count scales as $Re_c$ and the cost as $Re_c^{4/3}$, and it
cites [boundary-layer-conforming grids](/methods/boundary-layer-conforming-grid.md)
as a further saving. Medium-grid LES costs about as much as a workshop RANS
run, a 30-flow-pass run takes 48 min on 96 V100 GPUs, and Voronoi grid
generation is negligible. The authors conclude that wall-modelled LES of
high-lift aircraft is "on the threshold of readiness for industrial use",
ahead of the NASA CFD Vision 2030 target. The introduction also places the
work against [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md), wall-resolved
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) and
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) (delayed detached-eddy
simulation).

# Key claims

1. LES of a realistic aircraft in landing configuration is achieved with
   accuracy near industrial requirements and turnaround below one day on
   modest resources, ahead of the Vision 2030 "technology demonstration"
   target of the late 2020s (Abstract, §1, §5). **Shown**
2. $C_{L,\max}$ is "consistently being predicted to within five lift counts of
   the experimental value"; at maximum lift $\Delta C_L\le0.03$ and
   $\Delta C_D\le0.0010$, against an experimental repeatability of 0.03 in
   $C_L$ and 0.0015 in $C_D$ (Abstract, §1). **Shown**
3. The workshop results (35 participants, 79 data sets, mostly steady RANS
   with SA or SST) cluster in the linear lift range but scatter significantly
   near maximum lift (§1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
4. Earlier grid estimates were too pessimistic for integrated forces because
   they were not sensitized to particular quantities of interest; Reynolds
   stresses and wall-pressure fluctuations need more resolution than mean
   forces and pressures (§1). **Stated**
5. Resolution needs are driven in part by inviscid phenomena (strong
   leading-edge acceleration at high incidence), while those of laminar
   boundary layers and transition appear reduced at high incidence, where
   transition is abrupt near the leading edges (§1, §3, §3.2). **Stated**
   (the outboard under-resolution of Fig. 9 is **Shown**)
6. For wall-modelled LES the grid count scales as $Re_c$, and with the
   time-step reduction the cost as $Re_c^{4/3}$ (§2.2, §4; the grid count citing Choi & Moin
   2012). **Stated**
7. The error of the equilibrium wall model can be bounded by the resolution of
   the matching location relative to $\delta^*$ or $\theta$, even with pressure
   gradients, because the outer LES resolves the non-equilibrium effects (§2.2,
   citing Bose & Park 2018). **Stated**
8. The JSM flow has $Re_\tau\approx5000$, based on the peak skin friction and
   the LES-predicted $\delta_{99}$ (§2.2). **Shown**
9. In free air at baseline resolution both solvers agree ahead of stall
   ($\alpha<19^\circ$); charLES shows a lift shift of 0.05–0.08 at lower angles
   and approximately predicts $C_{L,\max}$ and stall onset, while Alya shows a
   deeper stall (§3.1, Fig. 5a). **Shown**
10. Free-air drag is shifted by $\Delta C_D\approx0.03$ against corrected
    tunnel data, as in all workshop submissions; the authors attribute this to
    the uncertainty of the tunnel corrections, since their in-tunnel runs show
    no shift against uncorrected data (§3.1, Fig. 5b). **Shown**
11. Friction drag is at most about 10% of the total at the lowest incidence
    (about 5% at the highest); high-lift flow is "similar to a bluff body flow"
    (§3.1). **Shown**
12. The free-air charLES pitching moment is within repeatability up to stall
    but misses the post-stall kink caused by inboard separation at the
    wing–body junction (§3.1, Fig. 5c). **Shown**
13. Refining Alya from 30 M elements (about 5 across the boundary layer) to
    180 M (about 10) brings the results inside experimental repeatability at
    all angles considered (§3.2, Fig. 6a). **Shown**
14. A targeted 2 B-element Alya run at $\alpha=21.57^\circ$ agrees with the
    intermediate grid in $C_L$, so the weak inboard separation against oil flow
    is attributed to missing tunnel effects, not to resolution (§3.2, Fig. 7).
    **Shown**
15. Sectional $C_p$ at $\alpha=18.58^\circ$ agrees with experiment for both
    solvers on the finer grids, so the lift agreement is not a cancellation of
    errors (§3.2, Fig. 8). **Shown**
16. On the charLES sequence (9, 32, 157 M cells) $C_p$ collapses at all
    stations except on the coarsest grid at the outboard station, where the
    slat and main-element suction is under-predicted, linked to under-resolved
    leading-edge acceleration (§3.2, Fig. 9). **Shown**
17. Even at the finest free-air resolutions drag errors persist with both
    solvers (§3.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
18. In-tunnel charLES runs (10, 42, 185 M cells, nacelle on) converge $C_L$ at
    maximum lift to within repeatability on the medium and fine grids, converge
    drag to the uncorrected experiment on the finest grid, and recover the
    pitching-moment kickback with an incidence shift of about $1^\circ$ and the
    inboard separation seen in oil flow (§3.3, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
19. The fine in-tunnel grid over-predicts lift in the linear range because
    the flap separation bubble present in the experiment and on the coarser
    grids disappears; a similar collapse under refinement was reported for a
    Gaussian bump; cause and remedy are open (§3.3). **Shown**
20. The tunnel side-wall $\delta_{99}$ ahead of the model agrees within 10 mm
    with the measured 140 mm using about ten LES points across it (§3.3).
    **Shown**
21. charLES cost at $\alpha=18.58^\circ$, 30 flow passes (Table 1): 32 M cells,
    1.4k core-hours per pass, 21 h on 2000 Ivy Bridge cores or 48 min on 96
    V100 GPUs; 157 M cells, 12.3k core-hours per pass, 7.5 days on CPUs or 7 h
    on GPUs. **Shown**
22. Alya cost (Table 2): 30 M elements, 0.22k core-hours per pass, 9 h on 720
    CPUs; 180 M, 4.26k, 63 h on 2016 CPUs; 2000 M, 111.11k, 35 h on 96 000 CPUs.
    **Shown**
23. charLES strong-scales with efficiency above 80% down to about 2000 cells
    per core or $10^6$ cells per GPU; for 157 M cells this means about
    $80\times10^3$ cores or 160 GPUs and "a wall-clock time of <4 h" (§4).
    **Stated**
24. A workshop RANS reference (OpenFOAM, SA, 109 M cells) costs about
    $60\times10^3$ core-hours against about $40\times10^3$ (medium) and
    $360\times10^3$ (fine) for LES (§4). **Shown**
25. Voronoi grid generation is negligible: 157 M cells in about 2 min on
    $10^3$ cores, $O(10^{10})$-cell grids in under 30 min on $40\times10^3$
    cores (§4, Fig. 15). **Shown** / **Stated**
26. Extrapolating with cost $\propto Re_c^{4/3}$, a turnaround of a few days on
    about 100 GPUs is possible at $Re_c\sim10^7$, or several hours at the
    strong-scaling limit (§4). **Derived**
27. Low-dissipation discretization is essential: numerical dissipation is
    "extremely detrimental" for coarse LES, and many unsteady solvers inherit
    dissipative RANS discretizations (§2.3). **Stated**
28. Compressible (charLES) and incompressible (Alya) solutions differ only
    slightly, despite a peak $M\approx0.75$ on the slat near maximum lift
    (§2.1). **Stated** (no direct comparison figure)

# Printed slips

- **Viscous stress (p. 4).** Printed
  "$\tilde\sigma_{ij}=\mu(\tilde T)(\tilde S_{ij}-1/3\tilde S_{kk}\delta_{ij})$"
  with $\tilde S_{ij}=\frac12(\partial\tilde u_i/\partial x_j+\partial\tilde u_j/\partial x_i)$;
  the Newtonian deviatoric stress is $2\mu S^d_{ij}$, so the factor two is
  missing *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Table 2 caption.** "Computational cost summary for a grid sequence using
  the Alya solver for the JSM configuration at $\alpha=18.58^\circ$" includes
  the 2000 M row, while §3.2 and the captions of Figs. 6 and 10 place the
  2 B-element refinement at $\alpha=21.57^\circ$.
- **§4.** "a WMLES-type cost inflation of $N\propto Re_c$ as in Choi and Moin
  (1994)": the 1994 paper is the time-step study; the same section later
  attributes the grid-count scaling to Choi and Moin (2012).
- **§4, strong scaling.** At 12.3k core-hours per pass and 30 passes (Table 1),
  perfect scaling to $78.5\times10^3$ cores gives about 4.7 h, and 7 h on 96
  GPUs scaled to 157 GPUs gives about 4.3 h, both above the stated "<4 h" even
  at 100% efficiency *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Table 1, 157 M row.** $12.3\text{k}\times30/2000$ core-hours give 184.5 h,
  7.7 days, printed "7.5 days" *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §1: "the Japanese Exploration Agency (JAXA) Standard Model"; the abstract has
  "Japanese Aerospace Exploration Agency".
- §1: "the grand challenge problem of a wall-resolved (large eddy) simulation
  of a powered aircraft", while the quoted Vision 2030 challenge is "LES of a
  powered aircraft configuration across the full flight envelope" and the
  present simulations are wall-modelled.
- The total energy is defined as "$E=\bar\rho\tilde e+\ldots$" while Eq. (2.3)
  uses $\bar E$.
- Typos: "delayed detatched-eddy simulation" (§1); "low dissipation spatial
  discretion" (§2.3); "breakdown between fiction and form drag" (§3.1);
  "boundary-layer-confirming grids" (§2.2); "Pleaides cluster" (§4); "compared
  withviscous" (§2.2).
