# Summary

Luo & Wu (2021) modify WENO-Z+ of Acker et al. (2016), a fifth-order
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) variant whose weights carry an extra term
$\lambda\eta_k$ that raises the weight of the less-smooth substencil. The
paper has no turbulence-modelling content. It enters this bundle through
its WENO variant and its account of
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md).

The approximate dispersion relation (ADR), computed numerically by
Pirozzoli's method, shows that WENO-Z+ has negative dissipation around the
reduced wavenumber $w\approx1.1834$. Lowering $\lambda$ hardly removes it
and degrades the spectral properties elsewhere. WENO-Z+M makes two changes.
The extra term is weakened to $\eta_k^{1/q}$ with $q=2$, the smallest value
whose ADR shows no negative dissipation. The constant $\lambda$ becomes
$\lambda z$, where $z\le1$ is the ratio of the WENO-Z weight of the
less-smooth substencil to its ideal weight. $\lambda$ is then raised to 579,
the largest value with which the interacting-blast-wave problem runs.

The authors argue that WENO-Z+-type schemes can resolve better than their
underlying linear scheme, the fifth-order upwind UD5, whereas earlier WENO
schemes are always worse. They split the dissipation of a WENO scheme into
the linear dissipation of the underlying scheme and a nonlinear part from
the weighting. A less-smooth-substencil weight below its ideal value adds
dissipation; one above it adds negative dissipation that partly offsets the
linear part. They present this designed nonlinear mechanism as an
alternative to optimized linear coefficients, the route of
[low-dissipation low-dispersion schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md).
On one-dimensional Euler tests, two-dimensional Euler tests and a
shock/shear-layer interaction, WENO-Z+M is reported to resolve better than
WENO-Z, WENO-Z+, ninth-order WENO-Z and WENO-IS-I. These comparisons are
visual except for one two-dimensional accuracy table, where WENO-Z+M reaches
about half the UD5 error on the finest grid.

# Key claims

1. In WENO-Z the weights of the less-smooth substencils are always below the ideal weights; raising them (WENO-Z+) moves the nonlinear weights toward the ideal weights (§1). **Stated**
2. WENO-Z+ improves markedly on the Shu–Osher and Titarev–Toro problems, beating ninth-order WENO-Z there, but shows "little improvement" over WENO-Z on multiscale problems such as double Mach reflection and Rayleigh–Taylor instability (§1, recalling Acker et al. 2016). **Stated**
3. Acker et al. proved that WENO-Z+ keeps the ENO property for $\lambda=O(1)$; such a $\lambda$ can still cause negative dissipation in smooth regions (§2.3). **Stated** (proof in the cited work)
4. Acker et al. fixed $\lambda$ on the Titarev–Toro problem: $\lambda=\Delta x^{1/2}$ amplified smooth waves, $\lambda=\Delta x$ attenuated them, and $\lambda=\Delta x^{2/3}$ was the compromise (§2.3). **Stated**
5. For larger $\lambda$ WENO-Z+ has negative dissipation in a range of wavenumbers; lowering $\lambda$ increases dissipation non-uniformly, and at $w\approx1.1834$ the negative dissipation "is very hard to be eliminated", so reducing $\lambda$ "sacrifices the spectral properties in vain" (§3.1, Fig. 1). **Shown** (ADR)
6. Replacing $\eta_k$ by $\eta_k^M=\eta_k^{1/q}$ weakens the extra term, since the smoothness indicators greatly exceed the global indicator in smooth solutions; ADR shows no negative dissipation at $q=2$, and larger $q$ damages resolution (§3.2, Eq. 7). **Shown** (no curves for $q$ printed)
7. With Eq. (7), increasing $\lambda$ ($\lambda=1$, 25, 600) improves the ADR without negative dissipation (§3.2, Fig. 2). **Shown**
8. $z\le1$; $z=1+O(\Delta x^6)$ in smooth regions and $z=O(\Delta x^4)+\Theta(\varepsilon_W^2)$ at a discontinuity, so $\lambda^M$ weakens the extra term at shocks (§3.2, Eq. 8). **Stated** (orders cited from Borges et al. and Acker et al.)
9. WENO-Z+M runs the interacting-blast-wave problem with $\lambda$ up to 579 and fails at 580, so $\lambda=579$ is adopted (§3.2, Fig. 13). **Shown** (one problem, $N_x=401$)
10. WENO-Z+M has spectral properties markedly better than WENO-Z and WENO-Z+ (§3.2, Fig. 3). **Shown**
11. The ADR curves of WENO-Z+ cross those of UD5 at $w\approx1.3418$ (better below, worse above); WENO-Z+M is better than UD5 over "a much broader wavenumber range" (§4, Fig. 4). **Shown**
12. Earlier shock-capturing schemes, earlier WENO included, always have spectral properties worse than their linear schemes (§4). **Stated**
13. At $w_1=1.25$ WENO-Z+ gives the less-smooth substencil a weight above its ideal value at every interface; at $w_2=1.45$ its weights almost match those of WENO-Z, below the ideal ones (§4, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
14. A WENO scheme has two dissipation sources, the linear dissipation of the underlying scheme and the nonlinear dissipation of the weighting; a less-smooth-substencil weight below its ideal value adds positive dissipation, one above it adds negative dissipation that partly offsets the linear dissipation (§4). **Stated** (supported by Figs. 5–7)
15. At $w_3=1.1834$ WENO-Z+ raises the less-smooth weight too much and has negative dissipation, while WENO-Z+M controls the increase; at $w_2=1.45$ WENO-Z+M keeps that weight above its ideal value at every interface (§4, Fig. 7). **Shown**
16. Broadband linear wave ($N_x=25$, six cells per shortest wavelength): WENO-Z+M resolves better than UD5, WENO-IS-I and ninth-order WENO-Z; WENO-Z+ shows no visible gain (§5.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
17. Broadband sound waves ($N_x=129$): WENO-Z+M resolves markedly better than UD5, WENO-IS-I and ninth-order WENO-Z (§5.2.1, Fig. 9). **Shown**
18. Shu–Osher (201 points): WENO-Z+ improves the high-frequency waves but not the discontinuity; WENO-Z+M improves both (§5.2.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
19. Titarev–Toro (1001 points): WENO-Z+ resolves the high-frequency waves somewhat better than WENO-Z+M because $\lambda=\Delta x^{2/3}$ admits negative dissipation, and "falsely amplifies" the smooth waves ahead of and behind the shock; WENO-IS-I also shows negative dissipation, less severely; WENO-Z+M is free of it (§5.2.3, Fig. 11). **Shown**
20. Lax problem (101 points): WENO-Z+M is less dissipative than WENO-IS-I and oscillates less; ninth-order WENO-Z "seems to be less dissipative" but oscillates more (§5.2.4, Fig. 12). **Shown**
21. Interacting blast waves ($N_x=401$): ninth-order WENO-Z fails and seventh order is shown; WENO-Z+M is better than WENO-Z+, WENO-IS-I and seventh-order WENO-Z (§5.2.5, Fig. 13). **Shown**
22. Two-dimensional Euler accuracy test (Table 1): the WENO-Z error approaches that of UD5 on refinement, and WENO-Z+ has about twice the WENO-Z error. At $641\times641$ WENO-Z+M has $L_1=0.3913\times10^{-13}$ and $L_2=0.4494\times10^{-13}$ against $0.7896\times10^{-13}$ and $0.8770\times10^{-13}$ for UD5. On the two coarsest grids the printed WENO-Z+M $L_1$ errors ($0.4060\times10^{-5}$ at $21\times21$, $0.8288\times10^{-7}$ at $41\times41$) are above those of UD5 ($0.2612\times10^{-5}$, $0.8231\times10^{-7}$) (§5.3.1, Table 1). **Shown**
23. The explanation of claim 22: for a smooth enough solution WENO-Z+M always gives the less-smooth substencil a weight above its ideal value, so its dispersion and dissipation errors are below those of UD5 (§5.3.1). **Stated**
24. Two-dimensional Riemann problem, configuration 3 ($401\times401$): WENO-Z+ barely improves on WENO-Z; WENO-Z+M has markedly higher multiscale resolution than WENO-IS-I and ninth-order WENO-Z and a more symmetric solution (§5.3.2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
25. Double Mach reflection ($961\times241$): ninth-order WENO-Z needs CFL 0.1, so all schemes run at 0.1; WENO-Z+M gives the best roll-up resolution (§5.3.3, Fig. 15). **Shown**
26. Forward-facing step ($481\times161$, $t=4$): seventh- and ninth-order WENO-Z fail; WENO-Z+M resolves the shear layer best (§5.3.4, Fig. 16). **Shown**
27. Shock/shear-layer interaction (Navier–Stokes, $251\times51$, $t=120$): the resolution of WENO-Z+M "is the best among all the employed schemes" (§5.4, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
28. A well-designed nonlinear weighting can raise resolution beyond the linear scheme, an alternative to optimizing linear coefficients and against the view that nonlinear shock-capturing mechanisms always harm spectral properties (§6). **Stated**

The weights (Eqs. 4–9), on the five-point stencil of fifth-order WENO with
substencils $k=0,1,2$, ideal weights $d_k=(1/10,6/10,3/10)$ and Jiang–Shu
indicators $\mathrm{IS}_k$, written in the symbols of the
[WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) page (unnormalized weights $a_k$, nonlinear
weights $\mathsf w_k=a_k/\sum_la_l$, global indicator
$\mathrm{IS}_{\mathrm G}$, ratio $r_k$; see the Notation map):

$$a^{\mathrm Z}_k=d_k\Big[1+\Big(\frac{\mathrm{IS}_{\mathrm G}}{\mathrm{IS}_k+\varepsilon_W}\Big)^p\Big],\qquad a^{\mathrm{Z+M}}_k=d_k\big[1+r_k^p+\lambda_{\mathrm Z}z_{\mathrm M}\,r_k^{-1/q}\big],$$

$$r_k=\frac{\mathrm{IS}_{\mathrm G}+\varepsilon_W}{\mathrm{IS}_k+\varepsilon_W},\quad z_{\mathrm M}=\frac{1+r_{\min}^2}{\sum_kd_k(1+r_k^2)},\quad \mathrm{IS}_{\mathrm G}=|\mathrm{IS}_2-\mathrm{IS}_0|,$$

with $p=q=2$, $\lambda_{\mathrm Z}=579$, $\varepsilon_W=10^{-40}$; WENO-Z+ is
$d_k[1+r_k^p+\lambda_{\mathrm Z}r_k^{-1}]$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm Z}=\Delta x^{2/3}$. All schemes use
characteristic decomposition, global Lax–Friedrichs flux splitting and
third-order TVD Runge–Kutta; WENO-IS-I runs with $p=1$ following its
authors; viscous terms of §5.4 use sixth-order central differences.
