# Summary

Qi et al. (2022) propose the quasi-dynamic SGS kinetic-energy equation
model (QKM), a one-equation eddy-viscosity model for compressible
[large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md). The exact
compressible transport equation of $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ (Chai & Mahesh 2012)
is solved with the Favre-filtered equations, and every unclosed term is
modelled with the leading term of the series expansion of a filtered
product, $\overline{fg}-\bar f\bar g\approx C_0\Delta_k^2\,\partial_k\bar f\,\partial_k\bar g$:
the [gradient model](/methods/gradient-model.md) form, which is the
classical model for $C_0=1/12$. Two constraints replace the test filter of
the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md): $C_0$ is fixed
locally by requiring the trace of the expanded stress to equal
$2k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ (with density), and the
[Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) coefficient by matching the
anisotropic part of the Smagorinsky subgrid energy flux to that of the
expanded flux; the isotropic part of the stress is taken from
$k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ instead of Yoshizawa's model
([Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)). The same
$C_0$ closes the SGS heat flux (a dynamic $Pr_{\mathrm{sgs}}$), the SGS
species flux (a dynamic $Sc_{\mathrm{sgs}}$) and the
[pressure-dilatation](/definitions/pressure-dilatation.md), solenoidal and
dilatational dissipation terms of the $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ equation.

[A priori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) tests on DNS
of a Mach-1.5 [channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) give
correlation coefficients near or above 0.95 for the modelled subgrid
energy flux and heat flux, far above the dynamic Smagorinsky model (DSM)
and the dynamic $k$-equation model. A posteriori LES of channels at Mach
1.5 (three grids) and 3.0, a transitional Mach-2.25 flat-plate
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) (three grids,
van Driest [transformed](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
velocity) and a spherical converging Richtmyer–Meshkov instability put the
QKM closest to DNS, with the weakest degradation on coarse grids; on the
coarsest boundary-layer grid its subgrid energy flux reproduces the DNS
distribution including the [backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md)
regions. The structural ([gradient](/methods/gradient-model.md),
[scale-similarity](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md)) and
eddy-viscosity ([Vreman](/methods/vreman-model.md) and others) families are
reviewed in the introduction. The cost per step is about that of the
dynamic $k$-equation model.

# Key claims

The subgrid energy flux is the registry's $\Pi=-\tau_{ij}\tilde S_{ij}$
(the source's $\Pi_\Delta=-\Pi$); the expansion parameter $\alpha$ of the
source is written $\alpha_{\mathrm s}$ (page-local, as on the [gradient-model](/methods/gradient-model.md) page).

1. Most current SGS models assume approximately isotropic subgrid scales and were built for incompressible flow and extended directly to compressible flow, so reliable results are not ensured (§1). **Stated**
2. Eddy-viscosity models are purely dissipative, correlate poorly with the real SGS stress and cannot reproduce the distribution of the subgrid energy flux; gradient and scale-similarity models correlate highly but are unstable (Vreman et al. 1996) (§1). **Stated**
3. The isotropic part of the SGS stress and the subgrid kinetic energy satisfy $\tau_{kk}=2\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ (Eq. 2.12). **Derived**
4. Keeping the first term of the series expansion gives $\tau_{ij}\approx C_0\Delta_k^2\bar\rho\,\partial_k\tilde u_i\,\partial_k\tilde u_j$ (Eq. 3.12), the gradient model for $C_0=1/12$; the higher-order terms are "small enough compared with the first term" (citing Vreman et al. 1996), and dropping them avoids additional boundary conditions (§3.2). **Derived** (truncation) / **Stated** (smallness)
5. $C_0$ is determined locally, without test filtering, from $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$: $C_0=2k_{\mathrm{sgs}}/(\Delta_l^2\,\partial_l\tilde u_k\,\partial_l\tilde u_k)$ (Eq. 3.15). **Derived**
6. The Smagorinsky coefficient $C_s^2$ (the source's $C_{sm}$) is fixed by equating the anisotropic part of the Smagorinsky subgrid energy flux to the anisotropic part of the expanded one (Eq. 3.21); the isotropic part of the stress is taken from Eq. (2.12), not from Yoshizawa's $C_I$ model (Eq. 2.15) (§3.2). **Derived**; *(compiler check, not in the sources)* inserting Eq. (3.21) into $\mu_t=\bar\rho C_s^2\Delta^2\lvert\tilde S\rvert$ cancels $\Delta^2\lvert\tilde S\rvert$ and leaves $\mu_t=-\Pi^A_\Delta/(2\tilde S^d_{ij}\tilde S_{ij})$, which is independent of the definition of $\Delta$ and negative where $\Pi^A_\Delta>0$; the paper prints no clipping or averaging.
7. Because the same filter acts at a grid point, $\alpha_{\mathrm s}$ (hence $C_0$) is assumed equal for all expanded quantities ($\tau_{ij}$, $Q_j$, $\Pi_p$, $\varepsilon_s$, $\varepsilon_d$) (§3.2). **Stated** (assumption), supported a priori in Figs. 4 and 15. **Shown**
8. A dynamic SGS Prandtl number follows from equating the divergences of the modelled (Eq. 2.16) and expanded (Eq. 3.22) SGS heat fluxes (Eq. 3.23), and a dynamic SGS Schmidt number in the same way (Eq. 4.8). **Derived**
9. A priori (channel, Mach 1.5, DNS $900\times201\times300$, box filter in $x$ and $z$ at $6\Delta_z$): the QKM correlation coefficients of the subgrid energy flux and of the wall-normal SGS heat flux $Q_2$ are "almost higher than 0.95" across the channel; those of DSM and the dynamic $k$-equation model are "much lower" (Fig. 2a,b). **Shown**
10. A priori: the "modified gradient model" of QKM keeps a high correlation with $\tau_{12}$ and $\tau_{22}$, and the "modified SM" correlates better than DSM and the dynamic $k$-equation model (Fig. 2c,d). **Shown**
11. A priori: the correlation of the modelled $\partial_jJ_j$ is "nearly 1.0"; those of $\Pi_p$, $\varepsilon_s$, $\varepsilon_d$ are "relatively high" (Fig. 3). **Shown**
12. A priori: the $C_0$ inferred from each expanded term agrees with that of Eq. (3.15) in trend and value in the channel (Fig. 4) and at $y^+=15$ along the flat plate with filter scale $3\Delta_z$ (Fig. 15). **Shown**
13. Channel, Mach 1.5: QKM reproduces the DNS bulk parameters best; $Re_\tau$ (DNS 220) is 220 against DSM 213 and dynamic $k$-equation 215 on LES-grid1, 221 against 208 and 210 on LES-grid2, 218 against 201 and 208 on LES-grid3 (Tables 2–4). **Shown**
14. Channel, Mach 1.5: as the grid coarsens, QKM keeps the van Driest velocity and the mean temperature close to DNS in the log region, better than the dynamic $k$-equation model, itself better than DSM; the authors call this "a certain characteristic of scale adaptivity" (Fig. 5). **Shown**
15. Channel, Mach 1.5, LES-grid2: QKM gives better total and resolved Reynolds shear stress, turbulent heat flux, resolved r.m.s. velocities, $k$ and $\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$, and density and temperature fluctuations (Figs. 6–9). **Shown**
16. A posteriori, $C_0$ peaks at approximately 0.08 and $Pr_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ at approximately 0.8 in the channel (LES-grid2) (Fig. 10). **Shown** (values stated in the text)
17. QKM produces "more abundant" vortical structures, with many more small-scale vortices near the wall than DSM and the dynamic $k$-equation model (Fig. 11). **Shown**
18. Cost per step on 112 CPUs: DSM $4.13\times10^{-2}$ s, dynamic $k$-equation $7.38\times10^{-2}$ s, QKM $7.22\times10^{-2}$ s, described as "approximately the same time, slightly longer than the DSM" (Table 5). **Shown**; *(compiler check, not in the sources)* QKM costs 1.75 times DSM per step.
19. Channel, Mach 3.0, $Re=4880$ (one grid, $86\times97\times86$): QKM gives "obviously better" van Driest velocity, mean temperature, Reynolds stress and turbulent heat flux than DSM and the dynamic $k$-equation model; $Re_\tau$ 452 (QKM), 448, 444 (Table 7, no DNS row; Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
20. Supersonic flat plate (Mach 2.25): QKM predicts the transition onset, the transitional peak of $C_f$ and the van Driest velocity at $x/L=8.8$ on all three LES grids; on the coarsest grid C the DSM and dynamic $k$-equation results are not acceptable (Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
21. Flat plate, grid C: the QKM resolved wall-normal and spanwise intensities and turbulent heat flux are closer to DNS; streamwise intensities show "no great difference" except below $y^+=10$, where QKM is better (Fig. 18). **Shown**
22. Flat plate, grid C, a posteriori at $y^+=15$: the QKM subgrid energy flux resembles the DNS one in distribution, intensity, structure "and even the energy backscatters", and likewise the wall-normal SGS heat flux (Figs. 19–20). **Shown**
23. Spherical converging Richtmyer–Meshkov instability (SF$_6$/N$_2$, Mach about 1.5, Atwood number 0.678; DNS $2048^3$, LES $384^3$): QKM predicts the inner and outer mixing-layer radii and the bubble and spike heights well over the whole process; DSM and the dynamic $k$-equation model deviate for $0.075<t<0.12$ ms; the constant-coefficient Smagorinsky model (printed coefficient $C=0.1$, $Pr_{\mathrm{sgs}}=0.9$, $Sc_{\mathrm{sgs}}=0.35$) "grossly underestimates" the bubble and spike heights for $0.04<t<0.12$ ms (Figs. 22–23). **Shown**
24. The DSM and dynamic $k$-equation deviation in this flow is attributed ("Perhaps") to strong anisotropy and inhomogeneity, which violates the scale invariance required by the classical dynamic procedure (§4.3). **Stated** (conjecture)
25. The Smagorinsky failure in this flow is consistent with its known inability to predict transition (Piomelli & Zang 1991; Meneveau & Katz 2000; Sayadi & Moin 2012; Zhou et al. 2019) (§4.3). **Stated**
26. QKM combines the robustness of an eddy viscosity with the high correlation of the gradient model and, by predicting the subgrid energy flux more precisely, "can descript the turbulent cascade accurately" (§5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Prandtl number (§4.1, p. A22-9):** printed "$Pr=\mu C_p\kappa$ is 0.7 ($\kappa$ is the thermal conductivity …)". *(compiler check, not in the sources)* The product is not dimensionless; Eq. (2.7) uses $C_p\mu/P_r$ as a conductivity, consistent with $Pr=\mu C_p/\kappa$.
- **Eqs. (2.3) and (2.7):** (2.3) carries $-\partial\tilde q_j/\partial x_j$ and (2.7) prints $\tilde q_j=\frac{C_p\mu(\tilde T)}{P_r}\frac{\partial\tilde T}{\partial x_j}$ without a minus sign. *(compiler check, not in the sources)* Together they give $-\nabla\cdot(\kappa\nabla\tilde T)$, an anti-diffusive conduction term; the SGS heat-flux model (2.16) carries the minus sign and enters (2.3) the same way.
- **Eq. (3.9):** printed $\alpha=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}2x^2G(x,y)\,dx$, while the text says $C_0=1/12$ gives the gradient model. *(compiler check, not in the sources)* For a box filter of width $\Delta$ ($G=1/\Delta$ on $\lvert x\rvert<\Delta/2$) the printed integral is $\Delta^2/6$, i.e. $C_0=1/6$ with the printed Eq. (3.10), $\alpha=C_0\Delta_k^2$; the second moment without the factor 2 is $\Delta^2/12$. $C_0$ is computed dynamically by Eq. (3.15), so the model itself is unaffected.
- **Name of $\Pi_p$:** "pressure dilatation" in §3.1 (after Eq. 3.7), "pressure diffusion" in §3.2 before Eq. (3.24) and in the Fig. 3 caption; Eq. (3.6) defines a pressure–dilatation correlation.
- **§4.3 against Figs. 24–25:** the text shows fields "at two moments ($t$ = 0.08 and $t$ = 0.12 ms)"; the captions put panels (a2, b2, c2) at $t$ = 0.2 ms.
- **Fig. 23:** axis label "$h_{bs}$ (ms)" for heights defined as differences of radii (Eqs. 4.9–4.10).
- **Reference list:** "Yoshizawa, H.K. 1985" prints a single author with initials "H.K."; "Yu, C., Zuoli, X. & Xinliang, L. 2016" prints given names as surnames; Horiuti (1985) is printed "J. Phys. Soc. 54 (8)" without "Japan"; Métais & Lesieur (1992) is printed "Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 239, 157–94"; "m = 2.25" for the Mach number of Pirozzoli, Grasso & Gatski (2004).
