# Summary

Cogo et al. (2022) present a
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) database of four spatially
developing zero-pressure-gradient
[turbulent boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) over a
cold isothermal wall, $T_w/T_r=0.76$, at $M_\infty=2$ and $5.86$, each at
$Re_\tau\approx450$ and $\approx2000$. The low-Reynolds hypersonic case
matches case M6Tw076 of Zhang, Duan & Choudhari (2018). The open GPU code
STREAmS uses energy-preserving central schemes in smooth regions
([kinetic-energy preservation](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)),
WENO where the Ducros sensor flags shocks, and a locally conservative
Laplacian form of the viscous terms. Inflow is by recycling–rescaling;
the largest grids have $16384\times832\times2048$ points.

Outer-layer superstructures of length $O(10\delta)$ carry low- and high-speed
regions tied to high and low temperature streaks, while near-wall
temperature streaks nearly vanish at Mach 2, which the authors attribute to
stronger effective cooling (lower Eckert number). A density-weighted
momentum-defect threshold detects the turbulent/non-turbulent interface, and
uniform momentum zones, linked to the
[attached-eddy hypothesis](/definitions/attached-eddy-hypothesis.md), are
found at high Mach and Reynolds numbers, together with uniform temperature
zones, reported for the first time.

Among [compressible velocity transformations](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md),
those of Volpiani et al. (2020b) and Griffin, Fu & Moin (2021) collapse all
cases onto the incompressible [law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
through the whole layer, while van Driest and especially Trettel–Larsson
leave a log-law intercept mismatch in the hypersonic cases (Trettel–Larsson
being accurate in [channels](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)). The
generalized temperature–velocity relation of Zhang et al. (2014) beats Walz's.
Reynolds stresses in [Morkovin's scaling](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md)
agree with incompressible DNS at Mach 2; in semi-local scaling the
dissipation in the TKE budget is nearly Mach- and Reynolds-independent,
while production and transport keep a Mach dependence. Spanwise spectra are
compared under a proposed eddy-size scale built on the Griffin-transformed
mean shear.

# Key claims

1. Four DNS of zero-pressure-gradient boundary layers at $M_\infty=2.00$ and $5.86$, $T_w/T_r=0.76$, with $Re_\tau$ ranges 340–620, 1240–2300, 290–520 and 1080–1953 over the domains, extend the database of diabatic high-speed wall turbulence to moderate and high Reynolds numbers in the hypersonic regime (§2, Table 1). **Shown**
2. Matching $T_w/T_r$ does not match wall-cooling effects: the Eckert number $Ec=(\gamma-1)M_\infty^2T_\infty/(T_r-T_w)$ is 3.9 at Mach 2 and 8.0 at Mach 5.86, and the Mach 2 cases behave as more strongly cooled, consistent with Wenzel, Gibis & Kloker (2022) (§2, §3.1, Table 1). **Shown**/**Stated**
3. Resolution: $\Delta x^+\le7.9$, $\Delta z^+\le4.8$, $\Delta y^+_{min}\le0.7$, $\Delta y^+_{max}\le6.5$; the effective spacing $(\Delta x\,\Delta y\,\Delta z)^{1/3}$ is always smaller than $4\eta$ (§2, Table 1). **Stated**
4. Outer-layer superstructures of streamwise length $O(10\delta)$ exist at both Mach numbers, with low- and high-speed regions associated with high and low temperature streaks (§3.1, Figs. 2–3). **Shown**
5. Unlike adiabatic boundary layers (Bernardini & Pirozzoli 2011a), near-wall temperature streaks are hardly detectable at Mach 2 with the present cold wall, where temperature fluctuations are more isotropic than velocity fluctuations (§3.1, Fig. 2c). **Shown**
6. A density-weighted momentum-defect criterion, $M_{def}=0.001$ (Eq. 3.1), detects the turbulent/non-turbulent interface in highly compressible boundary layers, where a kinetic-energy-defect threshold (De Silva et al. 2013) gives inappropriate results; a threshold-sensitivity study shows minor deviations (§3.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
7. Uniform zones of velocity and of temperature exist in supersonic and hypersonic boundary layers at $Re_\tau\approx2000$; uniform temperature zones are documented for the first time (§3.2, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
8. Mean numbers of uniform zones over 84 $x$–$y$ planes: velocity 3.6 (M2H) and 2.5 (M6H); temperature 5.9 (M2H) and 4.9 (M6H); the temperature-to-velocity ratio is about 1.6 and 2. The authors attribute the difference to the non-unit Prandtl number and the diabatic wall, which reduce the velocity–temperature correlation (§3.2). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
9. No clear uniform zones are found for the total temperature (not shown) (§3.2). **Stated**
10. The van Driest and Trettel–Larsson transformations leave a log-law intercept mismatch, mainly for the hypersonic cases and for Trettel–Larsson (§4, Fig. 8a,b). **Shown**
11. The Volpiani et al. (2020b) and Griffin et al. (2021) transformations give "an excellent collapse through the whole boundary layer" for all four cases; they differ only in the wake, where Griffin collapses the two high-Mach cases better independently of $Re_\tau$; the database cannot decide between them (§4, Fig. 8c,d). **Shown**
12. The Griffin et al. (2021) transformation with the constant-stress assumption ($\tau^+\approx1$) deviates by no more than 0.3% from the total-stress form (§4). **Shown** (number stated, not plotted)
13. Case M2L transformed with van Driest agrees with the adiabatic Mach 2 case of Pirozzoli & Bernardini (2011b) (§4, Fig. 8a). **Shown**
14. The mean temperature peaks near the wall ($y^+\approx5$) by aerodynamic heating, more strongly and closer to the wall at high Mach number; the total temperature overshoots in the outer layer, more at Mach 6, "apparently independent of the Reynolds number" (§4, Fig. 9a,b). **Shown**
15. The Zhang et al. (2014) temperature–velocity relation (Eq. 4.4) is more accurate than Walz's (Eq. 4.3) at both Reynolds numbers; Walz's mismatch stays within 3%, which the authors attribute to a wall cooling too mild to stress the law (up to 10% at $T_w/T_r=0.18$ in Duan et al. 2011) (§4, Fig. 9c,d). **Shown**
16. Morkovin-scaled Reynolds stresses of the Mach 2 cases agree with incompressible DNS at matching $Re_\tau$ (Jiménez et al. 2010, $Re_\tau=445$; Sillero et al. 2013, $Re_\tau=1989$), with the streamwise peak increasing with Mach number (§5.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. The logarithmic growth with $Re_\tau$ of the near-wall streamwise and spanwise intensities due to inactive motions is confirmed in the supersonic regime; the wall-normal and shear components show an equilibrium-layer plateau (§5.1). **Shown**
18. At high Reynolds and Mach numbers the outer-layer wall-normal and spanwise intensities are reduced, indicating a less efficient redistribution of TKE from the streamwise component in hypersonic conditions, in accordance with Zhang et al. (2018) (§5.1, Fig. 10; Conclusions). **Shown**
19. Morkovin scaling collapses profiles at the same $Re_\tau$ but not across Mach numbers in $y^+$; semi-local $y^*$ increases the outer-layer discrepancies, but $Re_\tau^*$ is not matched among cases (§5.1). **Shown**
20. In semi-local scaling the dissipation is almost independent of $Re$ and $M$, while production and turbulent transport depend on Mach number; dissipation and production plus transport are Mach-independent for $y^+>20$ (§5.2, Fig. 11). **Shown**
21. Premultiplied production shows bulk production extending into the log layer as $Re$ increases, with the highest peak at M6H, attributed to the indirect Mach effect on $Re_\tau^*$ (§5.2, Fig. 12a). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
22. Production over dissipation is about 1 in the first part of the log layer in all cases, but the hypersonic profiles show excess production farther out, balanced by turbulent transport; the authors attribute it to a stronger outer–inner interaction of denser, colder, less viscous outer eddies (§5.2, Fig. 12b). **Shown** (mechanism **Stated**)
23. Density and temperature rms increase with Mach number; both cold-wall cases have weaker temperature fluctuations than the adiabatic case; in semi-local scaling the near-edge density-fluctuation peak at high Mach is smeared and the log-layer profile almost flat (§5.3, Fig. 13a–d). **Shown**
24. The pressure rms in wall scaling ($p_{rms}/\tau_w$) collapses at fixed $Re_\tau$, is controlled by $Re_\tau$ and almost Mach-independent except very near the wall and in the free stream (acoustic radiation growing with $M_\infty$); semi-local scaling does not improve it (§5.3, Fig. 13e,f). **Shown**
25. Spanwise spectra of $u$ show an inner peak at $y^+\approx15$, $\lambda_z^+=O(100)$, and an outer peak at $y/\delta\approx0.1$, $\lambda_z=O(\delta)$, at both Mach numbers (§5.4, Fig. 14). **Shown**
26. Temperature spectra show a strong inner peak ($y^+\approx20$) only at Mach 6, a weak plateau at Mach 2 (consistent with the missing near-wall temperature streaks), and an outer peak at both Mach numbers, displaced upwards at Mach 2 (§5.4, Fig. 14). **Shown**
27. Normalizing $\lambda_z$ with the eddy-size scale $l_{12}^*$ (Eq. 5.9) improves the collapse of the $u$ and $T$ spectra at $y/\delta=0.2$–0.8 over $\delta$; the proposed $l^*_{12,GR}$ (Eq. 5.10), built on the Griffin-transformed mean shear, improves it slightly further, especially at M6H; enthalpy and Rotta thicknesses as outer scales behave like $\delta$ (§5.4, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- **Table 2, M6L shape factor** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. With $H=\delta^*/\theta$ (caption) and the printed $\delta^*/\delta=0.43$, $\theta/\delta=3.2\times10^{-2}$, $H=13.4$; the table prints $H=10.9$. The other three rows agree within rounding (2.47 against 2.44, 2.33 against 2.34, 10.5 against 10.5).
- **Eqs. 5.9–5.10** *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. $l_{12}^*\sim(u_\tau\delta)(\partial\langle u\rangle_\rho/\partial y)^{-1/2}$ as printed has the dimension of a length times a velocity times a time to the power 1/2; a length requires the prefactor $(u_\tau\delta)^{1/2}$.
- **Van Driest reference.** The introduction credits the van Driest velocity scaling to Van Driest (1956); §4 and Fig. 8 to Van Driest (1951).
- **Legend labels.** Figs. 10 and 12 print "Siliero et al. (2013)" for Sillero et al.; Figs. 8, 10 and 13 print "Pirozzoli et al. (2011)" where the captions give Pirozzoli & Bernardini (2011b).
- **Figs. 15–16.** The legends list $\eta=0.2$–$0.7$; the captions say "$\eta=y/\delta=0.2-0.8$".
- **Conclusions.** The instantaneous analysis is described as using "streamwise velocity on x–y and x–z slices"; Fig. 1 ($x$–$y$) shows density.
- **Typos.** "high-entalphy" (p. 5), "turbulence statics" (p. 7), "promincences" (p. 11).
