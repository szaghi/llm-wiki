# Summary

Sun et al. (2023) present detonationFoam, a GPLv3 C++ solver for
compressible, multi-component reacting flow aimed at gaseous detonation,
built on the density-based rhoCentralFoam solver of
[OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md). Five changes are listed: species
transport with detailed finite-rate chemistry (stiff ODEs by the seulex
extrapolation solver in an operator-splitting step), not tabulated
chemistry (contrast [flamelet-based tabulated chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md));
a mixture-averaged transport model (Wilke viscosity, Mathur et al.
conductivity, mixture-averaged and thermal diffusion with a correction
velocity) in place of the Sutherland law of earlier rhoCentralFoam-based
detonation solvers; the HLLC-P approximate Riemann solver (HLLC plus a
pressure-dissipation term) with second-order MUSCL reconstruction;
two-dimensional [adaptive mesh refinement](/methods/mesh-adaptation.md)
obtained by modifying OpenFOAM's three-dimensional hexahedral library,
refining and coarsening on thresholds of $|\nabla\rho|$ with a
conservative mapping; and the dynamic load balancing of DLBFoam, which
moves chemistry work from overloaded to underloaded MPI ranks.

Validation covers 0D ignition delays against Cantera (maximum relative
difference below 7%), 1D unsteady diffusion against A-SURF, a shock tube
against the exact solution (maximum pressure error 1.78%, HLLC-P less
dissipative than KNP and HLLC), laminar H$_2$/air flame speeds within 4%
of Cantera, a 1D planar H$_2$/air detonation (2026 m/s against the
Chapman–Jouguet 1977 m/s), a 1D pulsating detonation, the double Mach
reflection, a 2D cellular H$_2$/O$_2$/Ar detonation (cell about
55 × 30 mm against 54 × 31 mm) and a wedge-stabilized oblique detonation;
on the last, refinement cuts the cell count to about 1/6 and refinement
with load balancing the CPU time to about 1/5. All cases are laminar or
inviscid: the paper has no turbulence model, no subgrid or wall model and
no turbulent test case. Its bearing on this bundle is limited to the
OpenFOAM solver family and to refinement for compressible reacting
flow.

# Key claims

1. detonationFoam solves the unsteady compressible multi-component reacting Navier–Stokes equations (mass, momentum, energy and $N_S-1$ species) with detailed chemistry and mixture-averaged transport; the viscous stress is $\sigma_{ij}$ without bulk viscosity (§2.1, Eqs. 1–20). **Stated**
2. Existing rhoCentralFoam-based detonation solvers usually take transport coefficients from the Sutherland law, so multi-species transport "cannot be accurately predicted", which "may lead to large discrepancy", e.g. for deflagration-to-detonation transition (§1). **Stated**
3. OpenFOAM's refinement supports only hexahedral cells in 3D and is "not appropriate for 2D detonation simulation"; detonationFoam modifies the library for 2D (§1, §2.2.2). **Stated**
4. Refinement and load balancing "have not been simultaneously used in detonation solvers"; the cited rhoCentralFoam-based detonation solvers are not open source, and rhoReactingCentralFoam was not comprehensively validated and did not solve the load imbalance (§1). **Stated**
5. The load balancing of Tekgül et al. achieved a speed-up "around 10" for deflagration simulations (§1). **Stated** (cited)
6. Convective fluxes by second-order MUSCL with HLLC-P; diffusive fluxes by second-order central differences; first-order implicit Euler for the non-reactive step; seulex for chemistry in an operator-splitting fractional step (§2.2.1). **Stated**
7. Second-order time schemes (backward, Crank–Nicolson) have "little influence on the results due to the very small time step in the order of $10^{-9}$ s" (§2.2.1). **Stated** (no evidence shown)
8. The HLLC-P flux is the HLLC flux plus a pressure-dissipation term scaled by $(f_{\mathrm P}-1)$, with $f_{\mathrm P}$ the cube of the minimum neighbouring pressure ratio (Eqs. 21–24), after Xie et al. (§2.2.1). **Stated**
9. 0D ignition delays of stoichiometric H$_2$/air (1000–1300 K; 0.1, 0.5, 1 atm) and n-heptane/air (700–1300 K; 10, 20, 30 atm) agree with Cantera, "the maximum of the relative difference is lower than 7%" (§3.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. 1D unsteady diffusion with the Soret effect of H$_2$ agrees "very good" with A-SURF at $t=1$ and 2 ms (§3.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. 1D shock tube: maximum relative pressure error 1.78% against the exact solution at $t=50$ μs (§3.3, Fig. 5a). **Shown**
12. HLLC-P "has lower dissipation" than KNP and HLLC and resolves the shock "more accurately" (§3.3, Fig. 5b). **Shown** (one profile)
13. 1D laminar premixed H$_2$/air flames (300 K, 1 atm): flame speeds within 4% of Cantera over the plotted equivalence ratios; the flame structure agrees satisfactorily (§3.4, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
14. 1D planar detonation in stoichiometric H$_2$/air (300 K, 1 atm): pressure and $Y_{\mathrm{O_2}}$ histories "almost identical" to A-SURF; peak pressure close to the von Neumann value 27.7 atm; detonation speed 2026 m/s against $V_{\mathrm{CJ}}=1977$ m/s (§3.5, Fig. 8). **Shown**
15. 1D pulsating detonation in C$_2$H$_4$/O$_2$/Ar (0.75:2.25:7, 300 K, 0.25 atm): the speed oscillates periodically about $U_{\mathrm{CJ}}$; the period 9.93 μs is called "closed to the reference value, 6.32 μs" of Sun et al. (PCI 39) (§3.6, Fig. 9). **Shown** (see Printed slips)
16. Double Mach reflection (Mach-10 shock, 60° wall, $\gamma=1.4$, refinement levels 0–4): density isopleths "almost consistent" with Woodward; shocks and contacts covered by the finest cells (§3.7, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. 2D cellular detonation in H$_2$/O$_2$/Ar (300 K, 6670 Pa) after Oran et al.: cell size about 55 × 30 mm against 54 × 31 mm (Oran et al.) and 54 × 30 mm (Wang et al.) (§3.8, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
18. In §3.8 the base cell is 200 μm with three refinement levels (finest 25 μm) and the induction length 1519 μm, so "more than 60 cells resolving the induction region" (§3.8). **Stated**; *(compiler check, not in the sources)* $1519/25=60.8$.
19. Oblique detonation (stoichiometric H$_2$/air, inflow 196.3 kPa, 814.4 K, 2418.9 m/s at 20 km and $M_0=9$; Euler equations; base cell 20 μm, two refinement levels): structure "almost identical" to Teng et al. (§3.9, Fig. 14). **Shown**
20. Against a uniform mesh without load balancing, refinement reduces the cell number "to about 1/6" and refinement with load balancing the CPU time "to about 1/5", with "almost same" steady structures, so they "have little influence on the accuracy" (§3.9, Figs. 15–16). **Shown** (hardware and core count not stated)
21. Grid and time-step dependence of the oblique-detonation case was established in the authors' earlier work: 5 μm cells resolve it (§3.9). **Stated** (cited); *(compiler check, not in the sources)* with the halving per level printed in §3.8 ("$200/2^3=25$ μm"), the finest cell here is $20/2^2=5$ μm.
22. detonationFoam "can accurately and efficiently simulate compressible, multi-component reactive flow and detonation processes" (§4). **Shown** (by the §3 tests)

# Printed slips

- **§3.6:** "The pulsating period obtained by detonationFoam is 9.93 μs and closed to the reference value, 6.32 μs". *(compiler check, not in the sources)* $(9.93-6.32)/6.32\approx0.57$: the two printed periods differ by about 57%.
- **Eq. (9) and text:** the sums run over $n=0,\dots,4$ with a further term $a_{n,5}/T$, while the text reads "$a_{n,k}$ with $n=1\sim5$ are the coefficients for the k-th species".
- **Eq. (6):** $P=\rho RT/W$, while the text defines "$\bar W$ is the mean molar weight".
- **Eqs. (21), (23):** the pressure-dissipation term is written $\Phi_P$ in Eq. (21) and $\Phi_p$ in Eq. (23).
- **§1:** HLLC-P is cited as "[36,37]", where ref. [37] is the blastFoam software entry; §2.2.1 cites HLLC-P as [36,48].
- **§3.7:** "Following the work of Woodward and Colella [58,59]": ref. [58] is printed with the single author "P.C.P. Woodward" and ref. [59] is a different work (Vevek, Zang, New 2018).
- **§3.7:** density isopleths "at $t$ = 0.2 s" for a "normalized inviscid gas model".
- **§3.4:** "detonation is able to simulate premixed flame propagation. Therefore, detonation can be used to simulate…", "detonation" written for detonationFoam.
- **References:** ref. [60] prints two authors as "J.W.W. Jr." and "J.D.A. Jr." without surnames; ref. [46] "B.V. Leer, J.V. Rosendale"; ref. [31] "geometris"; ref. [7] venue "AIAA Scitech, 2019, Forum, 2019".
- **Typo:** "we predicte a cell size" (§3.8).
