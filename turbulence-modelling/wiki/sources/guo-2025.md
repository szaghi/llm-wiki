# Summary

Guo & Wang (2025) modify the SST-based delayed
[detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) of
Gritskevich et al. (SSTDDES) in [OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md)-v2306 in
two ways. First, the eddy viscosity of the
[$k$-$\omega$ SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) is multiplied by
$(\min(L_t,\Delta_{\mathrm F})/L_t)^{4/3}$, where $L_t=\sqrt k/(\beta^*\omega)$
and $\Delta_{\mathrm F}$ is a resolvable filter width built from the time
step and the grid. This filtering technique comes from the authors' earlier
SSTFDES, and the model it gives here is SSTFDDES. Second, the grid scale of
the LES length $C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta$ switches between the largest cell
dimension $\Delta_{\max}$, used inside boundary layers ($f_d<0.99$) and in
regions of low eddy-viscosity ratio, and the least-squares subgrid length
$\Delta_{\mathrm{lsq}}$ of Trias et al. elsewhere, which in practice means
in separated regions. The combination, ADD-SSTFDDES, is implemented as two
new OpenFOAM classes.

Four variants (with or without each modification) are compared on the
[square cylinder](/benchmarks/square-cylinder-flow.md) at $Re_D=21400$ and
the [periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) at $Re_h=10565$, both with
[wall functions](/methods/rans-wall-function.md) (Spalding's
[law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) for $\nu_t$). On the
cylinder, ADD-SSTFDDES gives $C_L^{\mathrm{RMS}}=1.60$ against the DNS 1.71 of
Trias et al. and $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}=0.20$ against 0.205. Its mean drag of
2.71 lies above the DNS 2.18 and the experiment 2.1, but inside the range
Rodi collected. In Q-criterion views it keeps more small-scale wake vortices
than the baseline, which the authors read as suppressed
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md). On the hill
it best captures the reverse-flow plateau and the $\langle u'u'\rangle$ peak.
Most comparisons are qualitative statements about figures. The paper gives
no time step and no grid study of its own (it relies on the earlier paper's
for the cylinder), and it compares no costs between the four variants. The
introduction places DES among the [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md)
methods of [large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md)
practice.

# Key claims

Registry symbols; $\Delta_{\mathrm F}$, $L_t$, $l_t$, $\nu^{\mathrm F}_t$,
$\Delta_{\mathrm{lsq}}$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}$ are page-local (see the
Notation map).

1. ADD-SSTFDDES combines adaptive filtering of the eddy viscosity (Eq. 13) and an adaptive grid scale $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}$ (Eq. 15) on the SSTDDES base, implemented in OpenFOAM-v2306 (Abstract, §1, §2.1). **Stated**
2. The adaptive filtering uses $L_t=\sqrt k/(\beta^*\omega)$ (Eq. 10), $\Delta_{\mathrm F}=3\max\{|\boldsymbol u|\Delta t,\Delta^{1/3}\}$ (Eq. 11) and $l_t=\min\{L_t,\Delta_{\mathrm F}\}$ (Eq. 12); when $L_t\le\Delta_{\mathrm F}$ the RANS behaviour is kept, otherwise the length is limited "to suppress excessive eddy viscosity and promot[e] the development of small-scale turbulent fluctuations" (§2.1). **Stated**
3. The filtered eddy viscosity is $\nu^{\mathrm F}_t=(\min(L_t,\Delta_{\mathrm F})/L_t)^{4/3}\,a_1k/\max(a_1\omega,|\Omega|F_2)$ (Eq. 13), giving SSTFDDES. **Stated** (details deferred to the authors' earlier paper, ref. [24])
4. The adaptive grid scale is $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}=\Delta_{\max}$ if $f_d<0.99$ or $\mu_t/\mu<0.2$, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{lsq}}$ otherwise (Eq. 15); $\mu_t/\mu<0.2$ indicates "a laminar-like flow", $f_d<0.99$ "indicates that the flow resides within the boundary layer", so $\Delta_{\mathrm{lsq}}$ is active "only in separated flow regions" (§2.1, p. 7). **Stated**
5. $\Delta_{\max}$ is "considered overly conservative in the initial shear layer, since the streamwise and spanwise grid scales are often significantly larger than the wall-normal spacing", while $\Delta_{\mathrm{lsq}}$ "exhibits inherent robustness to grid anisotropy" (§2.1, p. 5). **Stated** (attributed to ref. [15], Trias et al.)
6. Using $\Delta_{\max}$ in boundary layers and low-viscosity regions avoids "excessive grid dependence" and "significantly reduce[s] computational cost" (p. 7). **Stated** (no cost data)
7. Implementation: the class kOmegaSSTFDDES derives from kOmegaSSTDDES by modifying correctNut(), and adaptiveDelta is a new LESdelta class, compiled with wmake; "lightweight and compatible with other hybrid RANS/LES models in the same framework"; code on request (§2.1, p. 8, Fig. 1). **Stated**
8. Square cylinder (Table 4), for MAX-SSTDDES / ADD-SSTDDES / MAX-SSTFDDES / ADD-SSTFDDES at $Re_D=21400$: $St$ = 0.127 / 0.120 / 0.135 / 0.127; $\langle C_D\rangle$ = 2.43 / 2.74 / 2.48 / 2.71; $C_L^{\mathrm{RMS}}$ = 1.26 / 1.43 / 1.43 / 1.60; $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}$ = 0.28 / 0.17 / 0.26 / 0.20; $L_r/D$ = 1.46 / 1.27 / 1.39 / 1.42. **Shown**
9. Reference values of Table 4: DNS of Trias et al. (ref. [34], $Re_D=22000$) $St=0.132$, $\langle C_D\rangle=2.18$, $C_L^{\mathrm{RMS}}=1.71$, $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}=0.205$, $L_r/D=1.04$; experiment of Lyn et al. (ref. [28]) $St=0.13$, $\langle C_D\rangle=2.1$, $L_r/D=1.4$; the range of Rodi (ref. [40]) $St$ 0.09–0.15, $\langle C_D\rangle$ 2.17–2.77, $C_L^{\mathrm{RMS}}$ 1.15–1.79, $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}$ 0.14–0.27, $L_r/D$ 0.94–1.68. **Stated** (cited)
10. All four mean drags are "slightly higher than the experimental (Exp.) data but remain within the solution range reported by Rodi"; the variants with $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}$ give slightly higher drag (§3.1, p. 14). **Shown**
11. ADD-SSTFDDES's $C_L^{\mathrm{RMS}}=1.60$ "closely matches" the DNS 1.71, and its $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}=0.20$ "lies within the LES/DES reference range of 0.18–0.22 and most closely matches the DNS value of 0.205" (§3.1, p. 14). **Shown**
12. MAX-SSTDDES predicts delayed separation, a narrower initial wake, a shortened recirculation zone and insufficient velocity recovery; ADD-SSTFDDES gives earlier separation, a broader initial wake and smoother recovery (§3.1, Fig. 10). **Shown** (qualitative)
13. In Q-criterion iso-surfaces ($Q=5\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}$) the MAX-SSTDDES vortices deteriorate in the mid wake, $\Delta_{\mathrm{add}}$ partially improves wake continuity, filtering "greatly enhances the resolution of small-scale vortices in the mid-to-far wake", and the combination is best; the authors infer that "the model effectively suppresses numerical dissipation and retains small-scale energy" (§3.1, Figs. 12–15). **Shown** (qualitative)
14. PDFs of $u'/\sigma_u$ at probes $x/D=1,2,4,8$ and $y/D=0,0.5,1,2$: at $x/D=1$ all models are strongly non-Gaussian; at $x/D=2,4$ MAX-SSTDDES and MAX-SSTFDDES show "peak sharpening and negatively skewed distributions"; ADD-SSTFDDES is "consistently closer to the reference normal distribution at all sampling points" (§3.1, Figs. 17–20). **Shown** (qualitative; the Gaussian is the reference, no DNS or experimental PDF is shown)
15. Periodic hill: all four models capture the main $C_p$ features; ADD-SSTFDDES best captures the near-wall reverse-flow plateau at $x/h=2$ and $\langle u\rangle$ at $x/h=6$, with slight deviations of $\langle v\rangle$ near reattachment attributed to the filtering's sensitivity to anisotropic flow and to limited wall-normal resolution near the centreline (§3.2, Figs. 22–24). **Shown** (qualitative)
16. Periodic hill Reynolds stresses: ADD-SSTFDDES captures the amplitude and location of the $\langle u'u'\rangle$ peak near $y/h\approx1$ at $x/h=0.5$; at $x/h=6$ MAX-SSTDDES "significantly overpredicts" the $\langle v'v'\rangle$ peak (§3.2, Fig. 25). **Shown** (qualitative)
17. Wall functions with $y^+<50$ on the periodic hill give "almost identical results" to wall-resolved $y^+<1$ grids, "as discussed in Gritskevich et al. [26]" (§2.2.2, p. 12). **Stated** (cited)
18. Cost: square cylinder on 64 CPU cores, "approximately 288 core-hours"; periodic hill on 48 cores, "total runtime of approximately 75 hours" (§3.1, §3.2). **Stated**
19. The square-cylinder grid was shown converged for mean drag, Strouhal number and RMS lift in a three-level study of the earlier paper (§2.2.1, p. 10). **Stated** (cited)
20. Limitations: tested only for incompressible isothermal flow; planned extensions to compressible hybrid RANS/LES, conjugate heat transfer, SSTIDDES, a parameter-free $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$ and switching thresholds, and wall-resolved low-$Re$ cases (§4). **Stated**
21. Literature: Probst et al. (ref. [19]) raised $C_{d1}$ of the DDES shielding function from 8 to 16; Jain et al. (ref. [20]) validated $C_{d1}=14$ and found 16 over-shielding in quasi-two-dimensional flat-plate flow; Gritskevich et al. (ref. [21]) raised it from 8 to 20 for SST-DDES (§1, p. 3). **Stated** (cited)

Background formulas as printed (§2.1), in registry symbols: SST-DES with
$l_{\mathrm{DES}}=\min[l_{\mathrm{RANS}},\,l_{\mathrm{LES}}/(1-F_{\mathrm{SST}})]$,
$l_{\mathrm{RANS}}=\sqrt k/(\beta^*\omega)$, $l_{\mathrm{LES}}=C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta$,
$F_{\mathrm{SST}}$ "equal to either $F_1$ or $F_2$" (Eq. 2); SST-DDES with
$l_{\mathrm{DDES}}=l_{\mathrm{RANS}}-f_d\max(0,l_{\mathrm{RANS}}-l_{\mathrm{LES}})$
(Eq. 6), $C_{\mathrm{DDES}}=0.78F_1+0.61F_2$ as printed (Eq. 7),
$f_d=1-\tanh([20r_d]^3)$ (Eq. 8) and
$r_d=(\nu_t+\nu)/(\sqrt{\partial_ju_i\partial_ju_i}\,\kappa^2d^2)$ (Eq. 9);
$\nu_t=a_1k/\max(a_1\omega,|\Omega|F_2)$ with $a_1=0.31$ (Eq. 4),
$\sigma_{\omega2}=0.856$; $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$ "originally calibrated as 0.65"
(§1). The $\omega$ wall function sets $\omega_w=6\nu/(\beta_1d^2)$ with
$\beta_1=0.075$ (Eq. 20).

- Eq. (23): $U_b=\frac{1}{2.0355H}\int_H^{3.035H}u_x\,dy$, while the printed limits span $2.035H$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (11): $|U|\delta t$ is a length, and $\Delta^{1/3}$ is one only if $\Delta$ is a cell volume, while the paper calls $\Delta$ the grid scale elsewhere *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- The caption of Fig. 11 labels both rows "$x/D=-0.125$", while the text (p. 16) places the profiles at $x/D=-0.125$ and $x/D=0.125$.
- Fig. 1 prints the switch "($f_d<0.99$) || ($\mu_t/\mu_\infty=0.2$)" against "$<0.2$" in Eq. (15), and labels the filtered branch "kOmegaSSTDDES" while the text names the class kOmegaSSTFDDES.
- p. 19 places the $C_p$ centreline "as indicated by the white line in Fig. 20", a PDF figure (the pressure field is Fig. 21); p. 20 says "Fig. 24 presents the key second-order turbulence statistics", which are in Fig. 25 (Fig. 24 is the mean velocity).
- p. 14: ADD-SSTDDES's $C_D^{\mathrm{RMS}}=0.17$ is "slightly lower than the experimental and DNS ranges", while Table 4 prints the DNS of Cao et al. (ref. [35]) as 0.117–0.181; the "LES/DES reference range of 0.18–0.22" (p. 14) differs from the LES/DES entries of Table 4.
- p. 21: "to evaluate each m〈v′v′〉 odel's ability" and "For (Fig. 25(d))" with the quantity missing.
- p. 3 names the earlier model "Shear Stress Transport Filtered DDES (SSTFDES)"; p. 14 "Verstappe et al. [42]" for Verstappen and Veldman; "Timer-averaged" (captions of Figs. 10, 21, 23); "period hills" and "COFTAC/IAHR" (p. 11); the nomenclature lists $u_{\tau*}^2$ as "Friction velocity".
