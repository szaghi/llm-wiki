# Summary

Wei et al. (2023) run wall-resolved [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md)
of supersonic hydrogen film cooling in the configuration of the
Burrows–Kurkov experiment (vitiated-air mainstream at $M=2.44$, sonic
hydrogen slot tangential to the wall) to ask how combustion of the fuel
film alters near-wall turbulence, skin friction and wall heat flux. The
in-house solver scramjetFoam, built on [OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md) 7,
uses implicit grid filtering, a discretely
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
split convective flux with AUSM-type diffusion switched on by a Ducros
[shock sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md), the dynamic
[one-equation subgrid kinetic-energy model](/methods/subgrid-kinetic-energy-equation-model.md)
(coefficients from the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md)),
a quasi-laminar turbulence–chemistry closure with a 7-species mechanism
(flamelet closures are named only as alternatives, see
[flamelet-based tabulated chemistry](/methods/flamelet-tabulated-chemistry.md)),
and synthetic-eddy [inflow](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)
built from a RANS precursor. The inviscid
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) shows that the
solver keeps the kinetic energy while two second-order OpenFOAM solvers
dissipate it ([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md));
a supersonic [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md)
checks the inflow, the resolved-energy fraction, the van Driest
[velocity transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
against the [law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) and
density-scaled Reynolds stresses in the spirit of
[Morkovin's hypothesis](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md), on a grid
with $\Delta x^+=120$, $\Delta z^+=40$ ([grid requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)).

Combustion moves the turbulent kinetic energy into the streamwise
component and suppresses wall-normal fluctuations; with the low-density
flame region this lowers wall-normal transport and gives a further 10%
friction reduction downstream with about 2% more heat load. A posteriori,
the [turbulent Prandtl and Schmidt numbers](/definitions/turbulent-prandtl-number.md)
range between 0 and 2 and the turbulent heat flux is misaligned with the
mean temperature gradient by about 60° to 75°, against the gradient-diffusion
form of the [Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md).
A [$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) RANS of the same cases
shows no friction reduction, and the authors recommend anisotropic closures
such as v2f or [Reynolds-stress models](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $c_k$, $c_\varepsilon$ the coefficients of the
one-equation model (registry §11); $Pr_{t,\mathrm{sgs}}$,
$Sc_{t,\mathrm{sgs}}$ the subgrid turbulent Prandtl and Schmidt numbers;
$Sc_t$ the turbulent Schmidt number; $Y$ the mixture fraction (as on the turbulent-Prandtl-number page); $\theta_{qT}$
the angle between turbulent heat flux and mean temperature gradient; $s_h$
the slot height; $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the no-film wall heat flux. In §IV the
paper's overbar and tilde denote time averages, written here $\langle\cdot\rangle$
and $\langle\cdot\rangle_\rho$.

1. scramjetFoam keeps the volume-averaged kinetic energy of the inviscid Taylor–Green vortex constant "over a long duration" on $64^3$ cells, owing to the energy-conserving flux; reactingFoam and rhoCentralFoam (both second order) dissipate it "to 65% and 30% of the initial value" (§III A, Fig. 4). **Shown**
2. scramjetFoam "can reasonably track the growth trend of enstrophy" of the Brachet et al. solution where the two OpenFOAM solvers fail; an enstrophy-conserving scheme is harder in finite volume and grid resolution contributes to the error (§III A, Fig. 4). **Shown**
3. In the supersonic boundary-layer test the velocity spectrum at $y/\delta=0.5$ recovers a $k^{-5/3}$ range (§III B, Fig. 5). **Shown**
4. 99.5% of the cells resolve more than 80% of the turbulent kinetic energy (Pope's criterion, the index of Eq. 34); the rest lie in the viscous sublayer (§III B, Fig. 6). **Shown**
5. The synthetic-eddy inflow needs about 50 mm of development; the wall shear reaches the Van Driest II value at $x/\delta=7$, a trend similar to Liu et al.'s recycling–rescaling LES (§III B, Fig. 9). **Shown**
6. The van Driest-transformed velocity approaches the log law after $x/\delta=7$ and converges at $x/\delta=14$, with an early departure from the linear law in the viscous layer (§III B, Fig. 10). **Shown**
7. Density-scaled Reynolds stresses agree with experiments (Refs. 73–75) and satisfy approximately $\sqrt{R_{xx}}:\sqrt{R_{zz}}:\sqrt{R_{yy}}=4:3:2$, the flat-plate estimate the paper cites from Wilcox (§III B, Fig. 11). **Shown**
8. Outlet mean temperature and species mole fractions of the inert case agree with Burrows–Kurkov, except a higher boundary-layer temperature attributed to the uncertain wall thermal condition (300 K isothermal assumed) (§IV A, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
9. The mean ignition point of the reacting case lies near $x/s_h=53$ (snapshot $x/s_h=45$); Burrows reported self-ignition oscillating in $x/s_h=27$–65 (§IV A, Fig. 15). **Shown**
10. The outlet maximum temperature, about 2318 K at $y/s_h=5$, is reproduced by the detailed Ó Conaire mechanism (RANS); the reduced Eklund–Stouffer mechanism under-predicts it by about 3%, and the detailed mechanism otherwise "shows no priority" (§IV A). **Shown**
11. The under-predicted hydrogen diffusion into the mainstream is attributed to neglect of Soret diffusion (§IV A, Fig. 17). **Stated**
12. The streamwise velocity of the reacting case drops by about 13% in the boundary layer after $x/s_h=50$; the combustion region acts as an obstacle and issues a compression shock (§IV B, Figs. 19–21). **Shown**
13. The film shields at least 70% of the wall heat load against $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$; combustion does not raise the mean heat load significantly against the non-reacting case but roughly doubles the oscillation of the instantaneous wall heat flux near $x/s_h=50$; the instantaneous flux reaches five times its mean but never exceeds $q_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (§IV D, Figs. 24–25). **Shown**
14. Wall shear under the film is reduced by at least 70% against the reference; the reacting case adds a 10% reduction after $x/s_h=75$, and combustion doubles the RMS wall shear near $x/s_h=50$ (§IV D, Fig. 26). **Shown**
15. The solver captures the combustion-induced friction reduction observed by Goyne et al., which the LES of Liu et al. did not reproduce (Abstract, §IV D). **Shown** (against cited literature)
16. The non-reacting case has lower wall fluxes than the inert one for $x/s_h=25$–65, attributed to different turbulence states of the RANS precursors, the molecular viscosity of oxygen exceeding that of nitrogen "by about 20%" (§IV D). **Stated**
17. The self-ignition point oscillates in $x=0.12$–0.2 m at low frequency; the production $P_k$ grows in an elliptic region near $x=0.15$ m correlated with the RMS heat-release rate; after $x=0.25$ m it falls below the inert value, attributed to higher viscous dissipation (§IV E, Figs. 29–30). **Shown**
18. At $y/s_h=1$ after $x/s_h=50$ the share of the normal stresses changes from $0.6:0.2:0.2$ to $0.8:0.1:0.1$ with combustion; wall-normal fluctuations are suppressed and the Reynolds shear stress drops at $x/s_h=75$, where friction drops further (§IV E, Figs. 27–28). **Shown**
19. Combustion raises the RMS of $T''$ about three times in the combustion region; $\langle u''T''\rangle$ grows more than five times and $\langle v''T''\rangle$ less than two times, while $-\langle\rho v''T''\rangle$ is only slightly larger than in the non-reacting case (a "two-step decrement mechanism": anisotropy, then low density) (§IV F, Figs. 31–32). **Shown**
20. The angle $\theta_{qT}$ (Eq. 39) is about 60° above $y/s_h=1$ for the inert and non-reacting cases (the paper cites 65° measured by Tavoularis & Corrsin in homogeneous shear) and about 75° with combustion, approaching 90° near the wall and near $y/s_h=3$ in the reacting case (§IV F, Fig. 32). **Shown**
21. Combustion barely affects mixture-fraction fluctuations (confined to $y/s_h<1$); $\langle\rho v''Y''\rangle$ is suppressed, and the negative streamwise flux $\langle\rho u''Y''\rangle$ runs against the gradient-diffusion picture (§IV F, Fig. 33). **Shown**
22. Without chemistry $Pr_t$ in the mixing layer decreases from 1.5 at $x=0.05$ m to 1.0 at $x=0.3$ m, $Sc_t$ likewise; combustion forms a narrow near-wall region of high $Pr_t$ and $Sc_t$ and a slender low-$Pr_t$ region near $y=0$; both lie between 0 and 2 (§IV F, Figs. 34–35). **Shown**
23. The failure of eddy-viscosity RANS here stems from the wide variation of $Pr_t$ and $Sc_t$ and from flux–gradient misalignment, the latter "more vital and intractable" (§IV F). **Stated** (from the LES statistics)
24. $k$-$\omega$ SST RANS over-predicts the wall fluxes against LES, shows no combustion-induced friction reduction and a significant heat-flux increase with combustion, inconsistent with experiment (Ref. 78) and LES (Ref. 33); redistributing the $0.6:0.2:0.2$ energy isotropically gives "a 65% increase in the fluctuating velocity in y component" (§IV F, Figs. 36–37). **Shown** (comparison) / **Derived** (estimate)
25. Anisotropic RANS models such as v2f or RSM are proposed for engineering prediction of such wall fluxes (§IV F, Conclusions). **Stated**
26. The boundary-layer hydrogen combustion is non-equilibrium (finite-rate), oxygen penetrating the flame front on the fuel-rich side; finite-rate combustion models are needed (§IV C, Conclusions). **Shown**

Model as printed (§II B, Eqs. 9–15): $\tau_{ij}=\bar\rho(\widetilde{u_iu_j}-\tilde u_i\tilde u_j)$,
$\tau^d_{ij}=-2\bar\rho\nu_t\tilde S^d_{ij}$, $\nu_t=c_k k_{\mathrm{sgs}}^{1/2}\Delta$,
$\Delta=(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$, dissipation $c_\varepsilon\bar\rho k_{\mathrm{sgs}}^{3/2}/\Delta$,
gradient-diffusion subgrid heat and species fluxes with
$Pr_{t,\mathrm{sgs}}=0.85$, $Sc_{t,\mathrm{sgs}}=0.7$; the subgrid
viscous-work flux is neglected. Resolved turbulent Prandtl number (Eq. 40):
$$Pr_t=\frac{\langle\rho u''v''\rangle\,\partial_y\langle T\rangle_\rho}{\langle\rho v''T''\rangle\,\partial_y\langle u\rangle_\rho},$$
and $Sc_t$ likewise with $Y$ for $T$ (Eq. 41).

- Eq. (23): the entry $a_{22}$ of the Cholesky factor of the Reynolds stress is printed $\sqrt{R_{xx}-a_{21}^2}$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with the printed $a_{11}=\sqrt{R_{xx}}$, $a_{21}=R_{yx}/a_{11}$ and $aa^{\mathrm T}=R$, the (2,2) entry must satisfy $a_{21}^2+a_{22}^2=R_{yy}$; the printed $a_{33}=\sqrt{R_{zz}-a_{31}^2-a_{32}^2}$ uses the matching diagonal.)*
- Eq. (24): $\rho'/\bar\rho=T'/\bar T$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: linearizing the paper's equation of state, Eq. (8), at fixed composition with $p'=0$ — an assumption the paper does not state — gives $\rho'/\bar\rho=-T'/\bar T$.)*
- Eq. (27): $v=u_0\cos(k_0x)\sin(k_0y)\cos(k_0z)$, printed without a minus sign (checked visually, p. 035112-7), while the text calls the initial field divergence-free. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with Eq. (26) the printed field has divergence $2u_0k_0\cos(k_0x)\cos(k_0y)\cos(k_0z)$, not zero.)*
- Eq. (20): the scheme $\mathbf U^{(l)}=\mathbf U^{(0)}+\alpha_l\Delta t\,\mathcal R(\mathbf U^{(l-1)})$ with $\alpha_l=1/4,1/3,1/2,1$ is called "third-order four-stage". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: for linear $\mathcal R$ the products $1$, $1/2$, $1/6$, $1/24$ reproduce the Taylor series to $\Delta t^4$, fourth order; for scalar $y'=f(y)$ the $\Delta t^3$ term is $\tfrac16f'^2f+\tfrac18f''f^2$ against the exact $\tfrac16(f'^2f+f''f^2)$, second order.)*
- §IV F: "a 65% increase in the fluctuating velocity in y component" from redistributing $0.6:0.2:0.2$ isotropically. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: the wall-normal share goes from 0.2 to 1/3, +67% in the variance and +29% in the RMS velocity.)*
- Eq. (36) has indices $ij$ on the left and the trace-like $\overline{u_i''u_i''}$ on the right, and reuses $\xi$, the enstrophy of Eq. (33); the friction velocity is $u_\tau$ in Eq. (36) and $u_s$ in the Nomenclature. Eq. (34) carries the overline on the numerator only.
- Conclusions: combustion amplifies the flux oscillations "by more than two times"; §IV D: "roughly doubles".
