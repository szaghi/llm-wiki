# Summary

Gao et al. (2023) build a partitioned, loosely coupled fluid–structure
interaction (FSI) framework with high-order solvers on both sides, and
apply it to an impinging oblique
[shock/turbulent boundary-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
over an elastic panel at Mach 2. The fluid solver is the split-form
arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian [DGSEM](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md)
of [FLEXI](/software/flexi.md), with the
[kinetic-energy-preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md)
two-point flux of Pirozzoli, BR1 viscous fluxes, and the metric identities
and [geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md)
in reference space. Turbulence is treated by wall-resolved
[implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md): no SGS model, only the
dissipation of the Riemann solvers. The structure is a Timoshenko beam
discretized by a Legendre spectral finite-element method with Newmark time
integration. The meshes match at the interface, and radial-basis functions
move the interior mesh.

Shock capturing is an "improved adaptive filter". The positivity-preserving,
minimum-entropy exponential [modal filter](/methods/explicit-modal-filtering.md)
of Dzanic & Witherden is applied only in elements flagged by a
[shock indicator](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) (Jameson, Persson–Peraire
or an improved Ducros indicator). The entropy tolerance then controls the
trade-off between shock sharpness and post-shock oscillation, and the
hyperbolic–parabolic splitting is no longer needed. The authors argue that
the confinement keeps filter [dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)
out of smooth and under-resolved turbulent regions. The rigid-panel
reference of [Gao et al. (2022)](/sources/gao-2022.md) uses
[subcell finite-volume shock capturing](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md)
instead.

Validation covers panel flutter (critical Mach 2.11, equal to the
analytical value) and a shock hitting a cantilever panel. A shock-free
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) with
synthetic-eddy and linear-forcing [inflow](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)
matches DNS in [van Driest](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
and [Morkovin](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md) scaling, in the
viscous sublayer and the [log region](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md).
On the elastic panel ($N=6$, 177 million degrees of freedom), the
separation area is 49.0 % larger than on the rigid panel, and a new
low-frequency unsteadiness appears at the panel frequency ($St\approx0.01$).
The separation-induced frequency $St\approx0.03$ is unchanged.

# Key claims

Registry translation: the source's polynomial degree $N$ agrees; its
Jacobian $J$ is $\mathcal J$; its filter strength $\alpha$, modal fraction
$m=i/N$ and order $2s$ are $\alpha_f$, $\vartheta_m$ and $s_f$ (filter
$e^{-\alpha_f\vartheta_m^{s_f}}$, $s_f=2$); its entropy $s=\rho\ln(p\rho^{-\gamma})$
and tolerance $\epsilon_s$ are $\mathcal S$ and $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}$, as
on [subcell finite-volume shock capturing](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md);
$\delta_r$ is the boundary-layer thickness at $x_r=x_{imp}-3\delta_r$.
Page-local: $f_{\mathrm p}$ the panel frequency (the source's $f_s$).

1. The work is claimed to be the first simulation of SWTBLI over an elastic structure with both fluid and structure solvers of high order, and the first in the DG community with split-form ALE DGSEM over deformable structures (§1). **Stated**
2. Implicit LES means no explicit SGS model, the dissipation of the interface Riemann solvers alone accounting for unresolved scales (Flad & Gassner 2017) (§1). **Stated**
3. The Pirozzoli (2011) kinetic-energy-preserving two-point flux is "a good compromise between computational efficiency and non-linear stability" (Krais 2020); split-form ALE DGSEM with this flux is the baseline (§3.1.2). **Stated**
4. The original adaptive filter of Dzanic & Witherden (2022), with one tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=10^{-4}$ for the whole field, can be excessively dissipative in complex flows, especially at high $N$ or on coarse meshes, because different phenomena generate different entropy growth ("according to our experiments") (§3.1.3). **Stated**
5. The original adaptive filter applied to a standard DG method is "found to be unreliable" against aliasing in under-resolved turbulence, since it aims at minimal dissipation while under-resolved turbulence needs filter dissipation for the cut-off eddies (Flad et al. 2016, 2020); this motivates the split form (§3.1.3). **Stated**
6. Confining the adaptive filter to troubled elements adds no dissipation elsewhere; a larger $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}$ gives sharper shocks with more oscillation, a smaller one smoother shocks; hyperbolic–parabolic splitting becomes unnecessary, saving computation and memory (§3.1.3). **Stated** (the $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}$ behaviour **Shown** in §4.1)
7. Supersonic panel flutter: $M_\infty=2.10$ decays and $2.11$ diverges, a predicted critical Mach number 2.11 equal to the analytical value of Mouronval et al. (2019) (§4.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
8. In the flutter case $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=0.0$ balances a sharp shock against little post-shock noise; $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=10^{-4}$ gives a sharper shock with more pronounced oscillations (§4.1, Figs. 5, 7). **Shown**
9. Shock impact on a deforming panel: $N=4$ and $N=5$ give almost identical tip displacement and sensor pressure ($L=0.05$ m) (§4.2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
10. Tip displacements for $L=0.04$ and 0.05 m agree overall with Sanches & Coda (2014) and Pasquariello et al. (2016), small deviations attributed to schemes and beam models; a higher sensor-pressure amplitude is attributed to the high-order DG method and less dissipative shock capturing (§4.2, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
11. With the same $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}$ the confined filter introduces "much less dissipation in smooth flow areas, especially in those complex vortical structures" than the original filter (§4.2, Figs. 14, 16). **Shown** (qualitative, Schlieren images)
12. The change of shock capturing between the static case (finite-volume subcells with an improved Jameson indicator) and the moving case (confined filter with an improved Ducros indicator) is believed not to influence near-wall phenomena, since no shock capturing is active there (§5). **Stated**
13. Performance: PID $3.26\times10^{-6}$ s, 48.0 h wall clock, $8.0\times10^5$ CPU hours on 16 384 cores (about 10 802 degrees of freedom per core), about 55.7 % above the standard split-form ALE DGSEM with prescribed mesh motion (§5.1, Table 4, Eq. 68). **Shown**
14. In Kempf et al. (2023) a boundary layer alone with the same zonal LES technique cost about 33.3 % performance (§5.1). **Stated**
15. Shock-free boundary layer: PID about 1.74 µs, about 34.6 % above the baseline of Kempf et al. (2023), attributed mainly to communication in the statistics collection (§5.2). **Shown**
16. The van Driest mean velocity matches the reference law in the viscous sublayer ($y^+\le5$) and the log region ($30\le y^+\le100$); the van Driest velocity and the Morkovin density-scaled Reynolds stresses match the DNS of Wenzel et al. (2018) at $17\delta_r$ and $35\delta_r$ despite a much coarser mesh (§5.2, Fig. 18). **Shown**
17. The van Driest II skin friction and the shape factor approach the DNS and the correlation $C_{f,\mathrm i}=0.024Re_{\theta,\mathrm i}^{-0.25}$ quickly after the forcing zone; the flow is in equilibrium before the interaction (§5.2, Fig. 19). **Shown**
18. The elastic panel oscillates at $f_{\mathrm p}=3225$ Hz with an overall downward deformation (§5.3, Fig. 20). **Shown**
19. The periodic pressure fluctuation upstream, $(x-x_{imp})/\delta_r\in[-15,-3]$, is due purely to the panel response; a low-frequency oscillation near $-3$ in both static and moving cases is attributed to separation-induced shock motion (§5.3, Fig. 21). **Shown**
20. Separation and reattachment at $-3.38$ and $-0.5$ (static), $-3.42$ and $0.87$ (moving), in $(x-x_{imp})/\delta_r$; the separation in the moving case is 49.0 % larger, with a longer pressure plateau, consistent with Willems et al. (2013) and Hoy & Bermejo-Moreno (2022) (§5.3, Fig. 26). **Shown**
21. The mean panel deformation creates an upstream expansion fan (accelerating the flow and postponing separation, so the onset moves only slightly upstream), then a compression (higher downstream pressure), then a downstream expansion fan (§5.3, Figs. 27, 28). **Derived** (qualitative)
22. The mean wall-temperature jump is essentially unchanged by elasticity; the instantaneous jump at $t=0.51$ ms is much larger than the mean one, and the instantaneous pressure amplitude slightly larger (§5.3, Figs. 28–29). **Shown**
23. Wall-pressure spectra resolve frequencies down to 1667 Hz ($St$ resolution 0.0047, $St=fL_{\mathrm{int}}/U_\infty$, $L_{\mathrm{int}}=3.8\delta_r$) from about 805 convective times; the extra low frequency $St\approx0.01$ is the panel frequency; after high-pass filtering the separation-induced frequency is $St\approx0.03$, about that of the static case, strongest near separation onset, so the coupling does not significantly change it in this case (§5.4, Fig. 30). **Shown**
24. Loose coupling is only first-order accurate but suited to aeroelastic problems with a small fluid-to-structure density ratio (§3.3). **Stated**
25. The high-order spectral finite-element beam avoids shear locking and allows matching meshes at the interface (§1, §6). **Stated**

Method details: the adaptive filter chooses $\alpha_f$ per element so that
$\rho\ge\rho_{\min}$, $p\ge p_{\min}$ and
$\mathcal S(U(\boldsymbol x,t+\Delta t))\ge\min_\Omega\mathcal S(U(\boldsymbol x,t))-\epsilon_{\mathcal S}$,
$\Omega$ the element and its direct neighbours (Eqs. 47–48). Indicators
and settings: Jameson indicator above 0.0008 with $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=0$
(flutter); Persson–Peraire threshold $-4.5$ with $\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=10^{-4}$
(shock–panel); improved Ducros indicator (parameter 10.0) with
$\epsilon_{\mathcal S}=10^{-4}$, plus the filter always active where the
initial $y>2.0\delta_r$ (SWTBLI). SWTBLI case: $M=2.0$, deflection 8°,
shock angle 37.2°, $Re_{\theta}=2188$ at $x_r$, domain
$55\times25\times3\,\delta_r$, $223\times89\times26$ elements, $N=6$,
$\Delta x^+=30$, $\Delta z^+=16$, $\Delta y^+$ from 1.6; inflow by the
reformulated synthetic eddy method with anisotropic linear forcing in
$7.0$–$11.0\delta_r$, sponge at the outflow.

# Printed slips

- **§5:** the entropy tolerance is printed "ϵσ = 10−4", elsewhere $\epsilon_s$.
- **Eqs. (14), (50), (51):** the beam density, defined as $\rho_s$ at Eq. (13), is printed $\rho$ ($\rho A$, $\rho I$), the symbol of the fluid density.
- **Eqs. (51), (58):** Eq. (51) carries $\frac{EA}{J}\int\ldots\,\mathrm d\xi$ while the stiffness entry of Eq. (58) prints $\frac{EA}{J^2}\sum_k\ldots\omega_k$; the change of factor is not explained.
- **§5.3, §5.4:** $f_s$ denotes both the panel frequency (3225 Hz) and the probe sampling frequency (10 MHz).
- **§4.1:** "the oscillation of the $M_\infty=2.1$ case" for the case 2.10.
- **References:** "Pope, S.B., Pope, S.B., 2000" (cited "Pope and Pope (2000)"); Pasquariello, Hickel & Adams (2017) printed "J. Fluid Mech. 823 (617), 014602–014619".
- **Typos:** "split-from ALE DGSEM" (§6, twice), "io further reduce" (§6), "free-steam" (§5.2).
