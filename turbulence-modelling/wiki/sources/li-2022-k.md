# Summary

Li et al. (2022) extend the Reynolds-stress-model-based IDDES of
[Wang et al. (2021)](/sources/wang-2021-l.md) (RSM-IDDES, on the
SSG/LRR-$\omega$ [Reynolds-stress model](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md))
into the "RSM-DynIDDES". In that model the dissipation of the Reynolds
stress is isotropic and set by the hybrid length,
$\varepsilon=k^{3/2}/l_{\mathrm{DES}}$, and in equilibrium turbulence the
LES branch is a [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md)-like model with
$C_{\mathrm{DES}}=C_kC_s$, $C_k$ fixed by the constants of the length-scale
equation. Computing $C_s$ by the compressible
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) of Moin et al. with
Lilly's least squares, a test filter that averages over the direct face
neighbours of a cell of a hybrid unstructured mesh, neighbour averaging and
clipping of negative $C_s^2$ (interpreted as
[backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md)), makes $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$
dynamic and removes its calibration on decaying isotropic turbulence, which
the authors call grid- and [scheme-dependent](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)
and overdissipative in mean shear.

The model, in the HUNS3D finite-volume solver (second-order Roe fluxes), is
tested on [decaying isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
(Comte-Bellot–Corrsin, $64^3$), where spectra agree with experiment and do
not depend on $C_k$; on the [periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md)
at $Re_h=10\,595$ on about $1.1$ million cells, where resolved Reynolds
stresses come closer to experiment, DNS, wall-resolved and
[wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) data than with the
constant-coefficient RSM-IDDES; and on the transonic
[hammerhead launch-vehicle buffet](/benchmarks/hammerhead-launch-vehicle-buffet.md)
case, against SST-IDDES ([$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)
based) and RSM-IDDES. On the hammerhead the mean $C_p$ is unchanged with
respect to RSM-IDDES, while the dynamic model gives less modelled subgrid
stress in the vortex development region, more high-frequency content and a
$C_p$ rms at the experimental peak location of 0.0554 against 0.0454
measured (RSM-IDDES 0.0719, SST-IDDES 0.0897). An appendix checks the
shielding of the boundary layer (no grid-induced separation) and the
absence of [log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md) on a
flat-plate [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md). The
paper belongs to [detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)
within [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md); all evidence is
numerical.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols, as on the [DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)
page: $l_{\mathrm{DES}}$, $l_{\mathrm{RANS}}$, $l_{\mathrm{LES}}$ the
hybrid, RANS and LES length scales, $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$, $\tilde f_d$, $f_e$,
$C_w$, $\Delta_{\max}$, $d_{wn}$ the IDDES ingredients; $\phi_\ell$ the
generic length-scale variable $C_\mu^{-1}k^m\varepsilon^n$ (the source's
$\phi$), $\ell$, $C_\ell$, $C_{\ell\varepsilon}$, $C_{\phi1}$, $C_{\phi2}$
its length scale and constants; $C_k$ the factor of Eq. (9) (registry
§11); $M_{ij}$ the model tensor of the dynamic procedure;
$\mathcal A(\cdot)$ the volume-weighted average over the direct
neighbours of a cell (registry §11; the source's $\langle\cdot\rangle$, not a Reynolds
average). $R_{ij}$ and $k$ are Favre-averaged (RANS) or subgrid (LES
region) quantities.

1. The RSM-DynIDDES applies the dynamic Smagorinsky model to the LES branch of the SSG/LRR-$\omega$ RSM-based IDDES; the DES coefficient becomes dynamic through $C_{\mathrm{DES}}=C_kC_s$ (Abstract, §II C, §IV, Eq. 9). **Derived**
2. Under the equilibrium assumption the dissipation is unified between RANS and LES, $\varepsilon=k^{3/2}/l_{\mathrm{DES}}$, with $l_{\mathrm{LES}}=C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta$ and $l_{\mathrm{RANS}}=\ell/(C_\mu^{1/n}C_\ell)$ (Eq. 6, from ref. 20). **Stated**
3. $C_{\mathrm{DES}}=C_s[C_{\phi1}/(C_{\ell\varepsilon}C_{\phi2})]^{3/4}$, with $C_{\ell\varepsilon}=0.09$; the LES branch is "Smagorinsky-like", a rigorous Smagorinsky model only in equilibrium turbulence (§II C, Eqs. 6, 8, 9). **Derived** (from ref. 20)
4. Constant-coefficient RSM-DES has two problems: $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$ must be calibrated on decaying isotropic turbulence, and the calibration depends on grid resolution and scheme dissipation (citing Mockett, ref. 38); a Smagorinsky dissipation calibrated there is overestimated in mean shear, damping large-scale fluctuations (citing ref. 25), while an underestimated $C_{\mathrm{DES}}$ can make the computation diverge (§I). **Stated**
5. The test filter on hybrid unstructured meshes is the volume-weighted average over the direct face neighbours $N(i)$ of cell $i$, $\hat\phi_i=\sum_{j\in N(i)}\phi_j\Omega_j/\sum_{j\in N(i)}\Omega_j$ with $\Omega_j$ the cell volume (Eq. 14, Fig. 1); $\hat\Delta=a\Delta$, $a>1$, "in general" $a=2$ (§II C). **Stated**
6. $C_s^2=\mathcal A(L^\rho_{ij}M_{ij})/\mathcal A(M_{ij}M_{ij})$ (Lilly), with $L^\rho_{ij}$ the source's density-weighted resolved stress of Eq. (11); negative $C_s^2$ (backscatter) is set to 0 for stability (Eq. 15). **Stated**
7. In decaying isotropic turbulence ($64^3$, Comte-Bellot–Corrsin, $Re_M=34\,000$, initial $t^*=42$, reference $t^*=171$) the RSM-DynIDDES spectra agree with the experiment including the high-wavenumber range, for both SSG and LRR branches (§III A, Fig. 3). **Shown**
8. The spectra "descend slightly with the increase in $C_k$" at high wavenumbers, a discrepancy "small enough to be ignored": the RSM-DynIDDES is not sensitive to $C_k$ (§III A, Fig. 3). **Shown**
9. $C_{k,\mathrm{SSG}}=1.67$ and $C_{k,\mathrm{LRR}}=2.78$ reproduce the HUNS3D RSM-IDDES values $C_{\mathrm{DES},\mathrm{SSG}}=0.3$ and $C_{\mathrm{DES},\mathrm{LRR}}=0.5$ at $C_s=0.18$ (§III A). **Stated** *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $1.67\times0.18=0.3006$, $2.78\times0.18=0.5004$.)*
10. Periodic hill ($Re_h=10\,595$, $160\times115\times60$, about $1.1$ million cells, printed as 1/200 of the DNS and 1/5 of the wall-resolved LES cell count): the mean streamwise velocity of RSM-IDDES and RSM-DynIDDES agrees with experiment, DNS and wall-resolved LES except near the lower wall at $x=4$, where both agree with the WMLES instead (§III B, Fig. 7). **Shown**
11. Mean vertical velocity: good at $x=4,8$; all five numerical results scatter about the experiment at $x=1,2$; the RSM results are close to the WMLES (Fig. 8). **Shown**
12. Resolved Reynolds stresses: in the upper half ($y>1$) both agree with the references, the dynamic version better; near the lower wall all components of RSM-DynIDDES fall within the scatter of experiment, DNS, wall-resolved and wall-modelled LES and nearly match the WMLES (which also uses dynamic Smagorinsky off the wall); RSM-IDDES underestimates the streamwise normal stress at $x=8$ (§III B, Figs. 9–11). **Shown**
13. RSM-DynIDDES captures more small-scale vortex structures ($Q$-criterion) than RSM-IDDES, attributed to "more appropriate assessment of the turbulent dissipation" (Fig. 12). **Shown** (qualitative)
14. Hammerhead: the shear-layer break points of RSM-IDDES and RSM-DynIDDES nearly coincide and lie upstream of that of SST-IDDES; the larger modelled subgrid stress of SST-IDDES in the shear-layer extension region (Region A) delays the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability (Spalart's viewpoint, ref. 47) (§III C, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
15. The dynamic model gives smaller modelled subgrid stress in the vortex development region (Region B), i.e. less subgrid dissipation and finer resolved structures (Fig. 16). **Shown**
16. The attachment line ($C_f=0$) and the recirculation region on the second-stage booster from RSM-DynIDDES and RSM-IDDES coincide and lie upstream of those of SST-IDDES (Fig. 17). **Shown**
17. Mean $C_p$ of all three methods agrees with experiment; RSM-DynIDDES ≈ RSM-IDDES, both closer to experiment than SST-IDDES; the dynamic model "only improves the prediction of the high-frequency part" (Fig. 18a). **Shown**
18. At the location of the maximum experimental $C_p$ rms: 0.0897 (SST-IDDES), 0.0719 (RSM-IDDES), 0.0554 (RSM-DynIDDES), experiment 0.0454; in the shock-oscillation region (adapter/first-stage junction) the three are nearly consistent (§III C, Fig. 18b). **Shown**
19. Velocity-fluctuation PSD along the shear layer: RSM-DynIDDES has the largest high-frequency PSD upstream of $x\approx0.65$, beyond which the three methods agree; the instability point of SST-IDDES lies downstream of the RSM ones (Figs. 20–21). **Shown**
20. Time-averaged normalized energy spectrum: the fraction of turbulent kinetic energy at high wavenumbers is largest for RSM-DynIDDES, then RSM-IDDES, then SST-IDDES (Fig. 22). **Shown**
21. The hammerhead grid (from refs. 20, 41) resolves more than 80% of the turbulent kinetic energy in the separation zone, as examined in ref. 20 (§III C). **Stated**
22. Appendix: the $\tilde f_d$ field on the periodic hill shows an intact thin RANS layer containing the boundary layer upstream of separation, so no modelled-stress depletion or grid-induced separation (App. 1, Fig. 23). **Shown**
23. Appendix: flat plate (NASA TMR, finest grid $545\times385$): $C_f(Re_\theta)$ follows Kármán–Schoenherr; the profile at $Re_\theta=10\,000$ matches Coles in all sublayers, so no log-layer mismatch (App. 2, Fig. 24). **Shown**
24. Mean quantities are similar for RSM-IDDES and RSM-DynIDDES; fluctuation quantities (Reynolds stress, pressure fluctuation) of RSM-DynIDDES are more consistent with experiment or high-fidelity data (§IV). **Shown**

The IDDES length (Eq. 5, after Shur et al., ref. 7) is
$l_{\mathrm{DES}}=\tilde f_d(1+f_e)l_{\mathrm{RANS}}+(1-\tilde f_d)l_{\mathrm{LES}}$,
with $l_{\mathrm{RANS}}=\sqrt k/(C_\mu\omega)$, $l_{\mathrm{LES}}=C_{\mathrm{DES}}\Delta$,
$\Delta=\min[\max(C_wd,C_w\Delta_{\max},d_{wn}),\Delta_{\max}]$, $C_w=0.15$,
and the Reynolds-stress dissipation $\varepsilon_{ij}=\tfrac23(k^{3/2}/l_{\mathrm{DES}})\delta_{ij}$
(Eq. 4). The constants of the SSG/LRR-$\omega$ model and of the
$\phi_\ell$ equation are not printed.

# Printed slips

- **Eq. (3), $\omega$ production.** The term is printed $+\frac{\gamma}{\upsilon_t}\bar R_{ij}\tilde U_{i,j}$, with $\bar R_{ij}$ and no $\bar\rho$, while every other term of the equation carries $\bar\rho$. *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with the paper's own $\bar\rho\tilde R_{ij}=\overline{\rho u_i''u_j''}$ and $\bar\rho P_{ij}=-[\tilde U_{i,k}\bar\rho\tilde R_{kj}+\tilde U_{j,k}\bar\rho\tilde R_{ki}]$, $\tfrac12\bar\rho P_{kk}=-\bar\rho\tilde R_{ij}\tilde U_{i,j}$, so the printed term has the sign opposite to the production $\frac{\gamma_\omega}{\nu_t}\tfrac12\mathcal P_{kk}$ of the SSG/LRR-$\omega$ $\omega$ equation as Liu et al. (2024) print it, Eq. 2, on the [Reynolds-stress model](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md) page.)*
- **$M_{ij}$, two meanings:** the fluctuating mass-flux term of the Reynolds-stress equation (Eq. 2, nomenclature) and the model tensor of the dynamic procedure (Eqs. 10, 12).
- **§II C after Eq. (9):** "the quantity of coefficient $C_{DES}$ … directly affects the quantity of coefficient $C_k$ of the Smagorinsky model"; in Eq. (9) $C_k$ is the length-scale-equation factor and $C_S$ the Smagorinsky coefficient.
- $C_s$ and $C_S$ are used interchangeably (Eqs. 10, 15, text).
- **Eq. (12):** the test-filter accent of the second term spans only the bracketed deviatoric strain $(\tilde S_{ij}-\frac13\tilde S_{kk}\delta_{ij})$ as typeset, not the whole product $\bar\rho|\tilde S_{ij}|(\cdot)$; recorded as printed.
- **Ref. 16** is printed "J. SolKeun and K. Shariff".
