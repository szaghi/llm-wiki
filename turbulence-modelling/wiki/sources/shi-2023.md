# Summary

Shi & Yan (2023) simulate the Mach 2.9 turbulent boundary layer over a
$24^\circ$ compression ramp ($Re_\infty=5.966\times10^6$ m$^{-1}$, the
Bookey et al. configuration), an oblique-shock
[shock–turbulent boundary layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md),
with the [flux reconstruction](/methods/flux-reconstruction.md) method
(correction procedure via reconstruction) in their in-house solver NFR, at
$N=2$ with Roe flux, BR2 viscous terms and SSP-RK3. The run is a
wall-resolved [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md) without an
explicit subgrid model, justified by earlier tests of
[DG](/methods/discontinuous-galerkin-spectral-element-method.md),
[spectral difference](/methods/spectral-difference-method.md) and
flux-reconstruction ILES. Shocks are handled by a KXRCF
[troubled-cell sensor](/definitions/shock-sensor.md) rewritten without the
grid-length scaling, the P-weighted [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)
limiter of Li, Wang & Ren with a mode-truncation rule, and the Zhang–Shu
positivity limiter; the introduction reviews the alternatives, among them
[artificial viscosity](/methods/artificial-viscosity-shock-capturing.md)
and [subcell finite-volume limiting](/methods/subcell-finite-volume-shock-capturing.md).
The inflow comes from a digital filter ([inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md))
with a time correlation in place of the streamwise one.

The solver is validated on the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md)
at $Re=1600$ and on the double Mach reflection. On the ramp, the incoming
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) (integral
quantities, van Driest [velocity](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md),
Reynolds stresses) agrees with [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
references, and the corner $C_f$ and wall pressure converge on the two
finer grids to the WENO DNS data; separation-shock unsteadiness at
$St\approx0.01$ is captured. The maximum amplification of $k$ is 3.34.
The $\langle u'u'\rangle$ peak lies upstream of the other normal-stress
peaks, about three times farther from the wall than the shear layer, and
follows the negative streamwise convective acceleration of the adverse
pressure gradient. A decomposition of the production into shear and
deceleration parts, also rotated to ramp-aligned axes, concludes that
deceleration by the separation shock dominates amplification near
separation, shear dominates in the separated region, and acceleration on
the ramp weakens production while the shear layer keeps amplifying the
turbulence downstream (a result for [shock-generated turbulence](/results/shock-generated-turbulence.md)).
The limiters add dissipation that the paper does not quantify
([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)).

# Key claims

1. Flux-reconstruction simulations of shock-dominated supersonic turbulent flows are rare, interactions on simple geometries having mainly been computed with high-order finite differences; the paper contributes a flux-reconstruction interaction simulation with a high-resolution WENO limiter (§I). **Stated**
2. Dissipative discontinuous methods (DG, SD, FR) are supported by numerical tests for implicit LES without an explicit subgrid model, and the study is run as ILES (§I, citing Li and Wang 2016). **Stated**
3. Qiu and Shu found the TVB, KXRCF and Harten subcell-resolution sensors best for RKDG with WENO limiters, but "still not perfect in accurately detecting the trouble cells" (§I). **Stated**
4. The KXRCF indicator (Eq. 6) is rewritten "based on the non-dimensionalization analysis" without the $h_j^{(N+1)/2}$ scaling (Eq. 7), so that it measures the relative magnitude of the inflow-face jump; density is the indicator variable and the threshold is $C_{\mathrm{KX}}=0.01$ (§II.B.1). **Stated**
5. The P-weighted limiter (Li et al. 2020) uses a smoothness indicator that weakens higher derivatives (Eq. 13), non-constant linear weights $d_l$ and regularizers $\epsilon_l$ (Eq. 14, $K_\epsilon=0.01$), and a truncation rule that zeroes the linear weights of lower modes when they oscillate more than higher ones (Eqs. 15–16, $K_{\mathrm{Trunc}}=1$; on the ramp $K_{\mathrm{Trunc}}=10$, $K_\epsilon=0.1$) (§II.B.2, §IV.A). **Stated**
6. The P-weighted limiter does not guarantee positive density and pressure, and nonphysical states crash high-order interaction simulations, so the Zhang–Shu positivity limiter is added on the Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto points with $\varepsilon=10^{-15}$ (§II.B.3, Eqs. 17–18). **Stated**
7. Taylor–Green vortex ($Re=1600$, $M=0.1$, $N=3$ on $32^3$, $64^3$, $96^3$ elements, 128 to 384 degrees of freedom per direction): the kinetic energy and its dissipation rate converge under $h$-refinement to the spectral $512^3$ reference (§III.A, Figs. 1–2). **Shown**
8. Double Mach reflection ($N=2$, $h=1/120$): the sensor marks the shocks and parts of the shear layer and vortex; the limiter does not harm their resolution, but "wrinkles" of the density contours show that the combination does "not completely remove the numerical oscillations" (§III.B, Fig. 3). **Shown**
9. The incoming boundary layer at $x/\delta=-5.4$ on grids C, M, F ($\delta=6.43$, 6.39, 6.36 mm; $\delta^*=2.41$, 2.41, 2.36 mm; $\theta=0.41$, 0.42, 0.41 mm; $10^3C_f=2.31$, 2.28, 2.33) is converged and within the references (Bookey et al. 2.25, Wu–Martin 2.17, Tong et al. 2.56, Zhu et al. 2.56) (§IV.B, Table III). **Shown**
10. The mean velocity at $x/\delta=-5.4$ falls between the Wu–Martin and Tong et al. DNS profiles; the van Driest velocity is converged and between the references (Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
11. The Reynolds stresses at $x/\delta=-5.4$ agree with the Pirozzoli et al. DNS at $M=3$, $Re_\theta=3311$ (Fig. 10). **Shown**
12. Corner-region $C_f$ and wall pressure on grid C differ from grids M and F although the incoming layer is converged on all three; M and F are converged and agree with the WENO DNS (Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
13. The initial transient vanishes after $37.8\,\delta/U_\infty$; statistics are averaged over $300\,\delta/U_\infty$, as Wu–Martin and Kokkinakis et al. (Tong et al. used 3000) (§IV.A–B, Figs. 6–7). **Shown**
14. The wall-pressure spectrum of the incoming layer peaks at $St=f\delta/U_\infty\approx1$; near separation ($x/\delta=-2.6$) and in the separation shock the characteristic frequency is $St\approx0.01$, so the shock oscillates at low frequency like the bubble (§IV.B, Eq. 32, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
15. Maximum amplification factors and locations $(x/\delta,y/\delta)$: $\langle u'u'\rangle$ 2.82 at $(-2.15,0.091)$; $\langle v'v'\rangle$ 3.81 at $(-0.85,0.384)$; $\langle w'w'\rangle$ 4.23 at $(-1.05,0.329)$; $k$ 3.34 at $(-1.75,0.164)$ (Table IV). **Shown**
16. The amplification of $k$, 3.34 for the ramp at $M_\infty=2.9$, is "within a reasonable range of accuracy" compared with 2.97 (Fang et al.) and 2.7 (Pirozzoli and Grasso) for a $33.2^\circ$ impinging shock at $M_\infty=2.25$ (§IV.C). **Shown** (comparison across configurations)
17. The different peak locations of $\langle u'u'\rangle$ and of $\langle v'v'\rangle$, $\langle w'w'\rangle$ indicate different amplification mechanisms (§IV.C, Fig. 23). **Shown**
18. At $x/\delta=-2.15$ the velocity inflection point is at $y/\delta=0.033$ and the $-\omega_z$ peak at 0.038, while the $\langle u'u'\rangle$ peak is at 0.091: its amplification is "not due to the roll-up of the shear layer" (§IV.C, Figs. 24–26). **Shown**
19. The shear layer detaches at $x/\delta\approx-2.6$, is farthest from the wall at the corner (about $0.5\delta$) and approaches the ramp downstream (§IV.C). **Shown**
20. The streamwise pressure gradient coincides with the streamwise convective acceleration $a_u=\langle u\rangle\partial_x\langle u\rangle+\langle v\rangle\partial_y\langle u\rangle$; near separation $a_u$ falls to $-0.1$ and $\partial_x\langle p\rangle$ rises to 0.5 (normalized), where the $\langle u'u'\rangle$ maximum leaves the wall: its amplification relates to the negative acceleration of the adverse pressure gradient (§IV.C, Eq. 35, Fig. 27). **Shown**
21. In the incoming layer production is dominated by shear (as in Fang et al.); in the early roll-up of the shear layer the streamwise-deceleration part dominates; the wall-normal part is weakly negative in the incoming layer and shear layer and strongest in the shock region (§IV.C, Eq. 36, Fig. 28). **Shown**
22. On the ramp the production does not weaken significantly relative to the flat plate (as in Tong et al.), unlike the impinging-shock case of Fang et al.: ramp and impinging-shock interactions differ physically (§IV.C). **Shown**
23. In ramp-aligned derivatives (Eqs. 37–39) two of the parts are almost zero; the shear group stays nearly constant along the shear layer on the ramp, while the negative streamwise-acceleration part weakens production at the start of the shear layer on the ramp (Figs. 29–31). **Shown**
24. Mechanism (Fig. 32, §V): separation-shock deceleration dominates production around separation, shear dominates in the separated region, acceleration on the ramp weakens amplification, and the shear layer keeps amplifying turbulence downstream. **Shown** (interpretive)

The production decomposition (Eq. 36) in registry notation, with
$\langle\cdot\rangle$ the time and spanwise Reynolds average (no Favre
weighting) and spanwise terms omitted:

$$\langle\rho\rangle P_k=-\langle\rho\rangle\langle u_i'u_j'\rangle\partial_j\langle u_i\rangle\approx\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle u'v'\rangle\big(\partial_y\langle u\rangle+\partial_x\langle v\rangle\big)}_{P_{\mathrm s}}\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle u'u'\rangle\partial_x\langle u\rangle}_{P_{x}}\underbrace{-\langle\rho\rangle\langle v'v'\rangle\partial_y\langle v\rangle}_{P_{y}},$$

normalized by $\rho_w^2u_\tau^4/\mu_w$ at $x/\delta=-5.4$. The amplification
factor of a quantity $q$ is the ratio of its wall-normal maximum at $x$ to
that at the reference station (Eq. 34).
