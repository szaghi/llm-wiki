# Summary

Zhao et al. (2023a) extend the
[high-order gas-kinetic scheme](/methods/high-order-gas-kinetic-scheme.md)
(HGKS), which the authors had used with fifth-order non-compact reconstruction
for [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md), in two directions. The first
is a seventh-order non-compact linear reconstruction, in the interface-normal
direction only (N7T5) or in the normal and tangential directions (N7T7), built
on the seventh-order [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) stencil with linear
weights. The second is a fifth-order compact reconstruction in the normal
direction (C5T5) after the compact gas-kinetic scheme of Ji et al. (2018). It
uses cell averages and cell-averaged derivatives, the latter obtained from
time-evolved interface values, with a fifth-order Hermite WENO on a three-cell
stencil. All variants share the second-order BGK flux, Gauss quadrature on the
faces and a two-stage fourth-order time integration.

An advection test confirms the design orders, but the seventh-order scheme
keeps its order only with $4\times4$ Gauss points per face; with $2\times2$
points it falls to about fourth order. The 16-point variant costs about twice
as much, so the turbulence runs use four points. No subgrid model is used. On
the [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at $Re=1600$
($256^3$), higher order, higher tangential order and compactness all improve
the kinetic-energy, dissipation and enstrophy histories against DNS. They also
lower the [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md),
measured as the residual of the exact compressible kinetic-energy budget; the
authors conclude that the fifth-order scheme "is still over-dissipative" for
ILES. In [turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) at
$Re_\tau=180$ and 395 (DNS of Moser et al. as reference), the same changes
improve the realized $Re_\tau$, the mean velocity, the rms profiles, the
Reynolds shear stress and the high-wavenumber spectra. The viscosity is set
from a target $Re_\tau$ through the [law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md).
The compact scheme approaches the seventh-order non-compact scheme at a cost
close to that of the fifth-order baseline. The introduction cites HGKS
[direct numerical simulations](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
and the explicit subgrid models of
[large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) as background.

# Key claims

1. HGKS with seventh-order non-compact reconstruction in the normal and tangential directions, and HGKS with fifth-order compact reconstruction in the normal direction, are developed and applied to ILES (Abstract, §3, §6). **Stated** (construction in §3)
2. The seventh-order non-compact scheme needs 16 Gauss points per cell interface to keep seventh order; with 4 points the $L_1$ order falls to about 4 under refinement (orders 6.29, 4.63, 4.07 with 4 points; 7.35, 7.46, 6.91 with 16 points) (Abstract, §4, Tables 3–4). **Shown**
3. Cited from Ji's thesis (ref. [39]): "To achieve a 2Mth- or (2M-1)th-order spatial accuracy, at least M×M Gaussian points are required for the cell interface" (§4). **Stated**
4. With $r_n$-th order normal and $r_t$-th order tangential reconstruction and the two-stage fourth-order time integration, the leading truncation error is $O(h_x^{r_n}+h_y^{r_t}+h_z^{r_t}+\Delta t^4)$; the accuracy tests take $\Delta t=0.3h^{r/4}$ ($r=5$ or 7) (§4, Tables 2–5). **Stated**
5. The fifth-order non-compact scheme with 4 points reaches fifth order ($L_1$ orders 5.13, 5.19, 5.06), and the fifth-order compact scheme its design order (4.91, 5.12, 5.02) (§4, Tables 2, 5). **Shown**
6. With 16 Gauss points the seventh-order non-compact scheme costs 1.964 s per step against 0.961 s with 4 points on a $64\times96\times64$ channel grid on 16 cores, "almost twice" (§4). **Shown**
7. Cost per step with 4 Gauss points on the same grid: N5T5 0.595 s, N7T5 0.649 s, N7T7 0.961 s, C5T5 0.618 s; the compact reconstruction adds little cost (§5.2, Table 7). **Shown**
8. For the same order the compact stencil is smaller: fifth-order non-compact reconstruction uses five cell averages, fifth-order compact reconstruction three cell averages and the cell-averaged derivatives of the two neighbours (§3.3, Fig. 2). **Stated**
9. Compact reconstruction "has the consistent physical and numerical domains of dependence" without information from cells with no direct physical connection to the target cell (Abstract, §1, §6, citing refs. [35, 36]). **Stated** (argument, not tested)
10. Taylor–Green vortex ($Re=1600$, $256^3$, $M=0.1$): the kinetic-energy histories of all schemes are "basically consistent with DNS" except at late times, where N7T7 is best, so "the high-order reconstruction in the tangential direction is also important for the present ILES" (§5.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
11. Higher-order and compact reconstruction, and a higher tangential order, improve the enstrophy-based dissipation $\varepsilon(\zeta)$ of the Taylor–Green vortex (§5.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. Q-criterion iso-surfaces at $t=5$ differ negligibly between N5T5 and N7T7 (§5.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. The numerical dissipation $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}=-\mathrm dK/\mathrm dt-(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3)$, with the solenoidal, bulk and pressure-dilatation terms evaluated by sixth-order central differences, is reduced by higher-order or compact reconstruction; "The 5th-order scheme for ILES is still over-dissipative, and reducing the numerical dissipation can improve the results" (§5.1, Eqs. 22–23, Fig. 7). **Shown**
14. Realized $Re_\tau$ at nominal 180: DNS 178.12, N5T5 171.33, N7T5 177.04, N7T7 178.44, C5T5 176.27 (§5.2, Table 8). **Shown**
15. Realized $Re_\tau$ at nominal 395 ($96^3$): DNS 392.24, N5T5 360.35, N7T5 378.97, N7T7 380.45, C5T5 377.05; "when using the higher-order reconstruction or compact reconstruction, the values of $Re_\tau$ are all improved" (§5.2, Table 9). **Shown**
16. Mean velocity at $Re_\tau=180$: seventh order is closer to DNS than fifth order, a higher tangential order improves further (near the centreline), and C5T5 is closer than N5T5; at $Re_\tau=395$ higher order or compactness "all can significantly improve the numerical results" (§5.2.1, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
17. At $Re_\tau=180$ the higher-order schemes improve the rms velocities and the Reynolds shear stress "significantly both in near wall and near center-line region", and C5T5 is better than N5T5 (§5.2.2, Figs. 10–11). **Shown**
18. At $Re_\tau=395$ the streamwise and wall-normal rms of N7T5, N7T7 and C5T5 are closer to DNS than N5T5; "The 5th-order compact scheme can almost achieve the accuracy of the 7th-order non-compact scheme"; spanwise rms and shear stress of all schemes match DNS well (§5.2.2, Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
19. Channel spectra at $Re_\tau=180$ ($y^+=30$ and 180): all schemes match DNS at low wavenumbers, the seventh-order non-compact and fifth-order compact schemes keep more energy at high wavenumbers (more so at $y^+=30$), and all show "an unnatural leveling-off of the energy spectrum near the cutoff wavenumber", attributed to the mesh resolution (§5.2.3, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
20. Higher-order and compact schemes resolve more vortical structures than N5T5 in Q-criterion iso-surfaces at $Re_\tau=180$, with hairpin vortices (§5.2.4, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
21. ILES, proposed in 1992 (Boris et al., ref. [20]), replaces the subgrid dissipation by numerical dissipation and "can overcome the problem of over-dissipation that arises in explicit LES models" (§1, refs. [21, 22]). **Stated**
22. A compact scheme "can achieve higher-order accuracy with the same stencils" and uses fewer cells than a non-compact scheme of the same order (§1). **Stated**
23. Future work: ILES at higher Reynolds and Mach numbers with these reconstructions (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Internal degrees of freedom (§2, p. 3).** "$N=(5-3\gamma)(\gamma-1)$" is printed as a product, which gives 0.32 at $\gamma=1.4$; §5.1 states "$N=2$ for the diatonic gas", which is the quotient $(5-3\gamma)/(\gamma-1)$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Viscosity at $Re_\tau=180$ (§5.2, p. 16).** "$\mu=\rho U_\tau H/Re_\tau=2.83\times10^{-4}$". The paper's own Eqs. 25–26 with $Y_c^+=180$ give $U_c^+=18.48$, $U_\tau=0.0541$ and $\mu=3.01\times10^{-4}$, and the realized values of Table 8 give $U_\tau/Re_\tau=3.01\times10^{-4}$; the same procedure at $Re_\tau=395$ reproduces the printed $1.24\times10^{-4}$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Table 6, grid G1.** $\Delta z^+=8.33$; with $L_z=\pi$, $N_z=64$ and $Re_\tau=180$ it is $8.84$, whereas the printed $\Delta x^+=17.66$ and the G2 spacings reproduce *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Index (§5.1, p. 12).** "$\omega_i=\varepsilon_{ijk}U_{k,j}$, … and $U_{k,j}=\partial U_i/\partial x_j$": the defined derivative should read $U_{i,j}=\partial U_i/\partial x_j$.
- **Superscripts.** Eq. 3 writes $A_l$, $A_r$, Eq. 6 and the compatibility condition $A^l$, $A^r$, $A^k$.
- **Typos.** "Introuction" (§1 heading), "diatonic gas" (§5.1), "is shown is Fig. 9", "The results show that that".
