# Summary

Yan et al. (2022) apply
[field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md)
to the [Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md). A spatial
field $\beta(\boldsymbol x)$ multiplies the SA production term. Field
inversion finds the maximum a posteriori $\beta$ by minimizing a data misfit
plus a Gaussian-prior penalty. The gradients come from the discrete adjoint
of ADflow (differentiated with Tapenade), with a constraint-augmented adjoint
that holds $C_L$ or the bulk velocity fixed. The observations are kept
scarce: $C_L$ for the S809 airfoil near stall, DNS mean-velocity profiles for
the parameterized hills of Xiao et al. at $Re_b=5600$, and $C_L$ and/or $C_D$
for the GLC305 airfoil with horn ice 944. The hill family is a different
configuration from the $Re_h=10{,}595$ case of the
[periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md) benchmark.

The "physical mechanism analysis" shapes the prior from nonequilibrium
turbulence in separating shear layers, where $P_k/\varepsilon$ is far above
the 1.4–1.8 of the equilibrium flows used for calibration. The inversion has
many acceptable solutions, an aspect of
[model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md). Without
regularization the correction field is irregular. Region-limited or uniform
priors give similar fields localized in the separating shear layer, where
the authors' earlier SPF $k$-$\overline{v^2}$-$\omega$ model activates its
nonequilibrium modifier and where Rumsey's separation fix of the
[k-ω SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) acts. The inversions lower
the production upstream and raise it downstream.

A multilayer perceptron with eight local features is trained on the inverted
fields and embedded in the solver. Lower regression loss does not mean lower
a posteriori error in $C_L$, so network size and epoch count are chosen on
the embedded error. This is an [a priori and a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)
gap of the kind that motivates
[model-consistent training](/methods/model-consistent-training.md). The
augmented model generalizes to other Reynolds numbers for S809, to unseen
hill steepness, and to iced-airfoil incidences in the linear range. All
evidence is from two-dimensional steady RANS.

# Key claims

1. Most turbulence models are calibrated in equilibrium shear flows, where the production-to-dissipation ratio of $k$ is about 1.4 to 1.8. In separating shear layers, corner separation, wakes and compressor boundary layers, production can be much larger than dissipation (§1, citing [5]–[7]). **Stated**
2. Ad hoc corrections generalize poorly. Rumsey found that enhanced mixing in a separated shear layer improves the periodic hill but worsens backstep flows. The LES of Fang et al. shows that production can be either increased or decreased in such regions, whereas Li et al.'s SPF correction only increased it (§1). **Stated**
3. The SA production term $P$ is multiplied by a spatial factor: $D\tilde\nu/Dt=\beta P-D+\frac1\sigma[\nabla\cdot((\nu+\tilde\nu)\nabla\tilde\nu)+c_{b2}(\nabla\tilde\nu)^2]$ (Eq. 5), with $\beta=1$ recovering SA (§2.1). **Derived** (model definition)
4. If $\beta$ and the data are Gaussian and pointwise independent, with prior mean 1, MAP inference reduces to minimizing Eq. (6), $\mathcal L=\sum_i\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-2}(d_i-h_i(\boldsymbol\beta))^2+\sum_j\sigma_\beta^{-2}(\beta_j-1)^2$. The second term is a regularization weighted by $\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $\sigma_\beta$ (§2.1). **Derived**
5. The gradient comes from the discrete adjoint (Eqs. 7–11) in ADflow with Tapenade. The constraints (fixed $C_L$, fixed mass flow) are handled by an augmented discrete adjoint (§2.2). **Stated**
6. Field inversion admits several correction fields that all reduce the error sufficiently (S809 with uniform and nonuniform $\sigma_\beta$, Figs. 4–5). A physics-based prior excludes solutions that contradict physical expectations (§3.1.1). **Shown**
7. For S809 at $M=0.10$, $Re_c=2.0\times10^6$, $\alpha=8^\circ$ and $14^\circ$, inversion on $C_L$ alone suppresses production mainly in the strong adverse-pressure-gradient region near the boundary layer and enlarges the separation bubbles (Fig. 6). It improves the $C_p$ distribution, which was not observed (Fig. 7) (§3.1.1). **Shown**
8. Lower training and validation loss does not mean lower a posteriori error of the embedded model. Across 12 network and epoch settings (Table 1), the $C_L$ error at $\alpha=8^\circ$ ranges from −2.0% to 15.1% with no monotone relation to the RMS error in $\beta$. The authors call this "a kind of overfitting" and attribute it to the network seeing out-of-distribution inputs during the iterations before convergence (§3.1.2). **Shown**
9. Hyperparameters are therefore chosen by the a posteriori error of the augmented model, not by validation loss. For S809 the choice is (16, 16, 8) hidden units and 300 epochs (Table 1: RMS error 0.060 / 0.064; $C_L$ error 2.4% at $8^\circ$ and −0.1% at $14^\circ$) (§3.1.2). **Shown**
10. The augmented S809 model improves $C_L$ and $C_D$ against experiment at $Re_c=1$, 2 and $3\times10^6$ over a range of incidence. It improves $C_D$ although $C_D$ was not used in the inversion (Fig. 10) (§3.1.2). **Shown**
11. For the periodic hill, unregularized inversion ($\lambda=0$) gives an irregular correction field with no general tendency ("might be not physical"). Limiting corrections to a rectangle over the separating shear layer ($\lambda=8\times10^{-6}$ inside) still reduces the error sufficiently. Production is then decreased at the start of the shear layer and increased downstream (Fig. 18, Table 2) (§3.2.1). **Shown**
12. A uniform prior ($\lambda=8\times10^{-6}$ everywhere) gives a correction similar to the region-limited one. The authors read this as evidence that inversion captures model-form uncertainty in the critical region with less human intervention. Reducing the error is harder with nonzero $\lambda$ (Fig. 14) (§3.2.1). **Shown**
13. The periodic-hill corrections lie almost in the same region as the nonequilibrium modifier of the SPF $k$-$\overline{v^2}$-$\omega$ model (Fig. 18(4)) and as Rumsey's separation-fix region (Fig. 11) (§3.2.1). **Shown** (visual comparison)
14. Inversion on mean velocity also improves the lower-wall $C_f$, which was not in the objective (Fig. 16). It reproduces the separation at the hill top and the reattachment location (Fig. 17) (§3.2.1). **Shown**
15. A (32, 16, 8) network trained on two hill geometries reproduces the inverted corrections offline (Fig. 19) and the inverted velocity profiles online (Figs. 20–21). At unseen steepness, factor 1.0 (interpolation) and 0.5 and 1.5 (extrapolation), the velocity profiles are closer to DNS than baseline SA in most regions, though "the prediction of the whole flow field … can still be further promoted" (Fig. 22) (§3.2.2). **Shown**
16. For GLC305/944 at $M=0.12$, $Re_c=3.5\times10^6$, baseline SA underpredicts $C_L$ (0.393 against 0.489 at $4^\circ$; 0.495 against 0.669 at $6^\circ$). Inversion FI1 (on $C_L$ and $C_D$) matches both experimental values to the printed digits. FI2 ($C_L$ only) matches $C_L$ but gives $C_D$ 0.0648 / 0.0950 against 0.0566 / 0.1024 (Table 3) (§3.3.1). **Shown**
17. At $6^\circ$ the SA separation bubble covers the whole airfoil, while the corrected bubble covers about half the chord. The reattachment raises $C_L$ (Fig. 25) (§3.3.1). **Shown**
18. The iced-airfoil correction lowers the production multiplier ahead of the separation bubble and raises it in the rear part, so turbulent mixing is both decreased and increased. The increase coincides with the region where the SPF model has $P_k/\varepsilon\gg1$ and its nonequilibrium modifier is active (Figs. 26–27) (§3.3.1). **Shown**
19. FI1 gives a better $C_p$ plateau than FI2, although $C_p$ is unobserved (Fig. 28): more observations make data assimilation more credible (§3.3.1). **Shown**
20. The augmented iced-airfoil model ((64, 32, 16), 2000 epochs, 131,328 samples) reduces the $C_L$ error from −21.5% to −7.0% at $4^\circ$ and from −26.8% to −5.5% at $6^\circ$, and the $C_D$ error from 6.4% to 3.5% and from −7.1% to −6.3% (Table 4) (§3.3.2). **Shown**
21. Over $0^\circ$–$7^\circ$ the augmented iced-airfoil model predicts $C_L$ much better than SA at most angles. It generalizes satisfactorily in the linear range ($\alpha<6^\circ$) and deteriorates beyond (Fig. 33) (§3.3.2). **Shown**
22. Neural networks are preferred to Gaussian processes and random forests because their evaluation cost does not depend on the size of the training set, which matters since the model is called at every grid point and iteration (§2.3). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **SA destruction term (p. 9).** The term is printed $D=C_{w1}f_w\left[\frac{\tilde\nu}{d}\right]$, without a square. $P=c_{b1}\tilde S\tilde\nu$ has the dimension of $\tilde\nu$ per unit time, and the printed $D$ does not, so the printed form is inconsistent with Eq. (2). $(\tilde\nu/d)^2$ would be consistent. *(Compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- **Equation numbering.** Eq. (11) is followed by Eq. (14), with no (12) or (13). Eq. (17) (periodic hill, p. 25) comes before Eq. (16) (iced airfoil, p. 36).
- **Eq. (17).** The penalty sums $\lambda_i(\beta_j-1)^2$ over $j$, an index mismatch. The text then uses a scalar $\lambda$.
- **Eqs. (14)–(15).** $\sigma_{\beta,i}$ is called a "variance" but enters as $\sigma^{-2}$. Eq. (15) conditions on "x<1.0 / x>1.0" without defining $x$.
- **Citation numbers.** "Papadimitriou et al. used the high-order adjoint method … [16]", but [16] is Xiao et al. (2016). "Oliver et al. … [17]. Dow and Wang used the adjoint method … [17]" gives one number to two works, and the list has no Dow & Wang entry. The shielding function $f_d$, "initially proposed for detached eddy simulations [39]", points to Rashad & Zingg, which [39] also cites as the augmented discrete adjoint.
- **Periodic-hill training geometries.** §3.2.2 says the inversions are "implemented in two cases with α being 0.5 and 0.8". The same section calls 0.5 an extrapolation case and 1.0 an interpolation case, and the captions of Figs. 19–21 name 0.8 and 1.2 as the inversion cases.
- **Sample counts.** The S809 count of 88,658 samples equals $457\times97\times2$ points on the stated grid. The iced airfoil uses the same stated $457\times97$ grid at two angles but prints 131,328 samples. The periodic-hill grid of $81\times77$ points has $80\times76=6080$ cells per geometry, i.e. 12,160 cells for two, against the printed 11,200 "grid cells". *(Compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- **Table 4 against Table 3.** From the Table 3 values, baseline SA errors are −19.6% ($C_L$, $4^\circ$), −26.0% ($C_L$, $6^\circ$), +9.2% ($C_D$, $4^\circ$, SA 0.0618 against 0.0566) and −3.7% ($C_D$, $6^\circ$, 0.0986 against 0.1024). Table 4 prints −21.5%, −26.8%, 6.4% and −7.1%. *(Compiler check, not in the sources.)*
- **Figure references.** §3.3.2: "A comparison of Fig. 25 and Fig. 26 indicates that the results from FI can be reproduced". Fig. 26 is the correction field; the comparison of the augmented model with the inversion is Figs. 31–32 against Figs. 25 and 28. Sub-captions repeat labels: "(4)" twice in Fig. 17, "(1)" for the second row of Figs. 19–21, and (1), (2), (4), (5) in Fig. 26.
- **Typos.** "tailing edge" (p. 15), "epocs" (p. 22), "Tapanade" (p. 12, "Tapenade" in ref. [38]). Ref. [7] prints garbled co-author names ("B Hkzc, B Lplc"), and ref. [3] prints "fows", "fuid".
