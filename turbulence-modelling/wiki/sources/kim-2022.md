# Summary

Kim et al. (2022) train a neural-network subgrid-scale model for
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) of incompressible
[turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) by deep
[reinforcement learning](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md),
without filtered-DNS flow fields: the only targets are the mean viscous
stress and mean resolved Reynolds shear stress profiles of filtered
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md). Training and testing
happen in the same LES environment, so the authors argue that the gap
between [a priori and a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)
performance of supervised learning is removed by construction, and that
DRL needs neither stored 3-D fields nor a differentiable solver
([model-consistent training](/methods/model-consistent-training.md)).
The actor is a pointwise network mapping the resolved velocity gradient and
the grid size, in wall units, to the six components of $\tau_{ij}$ (one
variant outputs $\nu_t$); the critic is a convolutional network. Four
modifications of DDPG/TD3 are proposed: a wall-normal localized reward, an
$N$-step discounted time-averaged reward replacing the Bellman bootstrap,
spatially and temporally correlated parameter-space noise, and supervised
pretraining on the static [Vreman model](/methods/vreman-model.md).
Reflectional equivariance and zero SGS stress at the wall are built into
the network; a [tensor-basis network](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md)
gave no conclusive gain.

At $Re_\tau=180$ the tensor models reproduce the filtered-DNS mean velocity
and Reynolds shear stress and improve rms velocities, mean SGS stress and
transport over Vreman, [dynamic Smagorinsky](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md)
and no-model LES; the eddy-viscosity variant overpredicts the mean velocity,
which the authors read against the [Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md).
Clipping [backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md) barely changes
the result. Training also succeeds on a coarser grid and at $Re_\tau=360$;
untrained Reynolds numbers give buffer-layer slope errors relative to the
[law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md), and an untrained coarse
grid fails. Filtered DNS shows $\tau^d_{ij}$ nearly orthogonal to
$\bar S_{ij}$ on average, the DRL models lie between it and the aligned
eddy-viscosity models, and the authors conclude that this non-alignment,
not the eddy-viscosity level, matters most.

# Key claims

1. DRL trained only on mean statistics (mean viscous stress and mean resolved Reynolds shear stress of filtered DNS) finds SGS models whose LES reproduces them "perfectly consistent with the filtered DNS" in the training environment (Abstract; §III C, Fig. 7a,b). **Shown**
2. The tensor models DRL180 and DRL180(clip) predict the resolved rms velocity, $\langle\tau_{12}\rangle$ and the mean SGS transport better than the static Vreman model (SVM), the dynamic Smagorinsky model (DSM), the eddy-viscosity DRL model and no-model LES at $Re_\tau=180$, $(\Delta x^+,\Delta z^+)=(70.7,35.3)$ (§III C, Fig. 7c,d,f). **Shown**
3. The eddy-viscosity DRL model, $\tau^d_{ij}=-2\nu_t\bar S_{ij}$, overpredicts $\langle\bar u\rangle$ in the buffer and log layers, like the DSM; "the eddy-viscosity assumption could seriously limit the performance" (§III C, Fig. 7a; §IV). **Shown**
4. Backscatter is not necessary to predict $\langle\bar u\rangle$ and $\langle\bar u'\bar v'\rangle$: DRL180(clip), with negative SGS dissipation clipped to zero, performs almost as DRL180, which itself shows little backscatter (§III C, Fig. 14). **Shown**
5. The mean SGS dissipation is not determined uniquely by the two targets: DRL models trained with the same settings give different values; an accurate mean SGS transport "appears to be a necessary condition" for accurate $\langle\bar u\rangle$ and $\langle\bar u'\bar v'\rangle$ (§III C, Fig. 7e,f). **Shown**
6. One-step TD learning (TD3 with the Bellman target) and the instantaneous reward both fail to raise the accuracy; the accumulated reward improves from $N^s=20$ and saturates for $N^s\ge50$; $\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}=0.99$ is optimal for $N^s=200$ (§III A, Fig. 4). **Shown**
7. The optimal reward time scale, $-\Delta t^{\mathrm{DRL}}/\ln\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}\approx100\Delta t^{\mathrm{DRL}}$, is "approximately 500 wall time units", about ten lifetimes of near-wall wall-shear-stress structures (about 60 wall units, Quadrio & Luchini 2003) (§III A). **Derived** (time scale) / **Stated** (lifetime)
8. Parameter-space noise from an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process gives a "critical improvement" over no noise and over decorrelated Gaussian action noise (20% of the local rms action); a target policy distance of 0.05 is fastest and most stable, 0.02 slow, 0.1 oscillating through LES divergence (§III A, Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. With 3 hidden actor layers DRL cannot find an optimal model on a coarse LES grid; 6 and 9 layers succeed and 6 is chosen for cost (§III A). **Shown** (not plotted)
10. The wall boundary constraint makes learning faster and more stable; without it exploration produces "nonphysically strong SGS mean shear stress near the wall"; its effect is small when the grid size is a network input (§III B, Fig. 6a). **Shown**
11. Without the reflectional-equivariance constraint a spanwise mean velocity appears, since spanwise statistics are not in the reward; with it the flow stays stationary (§III B, Fig. 6b). **Shown**
12. A tensor-basis network (Ling et al. 2016) for rotational invariance "did not conclusively help improve DRL performance, presumably due to strong anisotropy of the near-wall turbulence" (§II E). **Stated**
13. Pretraining on SVM or DSM both lead to successful DRL, SVM slightly faster; pretraining on the scale-similarity model makes DRL unstable because the LES is numerically unstable, which suggests clipping is needed ([scale-similarity model](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md)) (§II D). **Stated**
14. Multiplying the actor output by $\Delta^2$ improves generalization to grid resolution; without it the network can produce strong SGS stress even on DNS-resolution grids (§II C). **Stated**
15. DRL succeeds on the coarser grid $(94.2,47.1)$, where the filtered-DNS peak of $\langle\tau_{12}\rangle$ is about twice that of the finer grid and three times that of the DSM; DRL180c and DRL180c(clip) match filtered-DNS mean velocity, Reynolds stress, rms and $\langle\tau_{12}\rangle$ (§III C, Fig. 10). **Shown**
16. DRL also succeeds at $Re_\tau=360$ with the same hyperparameters (§III C, Fig. 11). **Shown**
17. At untrained Reynolds numbers with fixed wall-unit horizontal resolution, DRL180 underestimates the buffer-layer slope of $\langle\bar u\rangle$ at $Re_\tau=360$, DRL360 overestimates $\langle\bar u\rangle$ at $Re_\tau=180$, and both underestimate the slope at $Re_\tau=720$; their Reynolds shear stress stays "quite precise", while DSM and no-model LES overpredict it at all $Re_\tau$ (§III D, Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
18. DRL180 on an untrained coarse grid overpredicts the mean Reynolds shear stress and underpredicts $\langle\tau_{12}\rangle$: "a model trained in a single environment might not perform well in a different environment" (§III D). **Shown** (not plotted)
19. In filtered DNS, $\bar S_{12}$ and $-\tau_{12}$ are almost uncorrelated (correlation $-0.20$ at $y^+\approx15$, 0.19 at $y^+\approx50$), the DSM nearly perfectly (0.95, 0.94), DRL180 in between (0.34, 0.58) (Table IV, Figs. 15–16). **Shown**
20. The correlation between $\lvert\bar S\rvert^3$ and the SGS dissipation is 0.01 / 0.28 in filtered DNS at $y^+\approx15$ / 50, against 0.92 / 0.88 for the DSM and 0.56 / 0.73 for DRL180 (Table IV, Fig. 17). **Shown**
21. The angle between $-\tau^d_{ij}$ and $\bar S_{ij}$ is zero by construction for linear eddy-viscosity models; in filtered DNS its mean is near $\pi/2$ at all heights (its PDF is compared with that of two random trace-free symmetric tensors); in DRL180 the peak moves from about 0.3 rad near the wall towards $\pi/2$ with distance; in DRL180(clip) the mean is about 0.9 rad at all heights (§III E, Fig. 18). **Shown**
22. The ratio of the eigenvalues of $-\tau^d_{ij}$ and $\bar S_{ij}$ (twice a plane-averaged eddy viscosity) is "one order of magnitude larger" in filtered DNS than in every model, the models being similar to one another (DRL about twice DSM and SVM for $y^+\le15$); "the non-alignment nature between $-\tau^*_{ij}$ and $\bar S_{ij}$, rather than the level of eddy viscosity, plays a more important role" (§III E, Fig. 20). **Shown** (data) / **Stated** (interpretation)
23. Supervised network SGS models from local velocity gradients (Park & Choi 2021) reach a correlation of only about 0.5 with filtered-DNS SGS stress; their model with the same inputs as here did not predict $\langle\bar u\rangle$ and $\langle\bar u'\bar v'\rangle$ well and "highly overpredicted the magnitude of $\tau_{xy}$"; "DRL using only statistical data for training can be superior to the standard supervised learning using expensive filtered DNS data" (§III C, §III E). **Stated** (citing ref. 43)
24. Pretraining takes 50,000 iterations ("only a few minutes"); DRL takes 10,000–15,000 iterations, about 2–4 days on one NVIDIA Titan Xp GPU, with eight parallel LES environments (§II F). **Stated**
25. The vortical structures of all DRL models resemble those of filtered DNS; the DSM shows slightly more near-wall vortices and no-model LES many non-physical structures (§III C, Fig. 9). **Shown** (qualitative)

# Printed slips

1. Eq. (22) opens a parenthesis after $\lVert s^{l*}\rVert_2$ that is never closed before "/4"; the inline formula for the reflection-symmetrized policy is balanced.
2. §II F gives the episode length as "approximately $1.1\times10^5$ $(=\Delta t^{DRL}\times N^{DRL})$ in wall time units"; with $\Delta t^{\mathrm{DRL}}\approx5.4$ and $N^{\mathrm{DRL}}=10{,}000$–$15{,}000$ the product is $5.4\times10^4$–$8.1\times10^4$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
3. §III A: "$N^s=50$ corresponds to 250 wall time units", while $50\times5.4=270$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
4. The captions of Figs. 15–16 say "Correlation between $\bar S_{xy}$ and $\tau_{xy}$", while the text and Table IV use $-\tau_{xy}$.
5. "Reyonlds" (§III E), "occured" (§III C), "dealising".
6. The abstract writes "reflectional invariance" and the conclusions "reflection invariance"; the body calls the actor property equivariance.
7. §II F: "rectified linear unit with a slope of 2", printed without explanation.
8. The $N^s$ used for the production runs is not stated; $N^s=200$, $\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}=0.99$ is the optimum of Fig. 4 (a gap, not a slip).
