# Summary

Li et al. (2021) use gene-expression programming (GEP), an evolutionary
[symbolic regression](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md), to learn
an explicit algebraic subgrid-stress model for
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) of incompressible forced
[homogeneous isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md).
The deviatoric subgrid stress is written on Pope's ten-tensor
[integrity basis](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) of the
normalized resolved strain and rotation, scaled by $\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert^2$.
GEP searches the scalar coefficients as functions of four invariants. The
training data come from a $1024^3$ pseudospectral
[DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) at $Re_\lambda\approx260$,
filtered with a top-hat filter of width $\Delta=16$ DNS cells and coarsened
to $64^3$. The fitness is the mean relative Frobenius error of the stress.

[A priori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md), the
correlation with filtered DNS rises from 0.321 with one tensor to 0.888
with four, then plateaus and falls beyond seven. The retained four-tensor
model converges to constant coefficients. Its linear (eddy-viscosity)
coefficient is small, and an ablation shows that the two rotation-containing
tensors dominate.

A posteriori, $128^3$ LES compares GEP with the
[dynamic Smagorinsky](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) model, a dynamic
[mixed model](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md), the
[neural-network model](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md) of Xie et al.
(2020) and no model, all against filtered DNS. GEP and the network agree
closely with the filtered DNS in energy spectra, structure functions,
velocity-increment PDFs, stress PDFs and the PDF of the SGS energy flux,
including [backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md). The dynamic
models do not: the [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md)-type
eddy viscosity damps the spectrum near the cutoff and gives a strictly
positive flux. The authors relate the dominant tensor to the normalized
$Q$ criterion, that is to intense vortex filaments, and attribute the gain
"presumably" to that term and to the weak dissipative term. The
[gradient model](/methods/gradient-model.md) appears only in the
literature survey. GEP keeps its spectra at $64^3$, $32^3$ and at
$Re_\lambda=180$ and 250, and costs per iteration slightly more than dynamic
Smagorinsky and far less than the network. The DNS and LES code adds a
"high-order dissipative numerical method" at high wavenumbers, whose
[numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) the paper does
not separate from the model's.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $\mathsf s_{ij}=\sqrt2\,\bar S_{ij}/\lvert\bar S\rvert$,
$\mathsf w_{ij}=\sqrt2\,\bar\Omega_{ij}/\lvert\bar S\rvert$ (registry §11, as on
the [tensor-basis](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md) page), and
$T^{(n)}$ Pope's basis tensors of $\mathsf s$, $\mathsf w$.

1. GEP is applied, per the authors for the first time, to develop SGS models for fully developed isotropic turbulence, tested a priori and a posteriori against traditional models and a neural-network model (§I). **Stated**
2. Earlier GEP work for LES: Schoepplein et al. (2018) trained SGS models on premixed-flame DNS but tested them only a priori; Reissmann et al. (2021) trained them on the Taylor–Green vortex without analysing turbulence statistics or fully developed turbulence (§I). **Stated**
3. The deviatoric subgrid stress is modelled as $\tau^d_{ij}=\tfrac12\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert^2\sum_{n=1}^{10}g^{(n)}(I_1,\dots,I_4)T^{(n)}_{ij}$, the expansion of Pope (1975) (§II B, Eqs. 9–14). **Derived** (background expansion)
4. Mean a priori correlation coefficient of $\tau^d_{ij}$ on the test set, each value the best of 20 training runs: 0.321, 0.630, 0.640, 0.888, 0.889, 0.869, 0.883, 0.854, 0.774, 0.764 for one to ten basis tensors (Table II, Fig. 1). **Shown**
5. The correlation stops increasing at four tensors and decreases beyond seven, which the authors attribute to the search space growing at fixed hyperparameters (100 individuals, 500 generations) (§III B). **Shown** (trend) / **Stated** (cause)
6. The retained four-tensor model is $\tau^{\mathrm{GEP}}_{ij}=\tfrac12\Delta^2\lvert\bar S\rvert^2\big(-0.01T^{(1)}-0.1T^{(2)}+0.07T^{(3)}-0.11T^{(4)}\big)$ (Eq. 22). **Shown**
7. Over 30 four-tensor training runs the coefficients are mostly constants: $g^{(1)}$ in 25/30 runs (range $-0.02$ to 0), $g^{(2)}$ in 29/30 ($-0.11$ to $-0.10$), $g^{(3)}$ in 18/30 (0.06 to 0.10), $g^{(4)}$ in 29/30 ($-0.11$ to $-0.10$) (Table III). **Shown**
8. Ablation a priori: $T^{(2)}$ alone ($g^{(2)}=-0.11$) 0.5403; $T^{(4)}$ alone ($g^{(4)}=-0.10$) 0.6944; $T^{(2)}+T^{(4)}$ 0.8792; adding $T^{(1)}$ ($-0.02$) 0.8831; adding $T^{(3)}$ (0.07) 0.8874; all four 0.8885; $T^{(2)}$ and $T^{(4)}$, both containing the rotation, lead (Table IV). **Shown**
9. Energy spectra at $128^3$: the network and GEP nearly overlap filtered DNS; dynamic Smagorinsky and the dynamic mixed model are damped near the cutoff and "energy-rich" at lower wavenumbers; LES without a model deviates strongly near the cutoff (§III C, Fig. 2). **Shown**
10. Longitudinal structure functions of orders 2, 4, 6: all models agree at $r/\Delta=8$, 16; at small separations the dynamic models underpredict, more so at higher order, while GEP and the network stay close to filtered DNS (Fig. 3). **Shown**
11. PDFs of normalized velocity increments: all models agree at $r=8\Delta$; at $r=\Delta$, $2\Delta$, $4\Delta$ the network and GEP beat the dynamic models, especially in the tails (Fig. 4). **Shown**
12. PDFs of $\tau^d_{ij}$: dynamic Smagorinsky narrowest (pure dissipation), the mixed model better (similarity part), GEP much closer to filtered DNS, the network slightly wider than GEP, their differences only in the extreme tails (Fig. 5). **Shown**
13. SGS energy flux $\Pi=-\tau_{ij}\bar S_{ij}$: dynamic Smagorinsky is strictly positive (no backscatter); the dynamic models differ significantly from filtered DNS; GEP and the network predict both forward transfer and backscatter satisfactorily (Eq. 21, Fig. 6). **Shown**
14. Contours of $\tau^d_{22}$, $\tau^d_{23}$: the mixed model's structures are larger and more diffuse than filtered DNS, attributed to extra dissipation; the network and GEP capture fine-scale structures (Figs. 7–8). **Shown** (visual)
15. Statistics over the GEP LES (Table V): components of $T^{(1)}$ range over $-0.817$ to 0.816 (std 0.341), of $T^{(2)}$ over $-28.1$ to 20.5 (std 0.476), of $T^{(3)}$ over $-0.333$ to 0.334 (std 0.139), of $T^{(4)}$ over $-326.8$ to 167.2 (std 0.571); the extremes of $T^{(4)}$ mark high-vorticity events the SGS model must account for (§III D). **Shown**
16. $T^{(1)}$ and $T^{(3)}$ (strain only) are spread evenly in space, $T^{(2)}$ and $T^{(4)}$ concentrate in specific regions with spiky PDFs, and $\lvert T^{(4)}\rvert$ correlates strongly with the normalized $Q$ criterion (Figs. 9–10). **Shown** (visual)
17. The normalized $Q$ criterion is $\tfrac12(\lvert\mathsf w\rvert^2-1)$ in the source's normalization and $\lvert T^{(4)}\rvert=\lvert\mathsf w\rvert^2$, so $T^{(4)}$ brings in strong vortex filaments (Eqs. 24–25). **Derived** (see Printed slips)
18. The coefficient of $T^{(1)}$, the linear eddy-viscosity term, is much smaller than in Smagorinsky-type models, so the model is less dissipative; this and the vorticity terms are "presumably" why it predicts better (§III D, §IV). **Stated**
19. GEP predicts the energy spectrum accurately at $128^3$, $64^3$, $32^3$ (filter widths 16, 32, 64 DNS cells), "independent of the grid resolution", and at $Re_\lambda=180$ and 250 (§III E, Figs. 11–12). **Shown** (spectra only)
20. Cost per iteration on 16 cores: the network is much more expensive than all others, especially on larger grids; GEP is much cheaper than the network and the mixed model and slightly more expensive than dynamic Smagorinsky; no timings are printed (§III F, Fig. 13). **Shown**
21. GEP and the network reach similar accuracy, but GEP's explicit equation is interpretable and its cost "comparable to traditional models" (§III F, §IV). **Shown**/**Stated**
22. The model is trained and tested only for isotropic turbulence; other flows need investigation (§IV). **Stated**

The GEP fitness (Eqs. 15–16) and the a priori metric (Eq. 18), with
$\lVert\cdot\rVert_F$ the Frobenius norm, $n_p$ data points and
$\langle\cdot\rangle$ a volume average (the paper's notation):

$$\mathrm{fit}=\frac1{n_p}\sum_{k=1}^{n_p}\frac{\lVert\tau^{d,\mathrm{DNS}}_{ij}(k)-\tau^{d,\mathrm{model}}_{ij}(k)\rVert_F}{\lVert\tau^{d,\mathrm{DNS}}_{ij}(k)\rVert_F},\qquad
C(H)=\frac{\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)(H^{\mathrm m}-\langle H^{\mathrm m}\rangle)\rangle}{\big(\langle(H-\langle H\rangle)^2\rangle\langle(H^{\mathrm m}-\langle H^{\mathrm m}\rangle)^2\rangle\big)^{1/2}} .$$

# Printed slips

- Table II header: the sixth stress column is printed "$\tau^A_{12}$" a second time; Fig. 5 shows $\tau^A_{23}$ as the sixth component.
- §III A: "We choose every 17th grid point in each direction with $1024^3$ degrees of freedom and then get $16^3$ different datasets of $64^3$ coarse grid points"; $16^3$ offset datasets of $64^3$ points from $1024^3$ correspond to a stride of $1024/64=16$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- §III C cites "the DNS dataset in Sec. II B"; the dataset is described in Sec. III A (Sec. II B is the GEP modelling section).
- Eq. (24) prints $q=\frac{Q}{|S|^2}=\frac{1}{2|s|^2}(|\Omega|^2-|S|^2)=\frac12(|\omega|^2-1)$. Since Eq. (12) makes $|s|=1$, the middle expression equals $Q$, not $q$; the prefactor $\frac{1}{2|S|^2}$ gives the final expression *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (25) prints $|T^{(4)}|=|\omega|^2$. With $|T|=\sqrt{T_{ij}T_{ij}}$ (Eq. 23) and $|\omega|=\sqrt{\omega_{ij}\omega_{ij}}$ by analogy with Eq. (12), an antisymmetric $\omega$ gives $\omega^2$ the eigenvalues $0,-\tfrac12|\omega|^2,-\tfrac12|\omega|^2$, so $T^{(4)}=\omega^2+\tfrac13|\omega|^2I$ has norm $|\omega|^2/\sqrt6$; the proportionality the argument uses holds *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- Eq. (22) prints the coefficients $(-0.01,-0.1,0.07,-0.11)$, while the "all four terms" row of the ablation (Table IV) uses $(-0.02,-0.11,0.07,-0.10)$; the text does not say which set is ablated.
- Eqs. (2) and (7) print the viscous term $\nu\,\partial^2u_i/\partial x_jx_j$; §III D and the Table V caption call the normalized rotation "$\Omega$", which Eq. (12) names $\omega$; the Fig. 1 legend reads "Average Correlation efficient".
