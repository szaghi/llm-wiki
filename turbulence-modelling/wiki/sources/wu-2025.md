# Summary

Wu et al. (2025) modify the inversion step of
[field inversion and machine learning](/methods/field-inversion-machine-learning.md)
for the [k-ω SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md). They argue that a
classic correction field $\beta(\boldsymbol x)$ on the $\omega$-destruction
term spoils the model's log-layer calibration wherever $\beta\ne1$ in an
attached boundary layer, which conflicts with the
[law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md). They call the failure poor
"L2 generalizability" and set up a four-level scale (L1–L4). Their
**conditioned field inversion** multiplies the destruction term by
$(\beta-1)f_d+1$. Here $f_d$ is the shielding function of delayed
[detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md), so the
correction is off inside attached boundary layers both during inversion and in
prediction.

The inversion uses adjoint gradients from DAFoam on
[OpenFOAM](/software/openfoam.md) and the SLSQP optimizer, with LES velocity
data on a curved [backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md)
and on the [NASA wall-mounted hump](/benchmarks/nasa-wall-mounted-hump.md).
The inferred fields are then fitted by PySR into closed-form
[symbolic-regression closures](/methods/symbolic-regression-closure.md)
(SR-CLS from classic inversion, SR-CND from conditioned inversion). Every test
is a posteriori, with one a priori check
([a priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)).

Both models improve on SST on four kinds of separated flow:
- the parameterized [periodic hills](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md);
- the NLR-7301 two-element airfoil;
- the SAE notchback;
- the 25° [Ahmed body](/benchmarks/ahmed-body.md).

Only SR-CND reproduces SST on two attached flows: the zero-pressure-gradient
[turbulent boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) and the
[NACA 0012](/benchmarks/naca-0012-airfoil.md) at zero incidence. SR-CLS
overpredicts $C_f$ there and distorts the log layer. The correction field is
read as a measure of model error
([model-form uncertainty](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md)). The
production feature uses the [Boussinesq](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
Reynolds stress.

# Key claims

Symbols: $\beta_\omega$ and $\gamma_\omega$ are the SST
$\omega$-destruction and $\omega$-production coefficients (registry §11; the source's
$\theta$ and $\gamma$; $\beta$ and $\alpha$ in the equations of the SST page),
renamed by the source so that $\beta$ is free for the correction; $f_d$,
$r_d$ are the DDES shielding function and its argument (registry §11).
Page-local: $\lambda_1=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf S^2)$, $\lambda_2=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf W^2)$
and $\lambda_5=\mathrm{tr}(\mathsf W^2\mathsf S^2)$ are invariants of the mean
strain and rotation tensors normalized by $\beta^*\omega$ (the source's
$\hat S$, $\hat\Omega$). FI-CLS and FI-CND
are the classic and conditioned inversions, and SR-CLS and SR-CND the
symbolic models trained on them.

1. Classic FIML models can lose accuracy in attached boundary layers. The paper proposes a four-level generalizability scale (Table 1): L1, similar geometries; L2, no degradation of the baseline in simple attached flows; L3, the same separation mechanism with different geometry and Reynolds number; L4, different separation mechanisms. Most FIML studies test only L1 (§1, Table 1). **Stated**
2. Rumsey et al. [31] found that a model trained on an adverse-pressure-gradient plate fails on the zero-pressure-gradient plate. The probabilistic FIML of Pepper, Ho et al. [32] improved L2 at the cost of L1/L3. Jäckel's closed-form correction to SA [33] preserved the flat plate without reducing separated-flow error enough. The constrained recalibration of Bin et al. [34][35] preserves the law of the wall, but its parameters are constant in space (§1). **Stated**
3. The log-layer calibration of the $\omega$ equation requires $\beta_\omega-\beta^*\gamma_\omega=\kappa^2\sqrt{\beta^*}\sigma_\omega$ with $\kappa=0.41$ (Eq. 3, citing Wilcox [38]). A classic correction turns it into $\beta(\boldsymbol x)\beta_\omega-\beta^*\gamma_\omega=\kappa^2\sqrt{\beta^*}\sigma_\omega$ (Eq. 4), so $\beta$ must equal 1 in the log layer. Wake-law calibrations under pressure gradient are also affected by $\beta\ne1$ (§2.1). **Derived**
4. Conditioned field inversion replaces the destruction term $\beta_\omega\omega^2$ with $[(\beta(\boldsymbol x)-1)f_d+1]\beta_\omega\omega^2$, where $f_d=1-\tanh[(8r_d)^3]$ and $r_d=(\nu+\nu_t)/(\kappa^2d^2\sqrt{\partial_ju_i\,\partial_ju_i})$ (Eqs. 5–6, from Spalart et al. 2006). The model is identical to SST wherever $f_d=0$, which covers the attached boundary layer in practice (Fig. 2). The authors say no earlier work used such a filter in the field-inversion stage of FIML (§1, §2.1). **Stated** (construction) / **Shown** (Fig. 2)
5. Restricting the correction to outside the boundary layer is justified because errors in separation prediction arise mainly in the separated shear layer, outside the shielded region [39][40] (§2.1, §3.2). **Stated**
6. On the curved backward-facing step, FI-CND reduces the objective by 74.7% and FI-CLS by 88.3% (Fig. 4). Both fix the separation zone that SST overpredicts (Fig. 5). FI-CLS raises $\beta$ in the separated shear layer and also in the attached boundary layer upstream of the step, while FI-CND raises it only in the shear layer (Fig. 7) (§3.1.1). **Shown**
7. On the NASA hump both inversions converge in about 140 SLSQP iterations, and the objective falls by 66.7% (FI-CND) and 61.7% (FI-CLS) (Fig. 9). FI-CLS raises $\beta$ to about 2 near the shear layer and inside the attached boundary layer at the hump top. FI-CND raises it to 4–5 only around the separated shear layer (Fig. 12) (§3.1.2). **Shown**
8. Symbolic regression on the downsampled mixed datasets (about 3000 samples each) gives SR-CLS $\beta-1=-\frac{3}{500}\lambda_5\tanh(-0.092\lambda_2)$ and SR-CND $\beta-1=\min(0.00435\lambda_2^2,\ 3.806)$ (Table 4). Of the five candidate features, only $\lambda_2$ and $\lambda_5$ survive. **Shown**
9. Both expressions raise $\beta$ where the nondimensional rotation or strain rate is large. A larger $\beta$ lowers $\omega$, raising the eddy viscosity and the momentum diffusion into the recirculation, which shrinks the separation. This matches [39][40], who find that RANS models underestimate the eddy viscosity in separated shear layers far from the wall (§3.2, §4.2). **Stated** (mechanistic reading)
10. SR-CLS and SR-CND, evaluated at every solver iteration, increase the total runtime for the same number of iterations by about 15% and 10%. A random forest called every iteration made convergence about 30 times slower in Yin et al. [8] (§3.2). **Shown** (own timing, no hardware given) / **Stated** (the cited figure)
11. On the training cases both SR models improve the reattachment on the curved step, where SST reattaches at $x=6.0$ (Fig. 15), and $C_f$ in the reattachment region (Fig. 17) (§3.3.1). **Shown**
12. On the NASA hump, the mean square error of $C_f$ against experiment [55] in the separation zone is about 6.7% of SST's for both SR models. At the hump top SR-CND matches SST, while SR-CLS has 160% of SST's error because it raises $\beta$ there (Fig. 21b, Fig. 22) (§3.3.2). **Shown**
13. Periodic hills of Xiao et al. [56] at $Re=5600$, slope parameters 0.5, 0.8 and 1.0, three grids: the integrated friction differs by less than $1\times10^{-4}$ between grids and by about $5\times10^{-5}$ between the medium and fine grids. For the slope 0.8, SST reattaches on the next hill, while both SR models reattach at about $x=6.0$, against $x\approx5.2$ in the DNS. The SR-CND correction is concentrated in the shear layer, while the SR-CLS one is more diffuse and active near the attached upper wall (§4.1, Figs. 24–27). **Shown**
14. NLR-7301 two-element airfoil ($Re=2.51\times10^6$, $M=0.185$, run in CFL3D): $C_{L,\max}$ is 3.03 in the experiment, 2.84 with SST (6.3% error), 2.94 with SR-CLS (3%) and 3.00 with SR-CND (1%). The stall angle is 14.1° in the experiment, 11.1° with SST and 13.1° with both SR models (Table 5). The models run stably in a compressible code other than the training code (§4.2). **Shown**
15. At 13.1° on the NLR-7301, SST predicts separation from the rear of the main element and neither SR model does (Figs. 29c, 30). $\beta$ rises in two mixing regions, the main-element shear layer meeting the wake and the flap wake meeting the slot jet, where $|\lambda_2|$ and $|\lambda_5|$ are large (§4.2, Figs. 31–32). **Shown**
16. SAE notchback ($Re_L=2.3\times10^6$, 40 m/s, unstructured meshes of about $1\times10^7$, $1.8\times10^7$ and $3.3\times10^7$ cells): $C_D$ is 0.181 with SST (12.6% error), 0.211 with SR-CND (1.9%), 0.209 with SR-CLS (1.0%) and 0.207 in the experiment (Table 6). The rear separation ends at about $x=0.60$ m for both SR models and the PIV, and at about 0.63 m for SST (§4.3, Fig. 35). **Shown**
17. At the notchback end SR-CLS raises $\beta$ near the wall, suppresses the thin separation more strongly and distorts the upper-surface $C_p$ away from SST and experiment. SR-CND keeps $\beta=1$ near the wall and gives smaller $C_p$ errors (§4.3, Figs. 38–40). **Shown**
18. 25° Ahmed body ($Re_L=2.78\times10^6$, 40 m/s, structured meshes of about $3.6\times10^6$, $7.2\times10^6$ and $1.4\times10^7$ cells): the SR models' $C_D$ changes by about 0.005 from the coarse to the medium mesh and by 0.001–0.002 from the medium to the fine one, while SST's $C_D$ fluctuates with refinement. The velocity-profile error against PIV [64] is 0.677 for SST, 0.072 for SR-CND (10.6%) and 0.069 for SR-CLS (10.2%) (Table 7). Both SR models keep the flow attached along the slant, as in the experiment, while SST separates from the start of the slant (§4.4, Fig. 45). **Shown**
19. Zero-pressure-gradient flat plate ($Re_L=1.0\times10^7$, about $2\times10^5$ cells, $\Delta y^+\le0.05$): SST and SR-CND match the Wieghardt–Tillmann $C_f$ and the viscous and log layers. SR-CLS overestimates $C_f$, gives a wrong log layer and a thicker boundary layer. In the boundary layer SR-CLS gives $\beta\approx1.06$ and SR-CND $\beta=1$ (§4.5, Figs. 47–48). **Shown**
20. NACA 0012 at zero incidence ($Re_c=6\times10^6$, $M\approx0.15$, C-grid with 640 surface, 120 wake and 136 wall-normal cells, $\Delta y^+\approx0.6$): SR-CND gives nearly the same $C_f$ as SST, and SR-CLS overpredicts it (§4.5, Fig. 50). **Shown**
21. Conclusion: conditioned field inversion gives models with L3 generalizability similar to classic inversion while keeping the baseline's attached-flow accuracy (L2), a dual capability the authors say is hard to reach with classic inversion (§5). **Shown** (over the test set above)

# Printed slips

- **§3.2:** "the local flow features described in Table 3". The features are in Table 2, and Table 3 lists the element functions.
- **Fig. 44 caption (§4.4):** "given by the SST-CLS model", twice, for SR-CLS.
- **Table 7:** the caption reads "The MSEs of the velocity profiles" and the text "reduce the MSE by approximately 90%", but the row label reads "Velocity profile's RMSE".
- **§4.5:** "We use the C-type grid shown in Figure 45" for the NACA 0012. Fig. 45 shows the Ahmed-body streamlines, and the airfoil grid is Fig. 49.
- **§1:** "Andrea et al. [27]" uses the given name of the first author, Ferrero. "Joel Ho et al. [32]": the printed first author of [32] is Pepper.
- **Eqs. (1), (2), (5):** the eddy viscosity appears as $\mu_t$, $\mu_T$ and $\nu_t$, and only $\mu_t$ is defined.
- **Eq. (21):** $C_F=\frac{1}{\frac12\rho U_b^2L}\int C_f\,dx$ divides the dimensionless $C_f$ by a dynamic pressure *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Symbol reuse:** $\gamma$ is the SST production coefficient (Eq. 1) and the trivial-to-nontrivial sample ratio (Eq. 19). $N$ is the number of cells (Eq. 7) and the nontrivial-sample count (Eq. 19). $\alpha$ is both the sample weight and the hill parameter.
- **References:** ref. [59] title "Boudary layer measurements"; ref. [61] (Driver & Seegmiller 1985) is never cited in the text.
