# Summary

Wu et al. (2023) assess Self-Adaptive Turbulence Eddy Simulation (SATES),
the unified URANS–LES–DNS approach of Han and coworkers that earlier papers
called very-large eddy simulation (VLES), on the
[30P30N three-element high-lift airfoil](/benchmarks/30p30n-high-lift-airfoil.md)
(modified BANC-III geometry) at $Re_c=1.71\times10^6$, $M=0.17$,
$\alpha=5.5^\circ$. The name changes because the method resolves more than
90% of the turbulent kinetic energy, against Pope's definition of VLES
(less than 80% resolved). SATES keeps Menter's BSL
[$k$-$\omega$](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) transport equations and
multiplies the eddy viscosity by a resolution control function $F_r$
built from an integral, a cutoff and a Kolmogorov length; its cutoff
length is tied to the [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md)
constant. The paper places SATES among the
[hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) families
([DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md),
[PANS](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md), VLES), but stresses that
it does not treat the whole attached boundary layer in RANS mode.

Convection is discretized in the CARDC multi-block structured solver
PHengLEI by a fourth-order
[weighted compact nonlinear scheme](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md)
(WCNS) with Roe flux and fifth-order weighted interpolation, the weights
of WENO-Z type ([WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md)) with a regularization
parameter $10^{-6}$ (WCNS-Z) or 100 (WCNS-L, nearly linear); the
second-order alternative is a blended central/upwind scheme with 2%
upwinding. Three groups of runs vary the grid (7.2M, 19M, 27M cells), the
scheme and the turbulence treatment
([Smagorinsky LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) with
$C_s=0.1$, SST-IDDES, SATES). SATES with WCNS-L gives $C_L$ within 3% of
the experimental 2.83 on all grids; the blended scheme ($-11.0\%$),
WCNS-Z ($-6.7\%$), LES ($-6.7\%$) and IDDES ($-19.8\%$) fall short.
The [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) of the
nonlinear weights delays the instability of the slat-cove shear layer, and
linear interpolation is recommended for shock-free low-Mach flow. IDDES
separates early on the flap (attributed to grid-induced separation) and
carries excess eddy viscosity in the slat cove; SATES makes a faster
RANS-to-LES transition and resolves more turbulent kinetic energy than
IDDES. Evidence is numerical comparison with JAXA and FSU experiments.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols (registry §11 for the SATES and WCNS ones): $F_r$ the SATES resolution control function (modelled
fraction of the turbulent kinetic energy); $L_i$, $L_c$, $L_k$ the
integral, cutoff and Kolmogorov lengths of SATES; $C_x$ the cutoff-length
coefficient; $\beta_F$, $n_F$ the constants of $F_r$ (the source's
$\beta^*=0.002$ and $n$; the registry's $\beta^*=0.09$ is the source's
$\beta_1^*$); $\alpha_{\mathrm W}$, $b_{\mathrm W}$ the coefficients of the
WCNS flux difference; $\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}$ the regularization parameter of
the Z-type weights; SSL the slat-cove
separated shear layer; GIS grid-induced separation; MSD modelled-stress
depletion.

1. SATES (previously VLES, Han et al.) is renamed because its computations resolve "larger than 90%" of the turbulent kinetic energy even on coarse grids, while Pope defines VLES by "the filter and grid are too coarse to resolve 80% of the energy"; its performance "is very similar to that of the traditional LES method defined by Pope" (§2.1.1). **Stated**
2. SATES differs from DES-type methods, "which typically treat the entire attached boundary layer with a RANS approach", and bridges URANS, LES and DNS according to the numerical resolution and the local turbulence scales (§1). **Stated**
3. SATES uses the BSL $k$-$\omega$ transport equations unchanged and modifies only the eddy viscosity, $\mu_t=F_r\rho k/\omega$ (Eq. 4), with $F_r$ of Eq. (5) (§2.1.1; the model is taken from Han et al.). **Stated**
4. $F_r$ is the fraction of modelled in total turbulent kinetic energy: $L_c\gtrsim L_i$ gives $F_r\to1$ (URANS mode), $L_k<L_c<L_i$ gives $0<F_r<1$ (LES mode), $L_c\to0$ gives $F_r\to0$ (DNS mode) (§2.1.1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
5. The cutoff coefficient is tied to the Smagorinsky constant, $C_x=\sqrt{0.3}\,C_s/\beta^*$ (Eq. 7), with $C_s=0.1$, $\beta^*=0.09$, and Table 1 prints $C_x=0.61$ *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\sqrt{0.3}\times0.1/0.09=0.609$)* (§2.1.1). **Stated**
6. Convection uses a fourth-order WCNS (explicit flux difference with $\alpha_{\mathrm W}=9/8$, $b_{\mathrm W}=-1/24$, Roe flux, fifth-order weighted interpolation from three third-order substencils); viscous terms use fourth-order central differences (Eqs. 12–14, §2.2.1) *(compiler check, not in the sources: $\alpha_{\mathrm W}+3b_{\mathrm W}=1$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm W}+27b_{\mathrm W}=0$)*. **Stated**
7. The Z-type weights are used with $\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}=100$ (WCNS-L), "to neglect the nonlinear weight and approach the linear scheme", and with $\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}=10^{-6}$ (WCNS-Z) (§2.2.1). **Stated**
8. In the blended scheme (BCD), $\sigma_b=1$ is second-order fully upwind and $\sigma_b=0$ central; $\sigma_b=0.02$ is used "to reduce the numerical dissipation and maintain the numerical stability" (Eq. 15, §2.2.2). **Stated**
9. Grid study (SATES, WCNS-L): $C_L=2.85$, 2.75, 2.82 and $C_D=0.054$, 0.064, 0.070 on the coarse (7.2M cells, 60 spanwise cells, $\Delta t\,U_0/C=10^{-4}$), medium (19M, 80, $5\times10^{-5}$) and fine (27M, 80, $5\times10^{-5}$) grids; errors against 2.83 of 0.7%, $-2.8\%$, $-0.4\%$ (Table 2). **Shown**
10. Mean $C_p$ shows "low sensitivity" to the grid and agrees with JAXA and FSU data even on the 7.2M-cell grid; the peak rms $C_p$ on the slat decreases with refinement (Figs. 6–7, §3.2). **Shown**
11. Slat-cove turbulent-kinetic-energy profiles: the coarse grid gives a very low peak at line L1 and an overestimate at L7, read as a delayed shear-layer instability; refinement moves the transition upstream (Fig. 8, §3.2). **Shown**
12. Even on the coarse grid $k_{\mathrm{res}}>k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ at most points of the mid-span plane, with $k_{\mathrm{res}}=\tfrac12(u'^2+v'^2+w'^2)$ and $k_{\mathrm{sgs}}$ the modelled $k$ (Eq. 16); the resolved share grows with refinement, and the modelled energy stays significant near walls where $F_r\approx1$ (Figs. 9–10, §3.2). No integrated resolved fraction is reported. **Shown**
13. Schemes (fine grid, SATES): BCD $C_L=2.52$ ($-11.0\%$), WCNS-Z 2.64 ($-6.7\%$), WCNS-L 2.82 ($-0.4\%$); $C_D=0.072$, 0.074, 0.070 (Table 2). **Shown**
14. The lower dissipation of WCNS gives a faster instability of the slat-cove shear layer than BCD, which gives a "smoother SSL" (Fig. 11, §3.3). **Shown**
15. The Z-type weight attains the optimal order at first-order critical points but "still suffered a loss in accuracy near high-order critical points", adding dissipation in the shear layer; pure linear interpolation is "recommended when the WCNS scheme was used for simulations of low Mach flows without shock" (§3.3). **Stated** (mechanism) / **Shown** (effect on $C_L$, $C_p$)
16. Mean and rms $C_p$ agree best with experiment for WCNS-L; all schemes overestimate the peak spanwise vorticity at L1, and WCNS-L follows the shear-layer development best after L2 (Figs. 12–15, §3.3). **Shown**
17. Turbulent kinetic energy outside the cove shear layer disagrees with the PIV data for all schemes; the authors call the experimental values "unreasonably high", attribute this to poor PIV resolution from particle shortage (citing the WMLES study of Zhang et al.), and note that other BANC-III results show the same mismatch (Fig. 16, §3.3). **Stated**
18. Turbulence treatments (fine grid, WCNS-L): LES $C_L=2.64$ ($-6.7\%$), IDDES 2.27 ($-19.8\%$), SATES 2.82 ($-0.4\%$); $C_D=0.070$, 0.076, 0.070. The cited WMLES gives $C_L=2.848$, $C_D=0.067$; JAXA 2.82 and FSU 2.84 (Table 2). **Shown**
19. IDDES delays the shear-layer transition; LES and SATES transition faster with more small-scale structures; SATES predicts the reattachment point inside the slat cove best against SPIV, LES and IDDES give "more forward positions" (Fig. 17, §3.4). **Shown**
20. IDDES separates early on the flap suction side, "which may have been caused by the GIS", and this produces its large flap $C_p$ deviation; LES and SATES show only small separations near the flap trailing edge (Figs. 18, 23, §3.4). **Shown** (separation) / **Stated** (attribution)
21. "Although the turbulent boundary layer was partially protected to avoid modeled stress depletion (MSD) with the consideration of insufficient grid resolution, the hybrid RANS/LES (denoted IDDES) could be worse than the LES" (§3.4). **Shown** (on this case)
22. IDDES misses the small separation bubble on the main element, and LES predicts a larger one than SATES (Fig. 23, §3.4). **Shown**
23. IDDES gives larger $\mu_t/\mu$ in the attached boundary layers and considerable $\mu_t$ in the slat cove, linked to its transition delay; the SATES $\mu_t$ distribution resembles that of LES, and SATES has "a faster 'transition' from RANS to LES than IDDES" (Fig. 24, §3.4). **Shown**
24. The resolved share of turbulent kinetic energy is highest for LES; both hybrid treatments resolve less because of their RANS mode, SATES "significantly more turbulence than did the IDDES" (Fig. 25, §3.4). **Shown**
25. Terracol et al. used "2.6 billion grid cells and 6 million CPU hours" for wall-resolved LES of a three-element airfoil at $Re_c=1.23\times10^6$ (§1). **Stated**
26. DES, DDES and IDDES "suffer from grid-induced separation (GIS), a slow 'transition' from RANS to LES, and other limitations"; enhanced DES versions improve on these (§1). **Stated**
27. SATES is more accurate than Smagorinsky LES and IDDES and gives "more accurate wall-modeled behaviors" than LES; the Z-type weight "would lead to considerable numerical dissipation in supersonic turbulent flows", left for future study (§4). **Stated**

The model in registry notation (Eqs. 4–7):
$\mu_t=F_r\rho k/\omega$,
$F_r=\min\{1,[(1-e^{-\beta_FL_c/L_k})/(1-e^{-\beta_FL_i/L_k})]^{n_F}\}$,
$L_i=k^{3/2}/(\beta^*k\omega)$, $L_c=C_x(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$,
$L_k=\nu^{3/4}/(\beta^*k\omega)^{1/4}$, with $\varepsilon=\beta^*k\omega$
and Table 1 constants $\beta^*=0.09$, $\beta_F=0.002$, $n_F=2$,
$C_x=0.61$, $\sigma_{k1}=0.5$, $\sigma_{k2}=1.0$, $\sigma_{\omega1}=0.5$,
$\sigma_{\omega2}=0.856$, $\beta_1=0.075$, $\beta_2=0.0828$,
$\gamma_1=0.553$, $\gamma_2=0.44$. The LES comparator is
$\mu_t=\rho(C_s\Delta)^2|\bar S|$ with $\Delta=(\Delta_x\Delta_y\Delta_z)^{1/3}$
and no wall damping mentioned (Eq. 8); the IDDES length is
$l_{\mathrm{IDDES}}=\hat f_d(1+f_e)l_{\mathrm{RANS}}+(1-\hat f_d)l_{\mathrm{LES}}$
(Eq. 9, Gritskevich et al.). The WCNS flux difference is
$f'_j=\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm W}}{h}(f^*_{j+1/2}-f^*_{j-1/2})+\frac{b_{\mathrm W}}{h}(f^*_{j+3/2}-f^*_{j-3/2})$
(Eq. 12) with Z-type weights
$a_k=d_k(1+|\mathrm{IS}_0-\mathrm{IS}_2|/(\mathrm{IS}_k+\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}))$,
$\mathsf w_k=a_k/\sum_la_l$ (Eq. 14); $d_k$ and $\mathrm{IS}_k$ are not
printed. Time integration is second-order backward differentiation with
LU-SGS and Newton-like sub-iterations; runs restart from RANS, run 10
convective units $C/U_0$ and average over 10 more.
