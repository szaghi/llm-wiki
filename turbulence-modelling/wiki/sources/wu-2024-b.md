# Summary

Wu et al. (2024b) work on the nonlinear interpolation of the fifth-order
[weighted compact nonlinear scheme](/methods/weighted-compact-nonlinear-scheme.md)
(WCNS) used with
[self-adaptive turbulence eddy simulation](/methods/self-adaptive-turbulence-eddy-simulation.md)
(SATES), the [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) closure of
Han and coworkers built on the BSL $k$-$\omega$ model
([$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)). They first form WCNS-T,
a WCNS whose node-to-midpoint interpolation uses the TENO weighting of Fu et
al. (2016), as Hiejima (2022) did: a sharp 0/1 cut-off of normalized
smoothness measures ([WENO and TENO schemes](/methods/weno-scheme.md)).
Their argument is that marginally resolved turbulence holds no true
discontinuity, so the discontinuous weights of the sharp cut-off harm the
Newton-like sub-iterations of the implicit dual-time integration. The
contribution, WCNS-ST, makes the cut-off smooth,
$\delta^{\mathrm T}_k=\tanh((\chi^{\mathrm T}_k/C_T)^2)$, set to 1 above
$10C_T$; WCNS-STA adds the adaptive $C_T$ of TENO-A type, driven by a
discontinuity indicator of Ren et al. (2003)
([shock sensors](/definitions/shock-sensor.md)).

An approximate dispersion-relation analysis
([eigensolution analysis](/methods/eigensolution-analysis.md)) shows WCNS-ST
close to WCNS-T and WCNS-STA close to the linear scheme; the
two-dimensional Riemann problem of Lax & Liu shows TENO-type weights
resolving more small scales than Jiang–Shu and Z weights. In SATES runs in
[PHengLEI](/software/phenglei.md) of the
[30P30N airfoil](/benchmarks/30p30n-high-lift-airfoil.md) (BANC-III,
$Re=1.71\times10^6$, $M=0.17$, 5.5°, 27 M cells) and of the Herrin–Dutton
supersonic base flow ($M_\infty=2.46$, 7 M and 42 M cells), WCNS-JS is
over-dissipative ([numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)):
it suppresses the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability of the shear layers and
misplaces the flap pressure. WCNS-T converges poorly in the sub-iterations;
in the base flow its residual falls by one order of magnitude, against
three for WCNS-ST and two for WCNS-STA, and WCNS-ST predicts the slat-cove
vorticity and the base-flow kinetic-energy peaks better than WCNS-T. WCNS-ST
costs about 50% more CPU time than WCNS-JS. Evidence is spectral analysis,
an inviscid test and numerical comparison with experiments.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\gamma^{\mathrm T}_k$, $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k$,
$\delta^{\mathrm T}_k$, $C_T$, $d_k$ the TENO smoothness measure, its
normalized form, the cut-off function, threshold and optimal linear weights,
with $\mathrm{IS}_k$, $a_k$, $\mathsf w_k$ the WENO indicators and weights
(registry §11); $\tau_{\mathrm Z}=\lvert\mathrm{IS}_0-\mathrm{IS}_2\rvert$; $m$,
$\eta_i$, $C_r$, $\xi_{\mathrm R}$, $g(m)$, $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$ the
adaptive-threshold indicator and its constants; $n_{\mathrm T}$ the exponent
of the smooth cut-off; $F_r$, $L_i$, $L_c$, $L_k$, $C_x$, $\beta_F$, $n_F$ the
SATES quantities (registry §11).

1. The flux-difference step of WCNS does not affect its resolution; the nonlinear weighted interpolation dominates the resolution property (§I, citing Nonomura & Fujii 2009, ref. 32). **Stated**
2. The Z-type nonlinear weight is over-dissipative in multiscale turbulence simulations of a high-lift airfoil, with significant discrepancies from the linear scheme (§I, citing the authors' earlier work, ref. 14). **Stated**
3. The sharp TENO cut-off (Eq. 17, $C_T=1\times10^{-5}$) makes the nonlinear weights discontinuous between resolved and non-resolved scales; marginally resolved high-wavenumber turbulence has no true discontinuity, and this weight discontinuity "would lead to numerical instability or bad convergence in the SATES simulation" (§I, §II.D). **Stated** (the paper's motivating argument)
4. Smooth-TENO scale separation: $\delta^{\mathrm T}_k=\tanh((\chi^{\mathrm T}_k/C_T)^{n_{\mathrm T}})$, $n_{\mathrm T}=2$, rising smoothly from 0 to 1 around $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k=C_T$; set to 1 for $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k\ge10C_T$ to save the tanh evaluation (Eqs. 18–19, Fig. 2). **Derived** (contribution)
5. The WCNS-ST weights $\mathsf w_k=d_k\delta^{\mathrm T}_k/\sum_ld_l\delta^{\mathrm T}_l$ (Eq. 23) keep the interpolated variable continuous near discontinuities, whereas TCNS (Zhang et al. 2019), which switches between ENO-like stencil selections, "struggles to maintain the continuity of the interpolation variable near discontinuities" (§II.D). **Stated**
6. The adaptive threshold $C_T=10^{-\lfloor\beta_{\mathrm A}\rfloor}$ with $\beta_{\mathrm A}=\alpha_1-\alpha_2(1-g(m))$, $g(m)=(1-m)^4(1+4m)$, $\alpha_1=10.0$, $\alpha_2=5.0$; $m\approx1$ gives $C_T=10^{\alpha_2-\alpha_1}$ (strong nonlinear adaptation, shock capturing), $m\approx0$ gives $C_T=10^{-\alpha_1}$ (resolving high-wavenumber fluctuations) (Eqs. 20–22). This is WCNS-STA. **Stated** (background of Fu et al. 2018 and Ren et al. 2003, applied to the smooth cut-off)
7. In the approximate dispersion-relation analysis, WCNS-T keeps linear-scheme properties over a wide wavenumber range; WCNS-ST "approaches" WCNS-T (the smooth separation has "slight influences"); WCNS-STA approaches the linear scheme (§IV.A, Fig. 4). **Shown**
8. Two-dimensional Riemann problem (configuration 3 of Lax & Liu, 401×401, $t=0.8$): WCNS-ST resolves "significantly more small-scale structures" than WCNS-JS and WCNS-Z while capturing shocks sharply and stably; its resolution is consistent with WCNS-T; WCNS-STA resolves more small-scale structures than WCNS-ST (§IV.B, Fig. 5). **Shown**
9. SATES reaches LES mode in the fine-resolution limit: Taylor expansion of $F_r$ gives $F_r\to(L_c/L_i)^{n_F}$ (Eq. 43), "the form in the filtered URANS computation as shown in Eq. (33)", so "the SATES guarantees correct LES mode whereas the VLES fails" (§III.B). **Derived**
10. Speziale's VLES (Eq. 40) over-damps the Reynolds stress, can hardly recover URANS unless the mesh is unreasonably coarse ($\Delta/L_k\to\infty$), and tends to RANS behaviour as $Re\to\infty$ ($L_k\to0$) (§III.B). **Stated**
11. Self-adaptivity: damping the Reynolds stress lowers the integral length scale, which raises $F_r$ and counteracts the damping, so SATES "tends to the adaptive equilibrium state" (§III.B). **Stated**
12. 30P30N: WCNS-JS is over-dissipative and suppresses the separated-shear-layer instability in the slat cove; WCNS-Z is also dissipative near high-order critical points; WCNS-T and WCNS-ST predict more small-scale structures and promote the initial Kelvin–Helmholtz instability (§IV.C.1, Fig. 7). **Shown**
13. 30P30N time-averaged $C_p$: WCNS-JS deviates significantly, mostly on the flap, attributed to suppressed suction-side turbulence and stronger separation under adverse pressure gradient; WCNS-Z is better; WCNS-T, -ST and -STA agree with the experiment and with the pure fifth-order linear WCNS (Fig. 8). **Shown**
14. 30P30N residuals: WCNS-T converges worse in the sub-iterations, WCNS-ST better, WCNS-STA better than WCNS-T (Fig. 9); the authors read Fig. 9(d) as a WCNS-T sub-iteration residual stalling while that of WCNS-ST keeps decreasing. **Shown**
15. 30P30N slat-cove time-averaged spanwise vorticity on two lines: WCNS-ST agrees better than WCNS-T with the experiment; better sub-iteration convergence "improves the precision of Newton-like sub-iteration" (Fig. 10). **Shown**
16. Base-flow sub-iterations: the WCNS-T residual decreases by one order of magnitude, WCNS-ST by three orders, WCNS-STA by two orders (§IV.C.2, Fig. 13). **Shown**
17. Base flow: the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability of the shear layer is suppressed by the dissipation of WCNS-JS; TENO-based weights give more foot shocks under recompression and stronger annular and axial shear-layer instability (Fig. 12). **Shown**
18. Base-flow resolved turbulent kinetic energy at $x/R=1.8898$ and 2.5197: WCNS-JS on the 42 M fine grid predicts the peak accurately; on the 7 M coarse grid WCNS-JS and WCNS-Z are poor, WCNS-T and WCNS-ST better, WCNS-STA best; WCNS-ST predicts the peak better than WCNS-T, "largely attributed to better residual convergence in Newton-like sub-iteration" (Fig. 14). **Shown**
19. Cost (Table I, CPU hours normalized by WCNS-JS): 30P30N — linear 0.97, JS 1.0, Z 1.03, T 1.51, ST 1.52, STA 1.68; base flow — linear not given, JS 1.0, Z 1.02, T 1.58, ST 1.55, STA 1.54. WCNS-ST costs "approximately 50%" more than WCNS-JS, WCNS-STA "a slightly further increase" over WCNS-ST (Appendix). **Shown**
20. Conclusion: "Smooth scale separation is recommended to be used in TENO-based schemes, especially in simulating multiscale flows" (§V). **Stated** (recommendation from claims 14–18)
21. WCNS advantages over finite-difference WENO: slightly higher resolution, freedom of numerical flux (Roe, Steger–Warming, Van Leer), good freestream and vortex preservation on curvilinear grids; applicability to complex grids when the geometric conservation law is satisfied ([geometric conservation law](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md)) (§I, refs. 26, 27, 40). **Stated**

The scheme in registry notation (§II.B–§II.D, Eqs. 10–23). The midpoint
flux difference is sixth order,
$f'_j=\frac{75}{64h}(\hat f_{j+1/2}-\hat f_{j-1/2})-\frac{25}{384h}(\hat f_{j+3/2}-\hat f_{j-3/2})+\frac{3}{640h}(\hat f_{j+5/2}-\hat f_{j-5/2})$,
with the Roe flux $\hat f_{j\pm1/2}=f^*(U^L,U^R)$ and characteristic-variable
interpolation $\hat U_{j+1/2}=\sum_{k=0}^2\mathsf w_k\hat U^k_{j+1/2}$ from the
third-order candidates $(3U_{j-2}-10U_{j-1}+15U_j)/8$,
$(-U_{j-1}+6U_j+3U_{j+1})/8$, $(3U_j+6U_{j+1}-U_{j+2})/8$ with
$(d_0,d_1,d_2)=(1/16,10/16,5/16)$ *(compiler check, not in the sources: the
flux-difference coefficients give $75/64-75/384+15/640=1$)*. The weights are
$a_k=d_k/(\mathrm{IS}_k+\epsilon_{\mathrm Z})^2$ (JS), $a_k=d_k(1+\tau_{\mathrm Z}/(\mathrm{IS}_k+\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}))$
(Z, exponent 1 "to reduce the numerical dissipation"), and for TENO
$\gamma^{\mathrm T}_k=(1+\tau_{\mathrm Z}/(\mathrm{IS}_k+\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}))^6$,
$\chi^{\mathrm T}_k=\gamma^{\mathrm T}_k/\sum_l\gamma^{\mathrm T}_l$, sharp
$\delta^{\mathrm T}_k=0$ if $\chi^{\mathrm T}_k<C_T$ and 1 otherwise, with
$\epsilon_{\mathrm Z}=10^{-40}$ throughout. The indicator of WCNS-STA is
$m=1-\min(1,\eta_{i+1/2}/C_r)$,
$\eta_i=(\lvert2\Delta f_{i+1/2}\Delta f_{i-1/2}\rvert+\epsilon_{\mathrm R})/((\Delta f_{i+1/2})^2+(\Delta f_{i-1/2})^2+\epsilon_{\mathrm R})$,
$\epsilon_{\mathrm R}=0.9C_r\xi_{\mathrm R}^2/(1-0.9C_r)$, $\xi_{\mathrm R}=10^{-3}$,
$C_r=0.24$, $\eta_{i+1/2}=\min(\eta_{i-1},\eta_i,\eta_{i+1})$. SATES is
restated as in [wu-2024](/sources/wu-2024.md): $\mu_t=F_r\rho k/\omega$,
$F_r=\min\{1,[(1-e^{-\beta_FL_c/L_k})/(1-e^{-\beta_FL_i/L_k})]^{n_F}\}$,
$L_c=C_x\Delta$, $L_i=k^{3/2}/(\beta^*k\omega)$,
$L_k=\nu^{3/4}/(\beta^*k\omega)^{1/4}$, $\beta_F=0.002$, $\beta^*=0.09$,
$n_F=2$, $C_x=\sqrt{0.3}\,C_s/C_\mu=0.61$ with $C_s=0.1$, the last derived
from a Kolmogorov-spectrum filtering argument that reduces the filtered
limit to the [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) (Eqs.
28–45). The introduction cites the limitations of
[DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md) and
[PANS](/methods/partially-averaged-navier-stokes.md) as background. Time
integration is second-order backward differentiation with LU-SGS and
Newton-like sub-iterations.

# Printed slips

- **§II.B:** "the three sub-stencils, $K_1$, $K_2$, $K_3$, are used", while Eq. (12) and Fig. 1 label them $K_0$, $K_1$, $K_2$.
- **Eq. (6) text:** "$S_{ij}=(u_i/x_j+u_j/x_i)/2$", without the partial-derivative symbols.
- **After Eq. (22):** "When $m\approx0$, $g(m)\approx1$, and $C_T$ increases to $10^{-\alpha_1}$". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: with $\alpha_1=10$, $\alpha_2=5$, $C_T$ goes from $10^{-5}$ at $m\approx1$ to $10^{-10}$ at $m\approx0$, a decrease.)* The text names the Gauss bracket of "$\bar\beta$", Eq. (22) writes $\beta$ without a bar.
- **Eq. (36):** "$\langle uv\rangle\approx0.3k|S|=\varepsilon$". *(Compiler check, not in the sources: $\langle uv\rangle$ has the dimensions of a velocity squared, $0.3k\lvert S\rvert$ of a velocity squared per unit time.)*
- **Eq. (43) against Eq. (33):** the fine-resolution limit is printed $F_r\to(L_c/L_i)^n$ with $n=2$, and said to approach "the form in the filtered URANS computation as shown in Eq. (33)", where $F_{r,\Delta}=L_c/L_i$ to the first power.
- **Eqs. (32) and (42):** $L_c=\frac14\gamma\Delta$, $L_i=\mu_{RANS}/(\rho\sqrt{2k/3})$ in Eq. (32), and $L_c=C_x\Delta$, $L_i=k^{3/2}/(\beta_1^*k\omega)$ in Eq. (42).
- **§III.A:** "anisotropy factor $\gamma$ is used to compute corrected RMS velocity $u'_\Delta=\gamma u'_\Delta$", the same symbol on both sides.
- **Table I against the Appendix:** for the base flow WCNS-STA (1.54) costs less than WCNS-ST (1.55), while the text says WCNS-STA has "a slightly further increase in CPU hours compared with WCNS-ST".
- **§IV.C.1:** small scales in the slat cove "as shown in Fig. 6"; Fig. 6 is the airfoil geometry, Fig. 7 the structures.
- **References:** refs. 12, 13 and 36 are printed in "Numer. Methods Fluids", ref. 5 in "Prog. Aeronaut. Sci." (kept as printed).
