# Summary

Wang et al. (2024) ask whether the dispersion or the dissipation of the
convective discretization matters more when a scale-resolving simulation
must predict the turbulent-kinetic-energy budget, a higher-order statistic
than the mean velocity and the Reynolds stresses. Two schemes are compared
in a cell-centred finite-difference solver: KGP2, the second-order
Kennedy–Gruber–Pirozzoli split form of the convective term, which is
[kinetic-energy preserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) and
non-dissipative but has poor dispersion; and OPT6, the linear sixth-order
optimized upwind reconstruction of the OMP6 scheme with a Roe flux, a
[low-dissipation low-dispersion scheme](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md)
with some [numerical dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) at
high wavenumbers. An approximate-dispersion-relation analysis gives the
points per wavelength each scheme needs.

The inviscid [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) with
time reversal confirms that KGP2 conserves kinetic energy exactly and
recovers the initial field, while OPT6, MUSCL3 and a fifth-order
[WENO scheme](/methods/weno-scheme.md) decay monotonically. In
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) with the
[WALE model](/methods/wale-model.md) of [channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
at $Re_\tau=180$ both schemes give the same mean velocity, but OPT6 is closer
to DNS in the Reynolds-shear-stress peak and the budget, especially the
dissipation term. On the [periodic hill](/benchmarks/periodic-hill.md)
($Re_h=10595$, four grids) first-order statistics are grid-insensitive,
second-order statistics and budget terms are not; OPT6 agrees with the
references even on $48^3$, while KGP2 converges with refinement. The
authors recommend high-order upwind schemes for budget analysis, use the
budget imbalance as an indicator of under-resolution, and apply OPT6 to
[delayed detached-eddy simulation](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)
based on the [Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md)
of the 30P30N three-element high-lift airfoil, validated on $C_p$,
slat-cove profiles and slat-trailing-edge pressure spectra, and classify
the spatial patterns of the budget terms across the shear layers. The
paper runs explicit (WALE) LES, not [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)
(§3.2–3.3). The two schemes differ both in formal order (second against
sixth, §2.3–2.4) and in dissipation, and the paper tests no high-order
central scheme (§3).

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: KGP2 and OPT6 the two schemes; $a$, $b$ the free
parameters of OPT6; $k_{\max}h$ the resolved modified wavenumber, PPW the
points per wavelength; $x_{\mathrm{sep}}/H$ the separation point of the
periodic hill.

1. Central non-dissipative schemes preserve high-wavenumber content but, at or near zero viscosity, accumulate aliasing errors from the nonlinear terms on a discrete grid, which threatens stability (§1, citing [13], [14]). **Stated**
2. Earlier scheme comparisons (Mittal & Moin [33]; Park et al. [34]; Mohan Rai & Moin [35]; Timin & Esmail [32]) addressed mean velocity, Reynolds stress and spectra, not the turbulent-kinetic-energy budget, which this paper targets (§1). **Stated**
3. The KGP form arises from the general linear combination of five analytically equivalent convective forms (Eq. 11), printed "When $\xi=1/2$, $\alpha=\beta=\delta=1/4$, and $\varepsilon=0$", and has no numerical dissipation (§2.3, citing [17, 18, 36]). **Stated**
4. OPT6 (Eq. 12) is the linear part of OMP6 [30], with two free parameters controlling dissipation and dispersion, set to $a=0$, $b=0.001$ as recommended by Li et al. [30]; the interface states are combined by a Roe flux (Eq. 13) (§2.4). **Stated**
5. Approximate-dispersion-relation analysis (Fig. 1, Table 1): KGP2 dispersion-limited $k_{\max}h=0.48$, wavelength $13.09h$, 14 PPW ("poor"), dissipation $k_{\max}h=\pi$, $2h$, 2 PPW ("perfect"); OPT6 dispersion $1.65$, $3.81h$, 4 PPW ("good"), dissipation $1.15$, $5.46h$, 6 PPW ("good") (§2.5). **Shown**
6. Inviscid Taylor–Green vortex ($30^3$, flow reversed at $t=20L/V_0$, end at $40L/V_0$): with KGP2 the final vorticity iso-surface is identical to the initial one and the kinetic energy stays constant, "verifying that the KGP2 scheme has the property of non-dissipative energy conservation"; OPT6, MUSCL3 and WENO5 decay monotonically and do not recover the initial field, MUSCL3 differing most (§3.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
7. Channel flow at $Re_\tau=180$, WALE LES: the mean velocities of OPT6 and KGP2 are "not significantly different" (§3.2, Fig. 3a). **Shown**
8. Channel: around the Reynolds-shear-stress peak OPT6 is more consistent with DNS than KGP2 (§3.2, Fig. 3c). **Shown**
9. Channel: the budget terms of OPT6 agree better with DNS (Moser et al. [44]) than those of KGP2, "especially for the dissipation term" (§3.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
10. Periodic-hill separation point $x_{\mathrm{sep}}/H$: KGP2 0.26 ($48^3$), 0.24 ($64^3$), 0.20 ($96^3$), 0.20 ($196\times128\times186$); OPT6 0.20 ($196\times128\times186$); references 0.20 (Krank et al. DNS), 0.19 (Breuer et al. LES), 0.22 (Fröhlich et al. LES) (Table 2). **Shown**
11. "The higher the order of the statistics, the more challenging it becomes when trying to accurately simulate it using the same grids": mean velocities show little grid dependence, second-order statistics do, and accuracy improves with refinement (§3.3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. Coarse grids "can draw a qualitative conclusion same as the fine grids", so a qualitative budget analysis is admissible with insufficient resolution; this justifies the 30P30N study (§3.3). **Shown** (on one case)
13. A significant imbalance of the budget (sum of the terms not zero) signals insufficient grid resolution for turbulence statistics (§3.3, Figs. 5–6; reused in §4). **Shown**
14. OPT6 results, "even in $48^3$ grids", agree strongly with the reference, especially the balance term; the four OPT6 grids give similar values, whereas KGP2 at $x/H=0$ and 1 shows a noticeable convergence trend (§3.3, Figs. 5–6). **Shown**
15. The high-order upwind scheme with good dispersion and dissipation outperforms the second-order non-dissipative central scheme, perfect in dissipation but poor in dispersion, for the budget, especially on coarse grids; high-order upwind schemes are recommended for budget analysis (Abstract, §5). **Shown**
16. 30P30N DDES: the time-averaged $C_p$ agrees well with the data of Pascioni et al. [53] (§4, Fig. 8a). **Shown**
17. 30P30N: spanwise vorticity, streamwise velocity and turbulent kinetic energy along lines through the slat shear layer agree with Pascioni et al.; their maxima decrease with distance from the slat trailing edge (§4, Fig. 9). **Shown**
18. Three maxima of $k$ in the slat cove: the trailing-edge shedding region, the shear layer behind the slat leading edge, and the unsteady impingement location on the wall (§4). **Shown**
19. The slat-trailing-edge pressure spectrum agrees with the JAXA [54] and FSU [53] experiments: broadband content plus a tone at about 21 kHz (§4, Fig. 10). **Shown**
20. Budget patterns: production is a positive "monopole pattern" in strong-shear and wake regions; dissipation a slight negative "monopole pattern" in similar regions; diffusion negative at the shear-layer centre flanked by two positives ("outward pattern"); advection slightly positive between two negatives ("inward pattern") (§4, Fig. 11; §5). **Shown**
21. At the main-element trailing edge the transport of $k$ is not symmetric about the shear layer (§4, Fig. 11k–o). **Shown**
22. Along the slat-leading-edge shear layer (lines L1–L7) production dominates, maximal at the start of the layer and decaying to 10%; dissipation is minimal (attributed to insufficient grid resolution); diffusion, mainly turbulent transport, is the main balancing term; viscous diffusion is negligible; advection follows a "w" pattern symmetric to diffusion (§4, Fig. 12a–g). **Shown**
23. Along L8–L12 pressure diffusion is larger because of a large pressure gradient; advection predominates and is not symmetric to turbulent transport (§4, Fig. 12h–l). **Shown**
24. "In ideal conditions, the dissipation term of the TKE should balance the production term" (§4, citing Schanderl et al. [55]). **Stated**
25. The remaining budget imbalance in the 30P30N case is attributed to insufficient grid resolution, by analogy with the periodic hill (§4). **Stated** (no grid study on the airfoil)

The budget (Eq. 4) in registry notation, constant-density form applied to
low-Mach compressible data, $k=\tfrac12R_{ii}$:
$\partial_tk=-\langle u_j\rangle\partial_jk+P_k-\rho^{-1}\partial_j\langle p'u_j'\rangle-\partial_j\langle k'u_j'\rangle+\nu\partial_j\partial_jk-\nu\langle\partial_ju_i'\partial_ju_i'\rangle$,
the last term the pseudo-dissipation with its sign. The modelled (WALE or
DDES) eddy-viscosity contribution does not appear in Eq. (4). The
viscous stress is $(\mu+\mu_t)$ times twice the deviatoric strain (Eq. 3);
the formulas and constants of the WALE and SA-DDES models are not printed
(cited to refs. [43, 51]).

# Printed slips

- **§2.3, after Eq. (11):** "When $\xi = 1/2$, $\alpha=\beta=\delta=1/4$, and $\varepsilon=0$, the KGP scheme appears": $\xi$ does not occur in Eqs. (5)–(11), and the weight $\gamma$ of $\mathcal C^u$ is not given.
- **Eq. (2):** the viscous-flux vector lists $\tau_{1j},\tau_{2j},\tau_{3j}$, with free index $j$ where $i$ is expected.
- **§2.1:** Sutherland's law printed "$\mu=T^{3/2}(T_0+S)/(T+S)$", with no reference viscosity, while temperatures are given in K.
- **§4:** "turbulent diffusion term generated by pressure velocity correlation, pressure diffusion term generated by the third-order correlation of turbulent pulsation": the labels are swapped relative to Eq. (4).
- **§3.1:** for the inviscid case, "the compressible Navier-Stokes equation is computed".
- **§3.2:** kinematic viscosity printed "$v$" in $Re_\tau=u_\tau h/v$.
- **§3.3:** "as the grid becomes nicer"; **§4:** "nondenominational physical time step", "sheer layer"; **Fig. 1 caption:** "image part".
- **§3.3 against Table 2:** the text reports both schemes on all four grids, Table 2 lists order 6 only on $196\times128\times186$; Table 2 and Figs. 5–6 write "$Re=10595$" where the text writes $Re_b$.
- **Citations:** the WALE model is cited to [43] (an application paper of 2022) and the SA model to [43, 51] (application papers), not to their originators.
- **Ref. [3]:** Cockburn, Hou & Shu (1990), Math. Comput. 54:545–581, printed with DOI 10.1006/jcph.1998.5892, which does not match the printed venue.
- **Ref. [24]:** title with the duplicated phrase "Basic formulation: basic formulation".
