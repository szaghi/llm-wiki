# Summary

Liu et al. (2024) give a second-moment RANS closure a transition
capability. The SSG/LRR-ω [differential Reynolds-stress model](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md)
of Eisfeld et al. (2016), which blends the LRR model near the wall and the
SSG model outside with the SST function $F_1$ of the
[$k$-$\omega$ SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md), is coupled with
Menter's one-equation intermittency model of the
[local-correlation-based transition](/methods/local-correlation-based-transition-model.md)
family: the intermittency $\gamma$ multiplies the stress production and the
pressure–strain term, $\max(\gamma,0.1)$ multiplies the dissipation, and the
production of the $\omega$ equation becomes a $\gamma$-blend of a
Kato–Launder production and the original one, a coupling form credited to
Nie et al. (2018). The motivation is that linear eddy-viscosity models
([Spalart–Allmaras](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md), SST) built on the
[Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) need
rotation, curvature, QCR or non-equilibrium corrections for compressor
corner flows, while a stress-transport model captures anisotropy but lacks
transition. Scale-resolving alternatives ([DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md)
and other [hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) methods) are
dismissed in the introduction as too costly for routine design.

On the [ERCOFTAC T3 flat plates](/benchmarks/ercoftac-t3-flat-plate.md) the
coupled model predicts transition onset well at zero and favourable
pressure gradient and delays it under adverse pressure gradient, as SST-γ
does. On the low-speed PVD compressor cascade at $-7°$ and $0°$ incidence
and on the transonic NACA65 K48 cascade it resolves
[laminar separation bubbles](/definitions/laminar-separation-bubble.md) and
separation-induced transition on the suction side, reproduces the $C_p$
plateaus, and improves the loss, the exit angle and the corner-separation
topology against SST and the fully turbulent SSG/LRR-ω; it still
overpredicts the corner separation under strong adverse pressure gradient.
The authors explain the gain by the mid-span boundary layer: laminar
upstream, it separates and transitions, and the reattached turbulent
profile is fuller and resists separation downstream. The evidence is
comparison with experiments.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols, as on the [Reynolds-stress model](/methods/reynolds-stress-model.md)
page: $\mathcal P_{ij}$ the Reynolds-stress production,
$\phi_{ij}$ the pressure–strain term, $\varepsilon_{ij}$ the
dissipation tensor, $\mathcal D_{ij}$ the stress diffusion; $P_k=\mathcal P_{kk}/2$
(times $\rho$ as printed); $\alpha_\omega$ the production coefficient of
the $\omega$ equation; $F_{\mathrm{onset}}$, $F_{\mathrm{turb}}$,
$F_{\mathrm{length}}$ the switches of the $\gamma$ model; $Re_V=\rho d^2|S|/\mu$;
$Y_p$ the total-pressure-loss coefficient.

1. Linear eddy-viscosity models (SA, SST) need added modifications (rotation–curvature, QCR, non-equilibrium) to predict anisotropy and non-equilibrium; Reynolds-stress models represent the Reynolds stress and the Coriolis source terms exactly and are more sensitive to anisotropy (Introduction, refs. [28–36]). **Stated**
2. $\varepsilon$-based Reynolds-stress models (LRR, SSG, EB-RSM) suffer near-wall numerical stiffness; LRR needs a pressure-reflection term that reduces robustness further; Wilcox avoided it by using $\omega$; Eisfeld et al. blended the stress-$\omega$ model with SSG-$\varepsilon$ through $F_1$ into SSG/LRR-ω (Introduction, refs. [40–42]). **Stated**
3. The $k$-$k_l$-$\omega$ laminar-kinetic-energy model is calibrated on a $k$-$\omega$ closure with many trigger functions and empirical coefficients, hard to couple to SSG/LRR-ω; Menter's $\gamma$ model, calibrated on SST, is "more compatible" with SSG/LRR-ω and "overcomes the Galilean invariance defect of the γ–Reθ model" (Introduction). **Stated**
4. Coupling (Eqs. 20–21): $\gamma\mathcal P_{ij}$, $\gamma\phi_{ij}$ and $\max(\gamma,0.1)\varepsilon_{ij}$ in the stress equations; $\omega$-equation production $\alpha_\omega\rho(1-\gamma)|S||\Omega|+\gamma(\alpha_\omega/\nu_t)P_k$. **Stated** (definition; contribution: the pairing with Menter's $\gamma$ model and the OpenFOAM implementation)
5. The Kato–Launder production ($|S||\Omega|$) avoids turbulence overproduction at stagnation; the coupling was proposed by Nie et al. [58] for SSG/LRR-ω with γ-Reθ, to correct too-short transition lengths caused by the different near-wall $\omega$ production of SST and SSG/LRR-ω (section "Blending"). **Stated**
6. Kuya et al. [59] instead activate the modified $\omega$ production only in favourable pressure gradient, keeping the original one in adverse pressure gradient, to improve the lift of multi-element airfoils (section "Blending"). **Stated**
7. On the PVD cascade at 25% span, the coupled model with the modified $\omega$ production predicts separation-induced transition consistent with the oil flow; with the original $\omega$ production transition occurs further upstream, as bypass transition (Fig. 1). **Shown**
8. The 3D cascade computations with SSG/LRR-ω do not converge to a steady state because of wake shedding and separation bubbles; results are time-averaged URANS (time step about 1% of the passing period, 30 periods, 15 averaged), with first-order upwind advection of the turbulence variables and $\gamma$ (section "Flow solvers"). **Stated** (numerical practice)
9. T3A grid study on five levels with $y^+<0.4$: onset is insensitive from the upper-medium level; the fine level ($365\times193$ points) is adopted (Table 3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
10. In the T3 flows SST and fully turbulent SSG/LRR-ω give similar accuracy; downstream of transition the coupled model's skin friction is typically higher than that of the fully turbulent models, implying a fuller near-wall profile (Fig. 6). **Shown**
11. The coupled model agrees well with the measured onset at zero pressure gradient (T3A, T3B, T3A–; Fig. 6a–c) and favourable pressure gradient (T3C1, T3C5; Fig. 6d–e). **Shown**
12. Under adverse pressure gradient (T3C2–C4; Fig. 6f–h) the coupled model delays onset, "similar to the problem existing in the SST–γ model as documented in Ref. [52]"; T3C4 behaves as separation-induced transition. **Shown**
13. PVD at $-7°$: SST and SSG/LRR-ω both overestimate the suction-side corner separation; the coupled model resolves laminar separation bubbles near mid-chord and better matches the separation size (Fig. 8). **Shown**
14. PVD at $-7°$: the three models give similar $C_p$ at 54% and 89% span, but the coupled model captures the plateau from separation-induced transition near 45% axial chord and is closer to the measurement at 85–100% axial chord (Fig. 9). **Shown**
15. PVD at $0°$: SST predicts an endwall separation much larger than measured, SSG/LRR-ω and the coupled model agree better (Fig. 10); on the suction surface SST and SSG/LRR-ω separations are much larger than measured, the coupled model's topology is more reasonable but still too large (Fig. 11). **Shown**
16. PVD at $0°$: SST underpredicts $C_p$ at 50–100% axial chord, SSG/LRR-ω improves slightly, the coupled model further, including the plateau from laminar separation bubbles near 35% axial chord (Fig. 12). **Shown**
17. PVD at $0°$: SSG/LRR-ω improves slightly on SST but overestimates $Y_p$ and the exit angle; the coupled model improves both (Fig. 13a–b). **Shown**
18. PVD at $0°$: the coupled model improves the relative displacement thickness at 50–80% and 90–100% span but gives a bimodal distribution near 80% and 90% span where the experiment is unimodal, indicating two low-speed cores and missing momentum mixing (Fig. 13c). **Shown**
19. Remaining errors are attributed to insufficient treatment of anisotropy and non-equilibrium under strong adverse pressure gradient; further modifications "may involve modifications to pressure–strain terms or scale determination equations" (PVD 0° results, Conclusions). **Stated**
20. NACA65 K48 at design point: SST and SSG/LRR-ω place the separation lines near the trailing edge close to mid-plane; the coupled model captures separation bubbles near mid-chord and a better corner topology but still overpredicts the corner separation near the trailing edge (Fig. 15). **Shown**
21. NACA65 K48: the coupled model gives a fuller loss profile and the best overall agreement 40% chord downstream; the pitch-averaged loss improves below 30% span, with a deviation near 15% span (Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
22. Mechanism: upstream of transition the fully turbulent profile is fuller and resists separation, so SSG/LRR-ω misses the bubbles; in the coupled model $\gamma\mathcal P_{ij}$ is suppressed, the layer is near-laminar and separates, separation raises $|S|$ and triggers the $\gamma$ production, and after turbulent reattachment the profile is fuller than SSG/LRR-ω's, suppressing separation downstream at mid-span (Discussion, Fig. 18). **Shown** (interpretation of computed profiles)
23. SSG/LRR-ω is "only slightly more accurate" than SST for internal corner-separation flows, especially with strong adverse pressure gradients (Conclusions). **Shown**
24. Gao et al. [36] showed the importance of non-equilibrium turbulence transport for tip-leakage flow by running an eddy-viscosity model with frozen $k$, $\varepsilon$ fields from a Reynolds-stress model (PVD 0° results). **Stated**

The SSG/LRR-ω model as printed, in registry notation: $\rho R_{ij}$
transport with $\mathcal P_{ij}=-\rho R_{ik}\partial_k\langle u_j\rangle-\rho R_{jk}\partial_k\langle u_i\rangle$,
isotropic dissipation $\tfrac23\rho\varepsilon\delta_{ij}$ with
$\varepsilon=C_\mu k\omega$, $C_\mu=0.09$, generalized-gradient diffusion
(Daly–Harlow), and the SSG/LRR pressure–strain term in $b_{ij}$, $S^d_{ij}$,
$S_{ij}$, $\Omega_{ij}$ (Eqs. 1–8); every coefficient is blended as
$F_1\phi^{\mathrm{LRR}}+(1-F_1)\phi^{\mathrm{SSG}}$ (Eq. 9), with Table 1
listing the pressure–strain coefficients (see Printed slips). The $\gamma$
equation (Eqs. 10–19) has $P_\gamma=F_{\mathrm{length}}\rho|S|\gamma(1-\gamma)F_{\mathrm{onset}}$,
$F_{\mathrm{length}}=100$, $c_{e2}=50$, onset through
$Re_V/(2.2\,Re_{\theta c})$, $Re_{\theta c}=100+1000\exp(-Tu_lF_{PG}(\lambda_{\theta l}))$,
$\lambda_{\theta l}=-7.57\times10^{-3}(dV/dy)d^2/\nu+0.0128$. The constants
$\alpha_\omega$, $\beta_\omega$, $\sigma_\omega$, $\sigma_d$, $c_{a2}$,
$\sigma_\gamma$ and the function $F_{PG}$ are not printed, so the model
cannot be reproduced from this paper alone.

# Printed slips

- **Section heading (p. 3):** the section holding "Flow solvers" and "RANS closure" is headed "Results of PVD cascade at 0 ◦ incidence", the heading reused for the actual PVD 0° results (p. 8); the Introduction's outline refers to it in the same way.
- **Table 1:** the caption defines "$c_2^{(LRR)}=0.52$", yet the LRR row lists $c_2=0$ and the expressions in $c_2^{(LRR)}$ for $c_4$, $c_5$ stand in the SSG row; the labelling is internally inconsistent.
- **Coupling text against Eq. (20):** the text says production, pressure–strain "and dissipation term $\varepsilon_{ij}$" are multiplied "by the factor γ", Eq. (20) multiplies the dissipation by $\max(\gamma,0.1)$.
- **$c_1$:** the pressure–strain coefficient (Table 1) and the onset constant $c_1=2.2$ (Eq. 15) share the symbol.
- **$Re_V$:** called "vorticity Reynolds number" in the nomenclature and "eddy Reynolds number" in the text, while Eq. (16) builds it on the strain modulus $S$.
- **Grid levels:** "number of grid points … of adjacent levels doubled", while Table 3 dimensions are approximately doubled ($186\to365$).
- **Abstract:** "may fail short".
- **Page 1:** leftover footnote "Preprint submitted to Aerospace Science and Technology (AESCTE) ***, 2023."
