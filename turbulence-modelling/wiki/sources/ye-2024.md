# Summary

Ye et al. (2024) study the fluid–structure interaction of a thin flexible
panel set in the floor plate of a sharp-fin swept
[shock/turbulent boundary-layer interaction](/benchmarks/shock-turbulent-boundary-layer-interaction.md)
at $M_\infty=5$. Turbulence modelling is a fixed ingredient, not a subject
of the paper: an in-house CFD/CSD code solves the unsteady RANS equations
with the eddy viscosity of the [$k$-$\omega$ SST model](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md)
(Menter 1994) in a [Boussinesq](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
stress, and a heat flux with a [turbulent Prandtl number](/definitions/turbulent-prandtl-number.md)
whose value is not printed. Convection is AUSM+ with MUSCL reconstruction
($\kappa_{\mathrm M}=1/3$, called third-order upwind), viscous terms are
second-order central, and time is dual time stepping with LU-SGS. The
structure is a finite-element plate, loosely coupled to the flow, with
Wendland C2 radial-basis-function interpolation for load and displacement
transfer and mesh deformation.

The CFD is validated on the Schülein–Zheltovodov Mach-5 sharp-fin
experiment (fin angle $12^\circ$) through wall pressure at two stations,
and the coupling on Dowell's Mach-2 panel flutter. The rigid-panel flow is
quasi-conical, with lines of surface-flow coalescence (LC) and divergence
(LD) and a $\lambda$ shock. A $0.09\times0.05$ m simply supported panel is
placed with 25%, 50% or 75% of its area inside the separation (area
proportion AP, page-local). As AP grows, the response moves from
limit-cycle to multi-frequency irregular oscillation. The dominant
displacement frequency rises from 729.66 Hz through 753.59 Hz to 1116.96 Hz,
and the dominant structural modes move from the first and second to the
fourth and fifth. The quasi-conical symmetry is increasingly destroyed, the
separation shock is depressed over the deforming panel, and panel-induced
expansion waves strengthen and move away from the fin. All results are
single URANS realizations of about 0.1 s; no sensitivity to the turbulence
model is shown.

# Key claims

1. The flow solver is URANS: Navier–Stokes with $\mu_t$ from the SST $k$-$\omega$ model (ref. 38), Sutherland law for $\mu$; AUSM+ convective flux; MUSCL reconstruction with $\kappa_{\mathrm M}=1/3$ "corresponds to the third-order upwind difference scheme"; viscous fluxes second-order central (§II A, Eqs. 1–9). **Stated**
2. Time integration is dual time stepping, first-order in pseudo-time and second-order (three-level backward) in physical time, solved by LU-SGS (§II A, Eqs. 10–12). **Stated**
3. The structure is solved by FEM, $\mathsf M\ddot{\boldsymbol q}+\mathsf K\boldsymbol q=\boldsymbol A$ with $N_s\times N_s$ mass and stiffness matrices (§II B, Eq. 13). **Stated**
4. CFD/CSD coupling is the "classic serial method": converged CFD, loads to structural nodes by RBF, CSD displacement, displacement back to fluid nodes by RBF, mesh update, recompute, until both converge (§II C, Fig. 1). **Stated**
5. Mesh deformation interpolates the boundary displacement into the interior by RBF with Wendland's C2 function (§II D, Eqs. 14–16; ref. 39). **Stated**
6. CFD validation: Mach-5 sharp-fin interaction of Schülein and Zheltovodov (ref. 40), fin angle $12^\circ$, plate 500 mm × 200 mm, fin 100 mm high with leading edge 249.5 mm from the plate leading edge; flow conditions in Table I; computed wall pressure at $X=138$ and 168 mm "in good agreement" with experiment (§III A, Fig. 3). **Shown**
7. Validation grid: $\delta=3$ mm, 30 layers in the boundary layer on the floor and in the spanwise direction, first-cell height $5\times10^{-4}$ mm "to ensure that the $y^+$ … does not exceed 1", spacing $1\times10^{-2}$ mm near the shock, 1 303 760 cells (§III A). **Stated**
8. FSI validation: three-dimensional panel flutter at $M_\infty=2$ against Dowell (ref. 41), amplitude at 3/4 chord against the dimensionless dynamic pressure, "in good agreement" (§III B, Figs. 4–5). **Shown** (no numbers in the text)
9. Grid convergence at AP = 50% (steady state) with $0.6$, $1.3$ and $2\times10^6$ cells: medium and fine nearly identical, "when the number of grids exceeds 1.3×10^6, the effect of grid density is negligible"; medium grid chosen (§IV B, Fig. 7). **Shown**
10. Time-step convergence at AP = 50% with $\Delta t=8$, 4, 2 and $1\times10^{-5}$ s: minimal difference between the last two; $2\times10^{-5}$ s chosen (§IV B, Fig. 8). **Shown**
11. Rigid panel: the flow is quasi-conical with a virtual conical origin at (50.63, 0, 17.92) mm at the intersection of LC and LD, which are straight after an initial distance (§IV C 1, Fig. 9; following Gaitonde and Adler, ref. 2). **Shown**
12. Rigid panel: at section $r/\delta=80$ a $\lambda$ shock structure (inviscid, attachment and separation shocks, triple point) is present; the separated free shear layer forms a subsonic jet accelerated by expansion waves into a high-speed jet impinging near LD, "basically consistent with Refs. 12 and 42" (§IV C 2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
13. Panel displacement responses evolve in three stages (initial, transition, stable) for every AP (§IV D 1 a, Figs. 12, 15, 18). **Shown**
14. AP = 25%: zone A single frequency, zone B two frequencies (the second fading in stage III), zone C one plus low-energy frequencies; zones A and C limit-cycle-like phase portraits, zone B more complex (Figs. 12–13). **Shown**
15. AP = 50%: zone A LCO-like, zone B irregular, zone C beat oscillation (Figs. 15–16); AP = 75%: all zones irregular (Figs. 18–19). **Shown**
16. Dominant displacement frequency 729.66 Hz (AP 25%), 753.59 Hz (AP 50%), 1116.96 Hz (AP 75%); large-amplitude frequencies 729.66 Hz (25%); 753.59 and 978.03 Hz (50%); 995.13, 1116.96, 1026.54 and 1136.59 Hz (75%) (§IV D 1 a, Fig. 20). **Shown**
17. Stable cycles start at 0.08, 0.08658 and 0.0744 s; displacement extrema reciprocate along the stream for AP 25% and 50%, but oscillate between near-fin and far-from-fin positions for AP 75% (§IV D 1 b, Figs. 21–23). **Shown**
18. Spanwise cuts: one maximum and one minimum (AP 25%), two maxima and one minimum (AP 50%), two maxima and two minima (AP 75%) (Figs. 24–26). **Shown**
19. A projection on the first 50 plate modes (1950 panel points, overdetermined least squares) shows modes above order 20 negligible; dominant modes 1st–2nd (AP 25%), 2nd–3rd (AP 50%), 4th–5th (AP 75%); modes 8–20 take a larger share at AP 50% and 75% (§IV D 1 b, Eq. 17, Fig. 27). **Shown**
20. The pressure fluctuation on the panel moves from LCO-like to irregular with increasing AP, following the displacement; the analysis window starts at 0.06 s (§IV D 2 a, Figs. 28–30). **Shown**
21. The peak of the RMS pressure fluctuation ranks AP 25% < 50% < 75%, and the differences in its spatial distribution grow with AP (§IV D 2 a, Eq. 18, Fig. 31). **Shown**
22. Along LC, points P1–P3 near the fin behave as in the rigid case and the amplitude decreases downstream; along LD, P11–P15 are basically unchanged and P16–P19 amplitudes increase downstream (Fig. 32). **Shown**
23. Spectra at P7 (LC) and P19 (LD): at P19 the dominant frequencies are 729 Hz (AP 25%), 974 Hz (AP 50%) and 756 Hz (AP 75%); LC has "one more low frequency and two more high frequency" than LD (§IV D 2 a, Fig. 34). **Shown**
24. Quasi-conical symmetry is increasingly destroyed as AP increases; panel depressions generate expansion waves whose strength and area grow with the deformation (§IV D 2 b, Figs. 35–37). **Shown**
25. The separation shock is "depressed" over the deforming panel while the attachment and inviscid shocks hardly move; the depression grows with panel displacement, and the expansion-wave strength grows and its peak moves away from the fin as AP increases (§IV D 2 c, Figs. 38–40; Conclusion 4). **Shown**

The stress of Eq. (7), in registry symbols, is the viscous plus
Boussinesq turbulent stress $2(\mu+\mu_t)S^d_{ij}$, with no
$\tfrac23\rho k\delta_{ij}$ term printed; the heat-flux coefficient of
Eq. (6) is $c_p(\mu/Pr+\mu_t/Pr_t)$.

# Printed slips

- Eq. (12), introduced as obtained "by replacing" $Q^{n+1}$ with $Q^{m+1}$, prints $3Q^{m+1}$ in the physical-time term but keeps $R(Q^{n+1})$ on the right-hand side.
- The captions of Figs. 39 and 40 (AP = 50% and 75%) read "section $r/\delta=80$", while the text says "Figure 39 shows the pressure cloud diagram of section $r/\delta=86$ under AP = 50%" and "Figure 40 … section $r/\delta=96$ under AP = 75%".
- The captions of Figs. 22 and 23 (AP = 50% and 75%) give the snapshots as fractions of $T_{O1}$, which §IV D 1 b defines as the AP = 25% cycle ($T_{o2}$, $T_{o3}$ being the AP = 50% and 75% cycles).
- Instants are written "t = 1/6 s", "t = 1/8 s" in the text and in the captions of Figs. 35–40, while the captions of Figs. 21–23 write "t = 1/6 $T_{O1}$" for fractions of the cycle.
- §IV D 2 a: "the P19 with larger fluctuation amplitude is chosen to study the pressure fluctuation LC", while P11–P19 are the LD points (Fig. 6a).
- §IV D 2 a, P7 spectrum: "when 974 Hz is the dominant frequency, AP = 75% and AP = 50% are similar … The dominant frequency is 1117 Hz": both 974 Hz and 1117 Hz are called dominant for AP = 75%.
- §IV D 2 a: "except at z/b = is 0.8"; §IV B: "the thickness of the plane" for the panel.
- The text names $\mu_l$ the laminar viscosity, while Eqs. (6)–(8) print $\mu$.
