# Summary

Kim & Leonard (2024) give a first-hand history of
[direct numerical simulation](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
and [large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) from the
spectral simulations of decaying
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md) of
Orszag & Patterson (1971–1972) to the early 1990s; RANS is excluded. No new
result is derived.

The meteorological strand comes first: von Neumann's outlook, Corrsin's
estimate of $10^{12}$ grid points (see
[grid-point requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)), and
Smagorinsky's general-circulation eddy coefficient, which became the
[Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md), with the history of its
constant (0.17, 0.10, 0.206). Deardorff's and Schumann's
[channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md) LES used log-law wall
conditions (an early form of the [equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md)),
and Schumann added a
[subgrid energy equation](/methods/subgrid-kinetic-energy-equation-model.md).
At Stanford and NASA Ames came Leonard's filtered equations and the
deterministic stress-like term (the tensor-diffusivity term, the first term
of deconvolution; see the [gradient model](/methods/gradient-model.md) and
[scale-similarity models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md)), the first
[a priori test](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) (Clark et
al. 1979), the first wall-resolved LES, the
[energy-conserving](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) rotational
and skew-symmetric forms, Rogallo's homogeneous-shear DNS, and the channel
DNS of Kim, Moin & Moser (1987) at $Re_\tau=180$, which exposed an error in an
oil-channel experiment. Spalart's DNS of the zero-pressure-gradient
[boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md) follows. The
Center for Turbulence Research period covers free shear flows (the
[temporal mixing layer](/benchmarks/temporal-mixing-layer.md)), forced
isotropic turbulence, compressible DNS ([Morkovin's
hypothesis](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md)), the
[backward-facing step](/benchmarks/backward-facing-step.md),
[inflow generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md) by recycling,
[spectral elements](/methods/spectral-element-method.md), compact schemes
(see [low-dissipation schemes](/methods/low-dissipation-low-dispersion-scheme.md)),
the Q-criterion and the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md).
The close contrasts a $10^{12}$ growth in peak speed with the lasting use of
$Re_\tau=180$, and names [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)
as a design tool for high-lift aircraft.

# Key claims

1. The review covers DNS and LES from the first simulations of decaying homogeneous isotropic turbulence (1971–1972) to about 25 years later; RANS is not covered (Abstract, §1). **Stated**
2. Von Neumann (1949) saw computation as a way to "break the deadlock" in turbulence theory but judged even an $8^3$ simulation beyond the computers of the time (§2.1). **Stated**
3. Corrsin (1961) argued that universality needs $Re=10^4$ and, from Kolmogorov scaling, estimated at least $10^{12}$ grid points; Case et al. (1973) substantiated the estimate (§2.1). **Stated**
4. Orszag & Patterson (1971, 1972) simulated decaying isotropic turbulence on $16^3$ and $32^3$ meshes with a spectral method, initial $Re_\lambda=35$, on a 3-megaflop CDC 6600; a $32^3$ step took 30 s, 200 steps 1.5 h (§2.2, Fig. 2a). **Stated**
5. Orszag (1971a) estimated that an $N^3$ spectral simulation matches the accuracy of a $(4N)^3$ finite-difference computation at the cost of a $(2N)^3$ one; pseudospectral evaluation avoids the $O(N^6)$ convolution sums and requires "$O((N\log N)^3)$" operations (§2.2). **Stated**
6. Patterson & Orszag (1971) removed aliasing by spherical truncation and six shifted transforms; Rogallo (1981) removed it more cheaply by 2/3-rule truncation and phase shifts (§2.2, §3.3). **Stated**
7. Leonard and Patterson (unpublished, 1973) found that about 40% of the total nonlinear transfer to the subgrid scales came from the filtered nonlinear term, "in fair agreement with the estimate of Leonard (1974)" (§2.2). **Stated**
8. Smagorinsky's eddy coefficient (1963, 1965) was introduced because aliasing was known to cause nonlinear instability (Phillips 1959); a one-dimensional version existed earlier for shocks (von Neumann & Richtmyer 1950) (§2.5). **Stated**
9. Lilly (1967) showed consistency of the Smagorinsky model with the $-5/3$ law and gave $C_s\approx0.17$; Deardorff found this overdamped the large eddies and $C_s=0.10$ "seemed optimal" (1971); Kwak et al. (1975) obtained 0.206 by matching the decay of Comte-Bellot & Corrsin (1971), close to Lilly's unpublished 0.2 (§2.5, §3.1). **Stated**
10. Deardorff's (1970) channel LES used 6,720 grid points, a log-law wall condition and Arakawa differencing; Schumann (1975) used up to 65 K volumes, accounted for anisotropic grids and added a subgrid energy transport equation (§2.5). **Stated**
11. Leonard (1973, 1974) derived the LES equations by filtering the Navier–Stokes equations; the derivation revealed a deterministic stress-like term, now called the tensor diffusivity model (Leonard 2015), the first term of the deconvolution method (Stolz et al. 2001) (§3.1). **Stated**
12. Clark et al. (1979) first evaluated SGS models on a DNS database: "an a priori test, in contrast to an a posteriori test, by which the performance of an SGS model is evaluated after an actual LES with the model" (§3.1). **Stated**
13. Moin et al. (1978) performed the first LES of wall-bounded turbulence with the wall region computed; Deardorff (1970) and Schumann (1975) had used special wall conditions (§3.1). **Stated**
14. Mansour et al. (1979) showed by summation by parts that most numerical methods conserve energy with the nonlinear terms in rotational form, $\boldsymbol\omega\times\boldsymbol u+\tfrac12\nabla|\boldsymbol u|^2$; the skew-symmetric form $\tfrac12\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla\boldsymbol u+\tfrac12\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol u\boldsymbol u)$ serves the same purpose (Arakawa 1966; Zang 1991); the staggered mesh of Harlow & Welch (1965) conserves momentum and kinetic energy (Lilly 1965) (§3.1). **Stated**
15. Rogallo (1981) extended isotropic DNS to rotation, irrotational strain and homogeneous shear, first reported complete Reynolds-stress budgets and introduced periodic remeshing (the "windshield wiper transformation") (§3.3). **Stated**
16. Moin & Kim (1982) added a Van Driest-like damping to the Smagorinsky model in the channel, curing the excessive near-wall dissipation of the earlier LES; their 1980 visualization movie convinced the community that simulation reproduces realistic turbulence dynamics (§3.4, Fig. 3). **Stated**
17. Kim et al. (1987) targeted $Re_\tau=194$ (a Kreplin & Eckelmann oil channel) and obtained $Re_\tau=180$, which became the reference; the near-wall disagreement was traced to an experimental error (wall heat conduction), confirmed by new measurements — "probably the first incident in which a numerical simulation prompted a reexamination of the experimental data followed by a confirmation of an experimental error" (§3.4). **Stated**
18. The curved-channel DNS of Moser & Moin (1984, 1987), with divergence-free vector basis functions, preceded Kim et al. (1987) and "was probably the first DNS of a wall-bounded turbulent flow" (§3.4). **Stated**
19. Spalart (1988) performed the first DNS of a zero-pressure-gradient flat-plate boundary layer, with streamwise periodicity and a multiscale procedure for the streamwise growth, up to $Re_\theta=1{,}410$; strongly growing layers need inflow and outflow conditions (§3.5). **Stated**
20. Forcing of isotropic DNS (Siggia & Patterson 1978; Siggia 1981) models the large scales and computes the small ones, "the opposite of the LES approach", and gives a statistically steady state (§4.3). **Stated**
21. The first DNS of wall-bounded compressible turbulence was Buell's (1990) Couette flow; Coleman et al. (1995) did the first supersonic channel, Rai et al. (1995) the first supersonic boundary layer; Coleman et al.'s "nonphysical" experiment found direct compressibility effects small at $M=1.5$ and 3; Feiereisen et al. (1981) did the first LES of compressible homogeneous shear (§4.5). **Stated**
22. The recycling method of Lund et al. (1998), or a variant, "has since been used commonly" for turbulent inflow; the outflow uses an advective condition (Lowery & Reynolds 1986) (§4.6). **Stated**
23. Two early realizations are called important: the formal accuracy of the discretization in LES should be higher than that of the turbulence model, and the inviscid discretized equations should conserve energy for long-term stability (§4.7). **Stated**
24. Second-order Adams–Bashforth was largely replaced by low-storage third-order Runge–Kutta (Williamson 1980); Kim et al. (1987) switched to RK3 after publication, yet "many investigators still use the second-order A-B method following the original method used by Kim et al. (1987)" (§4.7). **Stated**
25. The dynamic SGS model (Germano et al. 1991) was conceived at the 1990 CTR Summer Program; the Germano identity applied to the resolved field fixes a space–time-varying Smagorinsky coefficient, and "most LES codes now employ a version of the dynamic SGS model" (§4.8). **Stated**
26. A universal SGS model presupposes that all energy-containing scales are resolved; many practical LES do not resolve them, so the SGS model must account for flow-dependent motions (sidebar, §4.8). **Stated**
27. The Q-criterion was first reported at the 1988 CTR Summer Program (Hunt et al. 1988); opposition control (Choi et al. 1994) became a reference control scheme; the minimal channel (Jiménez & Moin 1991) and minimal Couette flow (Hamilton et al. 1995) give insight from "nonphysical" flows (§4.8). **Stated**
28. Peak speed grew about $10^{12}$-fold from the CDC 6600 (1971) to Frontier (2022); isotropic DNS reached $4{,}096^3$ (Kaneda et al. 2003) and $8{,}192^3$ (Yeung et al. 2015) at $Re_\lambda=1{,}200$–$1{,}300$ (§5). **Stated**
29. A well-designed LES, "especially wall-modeled LES", can now be a design tool for aircraft in high-lift configuration (Goc et al. 2021) (§5). **Stated**

The Smagorinsky coefficient is printed (§2.5) as
$K=(c\Delta)^2\big[\partial_j\bar u_i(\partial_j\bar u_i+\partial_i\bar u_j)\big]^{1/2}$
with $\Delta$ "a representative mesh interval"; the bracket equals
$2\bar S_{ij}\bar S_{ij}$, so $K=(C_s\Delta)^2|\bar S|$ *(compiler check,
not in the sources)*.
