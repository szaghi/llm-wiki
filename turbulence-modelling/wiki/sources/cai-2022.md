# Summary

Cai et al. (2022) apply their
[immersed-boundary](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md) turbulence wall
modelling to the Ahmed body with a 35° slant, a case of massive separation
over the slant and the base, at $Re_H=7.68\times10^5$ against the Lienhart &
Becker experiment. The solver ProLB is a lattice Boltzmann method (D3Q19,
hybrid recursive regularized HRR-$\rho$ collision with a hyper-viscous
stabilization term) with the [Vreman](/methods/vreman-model.md) subgrid
model on octree Cartesian grids. The near-wall treatment, developed in the
authors' earlier and submitted work, has three ingredients: an explicit
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md), a blend of the
viscous and logarithmic [laws of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
inverted in closed form through the Lambert W function; interpolation of the
friction velocity, rather than the velocity, to the immersed-boundary points,
with least-squares gradients and a discrete wall-law correction of the
wall-normal gradient; and a constrained-LES hybrid eddy viscosity that
enforces near the wall the total stress of a mixing-length RANS model,
counting the resolved stress, so that it acts as RANS in attached and as LES
in separated layers ([hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md)).

"Wall-law" (wall model on the whole body) and "no-slip" (no-slip imposed on
slant and base) runs are compared on a coarse and a fine grid
([wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md)). On the coarse grid the
wall-law run stays attached on the slant, which the authors attribute to the
assumed alignment of wall stress and tangential velocity and to too little
near-wall turbulent stress; on the fine grid it separates massively and, by
the velocity profiles, outperforms the no-slip run, which separates too
early. Drag coefficients range from 0.2347 to 0.2912 against 0.279 (Meile et
al.) and 0.26 (Ahmed et al.), with body-fitted [DES](/methods/detached-eddy-simulation.md),
[$k$-$\omega$ SST](/methods/k-omega-sst-model.md) and EARSM values for
comparison. The 25° slant is left for future work.

# Key claims

1. Immersed-boundary methods coupled with wall models on Cartesian grids produce spurious oscillations of wall pressure and skin friction and over-estimate the mean skin friction; the cause is the non-constant wall distance of near-wall nodes combined with the steep under-resolved velocity profile, which makes the treatment sensitive to velocity interpolation and gradient computation (§I, §II.C.1). **Stated** (citing Refs. 17–21)
2. Interpolating the friction velocity rather than the flow velocity to boundary points gives much smoother surface quantities even with a low-order kernel such as inverse-distance weighting, because $u_\tau$ varies smoothly among near-wall nodes (§II.C.1). **Stated** (citing Ref. 18)
3. The explicit wall model (Eqs. 24–25) eliminates Newton iterations and enhances stability, so inverting it at neighbour points adds little cost (§II.C.1). **Stated**
4. The immersed boundary is not conservative; one consequence is that the total Reynolds stress is not maintained near the wall, so skin friction deviates in both RANS and LES and, in LES, turbulence development is strongly delayed (§II.C.1). **Stated** (citing Refs. 18, 23)
5. The hybrid eddy viscosity of Eq. (27) "approaches the RANS eddy viscosity for attached boundary layers and reduces to the LES viscosity for separated boundary layers automatically" (§II.C.1, abstract). **Stated** (no derivation or plot of the hybrid viscosity is shown)
6. In separated regions the wall stress is small, so the wall model works in the low-$y^+$ linear range; the flow is not very sensitive to the wall model in the recirculation zone, where the pressure is nearly constant (§II.C.2). **Stated** (citing Refs. 7, 32, 33)
7. The weakly compressible athermal Navier–Stokes equations ($M<0.3$) are recovered from the lattice Boltzmann equation by the Chapman–Enskog expansion (§II.A). **Stated**
8. BGK collision is unstable at high Reynolds number; the HRR-$\rho$ model adds hyper-viscosity through a weight "chosen as close to unity as possible" (§II.A, Eq. 7). **Stated**
9. At $Re_H=7.68\times10^5$ the estimates $\lambda/H=6.3\times10^{-3}$, $\eta/H=4.6\times10^{-5}$, $\delta/H=6.9\times10^{-2}$, $\delta_\nu/H=3.4\times10^{-5}$ (from $\lambda/H\approx5.5Re_H^{-1/2}$, $\eta/H\approx1.2Re_H^{-3/4}$, $\delta/L\approx0.37Re_L^{-1/5}$, $\delta_\nu/L\approx5.9Re_L^{-0.9}$) make resolving the Kolmogorov scale or the viscous sublayer infeasible on the hierarchical grid ([grid requirements](/results/les-grid-point-requirements.md)) (§III, Table I). **Derived**
10. Grids: coarse $\Delta x_{\min}=2.6\times10^{-3}$ m ($9.0\times10^{-3}H$, $1.4\lambda$, $1.36\times10^7$ cells, $\Delta t_{\min}=8.8\times10^{-6}$ s); fine $1.3\times10^{-3}$ m ($4.5\times10^{-3}H$, $0.7\lambda$, $4.03\times10^7$ cells, $4.4\times10^{-6}$ s); the Taylor scale is "only marginally represented" on the fine grid (§III, Table II). **Stated**
11. The Mach number is raised artificially from 0.12 to 0.24 by lowering the physical sound speed, to enlarge the time step, on the argument that the Mach number does not significantly affect nearly incompressible results (§III). **Stated** (citing Fares, Ref. 6)
12. Runs cover 200 flow-through times $H/U_\infty$, averaged over the last 150, from a previously developed turbulent solution (§III). **Stated**
13. Massive separation near the rear end appears in all runs, with rich wake turbulence despite the near-wall augmentation of the eddy viscosity (§IV, Fig. 4). **Shown**
14. The wall law on the slant yields a small C-pillar vortex that breaks in the wake and delays separation onset relative to the no-slip case (§IV, Figs. 4–6). **Shown**
15. A small separation near the slant end is found, especially with no-slip, consistent with Refs. 8 and 9; no-slip gives a larger bottom recirculation, extending to $x=0.2$ m as in the experiment (§IV, Figs. 5, 7). **Shown**
16. The flow in front of and on top of the body is well predicted by all runs; differences appear on the slant and in the wake (§IV, Figs. 8–9). **Shown**
17. On the coarse grid the wall-law case does not separate on the slant; the stated causes are the assumed alignment of wall stress and tangential velocity at boundary points with those of the near-wall fluid points, and a lack of near-wall turbulent stress; the normal velocity at boundary points is set to zero (§IV). **Shown** (attachment) / **Stated** (causes)
18. On the fine grid the wall-law case separates massively and the resolved fluctuations agree with experiment towards the end of the slant; "Without this enhancement [of the near-wall Reynolds stress], the flow stays attached even by refining the grid" (§IV). **Shown** (fine-grid separation) / **Stated** (no run without the enhancement is shown in this paper)
19. The no-slip case separates more easily: on the coarse grid it fits the slant data but gives a poor wake near the bottom; on the fine grid the wake is good but the slant deviates by early separation; "Overall on the fine grid, the wall-law boundary condition outperforms the no-slip boundary condition" (§IV, Figs. 8–9). **Shown** (velocity profiles)
20. Drag coefficients, near field / far field (Table III): wall-law fine 0.2347 / 0.2380, wall-law coarse 0.2742 / 0.2912, no-slip fine 0.2553 / 0.2588, no-slip coarse 0.2548 / 0.2486; references: Ahmed et al. 0.26 ($Re_H=1.2\times10^6$), Meile et al. 0.279 ($7.68\times10^5$), Liu et al. 0.32 ($1.4\times10^5$), and the body-fitted runs of Guilmineau et al.: $k$-$\omega$ SST 0.2999, EARSM 0.2603, DES 0.3156, IDDES 0.3452. **Shown**
21. "All present results are close to the existing values"; the fine-grid wall-law drag is closer to Meile et al. than the body-fitted IDDES, which the authors attribute to maintaining the total near-wall Reynolds stress and to the seamless RANS/LES switch (§IV). **Shown** against IDDES. From Table III the distances to 0.279 are 0.044 (wall-law fine, near field), 0.066 (IDDES), 0.037 (DES), 0.021 ($k$-$\omega$ SST), 0.019 (EARSM), 0.024 (no-slip fine) and 0.005 (wall-law coarse, the run that does not separate on the slant) *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
22. Grid resolution affects drag more in the wall-law case, because the flow topology differs between the coarse and fine grids (§IV). **Shown**
23. The wall-law condition suits both attached and detached regions "provided with adequate grid resolution"; the 25° slant, with separation and reattachment, remains future work (§V). **Stated**

# Printed slips

1. Eq. (16), quadratic Dirichlet reconstruction, printed $q_b=-\dfrac{-2q_s+dq_2+d^2q_2-2d^2q_1-4dq_1}{(d+2\Delta)(d+\Delta)}$ (the source's $\Delta$ is the reference-point spacing, written $h$ on the [immersed-boundary page](/methods/immersed-boundary-method.md)), is dimensionally consistent only for $h=1$. Quadratic Lagrange interpolation through $q_s$ at 0, $q_1$ at $d+h$ and $q_2$ at $d+2h$, evaluated at $d$ (the construction described, which reproduces the linear Eq. 17), gives $q_b=\dfrac{2h^2q_s+2d(d+2h)q_1-d(d+h)q_2}{(d+h)(d+2h)}$, equal to the printed form for $h=1$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. Eq. (17) keeps the spacing explicit, so the two use different unit conventions.
2. Eq. (18), quadratic Neumann condition, printed $q_b=(4q_1-q_2)/(3\Delta)$ ($\Delta$ the spacing $h$), equates a value to a value per length; a zero one-sided second-order gradient at the boundary point gives $q_b=(4q_1-q_2)/3$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
3. $\kappa$ is used in Eqs. (24)–(27) but its value is never printed (a gap, not a slip).
4. §III calls $\lambda$ "the Taylor scale describing the integral motions in the wake".
5. §IV: "is more closer", "for the all current simulations".
