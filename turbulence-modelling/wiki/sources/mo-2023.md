# Summary

Mo et al. (2023) improve the turbulence-fluctuation-library (TFL) method of
[inflow turbulence generation](/methods/inflow-turbulence-generation.md)
for [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) of compressible and
hypersonic flat-plate [boundary layers](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).
A TFL is a stored time series of velocity planes from a precursor DNS; at
the inflow of the target simulation the stored fluctuations are rescaled
and added to [Spalart–Allmaras](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) RANS
mean profiles. The in-house finite-difference code ACANS (seventh-order
WENO-Z [WENO](/methods/weno-scheme.md) inviscid fluxes, fourth-order
central viscous fluxes, third-order Runge–Kutta) is verified on a Mach 2.25
boundary layer with blowing–suction transition against Pirozzoli et al.,
with van Driest [velocity transformation](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md),
the [log law](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) and the generalized
Reynolds analogy of Zhang et al.; a 65 536-plane TFL at $Re_\tau\approx770$
is collected from it.

The velocity is mapped with a generalized form of Martin's (2007)
[Morkovin](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md) density scaling, with
inner and outer interpolation coordinates. Applied with the strong
Reynolds analogy (SRA) thermodynamic laws to a Mach 2.9 adiabatic layer,
the flow recovers within about $10\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ and matches the
experiment of Ringuette et al. For hypersonic cold walls the SRA fails, so
the authors derive a temperature law from the instantaneous Gaviglio-type
relation $T'\approx(\partial\langle T\rangle/\partial\langle u\rangle)u'$
combined with the generalized analogy, and a density law from the equation
of state keeping the $\rho'T'$ correlation, which guarantees positive
density. At Mach 7.25 ($T_w=562$ K) the simulation is stable and matches
the experiment of Williams et al.; RMS ratios and PDFs show the new laws
closer to the DNS than the SRA ones. The recovery distance grows to about
$24\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$, and two further libraries at $Re_\tau=350$ and 186
show that the friction-Reynolds-number mismatch between library and target
dominates it: with matched $Re_\tau$ it shortens to about
$11\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$, through less spanwise distortion of outer-layer
structures.

# Key claims

1. Inflow generation methods fall into four classes, transition-inducing, recycling–rescaling, synthetic turbulence and turbulence-library based, each with shortcomings (§I, refs 1–2). **Stated**
2. The transition-inducing method needs at least 50 boundary-layer thicknesses to reach fully developed turbulence (Mullenix et al.) (§I, ref 6). **Stated**
3. Schlüter et al. used a library from channel flow at $Re_h=2280$ to simulate $Re_h=6900$, fully developed after $10h$ ($h$ the channel half-width) (§I, ref 22). **Stated**
4. Only Martin (2007) had applied a library to DNS of a hypersonic flat-plate boundary layer, with incompressible fluctuations rescaled by Morkovin's hypothesis; Martin's thermodynamic laws "have obvious defects in hypersonic flows and could cause simulation instability" (§I). **Stated**
5. ACANS reproduces Pirozzoli's Mach 2.25 DNS in $C_f$ (a slight difference in the transition region is attributed to a different blowing–suction frequency), van Driest velocity, temperature–velocity relation, Reynolds stresses and $k$ budget (§II B, Figs. 2–4). **Shown**
6. At the verification station $Re_\tau$ is "nearly 800"; at the library plane $x=0.224$ m, $\delta=2.08$ mm and $Re_\tau\approx770$ (§II B, §III A). **Shown**
7. The library stores 65 536 planes at $\Delta t=5\times10^{-9}$ s, about $3\times10^{-4}$ s or $4.38\,\delta/u_\tau$ (§III A, Table IV). **Shown**
8. Only velocity is stored, because no accurate scaling laws exist between library and target thermodynamic fluctuations; $u_\tau$ and the mean density profile are stored too (§III A). **Stated**
9. According to Duan et al., Morkovin's hypothesis holds for Mach numbers below 12 and wall-to-recovery temperature ratios above 0.12 (§III B, refs 24–26). **Stated**
10. Mach 2.9 adiabatic case (RANS inflow at $\delta=6$ mm, $Re_\tau=340$): $C_f$ drops sharply behind the inflow, then rises to a peak and a plateau; the recovery distance $L_{\mathrm{rec}}$, from the inflow to the $C_f$ peak, is about $10\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ (§III C, Fig. 7). **Shown**
11. At station A the Mach 2.9 DNS gives $\delta^*=2.46$ mm, $\theta=0.48$ mm, $H=5.13$, $10^3C_f=2.14$, $u_\tau=31.2$ m/s against the experiment's 2.36, 0.43, 5.49, 2.17, 32.9 (Table II). **Shown**
12. The inflow $u'_{\mathrm{rms}}$ imposed by the library is slightly below the experiment but recovers; at station A its peak is at $y/\delta\approx0.05$ with $u'_{\mathrm{rms}}/u_e\approx0.13$ (§III C, Fig. 8). **Shown**
13. The SRA temperature relation (Eq. 18) needs $Pr=1$, negligible total-temperature fluctuation and an adiabatic wall, all violated in hypersonic cold-wall layers; Martin et al. report that the RMS ratio of its two sides departs from unity as Mach number and wall heat transfer grow (§IV A, refs 24–25). **Stated**
14. The new temperature law (Eq. 23) follows from Yu & Xu's instantaneous form $T'\approx(\partial\langle T\rangle/\partial\langle u\rangle)u'$ with the generalized analogy of Zhang et al. (§IV A, Eqs. 21–23). **Derived** (see Printed slips)
15. The new density law (Eq. 25) follows from the equation of state keeping $\rho'T'$ and dropping $p'$; its curves have asymptotes $\rho'/\langle\rho\rangle=-1$ and $T'/\langle T\rangle=-1$, so the density stays positive (§IV A–B, Fig. 10). **Derived**
16. With Eq. (19) at the Mach 7.25 inflow, $\rho'/\langle\rho\rangle<-1$ occurs at $y/\delta=0.8$, a negative density (§IV B, Fig. 10c). **Shown**
17. The Mach 7.25 DNS ($T_w=562$ K, RANS inflow at $\delta=7.5$ mm, $Re_\tau=155$, $\Delta t=2\times10^{-8}$ s) with the improved library is stable and fully developed before the buffer zone; $L_{\mathrm{rec}}\approx24\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ (§IV B, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
18. At $\delta=9.5$ mm the Mach 7.25 DNS agrees with the experiment in $C_f$, van Driest velocity and Morkovin-scaled $u'_{\mathrm{rms}}$, and with the generalized analogy (Figs. 12–14). **Shown**
19. In the Mach 7.25 layer the RMS total-temperature fluctuation reaches about 10%, breaking the assumption of Eq. (18) (§IV B, Fig. 15a). **Shown**
20. The SRA ratio $\Gamma_1$ (Eq. 26) is about 0.8 over most of the layer, the new-law ratio $\Gamma_2$ (Eq. 27) close to unity (Fig. 15b). **Shown**
21. At $y^+=10$, 20, 100 the $\rho'T'/(\langle\rho\rangle\langle T\rangle)$ term grows with $y^+$ and at $y^+=100$ exceeds the temperature and density fluctuations in magnitude; $|p'/\langle p\rangle|<0.2$ is the high-probability event at all three heights, so dropping $p'$ is justified (§IV B, Fig. 16). **Shown**
22. Density fluctuations from Eq. (19) depart increasingly from the DNS with $y^+$ and include values $\rho'/\langle\rho\rangle<-1$ at $y^+=20$ and 100; Eq. (25) avoids them and matches the DNS PDF better (Fig. 17). **Shown**
23. Martin (2007) multiplied the SRA right-hand side by a positive factor below 0.8 to prevent negative density, which worsens the PDF of positive density fluctuations (§IV B). **Stated**
24. The Morkovin scaling matches the peak of $u'_{\mathrm{rms}}$ and the interpolation its position; the transformed library agrees with the Mach 7.25 DNS for $y/\delta<0.2$ but shows a "trough region" around $y/\delta\approx0.4$, which the authors attribute to the interpolation and so to the Reynolds number ("we guess that the Reynolds number is the dominant parameter") (§IV C, Fig. 18). **Shown** (inference labelled a guess)
25. With libraries at $Re_\tau=350$ and 186 the recovery distance shortens from $22.7\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ (Ma7.25-1) to about $11\delta_{\mathrm{in}}$ (Ma7.25-2), Ma7.25-3 being the same; Ma7.25-3 matches the experimental van Driest velocity best (§IV C, Fig. 19). **Shown**
26. Mass-flux autocorrelations at the inflow are alike for the three libraries at $y^+=20$, but at $y/\delta=0.3$ show a spanwise distortion that grows with the $Re_\tau$ mismatch and vanishes when $Re_\tau$ matches (Figs. 21–22). **Shown**
27. Mechanism: the mapping scales $z$ by $\delta_\nu$ everywhere but $y$ by $\delta_\nu$ (inner) and $\delta$ (outer); since $Re_\tau=\delta/\delta_\nu$, unless $\delta_{\mathrm{TFL}}/\delta_{\mathrm{target}}\approx\delta_{\nu,\mathrm{TFL}}/\delta_{\nu,\mathrm{target}}$ the outer-layer structures are stretched differently in the two directions (§IV C). **Derived**
28. Within $y<0.2\delta$ the three libraries match the reference Reynolds stresses at the inflow; for $0.2\delta<y<0.5\delta$ the two higher-$Re_\tau$ libraries show a trough, and one $\delta$ downstream the stresses of the $Re_\tau=770$ library decay, causing the $C_f$ drop and the longer recovery (Fig. 23). **Shown**
29. Matching $Re_\tau$ between library and target is the dominant factor for the recovery distance; the improved method recovers in about 11 inflow thicknesses (§V). **Shown**

The method in registry notation (§III B, §IV A; $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the
time and spanwise mean of Eq. 14, primes its fluctuations, subscript $e$
the boundary-layer edge, $T_r$ the recovery temperature, $s_{\mathrm{RA}}$ the Reynolds
analogy factor $1.16$, $Pr=0.72$). The velocity mapping (Eq. 15) is
$$\sqrt{\frac{\langle\rho\rangle(y^\sharp)}{\langle\rho\rangle_w}}\frac{u_i'(z^+,y^\sharp)}{u_\tau}\bigg|_{\mathrm{target}}=\sqrt{\frac{\langle\rho\rangle(y^\sharp)}{\langle\rho\rangle_w}}\frac{u_i'(z^+,y^\sharp)}{u_\tau}\bigg|_{\mathrm{TFL}},$$
with $y^\sharp=y/\delta_\nu$ in the inner and $y/\delta$ in the outer
layer, blended by $\frac12\{1+\tanh[4(y-y_{\mathrm{mid}})/(y_{\mathrm{wake}}-y_{\mathrm{log}})]\}$,
$y_{\mathrm{log}}=0.2\delta$, $y_{\mathrm{wake}}=0.5\delta$ (Eqs. 16–17).
The generalized analogy (Eqs. 11–13) is
$$\frac{\langle T\rangle}{T_e}=\frac{T_w}{T_e}+\frac{T_r-T_w}{T_e}f_s\Big(\frac{\langle u\rangle}{u_e}\Big)+\frac{T_e-T_r}{T_e}\Big(\frac{\langle u\rangle}{u_e}\Big)^2,\qquad f_s(\varphi)=(1-s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr)\varphi^2+s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr\,\varphi,$$
the SRA laws are $T'/\langle T\rangle\approx-(\gamma-1)M^2u'/\langle u\rangle$
and $\rho'/\langle\rho\rangle=-T'/\langle T\rangle$ (Eqs. 18–19), and the new
laws are (Eqs. 23, 25, as printed)
$$T'=2\frac{\langle u\rangle}{u_e^2}\big[T_e-(1-s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr)T_w-s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr\,T_r\big]u',\qquad \frac{\rho'}{\langle\rho\rangle}=-\frac{T'/\langle T\rangle}{1+T'/\langle T\rangle}.$$

# Printed slips

- Eq. (22) prints $\partial\langle T\rangle/\partial\langle u\rangle=2(T_r-T_w)(1-s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr)\langle u\rangle/u_e^2+2(T_e-T_r)\langle u\rangle/u_e^2$. Differentiating the paper's own Eqs. (11)–(12) gives in addition the term $(T_r-T_w)s_{\mathrm{RA}}Pr/u_e$ from the linear part of $f_s$; the second line of Eq. (22), Eq. (23) and the ratio $\Gamma_2$ (Eq. 27) carry the same form *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. Whether the announced erratum concerns this is not known from the file.
- The library thickness is $\delta=2.08$ mm in §III A and Table IV, "$\delta=2.06$ mm" in the §III C summary.
- The recovery distance of the Mach 7.25 run with the $Re_\tau=770$ library is "$L_r\approx24\delta_{in}$" in §IV B and "$22.7\delta_{in}$" for Ma7.25-1 in §IV C.
- The precursor wall is isothermal at 322 K in §II B and Fig. 5; §III B says the library was "collected under specific conditions such as the $Ma_e=2.25$ and the adiabatic wall".
- Eq. (2) prints the dilatation term as $\frac23\mu\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}\delta_{ij}$ (index $i$ reused).
- §III A: the fluctuation is obtained by subtracting "$\bar V(y,z)$", while Eq. (14) defines $\bar V(y)$.
- Table II prints the column header "$\delta$ (mm)" twice; ref. 37 prints a coauthor "Finleyt". The Sutherland constant is not printed.
