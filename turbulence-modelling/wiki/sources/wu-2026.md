# Summary

Wu & Lele (2026) take up the gap between the a priori and a posteriori
performance of [neural-network subgrid-scale models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md)
trained on filtered DNS, framed by [a priori and a posteriori
testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md), and read it as
a distribution shift between training data and LES. Two spatial sources are
named. On the output side, subgrid-stress statistics depend on the filter
shape, and the filter implied by an LES grid and its numerics (e.g.
two-thirds dealiasing) differs from the filter, often a box, used to build
training data. On the input side, higher powers of the velocity-gradient
tensor are more sensitive to aliasing and to the numerical method. Shift
from time advancement is set aside.

The model is a hybrid [tensor-basis neural network](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md)
with an 8-term basis and a graph-network front end (about 24,000
parameters), trained on Johns Hopkins database forced
[isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
($Re_\lambda=433$) and [channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
($Re_\tau=1000$). Two remedies are tested together: multi-filter data
augmentation (box; a compact raised-cosine filter; box followed by a
two-thirds spectral cutoff) and "simple" inputs that drop the second and
third velocity-gradient invariants. A priori all networks agree within
5 %. A posteriori, in a Fourier-spectral and a sixth-order compact solver,
box-only networks with complex inputs pile up energy at high wavenumbers
and depend strongly on small architecture changes; two- and three-filter
networks are more accurate and robust, and simple inputs make all variants
consistent. One three-filter network runs in both solvers without
retraining. In [wall-modelled LES](/methods/wall-modelled-les.md) of channel
flow with the "classic" wall model of Bou-Zeid et al. (2005), whose form is
not printed (the paper does not say whether it is an
[equilibrium wall model](/methods/equilibrium-wall-model.md)), the box-only network fails and
the two-filter network reproduces mean velocity and Reynolds stresses.

Baselines are static [Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md) and the
[sigma model](/methods/sigma-model.md), both over-dissipative in coarse
isotropic turbulence; dynamic Smagorinsky ([dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md))
is set aside because it may need clipping. The paper argues against
learning solver peculiarities through [reinforcement
learning](/methods/reinforcement-learning-closure.md) or
[model-consistent training](/methods/model-consistent-training.md), which it
expects to transfer poorly between solvers (see
[discretization-consistent closure](/definitions/discretization-consistent-closure.md)),
and instead exposes the network to several plausible filters. The
LES filter is called "implicit", a product of grid and numerics, in the
filter sense of [implicit LES](/definitions/implicit-les.md), not the
no-model sense. Under- and over-dissipation of the models ([numerical
dissipation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md)) are the main
diagnostic; the PDFs of the subgrid energy flux per training filter are
shown without a discussion of [energy backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md).

# Key claims

$P_A$, $Q_A$, $R_A$ (registry §11) are the invariants of the filtered
velocity-gradient tensor $\bar A_{ij}=\partial_j\bar u_i$ (trace,
$\frac12(P_A^2-\mathrm{tr}\,\bar A^2)$, determinant). Page-local symbols: SSE the sum spectral
error $\sum_k\lvert\log E_{\mathrm{DNS}}(k)-\log E_{\mathrm{LES}}(k)\rvert$
over $k\in[0,24]$ (spectral solver) or $[0,35]$ (compact solver); $F(r)$
the longitudinal flatness of filtered velocity increments; DSCF the
discrete spatial cosine filter $G(r)=\frac1\Delta(1+\cos(2\pi r/\Delta))$
for $\lvert r\rvert\le\Delta/2$; BTF a box filter followed by a two-thirds
dealiasing cutoff; $h_{\mathrm{DNS}}$ the DNS grid spacing; network names
NN-[filters]-[Complex|Simple]-[Original|ALN]-[G|L] (ALN: three extra
layer-normalization layers; G/L: global or local input normalization).

1. The a priori/a posteriori gap of neural-network subgrid models can be read as a distribution shift between training data and LES, with two spatial mechanisms: explicitly filtered inputs differ from implicitly filtered LES fields, and subgrid-stress statistics differ between filtering operations (§1). **Stated**
2. Supervised models trained with a single filter (often a box) do not learn solver peculiarities but "can now overfit to filter peculiarities", causing a priori against a posteriori distribution shift (§1). **Stated**
3. Reinforcement-learning and end-to-end (differentiable-solver) approaches learn the combined effect of solver numerics and physics, so they are unlikely to transfer to other solvers, and training is costlier because LES runs during training (§1). **Stated**
4. A spectral cutoff (sinc) filter is not appropriate for general subgrid-stress modelling, because a filter oscillatory in physical space creates spurious small scales (§2.1). **Stated** (citing Sagaut 2006)
5. The DSCF is a single-mode approximation to the spectral cutoff with cutoff frequency $1/\Delta$; a $16h_{\mathrm{DNS}}$ filter needs a 33-point DSCF (cutoff $0.5/16=0.03125$) and a $32h_{\mathrm{DNS}}$ filter a 65-point DSCF ($0.0156$) (§3.0.1). **Derived**
6. The 33-point DSCF matches the initial decay of the box filter but suppresses its spectral oscillations (peak 0.028 against 0.22), and in the normalized angular frequency range 0.2–0.4 it is "over 4-5 times lower than the Gaussian" (§3.0.1, Fig. 1). **Shown**
7. Two-thirds dealiasing on top of box filtering "completely changes the subgrid stress field": higher intensity, extra oscillations and far larger variance of the $\tau_{11}$, $\tau_{12}$ and $\Pi$ PDFs than box or DSCF; box and DSCF fields look alike; box has the largest PDF peak, then DSCF, then BTF after a large gap (§3.2.2, Fig. 4). **Shown**
8. There is "no a priori performance degradation" from training on two or more filters rather than one: all RMSE, dissipation RMSE and correlation values lie within 5 % of each other, and complex against simple inputs makes a negligible a priori difference (§4, Table 2). **Shown**
9. Spectral solver, forced isotropic turbulence ($64^3$, $Re_\lambda=433$): static Smagorinsky ($C=0.18$, SSE 16.6) and sigma ($C=1.3$, SSE 13.34) are over-dissipative; NN-Box-Complex-Original-G is under-dissipative with high-wavenumber pile-up (SSE 18.87); NN-BoxBTF-Complex-Original-G best represents the spectra (SSE 4.0) (§5.1.1, Table 3, Fig. 5). **Shown**
10. The traditional models reproduce the longitudinal flatness better than the networks, attributed to over-dissipation lowering the variance in the denominator of $F(r)$; the networks reproduce the PDF of the resolved enstrophy production $\bar\omega_i\bar S_{ij}\bar\omega_j$ far better (§5.1.1, Fig. 5b–c). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
11. A BTF-only network beats a box-only network in the spectral solver (SSE 4.32 against 18.87); single-filter networks (box or BTF) remain worst for small-scale intermittency, and multi-filter networks are closest to filtered DNS overall (§5.1.1, Fig. 6, Table 3). **Shown**
12. With the ALN architecture the box-only network improves markedly (SSE 5.56), so single-filter results are "very sensitive to the neural network architecture"; two-filter results are robust (SSE 3.93 against 4.0) (§5.1.2, Fig. 7, Table 3). **Shown**
13. With simple inputs, spectral-solver spectra are consistent across all variants (SSE 3.68–4.98); two-filter networks still predict a higher, closer-to-DNS small-scale flatness; training on two filters "will often do no worse than training on one filter" (§5.1.3, Fig. 8, Table 3). **Shown**
14. Compact solver (sixth order, no dealiasing): the box-only complex network shows "an extreme buildup of high wavenumber energy" (SSE 32.58); box+DSCF gives SSE 11.93; the DSCF-only network is over-dissipative (SSE 35.33); the three-filter network (SSE 14.22) beats either single-filter network (§5.2.1, Table 4, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
15. One three-filter network (box, BTF, DSCF) runs in both solvers without retraining and outperforms the box-only network in both; it is not optimal, having trained on filters not applicable to the solver (BTF in a solver without dealiasing), possibly the cause of its over-predicted enstrophy-production tails in the compact solver (§5.2.1, §7, §8). **Shown**
16. Compact solver with ALN: both one- and two-filter networks predict the spectra accurately (SSE 9.50 and 5.59); the one-filter network's small-scale flatness changes strongly between architectures (§5.2.2, Fig. 11, Table 4). **Shown**
17. Compact solver with simple inputs: box-only networks also give reasonable spectra and the SSE spread is smaller (7.56–15.84); local normalization slightly under-predicts the enstrophy-production tails (§5.2.3, Fig. 12, Table 4). **Shown**
18. On a coarser $32^3$ grid ($\Delta=32h_{\mathrm{DNS}}$) two-filter training again corrects the under-dissipation of the box-only complex network; the sigma model is over-dissipative but less than at $64^3$ "despite using the same coefficient"; no model captures the enstrophy-production PDF accurately at this resolution (§6.1, Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
19. Channel flow at $Re_\tau=1000$ ($128\times96\times32$, Bou-Zeid et al. 2005 wall model, spectral solver): the box-only complex network "completely fails" with unphysical spectra and static Smagorinsky shows high-wavenumber pile-up; sigma and the box+BTF network reproduce the spectra, and the two-filter mean profile "lines up very well with the filtered DNS profile" (§6.2.1, Fig. 15). **Shown**
20. In the channel, the box-only network over-predicts the Reynolds stresses, especially $R_{11}$ and the wall-normal $R_{22}$; the box+BTF network gives "oftentimes ... the most accurate profiles even when compared to the Sigma model" (§6.2.1, Fig. 16). **Shown**
21. The ALN layers act "as a stabilizer that prevents the single-filter model from producing wildly inaccurate results, essentially masking its lack of robust physical knowledge"; two-filter training still improves $R_{12}$ and $R_{22}$ under ALN (§6.2.2, Figs. 17–18). **Shown**
22. Simple inputs reduce the input distribution shift because higher powers of the velocity-gradient tensor "suffer more from numerical artifacts such as aliasing, especially at the grid cutoff resolution" (§1, §3.1, §7). **Stated**, supported by **Shown** comparisons (Figs. 8, 12, 14, 19–20)
23. Combining multi-filter augmentation with simple inputs gives the most robust model; the trends hold across small architecture and normalization perturbations but come from two high-order solvers and "will need to be further evaluated on lower order solvers" (§7, §8). **Shown** (scope stated)
24. SSE is a log-spectrum metric, "artificially sensitive to deviations in the higher-wavenumber, dissipative range", which can rank dissipative spectra worse while under-weighting pile-up, so visual inspection is still needed (§5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **§1, p. 2:** "$Q = 0.5(P^2 - \mathrm{trace}(V_{ij}^2)))$" has an extra closing parenthesis.
- **Network names:** the three-filter network is "NN-BoxBTFDSCF-Complex-Original-G" in Table 1, "NN-BoxDSCFBTF-Complex-Original-G" in Table 2 and "NN-BoxDSCFBTF-Comp-Orig-G" in Tables 3–4.
- **Local-normalization networks:** Tables 3–4 list "NN-BoxBTF-Simple-Original-L" and "NN-BoxDSCF-Simple-Original-L", while Table 1 lists the two-filter local-normalization networks as "NN-BoxBTF-Simple-ALN-L" and "NN-BoxDSCF-Simple-ALN-L".
- **Table 2** lists 10 of the 18 networks of Table 1, while §4 speaks of "all" networks.
- **Conclusion (§8):** "A priori, neural networks suffer no performance degradation when trained on one filter as compared to two filters" reverses the comparison of the abstract and §4 (two filters against one).
- **Figure captions 5–12** write $\omega_iS_{ij}\omega_j$ without the overbars of the text.
- **References:** "Maejimam, Soju" (also "Maejimam & Kawai 2024" in §1) for Maejima; Xie et al. 2020a "Physical Review of Fluids" and 2020b "Physical of Fluids"; Kim et al. 2024 "doi: 10.48550. arXiv preprint arxiv.2307.13533"; Beck & Kurz 2021 journal printed "Surveys for Applied Mathematics and Mechanics".
