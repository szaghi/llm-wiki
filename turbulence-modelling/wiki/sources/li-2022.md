# Summary

Li & Liu (2022) test which modifications of the
[Spalart–Allmaras model](/methods/spalart-allmaras-model.md) best predict
the three-dimensional hub-corner separation of axial compressors. They run
steady RANS with CFL3D, which they modified for internal flows, on the
Cambridge/Rolls-Royce prescribed-velocity-distribution (PVD) linear cascade
($Re=2.3\times10^5$, zero incidence) and on the transonic NASA Rotor 67,
against experiment. The variants are of three kinds. Numerical or scope
fixes (SA-neg, SA-noft2) change nothing measurable. Production
modifications include the Dacles-Mariani rotation correction (SA-R), the
Spalart–Shur rotation/curvature correction (SA-RC), a Kato–Launder-type
production (SA-KL), a pure strain production (SA-S, new here) and the
helicity correction (SA-Helicity) of the authors' group. Nonlinear
constitutive relations, QCR2000 and QCR2013, depart from the linear
[Boussinesq hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md). Blends
combine each production variant with a QCR.

Baseline SA largely over-predicts the corner separation, with a peak eddy
viscosity ratio above 1100. SA-R, SA-RC, SA-KL and SA-S barely change it.
The QCR models improve on SA but still over-predict the separation.
SA-Helicity is the most accurate and has the lowest eddy viscosity ratio
(about 400), although its correction increases production. The authors
explain this by the nonlinearity of the system, and motivate the correction
by a correlation between helicity and
[energy backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md). Blends inherit the
behaviour of their production model, and the anisotropy invariants of the
QCR2013 blends barely differ. The authors conclude that the QCR and the
production modifications superpose with little interference. On Rotor 67,
SA under-predicts the stable operating range by about 14% and SA-QCR2013 by
about 7.9%, while SA-Helicity and SA-Helicity-QCR2013 predict the stall
point "very accurately". The authors conclude that non-equilibrium
transport in large vortices matters more than anisotropy for this flow,
but that both should be modelled.

# Key claims

1. Turbulence-model choice "contributes to a large part of numerical errors" in RANS of turbomachinery, because common models assume local production–destruction equilibrium or isotropy, while turbulence in massive separation and vortical turbomachinery flow is strongly non-equilibrium and anisotropic (§1). **Stated**
2. SA modifications fall into three kinds: scope and numerics fixes that barely change results (trip term, SA-neg, SA-noft2); production/destruction modifications for non-equilibrium transport (SA-R, SA-KL, SA-Helicity, SA-RC); nonlinear constitutive relations replacing the Boussinesq relation (QCR2000, QCR2013) (§1). **Stated**
3. The SA-S model, $\tilde S=\lvert S\rvert+\tilde\nu f_{v2}/(\kappa^2d^2)$ (Eq. 18), is "not mentioned in the existing references" and is tested here for the first time (§2.3). **Stated** (its results **Shown**)
4. SA-neg gives "exactly the same value" as SA on the PVD cascade (double-precision converged): the negative-$\tilde\nu$ branch is inactive in this case (§3.1). **Shown**
5. Every model and its noft2 version differ by less than 0.1%: $f_{t2}$ is negligible with a turbulent inlet at $Re>2\times10^5$ (§3.1). **Shown**
6. SA over-estimates the outflow angle and SA-R is worse; SA-RC is less accurate than SA at 50–65% and 80–95% span; SA-KL is close to SA and SA-S slightly further off; SA-QCR2000, SA-QCR2013 and SA-Helicity are better than SA (§3.1; Fig. 3). **Shown**
7. Near the blade root (90–100% span) SA-Helicity matches the outflow angle best; the QCR models differ from experiment at 80–100% span, so the nonlinear constitutive relation alone is "still not adequate" (§3.1). **Shown**
8. At 54% span SA, SA-R, SA-RC, SA-KL and SA-S give a $C_p$ well below experiment on the suction side beyond 40% axial chord; SA-Helicity is best on the pressure side and the QCR models on the rear suction side. At 89% span the QCR models lose accuracy on the suction side and SA-Helicity is best there (Fig. 4). **Shown**
9. Pitchwise mass-averaged total-pressure loss: SA-KL and SA-S equal SA, SA-R improves minimally, SA-RC little; the QCR models give a smaller separation but still over-estimate the loss (QCR2000 slightly better than QCR2013); SA-Helicity is close to experiment and best near the root (Fig. 5). **Shown**
10. On the measurement slice SA-Helicity, SA-QCR2000 and SA-QCR2013 reproduce the size of the corner separation; SA-Helicity's peak loss is below experiment; the QCR peaks are closer but spread spanwise; SA-R's core peak is "about 7% larger" than SA's although its averaged loss is lower (§3.1; Fig. 6). **Shown**
11. Relative displacement thickness at the trailing edge: SA-Helicity rises from 70% span and peaks at 91%, a little above experiment; the QCR peaks are "twice of the experimental data"; SA-RC's lower value comes from an over-predicted mid-span two-dimensional separation (§3.1; Fig. 7). **Shown**
12. Table 3 grades SA, SA-neg, SA-noft2, SA-R, SA-KL and SA-S "Overpredict" near mid-span and "Largely overpredict" in the corner; SA-RC "largely overpredicts" both; SA-Helicity "Slightly underpredict" near mid-span and "Accurate" in the corner; the QCR models "Slightly overpredict" and "Overpredict" (Table 3). **Shown**
13. SA's peak $\mu_t/\mu$ exceeds 1100, SA-QCR2013's is much lower and SA-Helicity's lowest (about 400); "Failure of the SA model is attributed to the overprediction of $\mu_T/\mu$ and the key point of EVM modification should be reducing the turbulent viscosity" (§3.2; Fig. 8). **Shown** (attribution **Stated**)
14. Although the helicity correction increases eddy-viscosity production, SA-Helicity gives the smallest $\mu_t/\mu$; the authors attribute this physically to strong backscatter in rotating turbulence and mathematically to the nonlinearity of the equation: more production enhances mixing between passage and separated flow, suppresses the separation and so avoids further production (§3.2). **Shown** (mechanism **Stated**)
15. SA-KL and SA-S have no significant effect because $\lvert\Omega\rvert$ exceeds $\lvert S\rvert$ only moderately in the corner vortex, unlike strong vortices such as the tip-leakage vortex (§3.2). **Stated**
16. "Just decreasing the production term (SA-R) is not effective"; rotation/curvature modifications "hardly work in the complex corner separation flow"; enhancing production "probably is an effective method for model modifications in large-scale vortices" (§3.2). **Shown** (as a general recommendation **Stated**)
17. SA-KL-QCR2000 and SA-S-QCR2000 equal SA-QCR2000 (likewise for QCR2013); the QCR2000 and QCR2013 versions of each model give nearly the same outflow angle and displacement thickness, and QCR2013 always a slightly higher loss (§4.1; Figs. 10–13). **Shown**
18. SA-Helicity-QCR2013 is the best blend: better than SA-Helicity at 70–80% span (loss) and 50–90% span (outflow angle), slightly worse near the root; the Helicity blends under-estimate the high-loss region, and SA-QCR2000 remains most accurate for the peak loss at the vortex core (§4.1; Figs. 10–12). **Shown**
19. In SA and SA-QCR2000 the modelled Reynolds stress is traceless because $k$ is absent, so $b_{ij}$ cannot be formed; only the QCR2013 blends are analysed for anisotropy (§4.2; Eqs. 35, 38). **Derived**
20. The Lumley invariants of the QCR2013 blends show "barely no difference"; the authors conclude that anisotropy effects are "relatively independent" of the production modifications, and the two influences "are superimposed but have little interference" (§4.2; Figs. 14–15). **Shown** (independence **Stated**)
21. The nonlinear constitutive relation is "not as important as the modifications on turbulent non-equilibrium transport characteristics" in corner separation, and "the performance of a blending model mainly depends on the EVM utilized" (§4.2). **Shown**
22. NASA Rotor 67: SA's last converged point is $\dot m/\dot m_{\mathrm{choke}}=0.902$ against an experimental smallest mass flow of about 0.886, so "the stable operating range is underestimated by about 14%"; SA-QCR2013 deviates by about 7.9% with a lower total-pressure ratio; SA-Helicity and SA-Helicity-QCR2013 predict the stall point "very accurately" (§5; Fig. 17a). **Shown**
23. All models over-predict the Rotor 67 adiabatic efficiency; the helicity correction lowers it by about 0.5%, toward the measurements (§5; Fig. 17b). **Shown**
24. Rotor 67 spanwise profiles: SA under-estimates total pressure (most above 60% span) and total temperature, SA-QCR2013 even more; SA-Helicity is best above 70% span, attributed to its tip-leakage-vortex prediction, and SA-Helicity-QCR2013 best near mid-span (§5; Figs. 18–19). **Shown**
25. The difference between SA-Helicity and SA-Helicity-QCR2013 "is similar to that between the SA and SA-QCR2013 models in every respect", further evidence that the two corrections superpose (§5). **Shown**
26. Turbulence modelling for turbomachinery should treat non-equilibrium in large vortices and anisotropy together (§6). **Stated**

# Printed slips

1. §3.1: "using the nonlinear constitutive relation modification along is still not adequate": "along" for "alone".
2. §1 calls QCR2000 a "more accurate estimation of the turbulent stresses", §4.2 "designed to obtain more accurate estimation of the turbulent viscosity [8]"; Eq. (23) changes the stress–strain relation and leaves $\mu_T$ unchanged *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
3. §2.3 calls $h$ the "helicity density", while Eq. (20) defines the normalized helicity $h=\lvert\omega_iu_i\rvert/(\Omega U)$.
4. §3.1 writes the trip term "$f_{t1}U^2$" without defining $f_{t1}$ or this $U$ (Eq. 22 defines $U$ as the velocity magnitude).
5. Ref. [20] (Nichols): "AIAA Scitech Forum, 2019 Jan 7-11, San Diego, California, 2009, AIAA Paper 2019-1342": the trailing "2009" contradicts the conference date and paper number.
6. The paper gives the value of $c_{v1}=7.1$ only; the other SA constants and the functions $f_{v2}$, $f_n$ are not defined (incompleteness, not an error).
