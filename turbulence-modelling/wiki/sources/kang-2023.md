# Summary

Kang et al. (2023) build a fully connected
[neural-network SGS model](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md) for
incompressible [LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) that predicts
the six components of $\tau_{ij}$ at a grid point from single-point
resolved inputs. Two input sets are compared: the resolved strain rate
$\bar S_{ij}$ alone, and $\bar S_{ij}$ together with the resolved stress
$L_{ij}$ of the Germano identity, the latter mirroring the dynamic mixed
models of the [scale-similarity family](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md).
The network has two hidden layers of 12 neurons and is trained on filtered
DNS of forced [homogeneous isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)
($Re_\lambda=106$ and/or 286) and of [turbulent channel flow](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
at $Re_\tau=180$.

With $\bar S_{ij}$ alone the network predicts a stress aligned with the
strain, predicts no [backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md), and
performs a posteriori almost exactly like the
[dynamic Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md). Adding $L_{ij}$
doubles the a priori correlation with the true stress, reproduces the
distribution of the SGS energy flux $\Pi$ including backscatter, and gives
stable LES without averaging or clipping, which the algebraic models of the
[dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md) need. Generalization to
untrained Reynolds numbers and grids rests on normalizing inputs and
outputs so that their distributions do not change; the output scale is the
volume-averaged norm of the [gradient-model](/methods/gradient-model.md)
stress in isotropic turbulence and of $L_{ij}$ in channel flow. The mixed
network outperforms one- and two-parameter dynamic mixed models a
posteriori despite lower a priori correlations, a case for the
[a priori against a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md)
distinction, and costs 0.71 times the dynamic Smagorinsky stress
evaluation. In channel flow a model trained at $Re_\tau=180$ on $48^3$
predicts $Re_\tau=395$ and other grids better than dynamic Smagorinsky; a
model trained on isotropic turbulence only is stable but underpredicts the
mean velocity, judged against the [law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md)
line and the filtered DNS. The DNS forcing (negative-viscosity against
deterministic) is documented in an appendix.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols: $\mathrm{Corr}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the correlation
coefficient of Eq. (3.1); model names follow the source (S = strain input,
SL = strain and $L_{ij}$, number = training Reynolds number, H = isotropic,
C = channel); DSM, DMM, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$
are the dynamic Smagorinsky, one-parameter dynamic mixed and two-parameter
similarity and nonlinear mixed models.

1. With $\bar S_{ij}$ as only input the network predicts a stress aligned with $\bar S_{ij}$: $\mathrm{Corr}(\bar S_{12},\tau_{12})=-0.8217$ (S-106H) against $-0.2806$ (filtered DNS) and $-0.3992$ (SL-106H), a priori, forced isotropic turbulence at $Re_\lambda=106$ (§3.1, table 3). **Shown**
2. S-106H "only rescales the given input $\bar S_{ij}$ to predict the SGS stress while maintaining its alignment"; like eddy-viscosity models it predicts no backscatter (§3.1, figure 4a). **Shown**
3. A posteriori (forced, $Re_\lambda=106$, $48^3$) S-106H performs almost identically to DSM: both overestimate the energy spectrum for $k\le5$, neither predicts backscatter, both give narrower $\tau_{23}$ distributions than filtered DNS (§3.1, figures 5–6). **Shown**
4. Adding $L_{ij}$ as input (SL-106H) raises the a priori correlation of the deviatoric SGS stress with the true stress from 0.3509–0.3692 (S-106H) to 0.7040–0.7176 (table 4). **Shown**
5. SL-106H predicts backscatter accurately a posteriori; "consequently, LES becomes stable without any ad hoc procedures, which is a clear advantage over the algebraic dynamic SGS models" (§3.1, figures 6a, 8). **Shown**
6. Normalization decides generalization: if the distributions of the normalized inputs and outputs stay unchanged with Reynolds number and grid, the model generalizes; otherwise a prior probability shift (training and test output distributions differ) degrades it (§3.2). **Shown** (figures 10–12) / **Stated** (dataset-shift argument, cited)
7. Among the volume-averaged norms of $\tau^{\mathrm G}_{ij}$, of the modified Leonard term and of $L_{ij}$, the gradient-model term has the most constant ratio to the norm of the true stress over $Re_\lambda$ and over training-to-test filter-width ratios $1/2,1,2$; it is the output normalization for isotropic turbulence (§3.2, figure 10). **Shown**
8. SL-106H, SL-286H and SL-106+286H perform almost identically, so training at one Reynolds number and one grid suffices in isotropic turbulence (§3.2, figures 13–14). **Shown**
9. Trained only on forced turbulence, the SL models predict decaying isotropic turbulence ($Re_\lambda=106$ on $24^3$, $48^3$, $96^3$; $Re_\lambda=286$ on $128^3$) more accurately than DSM and S-106H in spectra and resolved kinetic-energy decay, stably (§3.2, table 5, figures 13–17). **Shown**
10. A priori, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$ has the best stress correlations (0.9104–0.9135), DSM the worst (0.2718–0.3006); SL-106H (0.7040–0.7176) is comparable with DMM (0.7057–0.7170); $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ is intermediate (0.5181–0.5353) (§3.3, table 6). **Shown**
11. Higher a priori correlation does not guarantee better LES: SL-106H gives an energy spectrum nearly identical to $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$ in forced turbulence and the most accurate spectra, kinetic-energy decay and SGS-dissipation decay of all models in decaying turbulence (§3.3, figures 19, 21, 22). **Shown**
12. The algebraic dynamic models (DSM, DMM, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$) are stable only with homogeneous-direction averaging and clipping of negative coefficients; without these they "significantly overestimate both backscatter and forward scatter" and "the excessive backscatter causes LES to diverge within tens of time steps" (§3.3, figure 20a). **Shown**
13. In decaying turbulence the similarity coefficient of $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ becomes negative and is clipped to zero after $t/T_{e,0}\ge1.62$, after which the model behaves like DSM; consistent with Anderson & Meneveau (1999), where this happens when the test-filter width exceeds the integral scale (§3.3). **Shown** / **Stated**
14. Two hidden layers of 12 neurons suffice; SGS-stress evaluation times DSM : 12 : 24 : 48 neurons = 1 : 0.71 : 0.89 : 1.42 (§3.4, figure 23). **Shown**
15. The algebraic dynamic mixed models need 1.6–2.2 times the DSM time to evaluate the SGS stress, the mixed network 0.71 times (figure 24: DSM 1.00, DMM 2.24, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$ 1.68, $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ 2.05, network 0.71), on 16 CPU cores, 100 time steps, forward pass inside the LES solver (§3.4). **Shown**
16. Earlier neural-network SGS models were reported 1.3 (Park & Choi 2021), 1.8 (Wang et al. 2018), 2.4 (Yuan et al. 2020), 15 (Xie & Wang 2019) and 256 (Xie et al. 2020a) times costlier than DSM (§1). **Stated**
17. Channel flow a priori ($Re_\tau=180$): DMM has the best correlation (0.8797 for $\tau_{12}$, 0.9053 for $\Pi$), DSM the worst (0.1855, 0.2689); SL-180C (0.6528, 0.7994) beats S-180C (0.4860, 0.6997); SL-106H, trained on isotropic turbulence only (0.4149, 0.4203), beats DSM (§3.5, table 9). **Shown**
18. At $Re_\tau=180$ on $48^3$, DSM overpredicts the mean velocity and $\bar u_{\mathrm{rms}}$ and underpredicts $\bar v_{\mathrm{rms}}$, $\bar w_{\mathrm{rms}}$; SL-180C predicts the mean velocity, rms fluctuations and Reynolds shear stress better than DSM and no model, and gives the most accurate $C_f$ (0.0092 against 0.0085 filtered DNS; DSM 0.0072; no model 0.0103) (§3.5, figure 25, table 11). **Shown**
19. SL-180C and SL-106H180C outperform DSM on untrained coarser ($32\times48\times32$) and finer ($64^3$) grids and at untrained $Re_\tau=395$ ($48^3$, $64^3$); S-180C underpredicts the mean velocity at $Re_\tau=395$, more on the finer grid (§3.5, figures 26–27). **Shown**
20. SL-106H180C, trained jointly on isotropic turbulence and channel flow, is accurate in both channel flow and decaying isotropic turbulence (§3.5, figure 29). **Shown**
21. SL-106H, trained on isotropic turbulence only, is stable in channel flow and predicts the fluctuations well but underpredicts the mean streamwise velocity, attributed to the vanishing mean off-diagonal SGS stress of isotropic training data; "training an ANN-SGS model for a turbulent channel flow dataset seems to be essential to accurately predict the mean streamwise velocity" (§3.5). **Shown** / **Stated** (cause)
22. Plane-wise against volume-wise normalization makes no significant difference for SL-180C; for SL-106H plane-wise normalization makes the mean SGS shear stress vanish at the wall and improves the mean velocity (§3.6, figure 30). **Shown**
23. The gradient-model term "is known to have an incorrect near-wall scaling (Liu et al. 1994)", so in channel flow the output is normalized by the norm of $L_{ij}$ (§3.2, §3.5). **Stated**
24. $|\mathrm{Corr}(\bar S_{12},\tau_{12})|$ of filtered DNS is 0.0765 in channel flow but about 0.28 in isotropic turbulence; $L_{12}$ has a near-wall distribution similar to $\tau_{12}$, while $\bar S_{12}$ increases toward the wall (§3.7, tables 12–13, figure 31). **Shown**
25. In SGS anisotropy-invariant maps, SL-180C-V reaches the two-component limit at the wall like filtered DNS; S-106H-V stays near the isotropic origin; SL-106H-V/P follow the axisymmetric limit with underestimated anisotropy (§3.7, figure 32). **Shown**
26. "The most important factor for generalization to an untrained turbulent channel flow is considered to be the use of $L_{ij}$" (§3.7). **Shown** (interpretation of figures 31–33)
27. Among similarity inputs added to $\bar S_{ij}$ (modified Leonard term, $L_{ij}$, gradient-model term), $L_{ij}$ is best overall; the gradient-model input best predicts the kinetic-energy decay but gives the largest spectral errors at $k=1$ ($48^3$) and $k=2$ ($24^3$), attributed to its Taylor-series truncation error on coarse grids (appendix C). **Shown** / **Stated** (cause)
28. Wall-unit normalization and isotropic normalization by $u'$ and $\lambda$ fail on untrained grids because they ignore the change of filter width; output normalization by the norm of $L_{ij}$ is comparable with that of $\tau^{\mathrm G}_{ij}$ at sufficient resolution but overpredicts spectra on $24^3$ (appendix E, figures 37–38). **Shown**
29. With the negative-viscosity forcing the authors obtain $Re_\lambda=122.9$ instead of the 164 reported by Langford & Moser (1999); the deterministic forcing of Machiels (1997) gives 164.7 and reproduces their statistics (appendix A, table 14). **Shown**
30. The networks stay accurate and stable in decaying turbulence started from a random-phase initial field rescaled at $t/T_{e,0}=3.87$ (appendix D, figure 36). **Shown**

The models in registry notation: DSM $\tau^d_{ij}=-2C\Delta^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij}$ with $C=\langle L_{ij}M_{ij}\rangle/\langle M_{ij}M_{ij}\rangle$ and $\hat\Delta/\Delta=\sqrt5$ (Eq. 3.2); $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{sim}}$ $\tau_{ij}=-2C_1\Delta^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij}+C_2L_{ij}$ (Eq. 3.5); $\mathrm{DTM}_{\mathrm{nl}}$ $\tau_{ij}=-2C_1\Delta^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij}+C_2\Delta^2\partial_k\bar u_i\partial_k\bar u_j$ (Eq. 3.8); gradient term $\tau^{\mathrm G}_{ij}=\tfrac1{12}\Delta^2\partial_k\bar u_i\partial_k\bar u_j$. **Gap:** the second term of $H_{ij}$ in the DMM coefficient (Eq. 3.4) is illegible as printed (colliding accents) and is not reconstructed here.
