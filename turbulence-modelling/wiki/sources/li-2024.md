# Summary

Li et al. (2024) propose a transformer-based neural operator (TNO) as a
data-driven surrogate of [large-eddy simulation](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md)
of incompressible turbulence
([neural-operator surrogates of LES](/methods/neural-operator-les-surrogate.md)).
The TNO does not model the subgrid stress $\tau_{ij}$, unlike the
[neural-network SGS models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md). It replaces
the whole time advance. It maps the filtered velocity at the five previous
time nodes to the next node, and it feeds its own predictions back to roll out
long times. The grid is reshaped into a sequence of $N_xN_yN_z$ tokens. The
sequence passes through multi-head linear attention layers with a
three-dimensional rotary position embedding, at a cost linear in the sequence
length, and then through a spectral regressor: a Fourier-neural-operator
layer and a feed-forward network. The grid is never downsampled, so the model
runs directly on a $64^3$ field.

All tests are [a posteriori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md).
Each statistic is averaged over 10 initial fields that were not in the
training set, and filtered [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
is the reference. The competitors are a modified Fourier neural operator (FNO),
LES with the dynamic [Smagorinsky model](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md), and
LES with the dynamic mixed model
([scale-similarity and mixed models](/methods/scale-similarity-model.md)).
Both LES models use the [dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md)
with domain-averaged coefficients.

- **Forced [isotropic turbulence](/benchmarks/homogeneous-isotropic-turbulence.md)**
  ($Re_\lambda\approx100$, $256^3$ DNS filtered to $64^3$): the TNO is
  comparable to the dynamic Smagorinsky model and better than the FNO and the
  mixed model. Without retraining it is also applied at $Re_\lambda\approx160$
  and $250$. There it gives better structure functions and increment PDFs
  than both LES models, but a worse spectrum than the dynamic Smagorinsky
  model.
- **[Temporal mixing layer](/benchmarks/temporal-mixing-layer.md)**: the TNO
  is the most accurate model and stays stable to $650$ momentum-thickness time
  units, while the FNO degrades after 200–400.

The TNO has about half the FNO's parameters. Per time node it costs about
1 GPU·s, against about $10^2$ CPU·s for the LES models. The paper does not
analyse backscatter: classical LES stability is attributed to the models'
"inherent dissipative characteristics", with no
[energy-backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md) analysis.

# Key claims

1. The TNO predicts the large-scale dynamics of LES of forced isotropic turbulence with accuracy comparable to dynamic-Smagorinsky LES, and better than the FNO and dynamic-mixed LES (Abstract; §V A 2; §VI). **Shown**
2. In the free-shear mixing layer the TNO is more accurate than the dynamic Smagorinsky, dynamic mixed and FNO models (Abstract; §V B 2; Figs. 16–21). **Shown**
3. The TNO has $268.6\times10^6$ parameters against $563.9\times10^6$ for the FNO (Tables I–II). **Shown**
4. The TNO is stable over long rollouts in the mixing layer, where the FNO fails: the FNO momentum thickness "becomes unreliable" after 400 time units and its directional kinetic energies diverge after 200 (§V B 2, Figs. 16–17). **Shown**
5. Linear attention cuts the cost of attention from $O(N_s^2N_d)$ (Eq. 19) to $O(N_sN_d^2)$ (Eq. 21), with $N_s=N_xN_yN_z$ the sequence length, which reaches $10^6$–$10^7$ in three-dimensional turbulence (§IV). **Stated** (complexity argument)
6. Attention is a discretized kernel integral (Eq. 20); with Fourier-basis kernels, learnable parameters and mode truncation "the aforementioned operation is equivalent to the fourier neural operator" (§IV, p. 13). **Stated**
7. The three-dimensional rotary position embedding makes the attention score depend only on relative positions (Eq. 23); "It can be proven", but no proof is given (§IV). **Stated**
8. Transformer surrogates that downsample the grid "tend to sacrifice high-frequency information of turbulence"; the TNO avoids downsampling and infers "at a million-grid scale" (§I, p. 5). **Stated**
9. Merging the velocity components and the five input time steps into channels, so the FFT acts on three-dimensional rather than four-dimensional data, improves the accuracy and cost of the authors' earlier FNO; all FNO results use this modified version (§III, p. 10). **Stated** (no ablation shown)
10. Isotropic-turbulence spectrum: the TNO beats the dynamic Smagorinsky model for $3\le k\le17$ and the latter is better for $k\ge18$; the mixed model underestimates low wavenumbers and "significantly overestimates" high ones (§V A 2, Fig. 6b). **Shown**
11. Structure functions: the mixed model overestimates them at short separations; at $t/T_E\approx6$ the FNO drifts as $r/\eta$ grows; the TNO and the dynamic Smagorinsky model are accurate at short and long times (§V A 2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
12. Velocity-increment PDFs: both LES models deviate beyond a normalized increment of 0.5, while the TNO follows filtered DNS (§V A 2, Fig. 8b). **Shown**
13. All four models give stable rms velocity and vorticity; the dynamic Smagorinsky model is closest to filtered DNS, partly because it applies "large-scale forces identical to fDNS at the two largest wavenumbers"; classical LES stability comes from "inherent dissipative characteristics" (§V A 2, p. 21, Fig. 9). **Shown** / **Stated**
14. Vorticity PDFs: the dynamic Smagorinsky model and the TNO agree with filtered DNS; the FNO and the mixed model deviate significantly (§V A 2, Fig. 10). **Shown**
15. Cost per time node in isotropic turbulence: dynamic Smagorinsky 105.6 CPU·s, dynamic mixed 166.4 CPU·s, FNO 0.206 GPU·s / 4.366 CPU·s, TNO 1.021 GPU·s / 12.48 CPU·s (V100 GPU and Xeon Gold CPUs); the text calls the TNO "three times slower than the FNO" (§V A 3, Table I). **Shown**
16. Training takes 14.6 h (FNO) and 11.2 h (TNO) for isotropic turbulence, 15.2 h and 10.6 h for the mixing layer, where the TNO's training and testing losses are lower (§V A 1, §V B 1, Figs. 5, 15). **Shown**
17. Applied without retraining at $Re_\lambda\approx160$ and $250$, the TNO is less accurate than the dynamic Smagorinsky model in the spectrum but more accurate than both LES models in the second-order structure function and the increment PDFs, whose LES errors grow with $Re_\lambda$ (§V A 4, Figs. 12–13, 4 realizations each). **Shown**
18. At higher $Re_\lambda$ the LES models "require higher temporal resolution", while the TNO keeps its cost; the self-similarity of turbulence is offered as the "theoretical foundation" for training at low and applying at high Reynolds number (§V A 4, p. 26). **Stated**
19. Mixing-layer momentum thickness: the mixed model overestimates it; the dynamic Smagorinsky model underestimates it in early transition and overestimates it in linear growth; the TNO agrees in transition and stays stable in linear growth (§V B 2, Fig. 16). **Shown**
20. Directional kinetic energies: both LES models underestimate them early and overestimate them in linear growth; the TNO "closely matches the fDNS data … throughout the entire development" (§V B 2, Fig. 17). **Shown**
21. Mixing-layer spectra: the dynamic Smagorinsky spectrum is too low at 100 and too high from 300 time units; the mixed model is too high at low and too low at high wavenumbers; all models deviate at 650, the TNO slightly least (§V B 2, Fig. 18). **Shown**
22. Q-criterion iso-surfaces: both LES models predict vortex structures larger than filtered DNS; the FNO structures become unreliable after about 650 time units; the TNO reconstructs the small structures better (§V B 2, Fig. 20). **Shown**
23. Cost per time node in the mixing layer: dynamic Smagorinsky 83.55 CPU·s, dynamic mixed 97.86 CPU·s, FNO 0.426 GPU·s / 7.912 CPU·s, TNO 1.691 GPU·s / 20.18 CPU·s (§V B 3, Table II). **Shown**
24. Limitations: only simple periodic flows were tested, each flow needs its own training, and boundary conditions and non-uniform grids need extensions such as geo-FNO; temporal attention and "large models" are proposed (§VI). **Stated**
25. Every case is averaged over 10 independent a posteriori runs from random initial fields not in the training set (§V, §V A 1, §V B 1). **Shown** (protocol)

The training loss (Eq. 31) is the mean-square error of the rollout, with
$\bar u_{\mathrm m}$ the TNO prediction, $N_p$ the number of rollout steps
(value not stated) and $N_s$ the number of grid points:

$$\mathcal L=\frac{1}{N_p}\sum_{n=1}^{N_p}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{\boldsymbol x}\big(\bar u_{\mathrm m,i}^{\,n}(\boldsymbol x)-\bar u_i^{\,n}(\boldsymbol x)\big)^2 .$$

# Printed slips

- **Eq. 9 against Eqs. 10–11:** the dynamic mixed model is printed $\tau_{ij}=C_1\Delta^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij}+C_2(\cdots)$, while the Germano tensors use $h_{1,ij}=-2\Delta^2|\bar S|\bar S_{ij}$. The least-squares coefficients of Eqs. 10–11 solve $L_{ij}\approx C_1M_{ij}+C_2N_{ij}$ with $M_{ij}$, $N_{ij}$ built from $h_{1,ij}$, $h_{2,ij}$, which is consistent only with $\tau_{ij}=C_1h_{1,ij}+C_2h_{2,ij}$, so Eq. 9 differs by a factor $-2$ in the $C_1$ term *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Eq. 3 text:** "$\varepsilon=2v\langle S_{ij}S_{ij}\rangle$", $v$ for $\nu$.
- **Eq. 23:** $\psi(\mathbf q_i,\mathbf x_i)\cdot\psi(\mathbf k_j,\mathbf x_j)=\Theta(\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j)(\mathbf q_i\cdot\mathbf k_j)$ multiplies a rotation matrix by a scalar; the rotary-embedding identity places the relative rotation between the vectors, $\mathbf q_i^T\Theta(\mathbf x_j-\mathbf x_i)\mathbf k_j$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Eq. 20:** the sum runs over $j=1,\dots,N_s$ while the summand is indexed by $l$ and $j$ is a column index on the left.
- **Eqs. 28–29:** the outer integral carries the subscript "u" instead of the time domain; Eq. 29 has the prefactor $1/N_t$ with the sum to $N_p$.
- **Index ranges:** "$0<i<d$", "$0<j<N_s$" (p. 12) and "$0<=i<=N_s$" (p. 13) against 1-based sums elsewhere.
- **§V A 4:** the turnover time at $Re_\lambda\approx250$ is written "$\tau_h\approx0.6$" while the time is normalized "$t/\tau_m\approx26.7$".
- **Fig. 18 caption:** the third panel is labelled "(b)".
- **"Three times slower than the FNO" (§V A 3):** Table I gives $12.48/4.366\approx2.9$ on CPU but $1.021/0.206\approx5.0$ on GPU; for the mixing layer (Table II) the ratios are about 2.6 and 4.0 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Ref. 86:** "S. B. Pope and S. B. Pope, Turbulent flows".
