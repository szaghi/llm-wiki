# Summary

Xu et al. (2023) build subgrid closures for
[LES](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) of compressible wall-bounded
turbulence. Both the SGS stress $\tau_{ij}$ and the SGS heat flux are
nonlinear algebraic expansions: the Lund–Novikov basis for the stress (five
anisotropic tensors and four isotropic terms) and a four-vector basis for
the heat flux. The coefficients come either from the Germano identity
([dynamic procedure](/methods/dynamic-procedure.md)) or from small
feed-forward networks fed with invariants, whose last layer multiplies the
coefficients by the basis ([tensor-basis neural network](/methods/tensor-basis-neural-network.md),
[neural-network SGS models](/methods/neural-network-sgs-model.md)). Ten
networks are trained on filtered [DNS](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md)
of one isothermal-wall [channel](/benchmarks/turbulent-channel-flow.md)
($Re=3000$, $M=1.5$). The key modification, used in the models named
MDNA (dynamic coefficients) and MANA (network coefficients), multiplies
every velocity and temperature gradient by the local grid width in its
direction and sets the length scale to one. This builds the near-wall grid
anisotropy into the inputs and the basis.

In [a priori](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) tests,
[dynamic Smagorinsky](/methods/smagorinsky-model.md),
[Vreman](/methods/vreman-model.md) and [WALE](/methods/wale-model.md)
give correlation coefficients "almost lower than 0.6", which the authors
blame on the linear alignment of the [Boussinesq](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md)
form. MANA reaches correlations above 0.91 with relative errors below 0.4.
The [gradient model](/methods/gradient-model.md) serves only to normalize
the network targets and bases. A posteriori (sixth-order central differences), MANA is compared
with dynamic Smagorinsky, Vreman, WALE and [ILES](/definitions/implicit-les.md)
taken as no model. MANA matches the filtered-DNS mean
[van Driest](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md)
velocity, the r.m.s. and the fluxes. The test cases are the training
channel on an untrained grid, untrained channels at $M=3.0$, 1.0 and 0.2,
and a Mach-2.25 [boundary layer](/benchmarks/turbulent-boundary-layer.md).
MANA reproduces negative SGS fluxes ([backscatter](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md))
without clipping, and a step costs 0.79 of dynamic Smagorinsky.

# Key claims

Registry translation: the source's $\tau_{ij}=\widetilde{u_iu_j}-\tilde u_i\tilde u_j$
is the registry SGS stress per unit mass (positive trace, entering momentum
as $-\partial_j(\bar\rho\tau_{ij})$); its $\mu_{SGS}$, which multiplies
$\tilde S_{ij}$ in an equation per unit mass, is the registry $\nu_t$; its
$C_{sm}$ is $C_s$; $\langle\cdot\rangle_{xz}$ is the plane average
$\langle\cdot\rangle$. Page-local symbols: $Q_j=\widetilde{u_jT}-\tilde u_j\tilde T$
the SGS heat flux; $\tau^d_{ij}$ (registry) the deviatoric part of $\tau_{ij}$, the source's $\tau^A_{ij}$;
$\Pi_Q=-Q_j\partial_j\tilde T$ the SGS temperature-variance flux (the
kinetic-energy flux $-\tau_{ij}\partial_j\tilde u_i$ is the registry $\Pi$);
$\mathcal C$ and $E_r$ the plane-wise correlation coefficient and relative
error; $N_h$ the hidden-layer width; $\bar S^\Delta_{ij}$, $\bar\Omega^\Delta_{ij}$
the strain and rotation built from grid-normalized gradients (the source's
starred tensors).

1. Dynamic Smagorinsky (DSM), Vreman and WALE give correlation coefficients $\mathcal C$ "almost lower than 0.6" and relative errors $E_r$ "almost equal to 1.0" against filtered DNS of R1M15 with filter ratios $n_x=8$, $n_z=4$ (§2.2, figs 1–3). **Shown**
2. The authors attribute the poor correlations to the "unphysical linear alignment assumptions" between SGS stress and filtered strain, and between SGS heat flux and temperature gradient (§2.2, §3.3). **Stated**
3. DSM correlations are smaller near the wall than those of Vreman and WALE, while DSM has slightly larger correlations for $\tau_{11}$, $\tau_{22}$, $\tau_{33}$, attributed to its dynamically solved $C_I$ (zero in Vreman and WALE) (§2.2). **Shown** (the attribution **Stated**)
4. The dynamic nonlinear algebraic model (ODNA) has $\mathcal C>0.4$ for $y^+>50$ but small $\mathcal C$ near the wall and $E_r\approx0.98$ in most components; the authors ascribe the near-wall weakness to "the neglect of the anisotropy of the grid widths" (§3.1, fig. 4). **Shown**
5. The network model with unmodified gradients, OANA-2 ($N_h=20$), has $\mathcal C>0.6$ for most components ($\tau^d_{11}$, $\tau^d_{33}$ about 0.4) and $E_r\approx0.8$, better than eddy-viscosity models and ODNA but "still not a well-performing model"; the training and validation losses of $\tau^d_{23}$, $\tau^d_{12}$ and $Q_2$ oscillate strongly (§3.1, figs 6–7). **Shown**
6. Multiplying each gradient by the local grid width in its direction (Eqs. 3.27–3.30) raises the correlation of the dynamic model (MDNA against ODNA) most for $y^+<50$, and lowers $E_r$ slightly to about 0.95 (§3.2, fig. 8). **Shown**
7. MANA-1 ($N_h=10$) gives $\mathcal C>0.91$ and $E_r<0.4$ for all components of $\tau^d_{ij}$, $\tau_{kk}$ and $Q_j$ on the testing set (§3.2, fig. 10; §5). **Shown**
8. The hidden-layer width ($N_h=10$, 20, 40) has little influence on MANA a priori, so MANA-1 is used a posteriori (§3.2, figs 9, 11). **Shown**
9. A priori, the plane-averaged normalized fluxes $\langle\Pi^+\rangle$ and $\langle\Pi_Q^+\rangle$ of MANA "almost collapse" on the filtered DNS, while Vreman, DSM, WALE and MDNA "significantly underestimate" them (§3.2, fig. 12). **Shown**
10. Three ingredients each contribute significantly to the a priori performance of MANA: the nonlinear algebraic form, the grid-width modification and the network coefficients (§3.3). **Stated** (inference from the ODNA/OANA/MDNA/MANA comparison)
11. MDNA is "sometimes unstable" a posteriori when the grid in the homogeneous directions is coarse and costs about 2.54 times DSM per 1000 steps, so it is not shown a posteriori (§4.1). **Stated**
12. Cost per 1000 steps normalized by DSM: ILES 0.26, Vreman 0.28, DSM 1.0, WALE 0.29, MANA 0.79; DSM takes about 15.4 s on "80 2.40 GHz Intel Xeon Gold 6148 CPUs" (Table 4). **Shown**
13. The network SGS models of Park & Choi (2021), Wang et al. (2018b), Yuan et al. (2020), Xie et al. (2019c) and Xie et al. (2020a) cost "approximately 1.3, 1.8, 2.4, 15 and 256 times" DSM (§1). **Stated** (cited)
14. In R1M15 a posteriori (untrained LES grid $64\times65\times64$, $n_x=6$, $n_z=2$) the van Driest velocity of MANA in the log region is "fully consistent" with the filtered DNS; Vreman, DSM and WALE give larger values and ILES slightly lower ones, read by the authors as proper SGS dissipation for MANA, excessive dissipation for the eddy-viscosity models and too little for ILES (§4.1, fig. 13a). **Shown**
15. In R1M15 a posteriori, ILES and the eddy-viscosity models overpredict the r.m.s. velocity peaks and place them further from the wall; MANA matches the filtered-DNS r.m.s. velocities, resolved shear stress, temperature r.m.s. and streamwise turbulent heat flux (§4.1, figs 14, 16). **Shown**
16. The eddy-viscosity models produce only positive $\Pi$ and $\Pi_Q$; MANA produces both signs at $y^+=15$, as the filtered DNS does, and its p.d.f.s of $\Pi^+$ and $\Pi^+_Q$ collapse on the filtered DNS (§4.1, figs 18–20). **Shown**
17. A posteriori mean SGS fluxes in R1M15: Vreman and WALE underestimate them, DSM "strongly overestimates" the kinetic-energy flux and underestimates the temperature-variance flux; MANA is accurate except slightly larger values in the buffer layer (§4.1, fig. 17). **Shown**
18. Trained at one $Re$, $M$ and grid, MANA is more accurate than the other models in the untrained channels R2M30 ($Re=4880$, $M=3.0$, $n_x=n_z=4$) and R3M10 ($Re=7000$, $M=1.0$, $n_x=12$, $n_z=4$), with slightly larger buffer-layer peaks of the wall-normal r.m.s. and the shear stress and a slight underestimation of the spanwise r.m.s. peak in R2M30 (§4.2, figs 21–26). **Shown**
19. In the nearly incompressible channel R1M02 ($M=0.2$) MANA predicts the velocity statistics and the very small temperature fluctuations better than ILES and the eddy-viscosity models (§4.3, figs 27–28). **Shown**
20. In the spatially developing supersonic adiabatic boundary layer ($Re=635\,000$, $M=2.25$) at $x/L_x=0.8$, ILES and the eddy-viscosity models give much larger r.m.s. peaks than the filtered DNS; MANA matches the spanwise r.m.s. and the shear stress but slightly overestimates the streamwise and wall-normal r.m.s. and the magnitudes of the temperature r.m.s. and heat flux (§4.4, figs 30–31). **Shown**
21. Generalization to untrained grids is attributed to "the information of the grid resolutions" being "inherently embedded in the input and tensor bases" (§4.1, §5). **Stated**
22. "Black box" networks that map filtered quantities directly to SGS terms (Park & Choi 2021; Xie et al. 2019c) "cannot have a good performance in untrained cases", while MANA, combining "plenty of prior physical knowledge", can (§5). **Stated**
23. Park & Choi (2021) and Zhou et al. (2019) are cited for a network trained at one grid failing at another, and Maulik et al. (2019) and Park & Choi (2021) for low-$Re$ training generalizing to high $Re$ (§1). **Stated** (cited)
24. Mini-batch sizes in [500, 2000] have negligible influence; Adam beats Adadelta and Adagrad (clearly for OANA, slightly for MANA); sigmoid performs worst and ReLU poorly; ELU is costlier and worse than leaky ReLU, which is chosen (App., figs 32–33). **Shown**

Model details (§3): $\tau^d_{ij}=\Delta^2\big(C^{\mathrm A}_1\|\bar S\|T^{\mathrm A}_1+\dots+C^{\mathrm A}_5\|\bar S\|^{-1}T^{\mathrm A}_5\big)$
with $T^{\mathrm A}_1=\bar S^d$, $T^{\mathrm A}_2=\bar S\bar\Omega-\bar\Omega\bar S$,
$T^{\mathrm A}_3=(\bar S^2)^d$, $T^{\mathrm A}_4=(\bar\Omega^2)^d$,
$T^{\mathrm A}_5=\bar\Omega\bar S^2-\bar S^2\bar\Omega$ (Eqs. 3.4–3.9; page-local,
the LES analogue of the registry $T^{(n)}$), $\|\bar S\|=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\bar S^2)}=|\bar S|/\sqrt2$,
$\Delta=(\Delta_1\Delta_2\Delta_3)^{1/3}$; inputs the four invariant ratios
of $\mathrm{Tr}(\bar S^2)$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\bar\Omega^2)$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\bar S^3)$,
$\mathrm{Tr}(\bar\Omega^2\bar S)$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\bar\Omega^2\bar S^2)$ and a
local Reynolds number $\bar\rho|\tilde{\boldsymbol u}|d/\tilde\mu$; two
hidden layers, leaky ReLU, mean-squared loss on the merged output, about
$1.8\times10^7$ samples split 60/15/25 %. The grid-normalized form replaces
$\partial_j$ by $\Delta_j\partial_j$ (no sum) in $\bar S^\Delta_{ij}$,
$\bar\Omega^\Delta_{ij}$ and the temperature gradient, with unit length
scale (Eqs. 3.27–3.43). Vreman and WALE constants $C_v=2.5C_s^2$,
$C_w=10.6C_s^2$ with $C_s=0.1$, $C_I=0$, $Pr_t=0.9$.

# Printed slips

- **p. A4-15:** the leaky ReLU is printed as "$\sigma(x)=\max[-0.1x, x]$" (recorded as printed).
- **Eqs. (3.11), (3.37):** the left-hand side $\tau_{kk}$ is a scalar, while the right-hand-side terms $T^I_1=I\cdot\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde S)$, $T^I_2=I\cdot\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde S^2)$, $T^I_3=I\cdot\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde\Omega^2)$ are printed with the identity tensor $I$.
- **p. A4-47:** "the OANA model and MAMA model" for MANA.
- **Table 2:** the coefficients are labelled $C^A_i$, $C^I_i$, $C^Q_i$ without the star the text uses for MANA.
- **Correlation summaries:** §2.2 says the eddy-viscosity correlations are "almost lower than 0.6", §3.1 that they are "mostly smaller than 0.4".
- **§4.4:** "$x/L_x=0.8$ (i.e. $x=8.8$ in Pirozzoli et al. 2004)" with $L_x=6$ (inches). *(compiler check, not in the sources)* $0.8\times6=4.8$; no offset between the two origins is stated.
- **References:** Lilly (1992) printed as "Phys. Fluids A 238, 633–635"; Xie, Wang & Weinan (2020a) with third author "Weinan"; Jiménez et al. (2001) with fourth author "Béedat" (all recorded as printed).
