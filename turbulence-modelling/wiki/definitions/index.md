# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [A priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) — A priori testing evaluates or trains a closure on filtered or averaged reference data outside the solver; a posteriori testing runs it inside the solver. The sources show that a priori agreement neither guarantees nor is required for a posteriori accuracy, and name the mechanisms.
* [Boussinesq eddy-viscosity hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) — The linear closure that aligns the deviatoric Reynolds or subgrid stress with the strain rate through a scalar eddy viscosity; its RANS and LES forms, the sign conventions of the sources, and the evidence that one scalar viscosity cannot carry both the mean stress and the energy transfer.
* [Compressible velocity transformations](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md) — Maps that turn the mean velocity of a variable-property compressible wall layer into the incompressible law of the wall — van Driest, Trettel–Larsson, data-driven and total-stress-based — with semi-local scaling, the generalized Reynolds analogy for temperature, and the sources' ranking of the transformations.
* [Direct numerical simulation](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) — DNS solves the Navier–Stokes equations with no turbulence model and a grid fine enough for the dissipative range; this page gives its resolution criteria and cost scalings, the weaker quasi-DNS and DNS-embedded-in-LES variants, and its role as reference data for closure development.
* [Discretization-consistent closure](/definitions/discretization-consistent-closure.md) — An LES closure built for the discrete equations actually solved, so it accounts for the commutation error and the discretization-induced subgrid term as well as the physical subfilter stress; obtained by in-the-loop optimization or by training on discretize-first data.
* [Energy backscatter and the subgrid energy flux](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md) — Energy backscatter is a negative local subgrid energy flux, energy moving from subgrid to resolved scales; the sources define the flux, show that its local sign depends on the gauge of the subgrid stress and on the filter, and disagree in emphasis on whether models must reproduce it.
* [Hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) — Scale-resolving approaches that model all turbulence with RANS in some regions or limits and resolve part of it as in LES elsewhere; the distinction from wall-modelled LES, the three operating modes and unified against interfaced transitions, and the deficiencies the sources identify, modelled-stress depletion first.
* [Implicit large-eddy simulation](/definitions/implicit-les.md) — Implicit LES drops the explicit subgrid model and lets the discretization's own dissipation and filtering act as the closure; the sources use the term in at least three senses and report where it works, where it fails, and what it misses.
* [Large-eddy simulation: filtering and the subgrid-scale stress](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) — Large-eddy simulation resolves the filtered velocity and models the subgrid-scale stress; this page fixes the filtered equations, the filters and filter widths used by the sources, the commutation and discretization terms, and the gauge freedom of the subgrid stress.
* [Law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) — The inner-layer mean-velocity profile of wall-bounded turbulence — viscous sublayer, buffer layer and logarithmic layer in wall units — with the constants, composite fits and damping functions the sources use, and its role as the target every wall model must reproduce.
* [Log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md) — The shift, in wall units, between the mean velocity of a wall-modelled or hybrid simulation and the logarithmic law in the log layer: how the sources measure it, the mechanisms they attribute it to, the remedies they use, and its appearance in learned wall models.
* [Model-form uncertainty in turbulence closures](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md) — The epistemic uncertainty that comes from the structure of a turbulence closure rather than from its inputs or coefficients; the four layers of RANS assumptions, physics-based bounds by Reynolds-stress eigenspace perturbation and random matrices, Bayesian calibration and discrepancy fields, and the limits of identifiability.
* [Reduced MHD and Elsasser variables](/definitions/reduced-mhd.md) — Reduced MHD is the strong-guide-field, anisotropic limit of MHD written in Elsasser variables; it conserves each Elsasser energy, couples only counter-propagating fields, and has a rescaling symmetry that any scaling theory of MHD turbulence must respect.
