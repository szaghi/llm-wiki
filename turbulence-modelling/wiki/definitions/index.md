# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [A priori and a posteriori testing](/definitions/a-priori-and-a-posteriori-testing.md) — A priori testing evaluates or trains a closure on filtered or averaged reference data outside the solver; a posteriori testing runs it inside the solver. The sources show that a priori agreement neither guarantees nor is required for a posteriori accuracy, and name the mechanisms.
* [Attached-eddy hypothesis](/definitions/attached-eddy-hypothesis.md) — The attached-eddy hypothesis models the logarithmic region of wall turbulence as a hierarchy of self-similar eddies whose size scales with wall distance; this page gives the consequences the sources test (logarithmic normal stresses, inverse-wavenumber spectra, uniform momentum zones, inactive motions) and where compressible DNS up to Mach 11 depart from them.
* [Boussinesq eddy-viscosity hypothesis](/definitions/boussinesq-hypothesis.md) — The linear closure that aligns the deviatoric Reynolds or subgrid stress with the strain rate through a scalar eddy viscosity; its RANS and LES forms and sign conventions, measured misalignment, anisotropy slaved to the strain, the best-fit eddy viscosity, explicit non-linear extensions and their conditioning, and why one scalar viscosity cannot carry both mean stress and energy transfer.
* [Compressible velocity transformations](/definitions/compressible-velocity-transformation.md) — Maps that turn the mean velocity of a variable-property compressible wall layer into the incompressible law of the wall — van Driest, Trettel–Larsson, data-driven and total-stress-based — with semi-local scaling, temperature–velocity relations, their assessment on DNS of channels and supersonic to hypersonic boundary layers, and their use as validation yardsticks.
* [Direct numerical simulation](/definitions/direct-numerical-simulation.md) — DNS solves the Navier–Stokes equations with no turbulence model and a grid fine enough for the dissipative range; this page gives its resolution criteria, measured resolutions and cost, the discretizations, GPU codes and floating-point precision used for it, spatially developing boundary layers, weaker variants, looser uses of the term, and its role as reference data.
* [Discretization-consistent closure](/definitions/discretization-consistent-closure.md) — An LES closure built for the discrete equations actually solved, so it accounts for the commutation error and the discretization-induced subgrid term as well as the physical subfilter stress; obtained by in-the-loop optimization (reinforcement learning, adjoints), by training on discretize-first data, or on data generated by the deployment solver.
* [Energy backscatter and the subgrid energy flux](/definitions/energy-backscatter.md) — Energy backscatter is a negative local subgrid energy flux, energy moving from subgrid to resolved scales; the sources define the flux, show that its local sign depends on the gauge of the subgrid stress and on the filter, and disagree in emphasis on whether models must reproduce it, remove it for stability, or admit it in stable form.
* [Hybrid RANS/LES](/definitions/hybrid-rans-les.md) — Scale-resolving approaches that model all turbulence with RANS in some regions or limits and resolve part of it as in LES elsewhere; the distinction from wall-modelled LES, operating modes, continuous eddy simulation and sequential RANS-to-LES coupling, the deficiencies the sources identify, and applications.
* [Implicit large-eddy simulation](/definitions/implicit-les.md) — Implicit LES drops the explicit subgrid model and lets the discretization's own dissipation and filtering act as the closure; the sources use the term in at least three senses and report where it works, where it fails, and what it misses.
* [Laminar separation bubble](/definitions/laminar-separation-bubble.md) — A closed region of reversed flow formed when a laminar boundary layer separates under an adverse pressure gradient, transitions in the detached shear layer and reattaches turbulent; its signatures, unsteadiness and frequencies in scale-resolving simulations, and why RANS transition models struggle to size it.
* [Large-eddy simulation: filtering and the subgrid-scale stress](/definitions/large-eddy-simulation.md) — Large-eddy simulation resolves the filtered velocity and models the subgrid-scale stress; this page fixes the filtered equations, the filters and filter widths used by the sources (including high-aspect-ratio cells and element methods), the commutation and discretization terms, and the gauge freedom of the subgrid stress.
* [Law of the wall](/definitions/law-of-the-wall.md) — The inner-layer mean-velocity profile of wall-bounded turbulence — viscous sublayer, buffer layer and logarithmic layer in wall units — with the constants, composite fits and damping functions the sources use, its compressible and outer-layer (defect-law) extensions, and its role as the target every wall model must reproduce.
* [Log-layer mismatch](/definitions/log-layer-mismatch.md) — The shift, in wall units, between the mean velocity of a wall-modelled or hybrid simulation and the logarithmic law in the log layer: how the sources measure it, the mechanisms they attribute it to, the remedies they use, and its appearance in learned wall models.
* [Model-form uncertainty in turbulence closures](/definitions/model-form-uncertainty.md) — The uncertainty that comes from the structure of a turbulence closure rather than from its inputs or coefficients; the four layers of RANS assumptions, physics-based bounds, Bayesian calibration and discrepancy fields, stochastic discrepancies trained on indirect data, where to place the correction, non-unique inversions, identifiability, confidence scores and compensating errors.
* [Morkovin's hypothesis and Reynolds analogies](/definitions/morkovin-hypothesis.md) — Morkovin's hypothesis holds that, while density fluctuations stay small, compressible wall turbulence behaves like incompressible turbulence once mean property variations are scaled out; this page gives Morkovin scaling, the strong Reynolds analogy and its extensions, the turbulent Prandtl number in DNS and RANS closures, and where DNS to Mach 14 and separated-flow LES confirm or break them.
* [Reduced MHD and Elsasser variables](/definitions/reduced-mhd.md) — Reduced MHD is the strong-guide-field, anisotropic limit of MHD written in Elsasser variables; it conserves each Elsasser energy, couples only counter-propagating fields, and has a rescaling symmetry that any scaling theory of MHD turbulence must respect.
* [Wall law in adverse pressure gradient](/definitions/adverse-pressure-gradient-wall-law.md) — The inner-layer mean-velocity law of adverse-pressure-gradient turbulent boundary layers: a log law whose extent shrinks with the inner-scaled pressure-gradient parameter, a half-power law above it, the correlations calibrating both edges in Reynolds number and pressure gradient, and their use for RANS models and wall functions.
