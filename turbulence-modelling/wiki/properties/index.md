# Properties

Constraints and invariants a method can hold.

* [Critical balance and the anisotropic MHD cascade](/properties/critical-balance.md) — Critical balance equates the linear Alfvén time and the nonlinear time at every scale of strong MHD turbulence; it fixes the parallel spectrum k_par^-2, the anisotropy l_par ~ lambda^(2/3) of Goldreich–Sridhar, the weak-to-strong transition scale, and frames imbalance and residual energy.
* [Dynamic alignment and intermittency in MHD turbulence](/properties/dynamic-alignment.md) — Dynamic alignment is the scale-dependent alignment of Elsasser increments that weakens the MHD nonlinearity and gives a k_perp^-3/2 spectrum; Schekochihin (2022) recasts it, to respect the RMHD rescaling symmetry, as an intermittency effect with sin(theta) ~ (lambda/lambda_CB)^(1/4).
* [Entropy conservation and entropy stability](/properties/entropy-conservation.md) — A discretization is entropy conservative when its convective terms reproduce the vanishing global entropy production of smooth inviscid flow, and entropy stable when entropy can only be dissipated; this page gives the two-point fluxes that achieve it, its dependence on the equation of state, its extension to the Spalart–Allmaras RANS equation, and its measured effect in turbulence simulations.
* [Geometric conservation law and free-stream preservation](/properties/geometric-conservation-law.md) — On curvilinear grids a discretization preserves a uniform flow only if its discrete metric terms satisfy the metric identities, and on moving grids a temporal identity too; the sources meet this geometric conservation law with curl-form DGSEM and flux-reconstruction metrics, conservative finite-difference and gas-kinetic metrics, moving-grid flux reconstruction and moving-mesh DGSEM, and note curved elements as an aliasing source.
* [Kinetic-energy preservation and split forms](/properties/kinetic-energy-preservation.md) — A discretization is kinetic-energy preserving when its convective terms neither create nor destroy discrete kinetic energy; this page gives the split-form families and two-point fluxes, kinetic energy as an entropy, why a dissipation-free baseline is robust but thermalizes or disperses without help, and what it does not control.
* [Numerical dissipation in scale-resolving simulation](/properties/numerical-dissipation.md) — Numerical dissipation is the kinetic-energy loss a discretization adds itself; sources show where in wavenumber and in the element it acts, how it is measured, that it competes with or replaces the subgrid model, that RANS-level artificial dissipation spoils hybrid RANS/LES, that it trades against dispersion, and that both too much and too little of it degrade LES.
* [Pressure-equilibrium preservation](/properties/pressure-equilibrium-preservation.md) — A scheme is pressure-equilibrium preserving when uniform pressure and velocity stay uniform under density variations; for a general equation of state, evolving total or internal energy makes this exact only with a discrete chain rule (second order at best), while evolving pressure makes it exact at the price of total-energy conservation.
