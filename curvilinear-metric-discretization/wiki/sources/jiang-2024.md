# Summary

P. Y. Jiang, Z. Y. Liu, S. Y. Liu, J. Bao and G. Y. Fu, "Development of a
gyrokinetic-MHD energetic particle simulation code. I. MHD version",
*Physics of Plasmas* **31**, 073904 (2024), published online 11 July 2024
(submitted 11 February, accepted 6 April 2024); DOI 10.1063/5.0203252,
read from page 1 of the PDF. Version read: the publisher's version of record
(CC BY 4.0), 18 PDF pages: an AIP cover page, then article pages 073904-1 to
-17. Locators below are article page numbers (PDF page = article page + 1).

The paper presents GMEC_I, the reduced-MHD solver of the GMEC code family
([GMEC](/software/gmec.md)), a linear initial-value code for tokamak
instabilities in global field-aligned coordinates with high-order finite
differences, RK4 time stepping, MPI and TBB parallelism, and discrete
equations generated by a C++20 compile-time symbolic solver; it is
benchmarked against the eigenvalue code MAS on ballooning and tearing modes.

**Scoped read (D21 practice for application papers).** This bundle read the
abstract and introduction (pp. 1–2); §III, coordinates (pp. 3–4); §IV A–C
and F, operators, spatial discretization, boundary conditions and the
shifted-metric implementation (pp. 4–6); §V A, the expansion of the
curvilinear operators (p. 6); §VI C, the equilibrium interface (p. 8);
§VII, merging of coefficients (pp. 8–10); §VIII, the numerical instability
of field-aligned coordinates (pp. 10–11, figs. 10–12 on pp. 8–9); the
summary (p. 15); Appendices A (equilibrium input, Table I), C (VMEC to
Boozer) and D (DESC) (pp. 16–17); and the complete reference list (p. 17).
Equations were checked on page images (110-dpi renders of PDF pages for
pp. 3–6, 8–10, 16–17). Not read here: the reduced-MHD models (§II), the
symbolic-solver internals (§V B–C), diagnostics, parallel scaling and the
MAS benchmarks (§IX), which belong to the plasma physics, not to this
bundle. The reference list was merged complete.

# Key claims

1. **Field-aligned coordinates** $\xi^1=\Psi-\Psi_0$, $\xi^2=\vartheta$,
   $\xi^3=\varphi-\chi$ on straight-field-line (usually Boozer) coordinates,
   with contravariant and covariant bases, the integrated shear $I$ and the
   twist-shift condition (eqs. (13)–(26), p. 3).
   → [field-aligned coordinates](/definitions/field-aligned-coordinates.md),
   [covariant and contravariant bases](/definitions/covariant-and-contravariant-bases.md),
   [magnetic flux coordinates](/definitions/magnetic-flux-coordinates.md).
2. **Metric discontinuity and instability.** $I$ does not satisfy the
   twist-shift condition, so $\mathbf a_1$ is discontinuous at the $\xi^2$
   boundary; the resulting numerical instability is shown for an
   $n_\varphi=20$ ballooning mode, and a diffusion large enough to suppress it
   biases the growth rate 7% low (p. 3; §VIII, pp. 10–11).
   → [shifted metric](/methods/shifted-metric.md).
3. **Scott's shifted metric** with $\sigma_{[k]}'=g^{13}/g^{11}$, giving
   $\bar g^{13}=0$ and interpolated parallel differences (eqs. (27)–(35),
   pp. 3–4; B-spline interpolation, §IV F, p. 6), and a **new simple shifted
   metric** with $\sigma_{[k]}'=-I$, whose grid coincides with the toroidal
   coordinates at the cost of $\bar g^{13}\ne0$ (eqs. (36)–(38), p. 4). Both
   remove the instability with the same growth rate (p. 11).
   → [shifted metric](/methods/shifted-metric.md).
4. **Operators in component form** with $\mathbf B=\mathbf a_2/J$:
   parallel derivative, perpendicular divergence, $\hat{\mathbf b}\times\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi$,
   curvature and curl terms (eqs. (39)–(44), p. 4).
   → [field-aligned coordinates](/definitions/field-aligned-coordinates.md),
   [Jacobian](/definitions/jacobian.md), [divergence](/definitions/divergence.md),
   [curl](/definitions/curl.md), [Laplacian](/definitions/laplacian.md).
5. **Finite differences on precomputed metrics.** Arbitrary-order stencils
   (eqs. (50)–(51)), divergence-form operators expanded by the product rule
   into terms with precomputed metric-derivative coefficients (eqs. (47)–(48),
   (52)), merging of stencil and metric coefficients for a 6-fold speed-up
   (§VII), metrics and their derivatives computed outside the code from
   VMEC or DESC (Table I; App. C–D).
   → [GMEC](/software/gmec.md), [Laplacian](/definitions/laplacian.md),
   [metric tensor](/definitions/metric-tensor.md).

## Recorded as printed (in the sections read)

* **Eq. (38).** The two shifted metrics are said to coincide "only if the
  toroidal coordinates is orthogonal that $g^{\psi\theta}=0$, $g^{\psi\xi}=0$
  and $g^{\theta\xi}=0$". From eq. (29) the first two conditions suffice
  (compiler's remark, on the [shifted metric](/methods/shifted-metric.md)
  page).
* **Eq. (43)**, $\hat{\mathbf b}\times\boldsymbol\kappa\cdot\nabla A=(JB)^{-1}\sum g_{yi}\epsilon_{ijk}\kappa_j\partial_kh$
  in the source's symbols: the left side has $A$ and the right side $h$;
  and if $\kappa_j$ are covariant components, the derivation that gives
  eq. (42) with $(J^2B)^{-1}$ gives $(J^2B)^{-1}$ here too (compiler's check,
  hypothesis; the source does not say which components $\kappa_j$ are).
* **Eq. (52) against eq. (48):** the leading minus sign of the current in
  (48) is absent in (52), and the $B$ factors of the second line differ.
* **Self-contradiction on interpolation (§III C, p. 4):** "Both the shifted
  metric coordinates and the simple shifted metric coordinates need
  interpolation in y direction", then the simple variant is preferred
  "because interpolation is not needed"; the second statement refers to the
  field-to-particle interpolation of a PIC code.
* **"it can be proved that this is the minimum number of multiplications"**
  (§VII B, p. 10): no proof is given.
* **Symbols:** the toroidal angle is written $\xi$ in §III A and $\phi$ in
  eqs. (30), (37) and App. C, where $\phi$ is also the electric potential;
  $I$ is both the integrated shear and the Boozer covariant poloidal
  component (eq. (C9)); $O(\epsilon)$ in eq. (31) is undefined.
