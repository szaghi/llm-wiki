# Results

Theorems, proved properties, impossibility results.

* [Divergence theorem](/results/divergence-theorem.md) — For a vector field continuous and differentiable in a volume V bounded by a closed surface S, ∫_V ∇·v dV = ∮_S v·dS; with its corollaries ∫_V ∇φ dV = ∮_S φ dS and ∫_V ∇×v dV = ∮_S dS×v, its tensor form, and its use to turn integral conservation laws into differential ones.
* [Green's identities](/results/greens-identities.md) — Green's first and second theorems, ∮_S φ∇ψ·dS = ∫_V (φ∇²ψ + ∇φ·∇ψ) dV and ∮_S (φ∇ψ − ψ∇φ)·dS = ∫_V (φ∇²ψ − ψ∇²φ) dV, obtained from the divergence theorem applied to φ∇ψ.
* [Green's theorem in the plane](/results/greens-theorem-in-the-plane.md) — For P(x,y), Q(x,y) with continuous derivatives on a plane region R bounded by C traversed with R on the left, ∮_C (P dx + Q dy) = ∬_R (∂Q/∂x − ∂P/∂y) dx dy; the two-dimensional divergence theorem, valid for multiply connected regions with every boundary traversed positively.
* [Integral definitions of grad, div and curl](/results/integral-definitions-of-grad-div-curl.md) — The coordinate-free definitions ∇φ = lim (1/V)∮φ dS, ∇·v = lim (1/V)∮v·dS, ∇×v = lim (1/V)∮dS×v and (∇×v)·n̂ = lim (1/A)∮v·dx as the volume or area shrinks to a point, which the source shows reproduce the differential forms in Cartesian and (for div and curl) orthogonal curvilinear coordinates.
* [Quotient law](/results/quotient-law.md) — If a set of quantities A, contracted with an arbitrary tensor B, gives a tensor C in every coordinate frame, then A is itself a tensor; the practical test for tensor character, proved in the source for Cartesian second-order tensors.
* [Stokes' theorem](/results/stokes-theorem.md) — For a vector field on a two-sided open surface S with perimeter C, ∫_S (∇×v)·dS = ∮_C v·dx; with the corollaries ∫_S dS×∇φ = ∮_C φ dx and ∫_S (dS×∇)×b = ∮_C dx×b, and a tensor form.
* [Vector area of a closed surface](/results/closed-surface-vector-area.md) — The vector area ∮_S dS of every closed surface is zero; hence the vector area of an open surface depends only on its perimeter, S = ½∮_C x×dx, and the enclosed volume is V = ⅓∮_S x·dS.
* [Vector operator identities](/results/vector-operator-identities.md) — The product rules of grad, div and curl (Table 10.1), the identities curl grad φ = 0 and div curl v = 0, ∇·(∇φ×∇ψ) = 0, and curl curl v = grad div v − ∇²v; coordinate-independent, provable compactly with the ε–δ identity.
