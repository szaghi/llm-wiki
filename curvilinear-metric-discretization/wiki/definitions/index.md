# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Cartesian tensor](/definitions/cartesian-tensor.md) — A set of 3^N quantities whose values transform under every rotation of Cartesian axes x'_i = L_ij x_j like products of N coordinates, T'_ij…k = L_ip L_jq … L_kr T_pq…r; scalars, vectors and second-order tensors are orders 0, 1, 2, closed under addition, outer product and contraction.
* [Christoffel symbols](/definitions/christoffel-symbols.md) — The coefficients Γ^k_ij of the derivatives of the covariant base vectors, ∂_j a_i = Γ^k_ij a_k, symmetric in i and j, computable from the metric as Γ^m_ij = ½ g^mk(∂_i g_jk + ∂_j g_ki − ∂_k g_ij); they vanish in Cartesian coordinates and are not the components of a tensor.
* [Conservative vector field](/definitions/conservative-vector-field.md) — A vector field whose line integral between two points is path-independent; in a simply connected region this is equivalent to being a gradient of a single-valued scalar potential, to being irrotational, and to v·dx being an exact differential. Solenoidal fields are, dually, curls of a vector potential.
* [Covariant and contravariant bases](/definitions/covariant-and-contravariant-bases.md) — The two natural, generally non-unit and non-orthogonal bases of a curvilinear system — tangents a_i = ∂x/∂ξ^i to the coordinate curves and normals a^i = ∇ξ^i to the coordinate surfaces — which are reciprocal (a_i·a^j = δ_i^j) and define the contravariant and covariant components of a vector.
* [Covariant derivative](/definitions/covariant-derivative.md) — The tensorial derivative of tensor components in general coordinates, v^i_;j = ∂_j v^i + Γ^i_kj v^k and v_i;j = ∂_j v_i − Γ^k_ij v_k, which accounts for the variation of the base vectors (∂_j v = v^i_;j a_i), reduces to the partial derivative in Cartesians and annihilates the metric.
* [Curl](/definitions/curl.md) — The vector field ∇×v, the circulation per unit area about the normal: ε_ijk ∂v_k/∂x_j in Cartesians, a scale-factor determinant in orthogonal coordinates, and (∇×v)^i = (1/√g) ε^ijk ∂v_k/∂ξ^j in any coordinates, where the Christoffel terms cancel.
* [Differential form](/definitions/differential-form.md) — A p-form is a sum of coefficient functions times wedge products of p basis 1-forms dx^i, an object built to be the oriented integrand of a p-dimensional integral; in three dimensions 0-, 1-, 2- and 3-forms carry scalars, line, surface and volume integrands, and no p-form with p > D survives.
* [Divergence](/definitions/divergence.md) — The scalar field ∇·v, the net outflux per unit volume: ∂v^k/∂x^k in Cartesians, (1/h1h2h3)Σ∂(h_j h_k v̂_i)/∂ξ^i in orthogonal coordinates, and v^i_;i = (1/√g)∂(√g v^j)/∂ξ^j in any coordinates.
* [Exterior derivative](/definitions/exterior-derivative.md) — The operator d mapping p-forms to (p+1)-forms, defined by linearity, the Leibniz rule d(fα) = df∧α + f dα, the antiderivation rule d(α∧β) = dα∧β + (−1)^p α∧dβ, d(dα) = 0 and df = ∂_k f dx^k; in three dimensions it generates gradient, curl and divergence, and d∘d = 0 contains curl grad = 0 and div curl = 0.
* [Field-aligned coordinates](/definitions/field-aligned-coordinates.md) — Computational coordinates ξ^1 = Ψ − Ψ_0, ξ^2 = ϑ, ξ^3 = φ − χ(Ψ,ϑ) obtained from magnetic flux coordinates by shifting the toroidal angle along the field lines, so that B = ∇ξ^3×∇ξ^1 = a_2/J and the parallel derivative is ∂_2 alone; the price is a non-orthogonal metric that carries the integrated shear I and a twist-shift periodicity condition in ξ^2.
* [General curvilinear coordinates](/definitions/curvilinear-coordinates.md) — Three functions ξ^i of position that label points of space one-to-one through a differentiable, invertible mapping x(ξ), with coordinate surfaces ξ^i = const and coordinate curves; orthogonal when the three surfaces meet at right angles.
* [General tensor](/definitions/general-tensor.md) — A set of quantities whose contravariant (upper) and covariant (lower) indices transform under an arbitrary invertible change of curvilinear coordinates with the factors ∂ξ̄^i/∂ξ^a and ∂ξ^d/∂ξ̄^l respectively; the components on different tensor bases represent one coordinate-free object.
* [Gradient](/definitions/gradient.md) — The vector field ∇φ of a scalar field, with dφ = ∇φ·dx: ∂φ/∂x^k e_k in Cartesians, Σ(1/h_i)∂φ/∂ξ^i â_i in orthogonal coordinates and ∂φ/∂ξ^i a^i in any coordinates; normal to the level surfaces, and the limit of the closed-surface integral of φ dS per unit volume.
* [Hodge star operator](/definitions/hodge-star.md) — The operator ⋆ mapping a p-form to its complementary (D−p)-form, defined from a metric and an orientation: in Euclidean three-space ⋆1 = dx^1∧dx^2∧dx^3, ⋆dx^1 = dx^2∧dx^3 (cyclically), ⋆(dx^1∧dx^2) = dx^3, ⋆(dx^1∧dx^2∧dx^3) = 1, with ⋆⋆ = identity there but not in Minkowski space.
* [Jacobian of the coordinate mapping](/definitions/jacobian.md) — J = det[∂x^i/∂ξ^j] = a_1·(a_2×a_3), the signed volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the covariant base vectors per unit coordinate volume, with |J| = √g; it carries the volume element dV = √g dξ^1dξ^2dξ^3 and is a relative scalar under changes of curvilinear coordinates.
* [Laplacian](/definitions/laplacian.md) — The scalar operator ∇²φ = ∇·∇φ: Σ∂²φ/∂x_k² in Cartesians, the scale-factor form in orthogonal coordinates and (1/√g)∂_j(√g g^jk ∂_kφ) in any coordinates; applied to a vector field it acts on basis vectors as well as components outside Cartesian coordinates.
* [Levi-Civita symbol](/definitions/levi-civita-symbol.md) — The totally antisymmetric permutation symbol ε_ijk (+1, −1, 0 for even, odd, repeated index permutations of 1,2,3), which writes determinants, cross products and curls in index form and satisfies the contraction identity ε_ijk ε_klm = δ_il δ_jm − δ_im δ_jl.
* [Magnetic flux coordinates](/definitions/magnetic-flux-coordinates.md) — Curvilinear coordinates (Ψ, ϑ, φ) adapted to a magnetic field with nested flux surfaces: a flux-surface label Ψ with B·∇Ψ = 0, a poloidal angle ϑ and a toroidal angle φ; orthogonal variants make ∇Ψ normal to the other two directions, straight-field-line variants (Boozer, PEST) make the field-line pitch a flux function q(Ψ).
* [Metric tensor](/definitions/metric-tensor.md) — The symmetric second-order tensor g_ij = a_i·a_j (with inverse g^ij = a^i·a^j and determinant g) that completely characterises a curvilinear coordinate system at each point: it gives arc length, volume element, scalar products, and raises and lowers indices.
* [Relative tensor](/definitions/relative-tensor.md) — A set of components that transforms like a general tensor times the w-th power of the Jacobian determinant |∂ξ/∂ξ̄|; weight 1 is a tensor density, weight −1 a general pseudotensor, so the metric determinant g has weight 2, √g weight 1 and the permutation symbols ε_ijk, ε^ijk weights −1 and +1.
* [Scale factors](/definitions/scale-factors.md) — The lengths h_i = |∂x/∂ξ^i| of the covariant base vectors, which convert coordinate increments into distances; for orthogonal coordinates they fix arc length, volume element and the metric g_ij = h_i² δ_ij.
* [Vector area element](/definitions/vector-area-element.md) — The oriented surface element dS = n̂ dS, outward for closed surfaces and right-handed with the perimeter for open ones; for a parametrised surface x(u,v) it is (∂x/∂u × ∂x/∂v) du dv, and on a coordinate surface ξ^i = const it is the cross product of the two in-surface base vectors.
* [Wedge product](/definitions/wedge-product.md) — The exterior product ∧ of differential forms: distributive, associative and antisymmetric on 1-forms (dx^i∧dx^j = −dx^j∧dx^i, so dx^i∧dx^i = 0); it builds p-forms, makes the signed Jacobian appear under a change of variables, and with the Hodge star reproduces the cross and triple products in three dimensions.
