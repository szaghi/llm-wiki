# Summary

Bai et al. study self-pulsation in a gas–liquid swirl coaxial injector of the
liquid-centered type (LCSC) with a 5 mm inner-post recess. Experiments use
water and air at atmospheric back-pressure; a quartz outer injector allows
high-speed backlit imaging inside the recess chamber, and a miniature
transducer measures the recess pressure. 2D axisymmetric-swirl unsteady
volume-of-fluid simulations with RNG k–epsilon turbulence, in an unnamed
solver, reproduce the baseline. The authors propose that the conical liquid
sheet blocks the annular gas, the recess pressure builds, the sheet is pushed
inward and thins, and the gas then tears it open. Experiment and simulation
agree on frequency (3165 Hz against 3246 Hz) and amplitude (0.13 MPa against
0.12 MPa) at 160 g/s liquid and 5 g/s gas. The frequency fits a line in the
liquid Reynolds number, jumps to an "ultra-high frequency" 8–9 kHz pulsation
at high gas flow and large liquid flow, and the lower and upper self-pulsation
boundaries are mapped in the liquid/gas momentum-flux plane, which the
authors claim is a first.

# Key claims

1. **Blocking mechanism** (§3.2, Figs. 5, 7, 8; Conclusion 1). Sheet blocks
   the annular gas; confined pressure peaks; sheet pushed inward and thinned;
   gas tears the sheet; pressure drops. Shown qualitatively from simulation
   contours and imaging; no causal test.
2. **Experiment–simulation agreement** (§3.2, Figs. 8–9). Liquid 160 g/s,
   gas 5.0 g/s: simulation (probe 5) 3246 Hz and 0.12 MPa, peak about twice
   atmospheric and minimum about 80 % of atmospheric; experiment (3.5 mm
   upstream of the exit) 3165 Hz and about 0.13 MPa. Shown.
3. **Spray frequency equals pressure frequency** (§3.3.1, Fig. 10a;
   Conclusion 2) "in most conditions"; the pressure frequency is adopted
   because image extraction degrades at high gas Reynolds number. Shown.
4. **Linear frequency law** (§3.3.1). At gas 5 g/s (gas Reynolds number
   35700), $f = 890 + 0.089\,\mathrm{Re}_l$ (Hz) for
   $6800<\mathrm{Re}_l<39000$; outside it, no self-pulsation. Empirical fit,
   unnumbered.
5. **Gas Reynolds number** (§3.3.1, Fig. 10b). At liquid 40 and 80 g/s, $f$
   rises slightly then falls and the pulsation disappears; at larger liquid
   flows $f$ stays roughly constant then jumps to 8000–9000 Hz; UHF window
   11.67–16.01 g/s gas at 160 g/s liquid, 12.55–16.97 g/s at 200 g/s. Shown.
6. **HF and UHF waveforms** (§3.3.2, Figs. 9, 11). HF: positive half-wave
   much larger, peak about 0.21 MPa absolute. UHF: nearly symmetric, peak
   about 0.13 MPa, 8332 Hz at liquid 160 g/s and gas 14.5 g/s. Sonic annular
   gas at UHF and the force-balance explanation are stated, not shown.
7. **Boundaries** (§3.4, Fig. 12; Conclusion 3). Lower threshold rises with
   liquid flow ("skewed parabola or exponential-like", consistent with
   Bazarov and Im et al.); upper threshold falls quickly at small liquid flow,
   rises slightly at large; UHF band with nearly parallel boundaries; no
   oscillation between HF and UHF; claimed first such map. Shown.
8. **Recess flow patterns** (§3.1, Figs. 5–6). Whole-sheet oscillation at
   small liquid flow, downstream-only at large; stable sprays are solid cones
   whose angle decreases with gas flow. Shown.
9. **Literature** (§1). Bazarov (recess length most significant; petal-valve
   impedance model), Huang et al. (gas-passage/gas-core resonance), Im et al.
   ($f$ linear in both Reynolds numbers; ambient pressure suppresses;
   dominant sheet wave), Eberhart et al. (self-pulsation without recess,
   Kelvin–Helmholtz via POD), Kang et al. (mixing regimes), Yang et al.
   (optimum recess angle). Stated.

# Methods

* **Injector** (§2.1, Fig. 2, Table 1). Inner post 4.7 mm diameter,
  40.05 mm long; annular gas gap between 8 and 9 mm diameters (0.5 mm);
  swirl chamber 10.2 mm; four tangential 2 mm entries every 90°, swirl radius
  4 mm; convergence angle 90°; recess 5 mm. The swirl stage copies Liu et al.
* **Experiment** (§2.1, Fig. 1, Table 2). Pressurized water and dried air
  into atmospheric ambient; manifold pressure sensors and turbine flowmeters
  (0.5 % FS); Photron Fastcam SA-Z, 30000 frames/s, 1024 × 688 pixels,
  80.4 µs exposure; Kulite XCQ-080-7BARA 3.5 mm upstream of the exit,
  1 MHz for 2 s. Imaging and pressure in separate runs. Spray frequency from
  summed gray values at two points and an FFT. Mass-flow deviation at most
  4 % (liquid) and 8 % (gas); no frequency or amplitude uncertainty.
* **Test matrix** (Table 2, recess 5 mm). Test 1: liquid 43.1–285.3 g/s at
  gas 5 g/s. Tests 2–7: liquid 200, 180, 160, 120, 80, 40 g/s with gas swept
  over 0.545–31.732 g/s.
* **Simulation** (§2.2). 2D unsteady axisymmetric-swirl model, tangential
  ports replaced by a wall slit; ideal-gas air, incompressible water; VOF
  with continuum surface force; RNG k–epsilon with non-equilibrium wall
  functions; CICSAM interface, PRESTO pressure, PISO coupling, second-order
  upwind momentum and energy, explicit volume fraction; time step 0.1 µs.
  Solver not named. Grids of 123232, 193360 and 258300 cells: intermediate
  to fine deviation below 0.012 %, intermediate to coarse above 15.4 %
  (quantity unnamed); intermediate used. Six probes (three in feed lines,
  three in the recess). Only the baseline is simulated.

# Tests and results

* Baseline (§3.2): simulation 3246 Hz, 0.12 MPa; experiment 3165 Hz, about
  0.13 MPa; both spectra also show a second peak near 6.3–6.5 kHz (read from
  Figs. 8b, 9b; not discussed).
* Fig. 10a (gas 5 g/s): $f$ from about 1700 Hz at
  $\mathrm{Re}_l\approx7000$ to about 4100 Hz at $\mathrm{Re}_l\approx38000$
  (read from the figure).
* Fig. 10b: small liquid flows about 1.2–3.2 kHz; at 160 and 200 g/s about
  3–3.7 kHz, jumping to 8–9 kHz at gas Reynolds numbers of about
  0.85–0.9×10⁵ (read from the figure).
* Fig. 11: 8332 Hz, waveform about 0.07–0.13 MPa (read from the figure).
* Fig. 12: liquid momentum flux up to about 2.6×10⁵ kg m⁻¹ s⁻², gas up to
  about 1.3×10⁵ kg m⁻¹ s⁻² (figure axes).
