# Summary

The paper reports direct-connect experiments with non-premixed ethylene–air
continuous rotating detonation in a hollow (centerbody-less) cylindrical
chamber, 100 mm in diameter and 90 mm long, exhausting through a Laval
nozzle; seven nozzles give contraction ratios of 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12.
With air at 540–560 g/s and equivalence ratio 0.33–1.35 it maps the operating
domain (lean limit against contraction ratio) and identifies three
propagation modes: single wave, two co-rotating waves (only at contraction
ratio 12 and equivalence ratio at least 0.99), and a "sawtooth" wave near the
lean limit at contraction ratios 1, 2 and 4. Propagation stability is rated by
the relative standard deviation of the instantaneous frequency. The headline
is a single wave at 6.10 kHz and 1915.40 m/s, slightly above the computed
Chapman–Jouguet speed, in contrast with the deficits above 50 % and sawtooth
waveforms that earlier ethylene–air annular work reports.

# Key claims

1. **Operating domain** (§3, Fig. 4, Abstract). Rotating detonation over
   $\phi$ = 0.33–1.35 (tested range); the lean limit rises and the domain
   shrinks as the contraction ratio increases: 0.4 at $\varepsilon_c$ = 1,
   about 0.7 at $\varepsilon_c$ = 12 (read from the figure). Rich limit not
   sought. Shown.
2. **Why it is easy here** (§3.1). The hollow chamber satisfies the
   "channel width above half the cell size" criterion (attributed to
   Bykovskii et al.) and a recirculation zone at the sudden enlargement holds
   a deflagration flame as an ignition source; the lean-limit trend is
   inferred from lower main-stream velocity and wave height. Stated, not
   measured.
3. **Test 1** (§3.2.1, Figs. 6–7, Eqs. 1–5). $\varepsilon_c$ = 4,
   $\dot m_a$ = 543 g/s, $\phi$ = 1.09: single wave, mean 6.10 kHz (FFT
   6.02 kHz), $D$ = 1915.40 m/s against $D_{\mathrm{CJ}}$ = 1845.91 m/s
   (1 atm, 300 K), about 103.8 % (ratio computed by the reader); called the
   highest ethylene–air rotating-detonation speed reported; "correction
   methods" deferred to the literature. Shown.
4. **Test 2** (§3.2.2, Figs. 8–9). $\varepsilon_c$ = 12, 553 g/s,
   $\phi$ = 1.00: two waves; apparent 2775.76 m/s; delay ratio about 2/3
   gives $n_w$ = 2, hence 1387.88 m/s per wave, 74.62 % of $D_{\mathrm{CJ}}$;
   weaker pressure rise; a secondary peak behind each main peak attributed
   to the oblique shock reflected from the nozzle contraction (consistent with
   Zhang et al., attributed); 8.84 kHz (FFT 8.79 kHz). Shown.
5. **Mode transition** (§3.1, Fig. 5b). At $\varepsilon_c$ = 12 the frequency
   steps at $\phi\approx$ 0.99 from below the single-wave critical frequency
   $D_{\mathrm{CJ}}/(\pi d_c)$ to between the single- and two-wave values.
   Shown.
6. **Sawtooth wave** (§3.2.3, Figs. 10–13). Very low pressure rise, no steep
   front. Test 3 ($\varepsilon_c$ = 2, $\phi$ = 0.43): about 65 ms, then
   spontaneous quench; 4.32 kHz, 1356.48 m/s, 92.97 % of 1459.11 m/s. Test 4
   ($\varepsilon_c$ = 1, $\phi$ = 0.47): about 350 ms at 4.82 kHz; in blowdown
   the nitrogen purge pushes extra ethylene in and the wave becomes a
   high-pressure-rise detonation at about 6 kHz. Shown.
7. **Sawtooth interpretation** (§3.2.3). "A manifestation of high-frequency
   tangential instability in rocket combustor" and a critical condition of
   rotating detonation (too little heat release or pressure to couple the
   inducing shock to the flame). Stated, not demonstrated.
8. **Stability metric** (§3.3, Figs. 14–15, Eqs. 6–7). The relative standard
   deviation of the instantaneous frequency decreases with $\phi$ for
   $\varepsilon_c$ = 1, 2, 4, 6 (attributed to the nozzle isolating back
   pressure) and increases for 8, 10, 12 (attributed to the reflected oblique
   shock); below 1 % for two near-lean-limit runs at $\varepsilon_c$ = 8;
   highest for the two-wave mode; around stoichiometric, $\varepsilon_c$ = 2
   and 4 give the lowest value and super-CJ speeds; $\varepsilon_c$ = 2–4 is
   the best choice. Shown; attributions interpretive.

# Methods

* **Facility** (§2, Fig. 2). NUDT Scramjet Laboratory direct-connect rig;
  hollow chamber 100 mm × 90 mm; 200 mm Laval nozzle, seven interchangeable
  nozzles, contraction ratio defined as inlet over throat area. Slit–orifice
  collision injection: air through a convergent–divergent radial slit with a
  0.7 mm throat, ethylene through 90 orifices; sudden enlargement downstream
  of the injector; exhaust to atmosphere.
* **Initiation** (Fig. 3). Tangential spark-ignited H₂/O₂ pre-detonator.
  Sequence: air, ethylene, pre-detonator fill, spark. Blowdown: nitrogen
  valve opens, ethylene closes.
* **Metering.** Sonic nozzles; turbine flowmeter calibrated within 1 %;
  $\dot m_a$ = 540–560 g/s, $\dot m_f$ = 12.8–47.5 g/s. Chamber and plenum
  pressures not reported.
* **Diagnostics.** Two PCB 113B24 sensors (0.725 mV/kPa, resolution
  0.035 kPa) at one axial station 120° apart (about 40 mm from the
  injection plane, read from the sketch), 2 MHz sampling, timing error within
  1 %; high-pass filtering of thermal drift; FFT on one sensor.
* **Chapman–Jouguet speed.** "Computed" at 1 atm and 300 K; code not stated.
* **Uncertainty.** Only the 1 % flow and timing figures; no repeatability or
  velocity error bars. No simulation.
