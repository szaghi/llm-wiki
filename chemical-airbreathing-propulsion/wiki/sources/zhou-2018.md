# Summary

The authors run hydrogen–air rotating detonation experiments in an annular
combustor (inner and outer diameters 94 and 106 mm) that discharges through a
convergent section and a turbine guide vane into an axial-flow turbine of
66 mm outer diameter, so the combustor drives a real rotating turbine in an
open cycle. Two ignition devices are compared, an automotive spark plug
(test 1) and an H₂/O₂ pre-detonator (test 2), at an air flow of 170 g/s and
equivalence ratios of 0.6–1.4. In both cases a single stable wave forms after
a "formation process" lasting on average about 0.159 s with the spark plug
and 0.022 s with the pre-detonator; the final state does not depend on the
igniter. With spark ignition the formation passes through two co-rotating
waves, a "two-peak" wave and strong–weak alternation before one stable wave.
Measured wave speeds are about 20 % below CEA Chapman–Jouguet values.

# Key claims

1. **Stable operation with a turbine** (§3.1–3.2, Fig. 6, Conclusion 1). A
   stable single wave at every tested $\phi$ except 0.6, with either igniter.
   Shown.
2. **Speed from frequency** (§3.1, Eq. 1). $D=\pi d_c f/n_w$ with $d_c$ the
   106 mm outer diameter and $f$ the FFT dominant frequency. Standard
   kinematic relation.
3. **Composite operation** (§3.1, Fig. 3). At $\phi$ = 1.17, 4.79 kHz and one
   wave give 1.59 km/s; mean pressure at the head sensor about 0.6 MPa
   (6 bar). Shown.
4. **Guide-vane shocks** (§3.1, Fig. 3d). Shocks in front of the guide vane;
   part of the oblique shock reflects into the combustor from the concave
   blade surfaces (incidence about 25°); minor influence stated. A 64 %
   pressure reduction across the vane and 0.04 MPa (0.4 bar) at the vane exit
   are attributed to the authors' earlier paper.
5. **Igniter sets the formation time** (§3.2, Figs. 4–5). At $\phi$ = 1:
   spark plug about 0.15 s, then 4.73 kHz and 1.57 km/s; pre-detonator
   0.02 s, then 4.7 kHz and 1.56 km/s; final state independent of the
   igniter, in agreement with the literature (attributed). Shown.
6. **Formation time against $\phi$** (§3.2, Fig. 6). Decreasing in $\phi$;
   means 0.159 s and 0.022 s; $\phi$ = 0.6 never stable (plotted at 0.4 s).
   Read from Fig. 6: test 1 from about 0.21 s at $\phi\approx$ 0.68 to about
   0.12–0.13 s at $\phi\approx$ 1.33–1.41; test 2 from about 0.05 s to under
   0.01 s. Shown.
7. **Turbine effect** (§3.2). The 0.15 s formation time against 2.23 ms in
   the authors' earlier turbine-free combustor is read as "relatively large
   effects of turbine", the $\phi$ and igniter dependence attributed to
   turbine spin-up; steady turbine speed 12000 rpm (200 Hz) against a wave
   frequency of about 4.5 kHz; a rule linking the two is "hard" to extract.
   Hypothesis; no turbine-free control or speed history.
8. **Formation sequence with spark ignition** (§3.3, Fig. 7). Two co-rotating
   waves (7.76 kHz text, 7.67 kHz figure; sensors 90° apart about 180° out of
   phase); after about 20 ms a "two-peak" wave at 7.53 kHz, speculated to be
   a detonation flame split in two; strong–weak alternation (one strong, two
   weaker) for about 0.1 s at 4.91 kHz; stable single wave at 0.148 s. Shown.
9. **Reaction-zone postposition** (§3.3, Figs. 8–9). For about 5 ms before
   the stable state the downstream sensor reads higher than the upstream one,
   interpreted as the reaction zone moving backward. Trace shown;
   interpretation.
10. **Repeatability** (§3.4, Fig. 10). Nine runs at $\phi$ = 0.85: 1.5–1.55
    km/s. Shown.
11. **Velocity deficit** (§3.4, Fig. 11). Speed rises with $\phi$, about 1.32
    to 1.65 km/s over $\phi$ = 0.6–1.4, against CEA CJ of about 1.70 to
    2.07 km/s and an isobaric sound speed of about 0.97 to 1.16 km/s (read
    from the figure); stated deficit about 20 %, attributed without proof
    mainly to the engine structure and reactant mixing.

# Methods

* **Rig** (§2, Fig. 1). Hydrogen and air supplies, annular combustor
  (94/106 mm, 6 mm gap), convergent section, turbine guide vane, axial-flow
  turbine (66 mm) with output shaft. Axial lengths, blade count and turbine
  geometry not given.
* **Injection.** Slot–orifice: 120 hydrogen orifices of 0.8 mm, 1.5 mm
  annular air slot; hydrogen supply about 0.28 MPa (2.8 bar), rising with
  flow; near-sonic injection after detonation (stated); sonic-nozzle
  metering; reactants and ambient at 282 K and 1 atm; dry air (N₂/O₂/Ar/CO₂ =
  78.09/20.94/0.94/0.033 vol%).
* **Operation.** Air 170 g/s, $\phi$ = 0.6–1.4, 0.4 s supply, about 0.05 s
  more on residual plenum gas. Spark plug or vertically mounted
  pre-detonator (H₂/O₂, 2 g/s, $\phi$ = 1) at the same location.
* **Diagnostics.** Three piezoresistive sensors (0.1 % FS, response under
  1 ms) in front of the vane and in the two plenums; two PCB sensors at
  550 kHz on the outer wall, 8 and 16 mm from the head, 90° apart; one PCB
  sensor in front of the vane; an ion probe at the first sensor's axial
  station; NI X-series acquisition.
* **Reduction.** FFT dominant frequency, per-cycle frequency from peak
  intervals, Eq. 1; CJ speed and isobaric sound speed from NASA CEA.
  Fig. 11 error bars undefined. No simulation.
