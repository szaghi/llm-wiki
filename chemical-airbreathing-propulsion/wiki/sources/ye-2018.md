# Summary

The paper designs a kerosene-fuelled RBCC combustor whose expansion angle
and geometric-throat area are set by hydraulic actuators to suit the
inflow (design range Mach 2–7, Table 1), and tests it in ground
direct-connect experiments at simulated flight Mach 2, 3, 4 and 6 on the
NWPU RBCC test bench. A low-flow primary rocket in the strut acts as a
pilot flame for the secondary kerosene; at Mach 4 and 6, shutting the
rocket off gives a smooth rocket-ramjet to ramjet mode transition. Steady
3-D RANS simulations in CFD++ (cubic k–ε, Eulerian dispersed-phase spray,
Westbrook–Dryer quasi-global kerosene mechanism) compare the
variable-geometry combustor (geometric throat) with a fixed-geometry one
(thermal throat) at Mach 3 and 6. The variable combustor has a shorter,
more concentrated heat-release zone, a lower combustor Mach number, higher
combustion efficiency (0.90 against 0.81 at Mach 3, 0.92 against 0.78 at
Mach 6), higher exit total-pressure recovery and higher specific impulse
(+18.6% at Mach 3, +26.2% at Mach 6).

# Key claims

1. **Design relations** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–4). Assuming isobaric heating, under
   which the velocity stays constant, with $\tau_e=T_{te}/T_{ti}$ and $k$
   the specific-heat ratio:
   $$\frac{Ma_e}{Ma_i}=\left(\tau_e\left(1+\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_i^2\right)-\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_i^2\right)^{-1/2}$$
   (Eq. 1); neglecting viscous drag and added fuel mass,
   $$\frac{A_e}{A_i}=\tau_e\left(1+\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_i^2\right)-\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_i^2$$
   (Eq. 2); the total-pressure ratio
   $$\frac{p_{te}}{p_{ti}}=\left(\frac{1+\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_e^2}{1+\frac{k-1}{2}Ma_i^2}\right)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}$$
   (Eq. 3); and the geometric throat area from the mass-flow formula,
   $A_t=\dot m\sqrt{T_{te}}/(K\,p_{te})$ (Eq. 4). Stated, not derived;
   Eq. 2 is the inverse square of Eq. 1, as constant velocity requires.
2. **Configuration schedule** (Table 1). Expansion ratio and throat ratio
   decrease with flight Mach over 2–6; at Mach 7 the combustor switches to
   an expanded configuration without a geometric throat.
3. **Rocket pilot** (§4.1, Fig. 6, Conclusion 1; experiment). The secondary
   kerosene does not ignite reliably without an ignition source; a primary
   rocket at 0.12 kg/s (≤5% of the Mach 2 airflow) gives reliable ignition
   and stable, efficient combustion at Mach 2–6. Shown.
4. **Mode transition** (§4.1, Figs. 6c–d, 7, Table 5; experiment). Turning
   the rocket off at Mach 4 and 6 moves the engine from rocket-ramjet to
   ramjet mode; the precombustion shock train moves toward the combustor
   and stays in the isolator. Thrust falls by 10.8% (Mach 4) and 12.2%
   (Mach 6); combustion efficiency rises from 0.898 to 0.947 (Mach 4) and
   from 0.890 to 0.966 (Mach 6). Specific-impulse gains are printed as
   28.2% and 37.1% (see Recorded as printed). Shown.
5. **Mach 3, geometric against thermal throat** (§4.2.1, Figs. 8–10;
   simulation). In the variable combustor heat release is concentrated
   between the fuel pylons and the geometric throat, in a zone 20% shorter
   than in the fixed combustor (stated to help thermal protection), and
   combustion is isobaric. The fixed combustor (2° expansion) has pressure
   rising before secondary injection and then falling. Both run in ramjet
   mode; the fixed one forms a thermal choke with a complex
   three-dimensional sonic surface. Shown.
6. **Mach 6** (§4.2.2, Figs. 11–13; simulation). The variable combustor
   forms a precombustion shock train in the isolator and runs fully in
   ramjet mode; the fixed one keeps supersonic flow outside the pylons, a
   "ramjet-scramjet mixed mode". Shown.
7. **Total-pressure recovery** (§4.3, Fig. 14; simulation). Recovery
   (mass-weighted total pressure over the isolator-inlet total pressure)
   at the combustor exit, variable against fixed: 0.63 against 0.58 at
   Mach 3 (+8.6%), 0.32 against 0.24 at Mach 6 (+33.3%). The variable
   combustor loses more in the isolator (stronger shock train) and less to
   combustion (lower Mach number). Shown.
8. **Combustion efficiency** (§4.3, Fig. 15; simulation). At Mach 3 heat
   release starts at the rocket exit ($x/H=10$) and efficiency rises
   quickly after secondary injection ($x/H=14$); exit values 0.90 against
   0.81 (Mach 3) and 0.92 against 0.78 (Mach 6). Shown.
9. **Performance** (Table 6; simulation). Specific impulse 1096 against
   924 s at Mach 3 and 1016 against 805 s at Mach 6, attributed jointly to
   lower combustor Mach number and higher combustion efficiency
   (Conclusion 3). Shown.
10. **Definitions** (§4.1). Specific impulse $I_{sp}=F/(g_0\dot m_{fuel,total})$
    with rocket propellant included; combustion efficiency
    $\eta_b=(T_t(x)-T_{ti})/(T_{AFT}-T_{ti})$ with the CEA adiabatic flame
    temperature. In tests the throat is assumed choked, so throat total
    pressure follows from throat static pressure and total temperature
    from the mass flow; pressure force from integrated wall pressure, drag
    from CFD.

# Methods

* **Facility** (§2.2, Figs. 2–4). NWPU RBCC direct-connect bench; a
  gas-oxygen–alcohol vitiated heater with oxygen made up to a mass fraction
  of 0.23; a facility nozzle sets the combustor-inlet Mach number.
* **Model engine.** Facility nozzle, isolator (channels on both sides of a
  central strut), primary rocket in the strut, rectangular combustor
  (straight section, fixed section with fuel pylons, variable section),
  inner nozzle. The variable section is four rods with hinge supports,
  moved by two parallel hydraulic actuators; the geometric throat sits at
  the combustor/inner-nozzle junction. The fixed combustor is the variable
  section set to an expansion without a throat. Secondary liquid kerosene
  through streamwise-vortex pylons.
* **Primary rocket** (Table 4). 0.12 kg/s, O/F about 1.0, chamber pressure
  1.5 MPa, chamber temperature 1850 K; mass fractions (%) CO₂ 0.1, H₂O 0.1,
  H₂ 5.7, CO 94.1.
* **Inflow** (Table 2):

| Flight Mach | Air flow (kg/s) | Total temperature (K) | Total pressure (MPa) | Isolator Mach | $\phi$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2.8 | 400 | 0.3 | 1.3 | 0.6 |
| 3 | 4.7 | 600 | 0.6 | 1.6 | 0.6 |
| 4 | 4.0 | 880 | 0.9 | 2.0 | 0.7 |
| 6 | 3.3 | 1650 | 1.3 | 2.5 | 1.0 |

* **Diagnostics.** Wall pressure sensors (−0.1 to 1 MPa, 0.25% full-scale
  uncertainty, up to 1000 Hz); outlet flame photographs. Combustor total
  temperature and internal drag not measurable. Sequence (Fig. 4):
  secondary fuel on, rocket on and ignition, rocket off.
* **Numerics** (§3). CFD++ (Metacomp), steady 3-D nonlinear RANS, cubic
  k–ε; Eulerian dispersed-phase spray; turbulence–chemistry interaction
  neglected; surrogate C₁₀H₂₀ with the Westbrook–Dryer quasi-global
  mechanism, 10 species and 12 reactions (Table 3). Half domain from the
  facility-nozzle entrance to the combustor exit; inlet static temperature
  and mass flow from the test; rocket species and temperature from NASA
  CEA; supersonic outflow; adiabatic no-slip walls. ICEM grid, 1.2 million
  nodes, first cell 0.01 mm, maximum combustor $y^+$ 30; 64 processors,
  about 5 days per case. Scheme and order not stated; no grid-independence
  study for this geometry (an earlier one at Mach 5.5 is cited, ref. 32).
  Validation against sidewall pressure at Mach 3 and 6 judged "well fits"
  (Fig. 5), without a metric. Setup validated earlier on HIFiRE-2 (refs.
  26, 28, 29).

# Tests and results

* **Table 5** (experiment; forces in N):

| Flight Mach | Rocket | Pressure force | Drag | Thrust | $I_{sp}$ (s) | Combustion efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | on | 2044 | −235 | 1809 | 730 | 0.713 |
| 3 | on | 3705 | −257 | 3367 | 1103 | 0.927 |
| 4 | on | 3650 | −340 | 3310 | 1011 | 0.898 |
| 4 | off | 3292 | −340 | 2952 | 1408 | 0.947 |
| 6 | on | 3086 | −471 | 2737 | 808 | 0.890 |
| 6 | off | 2752 | −471 | 2403 | 1026 | 0.966 |

* **Table 6** (simulation, fixed / variable): Mach 3 pressure force
  3163 / 3684 N, drag −274 / −257 N, thrust 2889 / 3427 N, $I_{sp}$
  924 / 1096 s, exit efficiency 0.81 / 0.90, exit recovery 0.58 / 0.63;
  Mach 6 pressure force 2427 / 2849 N, drag −582 / −471 N, thrust
  1845 / 2378 N, $I_{sp}$ 805 / 1016 s, efficiency 0.78 / 0.92, recovery
  0.24 / 0.32.
* **Fig. 6** (experiment). Cold flow at Mach 2 and 3: expansion at the
  isolator exit, pressure rise from pylon blockage, slight rise ahead of
  the throat, exit near atmospheric. Hot flow: boundary-layer separation
  and a normal shock train in the isolator, subsonic combustor inflow,
  isobaric combustion ahead of the geometric throat.
* **Fig. 7.** A bright Mach disk at the outlet in ramjet mode
  (under-expansion against the atmosphere).
* **Fig. 11.** At Mach 6 with the rocket off, recirculation behind the
  rocket lets the flame propagate upstream.
