# Summary

The paper studies how the axial distribution of heat release in a
kerosene-fuelled RBCC engine with an embedded strut rocket sets the
"matching" between the pre-combustion shock train in the isolator,
combustion, and thermal choking in the combustor and nozzle, at flight
Mach 3 to 6. It uses 3D RANS (ANSYS FLUENT) simulations of the integrated
flow path (inlet, isolator, two-section combustor, nozzle; half model,
about 3.3 million cells) with SST k–ω, a Lagrangian kerosene spray and a
three-step global kerosene mechanism, validated against a Mach 3
direct-connect test and against Mach 4 and 5 freejet tests in a CARDC
shock tunnel. The shock train balances the intake–combustion pressure
difference by strengthening as it moves upstream. At Mach 3 the main heat
release should sit in the downstream (larger-area) cavity, with thermal
choking at the combustor exit, and it should move upstream as flight Mach
rises. Splitting the fuel between injection stations ("distributed heat
release") keeps the inlet started and raises combustion efficiency; at
Mach 6 with the rocket off, isolator injection sustains scramjet
combustion.

# Key claims

1. **Shock-train self-balancing** (§4.1, Fig. 7, Case 3, Mach 5; unsteady
   CFD). After ignition at $t_0$ combustion grows behind the pylon until
   $t_0$+5.7 ms, then pushes the pre-combustion shock upstream until
   $t_0$+9.7 ms. During this motion the shock angles and pre-shock velocity
   rise, so the pressure ratio across the shock rises until it balances
   the combustion pressure; the shock then stabilises in the forepart of
   the isolator. Thermal choking appears at the exit of the frontal
   cavity. Shown.
2. **Shock strength** (Eq. 4, attributed to Emanuel, ref. 37): the
   normal-shock static-pressure ratio from the Mach number ahead of the
   shock, $\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\mathrm{Ma}_1^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}$.
   Stated.
3. **Distributed heat release at Mach 4** (§4.2, Fig. 8). Case 2-1 (all
   fuel at I1, $\phi=0.9$) puts the shock at the isolator entrance, the
   limit of inlet operation. Case 2-2 ($\phi$ 0.7 + 0.2) moves it back into
   the isolator and raises total heat release by 18%; thermal choking moves
   downstream. Case 2-3 ($\phi$ 0.3 + 0.6) lowers combustion efficiency,
   with total heat release 8% below Case 2-2. Final efficiencies read from
   Fig. 8d: about 89% (2-2) and about 85% (2-3). Shown.
4. **Mach 3** (§4.2, Fig. 9). Front-heavy injection (Case 1-1, $\phi$
   0.6 + 0.3) raises pressure to 4.5 times the reference pressure, pushes
   the shock into the inlet throat, spills more air through the bleed and
   cuts capture by 17%. Case 1-2 ($\phi$ 0.3 + 0.6) restores inlet
   operation and moves thermal choking downstream; thrust coefficient
   0.458 against 0.402, stated as "+12%". Shown.
5. **Mach 6 scramjet mode** (§4.2, Fig. 10). Splitting fuel between I0
   (isolator, $\phi=0.4$) and I1 ($\phi=0.6$) raises combustion efficiency
   (about 89% against about 81% for all fuel at I1, read from Fig. 10a)
   and the pressure in region L1. No clear thermal choking forms;
   subsonic and supersonic regions coexist. Shown.
6. **Best thermal-choking location** (Fig. 11, Table 5). At the combustor
   exit (large-expansion section) at Mach 3, moving upstream
   (smaller-expansion region) as flight Mach rises. Shown.
7. **Freejet validation** (§4.3, Figs. 12–14). At Mach 4 (Case 2-2
   injection) ignition at $t_0$+2.7 ms, pressure rise to $t_0$+8.3 ms,
   stable at $t_0$+30 ms with a peak of about 4.3 times the reference
   pressure; shock train stabilised at the isolator entrance, $x/h=22$. At
   Mach 5 (Case 3 injection) peak 3.9 times the reference pressure. CFD
   agrees with measured wall pressure in "range and trend" (no metric).
   Schlieren shows the external compression shocks unchanged by
   combustion. Shown.
8. **Rocket-off scramjet combustion at Mach 6** (§4.3, Fig. 15;
   direct-connect test). With $\phi$ 0.4 + 0.6, combustion pressure 3.8
   times the reference pressure in the forepart of the combustor with the
   rocket off; combustor-entrance pressure only 0.2 reference pressures
   below the rocket-on case. Shown (test conditions not stated).
9. **Literature** (§1, attributed). Laurence (ref. 11): in HyShot II the
   combustion shock forms and moves upstream only for $\phi>0.38$. Fotia
   (ref. 12): shock-train strength falls with fuel flux. Tamaki (ref. 13):
   the shock changes from λ to X shape as mainstream velocity rises.
   Doolan (ref. 14, printed first author Byrne): thermal choking forms
   more easily in a constant-area section. Riggins (ref. 16): a gradient
   heat-release model is superior to a linear one. Wang (ref. 17): an
   upstream injector and faster heat release move the thermal throat
   forward. Thomas (ref. 18): direct-connect and freejet tests differ in
   shock-train length and ignition.

# Methods

* **Engine** (§2, Figs. 1–2, Table 1). Mixed-compression inlet with
  design Mach 5.5, compression angles 8.3° and 9.8° (Fig. 2: 8.3° and
  18.1° cumulative), boundary-layer bleed block; isolator; two-section
  rectangular combustor with two cavities, pylon and wall injection;
  nozzle with design Mach 4.0 and initial expansion angle 20°. Stations
  (Fig. 2): inlet 0–25.1 $x/h$, isolator to 35.3, combustor to 63, nozzle
  to 89; injectors I0 at $x/h=28$, I1 at 42, I2 at 50. Area ratios: section
  1 exit 1.2H, section 2 exit 1.8H.
* **Strut rocket** (§2.2). GOX/liquid kerosene, 0.15 kg/s, mixture ratio
  1.2, nozzle expansion ratio 6. In CFD a mass-flow inlet at 1855 K with
  CEA composition (mass fractions CO 0.941, H₂ 0.057, CO₂ 0.001, H₂O
  0.001); rocket combustion not simulated.
* **Numerics** (§3.1–3.3). ANSYS FLUENT 3D RANS, unsteady for the
  shock–combustion transient and steady for the heat-release studies;
  SST k–ω (low-Reynolds constants printed); turbulent Prandtl and Schmidt
  numbers 0.9; Lagrangian spray with non-equilibrium evaporation and
  boiling; laminar finite-rate three-step kerosene mechanism (Table 2).
  Discretization scheme, order, time step and solver type not stated.
  Structured half-model grid of about 3.3 million cells with an external
  field. Pressure far-field entrance, pressure outlets, adiabatic no-slip
  walls. Flight conditions: Mach 3.0 at 12 km, 4.0 at 17 km, 5.0 at 21 km,
  6.0 at 24 km.
* **Validation** (§3.3, Fig. 4). Mach 3 direct-connect test: air
  4.36 kg/s, 0.56 MPa, 600 K, I1 $\phi=0.5$, rocket on. Combustor meshes of
  2.2 and 6.86 million cells differ by less than 7%; agreement with the
  test is "high accordance" without a metric. Maximum discrete-phase error
  accumulation 1.831% (refs. 34, 35).
* **Shock tunnel** (§3.4, Table 3, Figs. 5–6). CARDC combustion-heated
  (H₂/O₂) shock tunnel, nozzle exit diameter 2400 mm, test window 400 ms.
  Freejet at Mach 4 (17 km; total pressure 0.52 MPa, total temperature
  845 K, Re $9.92\times10^6$) and Mach 5 (21 km; 1.35 MPa, 1124 K, Re
  $8.97\times10^6$), angle of attack 0°. 13 inlet, 34 combustor and 6
  nozzle pressure taps; schlieren. Measurement uncertainty not stated.

by the freestream dynamic pressure times the capture area.
