# Summary

The paper builds a phenomenological aging constitutive model for an HTPB
coating, the buffer and insulation layer between propellant and case of a
solid rocket motor. The aging state is the NMR crosslink density rather than
time and temperature. Samples were aged thermo-oxidatively at 323.15,
333.15, 343.15 and 353.15 K for 50 days, with uniaxial tension and NMR
crosslink density measured every 5 days. Of three empirical laws, a power law
in time with exponent 0.3 fits the crosslink density best; its rate constant
follows a modified Arrhenius law with a temperature-power factor. The stress
is an aging function of strain and crosslink density, a sum of two
exponentials, times a second-order polynomial (five-parameter,
Mooney–Rivlin-type) hyperelastic law fitted to the multi-step-relaxation
equilibrium curve. A held-out 313.15 K aging test gives crosslink-density
prediction errors of 0.19–0.58 %; tensile curves are reproduced
qualitatively.

# Key claims

1. **Equilibrium curve from multi-step relaxation** (§2.2, Fig. 3). Single
   step: 0.23 → 0.12 MPa in 600 s, 0.11 MPa at 1800 s, so 91.67 % of the drop
   in 600 s, justifying 600 s holds. Shown.
2. **Aging stiffens the coating** (§2.3, Figs. 4–5). Longer or hotter aging
   lowers maximum elongation, raises modulus and strength; curves show
   elastic, "yield" and stress-intensification stages. Shown; micromechanics
   stated.
3. **Crosslink density rises monotonically** (§2.4, Fig. 7). Fast then slow,
   same shape at all temperatures (one mechanism); post-curing and oxidative
   crosslinking early, oxidation slightly outweighing chain scission later
   (attributed). From about 3.0×10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³ to about 4.85, 5.2, 6.1 and
   6.55×10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³ after 50 days (read from the figure). Shown.
4. **Kinetic model selection** (§3, Eqs. 1–3, Table 1). In the paper's
   notation: logarithmic $\nu_e=\nu_{e0}+k\lg t$, power
   $\nu_e=\nu_{e0}+kt^{\alpha}$, and an "exponential" law misprinted as the
   logarithmic one; power law with $\alpha$ = 0.3 best ($r$ =
   0.9684–0.9828; logarithmic 0.8397–0.8887; exponential 0.8734–0.9353).
   Shown; the exponent scan not tabulated.
5. **Modified Arrhenius** (§3, Eq. 4, Table 2). $\ln k=\ln A+m\ln T-E/(RT)$,
   $R$ = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; $\ln A$ = 213.5, $m$ = −30.21,
   $E$ = 1.062×10⁵ (unit not printed), $r$ = 0.9971; form attributed. The
   reader's recomputation gives rate constants 0.557, 0.726, 0.909, 1.095
   against Table 1's 0.5875, 0.7417, 0.9772, 1.1370 (2–7 % apart). Fitted.
6. **Hyperelastic part** (§4.1, Eqs. 12, 14, 15, Table 3). Incompressible
   uniaxial tension, $\lambda=1+\varepsilon$; second-order polynomial strain
   energy in $I_1$, $I_2$; $C_{01}$ = −0.1957, $C_{02}$ = −0.1390,
   $C_{10}$ = 0.4001, $C_{20}$ = −0.005041, $C_{11}$ = 0.0333, $r$ = 0.9999,
   strains 0–3 (Fig. 8); $N$ = 2 from "application experience". Fitted.
7. **Aging function** (§4.2, Eqs. 5, 16, 17, Table 4, Figs. 9–10).
   $\sigma_x=g(\nu_e,\varepsilon)\,\sigma_e(\varepsilon)$, $g$ measured as
   tensile over equilibrium stress, $g=ae^{b\varepsilon}+ce^{d\varepsilon}$;
   $a=-0.009045\nu_e^2+0.01206\nu_e+0.9628$ ($r$ = −0.9417),
   $b=-0.8583\nu_e+0.4713$ (−0.9023), $c=0.5303\nu_e-0.5374$ (0.9147),
   $d=-0.02202\nu_e+0.1428$ (−0.8971), $\nu_e$ in 10⁻⁴ mol cm⁻³; full model
   Eq. 18. Fitted.
8. **Verification** (§5, Table 5, Fig. 11). 313.15 K, 10–50 days: errors
   0.58, 0.40, 0.51, 0.45, 0.19 %; tensile curves "accurate" without a
   metric, worse at 10 days and near fracture (damage neglected). Shown
   qualitatively.

# Methods

* **Material.** One batch of HTPB coating (State-owned No. 845 Plant,
  Xi'an), HTPB rubber with corrosion-resistant and anti-abrasive fillers,
  composition not stated; 2 mm dumbbells to ASTM D624-00(R2007).
* **Aging** (§2.1, Fig. 1). GB/T3512-2001, DU288 oil-bath thermostats,
  ±1 K, humidity below 30 %, 50 days, 10 samples per 5-day sampling (5
  tension, 5 NMR); a fifth thermostat at 313.15 K for verification.
* **Relaxation** (§2.2). INSTRON 5982 at 100 mm/min; 10 % strain steps with
  600 s holds to fracture; single-step test to 1800 s (strain level not
  stated).
* **Tension** (§2.3). QJ916-85, 100 mm/min to fracture, 5 samples averaged;
  test temperature not stated.
* **NMR** (§2.4). VTMR20-010V-T (NIU MAG); 8×2×2 mm strips; 293.15 ± 2 K,
  10 repeats with outliers removed; inversion to crosslink density not
  stated.
* **Fitting.** Least squares; no confidence intervals. No finite-element
  computation.
