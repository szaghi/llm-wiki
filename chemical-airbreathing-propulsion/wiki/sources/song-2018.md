# Summary

The paper reports direct-connect experiments on lean blowoff of an
ethylene-fuelled model scramjet combustor with a cavity flameholder and
fuel injected upstream of the cavity ("cavity passive fuel injection"),
with inflow at Mach 2.52 and 1600 K stagnation temperature (flight Mach 6
simulation). Lean blowoff limits are measured as minimum injection
pressure against injection distance for one 2 mm orifice and four 1 mm
orifices, sized to give the same global equivalence ratio at equal
injection pressure. The limits rise with injection distance and are higher
for the single orifice. Four-orifice flames never blow off at constant
fuel supply; single-orifice flames show intermittent local extinction and
reignition near the limit. Below the limit, luminosity imaging at
20 000 frame/s resolves the blowoff in five steps. FLUENT RANS simulations
(SST k–ω, one-step ethylene chemistry) of the 10 mm cases explain the
difference through the cavity temperature and fuel distributions.

# Key claims

1. **Lean blowoff limit trend** (§3.1, Fig. 5, Conclusion 1). The limit,
   defined as the minimum injection pressure keeping combustion
   continuous, rises with injection distance and is higher for one 2 mm
   orifice than for four 1 mm orifices. Shown. Reader's readings of Fig. 5
   (stable / failed point, MPa): single orifice at about 10, 30, 50, 110
   and 180 mm: 0.8/0.6, 1.4/1.2, 1.4/1.2, 1.6/1.4, 1.8/1.6; four orifices
   at about 10, 30, 60, 180 mm: 0.4/0.2, 0.4/0.2, 0.6/0.4, 1.0/0.8. The
   right axis maps injection pressure linearly to global equivalence ratio
   (0.2 at 2 MPa), so the limits lie at equivalence ratios of about
   0.02–0.18.
2. **Explanation of the distance trend** (§3.1). Stated, not computed: a
   jet-penetration correlation (ref. 48) implies more penetration above the
   cavity and less fuel entrained into the shear layer at larger distance;
   the much lower single-orifice limit at 10 mm is attributed to jet–cavity
   interaction pumping fuel into the recirculation zone, after Wang et al.
   (ref. 14).
3. **Flame stability** (§3.1, Figs. 6–7). Four-orifice flames are
   basically steady and stabilised in the cavity shear layer; near the
   limit (0.4 MPa) the flame lies only below the cavity mouth.
   Single-orifice flames (0.8 and 1.8 MPa) are strongly unsteady, with
   intermittent local extinction in the shear layer. "No obvious change"
   with injection distance. Shown.
4. **Wall pressure** (Fig. 8). Combustion raises lower-wall pressure only
   slightly (peak about 122 kPa near the cavity rear, cold flow about
   110 kPa). At equal injection pressure (0.8 MPa) four orifices give the
   higher rise in the cavity and the lower rise downstream. At the
   respective lean boundaries the single orifice gives the higher rise
   (about 98 kPa at 300 mm against 74 kPa for four orifices at 0.4 MPa and
   62 kPa cold), read by the authors as single-orifice flames needing more
   combustion intensity to persist. Pressures at tap A are steady over
   0.5 s. Shown (figure readings).
5. **CFD rationale** (Figs. 9–10). With four orifices the cavity is hot
   throughout, the spanwise distribution is uniform and the fuel jet is
   closer to the cavity; with one orifice high temperature is confined to
   the cavity rear and fuel stays in the jet wake. Shown numerically.
6. **Local extinction and reignition** (§3.2, Fig. 11; single orifice,
   10 mm, 0.8 MPa). 0.00–0.15 ms shear-layer flame weakens; 0.20 ms local
   extinction near the trailing edge splits the flame; 0.25 ms the
   downstream part blows off and the rest shrinks into the cavity;
   0.30–0.45 ms the flame re-spreads; 0.50 ms it breaks again; 0.65–1.00 ms
   it strengthens and re-spreads to the main stream. Shown. Mechanism
   stated, not measured: the shear layer moves down under an inflow
   disturbance (left for further study); flame stretch beyond what the
   flame withstands near the trailing edge causes extinction (ref. 35); a
   weaker flame lifts the shear layer less, strengthening the shear-layer
   and trailing-edge interaction, which entrains more fuel into the
   recirculation zone and drives reignition.
7. **Five-step blowoff** (§3.3, Fig. 12; single orifice, 0.6 MPa). (i)
   shear-layer flame weakens; (ii) partial extinction near the trailing
   edge; (iii) flame shrinks into the rear of the cavity; (iv) it moves to
   the front half, near the front wall; (v) complete extinction. Shown.
   Four-orifice flames show no blowoff at constant fuel supply; below
   their boundary spark ignition fails, and an ethylene flame lit by a
   hydrogen pilot dies when the hydrogen is cut.
8. **Region migration** (Fig. 13; non-reacting CFD). The shear layer has
   the highest velocity and lowest static temperature, the front half of
   the cavity the highest temperature and lowest velocity, the rear part
   intermediate; the authors read the blowoff migration as a move toward
   the "more combustible" region. Qualitative.
9. **Literature correlation** (§1, attributed to Driscoll & Rasmussen,
   refs. 22, 23). A modified Damköhler number with a non-premixed flame
   time from shear-layer similarity; as printed, its inverse equals the
   overall equivalence ratio for lean blowoff limits and the inverse of
   that ratio for rich limits. Stated.

# Methods

* **Facility.** NUDT direct-connect rig (ref. 43): air/O₂/alcohol vitiated
  heater, stagnation temperature 1600 K, stagnation pressure up to
  1.65 MPa, Mach 2.52 nozzle (flight Mach 6), about 1.0 kg/s; heater run
  6–7 s, fuel injection 1.8–2 s; uncooled walls.
* **Geometry** (Figs. 2–4). Isolator 315 mm, combustor 512 mm, expansion
  section 270 mm (3°); entrance 50 mm × 40 mm; lower wall diverging at 3.1°
  behind the cavity. Cavity on the lower wall: depth 16 mm, aft depth
  16 mm, length 64 mm, aft ramp 45°. Quartz windows on sides and top.
* **Injection** (Fig. 3). Four 1 mm orifices at 10, 30, 60, 180 mm upstream
  of the cavity, or one 2 mm orifice at 10, 30, 50, 110, 170 mm
  (cumulative distances from Fig. 3b). Ethylene at room temperature.
* **Procedure.** Injection pressure lowered in 0.2 MPa steps from stable
  combustion, set by a pressure-reducing valve with a 0.1 MPa display; each
  point repeated at least 5 times at and below the boundary. Spark-plug
  ignition through the cavity floor; a hydrogen pilot is mentioned in
  §3.3.
* **Diagnostics.** Flame luminosity at 20 000 frame/s, shutter 1/20416 s,
  f-number 1.4. Lower-wall static pressure at 100 Hz, ±0.5% transducer
  uncertainty (ref. 44); 0.5 mm taps.
* **Numerics.** 3D RANS with SST k–ω, FLUENT density-based coupled double
  precision; Finite-Rate/No TCI with a one-step ethylene–air mechanism
  (validation attributed to Huang et al., ref. 47). Grids: 812 000 cells
  (single orifice), 1 232 000 cells (four orifices), clustered at walls,
  cavity and shear layer. Domain 46 mm upstream of the cavity, injector
  36 mm from the inlet, 66 mm downstream, outlet height 43.6 mm. Inflow per
  experiment, atmospheric pressure outlet, no-slip walls. Only 10 mm cases
  simulated. Scheme, order, steady or unsteady, grid independence and a
  quantitative validation not stated.

# Tests and results

* §3.1, Fig. 5: lean blowoff limits for 2 schemes × 4–5 distances (Key
  claim 1).
* §3.1, Figs. 6–7: luminosity sequences 0.05 ms apart for four orifices at
  0.4 and 0.8 MPa and one orifice at 0.8 and 1.8 MPa (10 mm).
* §3.1, Fig. 8: wall pressure from −200 to 300 mm, about 62–122 kPa; tap-A
  inset about 114 kPa (four orifices, 0.8 MPa), 113 kPa (one orifice,
  0.8 MPa), 110 kPa (four orifices, 0.4 MPa), 102 kPa (cold).
* §3.2, Fig. 11: extinction–reignition cycle over 0.00–1.15 ms.
* §3.3, Fig. 12: blowoff over the last 0.60 ms before extinction.
* §3.3, Fig. 13: non-reacting temperature (scale 200–1400, K implied) and
  velocity (100–1300, m/s implied).
