# Paraffin-wax hybrid rocket fuel

Paraffin wax is used as a liquefying fuel in hybrid rocket motors. Its
structural weakness, brittleness at room temperature and loss of strength
near its melting onset, makes the mechanical properties of the grain an input
to a structural-integrity analysis of the kind used for solid-propellant
grains, which is the stated purpose of
[Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §1, §5. The material
characterized there is SasolWax 0907, a microcrystalline, brittle-class
paraffin (C₅₀H₁₀₂), pure (PW00) and loaded with 40 wt% aluminium powder of
100 µm mean particle size (AL40).

## Material

* **Isotropy.** Randomly oriented crystal nuclei make the wax
  quasi-isotropic (attributed); individually cast specimens showed no
  noticeable difference from specimens machined from full grains
  ([Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §2).
* **Viscoelasticity.** Paraffin behaves viscoelastically between 253 K and
  313 K (−20 °C to +40 °C), stated without citation (§2).
* **Thermal transitions.** DSC gives a melting onset of 312.90 K (39.75 °C;
  the Fig. 5 annotation reads 39.86 °C) with a peak at 349.74 K (76.59 °C),
  and a crystallisation onset of 362.42 K (89.27 °C) on cooling; the supplier
  quotes a congealing range of 356–367 K (83–94 °C) and a melting point of
  381 K (108 °C), definitions the paper does not reconcile (§2–3, Fig. 5).
  Above the melting onset the wax is too soft to test, which sets 313.15 K
  (40 °C) as the highest test temperature.

## Tensile properties

[Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §4.1, Tables 1–2, Figs. 6–11,
ASTM D638 Type III specimens, five per case, crosshead extension rates of
1, 10 and 100 mm/min (which the paper calls "strain rates"):

| Fuel | Temperature | Rate (mm/min) | $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ (MPa) | Strain at $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ (%) | $E$ (MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PW00 | 296.15 K (23 °C) | 1 | 1.78 | 1.05 | 512 |
| PW00 | 296.15 K | 10 | 2.27 | 0.86 | 678 |
| PW00 | 296.15 K | 100 | 2.97 | 0.8 | 781 |
| AL40 | 296.15 K | 1 | 2.02 | 1.01 | 1025 |
| AL40 | 296.15 K | 10 | 2.71 | 0.82 | 1162 |
| AL40 | 296.15 K | 100 | 3.30 | 0.68 | 1282 |
| PW00 | 303.15 K (30 °C) | 1 | 1.07 | 1.86 | 308 |
| PW00 | 313.15 K (40 °C) | 1 | 0.57 | 1.59 | 156 |
| PW00 | 303.15 K | 10 | 1.43 | 1.56 | 368 |
| PW00 | 313.15 K | 10 | 0.64 | 1.51 | 189 |
| AL40 | 303.15 K | 1 | 1.24 | 1.28 | 583 |
| AL40 | 313.15 K | 1 | 0.51 | 2.3 | 171 |
| AL40 | 303.15 K | 10 | 1.75 | 1.29 | 677 |
| AL40 | 313.15 K | 10 | 0.74 | 1.42 | 272 |

Standard deviations are printed in the tables. Shown:

* **Rate dependence.** At 296.15 K, $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ and $E$ rise and
  the strain at $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ falls as the rate increases;
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ rises by 60–70 % from 1 to 100 mm/min (§5).
* **Aluminium.** AL40 has a 10–20 % higher $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ than PW00
  at room temperature and roughly twice the modulus; the maximum strain is
  similar (§4.1).
* **Temperature.** $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ falls by 65–75 % between 296.15 K
  and 313.15 K (§5) and $E$ drops sharply. At 303.15 K and 313.15 K the
  failure becomes ductile: the maximum stress no longer coincides with
  failure, and the failure strain reaches 7.06 % for AL40 at 313.15 K and
  1 mm/min. The rate dependence is weaker at elevated temperature.

## Compressive properties

At 296.15 K (ASTM D695 cylinders, 12.7 mm × 25.4 mm), the ultimate
compressive strength exceeds $\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ at the same rate and the
compressive modulus is below the tensile one
([Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §4.2, Table 3, Figs. 12–13):

| Fuel | Rate (mm/min) | Ultimate compressive strength (MPa) | Strain at it (%) | Modulus (MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PW00 | 1 | 2.36 | 0.81 | 483 |
| PW00 | 10 | 3.34 | 1.01 | 547 |
| AL40 | 1 | 3.11 | 0.75 | 700 |
| AL40 | 10 | 4.03 | 0.71 | 882 |

Failure in compression proceeds by bulging, base cracking and then shear at
an angle. The paper compares its pure-wax values with Ryu et al. (attributed:
$\sigma_{\mathrm{UTS}}$ 2.118 MPa at 5 mm/min against 2.27 MPa here at
10 mm/min; compressive strength 3.503 MPa against 3.34 MPa) and notes that
Kobald et al. (attributed) found a significantly different elongation before
failure with another paraffin grade.

## Thermal properties and heat penetration

* **Conductivity.** $\lambda$ = 0.46 ± 0.03 W/(m·K) for PW00 and
  1.36 ± 0.03 W/(m·K) for AL40, about three times higher
  ([Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §3).
* **Penetration estimate.** A 1D semi-infinite transient conduction estimate
  with the surface held at 393.15 K (120 °C), radiation and convection
  neglected, puts the 303.15 K (30 °C) isotherm at 1.37 mm (PW00) and
  1.69 mm (AL40) after 1 s, less than the regression per second, so heat
  penetration is judged not significant (§3). The diffusivities used are not
  stated. The authors cite Santi et al. (attributed) for no measurable radial
  thermal propagation in an 80 s paraffin burn and caution that this may not
  hold for aluminised grains.

## Regression rate

In the authors' laboratory-scale N₂O motor (attributed to a thesis), the
predicted time-averaged $\dot r$ is 1.63 mm/s (PW00) and 1.82 mm/s (AL40);
the measured values are about 1.4 mm/s and 1.6 mm/s, the aluminised motor not
being tested at peak performance
([Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §3). The motor conditions
($O/F$, $G_o$, burn time) are not given.

## Casting

Pure wax is melted at 383.15 K (110 °C) and poured into a mould preheated to
the same temperature, then cooled slowly in the switched-off kiln for about
12 h; aluminised wax is stirred continuously until the melt reaches
363.15 K (90 °C) before pouring into a mould preheated to that temperature.
Rejection modes are layer formation (pour too cold), particle settling (too
hot), localised solidification (inadequate mixing) and voids (too cold); 5 %
of specimens were rejected
([Veale et al. (2018)](/sources/veale-2018.md), §2.1–2.2, Fig. 3).
