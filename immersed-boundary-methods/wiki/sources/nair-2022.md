# Summary

Nair and Goza make strongly coupled immersed-boundary fluid–structure
interaction about as cheap per step as stationary-body methods
([fluid–structure coupling](/methods/fluid-structure-coupling.md)). In a
streamfunction–vorticity, projection-type formulation (after Goza and
Colonius), the interface iterations embed a large linear system; they
precompute it once on a sub-domain covering the body's range of motion and
interpolate the sub-domain operator to the moving body.

# Key claims

1. Weakly coupled methods impose severe time-step limits for large deformations or small structure-to-fluid mass ratios; strongly coupled methods are stable but solve flow-sized systems at each iteration (§1). **Stated**
2. Block-LU reformulations confine iterations to interface variables but leave a large system embedded in the small one (§1–§2). **Stated**
3. Precomputing the encapsulating matrix on a fixed sub-domain, with the moving-body interpolation operator obtained by interpolating the sub-domain one, leaves only small-dimensional systems per step (§3). **Derived**
4. The formulation covers rigid, torsionally mounted and deformable bodies; the tests are 2-D (§2, §4). **Stated**
5. Lift and deflection differ by less than 1 % and 2 % from computations with the exact operators, with an order-of-magnitude speed-up per step on one core; a compact sub-domain reduces storage (airfoil with torsional flap) (§4). **Shown**
6. A parallel implementation scales across processors (§3–§4). **Shown**
