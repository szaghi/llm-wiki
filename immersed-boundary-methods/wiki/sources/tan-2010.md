# Summary

Tan and Shu give high-order finite-difference schemes (fifth-order WENO) a
boundary treatment for Cartesian grids that the boundary cuts arbitrarily:
the [inverse Lax–Wendroff](/methods/inverse-lax-wendroff.md) procedure. At
inflow boundaries the PDE converts normal derivatives into time and
tangential derivatives of the boundary data; ghost values follow from a
Taylor expansion. Outflow quantities are extrapolated — WENO-type near
shocks. The treatment is GKS-stable for linear advection, does not tighten the
CFL condition and reaches fifth order, including curved boundaries, solid
walls and a double Mach reflection off a wedge not aligned with the grid
([shock–obstacle interaction](/benchmarks/shock-obstacle-interaction.md)).

# Key claims

1. The reflection technique at solid walls works only when grid points are symmetric about the wall; Cartesian finite-volume cut cells suffer a time-step restriction (h-box methods address it); previous embedded finite differences were second order (§1). **Stated**
2. Repeatedly using the PDE converts normal derivatives at the inflow boundary into time and tangential derivatives of the boundary condition — the inverse of the Lax–Wendroff procedure (§1–§2). **Derived**
3. Matching the boundary data of the intermediate Runge–Kutta stages as Carpenter et al. prescribe keeps third-order time accuracy (§2). **Stated** (cited)
4. A WENO-type extrapolation degrades to first order across a discontinuity and keeps high order otherwise (§2). **Derived**
5. The semi-discrete scheme with ILW inflow and linear-weight WENO interior is GKS stable for linear advection; outflow extrapolation is stable for all orders (§2). **Proved**
6. In practice the boundary treatment does not tighten the interior CFL condition (§2). **Stated**
7. Systems use local characteristic decompositions; near-zero eigenvalues are handled by least squares with an added extrapolation equation (§2). **Derived**
8. High-order Lagrange extrapolation at subsonic outflow was mildly unstable; least-squares extrapolation cures it (§2). **Shown**
9. Designed fifth order is observed for scalar, Euler, wave-equation and vortex problems, including a disk-shaped domain; the boundary treatment's share of CPU time is reported (§3). **Shown**
10. Double Mach reflection with the wedge not aligned with the grid and supersonic flow past a cylinder are computed with solid-wall conditions (§3). **Shown**
