# Summary

Xie, Lin and Stoesser trace the [spurious pressure oscillations](/definitions/spurious-force-oscillations.md) of moving-body Cartesian methods to local mass-conservation errors and enforce mass and momentum conservation in fluid and cut cells, with a source for the rate of change of solid volume computed consistently with the piecewise-linear boundary ([cut-cell method](/methods/cut-cell-method.md); [discrete conservation](/properties/discrete-conservation.md)). Implicit time stepping avoids any small-cell treatment; fresh and dead cells need only the body velocity ([fresh-cell problem](/definitions/fresh-cell-problem.md)).

# Key claims

1. Spurious pressure oscillations in moving-body Cartesian methods are associated with local mass-conservation errors (§1). **Stated**
2. Mass and momentum conservation in cut cells, with a solid-volume source term, are imposed with consistent fluxes in continuity, momentum and pressure correction (§2–§3). **Derived**
3. Implicit time integration needs no small-cut-cell treatment (§1, §3). **Stated**
4. Of no conservation, mass only, and mass plus momentum, only the last greatly reduces the oscillations, which also shrink with refinement (§4.2). **Shown**
5. The non-smooth volume change of cut cells is the major source of oscillations; the new moving-body algorithm makes its fluctuation at least an order smaller (§4.2, §5). **Shown**
6. Oscillating cylinders (in line; transverse at $Re=185$) and oscillating and free-falling spheres give smooth forces (§4). **Shown**
