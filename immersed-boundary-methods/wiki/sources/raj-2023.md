# Summary

Raj, Khan, Alam, Prakash and Roy present a sharp-interface reconstruction method ([ghost-cell method](/methods/ghost-cell-method.md)) for complex geometries of finite and zero thickness with sharp edges, reduce [spurious force oscillations](/definitions/spurious-force-oscillations.md) of moving bodies by field extension, and port the method to GPUs.

# Version read

Version of record, J. Comput. Phys. 478, 111985 (2023). §§1–3 read, §§4–5 for results. Title and authors match Crossref.

# Key claims

1. Intercepted and thin cells are found by ray casting from a tagged surface mesh, avoiding spurious holes (§2). **Stated**
2. Reconstruction along the nearest surface normal fails at sharp edges; multi-directional forcing fixes it (§2). **Stated**
3. Field extension to near-boundary solid points reduces spurious force oscillations and the divergence error (§2, §4). **Shown**
4. Oscillation amplitudes decay at rates above 2 in space and 0.5 in time, against 1.8 and 0.5 for Seo and Mittal (§4). **Shown**
5. More than 100-fold speed-up on an NVIDIA V100 for a 3-D oscillating sphere, larger for larger meshes; AMD and NVIDIA GPUs compared with OpenACC (§5). **Shown**
