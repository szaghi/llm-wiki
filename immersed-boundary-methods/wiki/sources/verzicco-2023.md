# Summary

Verzicco reviews immersed boundary methods looking backwards — to the marker-and-cell boundaries of Viecelli and the fictitious domains of the Russian school before Peskin ([immersed boundary method](/definitions/immersed-boundary-method.md)) — and forwards, to the [wall models](/methods/wall-models-for-immersed-boundaries.md) high-Reynolds-number flows need and to multiphysics applications.

# Key claims

1. Most key ideas of immersed boundary methods were conceived and implemented in the 1960s (Viecelli; Saulev; Yanenko) but computers limited them (history; summary point 1). **Stated**
2. Viecelli (1971) "includes most elements of modern IBMs" (history). **Stated**
3. High-Reynolds-number flows are the main limitation, because thin wall layers cannot be resolved by wall-normal refinement (summary point 2). **Stated**
4. All IB wall models derive from the thin-boundary-layer approximation; implementation and coupling affect reliability (summary point 3). **Stated**
5. Microfluidics, heat and mass transfer, phase change and electrolysis are natural applications free of that limitation (summary point 4). **Stated**
