# Summary

Zeng, Song and Scovazzi extend the [shifted boundary method](/methods/shifted-boundary-method.md) to the compressible Euler equations in a stabilized continuous P1 finite element method with entropy- or Lax-type shock capturing. Shifted slip-wall conditions give second order where unshifted variants give first; results agree with body-fitted computations, also with strong shocks; the shift also cures polygonal body-fitted walls and imposes the Kutta condition on immersed airfoils.

# Key claims

1. Without modified boundary conditions on the surrogate only first-order convergence is expected (§1). **Stated**
2. The shifted slip-wall condition converges at second order; zeroth-order and unshifted embedded variants are at best first order (§4.1). **Shown**
3. At comparable resolution SBM and body-fitted computations agree, with or without artificial viscosity (§4). **Shown**
4. Shifted conditions on body-fitted polygonal meshes restore second order on curved walls (§3.5, §4.3–§4.4). **Shown**
5. Extending the camber line as an embedded slip interface imposes the Kutta condition on immersed airfoils (§4.5). **Stated**
