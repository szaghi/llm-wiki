# Summary

Seo and Mittal analyse what a finite-difference sharp-interface method conserves when the body moves and find a spurious volume source from [fresh and dead cells](/definitions/fresh-cell-problem.md) plus transpiration through the stair-step boundary: a violation of the [geometric conservation law](/definitions/geometric-conservation-law.md) that they identify as the main cause of [spurious pressure oscillations](/definitions/spurious-force-oscillations.md). They keep the [ghost-cell method](/methods/ghost-cell-method.md) for momentum and apply cut-cell volume and face fractions only to the pressure Poisson and velocity-correction equations, with [virtual merging](/methods/cell-merging.md) of the mass balance of small cells.

# Key claims

1. Spurious pressure oscillations of moving bodies appear in reconstruction, discrete-forcing and distributed-forcing immersed boundary methods (§1). **Stated**
2. The mass balance actually enforced has a volume-source error from fresh and dead cells and transpiration errors from the shape and position of the stair-step boundary; the volume term per cell scales as cell volume over time step (§2). **Derived**
3. For an oscillating cylinder the volume term is about 40 times the transpiration terms, the drag peaks coincide with fresh- and dead-cell events, and the oscillation amplitude scales as $h^{1.9}\Delta t^{-0.8}$; a body moving at CFL exactly one shows no oscillations (§2). **Shown**
4. Diffuse-interface methods smooth the conservation error in time without removing it (§2). **Stated**
5. Cut-cell fractions in the pressure equation with virtual merging of cells of volume fraction below 0.5 reduce the oscillations by about an order of magnitude and keep the second-order accuracy of the original method (§3–§4). **Shown**
6. Remaining oscillations come from fresh-cell discretization, abrupt velocity changes near fresh and dead cells, and momentum conservation errors (§4). **Stated**
