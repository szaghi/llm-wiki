# Summary

Ou, Chi, Guo and Thévenin generalize the directional
[ghost-cell method](/methods/ghost-cell-method.md) of Chi et al. to arbitrary
boundary conditions in low-Mach reacting flow: each coordinate direction has
its own ghost value, reconstructed in 1-D with the condition imposed at the
grid line's intersection with the boundary. Robin conditions with
surface-reaction coefficients become straightforward, and the local accuracy is
nearly second order ([flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md)).

# Key claims

1. Continuous forcing cannot prescribe the boundary condition precisely at the interface, spreading it spuriously (§1). **Stated**
2. Independent ghost values per direction let the boundary condition be imposed at the intersection point of each grid line, like a real domain boundary, without normals (§2). **Stated**
3. Dirichlet ghost values by third-order Taylor extrapolation have truncation error $O(l\,\Delta x^2)$; Neumann and Robin conditions enter implicitly (§2.5). **Derived**
4. Large weights arise when a fluid point is too close to the intersection; a first-order fallback is used, lowering accuracy on coarse grids (§2.5, §3). **Stated**/**Shown**
5. As a discrete-forcing method it adds no instability, but Stefan flow stronger than the main stream adds a time-step restriction (§2). **Stated**
6. Cylinder flow and heat transfer with Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin conditions, mass transfer to a reacting sphere and a Stefan problem converge at nearly second order and agree with references (§3). **Shown**
