# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Agrawal, Kulachenko, Scapin, Tammisola & Brandt (2024) — An efficient isogeometric/finite-difference immersed boundary method for the fluid–structure interactions of slender flexible structures](/sources/agrawal-2024.md) — Partitioned fluid–structure interaction of slender flexible rods: geometrically exact Cosserat rods in isogeometric analysis coupled by direct forcing to a staggered finite-difference flow solver, with Lagrangian markers generated in the spline parameter space independently of the structural mesh.
* [Angot, Bruneau & Fabrie (1999) — A penalization method to take into account obstacles in incompressible viscous flows](/sources/angot-1999.md) — Convergence theorems and error estimates for volume penalization of obstacles in the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations: an L2 penalization (Darcy law in the body) and an H1 penalization (Brinkman law), with the body force recovered from the penalty term, verified on a square cylinder in a channel.
* [Atallah, Canuto & Scovazzi (2022) — The high-order Shifted Boundary Method and its analysis](/sources/atallah-2022.md) — High-order shifted boundary method: Taylor expansions of order m shift Dirichlet data from the true to a piecewise-linear surrogate boundary in a Nitsche formulation, with coercivity, inf–sup and optimal H1 and L2 error estimates for Poisson and Stokes, and optimal rates without high-order body-fitted meshes or cut-cell quadrature.
* [Badia, Dilip & Verdugo (2023) — Space-time unfitted finite element methods for time-dependent problems on moving domains](/sources/badia-2023.md) — Space-time unfitted finite element method for parabolic problems on moving domains: unfitted elements in space and discontinuous Galerkin in time, made robust to the cut location by slab-wise cell aggregation (or a space-time ghost penalty), with stability, condition-number and optimal error estimates.
* [Bayyuk, Powell & van Leer (1993) — A simulation technique for 2-D unsteady inviscid flows around arbitrarily moving and deforming bodies of arbitrary geometry](/sources/bayyuk-1993.md) — Euler flows around bodies that move and deform across a stationary adaptive quadtree Cartesian grid, with cut cells merged into combined cells that keep their intersection topology over each motion step, removing the small-cell time-step limit and the vanishing- and newborn-cell problems.
* [Bennett, Nikiforakis & Klein (2018) — A moving boundary flux stabilization method for Cartesian cut-cell grids using directional operator splitting](/sources/bennett-2018.md) — Extension of the Klein-Bates-Nikiforakis flux stabilization for cut cells, in which stable cut-cell fluxes are linear combinations of standard fluxes along a one-dimensional stencil in a dimensionally split scheme, to rigid bodies moving through the grid in compressible flow.
* [Berger & Giuliani (2021) — A state redistribution algorithm for finite volume schemes on cut cell meshes](/sources/berger-2021.md) — Introduces state redistribution, a postprocessing stabilization for explicit finite volumes on cut-cell meshes that merges small cells into possibly overlapping neighbourhoods and recombines overlap-weighted neighbourhood averages and gradients, conservative and linearity preserving, with second-order results for smooth and shocked gas dynamics.
* [Berger & Giuliani (2024) — A new provably stable weighted state redistribution algorithm](/sources/berger-2024.md) — Weighted state redistribution for cut-cell finite volumes whose weights vanish continuously at the volume-fraction threshold, proved conservative and monotone, TVD and GKS stable in many configurations.
* [Boscheri & Ciallella (2025) — High order treatment of moving curved boundaries: Arbitrary-Lagrangian-Eulerian methods with a shifted boundary polynomial correction](/sources/boscheri-2025.md) — Shifted boundary polynomial correction in a direct high-order ALE finite volume scheme on moving straight-sided simplicial meshes: boundary data are corrected by one extra evaluation of the cell polynomial at the true curved boundary, recovering design order up to fourth.
* [Boustani, Barad, Kiris & Brehm (2022) — An immersed interface methodology for simulating supersonic spacecraft parachutes with fluid–structure interaction](/sources/boustani-2022.md) — Higher-order immersed interface method on adaptive Cartesian grids, extended to membranes thinner than a cell and to fabric porosity imposed as a Darcy–Forchheimer jump condition, loosely coupled to a shell solver with contact, applied to supersonic disk-gap-band parachute inflation.
* [Burman, Claus, Hansbo, Larson & Massing (2015) — CutFEM: Discretizing geometry and partial differential equations](/sources/burman-2015.md) — Overview of the cut finite element method: unfitted finite elements on a background mesh with level-set geometry, Nitsche imposition of boundary and interface conditions, and ghost-penalty stabilization making accuracy and conditioning independent of the cut; interface, fictitious-domain, Stokes and surface problems, with the libCutFEM implementation on FEniCS.
* [Burman, Hansbo, Larson & Zahedi (2025) — Cut finite element methods](/sources/burman-2025.md) — Acta Numerica review of CutFEM: active meshes, weakly imposed conditions, error and condition-number analysis, weak (ghost-penalty) and strong (extension) stabilization, surface PDEs, Lagrange multipliers and time-dependent domains.
* [Chaudhuri, Hadjadj & Chinnayya (2011) — On the use of immersed boundary methods for shock/obstacle interactions](/sources/chaudhuri-2011.md) — Ghost-point direct-forcing immersed boundary method combined with a fifth-order WENO scheme for inviscid shock–obstacle interaction: image-point values by inverse-distance weighting that excludes solid nodes, per-direction ghost data at corners, validated against oblique-shock theory, bow-shock stand-off and experiments for moving shocks over a prism and a cylinder.
* [Colella, Graves, Keen & Modiano (2006) — A Cartesian grid embedded boundary method for hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/colella-2006.md) — Second-order unsplit Godunov embedded-boundary method that is formally consistent (fluxes interpolated to face centroids, O(h) truncation error at the boundary) and stable for small cut cells by hybridizing conservative and non-conservative updates and redistributing the difference; second order in L1 for smooth flows and robust for strong shocks.
* [Collins, Lozinski & Scovazzi (2023) — A penalty-free Shifted Boundary Method of arbitrary order](/sources/collins-2023.md) — Penalty-free shifted boundary method, inspired by the non-symmetric Nitsche method, with inf–sup stability and convergence for arbitrary-order elements, optimal observed rates for Poisson, and a one-order loss for elasticity with Neumann conditions.
* [Fadlun, Verzicco, Orlandi & Mohd-Yusof (2000) — Combined immersed-boundary finite-difference methods for three-dimensional complex flow simulations](/sources/fadlun-2000.md) — Compares feedback and direct (discrete-time) forcing in a second-order staggered finite-difference solver: direct forcing removes the gains and the time-step penalty, while the accuracy is set by how the forcing is interpolated — stepwise below first order, volume-fraction first order, linear velocity reconstruction second order; applied to a vortex ring, a sphere and LES of a piston–cylinder assembly.
* [Fedkiw et al. (1999) — A non-oscillatory Eulerian approach to interfaces in multimaterial flows (the ghost fluid method)](/sources/fedkiw-1999.md) — Introduces the ghost fluid method: a level set tracks a material interface, each grid point near it carries a ghost fluid of the other material with the real pressure and velocity and an extrapolated entropy, and each fluid is advanced with an unmodified single-phase ENO–Runge–Kutta scheme, keeping density profiles sharp without Riemann solvers or dimensional splitting.
* [Ghias, Mittal & Dong (2007) — A sharp interface immersed boundary method for compressible viscous flows](/sources/ghias-2007.md) — Ghost-cell sharp-interface immersed boundary method for viscous subsonic compressible flow on Cartesian or body-non-conformal curvilinear grids, with image-point bilinear interpolation in computational space that stays well posed for all boundary positions; second order locally and globally, validated on cylinders, an airfoil and a wing tip.
* [Giuliani (2022) — A two-dimensional stabilized discontinuous Galerkin method on curvilinear embedded boundary grids](/sources/giuliani-2022.md) — State redistribution for high-order discontinuous Galerkin methods on 2-D curvilinear cut-cell grids: neighbourhood projections in an overlap-weighted inner product give a conservative, p-exact stabilization at the background time step, with order p+1 in L1 and robustness for shocks.
* [Giuliani et al. (2022b) — A weighted state redistribution algorithm for embedded boundary grids](/sources/giuliani-2022-b.md) — Extends state redistribution to three dimensions, introduces a weighted, less dissipative version that activates smoothly as the cut-cell volume fraction decreases, applies it to advective, diffusive and reactive updates in compressible and incompressible flow, and describes its implementation in AMReX-based exascale codes.
* [Goldstein, Handler & Sirovich (1993) — Modeling a no-slip flow boundary with an external force field](/sources/goldstein-1993.md) — Introduces feedback forcing: a proportional–integral body force at prescribed boundary points brings the flow to rest on a virtual surface; implemented in a pseudo-spectral code with Gaussian spreading, spectral smoothing and a back force against spatial oscillations, and tested on a start-up cylinder, turbulent channel flow with a virtual wall and riblets.
* [Griffith & Patankar (2020) — Immersed methods for fluid–structure interaction](/sources/griffith-2020.md) — Annual Review of immersed methods for elastic structures and prescribed kinematics: regularized-delta coupling with fibre and finite-element structures, immersed interface methods that apply jump conditions, kernels and quadrature, penalty and Lagrange-multiplier formulations, explicit-coupling stability, benchmarks and biomedical applications, and open issues.
* [Huang, Yang & Cai (2024) — A high-order no image point sharp interface immersed boundary method for compressible flows](/sources/huang-2024.md) — Third-order ghost-point immersed boundary method for 2D compressible flow without image points: ghost values from a constrained weighted-least-squares Taylor polynomial with the boundary condition at grid-line intersections, in an upwind compact scheme needing one ghost layer.
* [Jones, McNally & Reynolds (2026) — A constrained-transport embedded boundary method for compressible resistive magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/jones-2026.md) — Embedded boundaries for compressible resistive MHD on Cartesian grids: Forrer–Jeltsch cut cells without a small-cell time-step limit, face-centred staggered constrained transport kept divergence free at the boundary, and a ghost fluid coupling for moving two-material interfaces.
* [Kim, Kim & Choi (2001) — An immersed-boundary finite-volume method for simulations of flow in complex geometries](/sources/kim-2001.md) — Direct-forcing immersed boundary method on a staggered finite-volume grid with forcing applied only on or inside the body, a stable second-order linear/bilinear velocity interpolation, and a mass source/sink in cells cut by the boundary that restores continuity and reduces the error; validated on decaying vortices, a cylinder and a sphere.
* [LeVeque & Li (1994) — The immersed interface method for elliptic equations with discontinuous coefficients and singular sources](/sources/leveque-1994.md) — Introduces the immersed interface method: on a uniform grid, irregular points next to an interface get six-point stencils and right-hand-side corrections derived from Taylor expansions in local coordinates and the jump conditions, giving global second-order accuracy for discontinuous coefficients, singular sources and prescribed jumps, where a discrete-delta source is only first order.
* [Liu, Jiang, Shu & Zhang (2025) — High-order inverse Lax-Wendroff procedure for compressible fluid-structure interaction problems](/sources/liu-2025.md) — Partitioned third-order coupling of compressible Euler flow on fixed Cartesian grids (finite-difference WENO) with linearly elastic solids (Lagrangian DG) through an inverse Lax–Wendroff interface treatment that solves both media's PDEs and the interface conditions together, stable for light and heavy solids without sub-iterations.
* [Liu, Li, Cheng, Jiang, Shu & Zhang (2024) — A new type of simplified inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment I: hyperbolic conservation laws](/sources/liu-2024.md) — A simplified inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment that interpolates to auxiliary points before extrapolating, chosen by eigenvalue analysis to stay stable at the periodic CFL number with fewer ILW terms than the original simplified ILW.
* [Main & Scovazzi (2018) — The shifted boundary method for embedded domain computations. Part I: Poisson and Stokes problems](/sources/main-2018.md) — Introduces the shifted boundary method: boundary conditions are applied on a surrogate boundary of mesh faces near the true boundary, with values shifted by a first-order Taylor expansion along the distance vector and imposed weakly by Nitsche's method; stability and convergence analysis for Poisson and tests for Poisson and Stokes, free of the small-cut-cell problem.
* [Marashi & Hejranfar (2025) — A high-order hybrid compact-WENO finite-difference immersed boundary method for computing two-dimensional inviscid compressible flows](/sources/marashi-2025.md) — Ghost-point immersed boundary method in a fifth-order hybrid compact–WENO finite-difference scheme for 2D Euler flows, more accurate and cheaper than WENO-based immersed boundaries with somewhat more oscillation at shocks.
* [Meinke, Schneiders, Günther & Schröder (2013) — A cut-cell method for sharp moving boundaries in Cartesian grids](/sources/meinke-2013.md) — Compressible viscous cut-cell method on octree Cartesian grids for sharp moving boundaries from level sets, with a smooth discrete formulation for emerging and vanishing cells against spurious oscillations, several level sets for independently moving parts, and multiply cut and split cells.
* [Mittal & Iaccarino (2005) — Immersed boundary methods](/sources/mittal-2005.md) — Annual Review of Fluid Mechanics survey that defines immersed boundary methods broadly, formalizes the split into continuous and discrete forcing, compares feedback, penalization, direct-forcing, ghost-cell and cut-cell approaches for rigid bodies, discusses grid-size scaling with Reynolds number and the fresh-cell problem of moving sharp boundaries, and reviews applications.
* [Mittal & Seo (2023) — Origin and evolution of immersed boundary methods in computational fluid dynamics](/sources/mittal-2023.md) — Invited history and perspective of immersed boundary methods from the marker-and-cell era to the 2020s, with a five-condition definition of sharp-interface methods by equivalence with body-conformal methods.
* [Mittal et al. (2008) — A versatile sharp interface immersed boundary method for incompressible flows with complex boundaries](/sources/mittal-2008.md) — Three-dimensional sharp-interface ghost-cell immersed boundary method for incompressible flow around stationary, moving and deforming bodies described by triangulated surfaces on non-uniform Cartesian grids: normal-probe image points with trilinear interpolation, a fresh-cell treatment, geometric multigrid, and validation from cylinders and spheres to a deforming fish fin.
* [Munschy, Bourne, Dif-Pradalier, Donnel, Ghendrih, Grandgirard & Sarazin (2024) — Kinetic plasma-wall interaction using immersed boundary conditions](/sources/munschy-2024.md) — Kinetic 1D-1V plasma–wall (sheath) computations with the wall imposed by penalization as an immersed boundary condition, recovering kinetic sheath physics and larger heat transmission factors than usually predicted.
* [Murman, Aftosmis & Berger (2003) — Implicit approaches for moving boundaries in a 3-D Cartesian method](/sources/murman-2003.md) — Moving boundaries in a 3-D Cartesian cut-cell method with an implicit dual-time scheme, a rigid domain motion in arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian form, a relative body motion analysed in space-time, and a hierarchy of conservative approximations of the swept geometry.
* [Ménez, Parnaudeau, Beringhier & Goncalves da Silva (2023) — Assessment of volume penalization and immersed boundary methods for compressible flows with various thermal boundary conditions](/sources/menez-2023.md) — Compares a continuous-forcing immersed boundary method with compressible volume penalization for isothermal and adiabatic obstacles, derives an implicit adiabatic penalization using ghost cells, finds both first order, penalization far cheaper, and proposes a porosity model that cuts the cost of shock reflection 36-fold.
* [Nair & Goza (2022) — A strongly coupled immersed boundary method for fluid–structure interaction that mimics the efficiency of stationary body methods](/sources/nair-2022.md) — Removes the bottleneck of strongly coupled immersed-boundary FSI — large flow-sized linear solves inside each interface iteration — by precomputing the encapsulating matrix on a sub-domain enveloping the body motion and interpolating the interpolation operator, so each step solves only small systems; parallel, accurate within 1–2 % and an order of magnitude faster.
* [Natarajan, Grout, Zhang & Day (2022) — A moving embedded boundary approach for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations in a block-structured adaptive refinement framework](/sources/natarajan-2022.md) — Moving embedded boundaries for compressible viscous flow in AMReX: conservative unsplit cut cells with hybrid divergence and flux redistribution, a reflected ghost state for the flux on moving boundary faces, volume-averaged initialization of freshly cleared cells, least-squares wall stresses, and tests from acoustics to shocks and oscillating cylinders.
* [Ni, Li, Zhang, Zhou & Cui (2022) — An immersed boundary-material point method for shock-structure interaction and dynamic fracture](/sources/ni-2022.md) — Couples a finite-volume shock solver to the material point method with a Lagrangian continuous-forcing immersed boundary that sets the fluid velocity of boundary cells located from the material points and returns the reaction force to them, without interface reconstruction; validated on shock diffraction, shock-loaded panels and detonation-driven fragmentation.
* [Okafor, Seshadri, Roy & Udaykumar (2026) — High-order Eulerian sharp interface numerical techniques for micro- to macro-scale simulation of shocked energetic materials](/sources/okafor-2026.md) — Fifth-order WENO-Z with narrow-band level sets and an HLLC Riemann-based ghost fluid method for shocked elastoplastic and reactive multi-material flows, from pore collapse to shock-to-detonation transition.
* [Ou, Chi, Guo & Thévenin (2022) — A directional ghost-cell immersed boundary method for low Mach number reacting flows with interphase heat and mass transfer](/sources/ou-2022.md) — Directional ghost-cell immersed boundary method for a sixth-order low-Mach reacting-flow solver: one ghost value per coordinate direction, reconstructed along the grid line with Dirichlet, Neumann or Robin conditions imposed at the boundary intersection by Taylor-series extrapolation; nearly second-order local accuracy for flow, heat and species transfer and surface reactions.
* [Pember et al. (1995) — An adaptive Cartesian grid method for unsteady compressible flow in irregular regions](/sources/pember-1995.md) — Embedded-boundary (cut-cell) method for the time-dependent Euler equations: volume and area fractions describe the body on a Cartesian grid, an unsplit Godunov predictor ignores the body, a conservative but unstable cut-cell correction is replaced by a stable non-conservative update whose deficit is redistributed, mass-weighted, to neighbours; coupled to block-structured adaptive mesh refinement in 2-D, axisymmetric and 3-D.
* [Peskin (1972) — Flow patterns around heart valves: a numerical method](/sources/peskin-1972.md) — The founding immersed boundary paper: an elastic boundary moving with a viscous incompressible fluid is replaced by a force field on a rectangular mesh, linked to the Lagrangian boundary points by a semidiscrete delta function, with an implicit force computation that removes the instability of explicit forcing; applied to a two-dimensional mitral valve.
* [Peskin (2002) — The immersed boundary method](/sources/peskin-2002.md) — Acta Numerica review of the mathematical structure of the immersed boundary method: derivation of the Eulerian–Lagrangian equations from the principle of least action, spatial discretization, the identities that make spreading the adjoint of interpolation and conserve force, torque, momentum and energy, the postulates and construction of the 4-point delta function, Runge–Kutta time stepping, open problems and applications.
* [Qi, Wang, Zhu, Tian & Zhao (2024) — High-order discontinuous Galerkin method with immersed boundary treatment for compressible flows on parallel adaptive Cartesian grids](/sources/qi-2024.md) — Discontinuous Galerkin and direct DG on parallel adaptive octree grids with a multi-resolution WENO limiter and a ghost-cell immersed boundary whose image-point values are evaluated from the DG polynomial of the host cell.
* [Raj, Khan, Alam, Prakash & Roy (2023) — A GPU-accelerated sharp interface immersed boundary method for versatile geometries](/sources/raj-2023.md) — Sharp-interface reconstruction immersed boundary method on a staggered grid for thick, thin and sharp-edged moving bodies, with multi-directional forcing, field extension against spurious force oscillations, and GPU acceleration exceeding 100-fold.
* [Reiss (2022) — Pressure-tight and non-stiff volume penalization for compressible flows](/sources/reiss-2022.md) — Compressible volume penalization by a reduced volume fraction that scales mass, momentum and energy and keeps the speed of sound, making bodies pressure-tight without stiffness, with slip, no-slip and adiabatic walls, mass and energy conservation and the skew-symmetry of the interior scheme.
* [Schneiders, Günther, Meinke & Schröder (2016) — An efficient conservative cut-cell method for rigid bodies interacting with viscous compressible flows](/sources/schneiders-2016.md) — Strictly conservative compressible cut-cell method for freely moving rigid bodies, with a predictor-corrector reformulation of multistage Runge-Kutta that builds the moving geometry once per step, a weighted-Taylor small-cell reconstruction, conservative handling of emerging and submerging cells, and strong fluid-body coupling.
* [Schneiders, Hartmann, Meinke & Schröder (2013) — An accurate moving boundary formulation in cut-cell methods](/sources/schneiders-2013.md) — Three-dimensional compressible viscous cut-cell method in which the small-cell treatment (interpolation plus redistribution) and the least-squares stencil weights vary smoothly as the boundary moves, removing the spurious force oscillations that abrupt operator changes, including cell merging, produce.
* [Seo & Mittal (2011) — A sharp-interface immersed boundary method with improved mass conservation and reduced spurious pressure oscillations](/sources/seo-2011.md) — Analysis tracing the spurious pressure oscillations of sharp-interface immersed boundary methods with moving bodies to geometric-conservation errors of fresh and dead cells, and a ghost-cell method made locally mass conservative by cut-cell fractions in the pressure equation only, with virtual merging of small cells.
* [Sharan, Brady & Livescu (2022) — High-order dimensionally-split Cartesian embedded boundary method for non-dissipative schemes](/sources/sharan-2022.md) — Dimensionally split finite-difference embedded-boundary stencils satisfying discrete primary and secondary (energy) conservation in summation-by-parts form, provably time stable without dissipation, up to sixth order inside and fourth order globally, and free of the small-cell problem.
* [Sotiropoulos & Yang (2014) — Immersed boundary methods for simulating fluid–structure interaction](/sources/sotiropoulos-2014.md) — Review of immersed boundary methods for fluid–structure interaction: diffused-interface (classic, direct forcing, penalization) and sharp-interface (cut cell, immersed interface, hybrid Cartesian, curvilinear immersed boundary) methods, loose and strong partitioned coupling with added-mass issues and convergence acceleration, iterative solvers, and applications from vortex-induced vibration to renewable energy.
* [Taira & Colonius (2007) — The immersed boundary method: a projection approach](/sources/taira-2007.md) — Treats immersed-boundary forces as Lagrange multipliers of the no-slip constraint alongside the pressure, so the discretized system is a KKT system solved by a block-LU fractional step whose single projection removes divergence and slip together; no gains, CFL up to about one, second order in time and better than first order in space.
* [Tan & Shu (2010) — Inverse Lax-Wendroff procedure for numerical boundary conditions of conservation laws](/sources/tan-2010.md) — Introduces the inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment for high-order finite differences on Cartesian grids cut arbitrarily by the boundary: normal derivatives at inflow boundaries from the PDE and the boundary data, Taylor-expanded ghost values, Lagrange, least-squares or WENO-type extrapolation at outflow, GKS stability, and fifth-order results including curved walls with shocks.
* [Taylor, Wilcox & Chan (2025) — An energy stable high-order cut cell discontinuous Galerkin method with state redistribution for wave propagation](/sources/taylor-2025.md) — Proof that state redistribution, viewed as an L2-contractive filter, preserves the energy stability of a skew-symmetric high-order DG discretization of the acoustic wave equation on Cartesian cut meshes with arbitrary quadrature.
* [Tseng & Ferziger (2003) — A ghost-cell immersed boundary method for flow in complex geometry](/sources/tseng-2003.md) — Introduces a ghost-cell immersed boundary method on Cartesian grids: ghost values inside the body are extrapolated by linear or quadratic polynomial reconstruction (or through an image point) so that Dirichlet, Neumann or Robin conditions hold on the boundary; second order overall, applied to a cylinder, LES over a wavy wall and an ocean model.
* [Turner, Seo & Mittal (2024) — A high-order sharp-interface immersed boundary solver for high-speed flows](/sources/turner-2024.md) — A ghost-cell immersed boundary method for compressible flow with shocks whose ghost values come directly from a high-order Taylor polynomial fitted by weighted least squares, in a compact finite-difference solver with hyperviscosity; third order or higher kept in supersonic tests.
* [Uhlmann (2005) — An immersed boundary method with direct forcing for the simulation of particulate flows](/sources/uhlmann-2005.md) — Combines Peskin's regularized-delta transfer with an explicit direct forcing evaluated at Lagrangian points that move rigidly with suspended particles, giving smooth hydrodynamic forces on moving bodies without time-step penalties; validated on cylinders and settling discs and spheres, up to 1000 particles.
* [Verzicco (2023) — Immersed boundary methods: historical perspective and future outlook](/sources/verzicco-2023.md) — Annual Review tracing immersed boundary ideas to Viecelli, Saulev and Yanenko in the 1960s, identifying high-Reynolds-number wall layers as the main limitation and reviewing wall models, and surveying multiphysics applications where the limitation disappears.
* [Xie, Lin & Stoesser (2022) — A conservative and consistent implicit Cartesian cut-cell method for moving geometries with reduced spurious pressure oscillations](/sources/xie-2022.md) — Three-dimensional implicit Cartesian cut-cell finite volume for moving bodies with consistent mass and momentum fluxes and a solid-volume source in cut cells, showing that conservation in cut cells suppresses spurious pressure oscillations without small-cell treatment.
* [Yang, Bi & Jiang (2026) — An improved WCNS-Z-based ghost-cell immersed boundary method and its application in shock-obstacle interaction simulations](/sources/yang-2026.md) — Ghost-cell immersed boundary method blending third-order weighted-least-squares and first-order ghost extrapolations with one nonlinear weight, coupled to a third-order weighted compact nonlinear scheme that keeps its order at critical points, for shock–obstacle flows.
* [Ye, Mittal, Udaykumar & Shyy (1999) — An accurate Cartesian grid method for viscous incompressible flows with complex immersed boundaries](/sources/ye-1999.md) — Two-dimensional cut-cell finite-volume method for unsteady incompressible flow: collocated variables with separately updated face velocities, cut cells reshaped into trapezoids by merging, a compact linear–quadratic interpolant for face fluxes that keeps local and global second-order accuracy, and a line-SOR-preconditioned BiCGSTAB pressure solver; validated on Wannier flow and cylinder flows.
* [Zeng, Song & Scovazzi (2025) — A Shifted Boundary Method for the compressible Euler equations](/sources/zeng-2025.md) — Shifted boundary method for compressible Euler flow with and without strong shocks in a stabilized linear finite element method, matching body-fitted results, correcting polygonal body-fitted walls, and imposing the Kutta condition on immersed airfoils.
* [de Prenter, Verhoosel, van Brummelen, Larson & Badia (2023) — Stability and conditioning of immersed finite element methods: analysis and remedies](/sources/deprenter-2023.md) — Review of the stability and conditioning problems that small cut elements cause in immersed finite element methods, with analysis in terms of norm equivalences and detailed treatment of Schwarz preconditioning, element aggregation and ghost-penalty stabilization, including a unified analysis of the last two.
