# Summary

Main and Scovazzi propose the [shifted boundary method](/methods/shifted-boundary-method.md):
move the location of the boundary condition from the true boundary to a
surrogate boundary made of mesh faces, and correct its value with a Taylor
expansion so that the convergence rate is kept; impose it weakly with Nitsche's
method. No cut cell is integrated, so the
[small-cell problem](/definitions/small-cell-problem.md) does not arise. They
analyse the Poisson problem and test Poisson and Stokes flows up to a complex
3-D shape, and argue against the cost of [CutFEM](/methods/cutfem.md)-type
approaches.

# Key claims

1. Immersed boundary methods (forcing in the continuous equations, solved inside and outside) are penalty-like, smear the interface and are typically first order; embedded boundary methods solve only in the physical domain; the literature uses the two terms interchangeably (§1). **Stated**
2. Nitsche–XFEM suffers on small-cut elements; bubbles, weighted interface conditions, ghost penalty and extended B-splines all need cut-element geometry and quadrature, which are complex and costly (§1). **Stated**
3. The surrogate boundary of nearest mesh faces with a closest-point map and distance vector $\mathbf d$ needs only $\mathbf n\cdot\tilde{\mathbf n}\ge0$, a geometric-resolution condition (§2). **Stated**
4. Without modifying the shifted condition only first order is possible; the first-order Taylor correction restores optimal rates (§1, §3). **Derived**
5. Symmetric and unsymmetric forms are coercive and bounded; first order in the energy norm by Strang's lemma; an $L^2$ estimate of order $h^{3/2}$, with second order observed (§3.4). **Proved**/**Shown**
6. For Stokes with stabilized equal-order elements the pressure converges at the predicted rate $1.5$, and the method is insensitive to small cut cells (§4). **Shown**
7. Flow around a circle and a rectangle agrees with conformal Nitsche solutions; a 3-D "monkey trefoil" geometry is computed robustly (§4). **Shown**
