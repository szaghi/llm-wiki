# Summary

Kim, Kim and Choi apply Mohd-Yusof's [direct forcing](/methods/direct-forcing.md)
in a staggered finite-volume fractional-step solver (RK3 convection,
Crank–Nicolson diffusion; Cartesian and cylindrical coordinates), but only on
the body surface or inside the body, unlike Fadlun et al. The target velocity
inside the body mirrors the outside flow, obtained by a second-order linear
or bilinear interpolation designed to stay stable whatever the position of
the boundary relative to the grid. A mass source/sink in each cell cut by the
boundary restores continuity for its fluid part
([discrete conservation](/properties/discrete-conservation.md)). Decaying
vortices confirm second order; [flow past a circular cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-circular-cylinder.md)
and [flow past a sphere](/benchmarks/flow-past-sphere.md) agree with
body-fitted results.

# Key claims

1. Feedback forcing induces spurious oscillations and restricts the time step (CFL $O(10^{-3}$–$10^{-2})$ in Goldstein et al.); Mohd-Yusof's forcing does not (§1). **Stated** (cited)
2. Fadlun et al.'s reconstruction effectively forces the first grid point in the fluid, with an arbitrary interpolation direction; bilinear interpolation there would require iteration with approximate factorization (§1). **Stated**
3. Ye et al.'s cut-cell method needs an iterative solution of the momentum equations because boundary-cell stencils differ (§1). **Stated**
4. Forcing is applied only on or inside the body; at a forcing point on $\Gamma$ the velocity is zero, inside it is the negative of the corresponding outside velocity (§2.1, Fig. 1). **Stated**
5. Forcing the intermediate velocity of the fractional step costs $O(\Delta t^2)$ at the boundary and keeps second-order time accuracy (§2.2). **Stated**
6. The target is obtained by bilinear interpolation using the no-slip point on the wall-normal line, or by linear interpolation along a grid line that switches from the first to the second outside point when the forcing point is far from $\Gamma$ (eqs. 10–11); the naive extrapolation diverged for $h/y_A=100$, and the proposed scheme never became unstable in the cases studied (§2.2). **Shown**
7. The 3-D extension uses seven, three or one neighbouring velocities according to where the wall-normal intersection lies (§2.2, Fig. 5). **Stated**
8. A mass source $q_m$ at the centre of each cut cell, from the difference between full-cell and fluid-part fluxes with provisional velocities, makes the fluid part conserve mass; the sources sum to zero (§2.3). **Derived**
9. Decaying vortices at $Re=10$ with the boundary parallel and oblique to the grid: second order in space; with the mass source the error is much smaller than without and comparable with no immersed boundary (§3.1, Fig. 8). **Shown**
10. Cylinder at $Re=40$ and $100$ ($331\times193$, 30 points per diameter): $C_D$, lift amplitude and $St$ agree with body-fitted results of Park et al. (1998); forces are integrals of the momentum forcing (§3.2, Table I). **Shown**
11. Sphere: results agree with previous numerical and experimental data (§3.3). **Shown**
