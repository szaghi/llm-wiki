# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Cell merging for cut cells](/methods/cell-merging.md) — Stabilization of cut-cell discretizations that combines a small cut cell with one or more neighbours into a single control volume large enough for the full-cell time step; Bayyuk et al.'s merging criteria for moving bodies, the space-time view that equates it with flux telescoping, virtual merging of the mass balance, and the oscillations its on-off switching causes.
* [Classical (Peskin) immersed boundary method](/methods/classical-immersed-boundary-method.md) — Peskin's formulation of fluid–structure interaction: an elastic structure described in Lagrangian coordinates exerts a force density that is spread to a fixed Eulerian grid with a regularized delta function and moves at the interpolated fluid velocity; its derivation, discretization, time stepping and the stiffness that limits explicit force computation.
* [Cut-cell (embedded-boundary) finite-volume method](/methods/cut-cell-method.md) — Sharp-interface finite-volume method on a Cartesian grid whose cells are clipped by the boundary: volume and face-area fractions describe the geometry, fluxes on the clipped faces and the wall flux enter a conservative update, and the small cut cells are stabilized by merging or redistribution; the compressible (Pember et al.) and incompressible (Ye et al.) founding versions.
* [CutFEM (cut finite element method)](/methods/cutfem.md) — Unfitted finite element framework in which the geometry is represented on a background mesh (typically by a level set), boundary and interface conditions are imposed weakly by Nitsche's method, and ghost-penalty stabilization on faces of cut elements makes accuracy and conditioning independent of how the boundary cuts the mesh.
* [Direct (discrete-time) forcing](/methods/direct-forcing.md) — Discrete-forcing immersed boundary method in which the force at a forcing point is solved from the discretized momentum equation so that the updated velocity equals a target value reconstructed from the boundary velocity; free of gains and of the stiffness of feedback forcing, with accuracy set by the velocity reconstruction and conservation of cut cells restored by a mass source.
* [Feedback forcing](/methods/feedback-forcing.md) — Continuous-forcing immersed boundary method that drives the fluid velocity at boundary points to the prescribed boundary velocity with a proportional–integral controller; a damped-oscillator interpretation fixes the gains, which make the equations stiff and limit the time step.
* [Fluid–structure coupling in immersed boundary methods](/methods/fluid-structure-coupling.md) — How immersed boundary methods couple the flow with a moving or deforming structure: monolithic versus partitioned schemes, loose (explicit) versus strong (iterated or implicit) coupling, the added-mass instability and density-ratio limits, convergence acceleration, the cost of strongly coupled methods and its reduction, and the solid models (fibres, finite elements, material points) the sources couple.
* [Flux redistribution for cut cells](/methods/redistribution.md) — Stabilization of explicit cut-cell finite-volume schemes that takes a stable non-conservative update in each small cut cell and redistributes the resulting conservation deficit to neighbouring cells, so that the scheme stays conservative with a time step set by the full cells; Pember et al.'s mass-weighted algebraic version and its adaptive-mesh extension.
* [Ghost fluid method](/methods/ghost-fluid-method.md) — Sharp-interface Eulerian method for multimaterial compressible flow: a level set locates the interface, every grid point near it carries the real fluid and a ghost fluid of the other material whose pressure and normal velocity are copied and whose entropy is extrapolated, and each fluid is advanced with an unmodified single-phase scheme.
* [Ghost-cell immersed boundary method](/methods/ghost-cell-method.md) — Sharp-interface discrete-forcing method that assigns values to ghost points inside the body so that the boundary condition holds at the boundary point on the normal through each ghost point, usually by interpolating at an image point in the fluid and extrapolating along the normal; its stencils, conditioning fixes, Neumann and Robin conditions, compressible and high-order versions.
* [High-order immersed boundary treatments for compressible flow](/methods/high-order-immersed-boundary.md) — Boundary treatments that keep third- or higher-order accuracy at an immersed boundary for compressible flows with shocks: inverse Lax–Wendroff ghost values, high-order polynomial weighted-least-squares ghost values with or without image points, ghost values from discontinuous Galerkin polynomials, summation-by-parts embedded boundaries, and hybrid compact–WENO interiors; what each needs and the orders reported.
* [Immersed boundary projection method](/methods/immersed-boundary-projection-method.md) — Taira and Colonius's formulation that treats the immersed-boundary forces as Lagrange multipliers of the no-slip constraint, grouped with the pressure, so that one projection of a block-LU fractional step removes both divergence and slip; no gains, no-slip and continuity satisfied exactly at the new time level, symmetric positive-definite systems.
* [Immersed interface method](/methods/immersed-interface-method.md) — Sharp-interface finite-difference method for problems with discontinuous coefficients, singular sources or prescribed jumps across an interface not aligned with a uniform grid: at irregular points the stencil coefficients and a right-hand-side correction are derived from Taylor expansions about the interface and the jump conditions, giving global second-order accuracy.
* [Inverse Lax–Wendroff boundary treatment](/methods/inverse-lax-wendroff.md) — High-order finite-difference boundary treatment for hyperbolic conservation laws on Cartesian grids that cut the boundary arbitrarily: at inflow boundaries the PDE is used repeatedly to convert normal derivatives into time and tangential derivatives of the boundary data, ghost values follow from a Taylor expansion, and outflow quantities are extrapolated (WENO-type near shocks).
* [Shifted boundary method](/methods/shifted-boundary-method.md) — Approximate-domain embedded method that applies boundary conditions on a surrogate boundary made of mesh faces near the true boundary, shifting their values by a Taylor expansion along the distance vector and imposing them weakly with Nitsche's method; no cut cells are integrated, so the small-cut-cell problem does not arise, and high-order versions keep optimal rates with a piecewise-linear surrogate.
* [State redistribution](/methods/state-redistribution.md) — Postprocessing stabilization for explicit cut-cell finite volumes: after an unstable update with the full-cell time step, each small cut cell is temporarily merged into a possibly overlapping neighbourhood, and cell values are replaced by overlap-weighted averages of neighbourhood polynomials; conservative, linearity preserving and second order at the cut cells, extended to 3-D, a weighted less dissipative form, diffusion and reacting flows.
* [Volume penalization (Brinkman penalization)](/methods/volume-penalization.md) — Fictitious-domain immersed boundary method that solves the flow equations in the whole domain and models the body as a porous medium of vanishing permeability through a penalty term on its volume; the L2 (Darcy) and H1 (Brinkman) penalizations, their convergence as the penalty parameter vanishes, and force computation from the penalty term.
* [Wall models for immersed boundaries](/methods/wall-models-for-immersed-boundaries.md) — Near-wall models that let immersed boundary methods compute high-Reynolds-number turbulent flows without resolving the wall layer: all apply a thin-boundary-layer approximation (law of the wall, Reichardt profile, integral relations) between the boundary and a point in the flow, combined in RANS with anisotropic local refinement and in LES mostly without; why the Cartesian grid makes them necessary.
