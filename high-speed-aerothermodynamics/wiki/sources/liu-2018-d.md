# Summary

The paper propagates aleatory uncertainty in five freestream and wall
inputs ($\mathrm{Ma}_\infty$, unit Reynolds number, $\alpha$, $T_\infty$,
$T_w$) through 3-D RANS-SST simulations of the two-ramp hypersonic
inlet/isolator of Li et al.'s shock-tunnel model at a baseline
$\mathrm{Ma}_\infty=5.9$. The baseline sits inside the Mach-number
hysteresis loop, so the started and the unstarted flow are analysed at
the same freestream condition. A point-collocation non-intrusive
polynomial-chaos surrogate (Legendre basis, optimal Latin hypercube
sampling, oversampling ratio 2) gives the mean, 95% intervals and Sobol'
indices of the pressure-coefficient field and of three isolator
performance parameters at $x=316$ mm (normalised mass capture rate, exit
Mach number, $\sigma_0$). Mach number and angle of attack dominate the
pressure-coefficient uncertainty in nearly complementary regions; the
performance-parameter uncertainty in the started flow is about twice that
in the unstarted flow; Mach number dominates the $\sigma_0$ uncertainty;
wall temperature is negligible. A grid study with a cell-Reynolds-number
criterion and an error-accumulation reliability check (Smirnov) support
the numerics.

# Key claims

1. **Hysteresis** (§4.5, Fig. 8; shown): over $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty$ 3.3–7
   unstart and restart follow different paths in mass capture and
   $\sigma_0$; on restart a step at Mach 5 does not start the inlet
   because a separation bubble remains at the entrance. Mach 5.9 is chosen
   because both modes exist there, far from the critical Mach numbers.
2. **Initial-condition independence** (§4.5, Figs. 9–10; shown): routes
   7 → 5.9 and 7 → 6.5 → 5.9 give identical wall pressure distributions;
   density residual drops 9–10 orders.
3. **Pressure-coefficient intervals** (§5.2, Fig. 14; shown, attribution
   interpretive): started flow, intervals wider than 1.0 at shocks;
   unstarted flow, large intervals at the separation shock, behind the
   cowl lip and at isolator impingements, with much narrower intervals at
   the reflected shocks, attributed to weaker reflected shocks and the
   entrance bubble attenuating sensitivity.
4. **Cumulative effect along the isolator** (§5.2, Figs. 15–16): large
   intervals shrink and small ones grow downstream, called a "cumulative
   effect of uncertainty due to the multiple reflections of shock waves".
5. **Performance parameters at $x=316$ mm** (§5.2, Table 6; shown):
   baseline / surrogate mean / 95% interval width — started: exit Mach
   2.93 / 2.91 / 0.536, normalised mass capture $4.42\times10^{-3}$ /
   $4.40\times10^{-3}$ / $1.06\times10^{-3}$, $\sigma_0$ 0.445 / 0.442 /
   0.111; unstarted: 1.80 / 1.78 / 0.205, $3.35\times10^{-3}$ /
   $3.32\times10^{-3}$ / $1.00\times10^{-3}$, 0.104 / 0.103 / 0.053.
   Started widths about twice unstarted except for mass capture.
6. **Sobol' indices of the pressure coefficient** (§5.3, Figs. 17–18;
   shown): Mach number and angle of attack dominate in nearly
   complementary regions; Reynolds number matters at the unstarted
   reflected shocks; $T_\infty$ only at the separation-induced shock near
   the throat (started); $T_w$ negligible; all five comparable near the
   separation bubble.
7. **Oblique-shock argument** (§5.3, Eqs. 24–26; stated): the ratio of the
   post-shock pressure derivatives with respect to shock angle and to
   Mach number is approximately Mach number times the cotangent of the
   shock angle, much larger than 1, used to explain the angle-of-attack
   dominance behind shocks. The derivation contains slips (below).
8. **Sobol' indices of performance parameters** (§5.3, Fig. 19; shown):
   Mach number and angle of attack drive mass capture; Mach number
   dominates $\sigma_0$ and leads for exit Mach number; Reynolds number and
   the two temperatures weigh more in the unstarted flow.
9. **Linear correlations** (§5.3, Figs. 20–21; coefficients read from the
   rendered Fig. 20): Mach number with mass capture 0.8180 (started) /
   0.9005 (unstarted), with exit Mach 0.8594 / 0.6817, with $\sigma_0$
   −0.9117 / −0.9665; mass capture correlates negatively with angle of
   attack; nothing correlates with $T_w$. Stated consequence: maximum mass
   flow and maximum $\sigma_0$ cannot both be reached by varying Mach
   number alone, nor angle of attack alone.
10. **Error-accumulation reliability** (§4.4, Table 4, Eqs. 21–23, after
    Smirnov et al.; 5% tolerance, 30000 steps): coefficients 6.62–7.13
    (coarse), 62.62–73.60 (medium), 282.82–392.14 (fine), all well above 1.

# Methods

* **Solver**: unnamed in-house multi-block structured finite-volume code
  (stated second-order); compressible 3-D RANS non-dimensionalised by
  freestream values; $\mathrm{Pr}=0.72$, turbulent Prandtl number 0.9,
  Sutherland viscosity, perfect gas. Roe flux-difference splitting with
  second-order MUSCL (limiter not stated), central viscous fluxes,
  implicit LU-SGS, CFL 3.0, 30000 steps.
* **Turbulence**: Menter SST k–ω with printed constants
  ([SST k–ω turbulence model in high-speed flow](/methods/sst-k-omega-model.md)).
* **Geometry and boundaries** (§4.1, Fig. 1): ramps at 9° and 14°,
  constant-height isolator 10 mm × 194 mm after the throat; spanwise-
  periodic slab; pressure far field, pressure outflow, isothermal no-slip
  walls.
* **Grids** (§4.3, Tables 2–3): 360×120×20 (minimum cell Reynolds number
  30, 3,646,300 points), 450×150×30 (20, 4,824,420, adopted), 550×180×40
  (10, 6,204,660); at $x=316$ mm exit Mach 2.92 / 2.93 / 2.93, mass flow
  8.17 / 8.10 / 8.10 ×10⁻³ kg s⁻¹, $\sigma_0$ 0.444 / 0.445 / 0.445.
* **Validation** (§4.2–4.3): DLR GK-01 inlet at $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty=7.0$,
  unit Reynolds number $4.0\times10^6$ m⁻¹, $T_\infty=46.0$ K, $T_w=300.0$ K:
  wall $C_p$ within ±5.6% (graphical); Li et al. shock-tunnel inlet:
  schlieren qualitative, cowl-wall pressure agrees except slightly
  downstream in the isolator (±5% band).
* **Uncertainty quantification** (§3, §4.6, Table 5): point-collocation
  polynomial chaos with Legendre polynomials, optimal Latin hypercube
  sampling, oversampling ratio 2, least squares; 95% interval taken as
  3.92 standard deviations; Sobol' indices from the coefficients. Uniform
  inputs: $\alpha$ 0.0° ± 0.2°, $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty$ 5.9 ± 1.5%, unit
  Reynolds number $5.2\times10^6$ m⁻¹ ± 15%, $T_\infty$ 101.7 K ± 10%,
  $T_w$ 283.0 K ± 10%, bounds based on shock-tunnel conditions of Sriram et
  al. The expansion order and the sample count are not stated. Details on
  [Non-intrusive polynomial chaos and Sobol' indices](/methods/non-intrusive-polynomial-chaos.md).
