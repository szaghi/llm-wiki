# Summary

An experimental study, in a Mach 2.95 suction wind tunnel, of the
near-wall wake behind a sonic transverse nitrogen jet (2 mm circular
orifice) on a flat plate, for seven momentum flux ratios $J$ = 2.3–28.9.
Nanoparticle planar laser scattering (NPLS) side and top slices and
surface oil flow are combined. The wake is divided into three regions:
(I) a V-shaped separation zone induced by the leeward "collision shocks"
formed where the flows wrapping around the jet meet, (II) a reattachment
region with inner and outer slip lines, and (III) a mixing and recovery
region. The included angle between the V-shaped separation lines is
33.2°–34.5° and is stated to be independent of $J$; the separation length
is fitted as $L_{\mathrm{sep}}/d_j=4.582\,J^{0.4135}$. The paper is a short
follow-up of the same group's study of the shock structure (Liang et al.
2018).

# Key claims

1. **Collision shocks** (§3, Figs. 4–6): at $J$ = 20.6 and 28.9 the V-shaped
   collision shocks are visible on the slices at 2 mm and 4 mm from the
   wall (clearer at 4 mm), read as evidence that the flow lateral to the
   jet is supersonic even at 2 mm; at $J$ = 7.7 they are too weak to
   identify. $J$ = 2.3 and 5.5 behave like 7.7, 11.2 and 16.0 like 20.6
   (images not shown). Shown; interpretation stated.
2. **Three-dimensionality** (§3, Fig. 7): in averaged NPLS images at
   $J=20.6$ the collision-shock footprint is much larger at 2 mm than at
   4 mm. Shown qualitatively.
3. **V-shaped separation** (§3, Fig. 8): oil flow at $J=20.6$ shows two
   oblique separation zones behind the jet whose separation lines end on
   a V-shaped slip line produced by the collision-shock/boundary-layer
   interaction; the collision shock intersects the shock reflected from
   the barrel shock by the Mach disk (the latter attributed to the
   companion paper). A schematic proposes a half-cone shock surface
   because the flow behind the collision is blocked by the leeward bubble.
4. **Three regions** (§3, Fig. 8a): I, V-shaped separation; II,
   reattachment and collision shock, with outer slip line I along the
   collision shock and an inner slip line between reattached wake and
   lateral flow; III, mixing and recovery, where the reflected shock
   generates outer slip line II. Proposed by this paper.
5. **Distinct from horseshoe separation** (§3): the V-shaped zone differs
   in mechanism and position from the parabolic separation zone of
   Glagolev et al., which the authors identify with the horseshoe
   separation induced by the bow shock. A reinterpretation, not a
   contradiction of those data.
6. **Low $J$** (§3, Fig. 9): at $J=7.7$ the collision and reflected
   shocks are weaker, the slip line decays quickly, and the outer slip line
   merges into the inner one after a short distance; separation is larger
   at $J=28.9$ than at 7.7 and 20.6.
7. **Separation angle** (§3, Figs. 8a, 9, 11a): included angle 34.50°
   ($J=28.9$), 33.20° ($J=20.6$), 34.20° ($J=7.7$); stated independent of
   $J$ because the collision-shock angle "is only dependent upon the
   free-stream Mach number".
8. **Power-law correlations** (§3, Eqs. 1–3, Fig. 11b): least-squares fits
   over $J$ = 0, 2.3, 5.5, 7.7, 11.2, 16.0, 20.6, 28.9 of the separation
   start position, $0.3167\,J^{0.7697}$ orifice diameters, and of the
   separation length $L_{\mathrm{sep}}/d_j=4.582\,J^{0.4135}$; the form
   follows jet-penetration correlations (Rothstein–Wantuck, Gruber). Width
   is said to follow from length through the constant angle; no width
   formula is printed. With $d_j=2$ mm the fit gives 12.9–36.8 mm over
   the seven $J$, consistent with the plotted data (reader's check).
9. **Use** (§3 end; stated): injectant and crossflow do not mix
   completely in the separation zone, so the correlation helps to avoid
   it in combustor design; the data are offered for validation.

# Methods

* Experiment only. Mach 2.95 suction tunnel, test section 400 mm long,
  200 × 200 mm²; stagnation pressure 101325 Pa, stagnation temperature
  300 K; $u_\infty\approx605$ m s⁻¹ (estimated); boundary-layer Reynolds
  number $3.5\times10^4$ at $x=-5$ mm.
* Flat plate with a 10 × 190 mm² roughness trip 10 mm from the leading
  edge, 250 mm of plate ahead of the orifice ($d_j=2$ mm). Incoming layer
  $\delta=6.0$ mm, its velocity profile (from 400 PIV images) matching a
  prediction after Touber, taken as fully developed turbulent (§2,
  Fig. 3).
* Jet: sonic nitrogen, total temperature 300 K; $J$ = 2.3, 5.5, 7.7, 11.2,
  16.0, 20.6, 28.9 by jet total pressure 110, 259, 375, 525, 750, 967,
  1354 kPa (Table 1).
* NPLS: CCD 4000 × 2672 px; TiO₂ particles of effective diameter 42.5 nm
  and relaxation time 66.3 ns; 0.0259 mm/pixel side view, 0.0332 and
  0.0344 mm/pixel top slices at 2 and 4 mm; the jet is unseeded. Laser
  parameters not stated.
* Oil flow: pigment in oil (composition not stated). Separation lines
  extracted at 95% of the brightest level and fitted with straight lines;
  stated systematic uncertainty 3% in angle, 5% in length.
