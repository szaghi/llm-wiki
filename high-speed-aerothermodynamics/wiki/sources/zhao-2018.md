# Summary

The paper derives geometric design relations for two "constant swept"
osculating-cone waveriders whose leading edge contains a straight,
constant-sweep segment: a *delta-winged* waverider (straight segment outboard)
and a *cuspidal* waverider (straight segment near the symmetry plane, giving a
cusped apex). The inlet capture curve (ICC) and flow capture tube (FCT) are
built from straight-line, power-law and parabolic pieces joined by tangency,
intersection and perpendicularity constraints (Eqs. 3–15). A delta-winged
waverider is designed at Mach 8; a cuspidal waverider (design Mach 3.86) and
a general osculating-cone waverider (design Mach 4.4) are chosen to have the
same volumetric efficiency $\eta_V$ (Fig. 6). Euler and RANS (SST k–ω) Fluent
simulations confirm shock attachment at design, and a matrix of Mach
3.0–10.0 and angles of attack −2° to 10° at 25 km compares lift-to-drag
ratios. The cuspidal waverider beats the general one at every condition
(maximum +18.5%), the delta-winged one is worse, and only the cuspidal
waverider shows a nonlinear lift rise above about 6° at Mach 8, which the
authors call vortex lift.

# Key claims

1. **General osculating-cone inputs** (§2, Eqs. 1–2, Fig. 1). ICC = central
   straight line plus power-law branches; FCT = parabola. The osculating-cone
   method is stated (citing Sobieczky et al., ref. [23]) to be an inverse
   design approach whose main purpose is airframe–inlet integration.
2. **Delta-winged design** (§2.1, Eqs. 3–10, Figs. 2–3). ICC = vertical
   straight segment + power-law segment + slanted straight segment; FCT =
   parabola + tangent straight line. Constraints: the angle relation of
   Eq. 6 between the sweep, the design shock angle and two geometric angles;
   FCT and ICC tangency (Eqs. 7–8); perpendicularity (Eq. 9); end-point
   intersection (Eq. 10). Fig. 3 gives the iterative solution procedure.
   Derived.
3. **Cuspidal design** (§2.2, Eqs. 11–15, Fig. 4). ICC = vertical straight
   segment + power-law segment; FCT = slanted straight segment tangent to a
   parabola. Constraints: tangent of sweep = tangent of FCT inclination over
   tangent of shock angle (Eq. 13), tangency (Eq. 14), end intersection
   (Eq. 15); solved explicitly in sequence. Derived.
4. **Novelty** (§1). Earlier constant-swept designs (Rodi, refs. [18–20];
   Duan et al. [21]; Liu et al. [22]) used a circular-arc ICC and a straight
   FCT, restricting the design space; power-law and parabolic curves are
   "more flexible". Stated; the paper also says Rodi did not show how to
   generate the geometry.
5. **Volumetric efficiency** (§3.2, Eq. 16, Fig. 6). $\eta_V$ of general and
   cuspidal waveriders increases monotonically with design Mach number
   (approximate readings: about 0.111 at Mach 4 to 0.132 at Mach 14, general;
   about 0.102 at Mach 3 to 0.122 at Mach 5, cuspidal). Iso-$\eta_V$ matching
   to the Mach 8 delta-winged waverider gives design Mach 4.4 (general) and
   3.86 (cuspidal). CFD-measured $\eta_V$ deviates from the general one by
   4.6% (delta-winged) and 7.3% (cuspidal), "within the range for
   acceptance". The $\eta_V$ program is verified in ref. [16], not here.
6. **Design-point check** (§4, Figs. 9–11). At 25 km, $\alpha=0$ and the
   design Mach numbers, the inviscid shock in the base plane coincides with
   the designed shock profile curve and high pressure stays below the lower
   surface; in viscous CFD the shock is deflected downward by the boundary
   layer. Shown, qualitative.
7. **Trends** (§5, Fig. 12). For all three, $C_L$ and $C_D$ rise and $L/D$
   falls with $\alpha$; the ordering cuspidal > general > delta-winged holds
   at every Mach number tested. Shown.
8. **Mach 8 coefficients** (§5, Tables 4–6, viscous, base drag excluded),
   $L/D$ at $\alpha=-2,0,2,4,6,8,10$°: delta-winged 2.9464, 2.8415, 2.6506,
   2.4405, 2.2386, 2.0547, 1.8908; cuspidal 3.4976, 3.4111, 3.1639, 2.8833,
   2.6205, 2.3864, 2.1805; general 2.9641, 2.9363, 2.7688, 2.5627, 2.3558,
   2.1613, 1.9862. $C_L$ ranges: delta 0.5902–1.6038, cuspidal
   0.4888–1.6989, general 0.5141–1.5357; $C_D$: delta 0.2003–0.8482, cuspidal
   0.1398–0.7791, general 0.1734–0.7732. Shown; $C_L/C_D$ reproduces the $L/D$
   column to within 0.0012.
9. **Increments** (§5, Fig. 13). The cuspidal-over-general $L/D$ increment
   falls with $\alpha$ and generally rises with Mach number: stated to exceed
   10% at Mach 8 and 10 for all $\alpha$ and 6% for Mach ≤ 4.4; maximum 18.5%
   (§7). The general-over-delta increment rises then falls with $\alpha$
   (peak about 7% at Mach 3, $\alpha$ about 4°) and grows as Mach decreases.
   Shown (plot readings approximate).
10. **Thickness** (§6.1, §6.2, Figs. 14, 20). Tangent of the symmetry-plane
    surface angle (SOLIDWORKS): cuspidal 0.2471, general 0.2626,
    delta-winged 0.3046; the thinner body has the higher $L/D$. Stated as a
    qualitative explanation.
11. **Lower-surface pressure** (§6.1–6.2, Figs. 15–17, 21). At Mach 8,
    $\alpha=0$, the upper-surface pressure equals freestream for both; the
    cuspidal lower surface has lower pressure (high centre, low sides) than
    the general one (low centre, high sides), hence a weaker shock and lower
    wave drag; at $\alpha=10$° the lower-surface pressures are close. The
    delta-winged lower surface has a significantly higher pressure, hence
    higher wave drag and $C_L$. Shown; that wave drag dominates at high speed
    is stated, not shown.
12. **Vortex lift** (§6.1, Figs. 18–19, 22). The cuspidal $C_L(\alpha)$ is
    nonlinear above about 6°; at 10° $C_L$ is "almost 8%" above the linear
    function. The general (Fig. 18b) and delta-winged (Fig. 22) curves are
    almost linear. Fig. 19 (cuspidal upper-surface pressure, $\alpha$ = 0,
    4, 8, 12°) shows a growing low-pressure region attributed to a vortex or
    separation; the mechanism (separation bubble enhanced by $\alpha$) is
    attributed to Miller & Wood (ref. [40]) and Rodi (ref. [20]). Shown
    ($C_L$ curves, surface pressure); mechanism attributed; the authors state
    that hypersonic upper-surface separation needs further study.
13. **Low absolute $L/D$** (§5). All three bodies are short and have low $L/D$
    because the basic cone half-angle is 20°; the comparative conclusions are
    argued to hold for higher-$L/D$ waveriders. Stated, not shown.

# Methods

* Design: osculating cones, inverse design from ICC and FCT with streamline
  tracing in local conical flows; basic cone half-angle 20°. Design shock
  angle not stated.
* Design parameters (Tables 1–3, printed in mm): delta-winged, design Mach 8,
  half-span 400 mm, straight-segment length 48 mm, height 338 mm, sweep 60°,
  FCT inclination 18.9°, power-law exponent 4; cuspidal, design Mach 3.86,
  half-span 400 mm, straight length 148 mm, height 338 mm, sweep 45°, FCT
  inclination 26.5°, exponent 4; general, design Mach 4.4, half-span 400 mm,
  straight length 48 mm, height 338 mm, exponent 4. Units of the parabola and
  power-law coefficients not stated.
* CFD (§4): ANSYS Fluent, 3D steady RANS, coupled implicit solver,
  second-order upwind; Euler equations for inviscid fields; SST k–ω; ideal
  gas, no reactions; half-domain. Grid, grid independence, wall boundary
  condition, reference area and length not stated; validation deferred to
  refs. [16, 37, 38].
* Freestream (§5): 25 km, $T_\infty=221.552$ K, $p_\infty=2549.22$ Pa;
  $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty$ = 3.0, 3.86, 4.0, 4.4, 8.0, 10.0; $\alpha$ = −2 to 10°
  in 2° steps. Coefficients viscous, base drag excluded.
