# Summary

The paper parameterises a 3-D curved scramjet isolator with variable
cross-section ("shape transition") in two steps: entrance and exit
sections are linearly blended after Barger, each intermediate section is
rescaled to a prescribed area distribution, and the sections are
translated along an offset line defined by a B-spline through five
control points. Three free variables of the offset line are optimised
with a multi-island genetic algorithm (Isight) coupled to 3-D RANS (SST
k–ω, second-order Roe), maximising exit total-pressure recovery at
entrance Mach 2 without back pressure. Three cases (rectangular-to-
circular, circular, rectangular; 800 mm long, 40 mm offset) are
optimised. The authors argue that, once the offset line is optimised, the
remaining loss is governed by wetted area. The optimised
rectangular-to-circular isolator recovers 1.59% more total pressure than
an existing baseline design and holds its shock-train leading edge
furthest downstream at a back pressure four times the entrance pressure.
Under back pressure a switch of the separation from the bottom to the top
wall is observed in the curved ducts.

# Key claims

1. **Section blending** (§2.1, Fig. 3; shown): linear blend of entrance
   and exit sections weighted by normalised position, after Barger; the
   unscaled blend overshoots the area by up to about 16% at mid-length
   (read from the plot); rescaling by numerical iteration matches the
   design distribution.
2. **Offset line** (§2.1, Fig. 5, Table 1; shown): B-spline through five
   control points with zero end slopes, three free variables; stated to
   be monotone.
3. **Optimiser** (§2.2, Fig. 6): MIGA in Isight; objective exit
   total-pressure recovery without back pressure, chosen because
   back-pressured runs are too costly.
4. **Validation** (§3, Fig. 7; shown): Kawatsu et al. constant-area duct
   (30 × 30 mm, 290 mm, Mach 2.3, total temperature 298 K, total pressure
   100 kPa, back-pressure ratio 3.8): wall pressure overpredicted upstream
   of the shock train by up to 52%; shock-train leading edge reproduced;
   minimum deviation 1.3% in the shock train.
5. **Grid study** (§3, Fig. 7): 41×41×81, 65×65×100, 81×81×120 on the
   validation duct; coarse deviates at the pressure rise, medium and fine
   agree. The grid used for the optimisation geometry is not stated.
6. **Step-count study** (Table 3): recovery 0.86201, 0.82751, 0.81987,
   0.81985 after 1000, 2000, 5000, 10000 steps; 5000 used.
7. **Optimisation histories** (§4.1, Figs. 8–9): no variable converges;
   the first settles near 0.3 after 300 iterations; the objective
   converges after about 200 iterations to about 0.89 (Case1); optimised
   circular 0.91, rectangular about 0.87. Read as evidence of a nonlinear
   objective.
8. **Ranking without back pressure** (§4.2, Figs. 10–11): circular
   highest, rectangular lowest (0.87), rectangular-to-circular between;
   Case1 1.59% above Case0. Loss attributed to wetted area (stated,
   wetted areas not computed).
9. **Flow field** (§4.2, Fig. 12): Case0 slightly better for $x<0.2$ m,
   Case1 better downstream, exit Mach 1.96 with better uniformity.
10. **Back pressure** (§4.3, Figs. 13–15; shown): back-pressure ratios 1,
    2, 3, 3.5, 4 until the shock train reaches the entrance; separation and
    a shock at 2 in all four isolators; asymmetric shock trains with a
    switch from bottom-wall to top-wall large-scale separation "without
    any changes of the boundary conditions", related to [31,32]; at 4 the
    leading edge is furthest downstream for Case1; recovery unaffected
    below 1 and "near equivalent" above 3.
11. **Waltrup–Billig correlation** (§1, Eq. 1; stated, attributed to
    Waltrup & Billig 1973), printed as
    $\dfrac{S_{CL}(M_2^2-1)\mathrm{Re}_\theta^{\alpha}}{D^{1/2}\theta^{1/2}}=50\left(\dfrac{P_f}{P_a}-1\right)+170\left(\dfrac{P_f}{P_a}-1\right)^2$,
    with the exponent given only as a symbol. Not used in the method.

# Methods

* **CFD** (§3): 3-D RANS, finite volume, SST k–ω, second-order upwind
  Roe, implicit, steady state by iteration (5000 steps); solver, gas
  model, wall thermal condition, inflow boundary layer and $y^+$ not
  stated.
* **Boundary conditions**: optimisation inflow Mach 2, total pressure
  100 kPa, total temperature 300 K; outflow without back pressure, or a
  prescribed static back-pressure ratio to the (undefined) reference
  pressure P1, presumably the isolator-entrance static pressure.
* **Cases** (Table 2): Case0 original rectangular-to-circular, Case1
  optimised rectangular-to-circular, Case2 circular (R = 66 mm), Case3
  rectangular (240 × 56 mm), all 800 mm long with 40 mm offset.
* **No own experiment**; uncertainty limited to the step-count study and
  the validation deviation.
