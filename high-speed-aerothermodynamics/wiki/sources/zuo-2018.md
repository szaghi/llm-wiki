# Summary

The paper first compares, by inviscid parent-flow computations at
$\mathrm{Ma}_\infty=4$ and exit Mach 1.4, the authors' inward-turning
("wavecatcher") parent flow, Internal Conical Flow C (an internal conical
flow A coupled with a truncated Busemann flow), with 2-D multi-wedge, 2-D
isentropic and Busemann compressions; against the multi-wedge it gains
28.0% total-pressure recovery and needs 5% less length, 15.7% less wetted
area and 12.1% less pressure drag (§2.3, Tables 1–2). It then argues
qualitatively that the inward-turning compression makes the
shock/boundary-layer interaction harder (§2.4). A section-controllable
wavecatcher intake (design Mach 4, osculating-axisymmetric design,
$\mathrm{CR}\approx5.96$, $\mathrm{CR}_i\approx2.18$, 2920 mm long) is
simulated by steady RANS with the k–ε model with and without a 20 mm
streamwise bleed slot at the reflected-shock impingement (§3). Bleed of
2.528% of the captured mass removes the weak normal shock and spanwise
vortices near the throat, raises throat recovery from 0.740 to 0.764, and
raises the sustainable back-pressure ratio from 73.4 to 76.7 with exit
recovery from 0.509 to 0.528; the terminal-shock position at a given back
pressure is unchanged.

# Key claims

1. **Parent-flow comparison** (§2.3.2, §2.3.4, Tables 1–2, Fig. 5; shown,
   inviscid): recovery 0.683 / 0.740 / 0.984 / 0.874 for wedge /
   isentropic / Busemann / Internal Conical Flow C; full table on
   [Inward-turning intake design](/methods/inward-turning-intake-design.md).
   The advantages are always stated against the wedge; Busemann has the
   highest recovery.
2. **One-dimensional relations** (§2.3.2, Eqs. 1–2; stated): recovery and
   static pressure ratio are the contraction ratio times mass-flow-function
   ratios, so at fixed entrance and exit Mach numbers they depend only on
   $\mathrm{CR}$.
3. **Pressure drag** (§2.3.4, Eq. 3, Table 2): pressure-drag coefficient
   0.430 / 0.423 / 0.401 / 0.378; pressure drag stated to be 70–80% of
   total intake drag (attributed to [35]); a net-thrust example is
   attributed to [34].
4. **Spillage** (§2.3.1, Figs. 3–4; qualitative): an enclosed capture
   section prevents lateral spillage at design; a V-shaped cowl closure
   spills less than planar intakes at low Mach number.
5. **Harder interaction** (§2.4, Figs. 7–8; qualitative): no
   constant-pressure plateau before the reflected shock, pressure ahead of
   it about twice the 2-D value, a "much more shrunken" boundary-layer
   profile at the shoulder, strongest pressure gradient near the
   centreline.
6. **Ramjet design tension** (§2.2; stated): osculating planes cannot all
   keep the same contraction ratio, so the throat Mach number is
   non-uniform and planes near Mach 1 risk unstart; a scramjet intake for
   Mach 6 typically has throat Mach 3.0.
7. **Bleed effect on structure** (§3.3, Figs. 14, 16–17; shown): the weak
   normal shock near the throat disappears and spanwise vortices
   (Q-criterion) are removed; throat Mach 1.461 → 1.622, throat recovery
   0.740 → 0.764.
8. **Back-pressure characteristic** (§3.4, Tables 4–5, Figs. 18–20; shown):
   sustainable back-pressure ratio 73.4 → 76.7, exit recovery 0.509 →
   0.528 (reported as +3.73%); terminal-shock position unchanged at equal
   back pressure.
9. **Slot sizing** (§3.3; attributed to Schulte et al. and Hamed et al.):
   slot length between $\delta$ and $2\delta$; here $\delta\approx18.1$ mm
   at the impingement gives 20 mm.
10. **Design method** (§2.1, Fig. 2; stated, deferred to You & Liang
    2007): osculating-axisymmetric reduction to 2-D planes, streamline
    tracing in the parent flow; Mölder (1967) credited with the four types
    of straight-shock internal axisymmetric conical flows.

# Methods

* **Parent flows** (§2.3): inviscid, method and code not stated;
  $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty=4.0$, mean exit Mach 1.4, equal capture area. The
  boundary-layer profiles of Fig. 7 come from a k–ε computation whose
  details are not stated.
* **Intake geometry** (§3.1.1, Figs. 9–10, 15): capture section of three
  straight lines and an ellipse, 88460 mm² (width 450.6 mm); throat of two
  ellipses, 14851 mm²; cowl-closure section 32381 mm²; 1800 mm divergent
  duct, exit diameter 240 mm, equivalent expansion angle 1.6°; shoulder
  smoothing and displacement-thickness correction. The quoted ratios are
  consistent with the areas.
* **Numerics** (§3.1.2): steady compressible RANS, finite-volume time
  marching, calorically perfect gas, standard k–ε, "high resolution"
  advection and turbulence schemes (order not stated), solver not named.
  Half domain with symmetry plane, structured multi-block grid, far field a
  cylinder of about 5 entrance equivalent diameters, first cell 0.1 mm,
  $y^+\approx30$–60, adiabatic no-slip walls. Freestream
  $\mathrm{Ma}_\infty=4.0$, $p_\infty=2930.7$ Pa, $T_\infty=220.6$ K.
  Convergence: mass flow, recovery and exit Mach vary less than 0.05%
  over thousands of iterations. Back-pressure and bleed-exit boundary
  condition types not stated.
* **Grid study** (§3.1.2, Table 3, Figs. 12–13): 3.5, 5.21 and 6.52 million
  cells at equal $y^+$; exit recovery 0.621 / 0.622 / 0.622; fine grid
  used.
* **Validation**: stated only, by reference to Huang, Zuo & Qiao (2018)
  against wind-tunnel data on a wavecatcher intake.
* **Vortex identification** (§3.3, Eq. 4): Q-criterion, attributed to
  Jeong & Hussain.
