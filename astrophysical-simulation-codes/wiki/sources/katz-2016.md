# Summary

First paper of a series on white-dwarf (WD) mergers with
[Castro](/software/castro.md); it documents the code changes since Almgren et
al. (2010) and verifies them on merger-relevant tests. Physics: hydrodynamics,
Poisson self-gravity, rotation and the Helmholtz EOS; reactions and radiation
are disabled in every run (§2.2). The main changes are (i) a **conservative
gravitational energy source** evaluated at cell faces from the Riemann mass
fluxes and potential differences with a time-centred potential (after Springel
2010), with a proof of total-energy conservation (App. B), while the momentum
source is deliberately kept as the cell-centred $\rho\mathbf g$; (ii) a
**rotating frame** with Coriolis and centrifugal sources, an implicit Coriolis
update and the same face-based energy coupling through a rotational potential;
(iii) **multipole boundary conditions** for the Poisson solve (default order 6)
replacing the monopole; (iv) PPM changes — the Colella & Woodward (1984)
limiter, new reference states, a passive-scalar tracing fix, a lagged
extrapolation of sources in the edge-state predictor, and parabolic source
tracing (App. A); and (v) robustness devices: density-floor resets, an EOS
temperature floor that keeps the energy, an outer sponge, and a time-step ramp.
Merged into [Castro](/software/castro.md),
[conservative self-gravity](/methods/conservative-self-gravity.md) and
[general equations of state](/methods/general-equation-of-state.md).

# Key claims

1. **Equations and scheme** (§2.1, eqs. 1–3). Euler equations in conservative
   form; unsplit PPM (Miller & Colella 2002) with the App. A modifications.
2. **Limiter** (§2.1). The original Castro default, a "slightly modified"
   Colella & Sekora (2008) extremum-preserving limiter (`castro.ppm_type = 2`),
   "can yield negative densities" at steep density gradients such as the stellar
   surface, because it interprets them "as being true minima". The paper uses
   the Colella & Woodward (1984) limiter (`castro.ppm_type = 1`), "strictly
   monotonicity preserving". Asserted; no figure.
3. **Density floor** (§2.1). A non-conservative reset is preferred to a
   positivity-preserving diffusive flux: below the floor (typically
   $10^{-5}$ g cm$^{-3}$) a zone copies density, momentum, energy and
   temperature from its densest neighbour if that is above the floor; otherwise
   density goes to the floor, temperature to the temperature floor (typically
   $10^5$ K), velocity to zero ("very rare", "last resort").
4. **AMR** (§2.1, §2.3). $r_{\mathrm{ref}} \in \{2, 4\}$; 3D Cartesian with
   uniform spacing only; subcycling with as many substeps as the refinement
   ratio; refined grids may not reach the domain boundary. The final multilevel
   gravity synchronization of Almgren et al. (2010) is **disabled** for merger
   runs "because the grid boundaries never lie in regions of high density".
5. **EOS** (§2.2). Helmholtz EOS (Timmes & Swesty 2000); $(\rho, e) \to T$ by
   Newton–Raphson, default relative tolerance $10^{-8}$; on non-convergence a
   temperature floor (typically $10^5$ K). Of the two options — keep $e$ and
   accept thermodynamic inconsistency, or reset $e$ to match $T$ (more accurate
   for strong 1D shocks reaching the floor, but drifting total energy over long
   runs) — the paper **keeps $e$**, the energy-conserving choice.
6. **Momentum source** (§2.3.1, eqs. 5, 7–8). Cell-centred $\rho\mathbf g$,
   time-centred by a predictor–corrector: add $\Delta t\,\rho^n\mathbf g^n$,
   then after the hydro step and a new Poisson solve add
   $-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\rho^n\mathbf g^n + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\rho^{n+1}\mathbf g^{n+1}$.
   In 1D with the three-point Laplacian and a central gradient the source
   telescopes (eq. 7), so momentum is conserved up to leakage through ghost
   cells; in 3D "depending on the discretization these may not be cancelled at
   all". The stress-tensor form (eq. 8), which conserves momentum to round-off
   and is used by Jiang et al. (2013) for Athena, is **rejected**: with it "the
   divergence of the gravitational acceleration is no longer guaranteed to
   equal the zone density", and truncation error can imply a density "much
   different than the zone's actual density", harmful in low-density ambient
   gas. Asserted; no test shown.
7. **Conservative energy source** (§2.3.1, eqs. 9–11, after Springel 2010).
   The conserved quantity is $\int (E + E_{\mathrm g})\,\mathrm dV$ with
   $E_{\mathrm g} = \tfrac12\rho\Phi$ (eq. 9). Moving the face mass, given by the
   Riemann mass flux $\hat f^{\rho}$ times $\Delta t$ and the face area over the
   cell volume (eq. 10), from the cell centre to a face at potential
   $\tfrac12(\Phi_i + \Phi_{i+1})$ does work, and the energy update is minus one
   half of the sum over the cell's faces of that face mass times the potential
   difference between the zone centres on either side (eq. 11). Second order in
   time needs the time-centred $\tfrac12(\Phi^n + \Phi^{n+1})$, available
   because Castro re-solves Poisson after the hydro; the source is applied at
   the end of the step. Eq. 11 applies to any conservative potential. Option
   `castro.grav_source_type = 4`.
8. **Energy-conservation proof** (App. B, eqs. B1–B4). Summing eq. 11 over
   cells, the interface terms cancel antisymmetrically, leaving
   $$\sum_i (E + E_{\mathrm g})^{n+1}_i - \sum_i (E + E_{\mathrm g})^{n}_i = \frac{1}{8\pi G}\sum_i\left(\Phi^{n+1}_i\nabla^2\Phi^n_i - \Phi^n_i\nabla^2\Phi^{n+1}_i\right),$$
   which vanishes by Green's second identity when boundary terms vanish.
   **Proved** in the continuum; "The analogous result holds for the
   discretized form" is **asserted**. Not exact in practice: finite domain and
   Poisson residual at the multigrid tolerance.
9. **Rejected energy alternatives** (§2.3.1). Cell-centred
   $\rho\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf g$ "can result in a significant leaking of
   energy" because mass moves at faces. Evolving the total energy including
   $\rho\Phi$ (Jiang et al. 2013; Springel 2010) is avoided because recovering
   $E$ by subtracting the potential term in low-density gas near the WD edges
   gives "unphysical energies or temperatures"; Jiang et al.'s gravitational
   flux has terms not proportional to $\rho$.
10. **Poisson boundary conditions** (§2.3.2, eqs. 12–16, App. C). Direct
    summation over all cells costs $O(N^5)$ (kept as a reference option). A
    multipole expansion of the Green's function with interior moments, origin
    at the domain centre, costs $O(N^3)$ per solve; the potential is given on
    cell edges. On a binary WD of mass ratio 2/3 the relative $L^2$ error of
    $\Phi$ is "already well below $10^{-4}$" at order 6, the default, and the
    expansion breaks down at order $\gtrsim 18$ because ambient mass in the
    domain corners lies outside the sphere through the boundary points
    (Fig. 1).
11. **Multigrid tolerance** (§2.3.3). Relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ in all
    tests and science runs.
12. **Rotating frame** (§2.4, eqs. 21–33). Coriolis and centrifugal sources
    (eq. 21), cell-centred, in the edge-state prediction and the same
    predictor–corrector as gravity. The new-time Coriolis term is solved
    implicitly in closed form (eqs. 22–26); "this improvement is small".
    Centrifugal work goes through a rotational potential (eq. 28) with the
    face-based update of eq. 30, total energy as in eq. 29. Rotation is applied
    after gravity. Option `castro.rot_source_type = 4`. Output is transformed
    back to the inertial frame (eqs. 31–33).
13. **Angular momentum** (§2.4). Castro conserves linear, not angular,
    momentum; the rotating frame makes angular-momentum conservation "much
    easier"; an angular-momentum-conserving form is not implemented. In the
    inertial frame at moderate resolution "a secular loss of angular momentum
    … eventually will result in a spurious merger".
14. **Initial conditions and time step** (§3.1–3.2). Isothermal 1D hydrostatic
    WD by Newton–Raphson on the discrete balance eq. 34 (1D spacing 6.25 km),
    mapped to 3D with 4 sub-zones per direction; no relaxation step. The time
    step starts a few orders of magnitude below the CFL limit and grows by 1%
    per step; the maximum is one half of the CFL limit.
15. **Refinement and sponge** (§3.2, eqs. 36–38). Coarse grid $256^3$ on a
    $1.024\times10^{10}$ cm domain; the coarse level tags zones within twice
    the Eggleton (1983) Roche radius, finer levels tag $\rho > 1$ g cm$^{-3}$;
    the outer domain is never tagged. A post-hydro momentum sponge (half-cosine
    ramp from 75% to 85% of the distance to the boundary, time-scale 0.01 s)
    suppresses ambient instabilities that in the rotating frame cut the time
    step "by up to an order of magnitude".
16. **Hydro changes** (App. A). Lagged linear extrapolation of the summed
    source to the half step in the edge-state predictor (eq. A9; not applied
    without $n-1$ data), "slightly more accurate in absolute terms" (no data);
    sources reconstructed parabolically and traced under each characteristic
    (eqs. A10–A13, `castro.ppm_trace_sources`), so gravity also enters the
    transverse Riemann solves, with the argument that the PPM paper's
    $\Delta t$ factor should be $\Delta t/2$; passively advected quantities
    traced by the contact speed only (eq. A8), fixing tracing under the fastest
    wave; flattening per Zingale & Katz (2015).
17. **Parallelism** (§5). BoxLib boxes over MPI; default Morton space-filling
    curve, but a knapsack distribution (efficiency demand 0.9) "is actually" the
    most efficient here; boxes a multiple of 16 zones per dimension (multigrid
    efficiency "sharply decreases" otherwise), maximum grid size 32–64; OpenMP
    tiling with one parallel region per hydro call (Zhang et al. 2015) replaces
    loop-level OpenMP; a GPU port is in development.

**Tests** (§2.3, §4, §5). Poisson convergence (§2.3.3, Fig. 2): uniform sphere
"substantially better than second-order" at low resolution, then stalling
because the mass on the grid changes with resolution; mass-normalized sphere
slightly above second order; cube with the Waldvogel (1976) potential
(corrected by a factor $\tfrac12$, fn. 5) "perfect second-order scaling at all
resolutions". Hydrostatic $0.9\,M_\odot$ WD for 200 s at effective 256, 512,
1024 (§4.1, Figs. 4–5): the star expands to a larger equilibrium, less so at
higher resolution, and the final kinetic energy is not lower at the highest
resolution. Free fall of two WDs (§4.2, Fig. 6): "excellent agreement" with the
analytic curve. Galilean invariance (§4.3.1, Figs. 7–9): Kelvin–Helmholtz with
Robertson et al. (2010) and McNally et al. (2012) initial conditions and bulk
flow up to 100 (about Mach 70), $64^2$–$512^2$ plus stationary references to
$4096^2$; the sharp IC A fails to develop at low resolution and high speed and
does not converge even qualitatively, the smooth IC B converges to the $v = 0$
result at $512^2$; McNally runs to $t = 10$ have no converged late-time
reference. Moving star (§4.3.2, Fig. 10): expands to nearly twice its radius at
the lowest resolution, "much worse at maintaining hydrostatic equilibrium".
Keplerian orbits (§4.4, Fig. 11, Table 1), $\vert\Delta E/E\vert$ after one
100 s orbit: cell-centred energy source $4.6$–$5.7\times10^{-2}$; resetting $E$
after the momentum update 1.1–2.8; the face-based scheme
$3.1\times10^{-4}$–$1.3\times10^{-3}$, "nearly two orders of magnitude better";
rotation coupling matters much less. 25 orbits (Figs. 12–14): about 10%
separation decrease in the inertial equal-mass case, angular momentum within
10% and energy "about an order of magnitude better"; energy conservation worse
than Marcello & Tohline (2012) because negative internal energy is reset to the
temperature floor. Gravitational-wave strain (Fig. 15) in "very good
agreement" with point-mass formulas. Orbit resolution study (Figs. 16–17):
convergent at $1024^3$ effective in the inertial frame, slower in the rotating
frame; at least 200 km resolution is recommended. Scaling on Blue Waters
(Figs. 18–19): weak scaling $128^3$ to $2048^3$ on 8 to 32,768 processors,
"excellent" through 4,096 and "slightly less than 1.5 times" the smallest
run's time per step at 32,768, attributed to multigrid, "a significant
improvement" over Almgren et al. (2010); strong scaling good with at least two
grids per processor.
