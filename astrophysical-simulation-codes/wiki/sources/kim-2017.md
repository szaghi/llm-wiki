# Summary

TIGRESS is a set of physics modules built into [Athena](/software/athena.md)
for a local, vertically stratified shearing-box patch of a galactic disc
holding a self-gravitating, magnetized, three-phase interstellar medium with
self-consistent star formation and supernova (SN) feedback. The code
contributions are: sink/star particles after Gong & Ostriker (2013) with a
Larson–Penston density threshold, flux-based accretion only under
convergence along all three axes, mass-weighted ageing and a symplectic
kick–drift–kick integrator for the shearing box (Quinn et al. 2010,
generalized to arbitrary shear); time-variable far-ultraviolet (FUV)
photoelectric heating from population synthesis; stochastic SNe from
clusters and runaway OB stars; and a three-way SN feedback prescription
chosen per event from the resolved mass of the feedback region. Each module
has a unit test. A fiducial Solar-neighbourhood run at 4 pc reaches a
self-regulated state over about 700 Myr, and a resolution study from 2 to
64 pc concludes that star formation rate, wind mass-loss rate, scale
heights, velocity dispersions and phase volume fractions are converged at a
cell size of 8 pc or finer. Merged into [TIGRESS](/software/tigress.md),
[Athena](/software/athena.md), [sink particles](/methods/sink-particles.md),
the [Jeans resolution criterion](/definitions/jeans-resolution-criterion.md),
[cooling integration](/methods/radiative-cooling-integration.md) and
[unsplit Godunov integrators](/methods/unsplit-godunov-integrators.md).

# Key claims

1. **Equations** (§2.1, eqs. 1–6). Ideal MHD in a local shearing box, in
   Gaussian units, with Coriolis force, tidal potential (shear parameter
   $q \equiv -d\ln\Omega/d\ln R = 1$), self-gravity of gas plus particles,
   a fixed external potential (stellar disc plus dark matter, eq. 6) and a
   net cooling term $\rho\mathcal L = n_{\mathrm H}[n_{\mathrm H}\Lambda(T) - \Gamma]$.
   The particle density is deposited on the nearest $3^3$ cells by
   triangular-shaped-cloud (TSC) assignment and added to the Poisson source.
2. **Numerics** (§2.1). Athena's directionally unsplit Godunov scheme with
   [constrained transport](/methods/constrained-transport.md) (Stone et al.
   2008); the van Leer predictor–corrector of Stone & Gardiner (2009);
   piecewise-linear reconstruction; Roe Riemann solver. The H-correction
   (Sanders et al. 1998) is applied "when the difference in signal speed is
   larger than 20 km s⁻¹", to suppress the carbuncle in SN blast waves. The
   first-order flux correction of Lemaster & Stone (2009) is applied "to
   cells with negative pressure and/or density after the second-order
   update". FFT Poisson solver with shearing-periodic horizontal (Gammie
   2001) and vacuum vertical (Koyama & Ostriker 2009) boundaries. No
   explicit conduction, viscosity or Ohmic resistivity.
3. **Cooling** (§2.1, Fig. 1). Net cooling is "solved fully implicitly in an
   operator-split manner before the integrator step", sub-cycled when
   $t_{\mathrm{cool}} \equiv \vert P/[(\gamma-1)\rho\mathcal L]\vert < \Delta t$
   with "an adaptive time step corresponding to the instantaneous cooling
   time". $\Lambda(T)$: Koyama & Inutsuka (2002) below $10^{4.2}$ K,
   Sutherland & Dopita (1993) collisional ionization equilibrium at solar
   metallicity above. $\mu(T)$ is interpolated between 1.295 (atomic) and
   0.618 (ionized) and found "iteratively for a given pair of P and ρ". No
   chemistry, no molecular gas.
4. **Sink creation** (§2.2.1, eq. 7). A cell must (1) exceed the
   Larson–Penston threshold $\rho_{\mathrm{thr}} = 8.86\,c_s^2/(\pi G\Delta x^2)$,
   $c_s \equiv (P/\rho)^{1/2}$; (2) be a potential minimum over the $3^3$
   control volume; (3) converge along every axis, "not simply ∇·v < 0". The
   Truelove threshold has "the same parameter dependence, but with a
   coefficient of π/16 instead of 8.86/π". Control-volume radius
   $r_{\mathrm{ctrl}} = 1.5\Delta x$; no creation within $2r_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$
   of another sink. Of the potential-minimum and convergence checks the
   authors note that "if the resolution is high enough, the additional
   criteria are essentially always satisfied (see GO13)"; Jeans-instability
   and boundedness checks are not used and not discussed. On creation the control volume
   is reset like ghost zones (shear velocity removed before averaging and
   restored after; magnetic fields not reset), and the particle takes the
   mass and momentum difference.
5. **Threshold against resolution** (§2.2.1, eq. 8, Fig. 3). Collapse is
   "captured" only when the threshold pressure greatly exceeds the
   two-phase pressure, $3110\,k_{\mathrm B}$ cm⁻³ K $(\Gamma/\Gamma_0)$;
   Solar-neighbourhood conditions need $\Delta x < 20$ pc. Instantaneous
   sink-to-star conversion is stated valid up to $\Delta x = 8$ pc
   ($n_{\mathrm{thr}} \gtrsim 100$ cm⁻³, $t_{\mathrm{ff}} \sim 4$ Myr).
6. **Accretion and merging** (§2.2.2, eqs. 9–11). Accretion only when the
   face-summed mass fluxes in the particle frame converge onto the control
   volume along all three axes, evaluated at $t^{n+1/2}$; mass and momentum
   accretion from the Riemann fluxes at the control-volume boundary plus
   the control-volume change when the particle crosses cells. Positions are
   advanced before the MHD step to preserve Galilean invariance. Merging
   within $2r_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ at the centre of mass. Accretion stops at the
   first SN.
7. **Ageing** (§2.2.3–2.2.4, eq. 12). Particle classes growing (age below
   about 4 Myr), feedback (to 40 Myr), passive and runaway (massless);
   mass-weighted mean age updated on accretion.
8. **Particle integrator** (§2.2.5, eqs. 13–21). Explicit, implicit and
   semi-implicit kick–drift–kick schemes "are not symplectic in a shearing
   box"; the Quinn et al. (2010) symplectic integrator is generalized to
   arbitrary $q$. Each step begins with the previous step's second kick,
   because self-gravity at $t^{n+1}$ needs the updated gas.
9. **FUV heating** (§2.3.1, eq. 24). Uniform in space, varying in time, from
   STARBURST99 FUV luminosities of the particles (Kroupa IMF); no radiative
   transfer (ray tracing "underway", footnote 1).
10. **SN rates and runaways** (§2.3.2, eqs. 25–27). A SN fires when the
    expected number per step exceeds a uniform random number; one SN per
    95.5 $M_\odot$ of new stars; binary fraction 2/3 for runaways; Type Ia
    neglected.
11. **SN feedback selection** (§2.3.3). The feedback radius starts at
    $3\Delta x$ and grows by $\Delta x/2$; the ratio of enclosed mass to the
    shell-formation mass selects momentum injection (MC, ratio above 1),
    Sedov–Taylor energy injection of $10^{51}$ erg as about 72% thermal and
    28% kinetic (ST, ratio between 0.027 and 1), or pure kinetic ejecta (EJ,
    ratio below 0.027 even at the largest radius). ST and EJ first reset the
    region to its mean density, momentum and internal energy. The 0.027
    criterion corresponds to $\Delta x/r_{\mathrm{sf}} = 1/10$ at
    $3\Delta x$; a ratio near 1 is "marginal" and "unsafe" without the reset
    because of overcooling.
12. **Convergence verdict** (abstract, §5). "all these ISM properties are
    converged at ∆x ≤ 8 pc", "such that the Sedov-Taylor stage is resolved
    for most SN events"; "At low resolution (∆x ≥ 16 pc), the detailed ISM
    properties as well as the mean SFR cannot be properly captured". The
    requirement is "particular to the Solar neighborhood model".

**Tests.**
* *Non-isothermal sphere collapse* (§2.2.1, Fig. 2): a cold-neutral-medium
  sphere ($T_{\mathrm{center}} = 20$ K, edge pressure $2000\,k_{\mathrm B}$
  cm⁻³ K) with density and pressure doubled, at $\Delta x = 0.031$ and
  0.078 pc. The density approaches the Larson–Penston profile and
  $v_r \sim -3c_s$ at singularity formation; with the sink criterion both
  resolutions give "essentially the same" profiles; without a sink,
  "unphysical overshoots of density and out-of-equilibrium temperature"
  appear. Qualitative.
* *Epicyclic orbit* (§2.2.5, eqs. 22–23, Fig. 4): explicit kick–drift–kick
  gains energy secularly, implicit loses it, semi-implicit and Quinn et al.
  conserve it; Quinn et al. has smaller, more regular phase errors, and the
  mean phase error over $t\Omega = 40$ converges at second order in
  $\Delta t$.
* *Single radiative SN remnant* (§2.3.3, Fig. 6), $\Delta x = 4$ pc,
  ambient density varied, against the Kim & Ostriker (2015a) fits: all
  three injection modes agree for $\Delta x/r_{\mathrm{sf}} < 1/10$ and
  differ by "a factor of a few" at 1/3; pure thermal injection
  underestimates and pure kinetic overestimates the final momentum of
  unresolved remnants; the adopted prescription is within 25% in momentum
  "for all values of the density". Not the adiabatic Sedov–Taylor benchmark.
* *Fiducial model* (§3, Figs. 7–12): $1024\times1024\times4096$ pc at 4 pc
  (256×256×1024 cells, computed from the printed sizes), about 700 Myr;
  $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}} \sim 5\times10^{-3}\,M_\odot$ kpc⁻² yr⁻¹, hot-wind
  mass loading about 0.1. Footnote 4: numerical diffusion acts as a
  "numerical conductivity" larger than the physical one, thickening the
  cold/warm interface at low resolution.
* *Resolution study* (§4, Figs. 13–18): models at 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and
  64 pc. Star formation rate agrees except at 32 and 64 pc (64 pc
  "several times larger"); most SNe are ST up to 8 pc, more MC at coarser
  resolution; velocity dispersion, Alfvén-speed fluctuation and scale
  height converge for $\Delta x \le 16$ pc; phases "essentially converged"
  for 8 to 2 pc, with "thermal runaway" at 64 pc; winds converged for 2 to
  8 pc. Footnote 6: with overlapping remnants forbidden, low-resolution runs
  diverged in the opposite sense; "with insufficient resolution, the ISM
  properties fail to converge in unpredictable ways."

No performance, wall-clock or scaling numbers are reported.
