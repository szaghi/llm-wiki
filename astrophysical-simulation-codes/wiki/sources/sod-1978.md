# Summary

A review of finite-difference methods for the 1D Euler equations of an
ideal polytropic gas (Cartesian, inviscid, non-heat-conducting). All the
methods are run on one shock-tube problem, the origin of the
[Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-shock-tube.md), and compared by figure,
transition widths and CPU time. The methods: the standard schemes of
Godunov (in the finite-difference form of Godunov, Zabrodin & Prokopov),
two-step Lax–Wendroff, MacCormack, Rusanov and upwind; Glimm's random
choice method with Chorin's exact Riemann-solver iteration; Harten's
artificial compression method (ACM) as a corrective step; the
self-adjusting hybrid of Harten and Zwas; the antidiffusion
(flux-correction) method of Boris and Book; and Hyman's
predictor–corrector. Conclusions: without corrective procedures,
Godunov's and Hyman's methods are best; Glimm's method resolves shocks
and contacts with no transition zone and needs far fewer points; ACM
beats Boris–Book antidiffusion; every corrective and hybrid method
carries problem-dependent parameters. Sod calls the 1D shock tube a
"sitting duck" for Riemann-solver methods and holds that the true test is
multidimensional problems.

# Key claims

Equations are in the [registry](/notation.md) notation where it has the
symbols, otherwise in words (see the Notation map).

1. **Equations** (§1, eqs. 2–8). Conserved $\rho$, $\rho v_x$ and the
   energy per unit volume $E = \rho e + \tfrac12\rho v_x^2$; polytropic
   EOS $e = p/((\gamma - 1)\rho)$ (eq. 3); flux
   $f(u) = (\rho v_x,\ \rho v_x^2 + p,\ v_x(E + p))^T$ (eq. 5); weak form
   eqs. 6–8.
2. **Standard schemes** (Table I). Stability parameter (table footnote):
   the Courant number $\nu = \max(\vert v_x\vert + c_s)\Delta t/h$.
   * **Godunov**, first order, $\nu \le 1$: a half-step average of the two
     neighbouring states minus $\Delta t/h$ times the flux difference, then
     a conservative update with the half-step fluxes. This is the
     finite-difference scheme of Godunov, Zabrodin & Prokopov for the
     Eulerian equations, **with no Riemann solver**; the Riemann-solver
     Godunov method is described only for the Lagrangian form (§2, p. 7),
     where the star pressure and velocity of eqs. 18–19 replace the
     half-step values.
   * **Two-step Lax–Wendroff** (attributed to Richtmyer), second order,
     $\nu \le 1$; **MacCormack**, second order (forward predictor, backward
     corrector), $\nu \le 1$.
   * **Rusanov**, first order: a centred flux difference plus a viscosity
     whose coefficient is a constant times $(\Delta t/h)(\vert v_x\vert + c_s)$,
     averaged to the interfaces; stable for $\nu \le 1$ with the constant
     between $\nu$ and $1/\nu$.
   * **Upwind**, first order: the advective part of the flux upwinded by
     the sign of $v_x$, the pressure gradient centred; $\nu \le 1$.
3. **Glimm's random choice method** (§2, eqs. 9–10). The Riemann problems
   between neighbouring cells are solved exactly and sampled at a random
   point of the cell, equidistributed on $[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]$ in units
   of $h$, at the half step. Waves of different Riemann problems do not
   interact if $\Delta t < h/(2(\vert v_x\vert + c_s))$ (p. 5). Glimm's
   original choice, a new random number per grid point and time level, is
   "disastrous" at finite $h$ and can create a spurious state; Chorin's
   improvement is one random number per time step (§3, pp. 14–16).
4. **Exact Riemann solver** (§2, eqs. 12–19): Godunov's iteration as
   modified by Chorin. The left and right Lagrangian wave speeds are the
   pressure jump over the velocity jump across each wave (eqs. 12, 14);
   each equals $(\rho p)^{1/2}$ of its side times a function of the
   pressure ratio across the wave, a shock branch for ratios $\ge 1$ and a
   rarefaction branch for ratios $\le 1$ (eqs. 16–17). The star pressure
   (eq. 18) and star velocity (eq. 19) follow from the two wave speeds and
   the two states, iterated with eqs. 16 from a starting value. The
   Appendix (eqs. 46–62, flow chart Fig. 16) samples the solution in ten
   cases: either side of the contact, shock or rarefaction, inside or
   outside the fan.
5. **Lapidus artificial viscosity** (§2, eq. 20). A fractional step on
   the new state with an adjustable coefficient: the difference of
   (absolute one-sided difference times one-sided difference), times the
   coefficient and $\Delta t/h$; it approximates a nonlinear diffusion
   term proportional to $(\Delta t/h)\,h^3$. It is added to Godunov, MacCormack and two-step
   Lax–Wendroff, not in smooth regions, and **not to the mass equation**,
   "so as to minimize the amount of mass diffused across the contact
   discontinuity". Fourth- and sixth-order schemes with matching
   high-order viscosity made the time step "prohibitively small", and
   Lapidus's low-order viscosity removes their benefit (§1, p. 3).
   Artificial viscosity does not spread a contact: contact smearing comes
   from truncation error and grows as the $1/(q+1)$ power of the number of
   steps for a $q$th-order scheme (§1, p. 2, citing Harten).
6. **Harten's ACM** (§2, eqs. 21–27). An artificial compression flux,
   nonzero only inside the jump range and of the sign of the jump, turns
   contacts into shocks of the modified equation (eqs. 22–24). It is
   applied by operator splitting after a first-order step and is
   restricted to first-order schemes, because it relies on a viscous
   profile (p. 9).
7. **Harten–Zwas hybrid** (§2, eqs. 28–38). The flux is a switch-weighted
   blend of a first-order and a higher-order flux, the switch between 0 and
   1 and near 1 at discontinuities (eq. 31). Here: MacCormack plus a
   switch-weighted viscosity (eqs. 34–36), the switch the normalized
   difference of the absolute one-sided density differences, to the power
   1, with a threshold (eq. 37), and the larger of the two neighbouring
   values used at each interface. Stability
   $\nu \le 3^{1/2}/2$, stricter than MacCormack. ACM can be gated by the
   switch (eq. 38).
8. **Boris–Book antidiffusion** on two-step Lax–Wendroff (§2,
   eqs. 39–44). A diffusion with coefficient times the second difference
   (eq. 44c), then antidiffusive fluxes limited by a minmod-type bound
   against the neighbouring differences (eq. 44d). Stability $\nu \le 1$.
9. **Hyman's predictor–corrector** (§2, eqs. 45a–b). A five-point
   fourth-order central flux derivative, improved-Euler time stepping and
   a Rusanov-like viscosity with coefficient $\vert v_x\vert + c_s$ and a
   weight; a switch reduces the viscosity by a factor $\tfrac13$ where the
   coefficient rises by more than $h/3$ between neighbours (p. 13), which
   Sod calls "a type of artificial compression". Stability taken as
   $\le 1$.
10. **Conclusions** (§4, pp. 24–25). Best without corrective procedures:
    Godunov and Hyman. Best shock and contact resolution: Glimm, "at best
    first-order accurate". ACM "produces far better results than the
    antidiffusion method of Boris and Book". Antidiffusion needs slightly
    more storage (two time levels). Antidiffusion, the hybrid and ACM all
    need problem-dependent parameters (the diffusion coefficient, the
    switch tolerance, a rarefaction detector). Glimm with this Riemann
    solver is limited to Cartesian gas dynamics. The shock tube is a
    "sitting duck" for Riemann-solver methods: "The true value of a
    numerical method should be measured by its ability to solve
    multidimensional problems", where Riemann-based methods "may not have
    any advantage".

# Tests

**One problem: the shock tube** (§3, Figs. 2–3, p. 14).

* **States as printed.** Left (region 1): $\rho = 1.0$, $p = 1.0$,
  $v_x = 0$. Right (region 5): $\rho = 0.125$, $p = 0.1$, $v_x = 0$.
  $\gamma = 1.4$.
* **Grid.** "In all of the calculations $\Delta x = 0.01$"; the timings
  are "for 100 spatial grid points" (p. 24); the figure axes span
  $[0, 1]$.
* **Diaphragm position**: printed only as a symbol in Figs. 2–3; **no
  value is printed**. **End time: not printed** (no time in the captions,
  no step count for the figures).
* **Parameters.** Courant number $\nu = 0.9$ for all schemes. Rusanov
  constant 1.0; Boris–Book diffusion coefficient 0.125; Hyman weight 0.8;
  Lapidus coefficient 1.0, except Godunov with ACM, 2.0, "to ensure that
  the solution before application of artificial compression was
  oscillation free".

**Transition widths** (zones, as printed):

| Fig. | Scheme | Contact | Shock | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Godunov | 7–8 | 5–6 | rarefaction corners rounded; plateau between contact and shock not fully realized |
| 5 | Godunov + ACM (Lapidus 2.0) | 3–4 | 1–2 | slight undershoot at the right corner of the rarefaction; oscillations at the contact |
| 6 | Two-step Lax–Wendroff | 6–8 | 4–6 | very slight overshoots at the contact, more at the shock; rarefaction accurate |
| 7 | MacCormack | 7–8 | 5–6 | similar to Lax–Wendroff |
| 8 | Rusanov | 14–16 | 6–8 | "extremely diffusive"; contact barely visible |
| 9 | Rusanov + ACM | 2–3 | 1–2 | "greatly improved" |
| 10 | Upwind | — | — | non-physical: a discontinuity between the left state and the rarefaction head, from the centred pressure term |
| 11 | Glimm | 0 | 0 | "infinite resolution"; positions exact on average; rarefaction not smooth but close; constant states "perfectly realized" |
| 12 | Boris–Book on Lax–Wendroff | 5–7 | 1–2 | slight overshoot at the right corner of the rarefaction; much better than Lax–Wendroff alone |
| 13 | Harten–Zwas hybrid | 8–9 | 5–6 | oscillation-free |
| 14 | Hybrid + ACM | 3–4 | 2–3 | rarefaction as in Fig. 13 |
| 15 | Hyman (corrector once) | 6–8 | 3–4 | oscillation-free; rarefaction corners almost sharp |

The table also lists a Riemann invariant, defined in its footnote as
$c_s/(\gamma - 1) + v_x/2$ (half the usual one, consistent with the
printed values 2.958, 3.624, 2.646). The star values agree with the exact
solution of the problem to the printed three digits (checked at
ingestion: 0.4263, 0.9274, 0.3031, 0.2656). Nine points still show every
feature.

**Conservation** (Table III, Glimm, totals over steps 1–20). Total mass
fluctuates in 0.543–0.554 and the tabulated energy in 2.213–2.275; total
momentum rises from 0.018 to 0.190. Sod: mass and energy are "conserved
on the average"; "momentum is seen to increase linearly on the average".
