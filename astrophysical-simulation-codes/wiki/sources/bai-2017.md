# Summary

Bai runs 2D axisymmetric $(r,\theta)$ global MHD simulations of a
protoplanetary disk from 0.6 to 60 AU with
[Athena++](/software/athena-plus-plus.md), carrying all three non-ideal
MHD terms (Ohmic, Hall, ambipolar; §2, eq. 3), whose implementation is
credited to Bai & Stone (2017). Diffusivities come from tabulated
equilibrium chemistry (175 species, 2147 reactions, 0.1 µm grains) driven
by ray-traced column densities for cosmic rays, X-rays and far-UV
(§2.4). The temperature relaxes to a target value set by the far-UV
penetration column instead of following a cooling function (§2.3). The
numerical configuration is a logarithmic-$r$, stretched-$\theta$ grid of
$1152\times512$ reaching within 2° of the poles, a density floor of
$10^{-8}$ times the midplane density, a near-fixed inner boundary with a
damping buffer, and a staged switch-on of the Hall term (§2.5, §5,
App. A). The astrophysical result is a laminar disk accreting through a
magneto-thermal wind. The paper is an application: it presents no new
numerical method and reports no verification test, convergence study or
performance figure.

# Key claims

1. **Code** (§2). Athena++ is "the successor of the widely used Athena MHD
   code"; it "works for curvilinear coordinate systems ... where geometric
   source terms are properly implemented that guarantees angular momentum
   conservation", "employs flexible grid spacings", and "All three
   non-ideal MHD terms have been implemented (Bai & Stone 2017)".
2. **Equations** (§2.1, eqs. 1–4). Induction with Ohmic, Hall and
   ambipolar terms (eq. 3); the energy equation omits the non-ideal
   heating and Poynting flux, "not fully self-consistent" but "largely
   irrelevant" given the fast thermal relaxation. Printed in Gaussian
   units; the code absorbs $4\pi$ into the field.
3. **Thermal relaxation** (§2.3, eqs. 10–13). The temperature relaxes to
   a target on a time-scale of about one orbit in the disk and about a
   tenth of an orbit in the atmosphere, chosen to suppress the vertical
   shear instability; $\gamma = 5/3$, and a constant 7/5 gave no
   "appreciable difference".
4. **Ray tracing** (§2.3–§2.5). Radial rays from the star and polar rays
   at constant radius; it "involves global communications" and runs every
   0.5 inverse Keplerian angular frequencies at the inner radius ("at least
   a few thousand timesteps") "so that it does not affect the overall code
   performance".
5. **Ionization and diffusivities** (§2.4, eqs. 14–19). Cosmic-ray,
   X-ray (Bai & Goodman 2009 fit) and radionuclide ionization rates; a
   network of 175 species and 2147 reactions (UMIST 2012) evolved for
   $3\times10^6$ yr to equilibrium; tables of the Ohmic diffusivity, the
   Hall diffusivity over $\vert\mathbf B\vert$ and the ambipolar
   diffusivity over $B^2$. In the far-UV layer the smaller of the table and
   an analytic value is used, "which guarantees a smooth transition"; a
   boost factor (eq. 19) keeps the wind zone ideal.
6. **Grid** (§2.2, §2.5). Logarithmic in $r$ from 0.6 to 60 AU; polar
   spacing growing by a constant factor from midplane to pole (ratio 3.5),
   with a 2° cone left at each pole ("essential to properly accommodate the
   MHD disk wind"); $1152\times512$, about 15 radial and 20 polar cells per
   disk scale height at 3 AU. The disk is thickened on purpose "to
   adequately resolve the disk with available computational resources".
7. **Floor** (§2.5). Density floor $10^{-8}$ times the midplane density at
   the same radius; the atmosphere stays "well above the floor value" once
   the wind is launched.
8. **Initial field** (§2.5, eq. 20). After a hydro-only phase, the
   poloidal field is set from a vector potential so that
   $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B = 0$; fiducial plasma beta $10^5$.
9. **Inner boundary and buffer** (§2.5). An outflow inner boundary "would
   violate causality, and can become unstable in the presence of magnetic
   fields"; after "experimenting with a number of options" the inner ghost
   state is held nearly fixed, and a buffer from the inner radius to 1.5
   times it ramps all diffusivities linearly to zero and damps poloidal
   velocities on the local orbital time. Reflecting polar boundaries.
10. **Hall switch-on** (§5, App. A). With aligned field and the Hall term
    active from the start, the outcome depends on the initial condition
    (App. A, Fig. 22). The fiducial aligned run restarts from the Hall-free
    run and ramps the Hall term as the fourth power of the elapsed time
    over 5 local orbits; the procedure is "less successful for some other
    runs".
11. **Boundary artefacts** (§5.1, §5.2.3, §6.1, §8.1). The inner boundary
    "gradually attracts magnetic flux"; the inner few AU are "not very
    trustable" and are excluded.
12. **Domain** (§4.4, footnote 8, §8.2). The fast magnetosonic point lies
    outside the domain; containing it is stated to be "not crucial" to the
    wind kinematics (unpublished 1D tests). Bai suggests that the
    non-accreting solutions of Béthune et al. (2017) "may be related to
    their over-constraining domain size".
13. **Resolution** (§4.3, §6.2.1). Qualitative only: the accreting layer
    "is well resolved"; a strong current layer is "well resolved by more
    than 10 cells".

**Tests.** No code-verification test. Nine runs (Table 1), all on the
same $1152\times512$ grid, varying field polarity (aligned, anti-aligned,
no Hall), plasma beta ($10^5$, $10^4$) and far-UV column (0.03, 0.3 g
cm$^{-2}$). Internal consistency checks only: radial mass flux against a
predicted torque balance ("excellent agreement", Fig. 9), steady-wind
invariants along field lines ("generally well satisfied" in the Hall-free
fiducial run, "only approximately" elsewhere, Figs. 5, 15), and
sensitivity to the adiabatic index and the field-bending parameter (§2.3,
§2.5, App. A). No convergence study, no performance numbers.
