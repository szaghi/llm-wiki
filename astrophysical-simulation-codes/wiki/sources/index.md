# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Almgren et al. (2010) — CASTRO: A new compressible astrophysical solver. I. Hydrodynamics and self-gravity](/sources/almgren-2010.md) — Method paper of CASTRO, a BoxLib block-structured AMR code for multicomponent compressible Euler with a general EOS: unsplit PPM with characteristic tracing and full corner coupling, a two-shock-type Riemann solver, Strang-split reactions, time-centred gravity sources, subcycled Berger–Oliger AMR with refluxing, and a multilevel Poisson synchronization, with weak scaling to 64,000 processes.
* [Bai (2017) — Global simulations of the inner regions of protoplanetary disks with comprehensive disk microphysics](/sources/bai-2017.md) — Application of Athena++ to 2D axisymmetric global protoplanetary-disk simulations with all three non-ideal MHD terms (Ohmic, Hall, ambipolar) from tabulated chemistry, thermal relaxation in place of a cooling function, a density floor, a near-fixed inner boundary with a damping buffer and a ramped Hall term; no verification, convergence or performance data.
* [Berger & Colella (1989) — Local adaptive mesh refinement for shock hydrodynamics](/sources/berger-1989.md) — The original conservative patch-based AMR for 2D shock hydrodynamics: properly nested, grid-aligned rectangular patches refined by one ratio in space and time, restriction plus a flux-register correction of the coarse cells next to fine grids, bilinear/linear-in-time ghost filling, Richardson truncation-error tagging with bisection-and-merge clustering, the O(1) error of strong shocks crossing coarse–fine interfaces, and a Cray X-MP cost breakdown (integration about 80 %, about 8 times cheaper than a uniform grid).
* [Bernardini et al. (2021) — STREAmS: A high-fidelity accelerated solver for direct numerical simulation of compressible turbulent flows](/sources/bernardini-2021.md) — Presents STREAmS, a Cartesian finite-difference DNS solver for compressible wall turbulence (energy-preserving central flux hybridized with Lax–Friedrichs flux-split WENO under a Ducros sensor, low-storage RK3) kept in one Fortran source for CPUs and GPUs through CUDA Fortran kernels and device-resident data, with a 2.2x memory-layout speed-up on one V100, about 97% weak-scaling efficiency on 1024 V100 GPUs, and about 10x lower accuracy-matched time to solution than the flux-reconstruction code ZEFR.
* [Brio & Wu (1988) — An Upwind Differencing Scheme for the Equations of Ideal Magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/brio-1988.md) — First Roe-type upwind scheme for 1D ideal MHD: an exact Roe matrix only for adiabatic index 2 (an arithmetic-average Jacobian otherwise), renormalized eigenvectors that stay complete at the degeneracies, and the coplanar Riemann problem now called the Brio–Wu shock tube, whose slow compound wave the paper explains by proving ideal MHD nonconvex.
* [Bryan et al. (2014) — ENZO: An adaptive mesh refinement code for astrophysics](/sources/bryan-2014.md) — Method paper of Enzo 2.3, an open-source comoving-coordinate block-structured AMR code with four fluid solvers (split PPM, unsplit MUSCL with GLM cleaning, constrained-transport MHD, ZEUS), the dual-energy formalism, subcycled Berger–Colella AMR with refluxing, FFT plus grid-by-grid multigrid gravity, N-body, chemistry and radiation, 14 verification tests, MPI scaling to 13,824 and 16,384 cores, and a CUDA port about 5× faster per node.
* [Caddy & Schneider (2024) — Cholla-MHD: An Exascale-Capable Magnetohydrodynamic Extension to the Cholla Astrophysical Simulation Code](/sources/caddy-2024.md) — Adds ideal MHD to the GPU-native code Cholla with the VL+CT integrator, HLLD and characteristic PLM/PPM, keeps all data GPU-resident and recomputes rather than stores derived fields, verifies on 3D wave, field-loop, Riemann, blast and Orszag–Tang tests, and weak-scales to 74,088 MI250X GCDs of Frontier at about 80 % efficiency.
* [Colella & Woodward (1984) — The Piecewise Parabolic Method (PPM) for Gas-Dynamical Simulations](/sources/colella-1984.md) — Method paper of PPM: a MUSCL-type higher-order Godunov method with a monotonized parabolic reconstruction, contact steepening, characteristic domain-of-dependence averaging and one Riemann solve per interface, in advection, Lagrangian, Lagrange-plus-remap and direct-Eulerian forms, with flattening and a diffusive flux as the extra dissipation it shows such schemes need near shocks.
* [Colella (1990) — Multidimensional Upwind Methods for Hyperbolic Conservation Laws](/sources/colella-1990.md) — Origin of the corner transport upwind (CTU) scheme: a first-order upwind scheme that integrates across zone corners, stable up to a Courant number of 1 per direction, extended to a second-order unsplit predictor–corrector with characteristic tracing, transverse Riemann-problem corrections and one edge Riemann solve, on quadrilateral grids and for 2D gas dynamics with multidimensional flattening; twice the Riemann solves and 50% more time than splitting in 2D, and 12 (or 6) solves per zone in 3D.
* [Coleman (2020) — An extension of the Athena++ framework for general equations of state](/sources/coleman-2020.md) — Extends non-relativistic Athena++ (M)HD to any equation of state supplying pressure, internal energy and sound speed, adds a log-space bilinear tabular-EOS utility and a PVRS wave-speed estimate for HLLC, and publishes a verification suite with exact solutions for an ionizing-hydrogen and a Helmholtz EOS, on which HLLE fails to converge for asymmetric double shocks.
* [Dedner et al. (2002) — Hyperbolic Divergence Cleaning for the MHD Equations](/sources/dedner-2002.md) — Origin of GLM divergence cleaning: a scalar ψ couples the divergence constraint to the induction equation through elliptic, parabolic, hyperbolic or mixed operators; the mixed choice transports errors at c_h, set each step to the largest speed the time step admits, and damps them by an exactly integrated source, with c_p fixed by c_p²/c_h = 0.18; the conservative mixed GLM is recommended over the nonconservative EGLM and Powell source terms on four 2D tests on triangles.
* [Evans & Hawley (1988) — Simulation of magnetohydrodynamic flows: a constrained transport method](/sources/evans-1988.md) — Origin of constrained transport: the induction equation in integral form for the magnetic flux through each face of a staggered cell, updated by line-integrated edge EMFs that each enter two faces with opposite signs, written metric-free for general relativity on a moving, stretching grid, with a van Leer upwind EMF in 2D axisymmetry and kinematic tests only.
* [Federrath et al. (2010) — Modeling collapse and accretion in turbulent gas clouds: implementation and comparison of sink particles in AMR and SPH](/sources/federrath-2010.md) — Accreting sink particles in the block-AMR code FLASH: creation gated by a resolution density threshold plus refinement, converging-flow, potential-minimum, Jeans-instability, boundedness and proximity checks; bound-and-infalling accretion of the mass above threshold; a four-way gravity split with cubic-spline softening; subcycled leapfrog particle steps; and a 100 solar-mass turbulent cluster compared against SPH.
* [Fryxell et al. (2000) — FLASH: An Adaptive Mesh Hydrodynamics Code for Modeling Astrophysical Thermonuclear Flashes](/sources/fryxell-2000.md) — Code paper of the first version of FLASH: PARAMESH block-structured AMR (8-cell blocks with four guard cells, oct-tree, 2:1 level jumps, Morton-curve load balancing, fine-flux sums at coarse–fine faces, one global time step, Löhner refinement), split direct-Eulerian PPM from PROMETHEUS with a two-shock Riemann solver and a Colella–Glaz general EOS, gamma-law, Nadyozhin and Helmholtz EOS, α-chain networks with a Bader–Deuflhard integrator, a hydrodynamic test suite and scaling on the ASCI machines with an AMR-versus-uniform crossover.
* [Gardiner & Stone (2005) — An unsplit Godunov method for ideal MHD via constrained transport](/sources/gardiner-2005.md) — Origin of the 2D CTU+CT scheme behind Athena: consistency relations tying the finite-volume fluxes of the cell-centred field to the corner EMFs, proof that arithmetic EMF averaging halves the numerical viscosity of grid-aligned flow, three upwind-gradient corner-EMF constructions of which the contact-upwinded one is selected, multidimensional source terms in the PPM characteristic step and in the CTU transverse correction, and 2D tests with second-order linear-wave convergence in all families.
* [Gardiner & Stone (2008) — An unsplit Godunov method for ideal MHD via constrained transport in three dimensions](/sources/gardiner-2008.md) — 3D extension of the CTU+CT scheme behind Athena: minmod-limited multidimensional induction terms in the PPM interface states, the contact-upwinded corner EMF of 2005 reused unchanged, a 12-solve CTU (CFL ≤ 1) that is only sketched for MHD and a 6-solve CTU (stable for CFL < 1/2, observed) given in full and preferred, with 3D field-loop, linear-wave, circularly polarized Alfvén, inclined Riemann and low-beta blast-wave tests.
* [Godunov (1959) — Разностный метод численного расчета разрывных решений уравнений гидродинамики](/sources/godunov-1959.md) — Origin of Godunov's method: the best monotone two-level scheme for linear advection and acoustics, reinterpreted as the decay of the discontinuity (Riemann problem) at each cell interface followed by a conservative cell-average update, extended to 1D Lagrangian gas dynamics with an exact iterative ideal-gas Riemann solver; proves the monotonicity criterion, the absence of monotone linear second-order two-level schemes, consistency of convergent limits with the conservation laws, and a cell-average entropy inequality.
* [Harten et al. (1983) — On Upstream Differencing and Godunov-Type Schemes for Hyperbolic Conservation Laws](/sources/harten-1983.md) — Review that writes upstream schemes for 1D conservation laws as the average of the physical fluxes minus half a dissipation term, extends the Lax–Wendroff theorem to the entropy inequality, defines Godunov-type schemes (conservative averages of approximate Riemann solutions consistent with the integral conservation law and entropy condition), introduces the one-intermediate-state HLL solver and a two-intermediate-state solver, and shows that flux-vector-split schemes cannot resolve stationary discontinuities exactly; no numerical experiments.
* [Jansson et al. (2024) — Neko: A modern, portable, and scalable framework for high-fidelity computational fluid dynamics](/sources/jansson-2024.md) — Presents Neko, an object-oriented Fortran 2008 matrix-free spectral-element framework for incompressible flow that reaches CPUs, vector engines, GPUs and FPGAs through backend-specific extensions of abstract Fortran types, matches Nek5000 and NekRS on a Cray XC40 and in a Taylor–Green DNS, and models its own strong-scaling limit with a roofline-plus-postal performance model; read as the 2021 arXiv preprint.
* [Jiang et al. (2013) — A New Way to Conserve Total Energy for Eulerian Hydrodynamic Simulations with Self-Gravity](/sources/jiang-2013.md) — Writes the self-gravity energy equation in conservative form with a gravitational energy flux built from the potential and its time derivative, discretizes it in Athena with an extra Poisson-type solve for the time derivative from the Riemann mass fluxes, and shows round-off total-energy conservation and second-order Jeans-wave convergence, against energy errors, symmetry loss and spurious rotation of a non-conservative energy source in Jeans and polytrope collapse.
* [Kastaun, Kalinani & Ciolfi (2021) — Robust recovery of primitive variables in relativistic ideal magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/kastaun-2021.md) — Builds primitive-variable recovery for ideal GRMHD with an arbitrary EOS as the root of one scalar master function, proves a root exists in an explicit bracket for any input and is unique whenever the sound speed is subluminal, derives linear error bounds on all recovered quantities, and releases the RePrimAnd library, which never fails in stand-alone tests and needs at most 23 EOS calls.
* [Katz et al. (2016) — White dwarf mergers on adaptive meshes. I. Methodology and code verification](/sources/katz-2016.md) — Documents the changes to Castro since 2010 for white-dwarf mergers — a face-based gravitational energy source that conserves total energy up to the Poisson tolerance while the momentum source stays cell-centred, a rotating frame with an implicit Coriolis update, multipole Poisson boundary conditions, PPM and source-tracing changes, density and temperature floors — and verifies them on Poisson convergence, hydrostatic and moving stars, Galilean-invariance Kelvin–Helmholtz runs, Keplerian orbits and scaling to 32,768 processors.
* [Kim & Ostriker (2017) — Three-phase Interstellar Medium in Galaxies Resolving Evolution with Star Formation and Supernova Feedback (TIGRESS): Algorithms, Fiducial Model, and Convergence](/sources/kim-2017.md) — Introduces TIGRESS, a set of Athena modules for a vertically stratified, self-gravitating, magnetized shearing-box ISM: Larson–Penston-threshold sink/star particles with a symplectic shearing-box integrator, time-variable FUV heating, stochastic supernovae with a resolution-adaptive three-way feedback prescription, and a 2–64 pc resolution study finding convergence at a cell size of 8 pc or finer.
* [Krumholz, McKee & Klein (2004) — Embedding Lagrangian Sink Particles in Eulerian Grids](/sources/krumholz-2004.md) — First method for moving, accreting Lagrangian sink particles in an Eulerian (AMR or fixed-grid) code: creation where a cell violates the Truelove Jeans condition with J = 0.25, friends-of-friends merging, a kernel-weighted Bondi–Hoyle accretion rate with an angular-momentum filter, and a softened gas–particle force with Bulirsch–Stoer particle orbits, tested on Shu collapse, Bondi, Mach-3 Bondi–Hoyle and Keplerian-disk problems.
* [Lesur et al. (2023) — IDEFIX: a versatile performance-portable Godunov code for astrophysical flows](/sources/lesur-2023.md) — Method paper of IDEFIX, a Kokkos C++ finite-volume Godunov code for non-relativistic HD and MHD with PLUTO-compatible inputs, vector-potential-capable constrained transport, RKL2 super-time-stepping, non-ideal MHD and orbital advection on non-AMR curvilinear grids, run fully on the device, with 1240 Mcell/s per MI250X node and above 95% weak-scaling efficiency on 1024 GPUs.
* [Mathew & Federrath (2021) — The IMF and multiplicity of stars from gravity, turbulence, magnetic fields, radiation and outflow feedback](/sources/mathew-2021.md) — Application of FLASH4 (PARAMESH block AMR) to star-cluster formation in two ensembles of ten driven-turbulence MHD runs with and without protostellar outflows, documenting a production configuration: HLL5R solver, multigrid gravity, Jeans refinement at 16 cells, sinks with intrinsic spin and leapfrog integration, a piecewise-polytropic EOS and sub-grid heating and outflow modules, at one resolution and without a convergence study.
* [Mignone et al. (2012) — The PLUTO code for adaptive mesh computations in astrophysical fluid dynamics](/sources/mignone-2012.md) — Describes PLUTO-CHOMBO, the block-structured AMR version of PLUTO: a cell-centred unsplit CTU scheme with GLM divergence cleaning (chosen over CT to ease AMR), unsplit parabolic terms, a leaf-cell time step for stiff local sources, and subcycled Berger–Colella AMR from Chombo, tested on MHD and relativistic MHD problems with AMR speed-ups up to about 1978× in 1D and strong scaling to 1024–2048 cores.
* [Mullen, Hanawa & Gammie (2021) — An Extension of the Athena++ Framework for Fully Conservative Self-Gravitating Hydrodynamics](/sources/mullen-2021.md) — Discretizes the self-gravity momentum and energy source terms in Athena++ as exact discrete divergences of a gravitational stress tensor and energy flux, so total momentum and energy are conserved to round-off with periodic boundaries; shows the stress tensor of Athena and Athena++ v19.0 is not curl-free and causes anomalous accelerations near massive bodies with light atmospheres, and gives a corrected tensor.
* [Ohlmann et al. (2016) — Hydrodynamic moving-mesh simulations of the common envelope phase in binary stellar systems](/sources/ohlmann-2016.md) — First AREPO moving-mesh simulation of the common-envelope dynamical spiral-in, with an HLLC-type solver on a Voronoi mesh, tree gravity, individual time steps and mass-targeted refinement, conserving energy to 3 % and angular momentum to 1 %, and attributing the Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities it finds to the code's Galilean invariance.
* [Porth et al. (2019) — The Event Horizon General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamic Code Comparison Project](/sources/porth-2019.md) — Nine GRMHD codes (Athena++, BHAC, Cosmos++, ECHO, H-AMR, HARM-Noble, iharm3D, IllinoisGRMHD, KORAL) evolve one weakly magnetized Fishbone–Moncrief torus around a spinning black hole at three resolutions with their own grids, boundaries and floors; the spread in horizon fluxes falls with resolution (accretion-rate max/min 1.772 at the highest standard resolution), and the residual differences are traced to MRI resolution, polar boundaries, outer-boundary placement, floors and PPM versus PLM reconstruction.
* [Rosdahl et al. (2013) — RAMSES-RT: Radiation hydrodynamics in the cosmological context](/sources/rosdahl-2013.md) — Adds multigroup ionizing radiative transfer to the cell-based AMR code RAMSES as an explicit first-order M1 moment method in photon number with HLL or global Lax–Friedrichs fluxes, operator-split non-equilibrium H/He thermochemistry and a reduced speed of light whose validity it bounds by f_c = min(1, ~10 t_cross/tau_sim); RT is multi-stepped with the hydro across levels, not subcycled, so photons are conserved exactly in transport.
* [Rossazza et al. (2026) — The PLUTO Code on GPUs: A First Look at Eulerian MHD Methods](/sources/rossazza-2026.md) — First description of gPLUTO, a C++ rewrite of PLUTO that runs the whole ideal-MHD time step on NVIDIA GPUs through OpenACC, with flat coalesced arrays, Unified Memory, device-side halo packing, weak and strong scaling to 256–512 nodes, a node-to-node GPU/CPU ratio of about 9, and GPU-frequency energy savings of 8–11 %.
* [Ryu & Jones (1995) — Numerical Magnetohydrodynamics in Astrophysics: Algorithm and Tests for One-Dimensional Flow](/sources/ryu-1995.md) — Code paper of a 1D ideal-MHD code built on Harten's second-order TVD scheme with a Roe-type linearized solver: the full seven-wave eigensystem with Brio–Wu-renormalized eigenvectors and a continuity sign rule, arithmetic interface averages instead of Roe averages, a rotational-discontinuity steepener, and a nonlinear MHD Riemann solver with exact rarefactions used as the reference for the Ryu–Jones shock tubes (tests 1a–5b) and for the interpretation of compound waves as intermediate shocks.
* [Shankar et al. (2023) — GRaM-X: A new GPU-accelerated dynamical spacetime GRMHD code for Exascale computing with the Einstein Toolkit](/sources/shankar-2023.md) — Code paper of GRaM-X, a C++ dynamical-spacetime GRMHD code for the Einstein Toolkit that runs on GPUs through the CarpetX driver on AMReX, with Z4c spacetime, Valencia GRMHD, HLLE fluxes, a three-stage primitive-recovery chain, tabulated nuclear EOS in unified memory, device-resident data and block-structured AMR without subcycling; verified on relativistic shock tubes, a cylindrical explosion, the relativistic rotor and a magnetized TOV star, with up to 0.47e8 zone-cycles/s on one V100 and 40-50% weak-scaling efficiency at 2304 Summit nodes relative to one node.
* [Siegel et al. (2018) — Recovery schemes for primitive variables in general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/siegel-2018.md) — Stand-alone comparison of five conservative-to-primitive recovery schemes for ideal GRMHD with ideal-gas, Helmholtz and LS220 equations of state, showing that Newton–Raphson schemes with temperature as unknown need 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer EOS calls on tabulated EOSs, that the bracketed one-dimensional schemes are more robust, and recommending a 3D Newton–Raphson first pass with a one-dimensional fallback.
* [Skinner & Ostriker (2013) — A two-moment radiation hydrodynamics module in Athena using a time-explicit Godunov method](/sources/skinner-2013.md) — Describes Hyperion, a gray mixed-frame M1 radiation hydrodynamics module for Athena: explicit unsplit VL transport with an HLL solver using closed-form multidimensional M1 eigenvalues, radiation subcycled under a reduced speed of light bounded by c_hat >> v_max max(1, tau_max), and a local implicit quartic for the gas-radiation energy exchange; verified on nine tests.
* [Smith et al. (2017) — Grackle: a Chemistry and Cooling Library for Astrophysics](/sources/smith-2017.md) — Method and software paper of Grackle, an open-source library extracted from Enzo that integrates non-equilibrium primordial chemistry and radiative cooling as a point-local, sub-cycled source step for a host code: sequential first-order backward-difference species updates under a 10% change limit, Cloudy metal-cooling tables, dust, UV backgrounds with self-shielding, a C/Fortran/Python API, unit and answer tests, and serial and OpenMP performance.
* [Sod (1978) — A Survey of Several Finite Difference Methods for Systems of Nonlinear Hyperbolic Conservation Laws](/sources/sod-1978.md) — Review that runs the standard schemes (Godunov's finite-difference form, two-step Lax–Wendroff, MacCormack, Rusanov, upwind), Glimm's random choice with Chorin's exact Riemann solver, Harten's artificial compression, the Harten–Zwas hybrid, Boris–Book antidiffusion and Hyman's predictor–corrector on one ideal-gas shock tube, the origin of the Sod shock tube, comparing transition widths and CDC 6600 timings.
* [Stone et al. (2008) — Athena: A new code for astrophysical MHD](/sources/stone-2008.md) — Method paper of Athena, a second-order unsplit Godunov code for ideal MHD with face-centred constrained transport whose edge EMFs come from the face fluxes by upwinded gradients, CTU+CT integrators in 2D and 3D, HLLE/HLLC/HLLD/Roe solvers, MPI weak scaling above 85% to 20,000 processors, and a 1D–3D test suite showing second-order convergence for all wave families.
* [Stone et al. (2020) — The Athena++ adaptive mesh refinement framework: Design and magnetohydrodynamic solvers](/sources/stone-2020.md) — Method paper of Athena++, a C++ rewrite of Athena that abstracts the mesh from the physics: block-based tree AMR of fixed-size MeshBlocks with native face- and edge-centred data for constrained transport, a single global time step, task-list execution, method-of-lines VL2 and SSP Runge–Kutta integrators, curvilinear coordinates, and relativistic MHD, with 84–86% weak-scaling efficiency on 524,288 threads.
* [Stone et al. (2026) — AthenaK: A Performance-Portable Version of the Athena++ AMR Framework](/sources/stone-2026.md) — Method paper of AthenaK, a Kokkos reimplementation of the Athena++ block-based AMR framework with device-resident data and MeshBlockPacks, documenting Newtonian, SR and GR (M)HD, two-fluid MHD, GR radiation and particles, with 1077 Mzone-cycles/s per Grace Hopper device for 3D hydro and 80% weak-scaling efficiency on 65,536 Frontier GCDs.
* [Tejeda & Rosswog (2013) — An accurate Newtonian description of particle motion around a Schwarzschild black hole](/sources/tejeda-2013.md) — Derives from the low-energy limit of Schwarzschild geodesics a velocity-dependent generalized Newtonian potential whose equations of motion reproduce exactly the characteristic orbit radii, circular-orbit energy and angular momentum, trajectory shapes and pericentre advance, outperforms the Paczyński–Wiita and Nowak–Wagoner potentials on every test but hover thrust, and is added to a Newtonian SPH code for a tidal-disruption run.
* [Tomida & Stone (2023) — The Athena++ Adaptive Mesh Refinement Framework: Multigrid Solvers for Self-Gravity](/sources/tomida-2023.md) — Describes the geometric multigrid Poisson solver of Athena++: cell-centred 7-point Laplacian, red-black Gauss-Seidel with over-relaxation 1.15, V(1,1) cycles and full multigrid, and on AMR a full-approximation-scheme V-cycle over the whole hierarchy with a conservative fine-coarse ghost formula that needs no flux correction; second order, about 0.125 error reduction per V-cycle on uniform grids, and one FMG sweep costing about 15% of an MHD step.
* [Townsend (2009) — An exact integration scheme for radiative cooling in hydrodynamical simulations](/sources/townsend-2009.md) — Integrates the isochoric, operator-split optically thin cooling sub-step exactly for any time step through a temporal evolution function and its inverse, closed-form for piecewise power-law cooling curves, and shows that backward-Euler and Crank–Nicolson cooling updates can have multiple roots and jump between them as the time step varies.
* [Truelove et al. (1997) — The Jeans Condition: A New Constraint on Spatial Resolution in Simulations of Isothermal Self-gravitational Hydrodynamics](/sources/truelove-1997.md) — Origin of the Jeans condition: in multiple-grid simulations of isothermal self-gravitating collapse, discretization errors on scales above the Jeans length grow into artificial fragments; with a Jeans number (cell size over Jeans length) kept at or below 0.25 they did not in the authors' Gaussian-cloud tests, and resolving the Jeans length is stated as necessary but not in general sufficient for convergence.
* [Villasenor et al. (2021) — Effects of Photoionization and Photoheating on Lyman-α Forest Properties from Cholla Cosmological Simulations](/sources/villasenor-2021.md) — Extends the GPU code Cholla to cosmological hydrodynamics plus N-body (super-comoving coordinates, an Enzo-like single-threshold dual-energy switch, FFT gravity, cloud-in-cell kick–drift–kick particles, Grackle chemistry), validates it against Enzo, Nyx and Ramses including a new mean-cosmic-temperature test of the dual-energy switch, and runs 2048^3 Lyman-α forest simulations on 512 Summit GPUs.
* [Wibking & Krumholz (2022) — Quokka: A code for two-moment AMR radiation hydrodynamics on GPUs](/sources/wibking-2022.md) — Presents Quokka, a GPU-native subcycled block-structured AMR code on AMReX: RK2-SSP method-of-lines hydrodynamics with PPM, Miller–Colella flattening and HLLC; gray two-moment radiation under a reduced speed of light with a pluggable local closure, HLL fluxes and per-level radiation subcycling; a Newton–Raphson matter–radiation exchange; 18 verification tests, V100/A100 throughput and scaling, and a derivation that the reduced speed of light shifts closed-box equilibria.
* [Woodward & Colella (1984) — The Numerical Simulation of Two-Dimensional Fluid Flow with Strong Shocks](/sources/woodward-1984.md) — Review comparing seven difference schemes (MacCormack with Lapidus viscosity, BBC, ETBFCT, first-order Godunov, MUSCL, and PPM in Lagrange-plus-remap and direct-Eulerian forms) on three strong-shock problems it fixes as benchmarks — the interacting blast waves with a measured L1 error, the Emery Mach 3 step with a corner treatment, and the double Mach reflection of a Mach 10 shock — finding PPM first in accuracy per zone and per CPU second.
* [Zaghi et al. (2023) — Efficient GPU parallelization of adaptive mesh refinement technique for high-order compressible solver with immersed boundary](/sources/zaghi-2023.md) — Presents ADAM, a Fortran 2003 block-octree AMR library whose compute phase runs entirely on NVIDIA GPUs through CUDA Fortran with a block-index-contiguous device layout and CUDA-aware MPI, the octree being a serial CPU hash map, and its WENO / Lax–Friedrichs / SSP-RK3 Navier–Stokes application NASTO with an immersed boundary; on V100s the compute phase scales near-ideally to 8 GPUs and about 90% at 16, while the serial AMR update dominates at high block counts.
* [Zhang, Schive & Chiueh (2018) — Magnetohydrodynamics with GAMER](/sources/zhang-2018.md) — Extends the GPU-accelerated octree-patch AMR code GAMER to ideal MHD with Athena's CTU and VL unsplit integrators plus constrained transport, keeps the divergence free across levels with Balsara (2001) prolongation and coarse-fine EMF correction, and reports 1.2e8 (single) / 5.5e7 (double) cell updates per second on one P100 and about 70% weak and strong scaling efficiency on 1024 Blue Waters GPU nodes.
