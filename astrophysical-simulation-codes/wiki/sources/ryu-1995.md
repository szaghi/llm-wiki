# Summary

A 1D ideal-MHD code based on Harten's (1983) second-order explicit TVD
scheme, the second-order extension of a Roe-type upwind scheme, with the
linearized Riemann solver of Brio & Wu (§§1–2). The paper prints the full
seven-wave eigensystem in conserved variables, with Brio–Wu-style
**renormalized** right eigenvectors, the matching left eigenvectors and a
sign rule that keeps the columns continuous, so that others "can recover
them by following the descriptions" (§1). Roe averaging is replaced by
arithmetic interface averages, and an artificial steepener sharpens
rotational discontinuities. A nonlinear MHD Riemann solver in the style of
Dai & Woodward, with fast and slow rarefactions integrated exactly instead
of as "rarefaction shocks" (§3), is the reference for a set of 1D shock
tubes, the Ryu–Jones tests 1a–5b (§4). From 8192-cell runs the paper reads
the Brio–Wu slow compound wave as a "2-4" intermediate shock followed by a
slow rarefaction, and finds an analogous fast compound wave (§5). The paper
is strictly one-dimensional. See
[approximate Riemann solvers](/methods/approximate-riemann-solvers.md).

# Key claims

Equations are in the [registry](/notation.md) notation where it has the
symbols, otherwise in words (see the Notation map). Units are chosen "so
that factor of $4\pi$ does not appear in the equations" (§2.1), so the
test states carry $1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ factors.

1. **Equations** (§2.1, eqs. 2.1–2.9). 1D conservative ideal MHD with
   $u = (\rho, \rho v_x, \rho v_y, \rho v_z, B_y, B_z, E)^T$ and $B_x$
   constant; $P^* = p + \tfrac12 B^2$ (eq. 2.8);
   $E = \tfrac12\rho\vert\mathbf v\vert^2 + p/(\gamma - 1) + \tfrac12 B^2$ (eq. 2.9).
2. **Eigenvalues** (§2.2, eqs. 2.10–2.20): $v_x \mp c_f$, $v_x \mp c_A$,
   $v_x \mp$ the slow speed, and $v_x$, with $c_A^2 = B_x^2/\rho$ and the
   fast and slow speeds the roots of the usual quartic in $c_s$, $B^2/\rho$
   and $B_x^2/\rho$ (eqs. 2.18–2.19, read from the text layer).
3. **Renormalized eigenvectors** (§2.2, eqs. 2.21–2.38). The unnormalized
   right eigenvectors (after Jeffrey & Taniuti) are singular "near the
   point where either $B_x = 0$ or $B_y = B_z = 0$"; the Brio–Wu
   renormalization removes the singularities. It uses a fast normalizer,
   the square root of $(c_f^2 - c_A^2)$ over ($c_f^2$ minus the squared slow
   speed), a slow normalizer, the square root of $(c_f^2 - c_s^2)$ over the
   same denominator, and the two direction cosines of the transverse field
   (eqs. 2.33–2.36). Degenerate limits: the direction cosines are set to
   $1/\sqrt2$ when $B_y = B_z = 0$ (eq. 2.37), and both normalizers to 1
   when in addition $c_A = c_s$ (eq. 2.38). The fast, Alfvén, slow and
   entropy right eigenvectors are printed in full (eqs. 2.27–2.32); the
   left eigenvectors, orthonormal to them, use two further normalizers
   (eqs. 2.39–2.73).
4. **Continuity sign rule** (eq. 2.74). With this normalization "some
   columns are not continuous"; the sign of the transverse field ($B_y$,
   or $B_z$ when $B_y = 0$) multiplies the slow eigenvectors when
   $c_s > c_A$ and the fast ones when $c_s < c_A$.
5. **Limits** (eqs. 2.75–2.79). As the transverse field vanishes the fast
   eigenvectors become the sound-wave ones when $c_s > c_A$ (the roles swap
   otherwise), and the hydro shear eigenvectors are combinations of slow
   and Alfvén ones. With $B_z = v_z = 0$ the Alfvén eigenvectors are trivial,
   so "flows with two-dimensional magnetic fields do not produce
   structures associated with Alfvén modes like rotational
   discontinuities".
6. **Arithmetic averages instead of Roe averages** (§2.3,
   eqs. 2.80–2.86). Because Brio & Wu showed that no analytic Roe average
   exists for MHD with $\gamma \ne 2$, the interface state is the
   arithmetic mean of $\rho$, $\mathbf v$, $B_y$, $B_z$ and $P^*$. "Our
   tests for the cases with $\gamma = 2$ indicated that the above simple
   averaging would do just as well when compared to the full
   implementation of Roe's linearization technique."
7. **TVD update** (§2.3, eqs. 2.87–2.95, read from the rendered p. 13).
   $u_i^{n+1} = u_i^n - \frac{\Delta t}{h}(\hat f_{i+1/2} - \hat f_{i-1/2})$
   with $\hat f_{i+1/2} = \tfrac12[f(u_i^n) + f(u_{i+1}^n)]$ minus
   $\frac{h}{2\Delta t}$ times a sum over the seven characteristic fields
   of a flux coefficient times the right eigenvector. Each coefficient is
   Harten's: an entropy-fixed absolute Courant number of the field (plus
   a limited-slope correction) times the field's wave strength (the left
   eigenvector applied to the jump), minus the sum of two minmod-type
   limited corrections. The entropy fix replaces $\vert x\vert$ by
   $x^2/(4\varepsilon) + \varepsilon$ for $\vert x\vert < 2\varepsilon$,
   with $\varepsilon = 0.1$ for the fast, 0.2 for the Alfvén, 0.1 for the
   slow and 0.0 for the entropy fields (eq. 2.95).
8. **Time step** (§2.3, p. 14). $\Delta t = \nu h/\max(\vert v_x\vert + c_f)$
   over the interfaces, $\nu < 1$; "Typically we use" $\nu = 0.8$, the
   value of every test.
9. **Rotational-discontinuity steepening** (§2.3, eqs. 2.96–2.98). For the
   two Alfvén fields a steepening term, weighted by the normalized jump of
   the wave strengths between neighbouring interfaces, is added to the
   limited correction. Rotational discontinuities are then resolved
   "within 3-5 cells, otherwise within 6-8 cells". Steepening the contact
   is possible, but "the price ... is additional numerical oscillations";
   no test uses it (§4).
10. **Nonlinear MHD Riemann solver** (§3, eqs. 3.1–3.19). Eight states
    separated by fast, rotational and slow waves on each side and a
    contact or tangential discontinuity. Jump conditions in Lagrangian
    mass coordinates after Dai & Woodward; the fast and slow Lagrangian
    shock speeds in a form "equivalent to those given by DW, but ...
    somewhat simpler" and "more robust ... particularly in switch-on or
    switch-off shocks". **Fast and slow rarefactions are integrated
    exactly** through ODEs in the transverse field (after Jeffrey 1966),
    the main change from Dai & Woodward's "rarefaction shocks". Newton–
    Raphson with a numerical Jacobian on four unknowns, started from the
    linear eigenvector decomposition (eq. 3.19), until the jumps of $v_x$,
    $v_y$, $v_z$ and $P^*$ at the contact vanish to $10^{-6}$ relative
    tolerance. Switch-on and switch-off waves need the smaller transverse
    field next to them set to about $10^{-3}$ of the larger. Against Dai &
    Woodward: exact agreement when all waves are discontinuities, small
    differences with rarefactions, attributed to their approximation.
11. **Resolution** (Abstract; §4). Strong fast, slow and magnetosonic
    shocks in **2–4 cells** (weak shocks need more); rotational
    discontinuities **3–5 cells** with steepening; contacts **3–5 cells
    with** and **6–8 without** steepening, which "generates spurious
    numerical oscillations in many cases"; tangential discontinuities
    **more than 10 cells**. All comparisons are visual; no error norms or
    convergence rates.
12. **Compound waves** (§5, Figs. 6a–6b, 8192 cells). An exact Riemann
    solver that allows rotational discontinuities resolves the Brio–Wu
    problem as a rotational discontinuity plus a slow shock, whereas the
    dissipative code produces a compound wave. Measured slow-compound jump:
    pre-shock $\rho = 0.647$, $p = 0.484$, $v_x = 0.963$, $B_y = 0.536$;
    post-shock $\rho = 0.827$, $p = 0.758$, $v_x = 0.751$, $B_y = -0.0738$;
    lab-frame shock speed 0.284; Alfvénic Mach number $1.03 \to 0.910$: a
    **"2-4" intermediate shock followed by a slow rarefaction**. Fast
    compound: pre-shock $\rho = 0.6820$, $p = 0.5290$, $v_x = 1.617$,
    $B_y = 0.0555$; post-shock $\rho = 0.7361$, $p = 0.6031$,
    $v_x = 1.498$, $B_y = -0.3442$; shock speed 0.935; Alfvénic Mach number
    $1.027 \to 0.989$: a **"1-3" intermediate shock preceded by a fast
    rarefaction**. The authors do "not ... add to the controversy
    surrounding the reality of intermediate shocks."

# Tests

steepening, **512 cells** (every caption); dots are the code, lines the §3
Riemann solver. States are printed as $(\rho, v_x, v_y, v_z, B_y, B_z, E)$
with $B_x$ apart; the seventh entry is the gas pressure $p$ (see Recorded
as printed). Output times and the Dai & Woodward (DW) table numbers are
from the captions. The preprint read has no figures and no tables, only
captions.

Findings (§4):

* **Set 1** (planar field, no rotation): strong fast shocks in 2–4 cells,
  weak slow shocks wider, contacts "typically spread over 6-8 or so
  cells"; no rotational discontinuity, as expected.
* **Set 2** (rotating field plane): two rotational discontinuities; "With
  proper stiffening, the rotational discontinuities spread over only 3-5
  cells".
* **Set 3** ($B_x = 0$): magnetosonic shocks in 2–4 cells; the tangential
  discontinuity "spreads typically more than 10 cells", which the
  steepener does not prevent.
* **Set 4** (switch-on and switch-off): 4a handled "without any trouble";
  in 4b, designed to give only a switch-off fast rarefaction and a contact
  (to better than 0.3 % in the residual tangential field), the code shows
  "small, yet noticeable" signatures of a right-moving slow and a
  left-moving hydrodynamic structure; 4c agrees well, the tangential field
  switched off to about 0.04 %; 4d has a single rotational discontinuity.
* **Set 5** (compound): code and Riemann solver disagree around the
  compound structure (the solver gives a rotational discontinuity with a
  slow shock or a fast rarefaction); elsewhere agreement is "acceptable
  for most purposes".
* Overall agreement is "satisfactory, and mostly excellent, except in the
  tests involving compound structures".
