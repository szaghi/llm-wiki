# Summary

The paper builds a Roe-type (linearized Riemann solver) upwind scheme for
the 1D ideal MHD equations, a $7\times7$ system with constant normal field
$B_x$ (§IV). For $\gamma = 2$ it finds an exact Roe matrix as the Jacobian
at a mean state with square-root-density averages; for other $\gamma$ it
uses the Jacobian at arithmetic averages of density, velocity, field and
total pressure, which gives up the flux-difference property. It renormalizes
the Jeffrey–Taniuti right eigenvectors so that the set stays complete where
the normal or the transverse field vanishes, and solves for the wave
strengths in two decoupled $3\times3$ systems. The second-order version is
run on a coplanar MHD Riemann problem with Sod's hydrodynamic data,
$B_x = 0.75$, transverse field $\pm1$ and $\gamma = 2$ — the
[Brio–Wu shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md) — against
Lax–Friedrichs, Lax–Wendroff with Lapidus viscosity and FCT (§V). The run
shows a slow compound wave, and §VI proves with the renormalized
eigenvectors that ideal MHD is **nonconvex**.

# Key claims

1. **System** (§II, eqs. 1–4, 16–22). Ideal MHD in conservation form,
   with total pressure $P^* = p + \tfrac12\vert\mathbf B\vert^2$ and
   $E = \tfrac12\rho\vert\mathbf v\vert^2 + p/(\gamma-1) + \tfrac12\vert\mathbf B\vert^2$,
   in units "such that factors of $4\pi$ and $c$ do not appear". Hyperbolic,
   not strictly hyperbolic, and nonconvex.
2. **Roe construction** (§III, eqs. 6–15). Conservative update with
   $\hat f_{i+1/2} = \tfrac12\left(f(u_i) + f(u_{i+1})\right) - \tfrac12\sum_p\vert\lambda^{(p)}_{i+1/2}\vert\,(\text{wave strength})_p\,(\text{right eigenvector})_p$
   (eq. 15); properties: (9A) the matrix maps the state jump to the flux
   jump exactly, (9B) real eigenvalues and complete eigenvectors, (9C)
   consistency with the Jacobian. (9A) makes stationary discontinuities
   exact steady solutions. First order, may admit entropy-violating shocks;
   time step $\Delta t \le h/\max\vert\lambda^{(p)}_{i+1/2}\vert$.
3. **Exact Roe matrix only for $\gamma = 2$** (§IV.1, eqs. 24–30). With
   square-root-density averages of velocity and of the enthalpy
   $(E + P^*)/\rho$, the geometric mean of the densities, and a weighted
   average of the transverse field, the Jacobian at the mean state satisfies
   (9A). For $\gamma \ne 2$ "we are not able to solve it analytically"; the
   Jacobian at arithmetic averages of density, velocity, field and **total
   pressure** satisfies (9B)–(9C) but not (9A). Averaging the energy instead
   "may lead to a negative averaged pressure which is not allowed".
4. **Eigenvalues** (§IV.2, eqs. 31–33). Ordered fast, Alfvén, slow,
   entropy, slow, Alfvén, fast, with $c_A^2 = B_x^2/\rho$ and the
   magnetosonic speeds the roots of the usual quadratic. At $B_x = 0$ the
   slow and Alfvén speeds vanish and $v_x$ has multiplicity 5; where the
   transverse field vanishes the fast and slow speeds are the larger and the
   smaller of $c_s$ and $c_A$.
5. **Eigenvector renormalization** (§IV.3, eqs. 34–46). The Jeffrey–Taniuti
   eigenvectors become singular at $B_x = 0$ (0/0) and where the transverse
   field vanishes (determinant proportional to the square of the difference
   of the squared fast and slow speeds). Factors built from the fast, slow
   and Alfvén speeds and from the direction cosines of the transverse field
   rescale the fast, slow and Alfvén vectors so that the result "is well
   defined everywhere", with limiting values $1/\sqrt2$ for the direction
   cosines and 1 for the speed factors at triple degeneracy. For continuity
   of the fast (if $c_s < c_A$) or slow (if $c_s > c_A$) eigenvectors as
   the transverse field vanishes they are multiplied by its sign:
   irrelevant for the Roe flux, "crucial" for classifying the fields (§VI).
   The usual set "is not complete" (§I, §VI).
6. **Wave strengths** (§IV.4, eqs. 47–53). The jumps decouple into a
   $3\times3$ system with right-hand side the geometric-mean density times
   the velocity jumps and a $3\times3$ system with right-hand side the jumps
   of the transverse field and of $p/(\gamma-1)$ minus the transverse
   magnetic energy; the entropy strength follows (eq. 53).
7. **Nonconvexity** (§VI, eqs. 54–55, Fig. 10). With the renormalized
   eigenvectors, the derivative of the eigenvalue along its eigenvector is
   proportional to the transverse field near its zero (coplanar case) for
   the fast wave if $c_s < c_A$ and the slow wave if $c_s > c_A$: these
   fields are neither genuinely nonlinear nor linearly degenerate, so "the
   MHD system is nonconvex". Jeffrey & Taniuti's theorem of genuine
   nonlinearity of the fast and slow fields is "not correct, since the set
   (34) is not complete". Consequence: **compound waves**, a shock with an
   attached rarefaction of the same family.
8. **Intermediate shock** (§VI end). The transverse field changes sign
   across the slow shock of the compound wave, so it is an intermediate
   shock; "all numerical schemes tested in Section 5 admit" it, and
   resistive-MHD simulations (Wu 1987) show some intermediate shocks "can
   exist".
9. **Cost** (§VII). The upwind scheme needs "about 10 times more floating
   point operations per grid point per time step than the Lax–Wendroff
   scheme"; with 200 points (4 times fewer steps) it is "more efficient than
   the Lax–Wendroff scheme with 800 grid points".

# Tests

1D, uniform grid (§V, §VI).

1. **Coplanar MHD Riemann problem** (§V, Figs. 2–6), the
   [Brio–Wu shock tube](/benchmarks/brio-wu-shock-tube.md). Left
   $\rho = 1$, $\mathbf v = 0$, $p = 1$, $B_y = 1$; right $\rho = 0.125$,
   $\mathbf v = 0$, $p = 0.1$, $B_y = -1$; $B_x = 0.75$, $\gamma = 2$
   ($v_z$, $B_z$ not printed: coplanar, hence zero). Hydrodynamic data
   "identical to those in Sod's shock tube problem". 800 points, $h = 1$,
   $\Delta t = 0.2$ (Courant number about 0.8), output after 400 steps,
   discontinuity in the middle: $t = 80$ on a domain of length 800,
   i.e. $t = 0.1$ on a unit domain (derived at reading; no dimensionless
   time is printed). Waves: left fast rarefaction and slow compound wave;
   contact, slow shock and fast rarefaction to the right; checked with
   Riemann invariants and jump conditions.
   * Second-order Roe-type scheme (Harten's approach, artificial
     compression on the linearly degenerate field). The arithmetic-average
     variant agrees "up to 4 significant digits" in constant states and "up
     to 2" in transitions.
   * Lax–Wendroff with Lapidus viscosity: undershoot at the rarefaction tail,
     shocks over 4–5 points, oscillations around the compound wave, contact
     over 10–15 points.
   * Lax–Friedrichs: plateaus not formed, slow shock over about 20 points;
     with 20,000 points and 10,000 steps it coincides with the upwind result
     except at the contact.
   * FCT: 800 steps of $\Delta t = 0.1$ (Courant number about 0.4);
     undershoots and plateau oscillations; at 0.8 the oscillations increase
     "drastically".
2. **Variant with right $B_y = -0.4$** (p. 414). Upwind and Lax–Friedrichs
   give "an almost stationary slow intermediate shock"; Lax–Wendroff could
   not be made stable "with any value of artificial viscosity". No figure.
3. **High-Mach variant** (p. 416, Fig. 7). $B_x = 0$ and, as printed,
   left density 1000; a hydrodynamic Riemann problem in total pressure, with
   a right-moving shock of Mach 15.5. Artificial compression does not stop
   the tangential discontinuity spreading and causes a density undershoot.
4. **Compound-wave verification** (§VI, Figs. 8–10). From the numerical
   left state of the slow shock ($\rho = 0.6763$, $v_x = 0.6366$,
   $v_y = -0.2333$, $B_y = 0.5849$, $p = 0.4574$), the intersection of the
   shock-speed and slow-characteristic curves gives the rarefaction tail
   analytically as $\rho = 0.6965$, $v_x = 0.5987$, $v_y = -1.583$,
   $B_y = -0.5341$, $p = 0.5157$; the second-order upwind run gives
   0.6963, 0.5997, −1.578, −0.5341, 0.5133. "The agreement is excellent."
