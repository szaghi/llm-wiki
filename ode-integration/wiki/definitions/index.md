# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Asymptotic-preserving scheme](/definitions/asymptotic-preserving-scheme.md) — A discretization of a problem with small scale parameter ε is asymptotic-preserving when its ε→0 limit at fixed step and mesh is a consistent, stable scheme for the limit model; the page covers the definition, the uniform-accuracy argument, the time-integration mechanisms and the step-size restrictions they remove.
* [B-series, composition and substitution laws](/definitions/b-series.md) — A B-series is a formal expansion over rooted trees of elementary differentials weighted by a coefficient map; Runge–Kutta steps and exact flows are B-series, composition of methods is the Butcher product over ordered subtrees, and substitution of a modified vector field is the law over edge partitions that underlies backward error analysis.
* [Differential-algebraic equations and their Runge–Kutta treatment](/definitions/differential-algebraic-equation.md) — A differential-algebraic equation couples differential equations with algebraic constraints, My'=f(t,y) with singular M or semi-explicit form; its index governs which Runge–Kutta, Rosenbrock and BDF methods converge at full order, how stage systems are solved, and which software accepts it.
* [Linear stability of multirate methods and joint stability regions](/definitions/multirate-linear-stability.md) — Defines the scalar and 2D (Gear) test problems for multirate and GARK methods, their stability functions and scalar, real-2D and complex-2D A-, L-, A(α)- and L(α)-stability, the implication hierarchy between them, and the joint stability region used for IMEX multirate methods.
* [Order reduction](/definitions/order-reduction.md) — Order reduction is the drop of the observed convergence rate of a time integrator below its classical order $p$ on stiff problems, method-of-lines PDEs with boundary data, DAEs or split operators; this page collects its mechanisms, the reduced rates the sources predict and observe, and the remedies.
* [Runge–Kutta method](/definitions/runge-kutta-method.md) — The s-stage one-step method defined by a Butcher tableau (A, b, c): its stage-value and stage-derivative forms, the row-sum convention, the explicit, diagonally implicit and fully implicit classes and their solve costs, reducibility, and the additive, multiderivative and mass-matrix generalizations used by the sources.
* [Stability function, stability polynomial and stability region](/definitions/stability-function.md) — The stability function R(z) is the factor by which a one-step method multiplies the solution of y' = λy per step; it is rational for implicit methods and a polynomial P(z) for explicit ones, its order and degree structure constrain the method, and its region of absolute stability decides the linearly stable step sizes for method-of-lines systems.
* [Stage order](/definitions/stage-order.md) — Stage order q is the largest integer for which the quadrature conditions B(q) and the stage conditions C(q) hold, i.e. the stage-order residuals τ^(k) vanish for k ≤ q; it bounds the observed order on stiff problems, is at most 2 for DIRKs and 1 for explicit methods, and equals s for Gauss and Radau IIA.
* [Weak stage order](/definitions/weak-stage-order.md) — The weak stage order $q_{\mathrm w}$ of a Runge–Kutta method is the largest $q$ with $b^\top A^j\tau^{(k)}=0$ for all $j<s$ and $k\le q$: the weights are orthogonal to the $A$-invariant space of the stage-order residuals. It removes order reduction on linear problems and, unlike stage order, is reachable by DIRK and explicit methods.
