# Properties

Constraints and invariants a method can hold.

* [A-, L-, A(α)- and algebraic stability](/properties/a-stability.md) — Linear stability classes of one-step methods: A-stability (the stability region contains the left half-plane), L-stability (A-stability plus damping at infinity), A(α)- and L(α)-stability on a sector, and the nonlinear notions of B- and algebraic stability; with the facts the sources state about collocation, DIRK, Rosenbrock and multirate methods.
* [Positivity preservation in time integration](/properties/positivity-preservation.md) — A time integrator is positivity preserving if nonnegative data give nonnegative numerical solutions; within SSP theory it holds under $h\le\mathcal C h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, unconditional positivity is limited to first order for Runge–Kutta and multistep methods, and multiderivative, splitting and exponential reformulations obtain higher order, including uniformly in a stiffness parameter.
* [Stiff accuracy](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md) — A Runge–Kutta method is stiffly accurate when the last row of A equals b, so the step result is the last stage; with invertible A this gives R(∞) = 0 and turns A-stability into L-stability, tightens weak-stage-order barriers, and is imposed on DIRK, Rosenbrock, IMEX and multirate constructions, though it does not by itself prevent order reduction.
* [Strong stability (monotonicity) of Runge–Kutta methods for linear seminegative problems](/properties/strong-stability.md) — A one-step method is strongly stable on $y'=Ly$ with $\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0$ if $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert\le\lVert y_n\rVert$; for non-normal $L$ eigenvalue analysis cannot decide it, energy-method criteria can, explicit Taylor-type methods fail for $p\equiv1,2\pmod4$, and the property differs from SSP.
* [Symplecticity and quadratic-invariant preservation](/properties/symplecticity.md) — A method is symplectic when its one-step map is a symplectic transformation for every Hamiltonian system; splittings into exactly solvable Hamiltonian parts and Gauss–Legendre methods have it, it yields near-conservation of energy over exponentially long times through a modified Hamiltonian, and it is linked to the exact preservation of quadratic and linear invariants.
