# Weak-stage-order barriers

[Weak stage order](/definitions/weak-stage-order.md) $q_{\mathrm w}\ge q$ is
equivalent to the orthogonality of two invariant subspaces of an $s$-stage
[Runge–Kutta method](/definitions/runge-kutta-method.md) $(A,b,c)$, $c=A\mathbf 1$:
$\mathcal K_q$ is the smallest $A$-invariant subspace containing the
stage-order residuals $\tau^{(1)},\dots,\tau^{(q)}$ and $\mathcal Y$ the smallest
$A^\top$-invariant subspace containing $b$ (registry symbols). Then

$$q_{\mathrm w}\ge q\iff\mathcal Y\perp\mathcal K_q\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\dim\mathcal Y+\dim\mathcal K_q\le s$$

([Biswas et al. 2024](/sources/biswas-2024.md), Lemma 3.4, Eq. (14)). Order forces
$\dim\mathcal Y$ up, weak stage order forces $\dim\mathcal K_q$ up, and the stage
count bounds their sum: every barrier below is an instance of this squeeze. $n_c$ is
the number of distinct abscissae $c_i$ (registry). Page-local symbols:
$\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}=1$ if the method is [stiffly accurate](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md)
with invertible $A$ and $0$ otherwise; $\delta_{\mathrm{GE}}=1$ for a GEDIRK and $0$
otherwise (a GEDIRK is a DIRK whose first zero abscissa $c_j=0$ has $a_{jj}=0$;
explicit methods are GEDIRKs; Biswas et al. 2024, Def. 2.1).

## Statements for implicit methods

**Theorem (general Runge–Kutta; Biswas et al. 2024, Thm. 3.1).** Let the method have
$s$ stages, $n_c$ distinct abscissae, order $p$ and weak stage order $q_{\mathrm w}$.

1. If all $c_j\neq0$, then $q_{\mathrm w}\le2n_c-1$.
2. If some $c_j=0$, then either $q_{\mathrm w}\le2n_c-2$, or $q_{\mathrm w}=\infty$
   and $p=1$.
3. If $q_{\mathrm w}\le2n_c-1$, then
   $$q_{\mathrm w}+\left\lfloor\frac{p+1+\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}}{2}\right\rfloor\le s+n_c.$$

**Theorem (DIRK; Biswas et al. 2024, Thm. 3.2).** For a
[diagonally implicit method](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) with
$q_{\mathrm w}\le2n_c-1$,

$$\left\lfloor\frac{q_{\mathrm w}+\delta_{\mathrm{GE}}}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta_{\mathrm{GE}}+p\le s+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}.$$

At $q_{\mathrm w}=0$ this is the classical DIRK barrier $p\le s+1$. It covers SDIRK
methods and, as GEDIRKs, explicit methods (Biswas et al. 2024, §3, §8).

Both theorems hold when $p$ is only the order of the stability function,
$R(z)=e^z+\mathcal O(z^{p+1})$ (Remark 3.6). For fixed $s$, lowering $p$ by one
raises the admissible $q_{\mathrm w}$ by two (Remark 3.3); a DIRK with
$p=s-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ is limited to $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$ and a GEDIRK to
$q_{\mathrm w}\le4$ (see Qualifications for the printed form of this remark).

## How the bounds are obtained

**Lower bound on $\dim\mathcal Y$ from order.** The
[stability function](/definitions/stability-function.md) $R=N/D$ has
$\deg N,\deg D\le\dim\mathcal Y$ (Biswas et al. 2024, Thm. 3.8). With the Padé
barrier $p\le\deg N+\deg D$ and the Nørsett–Wolfbrandt barrier $p\le\deg N+1$ for
real poles (Thm. 3.7, recalled from Hairer–Wanner), this gives
$\lfloor(p+1+\delta_{\mathrm{SA}})/2\rfloor\le\dim\mathcal Y$ in general and
$p\le\dim\mathcal Y+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ for DIRK methods (Thm. 3.9).

**Lower bound on $\dim\mathcal K_m$ from the abscissae.** A polynomial construction
with the minimal polynomial of $\mathrm{diag}(c)$ gives
$\dim\mathcal K_m\ge\max\{m-n_c,0\}$ for $m\le2n_c-1$; for $m\ge2n_c$ (all
$c_j\neq0$) $\mathbf 1\in\mathcal K_m$, and for $m\ge2n_c-1$ (some $c_j=0$)
$c\in\mathcal K_m$ (Thm. 3.11). Because $b^\top\mathbf 1=1$ and $b^\top c=1/2$,
either membership contradicts $b\perp\mathcal K_m$, which is parts 1–2 of the
general theorem. For DIRK methods a truncation argument on the leading blocks of
$A$ sharpens this to
$\dim\mathcal K_m\ge\min\{\lfloor(m+\delta_{\mathrm{GE}})/2\rfloor,\,n_c-\delta_{\mathrm{GE}}\}$
(Lemma 3.13).

**Minimal polynomials.** Page-locally, let $\mu_{\mathcal Y}$ and $\mu_{\mathcal K}$
be the monic polynomials of least degree with $b^\top\mu_{\mathcal Y}(A)=0$ and
$\mu_{\mathcal K}(A)\tau^{(k)}=0$, $2\le k\le q_{\mathrm w}$. Then
$\deg\mu_{\mathcal Y}=\dim\mathcal Y$, $\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}\le\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}$,
and $\mu_{\mathcal Y}\mu_{\mathcal K}$ divides the characteristic polynomial of
$A$, so the eigenvalues of $A$ split between the two (Lemma 4.4, Remark 4.6). For
an SDIRK with diagonal entry $\gamma$ both are powers of $(x-\gamma)$ (Remark 4.7).
Combining, $\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}\le\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}\le s-\lfloor(p+1+\delta_{\mathrm{SA}})/2\rfloor$
for general methods and $\le s-p+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ for DIRK methods
(Cor. 4.5, corrected; see Qualifications).

**Necessary conditions on the diagonal of a DIRK.** For an S-irreducible
non-GEDIRK DIRK whose leading $3\times3$ block is invertible: $q_{\mathrm w}>1$
forces $a_{11}$ to be a root of $\mu_{\mathcal K}$; $q_{\mathrm w}>3$ forces $a_{11}$
and $a_{22}$ to be roots; $q_{\mathrm w}>5$ forces the minimal polynomial of the
leading $3\times3$ block to divide $\mu_{\mathcal K}$ (Biswas et al. 2024, Lemma 6.5).
Trailing entries such as $a_{ss}$ are roots of $\mu_{\mathcal Y}$ (Remark 6.6). A DIRK
with $c_1=c_2$ is S-reducible (Remark 6.4).

[Biswas et al. (2023)](/sources/biswas-2023.md), §4, quote from their then
unpublished companion paper three consequences used to design DIRK schemes:
$\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}\le1$ with invertible $A$ forces $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$, which is
the barrier of the eigenvector criterion (Thm. 4.1); the stage bound
$s-p+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}\ge\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}\ge\lfloor q_{\mathrm w}/2\rfloor$
(Thm. 4.2); and, for an irreducible DIRK with invertible $A$,
$q_{\mathrm w}>3$ and $\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}=2$, $\mu_{\mathcal K}(x)=(x-a_{11})(x-a_{22})$
(Thm. 4.3). These agree with Lemma 6.5 and the corrected Cor. 4.5 of Biswas et al.
(2024).

## Sharpness

- **$(s,p,q_{\mathrm w})=(2,2,3)$ DIRK.** Exactly two schemes,
  $$A=\begin{pmatrix}1\mp\frac{\sqrt2}{2}&0\\\frac12\pm\frac{\sqrt2}{2}&\frac12\end{pmatrix},\quad b=\begin{pmatrix}\frac12\pm\frac{\sqrt2}{4}\\\frac12\mp\frac{\sqrt2}{4}\end{pmatrix},\quad R(z)=\frac{1+z/2}{1-z/2}$$
  (Biswas et al. 2024, Ex. 7.1). Their stability function is that of the
  trapezoidal rule, so $|R(\infty)|=1$ and they are not L-stable *(compiler check,
  not in the sources)*.
- **$(3,3,3)$ DIRK.** A one-parameter family, sharp for the DIRK theorem; no member
  has $p=4$ or $q_{\mathrm w}=4$ (Ex. 7.2).
- **Stiffly accurate $(4,3,3)$ DIRKs** of Ketcheson et al. (2020) attain the bound
  (Ex. 7.3): $\lfloor3/2\rfloor+3=4=s+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ in the DIRK theorem
  *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
- **Gauss methods** attain the general theorem sharply (Ex. 7.4): with $p=2s$ and
  $n_c=s$, part 3 reads $q_{\mathrm w}+s\le2s$, so $q_{\mathrm w}=s$ *(compiler
  check, not in the sources)*, consistent with their stage order $s$.

## Explicit methods

For explicit methods nilpotency of $A$ sharpens the bounds
([Biswas et al. 2025](/sources/biswas-2025.md), Remark 4, extending Biswas et al.
2024, Thm. 3.11 and Lemma 3.13).

- **Lemma 2.1.** $p\le\deg R\le\dim\mathcal Y\le s-\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}$.
  Most practical explicit methods have $\deg R=s$ and hence $q_{\mathrm w}=1$; any
  explicit method with $p>1$ and $q_{\mathrm w}>1$ has $\deg R<s$, which requires a
  zero entry on the first subdiagonal of $A$.
- **Lemma 2.2.** If $p>1$ then $q_{\mathrm w}<n_c$; if $q_{\mathrm w}<n_c$ then
  $\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}\ge q_{\mathrm w}-1$.
- **Theorem 2.3 (stage bound).** An explicit method with $p\ge2$ satisfies
  $$p+q_{\mathrm w}\le s+1,$$
  with equality only if $\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}=q_{\mathrm w}-1$ and
  $\dim\mathcal Y=p$. It still holds when $p$ is only the order on linear problems
  (Remark 1).
- **Theorem 2.6.** An explicit method with $p\ge2$ has $q_{\mathrm w}=s+1-p$ iff it
  satisfies the Sylvester-equation characterization of Thm. 2.4; such methods are
  irreducible and $R(z)=\sum_{j=0}^pz^j/j!$.
- **Sharpness.** Table 3.1 exhibits methods with $s=p+q_{\mathrm w}-1$ for all
  $2\le p\le5$, $2\le q_{\mathrm w}\le5$; Theorem 3.1 builds $(p^2,p,p)$ methods for
  every $p\ge2$, so $q_{\mathrm w}$ is unbounded. See
  [explicit methods with high weak stage order](/methods/explicit-runge-kutta-high-weak-stage-order.md).
- **SSP incompatibility (Thm. 2.7, Cor. 2.8).** An explicit method with
  $A\ge0$, $b\ge0$ has $q_{\mathrm w}=1$, or $p\le1$ and $q_{\mathrm w}=\infty$.
  Hence every explicit method with $p\ge2$ and $q_{\mathrm w}\ge2$ has
  [SSP coefficient](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) zero; its
  linear SSP coefficient, which depends only on $R$, can still be positive.

For comparison, the DIRK theorem applied to an explicit method
($\delta_{\mathrm{GE}}=1$, $\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}=0$) gives
$\lfloor(q_{\mathrm w}+1)/2\rfloor+p\le s+2$, which is weaker than $p+q_{\mathrm w}\le s+1$
for $q_{\mathrm w}\ge2$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.

## Qualifications

- **Printed slips in Biswas et al. (2024)** (arXiv v2). Remark 3.3 prints "When
  $p=s+1-\sigma$" before stating the limits $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$ (DIRK) and $\le4$
  (GEDIRK); substituting $p=s+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ into the DIRK theorem gives
  $q_{\mathrm w}\le1$ and $\le2$, while $p=s-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$ gives the stated
  3 and 4, consistent with Example 7.2 being sharp. Corollary 4.5 prints the DIRK
  bound as $s-p-1+\sigma$; the derivation, and Biswas et al. (2023, Thm. 4.2), give
  $s-p+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$, and the printed form would be negative for the
  examples with $\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}=1$. The proof of Thm. 3.1 is headed "Theorem 3.2",
  and Example 7.3 cites "Theorem 3.1" where the DIRK theorem is the sharp one.
- **Stage lower bounds used in design.** Biswas et al. (2023, §5.1) read Thm. 4.2
  as "a 4th order DIRK with WSO 4 requires at least five stages" and "5th order with
  WSO 4 or 5 at least six"; those counts use $\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}=0$. With the
  stiff accuracy those authors impose, the same inequality gives 6 and 7 stages
  *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. Their searches found schemes with 7, 12 and 12 stages and
  failed to find fewer under their constraints; the bounds "may not be sharp".
- Biswas et al. (2024, §1.2) assert that the eigenvector criterion limits
  invertible-$A$ DIRK schemes to $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$ "as shown below"; the
  statement follows from Lemma 6.5(b) for S-irreducible schemes with invertible
  leading block, not from a separate theorem.
