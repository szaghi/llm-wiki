# Sources

One page per ingested source, with verified metadata, a notation map, and an
audit list of pages touched.

* [Abu-Labdeh et al. (2023) — Monolithic multigrid for implicit Runge–Kutta discretizations of incompressible fluid flow](/sources/abulabdeh-2023.md) — Monolithic geometric multigrid with an additive Vanka relaxation whose patches hold all Runge–Kutta stages, inside Newton–FGMRES, solves Gauss, Radau IIA and Lobatto IIIC stage systems for Stokes, Navier–Stokes and MHD index-2 DAEs with bounded iteration counts to 640 cores.
* [Akrivis et al. (2021) — The Energy Technique for the Six-Step BDF Method](/sources/akrivis-2021.md) — Extends the Nevanlinna–Odeh multiplier energy technique to six-step BDF, for which no classical multiplier exists, by relaxing the multiplier condition to trigonometric positivity and bounding the summed dissipation with the Grenander–Szegő theorem; proves BDF6 energy stability for self-adjoint parabolic problems.
* [Akrivis et al. (2024) — The variable two-step BDF method for parabolic equations](/sources/akrivis-2024.md) — Proves energy stability of variable-step BDF2 for self-adjoint parabolic problems whenever adjacent step ratios stay below about 1.9398, improving the earlier bounds 1.8685 and 1.9104, by testing with two multipliers and bounding the summed elliptic terms through a Grenander–Szegő-type lemma for non-Toeplitz matrices.
* [Al Jahdali et al. (2022) — On the performance of relaxation and adaptive explicit Runge–Kutta schemes for high-order compressible flow simulations](/sources/aljahdali-2022.md) — Short note measuring the cost of global and local relaxation with nine explicit Runge–Kutta schemes under CFL-based and error-based step-size control on industrial compressible-flow DG simulations; the secant method is the only robust root finder, and error-controlled high-order relaxation schemes cost about as much as low-order ones.
* [Bassenne et al. (2021) — Time-Accurate and highly-Stable Explicit operators for stiff differential equations](/sources/bassenne-2021.md) — Introduces TASE operators, rational preconditioners of the stiff linear(ized) operator built by Richardson extrapolation of (I - alpha h L)^(-1), which make an unchanged explicit Runge–Kutta method unconditionally or nearly unconditionally stable at the cost of p linear solves per evaluation, with stability theory and stiff PDE tests.
* [Bazavov (2026) — 2N-storage Runge-Kutta methods: Order conditions, general properties and some analytic solutions](/sources/bazavov-2026.md) — Characterizes Williamson 2N-storage explicit Runge–Kutta methods exactly by quadratic constraints on the Butcher coefficients, gives closed maps between the Butcher and 2N coefficients, corrects Williamson's 1980 special-case conversion formula, and derives the general third-order four- and five-stage families with new rational schemes.
* [Belardo et al. (2026) — On the construction of nested explicit Runge–Kutta methods via null rules](/sources/belardo-2026.md) — Short note showing that all embedded weight vectors of order at least p that share the stages of an explicit Runge–Kutta method form an affine family along the null space of the order-condition matrix, and using it to build alternative embeddings for midpoint, RK4, DOPRI5(4), a weak-stage-order scheme and Bogacki–Shampine 3(2).
* [Bencomo & Chan (2023) — Discrete Adjoint Computations for Relaxation Runge–Kutta Methods](/sources/bencomo-2023.md) — Derives the discrete tangent-linear and adjoint recurrences of relaxation Runge–Kutta methods (IDT and RRK) by implicit differentiation of the relaxation root, proves exact time-symmetry of the adjoint on skew-symmetric linear problems, and shows that only the full linearization gives consistent, accurate gradients.
* [Birke et al. (2024) — A Well-Balanced Semi-implicit IMEX Finite Volume Scheme for Ideal Magnetohydrodynamics at All Mach Numbers](/sources/birke-2024.md) — Combines a convective/pressure flux split, a two-stage semi-implicit IMEX Runge–Kutta method whose implicit stage is one linear pressure equation, and deviation well-balancing into a second-order finite-volume scheme for ideal MHD with gravity whose step is Mach-independent.
* [Biswas & Ketcheson (2023) — Multiple-Relaxation Runge Kutta Methods for Conservative Dynamical Systems](/sources/biswas-2023-b.md) — Generalizes relaxation to several invariants: multiple-relaxation Runge–Kutta methods combine embedded weight vectors sharing one stage matrix, prove existence of the relaxation parameters and order recovery at a shifted time, and give linear error growth on rigid-body, Lotka–Volterra, Kepler and IMEX KdV tests.
* [Biswas et al. (2023) — Design of DIRK schemes with high weak stage order](/sources/biswas-2023.md) — Builds the first diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta schemes with weak stage order above 3 — DIRK-(7,4,4), DIRK-(12,5,4), DIRK-(12,5,5), stiffly accurate and L-stable — from a reduced weak-stage-order system and constrained polynomial optimization, and shows that they remove order reduction on linear stiff ODE and PDE tests but not on nonlinear ones.
* [Biswas et al. (2024) — Algebraic Structure of the Weak Stage Order Conditions for Runge–Kutta Methods](/sources/biswas-2024.md) — Gives the first algebraic theory of weak stage order: it recasts the conditions as orthogonality of two invariant subspaces, proves barriers linking stages, order, weak stage order and distinct abscissae for general and diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta methods, and derives minimal-polynomial conditions that guide the construction of high-weak-stage-order DIRKs.
* [Biswas et al. (2025) — Explicit Runge–Kutta Methods that Alleviate Order Reduction](/sources/biswas-2025.md) — Extends weak stage order to explicit Runge–Kutta methods: proves the stage bound p+q_w ≤ s+1, characterizes stage-optimal schemes by Sylvester equations, shows that high weak stage order is incompatible with nonzero SSP coefficient, and gives optimized schemes up to (9,5,5) that remove order reduction on linear hyperbolic problems with time-dependent boundary data.
* [Blanes et al. (2024) — Splitting methods for differential equations](/sources/blanes-2024.md) — Acta Numerica survey of splitting and composition methods: Lie–Trotter and Strang, four characterisations of the order conditions, the negative-coefficient barrier, RKN and near-integrable methods, stability, backward error analysis, processing, PDE and complex-coefficient splittings, and a catalogue of recommended methods.
* [Bleecke & Ranocha (2026) — Relaxation Runge-Kutta Methods with First-Same-as-Last Structure](/sources/bleecke-2026.md) — Technical note removing the extra right-hand-side evaluation per step that relaxation costs with FSAL embedded pairs: FSAL-R interpolates the relaxed first stage, R-FSAL relaxes before the error estimate; both keep the local orders and match the naive scheme's accuracy at baseline cost on the BBM equation.
* [Boscheri & Pareschi (2021) — High order pressure-based semi-implicit IMEX schemes for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations at all Mach numbers](/sources/boscheri-2021.md) — Builds a third-order pressure-based semi-implicit IMEX finite-volume solver for compressible Navier–Stokes at all Mach numbers, whose stages need one linear pressure solve, with a formal low-Mach asymptotic-preserving proof of the first-order scheme and Mach-uniform convergence.
* [Boscheri & Thomann (2024) — A Structure-Preserving Semi-implicit IMEX Finite Volume Scheme for Ideal Magnetohydrodynamics at all Mach and Alfvén Numbers](/sources/boscheri-2024.md) — Builds a second-order semi-implicit IMEX finite-volume scheme for ideal MHD whose step is limited only by the material speed at any Mach and Alfvén number, using a three-way flux split, two decoupled linear implicit solves per stage and an exactly divergence-free vector potential.
* [Chinomona & Reynolds (2021) — Implicit-Explicit Multirate Infinitesimal GARK Methods](/sources/chinomona-2021.md) — Introduces IMEX-MRI-GARK methods, multirate infinitesimal schemes with an implicit-explicit slow scale, proves they are GARK methods, derives internal-consistency and third- and fourth-order conditions and a joint stability region, and constructs methods of orders 3 and 4.
* [Conte et al. (2024) — Stability Theory of TASE-Runge-Kutta Methods with Inexact Jacobian](/sources/conte-2024.md) — Linear stability theory of TASE-Runge–Kutta methods when the matrix inside the TASE operator is an approximation of the Jacobian: stability diagrams in the generalized-eigenvalue plane, conditions for conditional and unconditional stability through generalized eigenvalues and weighted fields of values, and efficiency against Rosenbrock methods.
* [Croci & Rosilho de Souza (2022) — Mixed-precision explicit stabilized Runge–Kutta methods for single- and multi-scale differential equations](/sources/croci-2022.md) — Defines order-preserving mixed-precision Runge–Kutta schemes and builds mixed-precision Runge–Kutta–Chebyshev and multirate RKC methods that evaluate only one or two right-hand sides per step in high precision, with order, error-propagation and rounding-error results and bfloat16/double experiments on stiff parabolic problems.
* [Dawes (2021) — Some properties of the Runge-Kutta-Legendre super-time-stepping explicit methods](/sources/dawes-2021.md) — Reinterprets the Runge–Kutta–Legendre super-time-stepping recurrence as forward-Euler steps plus linear extrapolation, gives a simplified algorithm, and shows that with Dirichlet boundaries the RKL schemes lose monotonicity and positivity near the boundary at diffusion numbers far below the forward-Euler limit, with mesh-coarsening remedies and heat-equation tests.
* [de León et al. (2025) — Pseudo-energy-preserving explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/sources/deleon-2025.md) — Defines the pseudo-energy-preserving order of a Runge–Kutta method, derives its conditions from the energy-preserving B-series characterization through order 6, and finds explicit methods whose energy error on canonical Hamiltonian systems is of higher order than the solution error, with better long-time behaviour than classical methods.
* [Doehring et al. (2024) — Many-Stage Optimal Stabilized Runge–Kutta Methods for Hyperbolic Partial Differential Equations](/sources/doehring-2024.md) — Parametrizes explicit stability polynomials by the roots of (P−1)/z, which lie on the stability boundary, and optimizes them from an equal-arc-length guess on the spectrum's convex hull, reaching degree 128; builds 2N-storage many-stage methods and analyses their internal stability and missing SSP property.
* [Doehring et al. (2025) — Fourth-Order Paired-Explicit Runge-Kutta Methods](/sources/doehring-2025.md) — Extends paired explicit Runge–Kutta (P-ERK) families to fourth order by sharing the last three subdiagonal entries across members so that the partitioned coupling condition holds by construction, optimizes the constrained stability polynomials with an arbitrary-precision SOCP, and cuts runtime by 1.35–3.86 against optimized standalone methods in Trixi.jl.
* [Faleichik & Moisa (2026) — Explicit Runge–Kutta–Chebyshev methods of second order with monotonic stability polynomial](/sources/faleichik-2026.md) — Constructs second-order Runge–Kutta–Chebyshev methods whose stability polynomial is positive and monotonically non-decreasing on the longest possible negative real interval, realized by a three-term recurrence with internally stable stages, trading a shorter stability interval for more convex regions and much smaller error constants.
* [Farrell et al. (2021) — Irksome: Automating Runge–Kutta Time-stepping for Finite Element Methods](/sources/farrell-2021.md) — Presents Irksome, a library that turns a UFL semidiscrete variational form and a Butcher tableau into the coupled Runge–Kutta stage equations for Firedrake, solved with PETSc's composable preconditioners, and shows order reduction, stage-solver cost, energy and invariant behaviour of Gauss–Legendre, Radau IIA, Lobatto IIIC and DIRK methods on finite element tests.
* [Fekete et al. (2022) — Embedded pairs for optimal explicit strong stability preserving Runge–Kutta methods](/sources/fekete-2022.md) — Constructs embedded lower-order weights for optimal explicit SSP Runge–Kutta methods of orders 2 to 4, keeping the SSP coefficient where possible, and tests them with I, PI, PID and Gustafsson step-size controllers against classical embedded pairs.
* [Gaburro et al. (2023) — High order entropy preserving ADER-DG schemes](/sources/gaburro-2023.md) — Builds a fully discrete entropy-conservative or entropy-dissipative ADER-DG scheme on unstructured triangles by combining Abgrall's cell-wise entropy-correction term in space with relaxation of the ADER predictor–corrector step in time, and shows design order and total entropy matched to machine precision on advection, shallow-water and Euler tests.
* [García-Archilla & Novo (2023) — Robust error bounds for the Navier–Stokes equations using implicit-explicit second-order BDF method with variable steps](/sources/garciaarchilla-2023.md) — Proves viscosity-independent second-order error bounds for fixed- and variable-step BDF2 with grad-div stabilized mixed finite elements for Navier–Stokes, with explicit convection (needing a time step bounded by the squared mesh width) or linearly implicit convection (no such restriction), and confirms the restriction in adaptive runs.
* [Gardner et al. (2022) — Enabling New Flexibility in the SUNDIALS Suite of Nonlinear and Differential/Algebraic Equation Solvers](/sources/gardner-2022.md) — Software-design report on the object-oriented redesign of SUNDIALS: generic vector, matrix, linear-solver and nonlinear-solver classes, fused vector operations, the MPIManyVector and Fortran 2003 interfaces, with demonstrations showing no performance loss and 4x–8x speed-up from a task-local Newton solver.
* [Glandon et al. (2022) — Linearly Implicit Multistep Methods for Time Integration](/sources/glandon-2022.md) — Introduces linearly implicit multistep methods (Limm, and the W-variant Limm-w), which need one linear solve per step, derives their variable-step order conditions by Taylor and B-series expansion, proves stiff convergence, designs methods of orders 1–5 with larger stability angles than BDF and compares them with BDF in a variable-step code.
* [González-Tabernero et al. (2024) — Boundary Treatment for High-Order IMEX Runge–Kutta Local Discontinuous Galerkin Schemes for Multidimensional Nonlinear Parabolic PDEs](/sources/gonzaleztabernero-2024.md) — Computes the boundary values of internal IMEX Runge–Kutta stages from the stage equations, the PDE and Lax–Wendroff conversion of time into space derivatives, for any IMEX tableau; this removes the order reduction from time-dependent Dirichlet data in LDG convection–diffusion–reaction tests at orders 3 and 4.
* [Gottlieb et al. (2022) — High Order Strong Stability Preserving MultiDerivative Implicit and IMEX Runge–Kutta Methods with Asymptotic Preserving Properties](/sources/gottlieb-2022.md) — Introduces the backward derivative condition and builds two-derivative implicit Runge–Kutta methods of orders 2–4 that are SSP for any step size, plus SSP IMEX multiderivative methods of orders 2 and 3 that are positivity- and asymptotic-preserving for stiff kinetic relaxation systems.
* [Han Veiga et al. (2021) — DeC and ADER: Similarities, Differences and a Unified Framework](/sources/hanveiga-2021.md) — Shows that modern ADER and deferred correction are the same iterative collocation scheme with different test functions, proves order min(M+1, K) for ADER, compares the stiff variants IMDeC and IMADER, and computes identical stability regions.
* [Hedayati Nasab & Vermeire (2022) — Third-order Paired Explicit Runge-Kutta schemes for stiff systems of equations](/sources/hedayatinasab-2022.md) — Extends paired explicit Runge–Kutta (P-ERK) families to third order with a three-register tableau of shared abscissae and weights, optimizes the members' stability polynomials for flux-reconstruction DG spectra, and reports wall-clock speedups of 4.06–4.51 over RK3,3 on compressible Navier–Stokes problems.
* [Hedayati Nasab et al. (2021) — Optimal Runge-Kutta Stability Polynomials for Multidimensional High-Order Methods](/sources/hedayatinasab-2021.md) — Computes optimal explicit Runge–Kutta stability polynomials from multidimensional von Neumann eigenspectra of flux-reconstruction DG on six element types, finds gains over 1D-optimized polynomials only on simplex and prism elements, and reports per-stage speedups up to 1.97 over RK4.
* [Hu & Shu (2025) — Uniform accuracy of implicit-explicit Runge-Kutta (IMEX-RK) schemes for hyperbolic systems with relaxation](/sources/hu-2025.md) — Proves, by a multiplier-matrix energy method, that ARS(2,2,2), ARS(4,4,3) and a modified BHR(5,5,3) IMEX Runge–Kutta scheme are uniformly stable and uniformly second order (and, for the BHR variant, third order) in ε on the linear hyperbolic relaxation system with Fourier–Galerkin discretization.
* [Jin (2022) — Asymptotic-preserving schemes for multiscale physical problems](/sources/jin-2022.md) — Acta Numerica survey of asymptotic-preserving schemes: the definition and the uniform-in-ε convergence argument, then AP time splitting, random batch methods, BGK penalization, exponential Runge–Kutta, micro–macro and parity schemes, stochastic AP and AP neural networks.
* [Kang & Constantinescu (2022) — Entropy–Preserving and Entropy–Stable Relaxation IMEX and Multirate Time–Stepping Methods](/sources/kang-2022.md) — Extends relaxation Runge–Kutta to additive IMEX methods and to the Constantinescu–Sandu second-order multirate method, including a multilevel level-block formulation, with root conditions and explicit energy relaxation parameters, and shows on Burgers with entropy-stable DG that relaxation keeps design order while IDT loses one.
* [Kemm (2024) — New options for explicit all Mach number schemes by suitable choice of time integration methods](/sources/kemm-2024.md) — Asks which classical explicit integrator stabilizes low- and all-Mach Roe-type finite-volume schemes whose spectra approach the imaginary axis; argues from stability regions and experiments that Adams–Bashforth methods of order three outperform explicit Runge–Kutta, blaming the unstable forward-Euler first stage of every Runge–Kutta step.
* [Ketcheson & Ranocha (2023) — Computing with B-series](/sources/ketcheson-2023.md) — Presents the Julia package BSeries.jl, built on RootedTrees.jl, for symbolic computation with truncated B-series: composition and substitution laws, order conditions, effective order, modified equations and modifying integrators, with worked examples and a 70-fold speed-up over pybs.
* [Koyaguerebo-Imé & Bourgault (2022) — Arbitrary high order A-stable and B-convergent numerical methods for ODEs via deferred correction](/sources/koyaguerebo-2022.md) — Builds a recursive hierarchy of deferred-correction schemes DC2, DC4, DC6, ... on the implicit midpoint rule, each level one midpoint-type nonlinear solve per step, and proves order 2j for every level, A-stability of all levels and B-convergence under a one-sided Lipschitz condition, with fixed-step stiff tests.
* [Ma et al. (2026) — Uniform accuracy of implicit-explicit backward differentiation formulas (IMEX-BDF) for linear hyperbolic relaxation systems](/sources/ma-2026.md) — Proves with Nevanlinna–Odeh multipliers that IMEX-BDF schemes of order 1 to 4 with Fourier–Galerkin discretization are uniformly stable and uniformly accurate in ε on every linear hyperbolic relaxation system satisfying Yong's structural stability condition, generalizing the Jin–Xin result of Hu and Shu.
* [Munch et al. (2024) — Stage-Parallel Fully Implicit Runge–Kutta Implementations with Optimal Multilevel Preconditioners at the Scaling Limit](/sources/munch-2024.md) — Implements stage-parallel Radau IIA solvers with matrix-free multigrid in deal.II: an LU-based real preconditioner and the exact complex diagonalization decouple the stages onto process subgroups, a performance model bounds the speed-up by $s$, and runs to 147,456 processes show gains below 10k unknowns per process.
* [Orlando & Bonaventura (2025) — Asymptotic-preserving IMEX schemes for the Euler equations of non-ideal gases](/sources/orlando-2025.md) — Derives low-Mach limits of the Euler equations for a general equation of state and proves formally that IMEX Runge–Kutta pressure-based schemes are asymptotic preserving: strongly for type I and type ARS methods, weakly (well-prepared data) for type II, with DG tests up to acoustic Courant 19300.
* [Orlando et al. (2025) — A quantitative comparison of high-order asymptotic-preserving and asymptotically-accurate IMEX methods for the Euler equations with non-ideal gases](/sources/orlando-2025-b.md) — Compares a pressure-based IMEX Runge–Kutta scheme with fixed-point stage solves against the linearly implicit semi-implicit IMEX approach on low-Mach Euler flows with non-ideal gases, at orders two to four, for accuracy, cost and robustness, and extends the semi-implicit method to a general EOS.
* [Ranocha et al. (2022) — Optimized Runge-Kutta Methods with Automatic Step Size Control for Compressible Computational Fluid Dynamics](/sources/ranocha-2022.md) — Shows that error-based PID step-size control of explicit embedded Runge–Kutta pairs matches or beats tuned CFL control in compressible CFD, optimizes controllers under step-size-control stability, and builds new low-storage 3S*+ pairs of orders 3–5.
* [Ranocha et al. (2025) — On Error-Based Step Size Control for Discontinuous Galerkin Methods for Compressible Fluid Dynamics](/sources/ranocha-2025.md) — Compares error-based PID step-size control of embedded explicit Runge–Kutta pairs with hand-tuned CFL control on DG compressible-flow problems in two codes, finding that tolerances are easier to choose, match or beat tuned CFL factors, and handle mesh changes, cold starts and new equations automatically.
* [Reynolds et al. (2023) — ARKODE: A Flexible IVP Solver Infrastructure for One-step Methods](/sources/reynolds-2023.md) — Software paper describing ARKODE, the SUNDIALS infrastructure for one-step methods: a shared time loop with error control, dense output, implicit predictors and solver interfaces, plus explicit, additive IMEX and multirate infinitesimal stepping modules, demonstrated on a Brusselator and a GPU reacting-flow weak-scaling study.
* [Roberts et al. (2021) — Implicit Multirate GARK Methods](/sources/roberts-2021.md) — Casts implicit–implicit multirate Runge–Kutta methods as GARK schemes, builds a scalar and 2D linear stability theory with two A-stability barriers, analyses Newton solves, and derives compound-fast MrGARK methods of orders two to four.
* [Song & Eisenträger (2022) — High-order implicit time integration scheme based on Padé expansions](/sources/song-2022.md) — Builds a one-step implicit scheme of order $2m$ for linear structural dynamics by replacing the matrix exponential with its $(m,m)$ diagonal Padé approximant, factored into $m$ Newmark-like real or complex sparse solves; order 2 reproduces Newmark's average-acceleration method.
* [Southworth et al. (2022) — Fast Solution of Fully Implicit Runge–Kutta and Discontinuous Galerkin in Time for Numerical PDEs, Part II: Nonlinearities and DAEs](/sources/southworth-2022.md) — Extends a real-Schur, block-triangular preconditioning framework for fully implicit Runge–Kutta stage systems to nonlinear PDEs and DAEs, with Newton-like stage-Jacobian approximations, a condition-number bound for the 2x2 blocks, and MFEM tests against SDIRK.
* [Southworth et al. (2024) — Implicit-explicit Runge-Kutta for radiation hydrodynamics I: Gray diffusion](/sources/southworth-2024.md) — Applies linearly implicit IMEX (LIMEX) Runge–Kutta methods to gray-diffusion radiation hydrodynamics: a temperature equation and partitions usable by any LIMEX scheme, a three-tableau variant that nearly reproduces the Lie–Trotter split, discrete energy conservation, and errors 3–5 orders below the split at the hydro CFL.
* [Steinebach (2023) — Construction of Rosenbrock–Wanner method Rodas5P and numerical benchmarks within the Julia Differential Equations package](/sources/steinebach-2023.md) — Constructs Rodas5P, a new coefficient set for the 8-stage stiffly accurate fifth-order Rosenbrock–Wanner structure of Rodas5, satisfying index-1 DAE, Prothero–Robinson, index-2 and W-method conditions with an A-stable embedded method and an order-4 dense output, and benchmarks it within the Rodas family in Julia's DifferentialEquations.jl.
* [Sun & Shu (2021) — Enforcing Strong Stability of Explicit Runge-Kutta Methods with Superviscosity](/sources/sun-2021.md) — Restores strong stability of explicit Runge–Kutta methods on linear seminegative problems with dispersive and diffusive superviscosity, as a modified operator or a post-step filter, with proven sharp critical coefficients and an adaptive filter for nonlinear problems.
* [Sun et al. (2022) — On Energy Laws and Stability of Runge–Kutta Methods for Linear Seminegative Problems](/sources/sun-2022.md) — Generalizes the energy-method analysis of Runge–Kutta methods on linear seminegative systems to implicit schemes through their rational stability functions, gives strong- and weak-stability criteria, and proves a unified energy law for all diagonal Padé approximations that truncates the continuous one.
* [Taddei et al. (2026) — High-order implicit Runge–Kutta time integrators for component-based model reduction of FSI problems](/sources/taddei-2026.md) — Applies Radau IIA to the DAE of incompressible ALE fluid–structure interaction, at full and reduced order, with all-stage Newton and static condensation on the interface; the IRK models stay stable where BDF2–Newmark fails, but observed rates fall well below $2s-1$.
* [Toh et al. (2026) — Asymptotic-preserving semi-implicit finite volume scheme for extended magnetohydrodynamics](/sources/toh-2026.md) — Finite-volume solver for extended MHD that keeps ideal MHD on the left-hand side and advances an explicit SSP-RK2 step with an operator-split implicit relaxation of the stiff electric-field and current sources, formally asymptotic-preserving in the ideal-MHD limit but first order in time when non-ideal effects matter.
* [Vermeire (2023) — Embedded paired explicit Runge-Kutta schemes](/sources/vermeire-2023.md) — Adds to each second-order paired explicit Runge–Kutta member a first-order embedded method with one fewer active stage and a slightly smaller optimized stability region, so that the embedded difference detects the stability limit for a PID controller; speedups of 4.95–7.11 over Bogacki–Shampine 3(2) on Navier–Stokes flows.
* [Zhang (2024) — An explicit 16-stage Runge–Kutta method of order 10 discovered by numerical search](/sources/zhang-2024.md) — Reports an explicit 16-stage Runge–Kutta method that satisfies all 1205 order-10 conditions to more than 3000 digits, found by BFGS minimization of the squared order-condition residuals without simplifying assumptions, conjecturally lowering the known stage count for explicit order 10 from 17 to 16.
