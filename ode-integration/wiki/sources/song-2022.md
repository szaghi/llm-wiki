# Summary

The paper builds a single-step implicit integrator of arbitrary even order for the
linear semi-discrete equations of structural dynamics $M\ddot u+C\dot u+Ku=f(t)$.
Time is rescaled within each step, the system is written in first-order form
with state $x=[h\dot u;\,u]$ and step-scaled state matrix $\mathcal A$, and the
load is taken polynomial of degree $p_{\mathrm f}$ in each step. The exact one-step
map is then $e^{\mathcal A}x_n$ plus a sum of closed-form load terms
(see [exponential integrators](/methods/exponential-integrators.md)).

The method replaces $e^{\mathcal A}$ by the $(m,m)$ diagonal Padé approximant
$\hat D_m(\mathcal A)^{-1}\hat N_m(\mathcal A)$, which gives order $2m$. This is the
[Padé-based time integration](/methods/pade-time-integration.md) scheme; its
propagator is the diagonal Padé [stability function](/definitions/stability-function.md).
The efficiency contribution is the implementation:
- $\hat D_m$ is factored into its $m$ roots $r_i$;
- each real root costs one real sparse solve, each complex-conjugate pair one complex solve;
- every solve is reduced to a Newmark-like system $(r^2M+rhC+h^2K)\xi_1=\dots$ of
  the original size, so $M^{-1}$ is never formed (see
  [implicit stage solvers](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md)).

Formulas are given for $m=1,\dots,4$ (orders 2, 4, 6, 8). The case $m=1$
with linear load is shown to be algebraically identical to the constant
average-acceleration [Newmark method](/methods/newmark-method.md).

Stability is asserted from the unit spectral radius of diagonal Padé (compare
[unconditional strong stability of diagonal Padé schemes](/results/diagonal-pade-strong-stability.md));
the scheme has no numerical damping. The paper never relates the scheme to Gauss
collocation, whose stability function is the same approximant (a link drawn by the
compiler on the [fully implicit Runge–Kutta](/methods/fully-implicit-runge-kutta.md)
page).

The tests are SDOF oscillators (rates 2, 4, 6, 8), 10,000-period amplitude and
period-elongation studies against Newmark, HHT-α, generalized-α and Bathe, a 2D
rod (up to 658,434 DOFs), Lamb's problem (821,762 DOFs) and a 3D SBFEM mountain
model (755,337 DOFs). The higher-order variants reach 1 % error at 6–15 times lower
cost than Newmark.

# Key claims

Page-local symbols follow the [Padé-based time integration](/methods/pade-time-integration.md)
page: $x$ the state, $\mathcal A$ the step-scaled state matrix, $m$ the Padé degree,
$\hat N_m$, $\hat D_m$ the unnormalized Padé numerator and denominator, $r_i$ their
roots, $\mathcal F_k$ the load coefficients, $\theta\in[0,1]$ the step-local time.

1. For linear constant-coefficient structural dynamics with step-wise polynomial load, $x_{n+1}=e^{\mathcal A}x_n+\sum_{k=0}^{p_{\mathrm f}}\mathcal B_k\mathcal F_k$ is exact (time discretization exact, spatial error not counted) (§2.1, Eq. (21)). **Derived**
2. The integrals $\mathcal B_k=e^{\mathcal A}\int_0^1(\theta-\tfrac12)^ke^{-\mathcal A\theta}\,d\theta$ satisfy $\mathcal B_k=\mathcal A^{-1}\big(k\mathcal B_{k-1}+(-\tfrac12)^k(e^{\mathcal A}-(-1)^kI)\big)$ with $\mathcal B_0=\mathcal A^{-1}(e^{\mathcal A}-I)$ (Eqs. (19)–(20)). **Derived**
3. Direct use of $e^{\mathcal A}$ is intractable for large problems: $M^{-1}C$ and $M^{-1}K$ are full for a consistent mass matrix, and $e^{\mathcal A}$ is full (§2.1). **Stated**
4. Replacing $e^{\mathcal A}$ by its $(m,m)$ diagonal Padé approximant yields a scheme of order $2m$; each increase of the order by two adds one real or complex sparse system per step (abstract, §2.2, App. B). **Stated** (order shown numerically, Figs. 2, 7, 14)
5. Taylor (polynomial) approximations of $e^{\mathcal A}$ give explicit, conditionally stable schemes; rational (Padé) approximations give implicit schemes that can be unconditionally stable (§2.2, citing [48]). **Stated**
6. Pre-multiplying by $\hat D_m$ gives $\hat D_m(\mathcal A)x_{n+1}=\hat N_m(\mathcal A)x_n+\sum_k\mathcal C_k\mathcal F_k$ with $\mathcal C_k=\hat D_m\mathcal B_k=\mathcal A^{-1}\big(k\mathcal C_{k-1}+(-\tfrac12)^k(\hat N_m-(-1)^k\hat D_m)\big)$, $\mathcal C_0=\mathcal A^{-1}(\hat N_m-\hat D_m)$; the $\mathcal C_k$ are polynomials in $\mathcal A$ (Eqs. (24)–(26)). **Derived**
7. Solving for $x_{n+1}+x_n$ ($m$ odd) or $x_{n+1}-x_n$ ($m$ even) cancels the highest power of $\mathcal A$ on the right-hand side ($\hat N_m\pm\hat D_m$) (Eqs. (27)–(31)). **Derived**
8. Factoring $\hat D_m(\mathcal A)=\prod_i(r_iI-\mathcal A)$ turns the step into $m$ successive shifted solves (Eqs. (29), (32)–(33)). **Derived**
9. A real-root solve $(rI-\mathcal A)\xi=\rho$ reduces to $(r^2M+rhC+h^2K)\xi_1=rM\rho_1-h^2K\rho_2$, $\xi_2=(\xi_1+\rho_2)/r$: no inverse mass matrix, and a sparse symmetric positive definite matrix "similar to the Newmark scheme" (§2.3.1, Eqs. (39)–(42)). **Derived**
10. A complex-conjugate pair is handled by partial fractions with one complex solve: $\xi=-\operatorname{Im}(\upsilon)/\operatorname{Im}(r)$ with $(rI-\mathcal A)\upsilon=\rho$; the complex matrix $r^2M+rhC+h^2K$ is symmetric but not Hermitian (§2.3.2, Eqs. (43)–(52)). **Derived**
11. The products $\mathcal A\xi=-\operatorname{Im}(r\upsilon)/\operatorname{Im}(r)$ and $\mathcal A^2\xi=\rho+2\operatorname{Re}(r)\,\mathcal A\xi-r\bar r\xi$ come free from the complex solve; for $m=4$, $\mathcal A^3x_{n+1}$ is also recovered from stored auxiliary vectors (Eqs. (53), (54), (87)–(90)). **Derived**
12. $m=1$, $p_{\mathrm f}=1$ ($\hat N_1(z)=2+z$, $\hat D_1(z)=2-z$, $r=2$, $\mathcal C_0=2I$, $\mathcal C_1=0$) is algebraically identical to Newmark's constant average-acceleration method ($\gamma_{\mathrm N}=1/2$, $\beta_{\mathrm N}=1/4$) with midpoint-averaged load $(f_n+f_{n+1})/2$ (§2.4.1, Eqs. (58)–(69)). **Proved**
13. Roots of $\hat D_m$: $m=1$: $2$; $m=2$: $3\pm\sqrt3\,i$; $m=3$: $4.644370709252171$ and $3.677814645373910\pm3.508761919567443\,i$; $m=4$: $4.207578794359259\pm5.314836083713504\,i$ and $5.792421205640749\pm1.734468257869007\,i$ (Eqs. (56), (71), (76), (82)). **Derived**
14. The load polynomial degree must equal the Padé degree, $p_{\mathrm f}=m$, for optimal convergence (except $m=1$, where $p_{\mathrm f}=1$) (footnote 7, §§2.4.1–2.4.4). **Stated**
15. The method is of arbitrary order, needs no inverse mass matrix, and its system size does not grow with the order, unlike the methods of Idesman and of Kim–Reddy (§1, end of §2.4). **Stated**
16. Padé-based integrators are unconditionally stable because the spectral radius of the rational approximation is 1, so there is no amplitude decay and no numerical damping (§3.2.1, §4, citing [48]). **Stated** (no amplification-matrix analysis)
17. SDOF cases #1–#6 show optimal rates 2, 4, 6, 8 with and without damping and load, down to an error plateau of about $10^{-9}\,\%$ attributed to round-off (Fig. 2). **Shown**
18. Amplitude error after 10,000 periods at the 1 % threshold needs $h=T_n/2$ at order 8, $h<T_n/3$ at order 6, $h<T_n/8$ at order 4, and $h<T_n/85$ for second-order methods (§3.2.2, Fig. 3a–b). **Shown**
19. Period elongation at $h=T_n/2$ is about 0 %, 0.5 %, 7.7 % and 56.5 % for the proposed schemes (presumably orders 8, 6, 4, 2); HHT-α ($\alpha=-0.05$) is worst at 61.7 %, Bathe best among second-order methods at 30.7 % (§3.2.3, Fig. 3c–d). **Shown**
20. All methods are very accurate when $h/T_n<0.01$ (§3.2.3). **Shown**
21. On the 2D rod (66–658,434 DOFs, 6,400 steps), the median wall-clock cost per step relative to Newmark is 1.09, 2.73, 3.92, 5.51 for orders 2, 4, 6, 8 (§3.3.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
22. On the rod with 1,280 elements, the steps for 1 % $L_2$ error are $8.4\times10^{-5}$ s (Newmark), $1.4\times10^{-3}$, $4.1\times10^{-3}$, $7.6\times10^{-3}$ s (orders 4, 6, 8), step increases of 17, 49, 90 and speed-ups of "roughly 6, 12, and 15" (§3.3.2, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
23. On Lamb's problem (821,762 DOFs) the cost relative to Newmark is 1, 4.55, 5.53, 9.96, the 1 %-error step factors are 12.14, 31.67, 49.88, and the speed-ups 2.67, 5.73, 5.01 (§3.4, Figs. 14–15). **Shown**
24. On the 3D mountain model (755,337 DOFs) the eighth-order scheme at $h_{\max}\approx9\times10^{-2}$ s matches a central-difference reference, Newmark needs $h_{\max}/50$, and the costs are 4.05 against 24.1 times Newmark at $h_{\max}$ (§3.5, Fig. 19). **Shown**
25. The order-2 scheme and Newmark give identical errors on the rod and Lamb tests (Figs. 7, 14). **Shown**
26. If damping is needed, a very small stiffness-proportional Rayleigh damping is adequate and "might even outperform" methods with high-frequency numerical damping (§4; preliminary, not shown). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §2.3.1, after (40b): "Eq. (40a) is rearranged with respect to x2"; the rearranged equation (41) comes from (40b).
- §2.4.4, after (86): "re-used to evaluate the right-hand side of Eq. (79)"; for $m=4$ the right-hand side is Eq. (85), and (79) is the $m=3$ one.
- Fig. 18 caption gives "∆t = 0.0018 s; Padé scheme of order O(1,1)", while §3.5 says the snapshots use order $O(4,4)$ at $\Delta t_{\max}\approx9\times10^{-2}$ s. The Fig. 12 caption has an unclosed parenthesis.
- Fig. 19(d) caption: "at point P3 (25% of the peak height)"; the 25 % point is P4 (P3 is the 50 % point).
- §3.5, observation-point coordinates mix decimal points and thousands commas ("3.224 m", "0,936 m" for 3,224 m and 936 m).
- App. A: "A has two imaginary eigenvalues ±i" for a matrix whose eigenvalues are $\pm i\omega_n$.
- App. C, Eq. (C.5): summation index $k$ against summand index $l$.
- §4: "accuracy of order 2p, where p is the polynomial degree of the diagonal Padé expansion"; the rest of the paper calls this degree $M$ and uses $p$ for the load degree.
- §3.3.2: from step factor 90 and cost ratio 5.51 the speed-up is about 16.3, not "15".
- §3.2.3: the period-elongation values are listed without saying which order each belongs to.
- Eqs. (76), (82): the last printed digits of the roots differ from high-precision values by up to $5\times10^{-15}$ (rounding only).
- Ref. [4] prints "Kim, J. N. Reddy" without the first author's initial; ref. [44] prints "Paronesso" while the text spells "Paranesso"; ref. [58] prints a reprint book-chapter DOI for the 1928 Math. Ann. article.
- Typos: "in trun" (§3.1), "multiplies of the actual period" (§3.2.2), "contnuous" (§3.5), "eighths-order" (§3.3.1).
