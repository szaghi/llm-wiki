# Summary

The paper fills what it calls "a notable gap in the publication record":
error-based [step-size control](/methods/step-size-control.md) for
[multirate infinitesimal (MRI) methods](/methods/multirate-infinitesimal-methods.md),
in which every slow stage solves a modified fast problem by subcycling an inner
one-step method with micro step $h=H/m$. The authors adapt the macro step $H$
**and** the integer multirate ratio $m$ together ("H-M controllers"): adapting
only $H$ at fixed $m$, or only $h$ at fixed $H$, wastes work or loses accuracy.

The total error is bounded by a slow error (the MRI method with exactly solved
stages) plus a fast error (the inner solver), modelled as
$e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1}=\varphi^{\{\mathrm s\}}_nH_n^{p^{\{\mathrm s\}}}$ and
$e^{\{\mathrm f\}}_{n+1}=\varphi^{\{\mathrm f\}}_n(H_n/m_n)^{p^{\{\mathrm f\}}}H_n$,
with $p^{\{\mathrm s\}}$, $p^{\{\mathrm f\}}$ the global orders of the slow and
fast embeddings. Following Gustafsson (1994), $\log E$ is tracked by a piecewise
constant or piecewise linear observer, which yields the Constant-Constant and
Linear-Linear controllers; two heuristic analogues of single-rate PI and PID
control, PIMR and PIDMR, are added. Higher-order observers were non-robust.

The slow error comes from the standard last-stage MRI embedding
([embedded pairs](/methods/embedded-runge-kutta-pair.md)). For the fast error
five estimators are compared: Full-Step, Stage-Aggregate (mean, max), and
Local-Accumulation-Stage-Aggregate (LASA, mean, max), which sums inner
local-error estimates at no extra cost. On seven ODE tests (bicoupling, stiff
[Brusselator](/benchmarks/brusselator.md), Kaps, the multirate
[Prothero–Robinson problem](/benchmarks/prothero-robinson-problem.md), forced
Van der Pol, Pleiades, a 3D four-body problem), four embedded MRI-GARK methods and
three tolerances, measured against a brute-force optimal $(H,m)$ sequence,
LASA-mean is the cheapest accurate estimator. The four multirate controllers
differ by at most 20 % in cost and all beat single-rate I, PI, PID and
Gustafsson controllers run at fixed $m=10$. A 1D reaction–diffusion Brusselator
not used in tuning shows the controllers raising $m$ during a stiff transient.
The paper addresses the multirate adaptivity that the
[ARKODE](/software/arkode.md) description of
[Reynolds et al. (2023)](/sources/reynolds-2023.md) records as open; its
Constant-Constant controller with LASA-mean is reused by
[Fish et al. (2024)](/sources/fish-2024.md).

# Key claims

1. The total MRI error splits as $e_n\le e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_n+e^{\{\mathrm f\}}_n$ through an "imagined" solution $y_n^*$ with exactly solved stages (triangle inequality) (§2, unnumbered equation before (2.1)). **Derived**
2. Error models (2.1): $e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1}=\varphi^{\{\mathrm s\}}_nH_n^{p^{\{\mathrm s\}}}+O(H_n^{p^{\{\mathrm s\}}+1})$ and $e^{\{\mathrm f\}}_{n+1}=\varphi^{\{\mathrm f\}}_n(H_n/m_n)^{p^{\{\mathrm f\}}}H_n+O((H_n/m_n)^{p^{\{\mathrm f\}}+1}H_n)$, with $p^{\{\mathrm s\}}$, $p^{\{\mathrm f\}}$ the **embedding** global orders; the fast model is the global error of a one-step method with step $H_n/m_n$ over an interval of length $H_n$, and stage-interval lengths are bounded by $H_n$ (§2). **Derived**
3. Setting $e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1}=\mathrm{tol}^{\{\mathrm s\}}$, $e^{\{\mathrm f\}}_{n+1}=\mathrm{tol}^{\{\mathrm f\}}$, $\mathrm{tol}=\mathrm{tol}^{\{\mathrm s\}}+\mathrm{tol}^{\{\mathrm f\}}$, gives log-space update laws (2.3) for $H_n$ and $m_n$; $m_n$ is real in the derivation and rounded up in use (§2, §4.2). **Derived**
4. Constant-Constant controller (2.9), with $q^{\{\mathrm s\}}_n=\mathrm{tol}^{\{\mathrm s\}}/e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_n$ and $q^{\{\mathrm f\}}_n$ likewise: $H_{n+1}=H_n(q^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1})^{\kappa_1/p^{\{\mathrm s\}}}$, $m_{n+1}=m_n(q^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1})^{(p^{\{\mathrm f\}}+1)\kappa_1/(p^{\{\mathrm s\}}p^{\{\mathrm f\}})}(q^{\{\mathrm f\}}_{n+1})^{-\kappa_2/p^{\{\mathrm f\}}}$; the $H$ part is a single-rate I controller (§2.2.1). **Derived**
5. The Linear-Linear controller (2.10)–(2.11) depends on $H_n^2H_{n-1}^{-1}$, $m_n^2m_{n-1}^{-1}$ and the error ratios of two steps (§2.2.2). **Derived**
6. Mixed-order observers (constant for the slow, linear for the fast error function) are valid but ruled out: the $m$ update inherits the longer history anyway, and the fast scale is expected to vary faster (§2.2). **Stated**
7. Quadratic and higher observers give non-robust, "bimodal" controllers that over-smooth and cannot change $H$ or $m$ fast enough; they are omitted (§2.3; Conclusions). **Stated** (no data shown)
8. PIMR (2.13) is Linear-Linear with one factor $H_n/H_{n-1}$ and $m_n/m_{n-1}$ removed, same exponents (2.11); PIDMR (2.15) adds error ratios of step $n-1$ with two more gains. Both are heuristic, not derived (§2.4–2.5). **Stated**
9. The slow error is estimated by the last-stage MRI embedding, $e^{\{\mathrm s\}}_{n+1}\approx\lVert y_{n+1}-\hat y_{n+1}\rVert$ (§3). **Stated**
10. The Full-Step fast estimate runs the whole MRI step twice (primary and embedded inner method), doubling slow evaluations; Stage-Aggregate halves the slow evaluations relative to Full-Step; LASA uses half the slow and about half the fast evaluations of Full-Step, with no evaluations beyond the primary inner solve (§3.1–3.3). **Derived** (cost counting)
11. All five fast-error strategies reach the target accuracy on average, mean Error Deviation $\log_{10}(e/\mathrm{tol})$: Full-Step −0.20, SA-mean −0.07, SA-max −0.15, LASA-mean −0.08, LASA-max −0.17; Full-Step and the max variants over-estimate the fast error (§4.5, Fig. 4). **Shown**
12. Mean Slow Cost Deviation (evaluations over the brute-force optimum): Full-Step 3.6, SA-mean 2.4, SA-max 2.4, LASA-mean 1.8, LASA-max 1.8; Fast Cost Deviation: 127.0, 49.8, 120.5, 27.9, 66.2. LASA-mean is "by far" closest to the optimal fast cost, used for all later results and recommended (§4.5, Fig. 5, §5). **Shown**
13. Tuned gains with LASA-mean: Constant-Constant $\kappa_1=0.42$, $\kappa_2=0.44$ (4.5); Linear-Linear $K_1=[0.82\ 0.54]^\top$, $K_2=[0.94\ 0.9]^\top$ (4.6); PIMR $K_1=[0.18\ 0.86]^\top$, $K_2=[0.34\ 0.80]^\top$ (4.7); PIDMR $K_1=[0.34\ 0.10\ 0.78]^\top$, $K_2=[0.46\ 0.42\ 0.74]^\top$ (4.8) (§4.6). **Shown** (numerical optimization)
14. Mean Error Deviation by controller: Constant-Constant −0.08, Linear-Linear −0.20, PIMR −0.08, PIDMR −0.11, single-rate I −0.11, PI −0.08, PID −0.33, Gustafsson −0.28; all controllers slightly over-solve (§4.7, Fig. 6). **Shown**
15. Mean Slow Cost Deviation: Constant-Constant 3.6, Linear-Linear 4.2, PIMR 3.5, PIDMR 3.7, I 80.0, PI 24.8, PID 4.3, Gustafsson 5.9; Fast: 5.7, 6.6, 6.1, 5.8, 123.9, 90.1, 10.4, 13.0. The multirate controllers spread by 20 % (slow) and 16 % (fast); the single-rate ones ran at fixed $m=10$ with the slow error only (§4.7, Fig. 7). **Shown**
16. The best single-rate controller (PID) is on par in slow cost but 58 % worse in fast cost than the worst multirate controller (Linear-Linear); I and PI are "dramatically worse"; I failed every forced Van der Pol run with MRI-GARK-IRK21a and MRI-GARK-ESDIRK34a (§4.7). **Shown**
17. Recommendation: Constant-Constant for relatively simple problems; test every multirate controller for complex ones, "no single method outperforming another" (§4.7). **Stated**
18. On the 1D stiff reaction–diffusion Brusselator (stiffness parameter $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, MRI-GARK-ERK45a, $\mathrm{tol}=10^{-4}$) all controllers keep $m=1$ for the first half, raise $m$ at $t\approx0.9$ when the reaction stiffens, and afterwards Linear-Linear returns to $m=1$ while the others keep $m=2$; Linear-Linear has failed steps at $t=1.0$ (§4.8, Fig. 8). **Shown**
19. On the same problem with $\mathrm{tol}\in[10^{-7},10^{-3}]$ every multirate controller over-solves by two to four orders of magnitude; Linear-Linear needs about 13 % more slow and 11 % more fast evaluations than the others (§4.8, Fig. 9). **Shown**
20. MRI-GARK (Sandu 2019) is the only infinitesimal family with embeddings that the authors know; embeddings for other MRI families are needed (§4.2, §5). **Stated**
21. A post-publication correction of the MRI-GARK-ERK45a embedding row $\hat\Gamma^{(0)}_6$, taken from the arXiv version of Sandu's paper, was used (§4.2). **Stated**
22. The only earlier error-based multirate adaptivity for multirate methods is Sarshar et al. (two simple schemes for MrGARK); Savcenco et al. is component-wise multirate adaptivity for single-rate methods (§1). **Stated**
23. The brute-force optimal baseline (efficiency $H_n/\text{cost}$, cost $=10\cdot(\text{slow evaluations})+(\text{fast evaluations})$) is "nearly impossible" for an adaptive controller to reach (App. A). **Stated**
24. H-h controllers with a real-valued micro step in place of the integer $m$ could be derived the same way and might be more efficient (§5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- p. 4, unnumbered equation between (2.2) and (2.3): "$\log(M_n)=\frac{(p+1)(\mathrm{TOL}^s)-\log(\phi^s_n))-\dots}{Pp}$" lacks "$\log($" before $\mathrm{TOL}^s$ (unbalanced parenthesis); (2.3) prints it correctly.
- §2.1.3, p. 6: "Both the piecewise constant and piecewise quadratic approaches above resulted in approximations (2.4) and (2.7)"; §2.1.2 is the piecewise **linear** approach.
- §2.1.2 calls $\nabla$ "the gradient with respect to the iteration number"; it is a backward difference.
- §4.1.2, p. 12: the stiff Brusselator prints the first right-hand-side component as $a-(w+1)u+u^2v$ but the splitting prints the slow first component as $a+(w+1)u+u^2v$, so the parts do not sum to $f$; the text writes "$\varepsilon=0.01$" where the equation uses $\epsilon$.
- Method names vary: "MRI-GARK-ESDIRK34a", "MRI-GARK-ESDIR34a" (p. 15), "MRIGARKESDIRK34a", "MRIGARKIRK21a" (p. 19), "MRIGARKERK45a" (p. 20).
- Algorithm A.1, p. 26: the loop "while $M<M_{max\_iter}$" never increments $M$, and FindH is called with an undefined "$M_{new}$".
- Algorithm A.2, p. 27: the first ComputeStep uses $H_{mid}$ before it is defined; "eff ← H/cost" uses $H$ before it is set; "$n\leftarrow n+1$" uses an undefined counter; inside the bisection the reference solution is computed at $H_{right}$ while the step uses $H_{mid}$.
- Conclusions say the multirate controllers "slightly outperform the PID controller ... in terms of both slow and fast function evaluations", while §4.7 reports PID on par in slow cost and 58 % worse in fast cost.
- Fig. 5 (LASA-mean averaged over the four controllers) gives slow/fast cost deviations 1.8/27.9; Fig. 7 (same problems, re-optimized gains) gives 3.5–4.2/5.7–6.6. The change is not explained.
- Grammar: "may be lead to overstepping" (p. 3), "a detailed description if this brute force algorithm" (p. 15), "in an practice" (p. 25).
