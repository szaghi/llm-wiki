# Summary

The paper builds a neural-operator surrogate, one of the
[learned time integrators](/methods/learned-time-integrators.md), for the
stiff implicit integration of zero-dimensional, adiabatic, constant-pressure
gas-phase kinetics, the chemistry sub-step of an
[operator-split](/methods/operator-splitting.md) reacting-flow solver. A
DeepONet maps the thermochemical state (temperature and $n_{\rm sp}$ mass
fractions) at the start of a segment to the whole trajectory on 101 uniformly
spaced output times ($10^{-7}$ s apart, segment length 100 spacings). In
effect it is a learned flow map with dense output on a fixed grid; long
horizons are covered by chaining segments autoregressively, which is valid
because the system is autonomous.

The framework, AMORE, combines: one DeepONet whose output neurons are split
into one group of basis functions and coefficients per state variable, with a
ResNet or a Jacobi-polynomial KAN trunk; a softmax on the trunk output that
makes each variable's basis a partition of unity; two gradient-free adaptive
loss weightings, per variable (Type A) or per variable and sample (Type B),
proportional to the previous epoch's relative errors with constant sum;
multi-output two-step training (trunk first, QR reparametrization, then
branch); and mass conservation by a penalty, by an invertible map of the
$n_{\rm sp}$ mass fractions to $n_{\rm sp}-1$ unit-interval coordinates, or by a
softmax output.

All evidence is numerical, on a syngas mechanism (11 species, 21 reactions)
and GRI-Mech 3.0 (24 retained states). Training data come from Cantera's VODE
[BDF](/methods/backward-differentiation-formula.md) solver (rtol $10^{-8}$,
atol $10^{-12}$). Mean test errors fall from 0.154% (plain DeepONet) to 0.041%
(two-step KAN trunk with Type-B weights) and 0.034–0.037% with the
mass-conserving map; autoregressive rollout over $10^{-3}$ s keeps every
variable's 90th-percentile error below 5%, with maxima of 44–53% for HCO near
ignition. No inference timing against the BDF solver is reported, and the
surrogate is not coupled to a flow solver. The paper also cites the
[Robertson problem](/benchmarks/robertson-problem.md) as a case where the
state variables do not sum to one.

# Key claims

1. In reacting flows the chemical time scales can be orders of magnitude below the hydrodynamic ones; fully implicit integration is infeasible for LES/DNS, so semi-implicit or operator-splitting schemes treat non-reactive terms explicitly and the stiff chemistry implicitly, at a direct-solve cost of $O(n_{\rm sp}^3)$ (§1, p. 2). **Stated**
2. A single DeepONet predicts all $n_{\rm sp}+1$ state variables, each as a sum over its own block of $p_{\rm b}$ branch–trunk products (§3.1, Eqs. 4–5). **Shown** (effective in §§4–5)
3. A softmax over the basis index of the trunk output, per state variable, enforces a partition of unity exactly: each basis function lies in $(0,1)$ and they sum to 1 (§3.2.1, Eqs. 8–9), unlike the penalty-based partition of unity of Goswami et al. (2024), which guarantees neither. **Derived** (by construction)
4. Type-A adaptive loss: the weight of variable $a$ is its sample-mean relative $L^2$-in-time error divided by the sum over variables, times the number of variables, so the weights always sum to that number (§3.3.1, Eqs. 12–13, B.5). **Proved** (sum identity)
5. Type-B adaptive loss: one weight per variable and sample, normalized to sum to the number of variables times the number of samples (§3.3.2, Eqs. 14–15); neither type adds a hyperparameter, unlike self-adaptive weights or residual-based attention (§3.3). **Stated**
6. Syngas, one-step training, mean relative $L^2$ test error over three runs: ResNet trunk 0.154/0.151/0.153% (non-adaptive), 0.076/0.077/0.076% (Type A), 0.064/0.062/0.061% (Type B); KAN trunk 0.099/0.099/0.101%, 0.052/0.050/0.054%, 0.042/0.043/0.043% (Table 2). **Shown**
7. Two-step training (trunk with a trainable coefficient tensor, QR of the trunk, least-squares coefficients, then the branch fitted to the triangular QR factor times the coefficients) is extended to multiple outputs with per-variable bases from one trunk and per-variable coefficients from one branch (§3.4, Eqs. 16–26; extension of Lee & Shin). **Derived**
8. Trunk-training errors (syngas, %): ResNet 0.1639/0.1638/0.1703 (non-adaptive), 0.0541/0.0542/0.0545 (A), 0.0120/0.0143/0.0132 (B); KAN 0.1499/0.1527/0.1505, 0.0281/0.0309/0.0287, 0.0081/0.0079/0.0088 (Table C.3). **Shown**
9. After branch training (KAN Type-B trunk, ResNet branch): 0.086/0.084/0.084% (non-adaptive), 0.044/0.046/0.045% (A), 0.041/0.040/0.041% (B) test error (Table 3); two-step training with a KAN trunk and Type-B weights gives the smallest mean error (§4.3). **Shown**
10. The error concentrates in the minor radicals HO$_2$ and HCO: reconstructed-trajectory mean errors 2.443% and 2.197% for the plain DeepONet fall to 0.250% and 0.182% with two-step Type-B training (Table 4); adaptive losses reduce both the mean and the spread across samples. **Shown**
11. The invertible simplex map gives 0.035/0.037/0.034% test error against 0.041/0.040/0.041% without it (Table C.4); mass fractions then sum exactly to one, whereas without it the sums range over $[0.99852,1.00162]$ on the test set (§4.4); the inverse map costs an extra linear solve at inference (§4.4, §7). **Shown**
12. Softmax on the mass-fraction outputs ($\exp$ on temperature) with a log-space loss (Eqs. 33–35): 0.0464/0.0462/0.046% (non-adaptive) and 0.0431/0.0431/0.0426% (Type B) test error (Table 5); it helps strongly without adaptive weights (0.099% to 0.046%), little with them; the coordinate map is better; the softmax variant is not possible with two-step training (§4.5). **Shown**
13. Extrapolation to an initial temperature of 1800 K with equivalence ratios 0.9 and 1.5 (outside $[1000,1500]$ K and $[0.7,1.3]$) and to $2\times10^{-3}$ s (training up to about $10^{-3}$ s) gives "good accuracy"; the one-step Type-B model underpredicts peaks at ratio 1.5 because of its $1.05\tanh$ output bound (Eq. 32), and two-step predictions are noisier (§4.6, Figs. 9, C.4). **Shown** (qualitative)
14. Autoregressive rollout over the test horizon: test errors 0.632/0.509/0.606% (two-step Type B) and 0.643/0.565/0.556% (with the simplex map) (Table C.6); every per-variable 90th percentile stays below 5% (largest 3.81% and 4.66%, both HCO), per-variable maxima reach 43.91% and 52.83% (HCO) (Table 6); the error distributions are strongly skewed and peak for $t\in[10^{-4},4\times10^{-4}]$ s, around ignition (Fig. 11). **Shown**
15. GRI-Mech 3.0 (24 retained states, 2.16 M parameters, two-step KAN trunk, Type B): trunk-training error 0.0303/0.0294/0.0288%, branch test error 0.0837/0.0754/0.0831% (§5.1); largest per-variable mean errors H$_2$O$_2$ 0.362% and CH$_2$O 0.284% (Table C.9). **Shown**
16. A Fourier neural operator on the same time decomposition: 0.166/0.172/0.169% test error non-adaptive, 0.125/0.128/0.128% with Type-B weights, 13.7–14.8 h of training for 3000 epochs (Table C.10, §6); the paper disclaims any FNO-versus-DeepONet comparison. **Shown**
17. Training on one NVIDIA L40S in double precision: one-step syngas 10.3–11.8 h; two-step 19.1–20.8 h (trunk) plus 9.3–10.4 h (branch); GRI 23.2–23.4 h plus 25.4–25.8 h (Table D.1); adaptive losses add little cost, two-step training more (§7). **Shown**
18. "Online (inference) costs are much smaller than implicit solvers" (§1, p. 2); no inference timing or comparison with the BDF reference is reported. **Stated**
19. The syngas DeepONet of Goswami et al. (2024) predicts only at $250$, $500$, $750$ and $1000$ steps of $10^{-8}$ s and so likely misses the stiff peaks; its reported "auto-regressive" error was, as those authors confirmed, not computed autoregressively (§1, p. 2). **Stated**
20. Mass conservation in mass-fraction form fails when the retained variables do not sum to one (GRI-Mech 3.0 with inactive species, Robertson), where the conservation loss must be dropped or modified (§8). **Stated**

The surrogate runs at a fixed output spacing and horizon and a fixed pressure
of 1 atm, with no step-size control, no convergence study in the spacing and no
stability guarantee under rollout; the paper does not claim these properties.

# Printed slips

- Table C.5: the column labels H and HO$_2$ are interchanged (its two-step Type-B row gives HO$_2$ 0.116 and H 0.25, while Table 4 gives H 0.116 and HO$_2$ 0.250 for the same model).
- Eqs. (B.10)–(B.12), inverse simplex map: with $z_{n_{\rm sp}-1}=y_{n_{\rm sp}-1}$ undivided (Eq. B.7b), the system for the auxiliary unknowns has right-hand side $(1-z_{n_{\rm sp}-1})\mathbf 1$, not the printed $[1,\dots,1]^\top$; (B.10) treats $y_{n_{\rm sp}-1}$ as $z_{n_{\rm sp}-1}d_{n_{\rm sp}-1}$ *(compiler check)*. The sum in (B.11) has a garbled upper limit "$d$".
- Eq. (B.3) writes $X_j$ and (B.4) $X_{bs,j}$ for the errors the text calls $X_a$, $X_{ba}$; the weights are printed both $\mathcal W_{ba}$ and $\mathcal W_{ab}$; §3.3.2 refers to the denominator of Eq. (14), which is in Eq. (15).
- §3.1: "$n1+1$" for $n_t+1$.
- Table C.8 caption describes a "[12 − 200 × 10 − 2280]" ResNet and two architectures; the table lists only the KAN-trunk model with a "[24 − 250 × 10 − 2280]" branch. Table D.1 labels the GRI branch row "for syngas problem".
- §4 (p. 14): $10^4$ output times are reported, but 99 segments of 100 spacings cover only 9900; 2080 training plus 520 test cases make 2600 of the 2601 generated.
- Captions of Fig. 5 and Fig. C.1 compare "DON-Ad-B" with the two-step model where §4.3 and Table 4 compare the KAN-trunk model.
- §4.4: the physical-space training errors lack the percent sign; the claim that the simplex-map model is "marginally better" is not uniform across species (N$_2$ 0.013 vs 0.002 in Table C.5).
- GRI-Mech 3.0: the fuel CH$_4$ is absent from the list of 23 retained species (Table C.7) without explanation.
- Typos: "DeepKAN" (Table 2), "Syngaas" (Table C.2), "one-step straining" (§4.2), "FON-NA" (App. C.3).
