# Summary

The paper introduces
[IMEX multirate infinitesimal stage-restart (IMEX-MRI-SR) methods](/methods/imex-mri-sr.md)
for the three-way problem $y'=f^{\{\mathrm f\}}+f^{\mathrm I}+f^{\mathrm E}$: the fast
part is treated infinitesimally and the slow part is split into an implicit and an
explicit part. Every slow stage **restarts** its fast problem from $y_n$ and
integrates it over $[0,c_iH]$, then performs a diagonally implicit solve in
$f^{\mathrm I}$. [IMEX-MRI-GARK methods](/methods/imex-mri-gark.md) instead
integrate from the previous stage over $[c_{i-1}H,c_iH]$, which needs sorted
abscissae and padding stages and has so far prevented embeddings. The restart
removes both constraints: the abscissae of the new methods are unsorted, and
IMEX-MRI-SR3(2) has $c_4=\frac{17}{15}>1$.

Written as a
[GARK method](/methods/generalized-additive-runge-kutta.md) with Kronecker-product
tables, the class has simple internal-consistency conditions, one third-order and
six fourth-order coupling conditions beyond the order of the slow base IMEX
method, and needs at least two forcing matrices $\Omega^{(k)}$ for order 3. Its
[linear stability](/definitions/multirate-linear-stability.md) function is written
with $\phi_k$ functions and three joint regions. Three embedded methods are given,
IMEX-MRI-SR2(1), SR3(2) and SR4(3) (4, 5 and 7 stages; 3, 4 and 5 implicit solves),
whose embeddings need no extra implicit solve. SR4(3), built on the
non-stiffly-accurate LIRK4, has an empty joint region.

The paper shows that the
[MERK methods](/methods/multirate-exponential-methods.md) are explicit
IMEX-MRI-SR methods ($\Gamma=0$) and proves with the new conditions that MERK2–4
keep their orders for **nonlinear** fast parts (MERK5 at least order 4). On the
multirate [Prothero–Robinson problem](/benchmarks/prothero-robinson-problem.md)
all methods reach their design orders; on a stiff
[Brusselator](/benchmarks/brusselator.md) SR2(1) beats Lie–Trotter and
Strang–Marchuk [splittings](/methods/operator-splitting.md) by two to three orders
of magnitude in error at equal runtime, SR3(2) is the most efficient method, and
with the H-M controller of [Fish & Reynolds (2023)](/sources/fish-2023.md) the
adaptive SR3(2) is generally the cheapest in fast evaluations. The class is a new branch of
[multirate infinitesimal methods](/methods/multirate-infinitesimal-methods.md).

# Key claims

1. IMEX-MRI-SR methods restart each fast problem at $t_n$ ($v_i(0)=y_n$, $\theta\in[0,c_iH]$) and follow it by an implicit slow solve; this removes the sorted-abscissae requirement of IMEX-MRI-GARK and "dramatically" simplifies the order conditions, permitting embeddings (Def. 1.1, Eq. 1.5; §1.1; §7). **Derived**
2. All existing implicit MRI-GARK and IMEX-MRI-GARK methods need non-decreasing $c$, uncommon for RK methods of order above 2, and trial-and-error padding stages; "no authors have successfully created IMEX-MRI-GARK methods with embeddings" (§1.1). **Stated**
3. Lie–Trotter and Strang–Marchuk splittings are limited to orders 1 and 2 by their initial-condition coupling, whatever the order of the component solvers (§1.1). **Stated**
4. The method is a GARK method with fast tables $A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm f\}}=C\otimes A^{\{\mathrm f\}}$, $A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm E\}}=A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm I\}}=\sum_k\Omega^{(k)}\otimes A^{\{\mathrm f\}}(c^{\{\mathrm f\}})^k$ and slow tables $A^{\{\mathrm s,\mathrm f\}}=C\otimes b^{\{\mathrm f\}\top}$, $A^{\{\mathrm s,\mathrm E\}}=\bar\Omega$, $A^{\{\mathrm s,\mathrm I\}}=\bar\Omega+\Gamma$, where $C=\operatorname{diag}(c)$, $\bar\Omega=\sum_k\Omega^{(k)}/(k+1)$, assuming the fast method satisfies $b^{\{\mathrm f\}\top}(c^{\{\mathrm f\}})^k=1/(k+1)$ for $k=0,\dots,n_\Omega-1$ (Eqs. 2.5, 2.7). **Derived**
5. With $f^{\{\mathrm f\}}=0$ the method reduces to a stiffly accurate IMEX "slow base method" with $A^{\mathrm E}=\bar\Omega$, $A^{\mathrm I}=\bar\Omega+\Gamma$ and weights their last rows; a base method that is not stiffly accurate is padded by appending $b^\top$ as a last row (§2.1, Remark 2.2). **Derived**
6. First order needs no coupling conditions beyond a first-order base method (Remark 2.1). **Stated** (from GARK theory)
7. Internal consistency holds if $\Omega^{(0)}\mathbf 1=c$, $\Omega^{(k)}\mathbf 1=0$ for $k\ge1$ and $\Gamma\mathbf 1=0$ (Thm. 2.3, Eq. 2.11). **Proved**
8. With internal consistency and second-order base and fast methods there are no further second-order coupling conditions (Remark 2.4). **Stated**
9. Third order: an internally consistent method with third-order base method and fast method of order $\max(3,n_\Omega+1)$ is third order iff $e_s^\top\big(\sum_k\frac{1}{(k+1)(k+2)}\Omega^{(k)}\big)c=\frac16$; of the four GARK third-order coupling conditions two hold automatically and two coincide because $A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm E\}}=A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm I\}}$ (Thm. 2.5, Eq. 2.12). **Proved**
10. Fourth order: with a fourth-order base method and a fast method of order $\max(4,n_\Omega+2)$, six conditions (2.15a–f) must hold in addition; of the 26 GARK fourth-order coupling conditions (21 when $A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm E\}}=A^{\{\mathrm f,\mathrm I\}}$), (2.16a–d) hold automatically (Thm. 2.6). **Proved**
11. Third order needs $n_\Omega\ge2$: with $n_\Omega=1$ the base condition $e_s^\top\Omega^{(0)}c=\frac12$ and the coupling condition $e_s^\top\Omega^{(0)}c=\frac13$ are incompatible (Thm. 2.7). **Proved**
12. Linear stability function on $y'=(\lambda^{\{\mathrm f\}}+\lambda^{\mathrm E}+\lambda^{\mathrm I})y$, $z^{(\cdot)}=H\lambda^{(\cdot)}$: $R_{\mathrm{mr}}=e_s^\top\big(I-(z^{\mathrm E}+z^{\mathrm I})\mathcal N(z^{\{\mathrm f\}})-z^{\mathrm I}\Gamma\big)^{-1}\phi_0(cz^{\{\mathrm f\}})$ with $\mathcal N(z)=\sum_k k!\operatorname{diag}\big(\phi_{k+1}(cz)\big)\Omega^{(k)}$ (Eq. 3.4, registry $\phi_k$). **Derived**
13. The full joint stability region (3.5) "may be overly-restrictive", since it demands A($\beta$)-stability in the implicit part and A($\alpha$)-stability in the fast part at once; implicit-only and explicit-only regions (3.6), (3.7) are also used (§3). **Stated**
14. IMEX-MRI-SR2(1): order 2, embedding order 1, 4 stages, $n_\Omega=1$, 3 implicit solves, $c=(0,\frac35,\frac4{15},1)$; free coefficients maximize the joint region, the embedding minimizes the Prince–Dormand C-statistic; $\mathcal J^{\mathrm I}_{\alpha,10^2}$ is A-stable for $0°\le\alpha\le45°$, about A(80°)-stable at $\alpha=65°$ and an enclosed bubble at $\alpha=85°$ (§4.1, Fig. 1, App. A). **Shown**
15. IMEX-MRI-SR3(2): order 3, embedding order 2, 5 stages, $n_\Omega=2$, 4 implicit solves, $c=(0,\frac{23}{34},\frac45,\frac{17}{15},1)$; $\mathcal J^{\mathrm I}_{\alpha,10^2}$ is A-stable for every plotted $\alpha$ except 85° (§4.2, Fig. 2, App. B). **Shown**
16. IMEX-MRI-SR4(3): order 4, embedding order 3, 7 stages, $n_\Omega=2$, 5 implicit solves, built from LIRK4 (Calvo, de Frutos & Novo 2001) padded to stiffly accurate form, so the last stage has no implicit solve; the joint region (3.5) is empty for every tried choice of free parameters and $\mathcal J^{\mathrm I}$ is not A-stable for $\alpha\ne0$, which the authors "suspect" comes from the missing last-stage solve (§4.3, Fig. 3, App. C). **Shown** (cause **Stated**)
17. None of the three embeddings needs an extra implicit solve ($\hat\gamma_s=0$) (§4, App. A–C). **Stated**
18. MERK methods (Luan, Chinomona & Reynolds 2020) are explicit IMEX-MRI-SR methods ($\Gamma=0$), derived for a linear fast part $f^{\{\mathrm f\}}=Ly$ (§5). **Derived**
19. MERK2 (3 stages) and MERK3 (4 stages) satisfy all coupling conditions up to order 3 with second- and third-order base methods, so they keep orders 2 and 3 for nonlinear $f^{\{\mathrm f\}}$ (§5.1–5.2, Eqs. 5.1–5.2). **Proved** (by checking the conditions)
20. MERK4 as an IMEX-MRI-SR method has 7 stages and $n_\Omega=3$; it satisfies all coupling conditions through order 4 with a fourth-order base iff $c_6=(3-4c_5)/(4-6c_5)$, which Luan et al.'s abscissae $c_5=\frac56$, $c_6=\frac13$ satisfy (§5.3, App. D). **Proved**
21. MERK5 has 11 stages and $n_\Omega=4$; it satisfies all coupling conditions through order 4 with a fifth-order base iff a condition on $c_9$ holds, met by Luan et al.'s choice; fifth-order coupling conditions are not available, so only "at least fourth order" is proved (§5.4, App. E). **Proved** (order 4); order 5 **Shown**
22. The explicit joint regions of MERK4 and MERK5 are empty, which the authors read as "a deficiency in these definitions of joint stability" rather than in the methods (§5.3–5.4). **Stated**
23. Multirate Prothero–Robinson convergence rates: MERK2 2.06, MERK3 3.17, MERK4 4.25, MERK5 5.32 (nonlinear fast part); Lie–Trotter 1.01, Strang–Marchuk 1.98, SR2(1) 2.01, SR3(2) 3.11, SR4(3) 3.85, IMEX-MRI-GARK3a 3.05, 3b 3.09, 4 4.11 (§6.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
24. Stiff Brusselator at fixed step, least-squares rates on 201/801 grid points: Lie–Trotter 0.76/0.72, Strang–Marchuk 1.59/1.24, SR2(1) 2.00/2.01, SR3(2) 3.09/2.90, SR4(3) 3.00/1.90, GARK3a 3.36/2.71, GARK3b 3.25/2.69, GARK4 3.41/2.42; both splittings and both fourth-order methods show significant [order reduction](/definitions/order-reduction.md) (§6.2.1, Fig. 6). **Shown**
25. Fixed-step stability limits on the stiff Brusselator: SR4(3) is unstable for $H>1/320$ (201 points) and $H>1/640$ (801 points); IMEX-MRI-GARK4 for $H>1/80$ and $H>1/160$ (§6.2.1). **Shown**
26. SR2(1) gives errors two to three orders of magnitude smaller than the splittings at equal runtime; SR3(2) is the most efficient method on the 801-point grid for errors between about $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-11}$; the third-order methods beat the fourth-order ones, and SR4(3) has a slight consistent edge over IMEX-MRI-GARK4 (§6.2.1). **Shown**
27. With adaptive steps (Constant-Constant controller, gains 0.42 and 0.44, LASA-mean fast error, tolerances split in halves) SR3(2) is generally the most efficient in fast evaluations and SR2(1) the least; SR4(3) sometimes stalls (same error at different costs), attributed to an embedding that minimizes $\lVert\hat\tau^{(4)}\rVert_2$ instead of the C-statistic; SR3(2) failed at tolerances $10^{-3},10^{-5}$ and SR4(3) at $10^{-4},10^{-5}$ through oscillating accept/reject (§6.2.2, Fig. 7). **Shown**
