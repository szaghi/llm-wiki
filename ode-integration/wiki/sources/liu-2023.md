# Summary

The paper builds a fully discrete scheme for the compressible Navier–Stokes
equations that is high order in space ($Q^k$ elements, $k=1,2,3$, on uniform
rectangular meshes), conservative, and
[positivity preserving](/properties/positivity-preservation.md) for density and
internal energy under a hyperbolic step bound $h=\mathcal O(\Delta x)$, instead
of the $h=\mathcal O(\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta x^2)$ of fully explicit
positivity-preserving schemes.

Time integration is deliberately low order. Following Guermond et al., one step
is a Strang [splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md) $H(h/2)\,P(h)\,H(h/2)$
between the compressible Euler part and a parabolic part (viscous and heat terms,
density frozen). The Euler half steps use Shu's SSP-RK3 (see
[SSP methods](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md)) with the
Zhang–Shu scaling limiter, positive because SSP-RK3 is a convex combination of
positive forward Euler steps. The parabolic step, written in velocity and
internal energy, applies Crank–Nicolson (implicit midpoint) to the velocity and a
first-order linearly implicit step (backward Euler for the heat term, viscous
heating at the midpoint velocity) to the energy: two decoupled linear systems.
The scheme is at most first order in time; the authors call it
"implicit-explicit" although it is a splitting, not an additive IMEX tableau.

Total energy is conserved because the IPDG forms are chosen so that the discrete
kinetic-energy loss of the midpoint step equals the discrete viscous heating
(Theorem 1). Positivity of the implicit step follows from monotonicity
(inverse-positivity) of its system matrix: proved here to be an M-matrix for the
$Q^1$ IIPG Laplacian for every $h$, and taken from earlier work for the $Q^2$/$Q^3$
spectral-element Laplacian, where it needs a **lower** step bound
$h>\frac{\mathrm{Re}}{3\lambda_{\mathrm h}}\max\rho\,\Delta x^2$. An adaptive
algorithm halves the explicit step on a positivity failure of a cell average and
**doubles** the implicit step on negative internal energy. Spatial orders 2, 2, 4
are observed for $Q^1$, $Q^2$, $Q^3$; shock tubes, a Sedov blast, shock
diffraction and Mach 10 reflection problems at Re 100–1000 run without other
limiters. The fit with this bundle is marginal: the paper is mainly about the
spatial discretization.

# Key claims

1. One step is the Strang splitting $U^n\xrightarrow{H,\,h/2}U^H\xrightarrow{P,\,h}U^P\xrightarrow{H,\,h/2}U^{n+1}$ into a hyperbolic (Euler) and a parabolic subproblem (§2.1, Eq. (4)). **Stated**
2. The hyperbolic substeps use Shu's third-order SSP Runge–Kutta method in Shu–Osher form with step $h/2$ (Eq. (5)); positivity of the DG Euler step follows because SSP-RK3 is a convex combination of forward Euler steps, each positive under the CFL condition (17) with the Zhang–Shu limiter (§3.1). **Stated** (cited)
3. The parabolic substep applies Crank–Nicolson to the velocity and a first-order semi-implicit step to the internal energy, as two decoupled linear systems (6a), (6b) for $u^*$ and $e^P$, then $u^P=2u^*-u^H$ (§2.1, Step 3). **Derived**
4. The scheme is at most first order in time and fourth order in space with $Q^3$; no temporal convergence study is reported (§1.4, §5). **Stated**
5. Theorem 1: if the quadratures of both subproblems are exact for polynomials of degree $k$, the fully discrete $Q^k$ scheme conserves density, momentum and total energy (proof for periodic boundaries) (§2.3). **Proved**
6. Total-energy conservation hinges on the midpoint: testing (9a) with $u^*_h=(u^P_h+u^H_h)/2$ gives the kinetic-energy identity (11), whose dissipation is cancelled exactly by the heating source of (9c) tested with 1 (§2.3, Eqs. (11)–(12)). **Proved**
7. Global conservation needs the NIPG discretization of the stress terms (§1.5, §2.2). **Proved** (part of Theorem 1)
8. Lemma 1: if the hyperbolic solution is admissible on the limiter points and the limiter is applied at the Gauss–Lobatto points, the $L^2$ projection to primitive variables keeps density and $\rho e$ above $\epsilon$ at every Gauss–Lobatto point (§3.2). **Proved**
9. Lemma 2: if density and $\rho e$ are at least $\epsilon$ at all Gauss–Lobatto points after the parabolic step, the projection back and limiting give an admissible state (concavity of $\rho e$, Jensen's inequality) (§3.2). **Proved**
10. Monotonicity reduction: for $(M_{\mathrm d}+hL_{\mathrm d})x=r$ with $M_{\mathrm d}$ diagonal positive, $L_{\mathrm d}\mathbf 1=0$ and $M_{\mathrm d}^{-1}r\ge\epsilon$, monotonicity of $M_{\mathrm d}+hL_{\mathrm d}$ implies $x\ge\epsilon$, since the rows of $(I+hM_{\mathrm d}^{-1}L_{\mathrm d})^{-1}$ are convex weights (§3.3). **Proved**
11. Lemma 3: with $(k+1)^d$-point Gauss–Lobatto quadrature and any penalties $\sigma_{\mathrm p},\tilde\sigma_{\mathrm p}\ge0$, every entry of the right side of (13) is positive when $\rho^H_h,e^H_h>0$ at the Gauss–Lobatto points (§3.3.1). **Proved**
12. The $Q^1$ IIPG system matrix is a nonsingular M-matrix **for every $h$** (Appendix A; 1D for $\tilde\sigma_{\mathrm p}>\frac12$, 2D for $\tilde\sigma_{\mathrm p}>\frac{\sqrt2}2$), so $e^H_h>0\Rightarrow e^P_h>0$ unconditionally in $h$ (§3.3.1, Remark 2). **Proved**
13. This is claimed to be the first M-matrix structure proven for IPDG beyond one dimension; the 1D case is "well-known" (§1.5, App. A). **Stated**
14. For the $Q^2$ spectral-element Laplacian, monotonicity of the system matrix of (15) holds under the lower bound $h>\frac{\mathrm{Re}}{3\lambda_{\mathrm h}}\max_{ij}\rho^H_{ij}\Delta x^2$ (Eq. (16a)), from Li & Zhang (2020) (§3.3.2). **Stated** (from a cited result)
15. For $Q^3$ monotonicity holds "in principle" under $h>C(\mathrm{Re},\lambda_{\mathrm h},\rho^H_h)\Delta x^2$ (Eq. (16b)), by extending a cited proof for $-\Delta u$ to $-\Delta u+cu$; the extension is not carried out (§3.3.2). **Stated**
16. The bounds (16) are lower bounds, so $h$ cannot be as small as $\Delta x^2$; with the upper CFL bound (17) they can be met together if $\Delta x$ is small enough (§3.3.2, §3.4). **Stated**
17. The forward-Euler positivity CFL of the hyperbolic part is $\frac{h^H}{\Delta x}\max_e\alpha_e\le\frac12\hat\omega$, $\hat\omega=\frac1{N(N-1)}$, $N$ the smallest integer with $2N-3\ge k$ (Eq. (17)). **Stated**
18. Adaptive stepping (Algorithms H and CNS): trial step (18); the SSP-RK3 stages restart with **halved** step when a cell average leaves the admissible set, and the parabolic step restarts with **doubled** $h$ when a negative internal energy appears; the sufficient conditions (16)–(17) exclude an endless loop (§3.4). **Derived**
19. The parabolic recomputation was triggered only in the first few steps of the $Q^2$ and $Q^3$ Sedov blast runs, never elsewhere (Remark 2). **Shown**
20. Spatial accuracy with $h=2^{-4}\cdot10^{-4}$, Re $=1$, $\lambda_{\mathrm h}=1$, $T=0.1024$: density rates $Q^1$ 1.693, 1.929; $Q^2$ 1.830, 1.927; $Q^3$ 2.996, 3.887; observed orders 2, 2 (NIPG suboptimal for even degree) and 4 (§4.1, Table 1). **Shown**
21. $Q^3$ on a coarser mesh ($\Delta x=1/120$) captures the roll-ups of the Mach 10 shock reflection–diffraction at Re $=1000$ better than $Q^1$ at $\Delta x=1/480$, "even though both schemes are first order accurate in time" (Figs. 1, 10). **Shown** (qualitative)
22. Premise: "spatial resolution is more important than the temporal for many fluid dynamics problems" (§1.4). **Stated**
23. With only the positivity limiter, the $Q^3$ scheme is non-oscillatory once the physical diffusion is resolved by mesh refinement (§4.7, Fig. 11). **Shown**
24. Alternatives: a fully implicit staggered pressure-correction scheme is positive and conservative but at most second order in space; fully explicit high-order positivity-preserving schemes need $h=\mathcal O(\mathrm{Re}\,\Delta x^2)$; Guermond et al.'s semi-implicit $P^1$ finite elements are positive under $h=\mathcal O(\Delta x)$ via the M-matrix property (§1.2). **Stated**
25. Forward Euler on the three-point heat stencil is monotone iff $h/\Delta x^2\le\frac12$; backward Euler gives an M-matrix and is monotone for every $h$ (§1.3). **Derived**
26. Apart from a fourth-order compact finite-difference scheme, no high-order scheme has an M-matrix structure; products of M-matrices are monotone ($Q^2$ spectral elements give a product of two, $Q^3$ of four) (§3.3.2). **Stated**
27. Priority claim: "the first time that an implicit conservative positivity-preserving scheme with high order elements like Q2 and Q3 elements is constructed for the compressible NS equations" (§1.5). **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Appendix A, 2D: "when the penalty parameter $\tilde\sigma>\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$, the diagonal entries of $A_{\mathcal D}$ are positive"; the printed diagonal entries are positive for any $\tilde\sigma\ge0$, and the condition is what makes the off-diagonal entries $\frac14-\frac{\tilde\sigma}{2\sqrt2}$ nonpositive. The 1D statement ("$\tilde\sigma>1/2$ … diagonal entries … positive") has the same misattribution. The conclusion stands.
- Appendix A, Lemma 4: "a nonsingular M-matrix if all the row sums of A are nonnegative and at least one row sum is positive" is false for reducible matrices without irreducibility; in the application all row sums are strictly positive, so the conclusion is unaffected (reader's observation).
- Lemma 3 is stated for "any penalty $\tilde\sigma\ge0$", weaker than what the M-matrix proof needs.
- §1.2 names "Grapas et al. in [4]"; ref. [4] is Grapsas et al.
- §1.3 uses $\lambda$ for $\Delta t/\Delta x^2$, elsewhere $\gamma/\mathrm{Pr}$; $e$ is both the internal energy and a mesh face.
- Typos: "postivity" (abstract), "Gauss–Labotto" (p. 4), "homogeneious" (p. 3), "Morever" (p. 5), "double rarefraction" (§4), "patition" (App. A), "contraint", "coefficent" (p. 16), "form time" and "trail Δt" (Algorithm CNS, p. 18), "splitted" (p. 3).
- Theorem 1 asks quadratures "exact for integrating polynomials of degree k", §2.2 "accurate enough for $Q^k$".
