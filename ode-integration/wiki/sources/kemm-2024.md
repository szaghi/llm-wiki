# Summary

Low-Mach and all-Mach finite-volume solvers for the compressible Euler
equations reduce the numerical viscosity on the acoustic waves to the
order of the flow speed, so that pressure fluctuations scale with the
square of the Mach number. As the flow speed vanishes the acoustic waves
are discretized by nearly central differences, and the semi-discrete
spectrum lies on ellipses between the upwind circle and a segment of the
imaginary axis, where the forward Euler method is unstable. The paper asks
which classical explicit time integrator best stabilizes such schemes,
taking as prototypes the low-Mach Roe solver of Fleischmann et al. (2020)
and the author's two blended Mach-consistent solvers.

The linear argument uses [stability regions](/definitions/stability-function.md):
the integrator must be stable on the convex hull of the ellipses, which
requires an imaginary-axis segment. Among minimal-stage explicit
Runge–Kutta methods only RK3 and RK4 have one; among Adams–Bashforth
[linear multistep methods](/methods/linear-multistep-method.md) order three
or higher is needed, with a Courant number that shrinks with the order.
[SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) theory does not
help, since it rests on forward-Euler stability.

The central thesis is heuristic: every explicit Runge–Kutta step begins
with a forward Euler stage, unstable for these discretizations, and this
"unstable first stage value" spoils the nonlinear (limited) scheme, while
multistep methods have no internal stage. It is an informal claim about
[internal stability](/definitions/internal-stability.md), not backed by an
analysis of internal stability polynomials. Experiments on perturbed
uniform low-Mach flow, a subsonic cylinder, a Mach 20 blunt body, the
Richtmyer–Meshkov instability, the Elling test and Kelvin–Helmholtz show
AB3, and often AB2, clearly better than RK3 when a limiter is active,
while at first order in space RK3 behaves as linear theory predicts. The
paper proves no theorem and recommends Adams-type, PECE and general linear
methods with stable stage values.

# Key claims

1. For low-Mach solvers with acoustic viscosity scaling with $\lvert u\rvert$, the eigenvalues of the discretized linear advection operator lie on ellipses between the upwind circle and the central-difference imaginary segment; the time integrator must be stable on their convex hull, which forward Euler cannot guarantee (§3.3, Fig. 1). **Derived**
2. Among minimal-stage explicit Runge–Kutta methods (order 2 with 2 stages, 3/3, 4/4, 5/6), only RK3 and RK4 have stability regions covering the required hull; RK2 does not, and "already the 5th order method loses the main desirable property" (§3.1, §3.3, Fig. 2 left). **Shown**
3. Among Adams–Bashforth methods ($k=1,\dots,5$ past values, root-locus curves) order three or higher is needed (§3.3, Fig. 2 right). **Shown**
4. Runge–Kutta methods keep the forward-Euler Courant number $\nu$, RK3 and RK4 even a somewhat larger one; Adams–Bashforth methods need $\nu$ reduced as the order grows (§3.3). **Stated** (read off Fig. 2)
5. Adams–Bashforth needs one right-hand-side evaluation per step, so Runge–Kutta and Adams–Bashforth of the same order cost "nearly identical" effort in the examples (§3.3). **Stated**
6. Explicit Runge–Kutta methods start every step with a forward Euler stage (strictly lower-triangular $A$), so "they might lack the desired stability when applied to nonlinear problems, whereas multistep methods like Adams-Bashforth do not suffer from this issue" (§§1, 3.1). **Stated** (heuristic, supported only by the experiments)
7. SSP Runge–Kutta and SSP multistep theory does not apply here, because it "hinges on the stability of the explicit Euler scheme", which fails for these solvers (§§3.1–3.2). **Stated**
8. For orders 2–4, Runge–Kutta methods with the same number of stages share one stability polynomial; Heun's RK2 and RK3 are used, and "a few other choices for 3rd order three stage Runge-Kutta" gave no difference (§4). **Shown** (not displayed)
9. Perturbed uniform flow (Mach number $1/20$, noise $10^{-6}$, $t=5$, second order in space): AB2 beats MUSCL–Hancock and RK2, whose perturbations grow and produce a significant density error at the extrapolation boundaries; AB2 is also linearly expected to amplify, but its error is much smaller (§4.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. Same test at third order in time: RK3 does not improve on RK2, while AB3 is "perfect" (no growth); the author reads this as the forward-Euler first stage "ruining" the scheme (§4.1, Fig. 4). **Shown** (interpretation **Stated**)
11. At first order in space (no limiter), RK3 performs as linear stability predicts: neither third-order method shows amplitude growth (§4.1, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. Subsonic cylinder, first order in space, Mach numbers $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-4}$: the original Roe solver is unphysical at low Mach number; the Fleischmann solver is reasonable; AB3 and RK3 behave alike here, which "might explain why this issue ... was not discovered earlier" (§4.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. Cylinder with second-order time integration: RK2 is inferior to AB2 "due to the unstable first stage value", and AB2 to AB3; at Mach number $10^{-3}$ the RK2 solution "is completely worthless due to heavy oscillations" (§4.2, Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
14. Mach 20 blunt body, Fleischmann solver, $t=0.5$: forward Euler, RK2 and RK3 ($\nu=0.45,0.45,0.65$) cannot prevent the carbuncle and give nearly identical results; AB2 and AB3 ($\nu=0.2,0.1$) reduce it, but AB3 still forms one; the Courant numbers are about half the linear limit (§4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown**
15. With the blended all-Mach solver all integrators give "perfect" blunt-body results, differing only in convergence speed to steady state; with one Courant number for all methods the results are identical (§4.3, Fig. 9). **Shown**
16. At $t=2$ with AB3, only the blended solvers prevent the carbuncle; Roe and the original Fleischmann solver do not (§4.3, Fig. 10). **Shown**
17. Richtmyer–Meshkov instability ($t=85$): RK3 shows significant instabilities in the low-density, very-low-Mach region, AB3 does not (§4.4, Fig. 12). **Shown**
18. Elling test: MUSCL–Hancock works; all Runge–Kutta and Adams–Bashforth runs fail with negative internal energy, also with the blended solvers and at first order in space (§4.5, Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. Kelvin–Helmholtz instability ($t=4$): Runge–Kutta and Adams–Bashforth give correct, nearly indistinguishable results, while MUSCL–Hancock shows the expected instability (§4.5, Fig. 14). **Shown**
20. Conclusion: explicit Runge–Kutta methods "are not the best choice" for these solvers; Adams–Bashforth behaves considerably better, and with very high spatial order even Runge–Kutta "may do the job". For PECE and general linear methods "the stability of stage values has to be taken into account since unstable stage values still could ruin the stability of the scheme" (§5). **Stated**
21. Variable-step Adams–Bashforth uses variable coefficients (Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner I, pp. 397–400) with the recurrence unrolled to save storage; the start uses lower-order schemes beginning with forward Euler, "in general a more elaborate procedure would be helpful" (§§3.2, 4). **Stated**
22. Stability for Euler flows is expected to carry over to Navier–Stokes flows when the step is small enough for the viscous part (§1, §3.3 footnote 2). **Stated**
