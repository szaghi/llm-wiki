# Summary

Runge–Kutta methods applied to stiff problems, whether stiff ODEs or
method-of-lines discretizations of initial-boundary-value problems, suffer
[order reduction](/definitions/order-reduction.md): in the stiff regime the
observed rate falls from the classical order $p$ towards the
[stage order](/definitions/stage-order.md) $q$. A
[diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md)
(DIRK) method has $q\le2$, and $q\le1$ when $A$ is invertible. The
[weak stage order](/definitions/weak-stage-order.md) $q_{\mathrm w}$ is a
weaker condition, $b$ orthogonal to the $A$-invariant Krylov space
generated by the stage-order residuals $\tau^{(k)}$, $k\le q_{\mathrm w}$.
It removes order reduction for linear problems with time-independent
operators and is compatible with DIRK structure. Earlier DIRKs with weak
stage order used the eigenvector criterion, which caps $q_{\mathrm w}$ at 3.

The paper takes the minimal-polynomial theory of the companion paper
([Biswas et al. 2024](/sources/biswas-2024.md), cited as [7]), part of the
[weak-stage-order barriers](/results/weak-stage-order-barriers.md), and
reduces the weak-stage-order conditions to a system on a two-dimensional
invariant space. On that system it sets up a constrained polynomial
optimization: minimize the squared residuals of the order-$(p+1)$
[order conditions](/results/runge-kutta-order-conditions.md) subject to
DIRK structure, order $p$, weak stage order $q_{\mathrm w}$,
[stiff accuracy](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md), non-negative abscissae
and [A-stability](/properties/a-stability.md) sampled on the imaginary
axis. The optimization yields the three
[DIRK schemes with high weak stage order](/methods/dirk-high-weak-stage-order.md)
DIRK-(7,4,4), DIRK-(12,5,4) and DIRK-(12,5,5), labelled
$(s,p,q_{\mathrm w})$. They are the first DIRKs with $q_{\mathrm w}>3$.

The tests are the [Prothero–Robinson problem](/benchmarks/prothero-robinson-problem.md)
and linear 1D/2D heat, Schrödinger, advection–diffusion, advection and
fourth-order PDEs. On these the new schemes recover order $p$ in $y$, and
in the spatial derivative when $q_{\mathrm w}=p$, while the weak-stage-order-1
references reduce. Beyond the linear theory the results are mixed. Slowly
varying coefficients behave well. Rapidly varying coefficients still show
reduction. Viscous Burgers gives order 3 only, and on Van der Pol the
reduction is not removed.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ step, $b$, $c$, $A$ the tableau, $\mathbf 1$ ones,
$q$ stage order, $q_{\mathrm w}$ weak stage order, $z=h\lambda$. Labels
$(s,p,q_{\mathrm w})$ give the stages, classical order and weak stage order.

1. The stage order of an irreducible DIRK is at most 2, and at most 1 when
   $A$ is nonsingular (Thm 2.2, quoted with its proof referred to Ketcheson
   et al. 2020). **Stated**
2. For the Prothero–Robinson problem $y'=\lambda(y-g(t))+g'(t)$ the global
   error $e_n=y_n-g(t_n)$ obeys
   $e_{n+1}=R(z)e_n+z\,b^\top(I-zA)^{-1}\mathcal E(t_n)+E_h(t_n)$, with
   $\mathcal E$ the stage truncation errors and $E_h$ the step truncation
   error (Eq. (8)). The middle term causes order reduction. In the stiff
   limit $z\to-\infty$ its expansion produces $b^\top A^{\ell}\tau^{(k)}$ with
   $\ell<0$, which the order conditions do not annihilate (§2). **Derived**
3. The middle term is $O(h^{q_{\mathrm w}+1})$ if
   $b^\top(I-zA)^{-1}\tau^{(k)}=0$ for $k=1,\dots,q_{\mathrm w}$
   (Eq. (14)). The weak stage order is formulated to ensure this. **Derived**
4. Def. 3.1 ($b\perp\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}$) and Def. 3.2 (an $A$-invariant
   space containing $\tau^{(k)}$, $k\le q_{\mathrm w}$, and orthogonal to $b$)
   are equivalent. Weak stage order is "the most general criterion to
   guarantee (14)" (§3, citing Ketcheson et al. 2020). **Stated**
5. The weak-stage-order expressions are linear combinations of Butcher-form
   order conditions, $b^\top A^j\tau^{(k)}=r_{j+k,k-1}-\frac1k r_{j+k,k}$ with
   $r_{\ell,k}=b^\top A^{\ell-k}c^{k}-k!/(\ell+1)!$. With
   $q_{\mathrm w}\ge p-1$ some order conditions become redundant: 1 of 4 for
   $p=3$, 3 of 8 for $p=4$, 6 of 17 for $p=5$, that is $(p-1)(p-2)/2$
   (Table 2). **Derived**
6. (From [7].) A DIRK with invertible $A$ whose minimal polynomial $\mu_{\mathcal K}$ of
   $\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}$ has $\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}\le1$ has $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$
   (Thm 4.1). This generalizes the eigenvector-criterion barrier.
   **Stated** (proof in the companion paper)
7. (From [7].) An $s$-stage DIRK with $n_c$ distinct abscissae, order
   $p\ge1$ and $q_{\mathrm w}\le2n_c-1$ satisfies
   $s-p+1-\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}\ge\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}\ge\lfloor q_{\mathrm w}/2\rfloor$,
   with $\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}=1$ if stiffly accurate and 0 otherwise (Thm 4.2).
   **Stated**
8. (From [7].) For an irreducible DIRK with invertible $A$,
   $q_{\mathrm w}>3$ and $\deg\mu_{\mathcal K}=2$, $\mu_{\mathcal K}(x)=(x-a_{11})(x-a_{22})$ (Thm 4.3,
   Eq. (21)). **Stated**
9. With $\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}=2$ the weak-stage-order conditions
   reduce to eigenvector relations (23) when $a_{11}\ne a_{22}$, or
   generalized-eigenvector relations (24) when $a_{11}=a_{22}$. They also
   require $\tau^{(k)}=\beta^{(k)}_1w_1+\beta^{(k)}_2w_2$ for
   $k=2,\dots,q_{\mathrm w}$ (25) and $b^\top w_1=b^\top w_2=0$ (26) (§4).
   This choice caps $q_{\mathrm w}\le5$ and needs
   $s\ge p+1+\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}$. **Derived**
10. $r$-confluent DIRKs ($c_1=\dots=c_r$, $2\le r\le s$) satisfy the
    weak-stage-order equations but are S-reducible to a smaller scheme
    (Lemma 4.4). **Proved**
11. Theorem 4.2 implies that order 4 with $q_{\mathrm w}=4$ needs at least 5
    stages, and order 5 with $q_{\mathrm w}=4$ or 5 at least 6. These bounds
    may not be sharp (§5.1). **Derived**
12. The searches found schemes of type (7,4,4), (12,5,4) and (12,5,5). They
    found no schemes with the same $(p,q_{\mathrm w})$ and fewer stages under
    the imposed constraints (A-stability, stiff accuracy, $|a_{ij}|\le20$)
    (§5.1). **Shown** (search evidence only)
13. The three schemes are stiffly accurate. A-stability was checked a
    posteriori, since only the sampled condition (CR.6) was imposed
    (Fig. 1: $|R(iy)|\le1$), so they are L-stable. Their error constants are
    optimized (§5.1, App. A). **Shown**
14. Restricting to branch (23) ($a_{11}\ne a_{22}$) excludes SDIRKs. A
    preliminary exploration of branch (24) with
    $\dim\mathcal K_{q_{\mathrm w}}>2$ found that SDIRKs with high weak
    stage order exist (Remark 5.1). **Stated** (no scheme given)
15. Prothero–Robinson with $\lambda=-10^4$: the weak-stage-order-1 schemes
    converge at about $q_{\mathrm w}$ for large $h$ and at $p$ for small $h$.
    DIRK-(7,4,4) and DIRK-(12,5,5) converge at $p$ throughout (§6.1,
    Fig. 2). **Shown**
16. Methods with $q_{\mathrm w}=p$ for ODEs and $q_{\mathrm w}=p-1$ for PDEs
    converge at rate $p$ in $y$. For PDEs the spatial derivatives can still
    reduce when $q_{\mathrm w}=p-1$, and $q_{\mathrm w}=p$ also fixes the
    derivatives (§6). **Stated** / **Shown**
17. Heat equation: time stepping creates numerical boundary layers of width
    $O(h^{1/2})$, so half an order is lost in $y_x$ when $q_{\mathrm w}<p$.
    DIRK-(7,4,4) and DIRK-(12,5,5) reach full order in $y$ and $y_x$.
    DIRK-(12,5,4) reaches full order in $y$ and 4.5 in $y_x$ (§6.2, Fig. 3).
    Schrödinger shows the same pattern (§6.3, Fig. 4). **Shown**
18. Advection–diffusion with a physical boundary layer ($\nu=10^{-3}$, the
    viscosity): physical boundary layers do not interfere with the
    weak-stage-order remedy (§6.4, Fig. 5). **Shown**
19. Linear advection: the numerical boundary layer has thickness $O(h)$, so
    a full order is lost in $y_x$ when $q_{\mathrm w}<p$. DIRK-(12,5,4)
    gives order 5 in $y$ and 4 in $y_x$. The weak-stage-order-1 references
    unexpectedly show order 3 instead of 2, which is unexplained (§6.5,
    Fig. 6). **Shown**
20. Fourth-order PDE $y_t=-y_{xxxx}+f$ with two boundary conditions per side:
    boundary layers of width $O(h^{1/4})$, a quarter order lost per
    derivative when $q_{\mathrm w}<p$. Schemes with $q_{\mathrm w}=p$ recover
    full order in $y$ and $y_x$ (§6.7, Fig. 8). **Shown**
21. Heat equation with space-dependent coefficient, and 2D
    advection–diffusion on a square with corners: the high-weak-stage-order
    schemes recover the expected orders (§6.6, §6.8, Figs. 7, 9). **Shown**
22. Time-dependent coefficient: for slowly varying $\kappa=\cos(0.1t+0.2)$
    order reduction is practically removed. For rapidly varying
    $\kappa=1+0.5\cos(30t+0.1)$ it persists, with smaller errors than the
    weak-stage-order-1 references (§7.1, Fig. 10). **Shown**
23. Viscous Burgers ($\nu=0.1$, exact solution $\cos t$): the
    high-weak-stage-order schemes converge at order 3, better than
    weak-stage-order 1 but below $p$. Ketcheson et al. (2020) had shown
    clean full order on this problem for $q_{\mathrm w}\le3$ DIRKs (§7.2,
    Fig. 11). **Shown**
24. Van der Pol ($\mu=500$): high weak stage order does not remove order
    reduction in the stiff range $10^{-2}<h<10^{-1}$ (§7.3, Fig. 12).
    **Shown**
25. Conclusion: weak stage order can exceed 3 for DIRKs. Globally optimal
    solvers for the polynomial problem, minimal-stage schemes, and
    EDIRK/SDIRK/explicit methods with high weak stage order remain open
    (§8). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* Table 3 was checked
independently. It satisfies $c=A\mathbf 1$, $a_{sj}=b_j$, the order conditions to order 4
(resp. 5) and $b^\top A^j\tau^{(k)}=0$ for $k\le q_{\mathrm w}$, all to
about $10^{-15}$. DIRK-(7,4,4) has order exactly 4. The two 12-stage
schemes have order 5 but not 6.

# Printed slips

- Fig. 10 caption prints "κ(x, t) = 1 + 0.5 cos(20t)", whereas the text
  (§7.1) and the panel titles give $1+0.5\cos(30t+0.1)$.
- Fig. 9 caption says "two-dimensional linear advection equation", whereas
  §6.8 treats a linear advection–**diffusion** equation. The figure also
  plots $\lvert\nabla u\rvert$, which the text does not discuss.
- §3: "the related conditions (cf. (1))" — Eq. (1) is the ODE; Table 1 is
  probably meant.
- Theorem 4.2: "(with $K_q$ and $P(x)$ defined in (15) and (20))" — $P$ is
  defined around (18)–(19).
- Notation overloads: $\phi$ names both the Prothero–Robinson solution and
  the residuals $\phi_{\ell,k}$; $B$ names both $B(\xi)$ and the
  restriction matrix of (18); $C$ names both $C(\xi)$ and
  $\mathrm{diag}(c)$. The paper also writes $\vec\tau_k$ in (20) and
  $\vec\tau^{(k)}$ elsewhere, and $\vec w^{(1)}$ in §5.1 and $\vec w_1$ in §4.
- Typos: "schems" (§7.2), "forRunge–Kutta" (ref. [27]), "a 5th DIRK
  scheme" (§5.1, "order" missing).
- §5.1 writes "a 4th order DIRK scheme with WSO 4 requires at least five
  stages", which uses $\delta_{\mathrm{SA}}=0$. With the stiff accuracy that
  the paper imposes, Theorem 4.2 gives 6 stages for (4,4) and 7 for (5,4)
  and (5,5). This is consistent but easy to misquote.
