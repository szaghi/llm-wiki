# Summary

The paper proves, for the first time for an explicit Runge–Kutta
discontinuous Galerkin (RKDG) method of order higher than one on a nonlinear
scalar conservation law $u_t+g(u)_x=0$ in 1-D, a
[fully discrete cell entropy inequality](/results/fully-discrete-cell-entropy-inequality.md)
for the square entropy $\eta(u)=u^2/2$:
$$\int_{I_j}\frac{\eta(u^{n+1})-\eta(u^n)}{h}\,dx+\hat{\mathcal F}_{j+\frac12}-\hat{\mathcal F}_{j-\frac12}\le0 .$$
It holds under the standard CFL condition $h\sim\Delta x$, on non-uniform
meshes, without smoothness assumptions. Summed over cells it gives $L^2$
[strong stability](/properties/strong-stability.md). This is a local form of
[entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md).

The time integrators are the one-parameter family of explicit three-stage
third-order RK methods with $c_3=a_{31}+a_{32}=1$ and positive weights,
parameterized by $c_2\in(1/3,2/3)$; $c_2=1/2$ is Kutta's method. The family
excludes the [SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md)
method SSP-RK3, for which a companion paper shows by counterexample that
$L^2$ stability fails with $P^2$ elements on Burgers under $h\sim\Delta x$.
The main results are for [Burgers' equation](/benchmarks/burgers-equation.md)
with $P^2$ and $P^3$ elements (Theorems 3 and 5); an appendix treats $P^1$
elements and strictly convex fluxes that extend to entire functions
(Theorem A.2), with a CFL constant that depends on $\lVert u\rVert_\infty$.

The proof has three steps. The [Runge–Kutta energy identity](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md)
splits the cell energy change into stage entropy terms, bounded by the
Jiang–Shu semi-discrete argument with a negative jump term, plus a quadratic
form with coefficients $b_i(b_l-2a_{il})$. The "Splitting Miracle"
(Theorem 1) writes that form, for $c_3=1$, as two negative squares of first
temporal differences plus one positive square of a second difference; the
identity is algebraic and holds for any nonlinear right-hand side. Finally
the positive term is dominated near the origin by local analysis in thin
pyramids around the singular lines, compactness elsewhere, and, for $P^3$, a
Gröbner-basis certificate. No numerical experiments are reported.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ is the step (source $\Delta t$), $Y_i$ the stage
values (source $y_i$), $c_2=a_{21}$ the family parameter (source $\omega$),
$\eta(u)=u^2/2$ the square entropy (source $\mathcal U$). The PDE flux is
written $g$ (source $f$), the scaled DG operator $\mathcal N[u]$ (source
$F[u]$), with $u_t=\mathcal N[u]/\Delta x_j$ on cell $I_j$; the mesh ratio
$h/\Delta x_j$ (source $\lambda_j$) is written out. The Lax–Friedrichs
viscosity $\alpha$ and the operators $P_1,P_2,P_3$ and differences $Q,S,R$
are page-local (see Notation map).

1. Explicit three-stage RK with $a_{31}+a_{32}=1$ and $b_1,b_2,b_3>0$ has
   $a_{21}=c_2$, $a_{31}=\frac{1}{c_2}\frac{1-3c_2(1-c_2)}{3c_2-2}$,
   $a_{32}=-\frac1{c_2}\frac{1-c_2}{3c_2-2}$,
   $b_1=1-\frac{3c_2+1}{6c_2}$, $b_2=\frac1{6c_2(1-c_2)}$,
   $b_3=\frac{2-3c_2}{6(1-c_2)}$, and $b_i>0$ iff $\frac13<c_2<\frac23$;
   $c_2=\frac12$ is Kutta's method (§2, Eq. (12)). **Stated** (cited to
   Butcher). These coefficients give $a_{31}+a_{32}=1$, $\sum_ib_i=1$, the
   three conditions of order three, and Kutta's tableau
   $a_{31}=-1$, $a_{32}=2$, $b=(\frac16,\frac23,\frac16)$ at $c_2=\frac12$
   *(compiler check)*.
2. Theorem 1 ("A Splitting Miracle"): if $a_{31}+a_{32}=1$,
   $$\begin{aligned}\sum_{i,l=1}^3b_i(b_l-2a_{il})\langle\mathcal N[Y_i],\mathcal N[Y_l]\rangle_j={}&-\tfrac{1-c_2}{12c_2^2}\lVert\mathcal N[Y_1]-\mathcal N[Y_2]\rVert_j^2-\tfrac{c_2}{12(1-c_2)^2}\lVert\mathcal N[Y_2]-\mathcal N[Y_3]\rVert_j^2\\&+\tfrac{4-9c_2+9c_2^2}{36(1-c_2)^2}\bigl\lVert(\tfrac1{c_2}-1)\mathcal N[Y_1]-\tfrac1{c_2}\mathcal N[Y_2]+\mathcal N[Y_3]\bigr\rVert_j^2\end{aligned}$$
   (§3, Eq. (22)). **Proved** ("verified by direct computation"). The
   identity holds exactly as a polynomial identity in $c_2$
   *(compiler check)*.
3. The splitting of Theorem 1 uses only the RK algebra and holds for all
   nonlinear ODEs, not only for DG (§1 end). **Stated**
4. For linear $g$, Theorem 1 gives the cell square-entropy inequality for all
   $P^k$ and all three-stage third-order RK, since these coincide on linear
   ODEs (Remark 1). **Stated**
5. For Burgers with the global Lax–Friedrichs flux, each stage satisfies
   $\langle Y_i,\frac1{\Delta x_j}\mathcal N[Y_i]\rangle_j+\hat{\mathcal F}^{(i)}_{j+1/2}-\hat{\mathcal F}^{(i)}_{j-1/2}\le-\frac\alpha{12}([[Y_i]]^2_{j-1/2}+[[Y_i]]^2_{j+1/2})$
   under (CFL1)–(CFL2) (§3, Eqs. (14)–(19)). **Proved**
6. Under (CFL1) $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le\frac1{2\gamma_k\lvert a_{21}\rvert\alpha}$
   and (CFL2)
   $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le\frac{2}{4\lvert a_{31}\rvert+9\lvert a_{32}\rvert}\frac1{\gamma_k\alpha}$,
   the stage maxima satisfy $\alpha/2\le\max\lvert(Y_i)_{j,l}\rvert\le3\alpha/2$,
   $i=2,3$ (Eqs. (15)–(18)). **Proved**
7. Theorem 2 ($k=2$): for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$,
   depending on $c_2$ and $\varepsilon$, with
   $\sum_iR^{(i)}(U,V,W)^2\le\varepsilon\sum_iQ^{(i)}(U,V,W)^2$ for
   $\lvert U\rvert,\lvert V\rvert,\lvert W\rvert\le\delta$ (§3 statement, §4
   proof). **Proved**
8. Theorem 3 (cell square-entropy inequality, $P^2$): for Burgers, $P^2$ and
   every RK3 with $a_{31}+a_{32}=1$, $b_i>0$, there is $C>0$ depending only on
   $c_2$ such that $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le C/\alpha$ implies the inequality, with
   entropy flux $\hat{\mathcal F}_{j+1/2}=\sum_ib_i\hat{\mathcal F}^{(i)}_{j+1/2}$
   (Eq. (20)) (§3). **Proved**
9. Theorem 4 ($k=3$): $\sum R^2\le\varepsilon\sum Q^2+\varepsilon\sum S^2$ near
   the origin; unlike $k=2$ the $S$ term is needed, on the pyramid around the
   constant direction (§3, §5). **Proved**
10. Theorem 5 (cell square-entropy inequality, $P^3$): the statement of
    Theorem 3 for $P^3$ (§3). **Proved**
11. Lemma 1: for $c_2\ne0$ the cubic leading parts of $Q^{(i)}$ vanish
    together iff $U=V=W$ or $U=-\frac23V=W$, i.e. on two lines, and the ratio
    $\sum R^2/\sum Q^2$ is singular along them in every neighbourhood of the
    origin (§4). **Proved**
12. Lemma 5 and Corollary 1: for $k=3$, $P_1[u]\equiv0$ on a cell iff the
    point values are in ratio $[1:1:1:1]$, $[3:3:-4:3]$ or $[3:-4:3:3]$,
    exactly the common zeros of the cubic parts (§5). **Proved**
13. Lemma 6 is proved by a reduced Gröbner basis (lexicographic order) of
    four polynomials in three variables, which is $\{1\}$, with an explicit
    integer certificate (§5, pp. 24–26). **Proved** (computer-algebra
    certificate)
14. The CFL constant of Theorems 3 and 5 is not computed: it is the minimum of
    (CFL1)–(CFL4), and (CFL4) $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le\delta/\alpha$ uses the
    non-constructive $\delta$ of Theorems 2 and 4 (§3). **Proved** (existence
    only)
15. (CFL3) $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le\frac{\min\{b_1,b_2,b_3\}}{6C_4}\frac1\alpha$
    makes the jump term (33) non-positive; positive weights are essential
    here (§3). **Proved**
16. Theorem A.2 (cell square-entropy inequality, $P^1$): for $g$ entire on
    $\mathbb C$ and strictly convex on $\mathbb R$, and every RK3 with
    $a_{31}+a_{32}=1$, $b_i>0$, there is $C(\lVert u\rVert_\infty)>0$,
    depending only on $\lVert u\rVert_\infty$ and $c_2$, such that
    $\max_jh/\Delta x_j\le C(\lVert u\rVert_\infty)$ implies the inequality
    (App. A). **Proved**
17. For $P^1$ the stage jump bound has constant $\alpha/16$ instead of
    $\alpha/12$ (Lemma A.4, Eq. (A.3)) under a condition (CFL1') involving
    $\max\lvert g'\rvert$ on a complex circle (App. A). **Proved**
18. Lemma A.6: for $P^1$ and strictly convex $g$,
    $P_1[u]_x=-(u^-_{j+1/2}-u^+_{j-1/2})^2g''(\xi)/\Delta x_j\le0$, with
    equality iff $u$ is constant on the cell (App. A.1). **Proved**
19. Before this paper there was no proof of the cell square-entropy
    inequality under $h\sim\Delta x$ for explicit RKDG of order above one on
    nonlinear conservation laws without smoothness assumptions; the
    techniques for linear laws do not extend (§1). **Stated**
20. Jiang & Shu (1994) proved the semi-discrete cell square-entropy
    inequality for DG of any order and general nonlinear flux, which implies
    the fully discrete inequality for implicit algebraically stable RK
    methods (§1). **Stated**
21. Earlier work of the authors: $L^2$ stability under $h\sim\Delta x$ for
    explicit RKDG with SSP-RK2 and $P^1$ on a class of convex laws including
    Burgers; a counterexample shows that $L^2$ stability fails for $P^2$ with a
    class of third-order RK methods including SSP-RK3 on Burgers under
    $h\sim\Delta x$, so no cell square-entropy inequality holds there (§1).
    **Stated** (cited)
22. [Relaxation Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/relaxation-runge-kutta.md), though
    explicit, give no local (cell) entropy inequality, because the relaxation
    parameter is global (§1). **Stated**
23. For first-order monotone schemes the inequality is known for all convex
    entropies; for strictly convex $g$ one strictly convex entropy suffices
    for uniqueness (§1). **Stated**
24. The paper contains no numerical experiments. (Observation.)

  *Interpolation and Approximation*, used correctly in Lemma A.2; relaxation
  RK is ref. [9] (Ketcheson).

  "$(s+sX-3sY,\ -\frac32s+4sY,\ s+sX+3sY)$", but the pyramid definition gives
  first component $s-sX+3sY$; the printed leading form
  $36\omega^2s^6(200X^2+9537Y^2)$ follows from the definition, so the point is
  a typo and the constants are right.
- Proofs of Lemmas 7 and 8, pp. 27–29: sums of $R^{(i)}$ printed with upper
  limit 3 where $k=3$ has four components (checked visually).
- p. 27: bases "perpendicular to $\mathcal L_1$, $\mathcal L_2$ and
  $\mathcal L_2$ respectively"; the last should be $\mathcal L_3$ (checked
  visually).
- Proof of Lemma 1, pp. 16–17: "$V=\frac{U+V}{2}$" (twice) for
  $V=\frac{U+W}{2}$ (checked visually).

  to be missing (not checked visually).
