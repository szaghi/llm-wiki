# Summary

The paper extends Ketcheson's [relaxation Runge–Kutta](/methods/relaxation-runge-kutta.md)
idea to two classes of partitioned methods. The step is rescaled by
$\gamma_n$ and assigned to $t_n+\gamma_nh$, so that the fully discrete
solution satisfies a discrete entropy equality or inequality
([entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md)). The first class is
additive [IMEX Runge–Kutta](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) methods, for stiffness
that separates by scale. The second is the second-order explicit multirate
partitioned method MRK2 of Constantinescu and Sandu, for grid-induced
stiffness on locally refined meshes. MRK2 is a partitioned multirate scheme
outside the [multirate GARK](/methods/multirate-gark.md) formalism, built from the
second-order [SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) Heun
method. It is treated first with two levels and then with any number of 2:1
refinement levels, organized into "level blocks" with an activation table.

Three propositions give the scalar root condition for $\gamma_n$: for IMEX
(2.1), two-level MRK2 (2.2) and multilevel MRK2 (2.3). The condition makes the
step entropy conservative or stable when the spatial discretization of each
partition is. Each proposition also gives the explicit $\gamma_n$ for the
energy $\eta=\frac12\lVert y\rVert^2$. For MRK2 one global $\gamma_n$ is
shared by all zones.

The tests use an ODE with an exponential entropy, the nonlinear pendulum, and
the inviscid [Burgers equation](/benchmarks/burgers-equation.md) with Gaussian
data, discretized by Gassner's skew-symmetric entropy-conservative or
entropy-stable DG ($N=3$) with a linearized-flux IMEX splitting and an
optional limiter. Relaxation-ARK keeps the design orders 2 and 3, while the
incremental direction technique (IDT) loses one order. Both conserve entropy
to $O(10^{-13})$. For MRK2 the IDT order loss appears only once errors
accumulate: at $t=1$, after the shock, IDT-MRK2 errors are at least 60 times
larger and its shock lags. The relaxation guarantee holds only at the
synchronization (coarsest) level, not within stages or substeps. The authors
therefore recommend entropy-stable fluxes for MRK2 on deeply nested meshes,
shown with 10 levels.

# Key claims

Notation: $y'=f(y)=f^{\mathrm E}(y)+f^{\mathrm I}(y)$; $(A^{\mathrm E},b^{\mathrm E},c^{\mathrm E})$
and $(A^{\mathrm I},b^{\mathrm I},c^{\mathrm I})$ the explicit and implicit
tableaux; $Y_{n,i}$ stage values, $f^{\mathrm E}_i$, $f^{\mathrm I}_i$, $f_i$
stage derivatives; $\eta'(Y_{n,i})$ the entropy variable at stage $i$;
$y^{\{z\}}$ the restriction to zone $z$, $y^{\{B,z\}}$ to zone $z$ of level
block $B$ (page-local).

1. Relaxation applies to IMEX-RK: $y^\gamma_{n+1}=y_n+\gamma_nh\sum_i(b^{\mathrm E}_if^{\mathrm E}_i+b^{\mathrm I}_if^{\mathrm I}_i)=\gamma_ny_{n+1}+(1-\gamma_n)y_n$. With entropy-conservative or entropy-stable discretizations of both parts and $\gamma_n$ a root of (14), the method is entropy conserving or stable (Prop. 2.1, Eqs. 12–14). **Proved** (the proof also uses $b^{\mathrm E}_i,b^{\mathrm I}_i\ge0$ and $\gamma_n>0$, which the proposition does not state)
2. For $\eta=\frac12\lVert y\rVert^2$: $\gamma_n=2\lVert y_{n+1}-y_n\rVert^{-2}h\sum_i\langle b^{\mathrm E}_if^{\mathrm E}_i+b^{\mathrm I}_if^{\mathrm I}_i,Y_{n,i}-y_n\rangle$ (Prop. 2.1). **Proved**
3. If $b^{\mathrm E}=b^{\mathrm I}$, the condition reduces to the monolithic form (15) in $f_i$, and $\gamma_n=2\lVert y_{n+1}-y_n\rVert^{-2}h\sum_ib_i\langle f_i,Y_{n,i}-y_n\rangle$ for a nonstationary solution (Cor. 2.1.1). **Proved**
4. For a general entropy, the root equation is a scalar nonlinear equation solvable by Brent's method, Levenberg–Marquardt or Newton (§2.1). **Stated**
5. Relaxation of two-level MRK2 (16) with one $\gamma_n$ for all four zones gives entropy conservation or stability under condition (18) (Prop. 2.2). **Proved**
6. For the quadratic entropy and $y^{\{z\}}_{n+1}\ne y^{\{z\}}_n$: $\gamma_n=2\big(\sum_{z=1}^4\lVert y^{\{z\}}_{n+1}-y^{\{z\}}_n\rVert^2\big)^{-1}h^2\sum_z\sum_ib^{\{z\}}_i\langle f^{\{z\}}_i,(Y^{\{z\}}_{n,i}-y^{\{z\}}_n)/h\rangle$ (Prop. 2.2). **Proved**
7. Multilevel relaxation MRK2 ((20)–(21), any $L_{\max}$) is entropy conserving or stable with $\gamma_n$ from (22); the explicit quadratic-entropy $\gamma_n$ sums over level blocks, zones, substeps and stages (Prop. 2.3). **Proved**
8. IDT, which assigns the relaxed solution to $t_n+h$ instead of $t_n+\gamma_nh$, is one order less accurate than relaxation (Remark 2). **Stated** (prior work); **Shown** in Tables 2–3
9. Exponential-entropy ODE ($y_0=(1,0.5)^\top$, $t\in[0,5]$, $h=0.1$): the entropy change is below $O(10^{-13})$ for Relaxation-ARK and IDT-ARK of orders 2–4 and $O(10^{-3})$ for plain ARK (§3.1, Fig. 3). **Shown**
10. Nonlinear pendulum ($h=0.9$, $t\in[0,1000]$): Relaxation-ARK and IDT-ARK stay on the orbit with entropy error $O(10^{-13})$; plain ARK2/3/4 have $O(1)$ entropy error and drift off (§3.1, Fig. 4). **Shown**
11. Burgers, $t=0.2$, 100 elements: ARK2 and Relaxation-ARK2 converge at order 2.00 with both fluxes, IDT-ARK2 at about 0.94–1.00. At $h=7.813\times10^{-5}$ (EC flux) the errors are $6.27\times10^{-8}$, $5.81\times10^{-8}$ and $8.49\times10^{-6}$ (Table 2). **Shown**
12. Third order: ARK3 and Relaxation-ARK3 reach about 2.98–3.00, IDT-ARK3 2.00–2.01. At $h=7.813\times10^{-5}$ (EC) the errors are $1.20\times10^{-10}$, $1.15\times10^{-10}$ and $3.96\times10^{-8}$ (Table 3). **Shown**
13. Relaxation-ARK errors are slightly lower than plain ARK errors (Tables 2–3). **Shown**
14. Burgers, EC flux, 800 elements, $t\in[0,2]$: relaxation and IDT ARK2/ARK3 conserve energy to $O(10^{-13})$; plain ARK2/3 lose energy slightly, attributed to IMEX acting as a high-frequency filter; explicit RK2 gains energy and blows up at twice its step (Fig. 5, footnote 7). **Shown**
15. ARK2 removes high-frequency Gibbs oscillations much better than RK2 at $t=1$; Relaxation-ARK2 and IDT-ARK2 remove fewer of them than ARK2 (Fig. 6). **Shown**
16. Burgers, ES flux: all methods are entropy stable with or without the limiter; IDT-ARK2 with the limiter misplaces the shock (Figs. 7–8). **Shown**
17. Mass, a linear invariant, is conserved to $O(10^{-14})$ by all IMEX variants with or without the limiter (Fig. 9). **Shown**
18. MRK2 on a mesh refined five times at the centre (2:1, $L_{\max}=5$, 196 elements): at $t=0.2$ MRK2, Relaxation-MRK2 and IDT-MRK2 are all second order with both fluxes. IDT stays second order because $\gamma_n$ differs little from 1 ("$O(10^{-5})$") and its error has not accumulated (§3.3, Table 4). **Shown**
19. At $t=1$ (after the shock, ES flux) the IDT-MRK2 error is at least 60 times the Relaxation-MRK2 error (at $h=1.563\times10^{-4}$: $8.22\times10^{-4}$ against $5.18\times10^{-6}$), and its order drops to 0.95 at the largest step (Table 4). **Shown**
20. MRK2, EC flux, 784 elements: relaxation and IDT (RK2 and MRK2) conserve total energy to $O(10^{-13})$; plain RK2 and MRK2 drift to $O(10^{-1})$ (Fig. 11). **Shown**
21. MRK2, ES flux: all methods are entropy stable, with entropy changes up to $O(10^{-1})$; IDT-MRK2 shows a phase error and a lagging shock, severe with the limiter (Figs. 13–14). **Shown**
22. Mass conservation with MRK2 is $O(10^{-14})$ without the limiter and $O(10^{-12})$ (RK2 family) or $O(10^{-13})$ (MRK2 family) with it (Fig. 15). **Shown**
23. Multirate relaxation is global: entropy conservation or stability is imposed only at the synchronization time, not in stages or substeps. Hence entropy-stable fluxes are recommended over entropy-conservative ones for MRK2, especially on deeply nested meshes (§3.3, §4). **Stated**
24. Relaxation-MRK2 with the ES flux is stable on a 10-level nested mesh (3099 elements, $h=0.002$, $t\in[0,1]$, no limiter) (Fig. 16). **Shown**
25. Relaxation-ARK can reduce instability during stage integration through its implicit correction at each stage; Relaxation-MRK2 cannot (§4). **Stated** (no evidence given)
26. Multirate methods need no linear or nonlinear solves and suit parallel computing when no good preconditioner is available (§4). **Stated**
27. Classical explicit Runge–Kutta and linear multistep methods cannot preserve general quadratic invariants (footnote 2). **Stated**

Unremarked data: in Table 4 (ES, $t=1$) the Relaxation-MRK2 orders are
erratic (2.40, 1.22, 2.53, 2.12), and at $h=6.25\times10^{-4}$ its error
($1.29\times10^{-4}$) exceeds plain MRK2's ($8.35\times10^{-5}$).

# Printed slips

- §2.2 stage solve: "$Q_{n,i}-\tilde a_{ii}\triangle t\,g_j=\dots$" has $g_j$ on the left where $g_i$ is meant.
- Remark 3: $L_i$ is used both as an operator ($L_iQ_{n,i}$ in (11)) and as a vector ($N_i=R_i-L_i$).
- Eq. (17): $b_i$ is printed without the zone superscript that (16b) and (18) carry.
- §2.3.2: "each fast buffer (f z) and slow buffer (sb)": "(f z)" should be "(f b)". The level-0 example advances $2\Delta t$ under the same label $n+1$ as level-1 blocks advancing $\Delta t$; the bookkeeping is unclear.
- Algorithm 2, lines 20–21: `cond = false` is set unconditionally in the third branch, and `idx[i+1]` is read for `i = length(idx)`.
- §3.3: "This agrees with Figure 7" refers to the ARK snapshots; the MRK2 snapshots are Figure 13. Fig. 13(f) is labelled "time = 0.998" for $t=1$.
- Figs. 5 and 14: captions say "total entropy", axes "Total energy". Table 2 caption "K = 100" against "NE = 100" in the text; Table 3 caption "Same as 2".
- Claim 18's "tiny relaxation parameters ($O(10^{-5})$)" means $\lvert\gamma_n-1\rvert$, not $\gamma_n$.
- Footnote 10 has no visible anchor. Fig. 2 contains a leftover slide-style bullet list.
- Abstract: "the relaxation parameter works the Burgers equation"; footnote 3 "sitff".
- References [47] and [49] are the same work (Giraldo, Kelly, Constantinescu 2013, SISC 35(5)); [16] prints "a stable" for "A-stable".
