# Summary

The paper builds a finite-volume solver, second order in space, for the quasi-neutral **extended MHD** equations, which carry Hall drift, electron inertia, displacement current, resistivity and a separate electron-entropy equation. It starts from the relaxation formulation of Zhao, Yang and Seyler and the PERSEUS code, whose results the authors find oscillatory and unable to reproduce the ideal-MHD Brio–Wu solution at high density. The momentum, energy and induction equations are rewritten so that the left-hand side is exactly the ideal-MHD conservation law. All extended physics then sits in source terms proportional to $(\mathbf J-\nabla\times\mathbf B)$ and $(\mathbf E+\mathbf u\times\mathbf B)$, which vanish in the ideal limit. Ideal-MHD Riemann solvers and constrained transport can then be reused.

The time-integration content is a stiff-relaxation [asymptotic-preserving](/definitions/asymptotic-preserving-scheme.md) splitting in the Jin–Xin and Caflisch–Jin–Russo tradition. An explicit [SSP-RK2](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) (Heun) step advances fluxes and non-stiff sources. At each Runge–Kutta sub-stage, a first-order [operator-split](/methods/operator-splitting.md) implicit relaxation step treats the stiff $\mathbf E$–$\mathbf J$ sources. This step reduces to a local $3\times3$ linear system per edge, solved directly without Newton iteration, and is followed by an implicit electron–ion temperature exchange. The implicit update is split by direction because the Hall term couples edge components that are not co-located. AP in the ideal-MHD limit ($\varepsilon_E=v^2/c^2$, $\varepsilon_J=\lambda_e^2/(L_0^2n_e)\to0$ at fixed $h$, $\Delta x$) is shown by a formal limit of the implicit update. That limit holds only if the electric-field flux update uses the updated magnetic field. A linear order analysis of a parameterized split sub-stage scheme concludes that the scheme is first order in time when non-ideal effects matter. Raising that order with an [IMEX](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) scheme is left as future work.

The tests (Brio–Wu at three densities, linear waves, magnetic-field diffusion, whistler and Hall drift waves, blast wave and AMR rotor) verify spatial order and physics. The paper fits an MHD or plasma bundle better; here it is an example of an AP splitting and of the order loss of split relaxation.

# Key claims

Notation: $h$ the step (the source's $\Delta t$); $\varepsilon_E$, $\varepsilon_J$ the relaxation parameters (the source's $\epsilon_E$, $\epsilon_J$); $\eta_{\mathrm{res}}$ the resistivity (the source's $\eta$); bold letters are the physical fields of the source. In claim 13 the eight coefficients of the source's split scheme are renamed (see the notation map), and $y'=-(L^{\mathrm I}+L^{\mathrm E})y$ is the linear test with $L^{\mathrm I}$ treated implicitly.

1. Rewriting extended MHD as Eqs. (8)–(14) puts ideal MHD on the left-hand side; in the ideal limit ($\mathbf E=-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B$, $\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B/\mu_0$) every right-hand side of (9)–(11) vanishes, which preserves the ideal-MHD jump conditions and allows LLF and HLLD Riemann solvers (§2.1, App. A). **Derived**
2. When $v\ll c$ and $\lambda_e\ll L_0$, the $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf J$ equations (15)–(16) relax to the non-relativistic Ampère law $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mathbf J$ (22) and the inertia-less generalized Ohm law $\mathbf E+\mathbf u\times\mathbf B-\frac{\lambda_i}{L_0n}\mathbf J\times\mathbf B=\eta_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf J$ (23) (§2). **Derived**
3. Time stepping: an explicit SSP-RK2 (Heun) predictor–corrector for fluxes and explicit sources, with an operator-split implicit update of the stiff $\mathbf E$, $\mathbf J$ and electron–ion exchange sources at each sub-stage (Algorithm 1; §3). **Stated** (algorithm description)
4. The implicit $\mathbf E$–$\mathbf J$ update (36)–(37) eliminates $\mathbf E^*$ and gives a $3\times3$ linear system $\mathbb A\mathbf J^*=\mathbf b^*$ per edge (38), solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting; there is no nonlinear iteration (Algorithm 1, step 14). **Derived**
5. The relaxation step is linear because the non-stiff variables are frozen during it ($U^{n+1}=U^{n+1/2}$), so "the whole scheme can be implemented without Newton-type iteration, which is an important feature of AP schemes" (§3, Eqs. (41)–(42)). **Derived**
6. The order of convection and relaxation steps is the reverse of the Jin–Xin splitting, justified because the solution can be initialized on the equilibrium manifold (§3, before Eq. (41)). **Stated**
7. AP in the ideal-MHD limit: with $h$ and $\Delta x$ fixed and $\varepsilon_E,\varepsilon_J\to0$ (resistivity tending to zero, Hall term negligible), the implicit update gives $\mathbf E^*=-\mathbf u^*\times\mathbf B^*$ (49) and $\mathbf J^*=\nabla\times\mathbf B^*$ (50), so the non-ideal sources vanish and the ideal-MHD solver is recovered (§3.1, Eqs. (43)–(50)). **Derived** (formal limit of one sub-stage; uniform stability and accuracy in $\varepsilon$ are not proved)
8. If the electric-field flux update is not split from the rest (Algorithm 1, step 11), the limit gives $\mathbf J^*=\nabla\times\mathbf B^n$ instead, leaving a non-zero $(\mathbf J^*-\nabla\times\mathbf B^*)$ source in the next sub-stage, so AP fails (§3.1, after Eq. (50)). **Derived**
9. Relaxation analysis without the Hall term, (67)–(69): for $h\ll\varepsilon_E,\varepsilon_J$ the update is backward Euler on the relaxation ODE; for $h\gg\varepsilon_E,\varepsilon_J$ it reduces to $\mathbf J^{n+1}=\mathbf J_0$, $\mathbf E^{n+1}=\mathbf E_0+\eta_{\mathrm{res}}\mathbf J_0$ (70); second-order expansions (71)–(72) give deviations of order $\varepsilon_J/h$ or $\varepsilon_E/h$ (§3.4). **Derived**
10. "Larger $\Delta t$ results in faster relaxation since more higher-order terms are damped out" (§3.3, referring to Eq. (71)). **Stated** (a statement about the backward-Euler relaxation map, not about accuracy)
11. Quasi-neutrality diagnostic: $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J^{n+1}\approx\frac{\varepsilon_E}{h}\big(\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{res}}\varepsilon_E}{h}-1\big)\nabla\cdot(\mathbf E_0-\mathbf E^*)+\frac{\varepsilon_J\varepsilon_E}{h^2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf J^*$ (73), which tends to zero in the ideal limit; at the first step $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J^1\approx\frac{\varepsilon_J\varepsilon_E}{h}\nabla\cdot(\nabla\cdot\mathbb J)$ (74) (§3.4). **Derived**
12. Temporal order: "The explicit second-order Runge–Kutta (RK2) method achieves second-order accuracy for the unsplit explicit flux and source term updates but only first-order accuracy for the implicit source term update. As a result, the overall temporal accuracy remains first order." (§3.5). **Derived** (through the analysis of claim 13)
13. For the parameterized split sub-stage scheme (75)–(80), $U_1=U^n-\theta_1\frac h\varepsilon R(U_1)$, $U_2=U_1-\vartheta_1h\,DF(U_1)$, $U_3=U_2-\theta_2\frac h\varepsilon R(U_3)-\theta_3\frac h\varepsilon R(U_1)$, $U_4=\omega_1U^n+\omega_2U_3$, $U_5=U_4-\vartheta_2h\,DF(U_4)$, $U^{n+1}=U_5-\theta_4\frac h\varepsilon R(U^{n+1})$, applied to $y'=-(L^{\mathrm I}+L^{\mathrm E})y$, second order requires 7 conditions (84)–(86) in 8 unknowns. The implemented coefficients $(\omega_1,\omega_2)=(\frac12,\frac12)$, $(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4)=(0,1,0,\frac12)$, $(\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2)=(1,\frac12)$ satisfy only the first-order set (84) (§3.5). **Derived** (conditions printed, derivation not shown; method after Caflisch, Jin & Russo)
14. The implicit $\mathbf E$–$\mathbf J$ update is split by direction (done $n$ times in $n$ dimensions, one edge orientation at a time, discarding the perpendicular components produced) to avoid spatial bias from the non-co-located Hall cross product; the update is then no longer fully implicit, which "may affect the stability" (§3.6, Algorithm 2). **Stated**
15. Cell-centre to edge and node interpolation picks the neighbour with the largest density, an "upwinding" that minimizes the Hall velocity, which "helps improve numerical stability" (§3.6). **Stated**
16. The flux Jacobian in primitive form is block lower-triangular (57), so the ideal-MHD eigenvalues are kept and three new wave speeds appear (64)–(65); no characteristic speed involves $c$ (§3.3). **Derived**
17. Time step: $h=C\,\Delta x/\max_i\lvert\lambda_i\rvert$ (94), with the wave speeds (59) and the electron sound speed $u\pm\sqrt{m_i/m_e}\,a$ (66), which the authors found larger than the three new speeds (§3.3, §4). **Stated**
18. A density-dependent factor $\psi(n_i)$ (52) multiplies the TVD limiter; its free parameter $(\lambda_0/\Delta x)^3=0.005$ is chosen on the shock tube, "akin to a local Knudsen number of around 0.17" (§3.2). **Shown** (tuned on Brio–Wu)
19. The electron–ion temperature exchange is updated implicitly in closed form (90)–(93), with the relaxation time frozen at the pre-update temperatures; $T_i$ is recovered from the total pressure with a floor of $10^{-10}$ (§3.9). **Derived**
20. Brio–Wu at $\rho_0=10^4$, $1$, $0.01$: the extended-MHD solver (LLF) matches ideal MHD (Athena++) at high density with $T_i=T_e$ everywhere at $\rho_0=10^4$; at low density it departs from ideal MHD through non-ideal effects; different CFL numbers give consistent solutions (Figs. 4–7, §4.1). **Shown**
21. Ideal-MHD linear waves: second-order $L^1$ convergence in 1D (Fig. 8a) and 2D (Fig. 9). The entropy wave loses second order at very fine resolution with $\varepsilon_E=1.11\times10^{-9}$ and recovers it with $\varepsilon_E=1.11\times10^{-11}$ (Table 2, Fig. 8b), which the authors attribute to the generalized Ohm law "not relaxing to the ideal MHD's Ohm law as the grid resolution increases" (§4.2). **Shown**
22. With Hall or resistive terms on, not all linear waves converge at second order; the slow wave worsens at high resolution with resistivity (§4.2). **Shown**
23. Gaussian field diffusion (resistivity $10^{-6}$, amplitude $10^{-6}$, domain $[-6,6]$, $N$ up to 4096): second-order convergence with LLF and HLLD; $L^1$ errors grow at lower $\rho_0$ (Fig. 10, §4.3). **Shown**
24. The whistler dispersion relation $\omega=k_x^2B/(\mu_0n_ee)$ is reproduced on 2400 points "even at high mode numbers" (Fig. 11, §4.4.1). **Shown**
25. Huba's Hall drift wave is reproduced, with maximum distortion near $x\simeq15$ cm, against Hall MHD and hybrid PIC results (Fig. 12, §4.4.2). **Shown**
26. 2D blast wave and AMR rotor (3 levels, Tóth–Roe divergence-preserving prolongation and restriction) at $\rho_0=1$ and $0.1$: lower density increases the out-of-plane $u_z$ and $B_z$ (Figs. 13–16). **Shown**
27. Future work: "IMEX or linear analysis ... to increase the order of accuracy for the implicit source term update, which is currently at most 1st-order in time" (§5). **Stated**
28. A third-order Shu–Osher SSP Runge–Kutta method could replace SSP-RK2 for the explicit part (§3). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* The model scheme of claim 13 evaluates the second explicit update at the averaged state $U_4$. With $L^{\mathrm I}=0$ and the implemented coefficients it gives $U^{n+1}=(I-\frac12hL^{\mathrm E})^2U^n$, which is first order (the left side of the condition $2\omega_2\vartheta_1\vartheta_2=1$ of (85) evaluates to $\frac12$), while Heun is $U^{n+1}=\frac12U^n+\frac12(U_3-h\,DF(U_3))$. The model therefore does not represent Heun, against the paper's own statement that the RK2 part is second order. The first-order conclusion follows from the model, and not necessarily from Algorithm 1.
