# Summary

The paper builds a [step-size controller](/methods/step-size-control.md) for
implicit time integration of the compressible Navier–Stokes equations in a
high-order discontinuous Galerkin (spectral/hp, Nektar++) solver. The step
is not set by a user-supplied temporal tolerance. It is set by balancing the
local temporal error against the spatial truncation error produced in the
same step. The one-step local error is written, to leading order, as the sum
of the temporal error of an ESDIRK step, $h$ times a $b$-weighted average of
the spatial truncation error, and $h$ times a $b$-weighted average of the
Newton (iterative) error. The step is the largest one for which the temporal
term equals $\beta h$ times the spatial one, $\beta<1$.

The temporal error comes from an
[embedded pair](/methods/embedded-runge-kutta-pair.md) whose embedded
solution is one order *higher* than the propagated one: Kværnø's stiffly
accurate [ESDIRK](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) pairs 2(3),
3(4) and 4(5). The spatial error comes from evaluating the DG operator at
degrees $P+1$ and $P$ on the end-of-step solution. The step is computed per
element and per variable with an elementary controller of exponent $1/p$,
averaged over elements with temporal-error weights, and minimized over
variables. The Newton tolerance of the Jacobian-free Newton–Krylov
[stage solve](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md) is tied to the temporal
error, with a linearized justification.

Tests: the [isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) (Newton
tolerance, error components against fixed-CFL stepping, independence from
the ESDIRK order), a steady flat-plate boundary layer, the
[Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) at $Re=1600$ and
turbulent [flow past a cylinder](/benchmarks/flow-past-cylinder.md) at
$Re=3900$. Without per-case tuning, the adaptive strategy runs within a
CPU-time ratio of 0.96–1.27 of hand-tuned fixed-CFL settings. The error
decomposition is a formal leading-order derivation; the evidence is
numerical.

# Key claims

Notation: the semi-discrete DG system is $y'=f(y)$ (the source's
$L_\delta$); $P$ is the DG polynomial degree, $\Delta x$ the mesh size, $p$
the order of the propagated ESDIRK, $\hat p=p+1$ the embedded order;
$E_t$, $\bar E_s$, $\bar E_{\mathrm{it}}$ the temporal, averaged spatial and
averaged iterative errors.

1. The local error of one implicit RK step is, to leading order, $y_{n+1}^{\mathrm{it}}-y(t_{n+1})\approx E_t+h\bar E_s+h\bar E_{\mathrm{it}}$, with $\bar\psi=\sum_ib_i\psi^{(i)}$; decreasing one term well below the largest does not help (§2.1.5, Eqs. 24–28). **Derived**
2. Balanced criterion: take the largest step with $\lVert E_t\rVert=\beta h\lVert\bar E_s\rVert$, $\beta<1$, so the total error stays spatially dominated and no temporal tolerance is needed (§2.2, Eq. 29). **Stated** (design principle, tested in §3)
3. Spatial error estimate: $\bar E_s\approx f^{P+1}(y_{n+1})-f^{P}(y_{n+1})$, the DG operator at degrees $P+1$ and $P$ (§2.2, Eq. 30). **Derived**
4. The temporal error is estimated with an embedded solution of order $\hat p=p+1$, without local extrapolation; the authors claim it is more accurate than the usual order-$(p-1)$ estimate and needs no problem-dependent calibration (§2.1.2, Eq. 15; §2.4). **Stated**
5. Per element $e$ and variable $m$: $h_{n+1,e,m}=h_n\bigl((\beta h\lVert\bar E_s\rVert_{e,m}+1.5^p\epsilon_m)/(\lVert E_t\rVert_{e,m}+\epsilon_m)\bigr)^{1/p}$ with $\epsilon_m=10^{-12}$ times the RMS of variable $m$; the global step is the temporal-error-weighted average over elements ($r=1$) and then the minimum over variables (Eqs. 33–36). A global $L_2$ norm can be dominated by one variable or by large structures (§2.2). **Stated**
6. Adaptive Newton tolerance $\tau=\eta\lVert e_t\rVert$, $\eta=0.1$. A sufficient condition for the iterative error to stay below the temporal error is $\lVert J(I/h-a_{ii}J)^{-1}R^{(i)}\rVert\le\eta\lVert e_t\rVert$, which for $h\to\infty$ becomes $\lVert R^{(i)}\rVert\le\eta a_{ii}\lVert e_t\rVert$, with $0.25<a_{ii}<0.45$ in the schemes used (§2.3, Eqs. 37–45). **Derived** (linearized)
7. Fixed relative Newton tolerances $\tau_1=\theta_1\lVert v_0\rVert$ or $\tau_2=\theta_2\lVert N(v_0)\rVert$ with large $\theta$ ($10^{-6}$, $10^{-3}$) degrade the temporal order on the isentropic vortex, small ones ($10^{-10}$, $10^{-7}$) recover it, and the adaptive tolerance gives observed order 3.27 with ESDIRK3 (§3.1.1, Fig. 2). **Shown**
8. The adaptive tolerance is within an order of magnitude of the largest tolerance $\tau_{\max}$ that keeps $\lVert e_t+h\bar e_{\mathrm{it}}\rVert$ within 1% of $\lVert e_t\rVert$; printed ratios 0.57, 0.14, 0.31 for $h=0.05,0.1,0.2$ (Table 1). **Shown** (two ratios disagree with the printed columns; see Printed slips)
9. On the isentropic vortex ($P=6$, ESDIRK3) the adaptive step keeps the temporal error below the spatial one on all meshes, so the total error converges at the spatial rate; CFL 10 is temporal-error dominated, CFL 0.2 wastes cost, CFL 1.0 changes regime with $\Delta x$ (§3.1.2, Figs. 3–4). **Shown**
10. With tolerance-based controllers the temporal-error curves against $\Delta x$ would be flat, since the tolerance does not depend on $\Delta x$ (§3.1.2). **Stated**
11. The adaptive run is close to the most efficient fixed-CFL run at every error level, and up to ten times cheaper at equal error than a naive CFL choice (§3.1.2, Fig. 5). **Shown**
12. With adaptive stepping ESDIRK2, 3 and 4 give almost the same total error on the isentropic vortex (§3.1.2, Fig. 6). **Shown**
13. Overhead: the embedded stage costs about $1/(s-1)$ extra ($s=3,4,6$); the $f^{P+1}$ evaluation costs about 1.5 evaluations of $f^P$, while an implicit stage costs more than ten (§2.4). **Stated**
14. Capping the step by a user $h_{\mathrm{limit}}$ does not improve results under Eq. 29; refine the mesh instead; the adapted step can serve as a mesh-refinement indicator (§2.4). **Stated**
15. Steady flat-plate boundary layer (Ma 0.1, $Re=1.6\times10^6$, $P=2$, $p=3$): with unchanged parameters the adaptive strategy converges almost as fast as a CFL ramp from 0.1 with growth factor 2 per step, and faster than growth 1.5 (§3.2, Figs. 9–10). **Shown**
16. The strategy is less efficient than dedicated steady-state accelerators (local time stepping, multigrid) but general (§3.2). **Stated**
17. Taylor–Green vortex (Ma 0.1, $Re=1600$, $32^3$, $P=4$, $p=4$): $\beta=0.1$ gives a slightly larger dissipation-rate error than $\beta=0.01$; $\beta=0.01$ almost matches fixed $h=0.1$ at 1.27 times its CPU time (§3.3, Figs. 11–12). **Shown**
18. For implicit LES, where the spatial truncation error acts as the subgrid model, $\beta=0.01$ is used (§3.3). **Stated**
19. Cylinder at $Re=3900$ (123,360 curved hexahedra, $P=2$): averaged adaptive steps 0.027, 0.043, 0.127 (ESDIRK2, 3, 4) against the fixed DIRK2 step 0.010 (CFL ≈ 40); CPU-time ratios 1.00 (DIRK2), 1.09, 1.36, 1.53: higher order takes larger steps but costs more (Table 2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
20. ESDIRK2 at averaged $h=0.027$ reproduces the time-averaged velocity profiles of DIRK2 at $h=0.010$, both matching experiment (Fig. 15). **Shown**
21. In the cylinder case the element step is smaller than the global step in fewer than 1% of elements; $\epsilon_m$ has negligible influence (§3.4, Fig. 16). **Shown**
22. CPU-time ratio adaptive/hand-tuned: isentropic vortex 0.96 and 1.01, boundary layer 1.21, Taylor–Green 1.27, cylinder 1.09 (Table 4). **Shown**
23. Limitations: the controller uses local errors, which cannot guarantee a spatially dominated global error, and the accuracy-based step does not guarantee stability (§4). **Stated**
24. The $1.5^p\epsilon_m$ term makes the step grow by at least a factor 1.5 in elements where the error is far below $\epsilon_m$ (§2.2). **Stated** (holds only in the limit $\lVert E_t\rVert_{e,m}\ll\epsilon_m$)

The controller has no accept/reject step: the computed step is used for the
next step.

# Printed slips

- Table 1 (p. 742): the printed ratios $\eta\lVert e_t\rVert/\tau_{\max}$ do not equal the quotients of the printed columns in two rows: $5.5\times10^{-5}/4.0\times10^{-5}=1.375$ (printed 0.14) and $6.3\times10^{-4}/2.0\times10^{-4}=3.15$ (printed 0.31) *(compiler check)*. Either those two column entries are ten times too large or the ratios are wrong; the text's conclusion needs the first reading.
- Eq. 33 justification (pp. 736–737): "a $1.5^N\epsilon_m$ is added to the numerator to maintain the elemental and the time step will at least grow by 1.5 times" is garbled, and the growth bound holds only in the limit of claim 24.
- §2.1.2 (p. 732): "discretize Eq. (3) in time", but Eq. (3) is the weak form; the time-dependent systems are Eqs. (1) and (8).
- §2.4 (p. 740): "the extra cost in temporal error estimating is more 10 times of that" is ungrammatical.
- §3.5 (p. 754) says Eq. (34) "is motivated by the truncation error of the time integration process"; §2.2 bases $\epsilon_m$ on "the rounding error of the mth variable".
- Table A2: $\alpha$ printed 0.292 893 218 800 000, i.e. $1-1/\sqrt2$ truncated at ten significant digits and zero-padded, while the other entries carry 15 digits. The rows still sum consistently, so the order conditions hold only to about $10^{-11}$.
- Table A4: the third abscissa is $c_3=0.135+0.87265\ldots+0.27\approx1.278>1$ *(compiler check)*; unusual but not shown to be an error.
