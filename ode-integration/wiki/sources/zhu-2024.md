# Summary

The paper builds a bound-preserving discontinuous Galerkin scheme for
multispecies reacting compressible Euler equations with stiff reaction
sources. Admissibility needs positive density and pressure and every mass
fraction in $[0,1]$ with unit sum. Following Du and Yang, each partial density
is kept positive by a scaling limiter, the numerical fluxes are *consistent*,
and the time integrator is *conservative*, so the partial densities sum to the
density and the upper bound on mass fractions follows. This is
[positivity preservation](/properties/positivity-preservation.md) extended to
a bound by conservation.

The reaction source is written as a
[production–destruction system](/definitions/production-destruction-system.md)
and treated by [modified Patankar](/methods/modified-patankar-methods.md)
schemes: explicit in the convective flux, linearly implicit in the source.
Besides the SSP-form MPRK2 of Huang & Shu and MPRK3 of Huang, Zhao & Shu, the
paper's own contribution is a **modified Patankar multistep (MPMS)** method of
orders two and three. It is built on the explicit
[SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) multistep methods
of Gottlieb, Shu & Tadmor. The Patankar denominators are weighted geometric
means of past values, $\varpi_i=\prod_k(y^{n-k}_i)^{e_k}$, with exponents
fixed by Taylor expansion so that $\varpi_i=y^{n+1}_i+O(h^p)$; this leaves a
one-parameter family at each order. Conservation is proved and unconditional
positivity is stated; only sufficient order conditions are given, with no
convergence proof or starting procedure.

Coupling with DG is delicate because DG positivity needs positive values at
interface points while the Patankar step preserves positivity only of the
point values it acts on. The algorithm takes the convective multistep step,
limits at Gauss points, applies the Patankar source step pointwise, and limits
again at the interface point set. Theorem 3.3 states bound preservation under
the convective CFL condition scaled by the SSP coefficient. ODE tests confirm
orders two and three; 1-D reacting shock tubes (a reacting variant of the
[Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-shock-tube.md) setting) and 2-D detonations
are qualitative. No step sizes or timings are reported for the PDE tests.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ is the step (source $\Delta t$), $y_i$ the partial
densities (source $c_i$), $p_{ij}$, $d_{ij}$ the production and destruction
rates of the [registry](/notation.md) PDS form, $\varpi_i$ the Patankar
denominators (source $\sigma_i$), $h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ the forward-Euler
positivity step of the convective part (source $\Delta t_0$). The exponents
of $\varpi_i$ are written $e_0,e_1,e_2$ (source $s,r,p$), with
$e_0$ on $y^n$, $e_1$ on $y^{n-1}$, $e_2$ on $y^{n-2}$.

1. Explicit SSP RK and multistep methods may impose small steps for stiff
   reaction sources; MP schemes "can handle stiff sources with relatively
   large time steps" (Abstract, §1). **Stated** (no step-size evidence; the
   Conclusions say "we take appropriately small time step")
2. Standard positivity-preserving techniques give positive density, internal
   energy and mass fractions but do not enforce mass fractions $\le1$; the
   upper bound follows from consistent fluxes and a conservative time
   integrator (Abstract, §1, end of the proof of Thm. 3.3). **Derived**
3. Patankar's original trick is linearly implicit but not conservative; the
   modified Patankar–Euler scheme of Burchard, Deleersnijder & Meister,
   Eq. (1.5), is positive and conservative for any step but first order (§1).
   **Stated**
4. The SSP-form MPRK2 (Eq. 2.2) has $\alpha_{10}=1$, $\alpha_{20}=1-\alpha$,
   $\alpha_{21}=\alpha$, $\beta_{10}=\beta$,
   $\beta_{20}=1-\frac1{2\beta}-\alpha\beta$, $\beta_{21}=\frac1{2\beta}$ and
   denominator exponent $\frac{1-\alpha\beta+\alpha\beta^2}{\beta(1-\alpha\beta)}$,
   with $0\le\alpha\le1$, $\beta>0$, $\alpha\beta+\frac1{2\beta}\le1$ (§2.1).
   **Stated** (recalled). The values used in the experiments are not given.
5. The MPRK3 of Huang, Zhao & Shu (Eq. 2.3) is "third-order accurate and
   unconditionally positivity-preserving"; its coefficients are not
   reproduced (§2.1). **Stated**
6. "To the best of our knowledge, there is no modified Patankar multistep
   method in the literature" (§5). **Stated**
7. MPMS2 (2.8),
   $y^{n+1}_i=\tfrac14y^{n-2}_i+\tfrac34y^n_i+\tfrac32h\bigl(\sum_jp_{ij}(y^n)\frac{y^{n+1}_j}{\varpi_j}-\sum_jd_{ij}(y^n)\frac{y^{n+1}_i}{\varpi_i}\bigr)$,
   has order two if $\varpi_i=y^{n+1}_i+O(h^2)$ (2.9), since each Patankar
   weight is then $1+O(h^2)$ and the scheme differs from the explicit SSP
   multistep base (2.7) by $O(h^3)$ (§2.2.1). **Derived**
8. With $\varpi_i=(y^n_i)^{e_0}(y^{n-1}_i)^{e_1}(y^{n-2}_i)^{1-e_0-e_1}$
   (2.10), condition (2.9) holds iff $e_1+2e_0-3=0$ (2.14) (§2.2.1).
   **Derived**
9. Lemma 2.1: MPMS2 is conservative,
   $\sum_iy^{n+1}_i=\tfrac14\sum_iy^{n-2}_i+\tfrac34\sum_iy^n_i$ (§2.2.1).
   **Proved**
10. Lemma 2.2: MPMS2 is unconditionally positive: $y^{n+1}_i>0$ for every
    $h>0$ when $y^{n-2},y^{n-1},y^n>0$ (§2.2.1). **Stated** (proof skipped as
    "similar to Lemma 2.7" of Kopecz & Meister)
11. Theorem 2.3: MPMS2 with (2.10) and $e_1+2e_0-3=0$ is second-order,
    conservative and unconditionally positive (§2.2.1). **Derived** (order) /
    **Stated** (positivity)
12. For MPMS3 (2.15), $\varpi_i=y^{n+1}_i+O(h^3)$ (2.16) suffices for third
    order (§2.2.2). **Derived** (by analogy; no expansion written)
13. With
    $\varpi_i=(y^n_i)^{e_0}(y^{n-1}_i)^{e_1}(y^{n-2}_i)^{e_2}(y^{n-3}_i)^{1-e_0-e_1-e_2}$
    (2.17), condition (2.16) holds iff $e_1=-3e_0+6$, $e_2=3e_0-8$ (2.21), from
    the expansion
    $\varpi_i=y^{n+1}_i+(e_2+2e_1+3e_0-4)hQ_1-(7e_2+12e_1+15e_0-16)\frac{h^2}2Q_2+\bigl((e_2+2e_1+3e_0)^2-4e_1-9e_0-e_2\bigr)\frac{h^2Q_1^2}{2y^{n+1}_i}+O(h^3)$
    with $Q_1=y_i'$ and $Q_2=y_i''$ at $t_{n+1}$ (p. 9). **Derived**.
    Substituting (2.21) annuls all three coefficients *(compiler check)*.
14. Theorem 2.4: MPMS3 with (2.17), (2.21) is third-order, conservative and
    unconditionally positive (§2.2.2). **Derived** / **Stated** (no positivity
    proof)
15. Definition 3.1: the numerical flux components are consistent if the
    density flux equals the species flux when that species' mass fraction is
    one; the Lax–Friedrichs flux is consistent (§3.1). **Stated**
16. Theorem 3.2 (recalled): if the DG polynomial is admissible on the point
    set (3.5), the forward-Euler cell-average update (3.4) is admissible under
    the CFL condition (3.6) (§3.2). **Stated**
17. MPMS2 with convection (3.8) is positive for $h\le\tfrac12h_{\mathrm{FE}}$,
    MPMS3 with convection (3.9) for $h\le\tfrac13h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (§3.3).
    **Stated** ("Clearly"). The factors are the SSP coefficients of the two
    base methods, $\frac34/\frac32=\frac12$ and
    $\min(\frac{16}{27}/\frac{16}9,\frac{11}{27}/\frac49)=\frac13$
    *(compiler check)*.
18. The algorithm (§3.5) limits twice per step: at Gauss points after the
    convective step, because the Patankar step preserves positivity of the
    point values it acts on, and at the interface point set after the
    Patankar step, which "may not yield positive numerical approximations on
    the cell interfaces" (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
19. Theorem 3.3: DG (3.1) with MPMS2 (3.8) or MPMS3 (3.9) is bound preserving
    under $h\le\min_i\frac{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}\Delta\tilde t$, with
    $\Delta\tilde t$ from (3.6) (§3.5). **Proved** at sketch level (forward
    Euler plus Patankar positivity, "convex combinations of Euler forward",
    then the upper bound from consistency and conservation); the Patankar part
    is not a forward Euler step, and a full composite argument is not written.
20. Example 1 (linear $2\times2$ PDS, $a=2.7$, $T=1$): MPMS2 orders 2.04,
    2.07, 1.99, 1.98 (errors 1.88e-02 to 6.97e-05 for $h=1/20$ to $1/320$);
    MPMS3 orders 3.02, 3.00, 2.96, 2.97 (1.03e-03 to 2.61e-07) (Table 1).
    **Shown**
21. Example 2 (nonlinear three-species PDS with added non-conservative
    terms, $a=1$, $T=1$): MPMS2 orders 2.26, 1.96, 2.06, 2.01 (2.13e-03 to
    6.76e-06); MPMS3 orders 3.32, 3.16, 3.07, 3.04 (3.67e-04 to 5.924e-08)
    (Table 2). **Shown**
22. At fixed $h$ the error depends on the free exponent $e_0$ and has a
    minimum; choosing it is left as future work (Remark 4.1, Fig. 1).
    **Shown**
23. Example 3 (1-D three-species reacting shock tube, $t=10^{-4}$): MPRK2,
    MPMS2 and MPMS3 show oscillations (no WENO-type limiter), but "the
    bound-preserving technique is enough to stabilize our numerical scheme"
    (§4.2, Figs. 2–4). **Shown** (qualitative)
24. Example 4 (2-D reactive Euler, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/120$, $t=0.2$): MPMS2
    and MPRK2 results are "comparable to the results in" Wang et al., with
    spurious oscillations (§4.3, Figs. 5–6). **Shown** (qualitative)
25. Example 5 (detonation diffraction, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/48$, $t=0.6$):
    MPRK2 and MPMS2 "agree with previous works"; without bound preservation the
    problem can produce negative density or pressure and blow up (§4.3, Figs.
    7–8). **Shown** (qualitative; no failing baseline shown)
26. Multistep methods need fewer function evaluations per step than RK, so
    they "work better" unless the characteristic speeds change wildly (§5).
    **Stated** (no cost or timing data)

# Printed slips

- p. 2: "$T=\rho/p$ is the temperature"; the temperature follows from
  $p=RT\sum_i\rho_i/M_i$.
- Eq. (2.1) and Thm. 2.4 run species sums to $N$ while the state has $M$
  components; Lemma 2.1 appends a spurious "for $i=1,2,\dots,M$".
- Eq. (2.15): $\sum_j$ binds only the production bracket as typeset.
- p. 12: "the third-order MP MS scheme (2.8)" should be (2.15); "Assumption
  2.1" is stated in §3.
- Eqs. (3.8)–(3.9): the Patankar denominators carry a cell index that their
  definition drops.
- Thm. 3.2: $\hat\omega_1$ is not defined; Thm. 3.3: $\alpha_i$, $\beta_i$ are
  not defined for the multistep schemes (the factors are $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac13$
  of §3.3), and $\alpha$ is also the Lax–Friedrichs viscosity.
- §3.3: "$k$-point Gaussian quadrature" against §3.2's $L\ge k+1$ Gauss
  points; $k$ is also the cell index.
- §3.4 step 2: "$\hat r_M=\bar r_M+\theta(r_M-\bar r_M)$" should use $c$;
  "$\bar w_k$" against "$\bar w_K$". §3.5 step 1: the state vector omits $n$.
- Proof of Thm. 3.3: it cites the modified Patankar–Euler paper for
  positivity of the multistep Patankar step.
- Example 2: "and $p_{ij}=d_{ij}=1$ for other sets of $i,j$" should be 0; the
  added terms $(c_1c_2c_3,c_3/c_2,c_2c_2c_3^2)$ are not conservative, and no
  exact solution is given although errors are called exact.
- Table 2: "4.115e-06" and "5.924e-08" carry one more digit than the other
  entries.
- Remark 4.1: "values of $s$ from −1 to 5" while all axes of Fig. 1 run from
  0 to 5; the fixed step is not stated.
- Figure captions name Examples 3–5 "Example 4.3", "Example4.4",
  "Example4.5".
- Example 3: "$\rho u^2+P$" against "$(E+p)u$"; the rate uses $C$, $E$ while
  the parameters are given as $C_0$, $E_0$.
- Example 5: the right state "(ρ, u, v, E, Y) = (1, 0, 0, 5, 5, 1)" has six
  entries for five variables.
- Ref. [14] prints pages "1181-1839"; ref. [26] has no year; ref. [8] is
  "accepted" without year or volume. Refs. [21], [22], [28], [31] are never
  cited in the text.
