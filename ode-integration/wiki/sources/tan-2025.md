# Summary

The paper builds a high-order finite-difference scheme for the 2D compressible Navier–Stokes equations in curvilinear coordinates, with temperature-dependent (Sutherland) viscosity and a general equation of state $p=p(\rho,e)$ (ideal gas and a simplified van der Waals gas). Inviscid fluxes use fifth-order WENO with Lax–Friedrichs splitting in Roe-type characteristic fields, whose eigenvectors the paper derives for a general equation of state. Viscous terms use fourth-order conservative central differences.

The time scheme is [explicit–implicit–null (EIN) time marching](/methods/explicit-implicit-null-method.md). A constant-coefficient Laplacian $a_1(\partial_\xi^2+\partial_\eta^2)\boldsymbol U$ is added to and subtracted from all four equations. Convection, the full nonlinear physical viscous terms and the subtracted copy are explicit; only the added copy is implicit. The pair is the third-order ARS(4,4,3) [IMEX Runge–Kutta](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) method, printed in full but not named so. Each implicit stage is a linear constant-coefficient system, whatever the viscosity law or equation of state, and is solved by a direct sparse solver (see [implicit-stage solvers](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md)). The paper presents EIN as the alternative to [linearly implicit IMEX methods](/methods/semi-implicit-imex-runge-kutta.md) for nonlinear stiff terms.

A numerical Fourier analysis of the scheme linearised about a fluid at rest gives a convective limit $\nu\le1.2$ and the coefficient rule $a_1=a_0\lambda_\mu^{\max}$, $a_0\ge0.54$, with $\lambda_\mu^{\max}$ the spectral radius of the viscous-flux Jacobian. The rule is independent of $\mu$, the Prandtl number and the equation of state. The step is then hyperbolic, with no parabolic restriction. The tests are viscous shocks with Becker's solution, Couette flow, a manufactured solution, the first Stokes problem, a viscous shock tube and a mixing layer. They show high-order convergence and non-oscillatory shocks. Against explicit SSPRK3 with the parabolic step limit, EIN is 1.54 to 14.71 times faster at $160^2$ as $\mu_T$ grows from 0.1 to 1, and 7.28 to 19.39 times faster from $160^2$ to $320^2$ at $\mu_T=0.5$.

# Key claims

Notation: $h$ is the step and $\Delta x$ the mesh width (source $\Delta t$, $\Delta x$). The implicit part from the added Laplacian is $f^{\mathrm I}$ and the explicit part $f^{\mathrm E}$ (source $\mathcal L$, $\mathcal N$), with tableaux $(A^{\mathrm I},b^{\mathrm I})$ and $(A^{\mathrm E},b^{\mathrm E})$. $\nu$ is the convective Courant number (source $C_u$). $a_1$, $a_0$ and the flow symbols are those of the source.

1. EIN adds and subtracts a term $g$ with the same wave-number scaling as the stiff terms, $y'=(f-g)+g$, and applies a standard IMEX method with $f-g$ explicit and $g$ implicit; the name is due to Duchemin & Eggers [12]. (§1) **Stated**
2. Here $g=a_1(\partial^2_\xi+\partial^2_\eta)\boldsymbol U$ is added to all four equations, continuity included, so that all physical viscous terms (nonlinear viscosity) are explicit and each implicit stage is a linear constant-coefficient system "for which very efficient methods exist"; the method is "not limited by the equation of state". (§1, §3.4, Eq. (3.2)) **Stated**
3. To the authors' knowledge EIN for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations has not been studied before; earlier EIN-like Euler and Navier–Stokes methods [8, 10, 14, 17, 24] add a gradient-type term aimed at low-Mach stiffness, and [18] (compressible MHD) is first order. (§1) **Stated**
4. Proposition 2.1: the spectral radius of the viscous Jacobian block matrix $\boldsymbol D$ is $\lambda^{\max}_\mu=\frac1J\max\{\frac43(x_\xi^2+y_\xi^2)\frac\mu\rho,\frac43(x_\eta^2+y_\eta^2)\frac\mu\rho,\kappa(x_\xi^2+y_\xi^2)\lvert\partial T/\partial E\rvert,\kappa(x_\eta^2+y_\eta^2)\lvert\partial T/\partial E\rvert\}$. (Eq. (2.5), called "elementary", no proof printed) **Stated**
5. Proposition 2.2: the inviscid spectral radii for $p=p(\rho,e)$ are $\lambda^{\max}_{c,1}=\max\lvert y_\eta u-x_\eta v\pm c_s\sqrt{x_\eta^2+y_\eta^2}\rvert$ and $\lambda^{\max}_{c,2}=\max\lvert x_\xi v-y_\xi u\pm c_s\sqrt{x_\xi^2+y_\xi^2}\rvert$, $c_s=\sqrt{p_\rho+pp_e/\rho^2}$. (Eq. (2.8), no proof printed) **Stated**
6. The time integrator is a third-order IMEX Runge–Kutta method from [1] with five stage values (tableau (3.5), which is ARS(4,4,3)); the authors "also considered other high order IMEX methods" but do not report them. (§3.4) **Stated**
7. The stability of the EIN scheme cannot be inferred from the scalar results [32, 33], because the inviscid and viscous Jacobians are not simultaneously diagonalizable; a system analysis is needed. (§3.5) **Stated**
8. Pure convection ($a_1=0$, no viscosity), linearised about $u_0=v_0=0$, ideal gas, spectral radius $\le1+10^{-5}$ over sampled $\rho_0,p_0$ and Fourier angles: stable for $h=\nu\min(\lambda^{\max}_{c,1}+\lambda^{\max}_{c,2})^{-1}\Delta x$ with $\nu\le1.2$. (§3.5, Eq. (3.8)) **Shown** (numerical eigenvalue search, not proved)
9. Pure diffusion: unconditionally stable in $h/\Delta x^2$, "regardless of the values of $\mu$ and $Pr$", provided $a_1=a_0\lambda^{\max}_\mu$ with $a_0\ge0.54$. (§3.5, Eq. (3.9)) **Shown** (numerical)
10. Full scheme: the largest stable Courant number depends on both $a_0$ and $h/\Delta x^2$ and is not monotone in $a_0$; $\nu=1.2$ is safe when $a_0\ge0.54$, and the scheme becomes unconditionally stable as $h/\Delta x^2$ grows. In Fig. 3.1 ($\mu=1$, $Pr=0.75$) the limit drops to 0 for $\lg(h/\Delta x^2)\ge2$ at $a_0=0.53$; at $a_0=0.54,1,10$ it is $\approx1.8$ for $\lg(h/\Delta x^2)=-6,-4$ and reaches the plotted cap 2.5 for $\lg(h/\Delta x^2)\ge0$; at $\lg(h/\Delta x^2)=-2$ it is $\approx1.8$ for $a_0=0.54,1$ and $\approx1.2$ for $a_0=10$. (§3.5, Fig. 3.1) **Shown**
11. The van der Waals equation of state gives the same stability results as the ideal gas. (§3.5) **Shown** (stated; results not plotted)
12. Proposition 3.1: for (3.2) on a Cartesian mesh, ideal or van der Waals gas, stability under small perturbations of a fluid at rest requires (3.10a) $\nu\le1.2$ and (3.10b) $a_1=a_0\lambda^{\max}_\mu$ with $a_0\ge0.54$. **Shown** (numerical; labelled "Proposition" but not proved)
13. The stability results depend on the background flow; at rest they are "basically independent of $\rho_0$ and $p_0$"; for flows with velocities far from 0 a larger $a_1$ or a smaller step may be needed, so the Courant number is problem-dependent in §4. (Remark 3.1) **Stated**
14. Not adding the Laplacian to the continuity equation ($A_1=\mathrm{diag}\{0,a_1,a_1,a_1\}$) is "far less stable" by linear analysis and experiments. (Remark 3.2) **Stated** (results not presented)
15. Practical rule (4.1): $a_1=a_0\lambda^{\max}_\mu$, $a_0\ge0.54$, and $h=\nu\min[\frac1J(\frac{\lambda^{\max}_{c,1}}{\Delta\xi}+\frac{\lambda^{\max}_{c,2}}{\Delta\eta})]^{-1}$ with $\nu\le1.2$. All tests use exactly $a_0=0.54$, because a larger $a_0$ gives larger errors and slower convergence [32]. (§4) **Stated**
16. Observed orders can be "significantly higher than 3", which the authors attribute to "the small time error of the problem itself". (§4) **Stated**
17. 1D viscous shock ($M_a=1.5$, $\mu=0.01$, $\nu=1$, $t_f=5$): orders about 4.8–5.1 in $L^1$, $L^2$, $L^\infty$ at $N=640$ (density $L^1$ error $1.460\times10^{-7}$, order 4.846). (§4.1, Table 4.1) **Shown**
18. 1D shock with $M_a=15$, $\mu=10^{-5}$, 321 points: agrees with the exact velocity and temperature, without oscillations. (§4.1, Fig. 4.1) **Shown**
19. 2D viscous shock on the non-orthogonal mesh (4.3), $t_f=3$: orders 4.2–4.6 at $N_1=240$ (density $L^1$ order 4.561). (§4.2, Table 4.2) **Shown**
20. Compressible Couette flow ($Re=1$, $M_a=0.9$, $\nu=1.2$, power-law viscosity $\beta=2$): density and velocity orders about 4.5–4.7 and temperature about 3.8 at $N=80$; the residual reaches $2\times10^{-14}$. (§4.3, Table 4.3, Fig. 4.2) **Shown**
21. Manufactured solution, Sutherland viscosity, $\nu=1.2$, $t_f=\pi$, mesh (4.4): orders tend to about 3 (2.84–3.08 at $N=160$) for the ideal gas (Table 4.4) and van der Waals gas $b_v=1/3$ (Table 4.5), the only tests where the third-order temporal error shows. (§4.4) **Shown**
22. CPU time at $160\times160$, $t_f=0.1$, serial Fortran 90 on an Intel Core i7-4790 with PARDISO: EIN 69 (one merged cell for all three $\mu_T$) against SSPRK3 106, 502 and 1015 for $\mu_T=0.1$, 0.5 and 1, ratios 1.54, 7.28 and 14.71. (§4.4.1, Table 4.6) **Shown** (units not printed; seconds presumed)
23. CPU time against mesh at $\mu_T=0.5$, $N=160$, 240, 320: EIN 69, 200, 459 against SSPRK3 502, 2668, 8901, ratios 7.28, 13.34, 19.39. (§4.4.1, Table 4.7) **Shown**
24. The SSPRK3 baseline [29] treats the viscous terms explicitly with $h\le\nu^*\min[\frac1J(\frac{\lambda^{\max}_{c,1}}{\Delta\xi}+\frac{\lambda^{\max}_{c,2}}{\Delta\eta}+\lambda^{\max}_\mu(\frac1{\Delta\xi^2}+\frac1{\Delta\eta^2}))]^{-1}$, $\nu^*=0.5$. (§4.4.1) **Stated**
25. First Stokes problem ($M_a=0.1$, $\mu=0.1$, $Pr=1$, stretched mesh with spacing ratio about 69.6, $21\times81$, $\nu=1.2$, $t_f=1$): agrees with the incompressible erf solution. (§4.5, Fig. 4.3) **Shown**
26. Viscous shock tube, $Re=200$, $401^2$, $\nu=0.1$ (chosen conservatively), $t_f=1$: structures agree with [9, 37], and the wall density is close to the gas-kinetic reference of [37]. (§4.6.1, Figs. 4.4–4.5) **Shown**
27. Viscous shock tube with a van der Waals gas ($b_v=1/480$) and Sutherland viscosity ($\mu_T=1$), $\nu=1.2$: qualitatively consistent, no reference solution. (§4.6.2, Fig. 4.6) **Shown**
28. Mixing layer, $M_c=0.8$, $Re=400$, Courant number 1.2, three meshes up to $301\times601$: the vorticity thickness is grid-converged and vortex growth and pairing are captured. (§4.7, Figs. 4.7–4.8) **Shown**
29. Conclusion: the scheme is non-oscillatory, has "designed third order accuracy" and needs "remarkably less CPU time than the third order SSP Runge-Kutta method" where nonlinear viscosity dominates; it needs block-structured body-fitted meshes; DG with EIN is future work. (§5) **Stated**

*(Reader checks, not in the source.)* The tableau (3.5) is ARS(4,4,3): implicit first column zero, SDIRK diagonal $\tfrac12$ on stages 2–5, both parts [stiffly accurate](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md), $c=(0,\tfrac12,\tfrac23,\tfrac12,1)$. The implicit part is the [SDIRK](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) with $\gamma=\tfrac12$. The "Propositions" 3.1 and the thresholds $\nu\le1.2$, $a_0\ge0.54$ are sampled eigenvalue computations about a fluid at rest with tolerance $10^{-5}$, not theorems. Fig. 3.1 shows a stable Courant number of about 1.8 at small $h/\Delta x^2$ where (3.8) gives 1.2; (3.8) is the minimum over sampled states, and the paper does not reconcile the two. The temporal order is never isolated: Tables 4.1–4.3 show orders 4–5 because $h\propto\Delta x$ and spatial errors dominate. The cost comparison is against explicit SSPRK3 only, not against an IMEX scheme with implicit nonlinear viscosity, and it pairs $\nu=1.2$ with $\nu^*=0.5$. Whether $a_1$ is updated each step is not stated.
