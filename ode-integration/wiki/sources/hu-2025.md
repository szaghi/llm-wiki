# Summary

The paper proves that a class of [IMEX Runge–Kutta](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) schemes keeps its design order **uniformly in the stiffness parameter $\varepsilon$** on the [linear hyperbolic relaxation system](/benchmarks/hyperbolic-relaxation-system.md) $\partial_tu+\partial_xv=0$, $\partial_tv+\partial_xu=\frac1\varepsilon(\kappa u-v)$, $\lvert\kappa\rvert<1$, discretized in space by a Fourier–Galerkin method. The error bound holds with a constant independent of $\varepsilon$, of the number of modes $N$ and of $h$, including the intermediate regime $0<\varepsilon<O(1)$ where IMEX schemes usually suffer [order reduction](/definitions/order-reduction.md). It extends the authors' IMEX-BDF result of 2021 to Runge–Kutta methods. The Runge–Kutta case is harder because intermediate stages can have [stage order](/definitions/stage-order.md) 1 only.

The tool is an [energy estimate](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md) with a multiplier matrix $\Pi$. Two algebraic conditions on $\Pi$ give coercive terms and uniform stability for schemes whose implicit part is of type CK (first row zero, trailing block invertible; a [DIRK](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) part) and implicitly [stiffly accurate](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md). Uniform second order then needs the second-order conditions, $c^{\mathrm E}=c^{\mathrm I}$, and condition (A): the null vector of $A^{\mathrm I}$ has a zero last component. Uniform third order also needs $\tau^{(2)}_i=0$ for both tableaux on stages $i\ge3$ and a vanishing-coefficient condition that isolates stage 2. ARS(2,2,2), ARS(4,4,3) and a new variant BHR(5,5,3)* ($c_4=1.5$) are uniformly second order. Only BHR(5,5,3)* is uniformly third order, which explains the reduction of ARS(4,4,3) to order 2 at intermediate $\varepsilon$. Numerical tests over $\varepsilon\in[10^{-7},1]$ confirm these orders. An appendix shows formally that implicitly stiffly accurate schemes satisfying (A) and two extra sums are [asymptotic-preserving](/definitions/asymptotic-preserving-scheme.md) (asymptotically accurate) without being globally stiffly accurate. The paper also separates AP from uniform accuracy. The theorems are collected on the [uniform-accuracy result page](/results/uniform-accuracy-relaxation-systems.md).

# Key claims

Notation: $h$ the step; the source's $\mu=\Delta t/\varepsilon$ is written out as $h/\varepsilon$; $(A^{\mathrm E},b^{\mathrm E},c^{\mathrm E})$ and $(A^{\mathrm I},b^{\mathrm I},c^{\mathrm I})$ the explicit and implicit tableaux; $w=v-\kappa u$ the relaxing variable; $\Pi$ the multiplier matrix and $\kappa$ the equilibrium slope (page-local, as on the [uniform-accuracy](/results/uniform-accuracy-relaxation-systems.md) and [relaxation-system](/benchmarks/hyperbolic-relaxation-system.md) pages); $\mathsf v$ the null vector of $A^{\mathrm I}$ normalized by $\mathsf v_1=1$. Error bounds are on squared $L^2$ norms, so $h^4$ is second order. See the notation map.

1. For the linear hyperbolic relaxation system (1.1), a class of second- and third-order IMEX-RK schemes keeps its design order uniformly in $\varepsilon$: $\lVert u(T)-U(T)\rVert_{L^2}+\lVert v(T)-V(T)\rVert_{L^2}\le Ch^q$ with $C$ independent of $\varepsilon$ (Abstract, §1, Eq. (1.4)). **Proved**
2. Most popular IMEX-RK schemes suffer order reduction in the intermediate regime; ARS(4,4,3), third order by design, reduces to second order for $\varepsilon$ between 0 and 1 (§1, citing Boscarino 2007). **Stated**, and **Shown** in §6.
3. ARS(2,2,2) has been observed numerically to keep uniform second order, and BHR(5,5,3) (Boscarino 2009; Boscarino & Russo 2009) uniform third order; these observations motivate the analysis (§1). **Stated**
4. Uniform stability (Thm 3.4): for type-CK, implicitly stiffly accurate schemes with a matrix $\Pi$ satisfying (M1)–(M2), the fully discrete Fourier–Galerkin scheme satisfies $(1-\kappa^2)\lVert U_N^n\rVert^2+\lVert W_N^n\rVert^2\le C\big((1-\kappa^2)\lVert u_{\mathrm{in}}\rVert^2+\lVert w_{\mathrm{in}}\rVert^2\big)$ for $nh\le T$, under $h\le\min(c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2,c)$, with $c,C$ independent of $\varepsilon$, $N$, $h$ (§3, Eq. (3.7)). (M1): $\Pi A^{\mathrm I}+(\Pi A^{\mathrm I})^\top$ is positive semidefinite of rank $s-1$; (M2): the same for $\Pi_*+\Pi_*^\top$, with $\Pi_*$ defined in (3.3). **Proved**
5. ARS(2,2,2), ARS(4,4,3) and BHR(5,5,3)* satisfy the hypotheses of Thm 3.4 and are therefore uniformly stable (§3, after Thm 3.4; App. 7.2). **Proved** for the ARS schemes (exact $\Pi$); for BHR(5,5,3)* the matrix $\Pi$ is verified numerically (second smallest eigenvalue $>0.01$), which Prop. 7.1 makes rigorous (§7.1–7.2).
6. Uniform second order (Thm 4.3): adding the second-order conditions, $c^{\mathrm E}_i=c^{\mathrm I}_i$, condition (A) and initial data consistent to order 6 gives $(1-\kappa^2)\lVert U_e^n\rVert^2+\lVert W_e^n\rVert^2\le C(h^4+N^{-8})$, with $C$ independent of $\varepsilon$, $N$, $h$ (§4, Eq. (4.3)). **Proved**
7. ARS(2,2,2), ARS(4,4,3) and BHR(5,5,3)* all satisfy Thm 4.3 and are at least uniformly second order (§4). **Proved**
8. Unlike IMEX-BDF, where the order equals the stage order and uniform accuracy follows from uniform stability, a second-order IMEX-RK scheme can have intermediate stages of stage order 1 only, which makes the proof "significantly harder" (Remark 4.4; Lemma 4.5). **Stated** / **Proved**
9. Local truncation errors of a second-order type-CK scheme with $c^{\mathrm E}=c^{\mathrm I}$: $E_W^{(i)}=O(h^2)$ for $i=2,\dots,s$ and $E_W^{n+1}=O(h^3)$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$ (Lemma 4.5, Eq. (4.6)). **Proved**
10. Componentwise, for every $h/\varepsilon>0$: $(I+\frac h\varepsilon A^{\mathrm I})^{-1}=O(1)$, $A^{\mathrm I}(I+\frac h\varepsilon A^{\mathrm I})^{-1}=O\big(1/(1+\frac h\varepsilon)\big)$, and under (A) $\big(A^{\mathrm I}(I+\frac h\varepsilon A^{\mathrm I})^{-1}\big)_{s,i}=O\big(1/(1+\frac h\varepsilon)^2\big)$ for $i<s$ (Lemma 4.6). **Proved** (the Neumann-series proof divides by $1+\frac h\varepsilon a^{\mathrm I}_{ii}$ and so uses $a^{\mathrm I}_{ii}>0$ for $i\ge2$, which is true for every scheme considered but not listed as a hypothesis).
11. Without the coercive stiff term the estimate is only $(1-\kappa^2)\lVert U_e^n\rVert^2+\lVert W_e^n\rVert^2\le C(T)\big(h^4+h^4/(\varepsilon+h)^2\big)$ (the source's $\Delta t^2\mu^2/(1+\mu)^2$ with $\mu=h/\varepsilon$), i.e. first-order uniform accuracy (second order if $\varepsilon=O(1)$) (Eq. (4.40), Remark 4.8). **Proved**
12. Uniform third order (Thm 5.1): adding the third-order conditions, the stage-order conditions (5.1) $\tfrac12c_i^2=\sum_ja^{\mathrm E}_{ij}c_j=\sum_ja^{\mathrm I}_{ij}c_j$ for $i\ge3$ (that is, $\tau^{(2)}_i=0$ for both tableaux), the vanishing-coefficient condition (5.2) $b^{\mathrm E}_2=0$, $a^{\mathrm I}_{i2}=0$ ($i\ge3$), and data consistent to order 8, the error satisfies a bound $C(h^6+N^{-12})$ (§5, Eq. (5.3)). **Proved**
13. Only BHR(5,5,3)* among the schemes considered satisfies Thm 5.1; ARS(4,4,3) satisfies Thm 4.3 but not Thm 5.1, which "rigorously" explains its reduction to uniform second order (§1, §5, §6). **Proved** (as sufficient conditions) / **Shown**
14. Condition (5.2) implies $\big(A^{\mathrm I}_{s,:}(I+\frac h\varepsilon A^{\mathrm I})^{-1}\big)_2=0$ and $\big((b^{\mathrm E})^\top(I+\frac h\varepsilon A^{\mathrm I})^{-1}\big)_2=0$ for all $h/\varepsilon>0$ (Lemma 5.3). **Proved**
15. Third-order local truncation errors: $E_W^{(2)}=O(h^2)$, $E_W^{(i)}=O(h^3)$ for $i\ge3$, $E_W^{n+1}=O(h^4)$ (Lemma 5.2). **Stated** (proof omitted as similar to Lemma 4.5)
16. For the original BHR(5,5,3) of Boscarino & Russo, with $c_4\approx2.3402$ chosen to minimize the fourth-order error constant, the authors could not find a matrix $\Pi$; with $c_4=1.5$ they could (Remark 7.2). **Stated**
17. Necessary conditions on $\Pi$: $\Pi A^{\mathrm I}+(\Pi A^{\mathrm I})^\top$ and $\Pi_*+\Pi_*^\top$ can never be strictly positive definite; (M1) implies $\mathsf v^\top\Pi=(*,0,\dots,0)$; (M2) implies $\mathbf 1^\top\Pi=(*,0,\dots,0,2)$; conversely each condition implies a zero eigenvalue, so (M1)–(M2) can be checked rigorously by computing the eigenvalues numerically (Prop. 7.1). **Proved**
18. Numerics: $\kappa=0.6$, $N=40$, $\varepsilon$ from $1$ to $10^{-7}$, start from the exact solution at $T_0=1$, $h=\frac8{N^2}2^{-k}$, $k=1,\dots,5$ (0.0025 to 0.00015625), $T=2$. ARS(2,2,2) and BHR(5,5,3)* reach uniform second and third order; ARS(4,4,3) is third order at $\varepsilon=O(1)$ and as $\varepsilon\to0$ but uniformly second order, with an error peak near $\varepsilon\approx10^{-4}$–$10^{-3}$ (§6, Fig. 1). **Shown**
19. Formal AP (Thm 7.4, first bullet) for the nonlinear system $\partial_tv+\partial_xu=\frac1\varepsilon(f(u)-v)$: an order-$q$ type-CK, implicitly stiffly accurate scheme that is globally stiffly accurate is AP, its limit being an order-$q$ explicit Runge–Kutta method for $\partial_tu+\partial_xf(u)=0$; this is already in Dimarco & Pareschi (2013). **Derived** (formal)
20. New (Thm 7.4, second bullet): if condition (A) holds instead, the limit scheme is consistent and at least first order; adding $\sum_j(b^{\mathrm E}_j-a^{\mathrm E}_{sj})=0$ gives second order; adding also $\sum_j(b^{\mathrm E}_j-a^{\mathrm E}_{sj})c^{\mathrm E}_j=0$ gives third order, hence AP. BHR(5,5,3)* belongs to this class and is AP without being globally stiffly accurate (§7.3). **Derived** (formal)
21. The paper's AP definition (Def. 7.3: order $q$ for $\varepsilon=O(1)$ and an order-$q$ consistent limit scheme at fixed $h$) is stronger than the classical AP definition of Jin and coincides with "asymptotically accurate" of Dimarco & Pareschi (§7.3). **Stated**
22. AP behaviour (at $\varepsilon=O(1)$ and as $\varepsilon\to0$) does not settle accuracy in the intermediate regime; asymptotic-analysis studies give error estimates depending on both $h$ and $\varepsilon$ (§1; App. 7.3). **Stated**
23. The multiplier-matrix method is inspired by Fu & Shu (§3.1 of their paper); for ARS-type schemes it recovers their energy estimates, and it also covers general type-CK schemes such as BHR(5,5,3)* (Remark 3.3). **Stated**

*(Compiler check, not in the sources.)* The stability and accuracy theorems need $h\le c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2$, a parabolic-type restriction that comes from the inverse inequality used in the energy proof, although §1 says IMEX schemes are expected to be stable under the convective CFL condition $h=O(1/N)$. The results cover a linear, constant-coefficient, periodic system only.
