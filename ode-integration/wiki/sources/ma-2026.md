# Summary

The paper studies linear, constant-coefficient, one-dimensional [hyperbolic relaxation systems](/benchmarks/hyperbolic-relaxation-system.md) $\partial_tU+F\partial_xU=\frac1\varepsilon QU$ with arbitrary relaxation time $\varepsilon>0$. The time discretization is the $k$-step [IMEX-BDF](/methods/imex-linear-multistep-methods.md) method: the stiff relaxation is treated by the implicit [BDF](/methods/backward-differentiation-formula.md) at $t_{n+k}$, and the convection by explicit extrapolation. Combined with a Fourier–Galerkin discretization in space, the scheme is proved uniformly stable and uniformly accurate of order $k$ in $\varepsilon$ for $k=1,2,3,4$. This generalizes Hu & Shu (2021), who proved the same for the Jin–Xin model, to every system satisfying Yong's **structural stability condition**, plus the extra assumption that the block $\mathcal H_2\hat S$ is symmetric. The condition requires (i) $Q$ similar to $\operatorname{diag}(0,\hat S)$ with $\hat S$ invertible, (ii) a symmetrizer $\mathcal H$ with $\mathcal HF=F^\top\mathcal H$, and (iii) a dissipative coupling between them.

The proof is an energy argument with the [multiplier technique](/methods/multiplier-energy-technique.md) of Nevanlinna and Odeh, Dahlquist's [G-stability](/properties/g-stability.md) and Akrivis and Katsoprinakis, in the form packaged by Hu & Shu. Testing the scheme with $U^{n+k}-\sum_j\mu_jU^{n+k-j}$ gives a telescoping G-norm and a non-negative dissipation from the implicit relaxation. The explicit convection is controlled under $h\le c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2$. Uniform truncation-error bounds rest on a uniform-in-$\varepsilon$ regularity theorem with an initial layer of length $O(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))$. Two classes of initial data are admitted: well-prepared data, or general smooth data with the scheme started after the layer. The main result is $\lVert U(\cdot,t_n)-U_N^n\rVert^2\le C(h^{2k}+N^{-4k})$ with $C$ independent of $\varepsilon$, $N$ and $h$ (see [uniform accuracy for relaxation systems](/results/uniform-accuracy-relaxation-systems.md)). Tests on the linearized Aw–Rascle–Zhang traffic model, the linearized [Broadwell model](/benchmarks/broadwell-model.md) and a linearized Grad moment system show orders 2, 3 and 4 at every $\varepsilon\in[10^{-7},1]$, without the intermediate-regime order reduction of many IMEX Runge–Kutta schemes. The paper is evidence on the [asymptotic-preserving](/definitions/asymptotic-preserving-scheme.md) page and the [linear multistep](/methods/linear-multistep-method.md) page.

# Key claims

Notation: $k$ the number of steps and order (the source's $q$); $h$ the step; $\alpha_j$ and $\beta_k$ the implicit BDF coefficients, $\beta^{\mathrm E}_j$ the explicit extrapolation coefficients (the source's $\alpha_i$, $\beta$, $\gamma_i$); $F$ the convection matrix and $\mathcal H=\operatorname{diag}(\mathcal H_1,\mathcal H_2)$ the symmetrizer (the source's $A$, $A_0$); $U=(V,W)$ the equilibrium and relaxing components; $\Sigma=-\mathcal H_2\hat S$ (the source's $M$). See the notation map.

1. For $k=1,2,3,4$, the fully discrete IMEX-BDF Fourier–Galerkin scheme (3.1) applied to a linear hyperbolic relaxation system satisfying the structural stability condition, with $\mathcal H_2\hat S$ symmetric, is uniformly stable: $\lVert U_N^n\rVert^2\le C\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\big(\lVert U_N^i\rVert^2+\frac h\varepsilon\lVert W_N^i\rVert^2\big)$ for $t_n\le T$, with $C$ independent of $\varepsilon$, $N$, $h$, under $h\le c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2$ (Thm 3.1, §3.1.2). **Proved**
2. First-order case: with $E^n=\frac12\int(U_N^n)^\top\mathcal HU_N^n$, $E^{n+1}-E^n\le\frac{Ch^2}{\kappa^2}N^2E^n$, so $h\le c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2$ gives $E^n\le e^{CT}E^0$; the implicit relaxation term is dissipative by condition (iii) and drops out (§3.1.1). **Proved**
3. The stability condition is $h\le c_{\mathrm{CFL}}/N^2$, quadratic in the number of Fourier modes rather than the hyperbolic $h\le c/N$; it comes from bounding $h^2\lVert\partial_xU_N\rVert^2\le h^2N^2\lVert U_N\rVert^2$ by $Ch\lVert U_N\rVert^2$ (Thm 3.1, proof; inequality (3.2)). **Proved** (as a sufficient condition)
4. Lemma 2.1 (from Akrivis & Katsoprinakis and Hu & Shu): for the IMEX-BDF coefficients with $k=1,\dots,4$ there exist a positive-definite quadratic form $G$, a positive-semidefinite form $\mathcal A$, linear forms $L_1$, $L_2$ and constants $d_1>0$, $d_2$ that give two exact multiplier identities; the $k=1,2$ data are printed (§2.2). **Stated** (proof in Hu & Shu)
5. Lemma 2.2 extends Lemma 2.1 to vector arguments with any symmetric positive definite weight $H$ through $\tilde u=H^{1/2}u$ (the source writes $H=M^2$, $\tilde u=Mu$) (§2.2). **Proved**
6. Uniform regularity: $\lVert U(\cdot,t)\rVert_{H^s}^2\le C\lVert U(\cdot,0)\rVert_{H^s}^2$ for all $t\ge0$; for $t\ge2\delta_0^{-1}s\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)$, $\lVert\partial_t^{r_1}\partial_x^{r_2}U\rVert^2\le C\lVert U(\cdot,0)\rVert_{H^s}^2$ ($r_1+r_2\le s$) and $\lVert\partial_t^{r_1}\partial_x^{r_2}W\rVert^2\le C\varepsilon^2\lVert U(\cdot,0)\rVert_{H^s}^2$ ($r_1+r_2\le s-1$) (Thm 3.2, (3.8)–(3.10); $s$ here is a Sobolev index, not a stage count). **Proved**
7. The initial layer has length $O(\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon))$: each time derivative costs one layer of length $2\delta_0^{-1}\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)$, obtained by Gronwall on $E'(t)\le-\frac{\delta_0}\varepsilon E+\frac{C\delta_0}\varepsilon\lVert\partial_t\partial_x^rV\rVert^2$ (Thm 3.2, proof, (3.11)). **Proved**
8. The local truncation error of (3.1) is bounded by $Ch^{k+1}$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$ for well-prepared ("Type 1") data with any $T_0\ge0$, or for general ("Type 2") data with $T_0\ge2\delta_0^{-1}(k+2)\varepsilon\log(1/\varepsilon)$ (Lemma 3.1). The stiff term contributes exactly zero, because it is evaluated at $t_{n+k}$ with the weight $\beta_k$ of the BDF derivative approximation. **Proved**
9. Main result: $\lVert U(\cdot,t_n)-U_N^n\rVert^2\le C(h^{2k}+e_{\mathrm{init}})$ with $e_{\mathrm{init}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\big(\lVert V(\cdot,t_i)-V_N^i\rVert^2+(1+\frac h\varepsilon)\lVert W(\cdot,t_i)-W_N^i\rVert^2\big)$ and $C$ independent of $\varepsilon$, $N$, $h$ (Thm 3.3). **Proved**
10. If $\lVert U(\cdot,T_0)\rVert_{H^{2k+1}}+\lVert\partial_tU(\cdot,T_0)\rVert_{H^{2k}}\le C$, then $\lVert U(\cdot,t_n)-U_N^n\rVert^2\le C(h^{2k}+N^{-4k})$, an error $O(h^k+N^{-2k})$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$ (Cor. 3.1). The key step is $\lVert W(\cdot,t_i)-W_N^i\rVert^2\le\varepsilon^2C/N^{4k}$ from the $W$-equation, which cancels the $h/\varepsilon$ weight. **Proved** (the proof takes the starting values to be Fourier projections of the exact solution)
11. The accuracy of the fully discrete schemes "is independent of the relaxation time in all regimes" (Abstract; §1). **Proved** for the linear, constant-coefficient, periodic one-dimensional setting only.
12. Many IMEX Runge–Kutta schemes "suffer from accuracy degeneration when $\varepsilon$ goes to zero", whereas experiments indicate uniform accuracy of certain IMEX multistep schemes over a wide range of $\varepsilon$ (§1). **Stated**
13. The structural stability condition is "tacitly respected by many well-developed physical theories" and has been shown proper for certain moment-closure systems; under it the zero-relaxation limit exists and is stable (§1, §2.1, citing Yong). **Stated**
14. After the change of variables $\tilde U=PU$, the symmetrizer $P^{-\top}\mathcal HP^{-1}$ is block-diagonal $\operatorname{diag}(\mathcal H_1,\mathcal H_2)$ (§2.1, citing Yong, Thm 2.2). **Stated**
15. Linearized Aw–Rascle–Zhang ($\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ to $1$, $T=1$, $N=100$): observed orders 2.00–2.07, 3.00–3.02 and 3.76–4.00 for $k=2,3,4$ at $\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$; for example, at $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ and $h=1.43\times10^{-3}\to1.79\times10^{-4}$, the $k=4$ errors fall from $1.08\times10^{-8}$ to $3.14\times10^{-12}$ (Table 4.1). The order 3.76 at the smallest step for $k=4$ is attributed to machine precision. **Shown**
16. At $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ (Aw–Rascle–Zhang) the second- and third-order rates dip slightly (1.89–2.02 and 2.90–2.95) (Table 4.1); the authors do not discuss it. **Shown**
17. Linearized Broadwell model (linearized at $\rho^\star=2$, $m^\star=0$, $z^\star=1$; $T=2$; $h=5\times10^{-3}\to6.25\times10^{-4}$): uniform orders 2, 3, 4 for $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ to $1$ (Table 4.2). **Shown** (with a printed slip in the second-order column, see below)
18. Linearized Grad moment system (moment order 5, $T=1$, $h=2.5\times10^{-3}\to3.13\times10^{-4}$): uniform orders 2.00–2.07, 2.99–3.02 and 3.97–4.00 for $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$ to $1$ (Table 4.3). **Shown**
19. Initial data must be consistent to $O(\varepsilon)$ for $k=3$ and to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ for $k=4$: the tests use $v(x,0)=-\frac12\rho-\frac\varepsilon2\partial_x\rho-\frac{\varepsilon^2}4\partial_{xx}\rho$ (Aw–Rascle–Zhang) and $z(x,0)=\frac12\rho-\frac\varepsilon4\partial_xm-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{16}\partial_{xx}\rho$ (Broadwell) (§4.1, §4.2). **Shown** (choice of data, consistent with Type 1)
20. The starting values $u^1,\dots,u^{k-1}$ are generated with the IMEX Runge–Kutta schemes ARS(2,2,2) ($k=2,3$, Aw–Rascle–Zhang) or ARS(4,4,3) ($k=4$ Aw–Rascle–Zhang; every $k$ for Broadwell and Grad) at step $h/500$ (§4). **Stated**
21. The spatial error is made negligible by fixing $N=100$ Fourier modes; the reference solution uses a much finer time step (§4). **Stated**
22. Uniform accuracy is proved "up to fourth order" because the multiplier Lemma 2.1 is available for $k\le4$ (§1, Lemma 2.1). **Stated**

*(Compiler checks, not in the sources.)* The $N^{-2}$ step restriction is a sufficient condition from the proof; the tests run with $hN^2$ up to 50 ($hN$ up to 0.5), well outside any modest $c_{\mathrm{CFL}}$. The symmetry of $\mathcal H_2\hat S$ is an extra hypothesis, not part of Yong's condition, although the statement of Thm 3.1 names only the latter.
