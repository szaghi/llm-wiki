# Summary

Most [operator-splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md) methods are built
for two operators. For an $N$-additively split problem
$y'=\sum_{\ell=1}^Nf^{[\ell]}(t,y)$ the standard general-$N$ methods are
first-order Lie–Trotter and second-order Strang. The paper characterizes
every second-order $N$-split method that uses at most $2N$ non-trivial
sub-integrations (sub-flows with nonzero coefficient after merging adjacent
flows of the same operator).

The tools are an $N$-term BCH formula up to third order, proved by induction
on $N$, a recursion for the exponent of the method after $k$ stages, and the
resulting [order conditions](/results/splitting-order-conditions.md): $N$ at
first order and $N(N-1)/2$ at second. A second-order $N$-split method needs at
least $2N-1$ sub-integrations, and Strang is the only one with exactly $2N-1$.
With $2N$ every operator is integrated exactly twice, and the methods are
indexed by a pivot operator $\ell^*$. Five cases result: the one-parameter
two-stage 2-split family OS22$\mu$; the **complex Lie–Trotter** (CLT) pair,
Lie–Trotter composed over the complex steps $\gamma_1h$, $\gamma_2h$ with
$\gamma_{1,2}=\tfrac12\pm\tfrac i2$, for $N\ge3$ (see
[complex-coefficient splitting](/methods/complex-coefficient-splitting.md));
Strang; **Strang-OS22$\mu$**; and **Strang-CLT$_{\ell^*}$** for $N\ge4$.

Three tests use Heun sub-integrators: a 5-split diffusive Molenkamp–Crowley
advection–diffusion problem, a 4-split two-dimensional advection–diffusion–reaction
problem (diffusion parts as in the [heat equation](/benchmarks/heat-equation.md)),
and a 3-split nonlinear complex PDE solved in complex form and as a real
system. All methods show order 2. Complex-coefficient methods lose on
wall-clock time for real problems because complex arithmetic costs more;
Strang-OS22$\mu$ with a grid-searched $\mu$ beats Strang in two of the three
tests; the complex PDE is cheaper in complex arithmetic than as a doubled real
system.

# Key claims

The coefficient of operator $\ell$ in stage $k$ is written $a^{[\ell]}_k$ (the
registry splitting coefficient with an operator index); $D^{[\ell]}$ is the Lie
derivative of $f^{[\ell]}$ (page-local).

1. For $N\ge2$ non-commuting $X^{[i]}$, $\exp(tX^{[1]})\cdots\exp(tX^{[N]})=\exp(Z^{\{N\}}(t))$ with $Z_1=\sum_iX^{[i]}$, $Z_2=\tfrac12\sum_{i<j}[X^{[i]},X^{[j]}]$ and $Z_3$ given explicitly (Prop. 3.1, Eq. 8), proved by induction on $N$ from the two-term BCH formula. **Proved**
2. The exponent of the method after $k$ stages is $h\sum_\ell c^1_{\ell,k}D^{[\ell]}+h^2\sum_{\ell_1<\ell_2}c^2_{\ell_1,\ell_2,k}[D^{[\ell_1]},D^{[\ell_2]}]+O(h^3)$ with the recursions (14)–(15) (Lemma 3.1). **Proved**
3. An $s$-stage $N$-split method is of order 2 iff $\sum_ka^{[\ell]}_k=1$ for all $\ell$ (16a) and $\sum_{k=2}^sa^{[\ell_1]}_k\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a^{[\ell_2]}_j=\tfrac12$ for all $\ell_1<\ell_2$ (16b): $N(N+1)/2$ conditions (Remark 3.1, Eq. 16). **Derived** (analogue of Hairer–Lubich–Wanner Thm 5.6)
4. Order conditions for $p\ge3$ grow "dramatically" with $N$ and are best generated by computer algebra; Auzinger et al. have a Maple program for $s$-stage 3-split methods up to order six that needs Lyndon words entered by hand (Remark 3.2). **Stated**
5. A first-order $N$-split method needs at least $N$ non-trivial sub-integrations, and Lie–Trotter is the only one with exactly $N$ (§3.3). **Stated**
6. A second-order $N$-split method needs at least $2N-1$ non-trivial sub-integrations, and the only one with $2N-1$ is $N$-split Strang, Eq. (3) (Lemma 3.2). **Proved**
7. A second-order $N$-split method with $2N$ non-trivial sub-integrations integrates each operator exactly twice (Lemma 3.3); an operator integrated three times forces a free parameter to vanish, collapsing to $2N-1$ sub-integrations (Tables 1–2, Eq. 16b). **Proved**
8. Without loss of generality the first $N$ sub-integrations are operators $1,\dots,N$ in order, and the method has the form (22): a pivot $\ell^*$ such that every pair $(\ell^*,\ell)$ with $\ell>\ell^*$ satisfies $a^{[\ell^*]}_2a^{[\ell]}_1=\tfrac12$ (Eq. 19) and every operator $\ell<\ell^*$ has $a^{[\ell]}_1=a^{[\ell]}_2=\tfrac12$ (Eq. 21) (§3.3, Eqs. 17–22; the subscript is the sub-integration index). **Proved**
9. $\ell^*=1$, $N=2$: the two-stage second-order 2-split methods form the one-parameter family OS22$\mu$ (Table 3, from earlier work). **Stated**
10. $\ell^*=1$, $N\ge3$: the only solutions are the conjugate pair $\gamma_1=\tfrac12\pm\tfrac i2$, $\gamma_2=\tfrac12\mp\tfrac i2$ with $\gamma_1+\gamma_2=1$, $\gamma_1\gamma_2=\tfrac12$, i.e. Lie–Trotter composed over the steps $\gamma_1h$ then $\gamma_2h$: the complex Lie–Trotter method (CLT) and its conjugate (Table 4, Eqs. 23–26; the $N=3$ case appeared in Auzinger, Koch & Thalhammer 2015, Remark 3.3). **Proved**
11. $\ell^*=N$, $N\ge2$: the method is Strang after merging the two sub-integrations of operator $N$ (coefficients $a$ and $1-a$, $a\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$) (Case 3, Table 5). **Proved**
12. $\ell^*=N-1$, $N\ge3$: operators $1,\dots,N-2$ take $\tfrac12,\tfrac12$ and the last two carry the OS22$\mu$ coefficients (Strang-OS22$\mu$, Table 6); $\mu=0$ recovers Strang, $\mu=0.5$ Strang with operator order $1,\dots,N-2,N,N-1$, and $\mu\in[0,0.5]$ gives non-negative coefficients (Case 4, Remark 3.4). **Proved** (see Printed slips for the coefficient sign)
13. $1<\ell^*<N-1$, $N\ge4$: operators $1,\dots,\ell^*-1$ take $\tfrac12,\tfrac12$ and operators $\ell^*,\dots,N$ take $\tfrac12\pm\tfrac i2$, $\tfrac12\mp\tfrac i2$: Strang-CLT$_{\ell^*}$ and its conjugate (Case 5, Eq. 27, Table 7). **Proved**
14. Hence every second-order $N$-split method with at most $2N$ sub-integrations is Strang, CLT, or a hybrid Strang-CLT$_{\ell^*}$ or Strang-OS22$\mu$ (OS22$\mu$ itself for $N=2$) (§3.3, §5); the forms "do not appear to have been previously published in full generality" (§3). **Proved** (enumeration) / **Stated** (novelty)
15. All these second-order methods can serve as base methods of higher-order $N$-split methods by composition (Remark 3.5, §5). **Stated**
16. $\mu$ in Strang-OS22$\mu$ can be chosen to minimize the leading error term, printed as $\mu=\frac{1-\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}$, or to maximize the stability of a fractional-step Runge–Kutta (FSRK) method; the experiments use a coarse grid search instead (Remark 3.6). **Stated** (see Printed slips)
17. Diffusive Molenkamp–Crowley, 5-split, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/50$, Heun sub-steps: CLT, Strang, Strang-OS22$_{0.3}$ and Strang-CLT$_3$ all converge at order 2 in the final-time $\ell_\infty$ error; CLT and Strang-CLT$_3$ are almost equally accurate; Strang-OS22$_{0.3}$ has the smallest error (about half Strang's) and is the most efficient; the complex methods are least efficient in wall-clock time (Figs. 2–3, §4.1). **Shown**
18. 2D advection–diffusion–reaction, 4-split, $\Delta x=\Delta y=1/40$: all four methods are of order 2 in the final-time $\ell_2$ error; the complex methods (CLT, Strang-CLT$_2$, nearly coincident, about twice Strang's error) are less efficient; Strang and Strang-OS22$_{0.1}$ perform comparably (Figs. 4–5, §4.2). **Shown**
19. With exact sub-flows, central-difference $\partial_{xx}$ and $\partial_{yy}$ on a uniform tensor grid commute and add no splitting error between them; with Heun sub-flows this is generally lost (Remark 4.1). **Stated**
20. Complex PDE (32), 3-split, $\Delta x=0.2$: CLT, Strang and Strang-OS22$_{0.3}$ converge at order 2 in the MRMS error (relative RMS over 100 output times); Strang-OS22$_{0.3}$ is about 2.5× more accurate than Strang; CLT and Strang nearly coincide (Fig. 6). Strang-CLT does not exist for $N=3$. **Shown**
21. For the complex PDE, complex arithmetic is more efficient than the real system of twice the size for all methods, and Strang-OS22$\mu$ is more efficient than Strang (Fig. 7, §4.3). **Shown**
22. Overall, the cost of complex arithmetic on present-day hardware makes the second-order complex methods less efficient than Strang and Strang-OS22$\mu$; with suitable $\mu$, Strang-OS22$\mu$ beats Strang in two of three examples (§5). **Shown**
23. Complex-valued methods can be chosen with positive real parts, "giving them favorable stability properties"; all the methods use almost minimal sub-integrations (abstract). **Stated** (not tested)
24. Fewer non-trivial sub-integrations generally make a method more efficient (§3.3). **Stated**

The CLT pair is the classical second-order complex composition of a
first-order method; the paper's novelty is its uniqueness within the class of
$2N$ sub-integrations for general $N$. No hardware is stated for the timings.

# Printed slips

- Table 3 (p. 8), Table 6 and the Case 4 text (p. 10): the second coefficient of the first operator of the pair is printed $\frac1{2\mu-2}$; with first coefficient $\frac{2\mu-1}{2\mu-2}$ the first-order condition requires $\frac1{2-2\mu}$, and so does the second-order condition $a^{[N-1]}_2(1-\mu)=\tfrac12$; Remark 3.4 holds only with $\frac1{2-2\mu}$ *(compiler check)*. Sign slip repeated three times.
- Remark 3.6 (p. 11): the printed error-minimizing value $\mu=\frac{1-\sqrt2}{\sqrt2}\approx-0.293$ is a sign slip for $\frac{\sqrt2-1}{\sqrt2}=1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\approx0.293$. With the corrected coefficients the leading BCH error coefficients of OS22$\mu$ are $-\frac{4\mu-1}{24(\mu-1)}$ and $-\frac{3\mu-1}{12}$; the sum of their squares is minimal at $\mu=1-1/\sqrt2$ ($2.0\times10^{-4}$), while the printed value gives $2.9\times10^{-2}$, worse than Strang's $8.7\times10^{-3}$ at $\mu=0$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*; the grid-searched $\mu=0.3$ agrees.
- Remark 3.1 (p. 6): the printed closed form of $c^2_{\ell_1,\ell_2,s}$ has the roles of $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ swapped; on 2-split Strang it gives $\tfrac12$ instead of $0$, while recursion (15) and condition (16b) are right *(compiler check)*.
- Proof of Lemma 3.1 (p. 6): "$\exp(A)\exp(B)$" with $A$ the stage exponent and $B$ the previous one should be $\exp(B)\exp(A)$ by Gröbner's ordering; the signs of (15) match the latter.
