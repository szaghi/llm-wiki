# Summary

Part II of the series begun in [Ern & Guermond (2022)](/sources/ern-2022.md)
treats Cauchy problems with a hyperbolic part and a parabolic part (diffusion
plus stiff relaxation) that admit an invariant domain $\mathcal A$: positive
density and internal energy for Euler and Navier–Stokes, the range of the
initial data for scalar convection–diffusion. After a space discretization
$My'=f^{\mathrm E}(y)+f^{\mathrm I}(y)$, with the hyperbolic part explicit and
the parabolic part implicit, the goal is an
[IMEX Runge–Kutta](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) scheme that is
[invariant-domain preserving](/methods/invariant-domain-preserving-time-stepping.md)
(IDP) and mass conservative at high order.

The paper makes **every** IMEX pair of an explicit RK and an explicitly-first
DIRK with shared abscissae IDP and conservative. As in Part I, stage $i$ is
built from a predecessor stage $i'$. Each stage splits into a hyperbolic
prediction and a parabolic update. Each half computes a low-order IDP update
(forward or quasi-linear backward Euler of length $(c_i-c_{i'})h$ with lumped
mass) and the high-order IMEX update, and a conservative limiter of the
[bound-preserving flux limiting](/methods/bound-preserving-flux-limiting.md)
family blends them. Theorem 3.3 proves well-posedness, IDP and conservation
for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}/\Delta c_{\max}$, where $h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ is the
hyperbolic forward-Euler IDP step; no sign condition on the Butcher
coefficients is needed, in contrast with
[SSP methods](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md). This is
[positivity preservation](/properties/positivity-preservation.md) generalized
to systems. Accuracy requires the high-order quasi-linearization to be frozen
at $y_n$, and a step costs $2s-1$ quasi-linear solves.

The authors compute the efficiency ratio $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/(s\Delta c_{\max})$
of classical pairs and derive four new pairs with $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ and
singly diagonal implicit parts: IMEX(3,3;1) (A-stable), IMEX(4,3;1),
IMEX(5,4;1) and IMEX(6,4;1) (L-stable). On a stiff $2\times2$ ODE the third-
and fourth-order schemes drop to second order in the stiff component for
$\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. A 2-D viscous scalar conservation law with $\mathbb P_1$
and $\mathbb P_3$ finite elements and limiting gives optimal rates once the
viscous layer is resolved.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ is the step (source $\tau$), $h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ the
hyperbolic forward-Euler IDP step (source $\tau^*$), $M$ the mass matrix
(source $\mathbb M$; $M^{\mathrm L}$ lumped, $M^{\mathrm H}$ consistent),
$f^{\mathrm E}$ and $f^{\mathrm I}$ the discrete hyperbolic and parabolic
right-hand sides (source $\mathbf F$, $\mathbf G$),
$(A^{\mathrm E},b^{\mathrm E},c)$ and $(A^{\mathrm I},b^{\mathrm I},c)$ the
explicit and implicit tableaux (source $A^{\rm e}$, $A^{\rm i}$), $i'(i)$ the
predecessor stage (source $l'(l)$), $\varepsilon$ the stiffness parameter.

1. Any IMEX pair of an $s$-stage explicit RK and an $s$-stage EDIRK with
   shared abscissae, $c_1=0$, $c_j\ge0$ and
   $A^{\mathrm E}\mathbf 1=A^{\mathrm I}\mathbf 1=c$ can be made IDP and
   conservative by the incremental two-limiter procedure (51)–(61) (§3.3,
   Thm. 3.3). **Proved**
2. Theorem 3.3: under Assumption 2.1 (low-order hyperbolic forward Euler IDP
   for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$; low-order quasi-linear parabolic backward Euler
   IDP), with limiters satisfying Defs. 2.4–2.5 and
   $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}/\Delta c_{\max}$, the scheme is well defined, IDP and
   conservative on the conserved components. **Proved**
3. The limiters conserve mass because the limiter coefficients are symmetric,
   the antidiffusive fluxes skew-symmetric, and the relaxation corrections
   vanish on conserved components (Lemma 2.6). **Proved**
4. The first-order Euler IDP-IMEX scheme with high-order space discretization
   (hyperbolic forward Euler plus limiter, then quasi-linear backward Euler
   plus limiter) is well defined, IDP and conservative for
   $h\in(0,h_{\mathrm{FE}}]$ (Lemma 2.7). **Proved**
5. The low-order Euler IMEX scheme (lumped mass, low-order fluxes) is IDP and
   conservative for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (Lemma 2.2; proof omitted as
   "somewhat standard"). **Stated**
6. Often only the hyperbolic step restricts $h$; the low-order parabolic step
   is IDP for every $h>0$ (Remark 2.3). **Stated**
7. High-order accuracy in time requires the high-order parabolic
   quasi-linearization to be the same, at $y_n$, in all stages; the low-order
   one is based on the hyperbolic prediction to invoke Assumption (27)
   (Remark 3.1). **Stated**
8. A step needs $2s-1$ quasi-linear solves: $s$ low-order and $s-1$
   high-order, since the last row is explicit ($a^{\mathrm I}_{s+1,s+1}=0$)
   (Remark 3.2). **Derived**
9. Efficiency ratio $c_{\mathrm{eff}}:=1/(s\Delta c_{\max})\le1$; the scheme is
   IDP for $h\in(0,c_{\mathrm{eff}}s\,h_{\mathrm{FE}}]$, with the largest step
   $s\,h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ attained for $c_j=(j-1)/s$ (Eq. (63), §3.3).
   **Derived**
10. Heun plus Crank–Nicolson, IMEX(2,2;1/2), has $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/2$;
    explicit plus implicit midpoint, IMEX(2,2;1), has $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$;
    both implicit parts have $R(z)=(2+z)/(2-z)$ (§4.2, Eqs. (76)–(77)).
    **Derived**
11. The ARS(2,3,2)-type pair with L-stable two-stage ESDIRK,
    $\gamma=1-1/\sqrt2\approx0.29289$, $\delta=-\tfrac23\sqrt2$, is printed
    with $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tfrac13(1-\gamma)\approx0.24$ and named
    IMEX(3,2;0.24) (Eq. (78)). **Derived**. Eq. (63) gives
    $1/(3(1-\gamma))\approx0.471$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
12. Strang splitting with explicit and implicit midpoint is a five-stage IMEX
    scheme with one implicit substep; $\max(c_i-c_{i'})=1/4$ and, counting
    the four flux-evaluating stages, $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ (Remark 4.1).
    **Derived**
13. The Crouzeix–Nørsett two-stage third-order ESDIRK with
    $\gamma=\tfrac12+\tfrac1{2\sqrt3}\approx0.78867$, paired with the explicit
    part of ARS §2.4, is A-stable but not L-stable,
    $R(-\infty)=1-\sqrt3\approx-0.73205$; $i'=(1,1,2)$; printed
    $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tfrac13\gamma\approx0.26$, named IMEX(3,3;0.26) (Eqs.
    (79)–(80)). **Derived**. Eq. (63) gives $1/(3\gamma)\approx0.423$
    *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
14. New IMEX(3,3;1): Heun's third-order explicit method ($c=(0,1/3,2/3)$,
    $b=(1/4,0,3/4)$) with a new three-stage third-order EDIRK. The second
    L-stability condition has no solution; minimizing $R(-\infty)$ gives an
    A-stable scheme with limit $1-\sqrt3$. The implicit part comes out singly
    diagonal with $\gamma\approx0.78867$ without imposing it, and has the
    stability function of (80) (Eq. (81)). **Derived**
15. New IMEX(4,3;1): $c_i=(i-1)/4$, L-stable ESDIRK with diagonal
    $0.4358665215084591$, $b=(0,2/3,-1/3,2/3)$, with $b^\top c^3=1/4$ also
    imposed; 12 equations in 13 unknowns solved in Julia at $10^{-15}$ (Eq.
    (82a)). **Derived**
16. The companion explicit method (82b), from Part I, is "the only
    four-stage, third-order ERK method with optimally distributed coefficients
    that is also fourth-order accurate on linear problems"; its
    $\lvert R(\mathrm i\epsilon)\rvert=1+\rho_6\epsilon^6+O(\epsilon^8)$ has
    $\rho_6=-1/72$ (§4.3.2). **Stated** (uniqueness asserted, not proved here)
17. The four-stage third-order pair of ARS §2.7 has efficiency "close to"
    $0.46$; the five-stage pair of ARS §2.8 has $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/8$ (Remark
    4.2). **Stated**
18. The fourth-order five- and six-stage IMEX schemes of Kennedy & Carpenter
    have efficiency ratios "far from being optimal" (§4.4). **Stated**
19. New IMEX(5,4;1): $c_i=(i-1)/5$, L-stable ESDIRK with diagonal
    $0.57281606248213512$ and $a^{\mathrm I}_{51}=0$; 18 equations, 19
    unknowns, Julia at $2\times10^{-16}$; implicit $\rho_6\approx-0.0846$;
    companion explicit part (10 equations, 10 unknowns, two coupling
    conditions included) with $\rho_6\approx-0.0148$ (Eqs. (83a)–(83b)).
    **Derived**
20. The six-stage fourth-order pair of Calvo, de Frutos & Novo with the
    Hairer–Wanner SDIRK (IV.6.16) as implicit part (stiffly accurate) is
    printed with $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1/12\approx0.08$ and named IMEX(6,4;0.08)
    (§4.4.2). **Stated**. With the SDIRK abscissae
    $(1/4,3/4,11/20,1/2,1)$ after $c_1=0$, $\Delta c_{\max}=1/2$ and Eq. (63)
    gives $1/3$ *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
21. New IMEX(6,4;1): $c_i=(i-1)/6$, L-stable ESDIRK with diagonal
    $0.2780538411364528$, the fifth-order linear conditions (71) also
    imposed, $b_4=0.47$ fixed; 24 equations, 26 unknowns, Julia at
    $4\times10^{-16}$; implicit $\rho_6\approx-1.06\times10^{-3}$; companion
    explicit part (14 equations, 15 unknowns) with
    $\rho_6\approx-9.67\times10^{-5}$, $b_4=0.47$ being chosen to make it
    negative (Eqs. (84a)–(84b)). **Derived**
22. On the stiff $2\times2$ ODE (85), $T=4$, all second-order methods are
    second order for both $\varepsilon\in\{1,10^{-6}\}$ (Fig. 1). **Shown**
23. IMEX(3,3;0.26), IMEX(3,3;1) and IMEX(4,3;1) are third order for
    $\varepsilon=1$, but the $y_1$ error drops to second order for
    $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, an [order reduction](/definitions/order-reduction.md)
    in the range $\varepsilon<h$ that the authors refer to Boscarino & Pareschi
    (Fig. 2). **Shown**
24. IMEX(5,4;1), IMEX(6,4;0.08) and IMEX(6,4;1) are fourth order for
    $\varepsilon=1$; at $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ $y_2$ stays fourth order and $y_1$
    drops to second order (Fig. 3). **Shown**
25. The step $h=\mathrm{CFL}\cdot s\cdot h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (91) equalizes the
    number of flux evaluations across methods with different stage counts.
    **Derived**
26. On the viscous scalar conservation law (88) with $\mathbb P_1$ elements all
    five optimal methods are second order once the mesh resolves the viscous
    layer of width $\varepsilon$, first order otherwise (Table 1). **Shown**
27. With $\mathbb P_3$ elements the rates are optimal once the layer is
    resolved: second order for IMEX(2,2;1), fourth order for the others, with
    apparent superconvergence of IMEX(3,3;1) and IMEX(4,3;1) (rates about
    4.07–4.09 at $\varepsilon=2\times10^{-2}$); first order when the layer is
    unresolved (Table 2). **Shown**
28. All methods tested are IDP and deliver the expected accuracy (§6).
    **Shown** (IDP asserted; no bound-violation statistics reported)

# Printed slips

- Eq. (62), Theorem 3.3: "$\tau\in\big(1,\frac{\tau^*}{\Delta c^{\max}}\big]$";
  the interval must start at 0 (Lemma 2.7 and the sentence after (63)).
- Eq. (46): the formula "$l'(l):=\min\{k\in\{1:l-1\}\mid c_l-c_k\ge0\}$"
  always returns 1 and contradicts the prose ("the largest index ... so that
  $c_l-c_{l'(l)}$ is the minimal value"), which the listed values follow.
- Efficiency ratios of three literature pairs: IMEX(3,2;0.24), IMEX(3,3;0.26)
  and IMEX(6,4;0.08) print $\Delta c^{\max}/s$ instead of
  $1/(s\Delta c^{\max})$ of Eq. (63); the values from (63) are about 0.471,
  0.423 and 1/3 *(compiler check, not in the sources)*. For IMEX(2,2;1/2) both expressions give
  1/2. The conclusion that only the new pairs are optimal is unaffected; the
  version of record was not checked.
- §5.1: "The solution is $y_1(t)=y_2^2(t)$, $y_1(t)=e^{-t}$"; the second is
  $y_2(t)=e^{-t}$.
- §4.4.2: "We also set $b_4=0.47$, giving six additional equations"; it is
  one equation (the count 19+4+1=24 confirms).
- Tables 1–2 label IMEX(2,2;1) as "IMEX(2,1;1)" and "(2,1;1)"; the text cites
  both tables as "Table 5.2".
- §5.2: "IMEX(4;4;1)" for IMEX(4,3;1); "third-oder" (§5.1).
- §5.2: the boundary set is defined twice in one display
  ("$\{x_L,x_R\}\times(y_B,y_T),:=(\{x_L\}\cup\{x_R\})\times(y_B,y_T)$");
  $x_0$ in (89) and the CFL number in (91) are not given.
- Eqs. (59)–(60): the implicit sums evaluate every term at the current stage's
  high-order update, while (57) uses the earlier stages for $k<l$.
- "theorem 3.3" typeset in lower case.
