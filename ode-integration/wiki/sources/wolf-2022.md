# Summary

The paper extends SeisSol, an open-source ADER-DG code for seismic waves, to
Biot's low-frequency poroelasticity: a 13-variable linear hyperbolic system
whose Darcy-friction source $Eq$ is stiff. The classical
[ADER](/methods/ader-time-integration.md) predictor, a Taylor series in time
built by the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya procedure, becomes unstable with a stiff
source. The authors set aside the [splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md)
alternative studied by de la Puente et al., saying it does not reach high
order. They use the element-local space–time DG predictor instead: an
implicit Galerkin-in-time solve per element and step, with temporal degree
equal to the spatial degree $N$. The overall scheme stays explicit, with no
global system, and the step is limited by the hyperbolic CFL condition.

Each element and step needs one linear solve with $Q\,B(N)(N+1)$ unknowns
($Q=13$ quantities, $B(N)=\binom{N+3}{3}$ spatial basis functions), 390 for
$N=2$ and 7,640 for $N=6$; storing LU factors would cost 146 MB per element
at $N=5$. The contribution is a block-wise back substitution
([solving implicit stage equations](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md)).
Lemma 1 proves that the stiffness matrices of an orthogonal hierarchical
basis are block upper triangular by degree, and the source matrix is upper
triangular. The solve then reduces to $BQ$ systems of size $(N+1)$ with
precomputed inverses, plus a chain of small GEMMs. The flop count falls by
5.08 ($N=2$) to 24.8 ($N=6$) and the storage by $2.42\times10^2$ to
$5.08\times10^4$ against stored LU factors.

Verification shows order $N+1$ (orders 3–7) on a 3D plane wave, and
agreement with semi-analytical and finite-difference references on a full
space, two half-spaces, a free surface and a new layer-over-half-space
benchmark (LOHp). Near the free surface and the interface the slow P-wave
needs 5 m meshes instead of 50 m to resolve the relative fluid velocity.
On SuperMUC-NG the kernels reach about 40 % of the roofline. Clustered
[local time stepping](/methods/local-time-stepping.md), inherited from
earlier SeisSol work and not analysed here, cuts the time to solution by
6–10 times against global time stepping.

# Key claims

Notation (see the notation map): $h$ the time step (the source's $\Delta t$);
$N$ the polynomial degree in space and time; $B=B(N)$ the number of spatial
basis functions; $Q$ the number of quantities; $E$ the stiff source matrix
and $E^*=hE$; $\mathcal A^*,\mathcal B^*,\mathcal C^*$ the scaled
reference-element flux Jacobians (the source's $A^*,B^*,C^*$).

1. Biot's low-frequency poroelastic system with Darcy friction is a linear hyperbolic system with a stiff reactive source $Eq$, with 13 unknowns (six stresses, three solid velocities, pore pressure, three relative fluid velocities) (§3, Eqs. (1)–(2)). **Stated**
2. $E$ is upper triangular, and this is essential to the solver (§3.2, Fig. 1). **Stated** (sparsity pattern shown)
3. With a stiff source the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya (Taylor) ADER predictor "becomes unstable"; the splitting alternative of de la Puente et al. "does not achieve high-order convergence", so the space–time predictor is used (§4.3). **Stated** (cited)
4. With the space–time predictor ADER-DG is "locally implicit": the stiff source is integrated "without a strict time step restriction", while the overall procedure stays explicit with no global system (§1, §4.3). **Stated**
5. ADER-DG with degree $N$ in space and time converges with order $N+1$ in space and time; it is one-step and stores no intermediate stages (§4.2). **Stated**; convergence **Shown** in §6.1
6. Stability requires a per-element CFL condition $h\le C(N)d/v$ with $C(N)=c/(2N+1)$, $c=\frac12$, $d$ the insphere diameter and $v$ the maximal wave speed; global time stepping takes the minimum over all elements (§4.2). **Stated** (cited)
7. Lemma 1: for an orthogonal basis ordered by non-decreasing degree, the stiffness matrices $K^\alpha$ are block upper triangular: $K^\alpha_{ij}=0$ for $i\in(B_{n-1},B_n]$, $j\in[1,B_n]$, $B_n=\binom{n+3}{3}$ (§5.1). **Proved**
8. In a suitable index unrolling the predictor system (8) is block upper triangular (Fig. 2), which permits block-wise back substitution over the spatial modes $m=B,\dots,1$ (Algorithm 1: $B$ solves of size $Q(N+1)$) (§5.1–5.2). **Derived**
9. Splitting $E^*=F+G$ into its diagonal and strictly upper triangular parts reduces the work to $BQ$ solves of size $(N+1)\times(N+1)$ with matrices $Z-E^*_{pp}I$, $Z=S^{-1}(W-K^\tau)$ the temporal operator (Algorithm 2); fusing the modes of one degree gives Algorithm 3 (§5.2). **Derived**
10. Flop bound of Algorithm 3: $2B\big(Q(N+1)^2+3(N+1)+3(N+1)Q^2+3(N+1)QB\big)+3QB(N+1)^2$; back substitution with stored LU factors costs $2s^2$ for $s$ unknowns (§5.2). **Derived**
11. Flop reduction against LU is 5.08, 9.52, 14.5, 19.7 and 24.8 for $N=2,\dots,6$, about linear in $N$; storage reduction 242, 1,500, 6,180, 19,500 and 50,800; LU storage 1.16, 8.26, 39.5, 146 and 446 MB per element (§5.2, Table 2). **Derived** (counts)
12. The method needs only the 13 inverses $(Z-E^*_{pp}I)^{-1}$ of size $N+1$, the $Q\times Q$ Jacobians and the 6 non-zeros of $E^*$ (§5.2). **Stated**
13. Plane-wave convergence on $[-1,1]^3$ (periodic, $4^3$ to $64^3$ cubes of 5 tetrahedra) gives orders 3–7 in $L^\infty$, $L^1$ and $L^2$ for stress, solid velocity, pressure and fluid velocity; order 7 saturates at machine precision on the finest mesh (§6.1, Fig. 3, App. A). **Shown**
14. Against the semi-analytical full-space solution of Karpfinger et al., phase misfit is below 0.2 % and envelope misfit below about 1 % (slightly exceeded for the fluid velocity at one receiver); in the viscous case the slow P-wave is too diffusive to reach the receivers (§6.2, Figs. 4–5). **Shown**
15. Two half-spaces in contact (inviscid): envelope misfit ≤ 0.26 % and phase misfit ≤ 0.05 % at the reflection receiver; up to 1.54 % and 0.28 % at the transmission receiver, attributed to the slower medium at equal mesh size (§6.3, Table 8). **Shown**
16. Free surface: the text calls all misfits "below 1 %", while Table 9 lists 1.22 % (see slips); the Gar6More3D reference contains an unphysical wave near 1.5 s (§6.4). **Shown**
17. LOHp at standard resolution (50 m, against finite differences at 20 m): solid velocities agree with envelope misfit below 4 %, but the relative fluid velocity near the free surface and the interface has misfit up to 20.5 %; with refinement (5 m near the surface, finite differences at 0.625 m) both converge to the same solution; large misfits are confined above 70 m depth (§6.5, Figs. 9–12). **Shown**
18. Solid velocities at the free surface are insensitive to mesh resolution, so the slow P-wave has negligible direct effect on ground motion (§6.5, Fig. 13). **Shown**
19. The diffusion length of the slow P-wave is about 0.7 m in the LOHp configuration (§6.5). **Stated** (cited)
20. Roofline: node peak 3,792.9 GFLOP/s and 226.8 GB/s (likwid); the proxy follows about 40 % of the roofline, up to 1,406.7 GFLOP/s per node at $N=6$; the kernels are compute-bound for $N=5,6$ (§7.2, Fig. 14). **Shown**
21. The elastic SeisSol kernels reach 2,241 GFLOP/s at $N=6$; part of the gap is that the libxsmm GEMM supports only the scaling $\alpha=1$, while the kernels need $\alpha=h$ (§7.2). **Stated**
22. At $N=6$ the method is "about a factor of 10" faster than an ideally performing LU solver, $T_{STP}\approx0.108\,T_{LU}$ (§7.2). **Derived**
23. Strong scaling from 12 to 400 nodes on a 7,334,942-element LOHp mesh: global time stepping keeps nearly constant node performance (peak 1,385 GFLOP/s); local time stepping is slower in FLOP rate and scales worse with order, from 1,056 GFLOP/s on 25 nodes to 766 GFLOP/s on 400 nodes at $N=6$ (about 72.5 % efficiency) (§7.3, Fig. 15). **Shown**
24. Local time stepping reduces time to solution by a factor 6 to 10 against global time stepping, depending on node count and order (§7.3, Abstract). **Shown** (no timing table printed)
25. The poroelastic predictor costs 1.087 Mflop per element at $N=6$, against 0.477 Mflop for the elastic Cauchy–Kovalevskaya predictor; the overall per-element cost is estimated at about 3.6 times the elastic one (§8.1). **Stated** (estimate)
26. The algorithm applies to any linear hyperbolic PDE with a stiff reactive source whose matrix is upper triangular, such as viscoelastic attenuation and damped Maxwell equations (§8.4). **Stated**

*(Reader caveats, not in the source.)* Claim 3 on splitting concerns the
particular splitting of de la Puente et al.; Strang and higher-order
splittings exist, so the claim does not hold for splitting in general.
Claim 4 is asserted without a stability analysis of the predictor with a
stiff source. The title's local time stepping is inherited from earlier
SeisSol work; the paper gives no algorithm, cluster rate or stability
statement for it, and claim 24 is its only result.

# Printed slips

1. Eq. (5), p. 7: "$q_p(t_i+\delta t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^jq_p}{\partial t_j}(t_i)\frac{\delta t^j}{j!}$" — the sum must start at $j=0$ (the term $q_p(t_i)$ is missing), and "$\partial t_j$" should be $\partial t^j$.
2. §6 opening, p. 13: "(Algorithm 3 in Section 4.3)" — Algorithm 3 is in §5.2.
3. Flop bound, p. 13: "$d_N$" in the term for lines 13–16 where Algorithm 3 has $d_n$; "$\sum_{n=1}^{N}d_n=B$" equals $B-1$ with $d_n=\binom{n+2}{2}$, and the degree index is shifted by one between Lemma 1 ($n\in[1,N+1]$) and the per-degree count. The bound is an upper bound, so Table 2 is not materially affected.
4. §6.4, p. 21: "all misfit values below 1 % (Table 9)", while Table 9 gives a phase misfit of 1.22 % for $w$ at the 0.5 m receiver.
5. §8.4, p. 30: $\epsilon$ called "the relative electric permeability" (permittivity), and Faraday's law printed as $\mu\,\partial_t\mathcal H=\nabla\times\mathcal E$ without the minus sign.
6. §7.3, p. 28: "pedictor".
