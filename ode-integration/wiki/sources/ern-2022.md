# Summary

The paper turns **every** explicit Runge–Kutta method into an
[invariant-domain preserving](/methods/invariant-domain-preserving-time-stepping.md)
(IDP) and mass-conservative method for method-of-lines discretizations of
nonlinear conservation laws. The usual route is the
[SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) paradigm, which
writes the update as a convex combination of forward Euler steps. It caps the
explicit order at four and gives low efficiency (one third for SSPRK(3,3)).
The authors instead ask two things of the space discretization: a low-order
scheme whose forward Euler step is IDP for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, and a
nonlinear limiter that makes a high-order update IDP whenever the low-order
update from the same state is IDP.

The construction rewrites the Butcher tableau in incremental form. Stage $i$
starts from the already-limited stage $i'$ whose abscissa is the closest one
not larger than $c_i$. The low-order update is a forward Euler step of length
$(c_i-c_{i'})h$; the high-order update uses the row increments
$a_{ij}-a_{i'j}$; a limiter blends the two. IDP holds for
$h\,\Delta c_{\max}\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, which gives the efficiency ratio
$c_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge1/(s\Delta c_{\max})$, equal to one for equidistributed
abscissae $c_i=(i-1)/s$. A conservative variant uses skew-symmetric
antidiffusive fluxes, as in
[bound-preserving flux limiting](/methods/bound-preserving-flux-limiting.md);
the result is a form of [positivity preservation](/properties/positivity-preservation.md)
for systems.

New efficiency-one methods are given, up to a seven-stage fifth-order RK(7,5;1)
whose design also shapes the [stability function](/definitions/stability-function.md)
along the imaginary axis. Tests with convex limiting run on 1-D and 2-D
[linear transport](/benchmarks/linear-advection-equation.md), the three-solids
rotation and 2-D [Burgers](/benchmarks/burgers-equation.md). The non-SSP
methods match the SSP ones at equal cost, are more robust as the CFL number
grows, and reach fifth order, which the SSP paradigm cannot. The authors
acknowledge that [Kuzmin et al. (2022)](/sources/kuzmin-2022.md) also limit
after every stage; they claim as original only the incremental rewriting and
the efficiency maximization. Entropy constraints enter only as invariant-domain
bounds ([entropy stability](/properties/entropy-stability.md)).

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ is the step (source $\tau$), $h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ the
largest step for which the low-order forward Euler step is IDP (source
$\tau^*$), $Y_i$ the limited stage values (source $U^{n,l}$), $i'=i'(i)$ the
predecessor stage (source $l'(l)$), with $c_{s+1}:=1$ and
$a_{s+1,j}:=b_j$. The efficiency ratio $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and the invariant
domain $\mathcal A$ are page-local (see Notation map).

1. Every $s$-stage explicit RK method with $c_j\ge0$ and row-sum condition
   $A\mathbf 1=c$ can be made IDP by Algorithm 1 under
   $h\,\Delta c_{\max}\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$ (Lemma 2.2, §2.3). **Proved**
2. The efficiency ratio of an $s$-stage IDP-ERK method satisfies
   $c_{\mathrm{eff}}\ge1/(s\Delta c_{\max})$, and $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ when
   $c_i=(i-1)/s$, $i\in\{1{:}s+1\}$ (Lemma 2.3). **Proved**
3. $1/s\le\Delta c_{\max}\le1$, with $\Delta c_{\max}=1/s$ for
   equidistributed stages (§2.3 after Eq. (13); Lemma 3.4). **Stated**
4. Efficiency ratios: $1/2$ for Heun's SSPRK(2,2), $1/3$ for SSPRK(3,3),
   $1/2$ for SSPRK(4,3), about $0.51$ for SSPRK(5,4); the midpoint rule and
   Heun's third-order method (both not SSP) have $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ (§2.4
   item 1). **Stated** (follows from Lemma 2.3 and the tableaux). The value
   0.51 is the IDP ratio of Kraaijevanger's tableau, not its effective SSP
   coefficient $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathcal C/s\approx1.508/5\approx0.30$;
   for SSPRK(2,2) and SSPRK(3,3) the two numbers coincide *(compiler check,
   not in the sources: $\mathcal C\approx1.508$ as recorded by Ciallella et
   al. (2022), Remark 3.1)*.
5. The conservative IDP-ERK scheme (31)–(34) is IDP for
   $h\in(0,h_{\mathrm{FE}}/\Delta c_{\max}]$, and $y_n$, all stages and
   $y_{n+1}$ carry the same mass $\sum_im_iy_i$ (Lemma 3.4). **Proved**
   (sketch: "combines the arguments" of Lemmas 2.2 and 3.3)
6. The conservative limited forward Euler step (29) is IDP for
   $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, and the low-order, high-order and limited updates
   carry the mass of $y_n$ (Lemma 3.3). **Proved**
7. The only accuracy barriers of IDP-ERK are those of explicit RK (e.g.
   $s>p$ for $p\ge5$), whereas SSP RK methods stop at order four unless they
   step backward in time (Ruuth & Spiteri, Thm 4.1) (§1.2, §2.4 item 3).
   **Stated**
8. Implicit SSPRK methods "cannot be more than first-order accurate"
   (Gottlieb et al., Prop. 6.2), which motivates Part II on IMEX (§1.2, §2.4
   item 4). **Stated**. The cited barrier is for unconditionally SSP implicit
   methods; implicit methods with finite SSP coefficient exist to order six
   ([Kuzmin et al. 2022](/sources/kuzmin-2022.md), §1;
   [Quezada de Luna & Ketcheson 2022](/sources/quezadadeluna-2022.md), §1).
9. RK(4,3;1) satisfies all conditions for order four on linear problems and
   is the only four-stage explicit RK with equidistributed abscissae to do so;
   it has order three on nonlinear problems. Hence no four-stage genuinely
   fourth-order explicit RK with equidistributed abscissae exists (§4.1 after
   Eq. (37)). **Derived** (asserted, no computation shown)
10. Lawson's class of six-stage fifth-order methods requires $c_6=1$, which
    caps the efficiency ratio at $5/6$; Lawson's RK(6,5) has
    $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=2/3$ (§4.1). **Stated**
11. A seven-stage fifth-order RK(7,5;1) with $c_i=(i-1)/7$ exists: 28
    unknowns, 6 row-sum conditions, 17 order conditions to order five, and
    $\lvert R(\mathrm i\epsilon)\rvert=1+\rho_6\epsilon^6+O(\epsilon^8)$ with
    $\rho_6=-0.009$, solved in Julia to $10^{-15}$; coefficients in Eq. (40) to
    16 digits. Its stability function satisfies $\lvert R(\mathrm iy)\rvert\le1$
    for $y\in[-1.562,1.562]$ (§4.1, Fig. 1). **Derived** (numerical
    construction). The printed tableau has row sums $(i-1)/7$ and
    $\sum_ib_i=1$, and satisfies $b^\top c^{k-1}=1/k$ ($k\le5$),
    $b^\top Ac=1/6$, $b^\top(c\cdot Ac)=1/8$, $b^\top Ac^2=1/12$,
    $b^\top A^2c=1/24$ and $b^\top A^3c=1/120$ to $10^{-12}$
    *(compiler check, not in the sources)*.
12. In all tests $h=\mathrm{CFL}\cdot s\cdot h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, so all methods
    make the same number of flux evaluations; all are IDP if
    $\mathrm{CFL}\in(0,1/(\Delta c_{\max}s)]$ (§4.2, Eq. (42)). **Derived**
13. 1-D linear transport, fourth-order finite differences, $L^\infty$: at
    CFL 0.25 SSPRK(2,2) leaves the asymptotic regime on the finest mesh (rate
    −0.50 at $I=3200$, error 5.36E-05), RK(2,2;1) keeps rate 1.99 (8.73E-06)
    (Table 1). **Shown**
14. Same setting, third order: at CFL 0.25 SSPRK(3,3) degrades (rates
    1.35–1.81, 5.39E-06 at $I=3200$), RK(3,3;1) less (2.40E-07), and RK(4,3;1)
    delivers fourth order (5.39E-09, rate 3.94) (Table 2). **Shown**
15. Fourth order: all three methods are optimal at CFL 0.05; at CFL 0.2 only
    RK(5,4;1) stays optimal on fine meshes (5.38E-09, against 9.29E-08 for
    SSPRK(5,4) and 7.69E-07 for RK(4,4;1/2) at $I=3200$); in unshown tests
    RK(4,4;1/2) is still optimal at CFL 0.125 and SSPRK(5,4) is not (Table 3).
    **Shown**
16. Fifth order: RK(6,5;2/3) and RK(7,5;1) reach the asymptotic range at CFL
    0.02 (space is fourth order); at CFL 0.025 RK(7,5;1) is slightly better on
    the finest mesh (5.56E-09 against 1.16E-08) (Table 4). **Shown**
17. 2-D $\mathbb P_1$ finite elements, smooth transport: all methods give
    optimal $L^1$ rates for CFL in $[0.4,0.7]$ (Table 5); in $L^\infty$ at CFL
    0.2 RK(4,3;1) gives fourth order and is the best overall, SSPRK(3,3)
    behaves poorly (Table 6). **Shown**
18. Three-solids rotation at CFL 0.25: the midpoint rule, "often shun ... for
    its lack of stability", performs very well with IDP limiting; $L^1$ rates
    are about 0.8 for RK(2,2;1) and RK(4,3;1) (Fig. 2, Table 7). **Shown**
19. 2-D Burgers (many sonic points), CFL 0.25: all IDP-ERK methods converge
    as well as the SSPRK methods, with printed rates 0.6–1.05 (the text says
    "close to 0.9") (Table 8). **Shown**
20. CFL sweep on Burgers ($401^2$ grid, CFL in $[0.05,1]$): SSPRK(2,2) loses
    accuracy for CFL $\gtrsim1/2$ and stability for CFL $\gtrsim0.9$;
    SSPRK(3,3) loses accuracy for CFL $\gtrsim1/3$ and stability for CFL
    $\gtrsim0.6$; RK(2,2;1), RK(3,3;1) and RK(4,3;1) behave properly over the
    whole range; the efficiency-one fourth-order methods are slightly more
    accurate than SSPRK(5,4) (Fig. 4, §4.4.2). **Shown**
21. Robustness with respect to CFL comes from maximizing the efficiency ratio;
    SSPRK(3,3) is "particularly inefficient" (§4.4.2). **Shown**
22. Fifth-order IDP time stepping is possible, "which is not possible within
    the SSP paradigm" (Abstract, §4.5). **Shown** (fifth order) / **Stated**
    (the impossibility, via Ruuth & Spiteri)
23. Flux-corrected transport is not appropriate when the constraints are not
    affine, which is almost always the case for nonlinear systems; invariant
    domains $\mathcal A=\bigcap_l\{\Psi_l\ge0\}$ with quasiconcave $\Psi_l$
    need convex limiting (§5.3, Eq. (60)). **Stated**
24. Strict local bounds degrade accuracy to first order near extrema; the
    tests relax the local bounds and enforce the global bounds to machine
    accuracy (Remark 5.2, §4.2). **Stated**
25. A diagonal positive low-order mass matrix is necessary for the maximum
    principle in scalar conservation laws (§3.1 after Eq. (22)). **Stated**
26. High-order accuracy survives limiting if the excursions of the unlimited
    update outside the invariant domain are small and infrequent (§1.2); no
    accuracy proof is given. **Stated**

# Printed slips

- Eq. (12): "$l'(l):=\min\{k\in\{1:l-1\}\mid c_l-c_k\ge0\}$". Read literally
  this is always 1, since $c_1=0$. The text ("the difference $c_l-c_{l'}$ is
  nonnegative and the smallest"), the statement $l'(l)=l-1$ for
  nondecreasing $c$, and the reported values $(1,1,2)$ for SSPRK(3,3) all
  require the index minimizing the nonnegative gap $c_l-c_k$.
- §4.1 after Eq. (40): "We have $\lvert\Re(is)\rvert\le1$ for
  $s\in[-1.562,1.562]$"; Fig. 1 plots $\lvert R(\mathrm iy)\rvert$, and $s$
  is also the stage count.
- Eq. (30): the sums inside $U^{n+1}_i$ are printed "$\sum_{i\in\mathcal V}$";
  they must run over $j\in\mathcal I(i)$.
- §3.2: "the limiting operator introduced in §9" should be Eq. (9).
- Table 4: the CFL 0.02 column is labelled "RK(6,5; 1/3)" for RK(6,5;2/3)
  (checked visually, PDF p. 18). Tables 5, 6 and 8 label the midpoint method
  "RK(2,1;1)".
- §4.3–4.4: tables are cited as "Table 4.3.1", "Table 4.3.2", "Table 4.4.1",
  "Table 4.4.2" (subsection numbers instead of table numbers).
- §4.1: "a seven-step, fifth-order method" (seven-stage).
- §4.4.2, Eq. (46): the initial square is "$\lvert x_1-\frac12\rvert\le1$ and
  $\lvert x_2-\frac12\rvert\le1$" in $D=(-0.25,1.75)^2$, which overruns the
  domain; $\le\frac12$ is likely meant, and the outer value $-a$ is never
  given.
- §4.3.1–4.3.2: the initial bump
  $\bigl(4\frac{(x-x_0)(x_1-x)}{x_1-x_0}\bigr)^6$ peaks at $0.3^6$; a unit
  peak needs $(x_1-x_0)^2$ in the denominator. Harmless for relative errors.
- §4.4.2: "rates are close to 0.9" while Table 8 prints 0.60–1.05.
- Typos: "SPPRK" (§1.2), "the ime-stepping method" (§2.1), "advantage over to
  the strong stability" (Abstract), "IDP for all $t\in(0,\tilde\tau]$" for
  $\tau$ (proof of Lemma 2.3), "INIRIA" (Acknowledgments).
