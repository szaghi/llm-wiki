# Summary

Many explicit Runge–Kutta methods are not [strongly stable](/properties/strong-stability.md),
meaning that $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert\le\lVert y_n\rVert$ fails, even for linear
seminegative systems $y'=Ly$ with $\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0$ when $L$ is
non-normal. The authors' earlier [energy analysis](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md)
showed, for example, that the $p$-stage, order-$p$ linear method is not
strongly stable when $p\equiv1,2\pmod4$.

This paper restores strong stability by adding superviscosity to the time
integrator. Superviscosity is a high-order penalty on high-frequency modes,
taken from spectral methods. The paper analyses two variants of these
[superviscosity-stabilized methods](/methods/superviscosity-stabilized-runge-kutta.md):

- the modified method runs the unchanged RK method on a modified operator
  $L_{\mathrm{sv}}$;
- the filtering method post-processes each update with
  $\big(I+\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}((hL)^\top)^{k^*-1}(hL)^{k^*}+\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}((hL)^\top)^{k^*}(hL)^{k^*}\big)$.

The $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ term is dispersive and the $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ term is
diffusive. Here $k^*$ is the leading index of the method's energy expansion.

Theorem 3.1 holds for methods with $k^*=\lceil(p+1)/2\rceil$. It proves that
both variants keep linear order $p$ and are strongly stable, for small
$\lVert hL\rVert$, if $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}<\nu_0=-\beta_{k^*}/2$ and, in
addition, $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=0$ for odd $p$ or $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}>\mu_0$ for
even $p$. Theorem 3.2 proves that the diffusive threshold is sharp. The
sharpness of $\mu_0$ is checked only numerically. Classical RK4 needs the
dispersive term: with $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=0$, even $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=-100$
fails. For nonlinear problems, an adaptive filter with a step-dependent
$\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ guarantees $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert\le\lVert y_n\rVert$
(Prop. 5.1). The paper relates this filter to relaxation Runge–Kutta.

The numerical tests use a non-normal $3\times3$ ODE, upwind and central DG for
linear advection (operator norms, convergence, long-time energy, discontinuous
data) and entropy-conservative DG for Burgers. The paper stresses that this
strong stability is norm monotonicity for linear seminegative operators. It is
distinct from [SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md)
strong stability, although SSP theory is cited for coercive operators.

# Key claims

Notation: $y'=Ly$ with $\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0$. The update is
$y_{n+1}=P(hL)y_n$ with $P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^s\theta_kz^k$. The dissipation
semi-inner product is $[v,w]:=-\langle hLv,w\rangle-\langle v,hLw\rangle$, with
semi-norm $[\![v]\!]$ (it includes the factor $h$; see the notation map).

1. Even for linear seminegative problems, many explicit RK methods fail to be strongly stable. From the authors' earlier work: $p$-stage, order-$p$ linear RK methods are not strongly stable if $p\equiv1$ or $2\pmod4$, and are strongly stable if $p\equiv3\pmod4$ (§1). **Stated**
2. For non-normal operators, eigenvalue analysis can give a wrong step bound or misjudge strong stability, so energy methods are needed (§1). **Stated**
3. For coercive operators, all linear RK methods are strongly stable (by the SSP formulation of Gottlieb–Shu–Tadmor, consistent with conditional contractivity) (§1). **Stated**
4. The energy change of a linear RK step can be rewritten as $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=\sum_{k=1}^s\beta_k\lVert(hL)^ky_n\rVert^2+\sum_{i,j=0}^{s-1}\gamma_{i,j}[(hL)^iy_n,(hL)^jy_n]$ (eq. 2.6). The rewriting uses repeated discrete integration by parts (2.5) and is done by a computer program (§2). **Derived** (recalled)
5. For $\lVert hL\rVert\ll1$, the sign of the energy change is decided by the leading index $k^*$ (the first nonzero $\beta_k$) and by $\beta_{k^*}$ (Prop. 2.1, eq. 2.7). **Proved** (in the earlier paper; restated)
6. For odd $p$: $k^*=(p+1)/2$ and the leading submatrix $\Gamma^*$ is negative definite. For even $p$: $k^*\ge p/2+1$, with equality for $p$-stage, order-$p$ methods (Prop. 2.2). **Proved** (earlier paper)
7. The modified and filtering methods differ only by a high-order term. The filtering method is a splitting of the modified ODE (RK for $y'=Ly$, then forward Euler for the superviscosity part) and "seems to be more efficient" (Remark 3.2). **Stated**
8. Lemma 3.1 gives two-sided bounds (3.6)–(3.7) on the change of $\lVert\cdot\rVert^2$ caused by the superviscosity step, valid for small $\lVert hL\rVert$ and any $0<\varepsilon<1$ (§3.2, proof §4). Here $\varepsilon$ is a free parameter of the estimates. **Proved**
9. Theorem 3.1: for an RK method with $k^*=\lceil(p+1)/2\rceil$, both variants are linearly order $p$ and strongly stable for $\lVert hL\rVert\le\lambda_\star\ll1$ if $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}<\nu_0=-\beta_{k^*}/2$ and either $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=0$ ($p$ odd) or $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}>\mu_0$ ($p$ even). **Proved**
10. The superviscosity term has norm $\mathcal O\big(h^{2k^*-1}(\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}+\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}h)\big)$ (eq. 3.10), so it adds $\mathcal O(h^{p+1})$ to the local error under the theorem's hypotheses. **Proved**
11. Theorem 3.2: the two variants are not strongly stable if $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}>\nu_0$. The proof uses an energy-conservative $L$. The diffusive threshold is sharp "up to an equal sign". **Proved**
12. Whether $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=\nu_0$ or $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=\mu_0$ may be taken depends on the method, and the sharpness of $\mu_0$ is verified only numerically (Remark 3.3, §6.1.1). **Shown**
13. Critical values for $p$-stage, order-$p$ linear RK, $p=1,\dots,6$ (Table 6.1): $\mu_0=-,-\tfrac14,-,\tfrac1{144},-,-\tfrac1{4800}$ and $\nu_0=-\tfrac12,-\tfrac18,\tfrac1{24},\tfrac1{144},-\tfrac1{720},-\tfrac1{5760}$. **Stated** (computed; the $\nu_0$ values were confirmed by the reader)
14. For $p=3$, $\nu_0=1/24>0$, so RK3 tolerates anti-superviscosity: it remains strongly stable for $0<\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}\le\nu_0$ (§6.1.1, Table 6.3). **Shown**
15. Dispersive superviscosity is necessary for RK4. With $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=0$, even $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=-100$ does not give $\lVert P(hL)\rVert\le1$ for small $h$ on the non-normal test (6.2) (Table 6.3). **Shown**
16. With $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ and $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ only 1% beyond the critical values, $\lVert P(hL)\rVert\le1$ holds for small enough $h$ on the $3\times3$ test and on the upwind DG operator (Tables 6.3, 6.5). **Shown**
17. With $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=\mathcal O(1)$ the stabilized methods keep order $p$ for $p=1,\dots,4$. With $\mu_{\mathrm{sv}}=\mathcal O(1)$, odd-order methods lose an order: RK3 with $(\mu_{\mathrm{sv}},\nu_{\mathrm{sv}})=(1,0)$ gives order 2.00 (Table 6.4). **Shown**
18. Upwind DG of degree $p-1$ with stabilized RK$p$, $p=1,\dots,5$, at $h=0.02\Delta x$: the design orders are attained, and the modified and filtering errors are nearly identical (Table 6.6). **Shown**
19. With degree-$p$ upwind DG and $p=1,2,5$ at $h=0.05\Delta x$, the original methods let the $L^2$ norm grow, while superviscosity makes it decay (Fig. 6.1). **Shown**
20. Anti-superviscosity ($\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=1/24$ with RK3) reduces the long-time damping of $\sin(5x)$ at $T=1000$, and similarly for central flux at $T=10\,000$. The amplitude still decays at larger $T$ (Figs. 6.2–6.3). **Shown**
21. For discontinuous data, $\mathcal O(h^4)$ superviscosity only mildly reduces over- and undershoots. Dispersive superviscosity pushes the oscillations to one side, and a strong $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=-2$ smooths central-flux oscillations (§6.2.4). **Shown**
22. Prop. 5.1: let $y_+$ be the unfiltered RK update. The adaptive filter $y_{n+1}=(I+\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}D^\top D)y_+$, with $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}=\min\{(\lVert y_n\rVert^2-\lVert y_+\rVert^2)/\lVert Dy_+\rVert^2,0\}$, gives $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert\le\lVert y_n\rVert$ if $\lvert\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}\rvert\lVert D\rVert^2\le1$ (§5). **Proved**
23. The adaptive filter leaves accuracy unaffected when $\lVert D^\top Dy_+\rVert/\lVert Dy_+\rVert^2=\mathcal O(1)$. For linear problems with $D=(hL)^{k^*}$ and even $p$, it can require $\lVert hL\rVert=\mathcal O(h^{1/(2k^*)})$, which is stricter than the usual step constraint (Remarks 5.1–5.2). **Derived**
24. Burgers equation with entropy-conservative DG and the adaptive filter: orders 2 (RK2) and 5 (Fehlberg six-stage, order 5) at $T=0.3$. The $L^2$ norm decreases with filtering and grows without it. At $T=1.5$, a strong fixed $\nu_{\mathrm{sv}}$ is needed to smooth the oscillations away from the shock (Table 6.7, Figs. 6.8–6.10). **Shown**

# Printed slips

- **Prop. 2.2(3) does not agree with Table 6.1 and $\nu_0=-\beta_{k^*}/2$.** The paper prints $\beta_{k^*}=\frac{(-1)^{(p+1)/2}}{(p+1)!}$ for odd $p$ and $\frac{(-1)^{p/2+1}}{p!(p+2)}$ for even $p$. A symbolic check of $\lvert P(iy)\rvert^2-1$ gives $\beta_{k^*}=1,\tfrac14,-\tfrac1{12},-\tfrac1{72},\tfrac1{360},\tfrac1{2880}$ for $p=1,\dots,6$, which reproduces Table 6.1. The printed formulas are therefore off by factors $-2$ (odd $p$) and $2$ (even $p$). The consistent forms are $\frac{2(-1)^{(p-1)/2}}{(p+1)!}$ and $\frac{2(-1)^{p/2+1}}{p!(p+2)}$. The journal version was not checked.
- Table 6.3, row $p=3$, $(\mu,\nu)=(0,0)$: the entries are positive at $\tau=10^{-3}$ to $10^{-5}$, contradicting the text "the original third order RK method itself is strongly stable". The same table prints "-3,70E-32" with a comma.
- Table 6.5 writes zero as "0.00E-99" and "0.00E-93 0.00E-92 0.00E-91".
- Eq. (3.4) mixes $u$ and $v$: "$[\![Z^{k^*-1}u]\!]^2$ … $\forall v\in V$".
- Lemma 4.3 proof: "For (4.11)" should read (4.12), and (4.16) has $Z^{k-1}v$ for $Z^{l-1}v$.
- Eq. (4.32) drops the factor $\xi(\lambda)$, and eq. (4.31) has $Z^{k^*}u$ for $Z^{k^*}u_0$.
- §4.3 announces the case "$\mu\ne0$, $p\ge2$", but the heading reads "($\mu=1$, $p\ge2$.)".
- §5: "$\|D^+u\|=0$" should read $\|Du_+\|=0$.
- §6.2.4: the text gives $1_{[\pi/2,3\pi/2]}$, while the captions of Figs. 6.4–6.7 give $1_{[-\pi/2,3\pi/2]}$. The Fig. 6.7 caption appears to be copied from Fig. 6.4.
- Figs. 6.2(b), 6.3(b) and 6.8 label the time axis "$x$".
- Ref. [52] has pages "51:107134", and ref. [44] gives "Clarendon Press, Oxford:69–82".
