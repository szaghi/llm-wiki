# Summary

The paper studies how Runge–Kutta methods reproduce the energy dissipation of
an autonomous linear seminegative system $y'=Ly$. Here $\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0$
and $L$ is bounded but not necessarily normal. The continuous law is
$\frac{d}{dt}\lVert y\rVert^2=-[\![y]\!]^2$, where $[\![\cdot]\!]$ is the
semi-norm induced by $-(L+L^\top)$. The authors' explicit-RK
[energy method](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md) is extended to any
method given by a rational [stability function](/definitions/stability-function.md)
$R=Q^{-1}P$, so implicit schemes are covered too.

The contributions are:

- a series expansion of the continuous energy law over one step, from a
  Cholesky-type factorization of a scaled Hilbert matrix;
- a general discrete energy identity, obtained from $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2$
  written in $w_n=Q(hL)^{-1}y_n$ by repeated discrete integration by parts;
- criteria for [strong stability](/properties/strong-stability.md). Strong
  stability is unconditional when the diagonal matrix $B$ and the matrix
  $\Upsilon$ are both negative semidefinite. Conditional weak($\kappa$), strong
  and not-strong verdicts follow from the first nonzero $\beta_\zeta$ and the
  largest negative-semidefinite leading block of $\Upsilon$;
- a unified energy law for every diagonal $(s,s)$ Padé approximation
  ([diagonal Padé strong stability](/results/diagonal-pade-strong-stability.md)).
  It factors $\Upsilon=-U^\top\hat DU$ with the same weights
  $\hat d_k=(k!)^2/((2k)!(2k+1)!)$ as the continuous law. This proves
  unconditional strong stability and shows that the discrete law truncates the
  continuous one, with energy-dissipation error $O(h^{2s})$ globally.

The diagonal Padé approximations are the stability functions of the Gauss
methods ([fully implicit Runge–Kutta](/methods/fully-implicit-runge-kutta.md)).
The paper does not claim the stability conclusion as new: A-stability with von
Neumann's lemma already implies it ([A-stability](/properties/a-stability.md)).
The new result is the explicit identity.

Worked examples cover backward Euler, Crank–Nicolson, two 2-stage DIRKs, the
(0,3) Padé approximation (weakly(4) stable) and the (4,1) Padé approximation
(conditionally strongly stable). The numerical tests use a non-normal $3\times3$
system, P1-DG advection and P0-LDG dispersion. The paper contrasts its approach
with eigenvalue analysis and with [SSP](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md)
arguments, which apply only to coercive problems.

# Key claims

Notation: $y'=Ly$ with $\langle Lv,v\rangle\le0$. The dissipation semi-inner
product is $[v,w]:=-\langle Lv,w\rangle-\langle v,Lw\rangle$, with semi-norm
$[\![\cdot]\!]$. The update is $y_{n+1}=R(hL)y_n$ with
$R=Q^{-1}P$, $P(z)=\sum_i\theta_iz^i$ and $Q(z)=\sum_i\vartheta_iz^i$, and
$s=\max(\deg P,\deg Q)$. The auxiliary vector is $w_n:=Q(hL)^{-1}y_n$.

1. Theorem 2.1 (eqs. 2.1–2.3, proof App. C): $\lVert y(t_n+h)\rVert^2-\lVert y(t_n)\rVert^2=-\sum_{k=0}^\infty\hat d_kh^{2k+1}[\![L^k\hat y^{(k)}]\!]^2$, where $\hat d_k=\frac{(k!)^2}{(2k)!(2k+1)!}$, $\hat y^{(k)}=\sum_{j\ge k}\hat\mu_{k,j}(hL)^{j-k}y(t_n)$ and $\hat\mu_{k,j}=\frac{(2k+1)!\,j!}{k!(j-k)!(k+j+1)!}$. **Proved**
2. As an operator inequality: $(e^{hL})^\top e^{hL}-I=\sum_k\hat d_kh^{2k+1}\hat U_k^\top(L^\top+L)\hat U_k\le0$ (Remark 2.4, eq. 2.5). **Proved**
3. $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=\sum_{i,j=0}^s\alpha_{i,j}h^{i+j}\langle L^iw_n,L^jw_n\rangle$, with $\alpha_{i,j}=\theta_i\theta_j-\vartheta_i\vartheta_j$ (Lemma 3.2, eq. 3.4). **Proved**
4. Repeated discrete integration by parts converts this into $\sum_{k=0}^s\beta_kh^{2k}\lVert L^kw_n\rVert^2+\sum_{i,j=0}^{s-1}\gamma_{i,j}h^{i+j+1}[L^iw_n,L^jw_n]$, with $\beta_k$ and $\gamma_{i,j}$ in closed form (3.9)–(3.10) (Lemma 3.3). **Stated** (the combinatorial proof is deferred to a paper in preparation)
5. Theorem 3.4 gives a general discrete energy identity (3.11) for any RK method. It needs a nonnegative diagonal shift $\Delta$ that makes $\Upsilon-\Delta$ negative semidefinite. **Proved**
6. Theorem 3.5: if $\Upsilon\le0$ and $B=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_k)\le0$, the method is unconditionally strongly stable: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert\le\lVert y_n\rVert$ for all $h\ge0$. **Proved**
7. Theorem 3.7.1: let $\zeta$ be the index of the first nonzero $\beta_k$, $\rho$ the largest order of a negative-semidefinite leading principal submatrix of $\Upsilon$, and $\kappa=\min\{2\zeta,2\rho+1\}$. Then the method is weakly($\kappa$) stable: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2\le(1+C(h\lVert L\rVert)^\kappa)\lVert y_n\rVert^2$ for $h\lVert L\rVert\le\lambda_0$. If moreover $h\lVert L\rVert^{1+1/(\kappa-1)}\le\lambda_0$, then $\lVert y_n\rVert^2\le e^{Ct_n}\lVert y_0\rVert^2$. **Proved**
8. Theorem 3.7.2: if $\zeta\le\rho$ and $\beta_\zeta<0$, the method is strongly stable for $h\lVert L\rVert\le\lambda_0$. **Proved**
9. Theorem 3.7.3: if $\beta_\zeta>0$, the method is not strongly stable for generic seminegative systems. A skew-symmetric $L$ gives $\lVert R(hL)\rVert>1$ for all $h\lVert L\rVert\in(0,\lambda_0]$. **Proved**
10. For semi-discrete hyperbolic problems $\lVert L\rVert=O(\Delta x^{-1})$. Strong stability then needs $h\le C\Delta x$, and weak($\kappa$) stability with an exponential bound needs $h\le C\Delta x^{1+1/(\kappa-1)}$ (Remark 3.8). **Derived**
11. Backward Euler: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=-h^2\lVert Ly_{n+1}\rVert^2-h[\![y_{n+1}]\!]^2$ (Ex. 4.1). **Proved**
12. Crank–Nicolson and the implicit midpoint rule: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=-h[\![w_n]\!]^2$, with $w_n=(I-\frac h2L)^{-1}y_n$ (Ex. 4.2). **Proved**
13. Qin–Zhang 2-stage symplectic DIRK: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=-h[\![w_n]\!]^2-\frac1{16}h^3[\![Lw_n]\!]^2$ (Ex. 4.3). **Proved**
14. Kraaijevanger–Spijker 2-stage DIRK: $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=-3h^2\lVert Lw_n\rVert^2-\frac34h^4\lVert L^2w_n\rVert^2-h[\![(I-\frac h2L)w_n]\!]^2-\frac32h^3[\![Lw_n]\!]^2$ (Ex. 4.4). **Proved**
15. The (0,3) Padé approximation is A($\alpha$)-stable, with $\alpha$ printed as "$\le88.23^\circ$". It has $\zeta=\rho=2$ and $\beta_2=1/12>0$, so it is weakly(4) stable and not strongly stable (Ex. 4.5). **Proved**
16. The (4,1) Padé approximation has $\zeta=\rho=3$ and $\beta_3=-1/1800<0$, so it is conditionally strongly stable (Ex. 4.6). **Proved**
17. Theorem 5.1: for the $(s,s)$ diagonal Padé approximation, $B=0$ and $\Upsilon$ is negative definite for every $s\in\mathbb Z^+$. The explicit factorization is $\Upsilon=-U^\top\hat DU$, with $\hat D=\operatorname{diag}(\hat d_k)_{k=0}^{s-1}$ and $U$ given in (5.5). The proof (§5.3–5.5, Apps. D–F) uses rational continuation in $s$, Pochhammer identities, telescoping sums and Watson's ${}_3F_2$ formula. **Proved**
18. Theorem 5.2: every diagonal Padé approximation satisfies $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2=-\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\hat d_kh^{2k+1}[\![L^ky^{(k)}]\!]^2$ (eqs. 5.6–5.7). Hence it is unconditionally strongly stable for general linear seminegative (possibly non-normal) systems. **Proved**
19. Theorems 5.4–5.5: the discrete law truncates the continuous one. The weights $\hat d_k$ for $k<s$ are the same, and the expansion coefficients agree for $k\le j\le s-1$. **Proved**
20. Energy-dissipation accuracy: the per-step error is $O(h^{2s+1})$ and the global error is $\Delta E=O(h^{2s})$ (Remark 5.6). **Derived** (no explicit remainder bound)
21. The discrete counterpart of claim 2 is $R(hL)^\top R(hL)-I=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}\hat d_kh^{2k+1}U_k^\top(L^\top+L)U_k\le0$ (Remark 5.7, eq. 5.9). **Proved**
22. Ex. 6.1 (non-normal $3\times3$ $L$, $t=8$, $h\in\{1.6,0.8,0.4,0.2\}$): the (3,3) and (4,4) Padé approximations show $l^2$-error and $\Delta E$ orders of about 6 and 8. For example, the (4,4) $l^2$ error goes from 2.77e-8 to 1.64e-15 with orders 7.96, 7.99 and 8.07 (Table 1). **Shown**
23. The measured dissipation $\lVert y_n\rVert^2-\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2$ is positive and agrees with identity (5.6) in all three examples (Fig. 1). **Shown**
24. The (2,2) Padé approximation runs robustly at $h=0.1$ for P1-DG advection and for P0-LDG dispersion $\psi_t+\psi_{xxx}=0$, both with $\Delta x=0.05$. An explicit method would need $h\le C\Delta x^3$ for the dispersion problem (Exs. 6.2–6.3). **Shown**
25. Recalled background (§1):
    - A-stability implies $L^2$ stability for linear seminegative systems (von Neumann's lemma).
    - For non-normal $L$, eigenvalue analysis is only a necessary condition.
    - Diagonal Padé approximations and the first two subdiagonals are A-stable; the others are not.
    - Truncated-Taylor explicit RK of order $p$ is strongly stable if $p\equiv3\pmod4$ and not strongly stable if $p\equiv1,2\pmod4$.

    **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **Ex. 4.3 (p. 9):** the paper prints $\Upsilon=\operatorname{diag}\{-1,-1/16\}=-U^\top DU$ with $D=\operatorname{diag}\{1,\frac14\}$ and $U=I$. That product gives $\operatorname{diag}\{-1,-1/4\}$. The value $D=\operatorname{diag}\{1,\frac1{16}\}$ is consistent with the printed energy identity and with a recomputation from (3.10).
- Eq. (3.17) (p. 7): the right-hand side ends with $\|L^\rho u^n\|^2$, where (3.13) and the later use have $\|L^\rho w^n\|^2$.
- §6, Ex. 6.1 (p. 18): "listed in section 6" should refer to Table 1.
- "principle submatrix" for "principal submatrix" (Lemma 3.6, Exs. 4.5–4.6) and "negativesemidefinite" (p. 10).
- Ex. 4.5 gives A($\alpha$)-stability "with $\alpha\le88.23^\circ$". The angle is a specific value.
- Ref. [39] reverses the author order of HW-II ("G. Wanner and E. Hairer") and misspells "Difterential". Ref. [25] gives "J. V. Neumann".
