# Summary

The paper attacks two obstacles to high-order non-oscillatory shock capturing
for hyperbolic conservation laws, especially in several dimensions and with
forcing terms. The Lax–Wendroff (Taylor-series) time discretization of the
original ENO schemes is hard to program and has TVD/TVB theory only up to
second order in time. Cell-average ENO schemes also need a reconstruction of
point values.

For the first obstacle, §II introduces **Runge–Kutta type TVD time
discretizations** of the method-of-lines system $y'=L(y)$. Any explicit
[Runge–Kutta method](/definitions/runge-kutta-method.md) is rewritten in the
convex form (2.12), later called the Shu–Osher form, with $\alpha_{ij}\ge0$ and
$\sum_j\alpha_{ij}=1$. If the forward Euler step is
[total variation diminishing](/definitions/total-variation-diminishing.md)
under a CFL bound, the Runge–Kutta step is a convex combination of Euler steps
and is TVD under that bound times $\min\alpha_{ij}/|\beta_{ij}|$
(Prop. 2.1). A negative $\beta_{ij}$ requires the downwind operator $\tilde L$,
a device the paper credits to Shu's 1986 report. Remark 2.1 extends the
argument from the total variation to any convex functional. Gottlieb et al.
(2001) recall this result and rename the class
[strong-stability-preserving Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md).
The paper gives a second-order method it calls optimal (Heun, CFL coefficient 1),
a third-order method it prefers after a search (coefficient 1; the SSPRK(3,3) for
which Ketcheson (2019) cites the paper), which
needs only the last stage derivative and so lowers storage
([low-storage methods](/methods/low-storage-runge-kutta.md)), fourth-order
methods that all need $\tilde L$, and a six-stage scheme copied from Lambert's
book.

For the second obstacle, §III builds ENO schemes on fluxes with a
Lax–Friedrichs flux splitting; this spatial part enters the bundle only as the
generator of the semi-discrete system. §IV tests the schemes on the 1-D and
2-D [Burgers equation](/benchmarks/burgers-equation.md), on 2-D
[linear advection](/benchmarks/linear-advection-equation.md) of a rotated
square, and on nonconvex Riemann problems. The paper prints proofs only for
Prop. 2.1 and the TVB bound (3.17). Its accuracy conditions come from
"straightforward but tedious Taylor expansions (which we omit)".

# Key claims

Registry notation: $h$ is the step (source $\Delta t$), $L$ the semi-discrete
operator of $y'=L(y)$, $\nu$ the CFL number (source $\lambda=\Delta t/\Delta x$)
and $\nu_0$ the forward-Euler TVD bound (source $\lambda_0$, so
$h_{\mathrm{FE}}=\nu_0\Delta x$). Stages are written in the source's 0-based
Shu–Osher form $y^{(i)}$, with $y^{(0)}=y_n$ and $y^{(s)}=y_{n+1}$
(source $u^{(i)}$, $m$ stages).

1. Linear stability analysis is "certainly not enough" for shock problems,
   because "linear stability does not imply convergence if shocks or other
   discontinuities are present"; it "does not apply to ENO schemes at all"
   because their stencils move (§I, p. 5). **Stated**
2. For Lax–Wendroff type time discretizations, "speaking theoretically, only
   second order in time TVD or TVB schemes exist of this type", and TVD/TVB is
   "not easy to prove" even when the semi-discrete system and its forward
   Euler version are TVD/TVB (§I, pp. 4–5). **Stated**
3. Multi-level TVD time discretizations (Shu's 1986 report) must start their
   first levels by another method of the same order and store all levels;
   one-step Runge–Kutta methods are self-starting and use less storage (§II,
   p. 7). **Stated**
4. A time discretization $y_{n+1}=S(y_n)$ is "$r$-th order TVD" if it is
   accurate to $O(\Delta x^{r+1})$ (2.4) and
   $\mathrm{TV}(S(y))\le\mathrm{TV}(T(y))$ (2.5), with $T=I+hL$ the forward
   Euler step; TVD (2.6a) or TVB (2.6b) of $T$ then transfers to $S$ (§II,
   Eqs. (2.3)–(2.6)). **Stated** (definition). As printed, (2.5) compares
   with the total variation after one Euler step, not with $\mathrm{TV}(y)$.
5. Any explicit Runge–Kutta method in stage-value form (2.10),
   $y^{(i)}=y^{(0)}+h\sum_{k<i}c_{ik}L(y^{(k)})$, can be rewritten for any
   $\alpha_{ik}\ge0$ with $\sum_k\alpha_{ik}=1$ as (2.12),
   $y^{(i)}=\sum_{k<i}\bigl(\alpha_{ik}y^{(k)}+\beta_{ik}hL(y^{(k)})\bigr)$,
   with $\beta_{ik}=c_{ik}-\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{i-1}c_{\ell k}\alpha_{i\ell}$
   (§II, p. 9). **Derived**
6. Prop. 2.1: scheme (2.12) is TVD under $\nu\le\nu_0\min_{i,k}\alpha_{ik}/|\beta_{ik}|$
   (2.13) if $L$ is replaced by $\tilde L$ wherever $\beta_{ik}<0$, where
   $\tilde L$ approximates the same spatial operator to the same order and
   $I-h\tilde L$ is TVD under the same bound (2.7)–(2.9); the scheme is "a
   convex combination of TVD (or TVB) operators" (§II, p. 10). **Proved**
   (one-line convexity argument). The paper credits the $\tilde L$ device
   to Shu's 1986 report ("a trick used in [13]"); the word "downwind" does
   not appear.
7. Remark 2.1: the total variation may be replaced by any convex map $G$ into
   the non-negative reals on a Banach space of grid functions; then
   $G(y_m)\le G(y_0)$ and the formal order is unchanged (p. 10). **Stated**
8. Classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta methods have all $c_{ik}\ge0$ but
   $\beta_{ik}$ that "may well be negative"; "most classical Runge–Kutta
   methods lead to small CFL numbers in (2.13) as well as negative
   $\beta_{ik}$'s", so the authors design directly in form (2.12) (p. 10).
   **Stated**
9. Second order, two stages: conditions (2.14), $\alpha_{20}=1-\alpha_{21}$,
   $\beta_{20}=1-\frac1{2\beta_{10}}-\alpha_{21}\beta_{10}$,
   $\beta_{21}=\frac1{2\beta_{10}}$, with $\beta_{10}$, $\alpha_{21}$ free. The
   "optimal" scheme "(considering CFL restriction (2.13) and whether $\tilde L$
   appears)" is (2.15),
   $y^{(1)}=y_n+hL(y_n)$, $y_{n+1}=\frac12y_n+\frac12y^{(1)}+\frac12hL(y^{(1)})$,
   CFL# $=1$, equal to Heun's (modified Euler) method (2.16) (p. 11).
   **Derived** (optimality "can be verified"; no proof printed)
10. Third order, three stages: a five-parameter family (2.17); "extensive
    searching" gives the preferred scheme (2.18),
    $y^{(1)}=y_n+hL(y_n)$,
    $y^{(2)}=\frac34y_n+\frac14y^{(1)}+\frac14hL(y^{(1)})$,
    $y_{n+1}=\frac13y_n+\frac23y^{(2)}+\frac23hL(y^{(2)})$, CFL# $=1$, without
    $\tilde L$. Stage $i$ needs only $L(y^{(i-1)})$, "lowering the storage
    requirement significantly" (p. 12). **Derived** (search; no optimality
    proof). In Butcher form (2.19) it is $c=(0,1,\frac12)$, $a_{21}=1$,
    $a_{31}=a_{32}=\frac14$, $b=(\frac16,\frac16,\frac23)$.
11. The authors "have been unable to identify (2.19) with any of the
    'classical' third order Runge–Kutta methods"; the classical third-order
    methods in Lambert lead to negative $\beta$ and small CFL numbers and "are
    hence inferior" (p. 12). **Stated**
12. Fourth order: 7 equations in 16 unknowns, 9 free parameters; "Extensive
    searching seems to indicate that we cannot avoid negative constants
    $\beta_{ik}$ this time" (pp. 12–13). **Stated** (numerical search, four
    stages; no proof)
13. Classical RK4 in form (2.12) is (2.20), CFL# $=\frac23$, and needs
    $\tilde L(y^{(0)})$ and $\tilde L(y^{(1)})$; the alternative third stage
    (2.21) raises the CFL# to $\frac{137}{200}$ (p. 13). **Derived** (see
    Printed slips for the last term of (2.21))
14. A second fourth-order scheme (2.22) has CFL# $=0.87$ and still needs
    $\tilde L(y^{(0)})$ and $\tilde L(y^{(1)})$ (p. 13). **Derived**
15. The paper writes "a fifth order method given on page 143 of [7]"
    (Lambert's book) in form (2.12) as the six-stage scheme (2.23), CFL#
    $=\frac7{30}$, needing $\tilde L(y^{(0)})$, $\tilde L(y^{(1)})$,
    $\tilde L(y^{(3)})$ (p. 14). **Derived**. The printed coefficients do not
    satisfy the third-order conditions; see Printed slips.
16. Explicit Runge–Kutta forms give "$(m+1)$-th order accurate methods … for
    $m\le3$; $m$-th order methods for $m=4,5,6$; or $(m-1)$-th order methods
    for $m=7,8$", citing Gear (p. 9). **Stated**. Here $m$ is the number of
    stages minus one; see Printed slips.
17. For multi-dimensional problems the methods of §II are used with "CFL
    numbers shrunk by a factor $(d)^{-1}$" (§III, p. 22); in Example 3
    "further experiments using 1-dimensional CFL numbers led to
    instability–overflow" (p. 27). **Stated** / **Shown**
18. Flux-based ENO with the Lax–Friedrichs splitting $f^\pm=\frac12(f\pm\alpha u)$
    and the correction (3.9) gives conservative schemes of spatial order $2m$,
    with constants $a_2=-\frac1{24}$, $a_4=\frac7{5760}$ (§III, Eqs. (3.1)–(3.15)).
    **Derived**
19. The second-order flux ENO scheme "is similar to the usual minmod second
    order TVD scheme" with minmod replaced by "choosing the value closer to
    zero", and "this is still a TVD method" (Remark 3.1(b), p. 18). **Stated**
20. With the bounded divided differences (3.16) the flux is
    $\hat f^{\mathrm{TVD2}}+\hat c$ with $|\hat c|\le M\Delta x^2$ (3.17),
    which "clearly implies TVB" (p. 19). **Proved** (sketch)
21. The non-conservative variant (3.18) gives the wrong shock location
    without (3.16) and severe smearing with it, on a Burgers Riemann problem
    with $u_{\mathrm{left}}=\frac12$, $u_{\mathrm{right}}=-1$; it was abandoned
    (p. 20). **Shown** (no figure or table printed)
22. ENO with Lax–Friedrichs building blocks keeps full order because the
    Lax–Friedrichs flux is $C^\infty$; Godunov, Engquist–Osher and
    entropy-fixed Roe fluxes are not smooth at sonic points and lose accuracy
    there (pp. 21–22). **Stated**
23. Example 1 (1-D Burgers, $t=0.3$, smooth): 3-3-LF-ENO (CFL 0.8, time
    scheme (2.19)) and 4-4-LF-ENO (CFL 0.6, time scheme (2.20)) reach
    $L_\infty$ orders 3.50, 2.89 and 4.25, 4.35, and $L_1$ orders 3.66, 3.13
    and 4.41, 4.76, for $\Delta x=\frac1{10}\to\frac1{20}\to\frac1{40}$
    (Table 1). **Shown**
24. Example 2 (2-D Burgers, CFL 0.4 and 0.3): $L_\infty$ orders 3.55, 2.85 and
    4.21, 4.36; $L_1$ orders 3.67, 3.08 and 4.40, 4.80, for
    $\Delta x=\Delta y=\frac15,\frac1{10},\frac1{20}$ (Table 3). **Shown**
25. Away from the shock ($|x-\text{shock}|\ge0.1$, $\Delta x=\frac1{40}$) the
    errors at $t=2/\pi$ and $t=1.1$ are of the size of the smooth case:
    $L_\infty$ 8.7e-4, 1.5e-4, 1.6e-4, 1.6e-5; $L_1$ 3.5e-5, 8.6e-6, 6.7e-6,
    1.4e-6 (3-3 and 4-4 at $t=2/\pi$, then at $t=1.1$) (Table 2). **Shown**
26. Example 3 (2-D advection of a rotated square to $t=16$): the
    fourth-order scheme resolves discontinuities better than the third-order
    one; overshoots and undershoots "if any, are negligible" (p. 27,
    Figs. 13–20). **Shown**
27. Example 4: both LF-ENO schemes converge to the entropy solutions for the
    nonconvex flux (4.4); on Buckley–Leverett, 3-3-LF-ENO smears more than
    3-3-EO-ENO (pp. 27–28, Figs. 21–38). **Shown**
28. There is no proof that ENO limit solutions satisfy the entropy condition
    and no rigorous TVB theory for semi-discrete ENO or its forward Euler
    version; analysis and experiments "strongly support that the total
    variation increase at each step is $O(\Delta x^r)$" (pp. 14–15, 27).
    **Stated**

# Printed slips

- **(2.21), last term:** the third stage is printed with the last term
  "$+\Delta tL(u^{(3)})$", inside the definition of $u^{(3)}$ itself. Read
  with $hL(y^{(2)})$, the stage reduces to $y^{(0)}+hL(y^{(2)})$, so (2.20)
  with this stage is classical RK4, and
  $\min\alpha/|\beta|=\frac{137/200}{1}=\frac{18769/80000}{137/400}=\frac{137}{200}$
  matches the printed CFL# *(compiler check, not in the sources: coefficient
  of $L(y^{(0)})$ is $-\frac{18769}{160000}+\frac{18769}{80000}\cdot\frac12=0$,
  of $L(y^{(1)})$ is $-\frac{137}{400}+\frac{137}{200}\cdot\frac12=0$)*.
- **(2.23), six-stage scheme:** as printed in the report, the Butcher form of
  (2.23) has $c=(0,\frac12,\frac14,\frac12,\frac34,1)$,
  $b=(\frac7{90},0,\frac{16}{45},\frac2{15},\frac{16}{45},\frac7{90})$ and
  $b^\top Ac=\frac8{45}$, not $\frac16$ *(compiler check, not in the
  sources)*, so the printed coefficients do not satisfy the third-order
  conditions. The printed CFL# $\frac7{30}$ is reproduced by
  $\min\alpha_{ij}/|\beta_{ij}|=\frac{87/200}{261/140}=\frac7{30}$. The
  paper presents the scheme as fifth order, taken from p. 143 of Lambert's
  book; the journal version was not seen.
- **Index $m$:** (2.10b) sets $u^{(m)}=u^{n+1}$ ($m$ stages), but the cases are
  labelled "Second order case, $m=1$" (two stages), "$m=2$" (three), "$m=3$"
  (four), "$m=5$" (six), and the stage–order statement of p. 9 uses $m$ =
  stages − 1 (pp. 9–14). In §III $m$ is half the spatial order.
- **(2.17):** "$\alpha_{21},\alpha_{30},\alpha_{31},\beta_{10}$ and $P$ … are
  free parameters", while the formulas use $\alpha_{32}$, not $\alpha_{30}$.
- p. 7: "an approximation to $-\tilde L$ to the spatial operator
  $-\mathcal L$" (garbled sentence).
- p. 21: "(3.9) - (3.6)" (reversed range); p. 22: "Range-Kutta"; p. 20:
  "assoicate"; p. 27: equation label "((4.4))".
- p. 23: the characteristic decomposition sums $\sum_{i=1}^d$ over
  eigenvectors, while $d$ is the space dimension of (1.1).
- **Table 1**, $L_\infty$, 4-4-LF-ENO at $\Delta x=\frac1{40}$: printed "5.4-5"
  with order "4.35"; $\log_2(1.1\times10^{-4}/5.4\times10^{-6})=4.35$, while
  $\log_2(1.1\times10^{-4}/5.4\times10^{-5})=1.03$ *(compiler check, not in
  the sources)*.
- Bibliographic page: key word "construction laws".
