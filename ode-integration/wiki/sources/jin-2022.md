# Summary

The paper is an Acta Numerica survey of [asymptotic-preserving](/definitions/asymptotic-preserving-scheme.md) (AP) schemes. Let $\mathcal F^\varepsilon_\delta$ discretize a microscopic model $\mathcal F^\varepsilon$ with numerical parameter $\delta$ (mesh size and/or step). The scheme is AP if its limit $\mathcal F^0_\delta$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ at fixed $\delta$ is a consistent and stable discretization of the macroscopic limit $\mathcal F^0$. A triangle-inequality argument (Golse, Jin & Levermore 1999) turns this into convergence uniform in $\varepsilon$ at the rate $O(\delta^{r/(s+1)})$. Section 2 reviews the physical asymptotic chains: quantum to classical, particles to kinetic, kinetic to hydrodynamic, transport to diffusion.

For time integration the core sections are 3 and 5.

- Time-splitting spectral methods for the semiclassical Schrödinger equation are AP in time for observables, because their sub-flows are integrated exactly ([operator splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md)).
- [BGK penalization](/methods/bgk-penalization.md) uses a first-order IMEX step that is implemented explicitly ([IMEX methods](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md)).
- An [exponential](/methods/exponential-integrators.md) reformulation of the kinetic equation makes any explicit Runge–Kutta method AP, with a parameter $\beta$ chosen for [positivity](/properties/positivity-preservation.md).
- [Micro–macro decompositions](/methods/micro-macro-decomposition.md) are given for Boltzmann and linear transport.
- The even/odd parity formulation for diffusive transport and the Boscarino–Pareschi–Russo reformulation replace the parabolic step restriction of the limit scheme by a hyperbolic one.

The remaining material is outside time integration: random batch methods for interacting particles (AP in the particle number), stochastic-Galerkin AP schemes with an $\varepsilon$-uniform CFL condition, AP neural networks against PINNs, and short surveys of relaxation systems, quasi-neutral, high-field, strong-magnetic-field, anisotropic-diffusion and low-Mach limits. Evidence consists of recalled theorems (proofs in the cited works), formal limit derivations and numerical examples reproduced from earlier papers. Nothing new is proved.

# Key claims

Notation: $\varepsilon$ the small scale parameter (semiclassical, Knudsen, Mach, Debye, as stated); $\delta$ a numerical parameter; $h$ the time step (the source's $\Delta t$ or $k$); $\Delta x$ the mesh width; $\mathcal M$ the local Maxwellian, $\mathcal M_{\mathrm g}$ the global one (see the notation map).

1. AP definition: $\mathcal F^\varepsilon_\delta$ is AP if its limit $\mathcal F^0_\delta$ as $\varepsilon\to0$ with $\delta$ fixed exists and is a good (consistent and stable) approximation of $\mathcal F^0$; it switches from micro to macro solver automatically when the small scales are not resolved. (§1, p. 418, Fig. 1.2(a)) **Stated** (definition)
2. Uniform convergence: if $\lVert\mathcal F^\varepsilon-\mathcal F^0\rVert=O(\varepsilon)$ (1.1), the classical estimate is $\mathcal E_1=\lVert\mathcal F^\varepsilon_\delta-\mathcal F^\varepsilon\rVert=O(\delta^r/\varepsilon^s)$, $1\le s\le r$ (1.2), and the AP requirements $\lVert\mathcal F^\varepsilon_\delta-\mathcal F^0_\delta\rVert=O(\varepsilon)$ uniformly in $\delta$ (1.3) and $\lVert\mathcal F^0_\delta-\mathcal F^0\rVert=O(\delta^r)$ (1.4) give $\mathcal E_2=O(\varepsilon+\delta^r)$ (1.5); the minimum yields $\lVert\mathcal F^\varepsilon_\delta-\mathcal F^\varepsilon\rVert=O(\delta^{r/(s+1)})$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$ (1.6), with crossover at $\varepsilon=O(\delta^{r/(s+1)})$. (§1, pp. 418–419, Fig. 1.2(b)) **Derived**
3. AP originated in capturing steady-state neutron transport in the diffusive regime (Larsen, Morel & Miller 1987; Larsen & Morel 1989); the term "asymptotic-preserving" was coined in Jin (1999). (§1 p. 417; §5 p. 448) **Stated**
4. Key features of AP schemes for kinetic equations: an implicit time discretization that can be implemented explicitly or easily (at least without Newton iteration on nonlinear algebraic systems), and automatic reduction to a good hydrodynamic scheme as the Knudsen number $\varepsilon\to0$. (§5, p. 449) **Stated**
5. Theorem 3.1 (Bao, Jin & Markowich 2002) for the time-splitting spectral method: $\lVert u^\varepsilon(t_n)-u^{\varepsilon,n}_{\rm int}\rVert_{L^2(0,1)}\le G_m\frac{T}{h}\left(\frac{\Delta x}{\varepsilon}\right)^m+\frac{CTh}{\varepsilon}$ (3.6), so an accurate wave function requires $h=o(\varepsilon)$ and $\Delta x=o(\varepsilon)$. (§3.2, p. 430) **Stated** (proof in the cited work)
6. Time splitting is AP in time for observables: the Lie/Strang splitting of the Schrödinger equation corresponds to a splitting of the Wigner equation whose $\varepsilon\to0$ limit (exact sub-steps, $h$ fixed) is the splitting scheme of the Liouville equation; hence $h=O(1)$ with $\Delta x=o(\varepsilon)$ gives accurate Wigner function and observables. (§3.3, eqs. 3.7–3.10, p. 431) **Derived** (formal); rigorous uniform-in-$\varepsilon$ estimates for first- and second-order splitting in Golse, Jin & Paul (2021) and sharper Strang observable estimates in Lasser & Lubich (2020) (Remark 3.2) **Stated**
7. Crank–Nicolson fails for observables: in Example 3.3 ($\varepsilon=10^{-3}$, $V=10$, $\Delta x=1/512$, $t=0.54$ after caustics) Crank–Nicolson spectral with $h=0.0001$ does not capture the correct weak limit, while Strang splitting with pseudo-spectral space (TSSP2) does with $h\gg\varepsilon$. (§3.3, Fig. 3.1) **Shown** (reproduced from Bao et al. 2002)
8. No scheme allows $\Delta x=o(1)$ for the semiclassical Schrödinger equation; the best known is $\Delta x=o(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ via Gaussian-beam/wave-packet methods. (§3.3, p. 432) **Stated**
9. Splitting for the Schrödinger–Liouville–Ehrenfest system is AP in $t,y,\eta$: Theorem 3.4 (Fang, Jin & Sparber 2018) gives convergence of the semi-discrete system (weak-*, up to subsequences) to the semi-discrete Liouville system, so $\Delta y,\Delta\eta\sim O(1)$; the time splitting (3.18)–(3.20) is AP in $t$; only $\Delta x=O(\varepsilon)$ is needed; described as "the first such result for highly oscillatory problems in spatial variables". (§3.4.2–3.4.3) **Stated** (theorem) / **Derived** (time AP). Relative $\ell^2$ difference between $h=0.01$ and $h=\varepsilon/10$: $1.65\times10^{-3}$, $1.69\times10^{-3}$, $1.70\times10^{-3}$ for $\varepsilon=1/256,1/1024,1/4096$. (Table 3.1) **Shown**
10. Observables against the wave function: in Example 3.7 with fixed $h=0.01$ the wave-function error grows as $\varepsilon$ decreases, while the errors in $\lvert\psi^\varepsilon\rvert^2$ and $\mu$ do not change noticeably. (Fig. 3.2) **Shown**
11. The random batch method reduces the per-step cost from $O(N^2)$ to $O(n_{\mathrm b}N)$ with batch size $n_{\mathrm b}$, basically $O(N)$ (§4.1). Theorem 4.1 (Jin, Li & Sun 2020b): $\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sqrt{\mathbb E\lvert\tilde r_i-r_i\rvert^2+\mathbb E\lvert\tilde v_i-v_i\rvert^2}\le C(T)\sqrt{h/(n_{\mathrm b}-1)+h^2}$ with $C(T)$ independent of $N$ (4.7); Theorem 4.2: under strong convexity (Assumption 1) the bound holds uniformly for $t\ge0$ with $C$ independent of $n_{\mathrm b}$ and $N$ (4.9). Hence the method is "AP in particle number $N$" in the mean-field regime. (p. 441) **Stated**
12. The $N\to\infty$ limit of the random batch method is $O(h)$ distant in Wasserstein-1 from the mean-field limit of the original system (Jin & Li 2022a), completing the AP diagram. (§4.1.2, p. 443) **Stated**
13. Random batch Ewald: importance sampling of Fourier modes with $P_k=S^{-1}e^{-k^2/(4\alpha)}$ gives $O(N)$ per-step complexity with $O(1)$ batch size, less CPU time than PPPM at equal accuracy ($L=10$, batch 100, $N$ up to $10^6$) and better parallel efficiency for all-atom water with $3\times10^5$ and $3\times10^7$ atoms. (§4.2.2, Figs. 4.2–4.3) **Shown** (reproduced; no hardware stated)
14. An explicit scheme for the Fokker–Planck–Landau collision operator requires $h=O(\varepsilon(\Delta v)^2)$. (§5.1, p. 451) **Stated**
15. BGK penalization is AP after an initial transient: the first-order IMEX scheme (5.2) is implemented explicitly (moments from (5.3), then $\mathcal M^{n+1}$, then $f^{n+1}$). Filbet & Jin (2010) showed formally that $f^n=\mathcal M^n+O(\varepsilon)$ implies $f^{n+1}=\mathcal M^{n+1}+O(\varepsilon)$; numerically $f^n=\mathcal M^n+O(\varepsilon)$ for $n\ge N$ regardless of $f^0$ (5.4), so the scheme is consistent with compressible Euler "after an initial transient time". (§5.1, p. 450) **Derived** (formal) and **Stated** (numerical, cited)
16. A two-step successive penalization (Yan & Jin 2013) removes the initial-layer problem and achieves AP in one time step. (Remark 5.1) **Stated**
17. The exponential reformulation $\partial_t[(f-\mathcal M)e^{\beta t/\varepsilon}]=\frac{Q-\beta(\mathcal M-f)}{\varepsilon}e^{\beta t/\varepsilon}$ (5.7) is equivalent to (5.6) and "can be easily extended to all explicit Runge–Kutta methods, which are not only of high order but also satisfy the AP property automatically"; the Li–Pareschi (2014) inhomogeneous version "preserves positivity, high-order accuracy and strong AP properties". (§5.2, pp. 451–452) **Stated**
18. Micro–macro AP theorem (Theorem 5.2, Bennoune et al. 2008): the scheme (5.15) gives, as $\varepsilon\to0$, a scheme consistent with compressible Euler, and for small $\varepsilon$ is asymptotically equivalent, up to $O(\varepsilon^2)$, to an explicit time discretization of Navier–Stokes; only $I-(h/\varepsilon)\mathcal L_{\mathcal M}$ (linear, negative spectrum) must be inverted. (§5.3, p. 453) **Stated**
19. Parity-based AP (Jin, Pareschi & Toscani 2000): rewriting (5.18)–(5.19) as (5.20)–(5.21) with $0\le\psi\le1/\varepsilon^2$ (simplest $\psi=\min\{1,1/\varepsilon^2\}$) makes the transport characteristic speeds independent of $\varepsilon$; the system is split into an implicit stiff relaxation (5.26)–(5.27) and an explicit non-stiff transport (5.28). With centred differences for $\nabla_xr$ the limit is a non-compact five-point (1D) diffusion stencil; staggered grids fix it. (§5.4.1) **Derived** (formal limit) / **Stated**
20. Micro–macro for linear transport (Lemou & Mieussens 2008): the scheme (5.33)–(5.34) (implicit $\mathcal Lg^{n+1}$, explicit $v\cdot\mathcal M_{\mathrm g}\nabla_x\rho^n$, flux of $g$ at $t_{n+1}$) tends to the explicit diffusion scheme $\frac{\rho^{n+1}-\rho^n}{h}+\nabla_x\cdot\langle D\nabla\rho^n\rangle=0$, on a staggered grid to the classical three-point explicit scheme; uniform stability $h\le C(\Delta x)^2$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$ was proved by Liu & Mieussens (2010). (§5.4.2, p. 457) **Derived** / **Stated**
21. In all the above the limit time discretization is explicit for the diffusion equation and imposes $h=O((\Delta x)^2)$; the Boscarino–Pareschi–Russo (2013) reformulation (5.37)–(5.38) with a free parameter in $[0,1]$ equal to 1 at $\varepsilon=0$ gives in the limit an implicit diffusion discretization with $h=O(\Delta x)$, and the value 1 "guarantees the largest stability region". (p. 457) **Stated**
22. Stochastic AP: Theorems 5.4 (uniform regularity in the random variable, $\varepsilon$-independent constants) and 5.5 ($\lVert f-f^{K_{\mathrm{gPC}}}\rVert_\Gamma\le C(T)/K_{\mathrm{gPC}}^k$, $C(T)$ independent of $\varepsilon$) of Jin, Liu & Ma (2017a); the stochastic-Galerkin micro–macro scheme (5.60) tends to the fully discrete scheme of the random diffusion limit, hence is sAP. (§5.5) **Stated**
23. $\varepsilon$-uniform CFL (Theorem 5.6): if $h\le\frac{\sigma_{\min}}3(\Delta x)^2+\frac{2\varepsilon}3\Delta x$ (5.62), the scheme (5.60) satisfies a discrete energy estimate and is stable; the bound tends to a parabolic CFL as $\varepsilon\to0$. (§5.5.3, pp. 461–462) **Stated**
24. Stochastic-Galerkin evidence: with $\sigma(z)=2+z$, $z\sim U(-1,1)$, $\varepsilon=10^{-8}$, $t=0.01$, $h=0.0002/3$, errors decay rapidly in $K_{\mathrm{gPC}}$ and then saturate at the spatial error (Fig. 5.1); with $K_{\mathrm{gPC}}=4$, $\Delta x=0.01$, $h=\Delta x^2/12$ the difference to the limit solution decays like $\varepsilon^2$ until numerical error dominates (Fig. 5.2). **Shown** (reproduced)
25. Hypocoercivity for stochastic-Galerkin nonlinear kinetic equations: Theorem 5.7 (Liu & Jin 2018; Daus, Jin & Liu 2019) gives energy decay $E(t)\le\eta e^{-\tau t}$ (incompressible Navier–Stokes scaling) and $\le\eta e^{-\varepsilon\tau t}$ (acoustic), constants independent of $K_{\mathrm{gPC}}$ and $z$; Theorem 5.8 gives spectral convergence with constants independent of $K_{\mathrm{gPC}}$ and $\varepsilon$. (§5.6) **Stated**
26. PINNs are not AP, APNN is: as $\varepsilon\to0$ the PINN residual loss tends to the loss of $\mathcal Lf=0$, which gives $f=\rho$ and not the diffusion equation; a loss built on the micro–macro system (APNN, Jin, Ma & Wu 2021b) tends to the diffusion equation. Example 5.9 ($\varepsilon=10^{-8}$): relative $\ell^2$ errors $9.40\times10^{-1}$ (PINN) and $2.76\times10^{-3}$ (APNN). (§5.7, eqs. 5.82–5.92, Fig. 5.4) **Derived** (formal) and **Shown**
27. Not every AP scheme yields an APNN: the parity formulation (5.18)–(5.19) does not. (Remark 5.10) **Stated**
28. Hyperbolic relaxation: for (6.1) with dissipative relaxation the Chapman–Enskog limit is parabolic (6.2), with positive diffusion iff the subcharacteristic condition holds (Chen, Levermore & Liu 1994); AP schemes, high-order IMEX schemes (Pareschi & Russo 2005; Dumbser, Enaux & Toro 2008) and AP-plus-positivity schemes (Hu & Shu 2019) are cited, and "a rigorous uniform accuracy proof of AP schemes for linear problems was recently made in Hu and Shu (2021)" (an IMEX-BDF paper). (§6.1, p. 472) **Stated**
29. Quasi-neutral limit: a typical stable time discretization of Euler–Poisson requires $h\le\varepsilon$; the Crispel–Degond–Vignal (2005) reformulation (6.11) is uniformly elliptic in $\varepsilon$, so a suitable time discretization is asymptotically stable. (§6.2) **Derived** (reformulation) / **Stated**
30. Low Mach: explicit shock-capturing schemes need $\Delta x=O(\varepsilon)$ and then $h=O(\varepsilon\Delta x)$; the Haack–Jin–Liu (2012) splitting (6.26) treats the non-stiff pressure remainder explicitly and the linear $\frac{a(t)}{\varepsilon^2}\nabla\rho$ implicitly (Poisson solves only) and reduces to a projection method as $\varepsilon\to0$. (§6.5) **Stated**

Reader's caution (not a claim of the paper): the uniform rate (1.6) needs (1.3) uniformly in $\delta$, which the bare AP definition does not deliver (BGK penalization with non-well-prepared data is an example), so the sentence "an AP scheme is convergent uniformly in $\varepsilon$" (p. 419) is stronger than the definition; and claim 17 asserts high order at fixed $\varepsilon$, not uniformly in $\varepsilon$.
