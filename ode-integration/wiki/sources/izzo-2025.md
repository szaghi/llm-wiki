# Summary

The paper carries the modified Patankar trick from Euler, Runge–Kutta (MPRK, SSPMPRK) and deferred correction (MPDeC) to explicit [linear multistep methods](/methods/linear-multistep-method.md) with nonnegative coefficients. The result is the class MPLM-$k$ of [modified Patankar methods](/methods/modified-patankar-methods.md) for positive, fully conservative [production–destruction systems](/definitions/production-destruction-system.md). Each production term at a past step is multiplied by $y^n_j/\varpi^n_j$ and each destruction term by $y^n_i/\varpi^n_i$. The Patankar weight denominators $\varpi^n$ are positive and independent of $y^n$. A step is linearly implicit: one $d\times d$ system whose transpose is a strictly diagonally dominant Z-matrix, hence an M-matrix.

The theory proves four results. First, unconditional [positivity](/properties/positivity-preservation.md) and unconditional conservation of $\mathbf 1^\top y$ for every $h>0$. Second, MPLM-$k$ is consistent of order $p$ if and only if the denominators approximate $y(t_n)$ to $\mathcal O(h^p)$. Third, convergence of order $p$ under that condition, for $C^1$ denominators and solutions bounded below by $\mu>0$, via a discrete Gronwall lemma. Fourth, the $\varpi$-embedding: the denominator of an order-$p$ method is computed by one step of an order-$(p-1)$ MPLM, recursively down to modified Patankar Euler, which gives arbitrarily high order. Zero initial components are replaced by realmin.

Five methods of orders 2 to 6 are tested on a linear two-species exchange and the nonlinear algal-bloom PDS. The other tests are the six-species original [Brusselator](/benchmarks/brusselator.md), an eight-compartment COVID-19 model and a finite-volume heterogeneous-diffusion PDE. The observed orders match the theory. MPLM is more efficient than third-order MPRK at order $\ge3$ or $\ge4$. Against MPDeC ([deferred correction](/methods/deferred-correction.md)), MPDeC wins on the two small problems and MPLM on the larger ones; on the COVID model with zero data the MPDeC code returns NaN. Reduced efficiency on the stiff [Robertson problem](/benchmarks/robertson-problem.md) is acknowledged without data. A stability analysis is left open. The paper cites [Torlo et al. (2022)](/sources/torlo-2022.md) for the accuracy issues of MPRK near vanishing states.

# Key claims

Notation: components and steps are written $y^n_i$ as in the registry's Patankar entry; $d$ is the dimension (source $N$) and $\mathbf 1$ the vector of ones (source $e$). The source writes an explicit $k$-step method backward-indexed, $y^n=\sum_{r=1}^k\bar\alpha_ry^{n-r}+h\sum_{r=1}^k\bar\beta_rf(y^{n-r})$; here $\bar\alpha_r=-\alpha_{k-r}$ and $\bar\beta_r=\beta_{k-r}$ in the registry's forward form with $\alpha_k=1$, $\beta_k=0$ (source $\alpha_r$, $\beta_r$). $\varpi^n_i$ are the Patankar weight denominators (source $\sigma^n_i$, "PWDs"). $P(y)=(p_{ij})$, $D(y)=(d_{ij})$.

1. MPLM-$k$ (Def. 2.1, Eq. (2.8)): for an explicit $k$-step method with $\bar\alpha,\bar\beta\ge0$ and denominators $\varpi^n_i>0$ independent of $y^n_i$, the scheme $y^n_i=\sum_r\bar\alpha_ry^{n-r}_i+h\sum_r\bar\beta_r\sum_j\bigl(p_{ij}(y^{n-r})y^n_j/\varpi^n_j-d_{ij}(y^{n-r})y^n_i/\varpi^n_i\bigr)$ is linearly implicit in $y^n$. **Stated** (definition)
2. With $k=3$, and with $k=4$, $\bar\alpha=(16/27,0,0,11/27)$, $\bar\beta=(16/9,0,0,4/9)$, (2.8) coincides with the second- and third-order modified Patankar multistep schemes of Zhu, Huang & Yang [46]; the $k=3$ coefficients are printed as $\bar\alpha=(3/4,0,1/4)$, $\bar\beta=(0,3/2,0)$, which are only first order by (2.7). (§2, p. 4) **Stated** (see Printed slips)
3. Lemma 2.2 (unconditional positivity): for a positive, fully conservative PDS and positive starting values, $y^n>0$ for all $h>0$, $n\ge0$; the system matrix $I-h\sum_r\bar\beta_rM^{n-r}$ has a transpose that is a strictly diagonally dominant Z-matrix, hence an M-matrix with nonnegative inverse. **Proved**
4. Lemma 2.3 (unconditional conservation): if $\mathbf 1^\top y^0=\dots=\mathbf 1^\top y^{k-1}$, then $\mathbf 1^\top(y^n-y^0)=0$ for all $h>0$, $n\ge0$, using $p_{ij}=d_{ji}$ and $\sum_r\bar\alpha_r=1$. **Proved**
5. Theorem 3.1 (sufficient condition): for data in $C^p(\Omega_0)$, $\Omega_0=\{0\le z_i\le\mathbf 1^\top y^0\}$, if $\varpi_i(y(t_{n-1}),\dots,y(t_{n-k}))=y_i(t_n)+\mathcal O(h^p)$ (Eq. (3.12)), MPLM-$k$ is consistent of order $p$ (local error $\mathcal O(h^{p+1})$). **Proved**
6. Theorem 3.2 (necessary condition; proof in App. A): order-$p$ consistency implies (3.12), shown on a linear PDS with one exchanged pair. **Proved** (the quantifier "for all PDS" is implicit; see the reader checks)
7. Lemma 3.4 (discrete Gronwall): $a_n\le b+\sum_{r=1}^lc_ra_{n-r}$ with $c=\sum_rc_r>1$ gives $a_n\le(a^*+b/(c-1))\exp(n(c-1))$; from Lambert [29, §3.4]. **Proved**
8. Theorem 3.5 (convergence): on $\Omega=\{\mu\le x_i\le\mathbf 1^\top y^0\}$, $\mu>0$, with data in $C^p(\Omega)$, starting errors $\mathcal O(h^p)$ and $C^1$ denominators satisfying (3.12), MPLM-$k$ converges with order $p$; the error bound is $(\max_m\lVert y(t_m)-y^m\rVert+\max_n\lVert\delta\rVert/(h(k+1)B))\exp((k+1)TB/(1-hB))$, $B=J\sum_r\bar\beta_r$, for small $h$ ($J$ a Jacobian bound). **Proved**
9. $\varpi$-embedding (§3.1, Eq. (3.20), Theorem 3.6): computing the denominator by one step of an order-$(p-1)$ convergent MPLM-$\tilde k$ ($\tilde k\le k$) from the past order-$p$ values, recursively down to MPE ($\varpi^n_i=y^{n-1}_i$), yields $C^1$ denominators satisfying (3.12), hence order-$p$ convergence for every $p$. **Proved** (induction)
10. MPE as denominator gives a second-order MPLM-$k$: $(I-h\Phi(y(t_{n-1})))^{-1}y(t_{n-1})=y(t_n)+\mathcal O(h^2)$, $\Phi(x)=(P(x)-\operatorname{diag}(D(x)\mathbf 1))\operatorname{diag}(1/x_i)$. (Proof of Thm. 3.6, citing [4, Thm. 3.11]) **Proved**
11. The embedding cannot be applied with a zero initial component; replacing zeros by realmin $\approx2.26\cdot10^{-308}$, as Kopecz & Meister [25] did for MPRK, works in the experiments. (End of §3; Tests 3–4) **Shown** (outside Theorem 3.5, which needs $\mu>0$)
12. Table 1 gives five methods MPLM-$k(p)$ of orders 2–6 with $\bar\alpha,\bar\beta\ge0$: MPLM-2(2) is the leapfrog (explicit midpoint) rule $\bar\alpha=(0,1)$, $\bar\beta=(2,0)$; MPLM-5(4), 7(5) and 10(6) have $\bar\alpha=(0,\dots,0,1)$. (Table 1) **Stated** (origin not given)
13. Test 1 (linear, $a=5$, $T=2$): observed orders at $h=2^{-11}$ are MPE 1.00, MPLM-2(2) 1.99, MPLM-4(3) 2.97, MPLM-5(4) 3.81, MPLM-7(5) 4.97; MPLM-10(6) reaches 6.26 at $h=2^{-10}$ (error $6.95\cdot10^{-13}$), then saturates. (Table 2) **Shown**

17. MPDeC solves several linear systems per step while MPLM needs a start-up and the denominator computation, so MPDeC is more efficient for small systems and MPLM gains as the number of components grows. (§4.3) **Stated** (interpretation, not measured)
18. Test 4 (SACEIRQD COVID-19, Italy parameters, 180 days, relative error): orders 1.00, 1.98, 2.97, 3.94, 4.85; MPLM-10(6) 5.62 at $0.35h=2^{-7}$, then saturates (orders listed at $0.35h=2^{-8}$). (Table 5) **Shown**
19. The MPDeC codes of [34] give "several NAN" on the COVID model; with zeros replaced by $10^{-10}$, and with data $10^4\mathbf 1$, MPLM needs fewer resources than MPDeC for $p>3$. (§4.4, Figs. 19–20) **Shown** (MPDeC did not receive the realmin treatment MPLM received)
20. Test 5 (finite-volume heterogeneous diffusion, $\Delta x=10^{-2}$): MPLM-4(3) against MPDeC(3) at $h=2^{-9}$ gives errors $2.73\cdot10^{-8}$ and $7.97\cdot10^{-9}$ for CPU $9.71\cdot10^2$ and $2.85\cdot10^3$; at $h=2^{-12}$, $9.64\cdot10^{-11}$ and $3.90\cdot10^{-11}$ for $8.43\cdot10^3$ and $2.77\cdot10^4$; MPLM-5(4) against MPDeC(4) at $h=2^{-9}$, $4.53\cdot10^{-9}$ and $5.33\cdot10^{-10}$ for $1.42\cdot10^3$ and $6.27\cdot10^3$; conservation residuals $10^{-15}$–$10^{-14}$ for MPLM. (Table 6; CPU units not stated) **Shown**

*(Reader checks, not in the source.)* All five Table 1 methods satisfy (2.7) exactly to their stated order and not the next, and all roots of their first characteristic polynomial lie on the unit circle (simple), so the underlying methods are zero-stable but only weakly stable. In each, some $\bar\beta_r>0$ has $\bar\alpha_r=0$, so their SSP coefficient as classical explicit multistep methods is 0; the Patankar positivity argument does not need SSP. Theorem 3.5 bounds $\sum_r\lvert\bar\alpha_r\rvert$ by $\sum_r\bar\alpha_r=1$, a nonnegative-coefficient argument, not a root-condition one. Unconditional positivity at order up to 6 does not contradict the order-1 barrier for unconditionally positive linear methods, because MPLM is nonlinear in the data. An order-$p$ step solves $p$ linear systems, one per embedding level; the paper does not state this, nor the embedding chain used for Table 1.

- Table 4, MPE column: at $0.80h=2^{-12}$ the error $2.21\cdot10^{-4}$ follows $4.42\cdot10^{-2}$ with order 1.00, which fits only $2.21\cdot10^{-2}$.
- p. 29: the initial condition "$f(x)=2-2\sin^2\left(\frac\pi2-\frac14\right)$" has no $x$-dependence, while Fig. 22 shows a non-constant profile.

- p. 29: the residual $r(h)=\Delta x\max_n\bigl(\sum_j\lvert v_j(t_n)-f(x_j)\rvert\bigr)$ measures the distance from the initial data; the reported $\sim10^{-15}$ fits the conservation defect $\lvert\sum_j(v_j(t_n)-f(x_j))\rvert$.
- (4.38) and Theorem 4.1 mix the indexings $0..N_x$ and $1..N_x$; the proof writes "$(e^Tv_j(t))'$".
- Lemma 2.2 drops the superscript $n$ of $\sigma$; §2 requires $k>1$ while MPE is used with $k=1$; Test 4 writes "$K_d$" for $k_d$; Appendix A is headed "Concistency".
