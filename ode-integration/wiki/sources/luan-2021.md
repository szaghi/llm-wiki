# Summary

The paper constructs explicit exponential Runge–Kutta (expRK)
[exponential integrators](/methods/exponential-integrators.md) for stiff
semilinear problems $y'=Ly+N(t,y)$ (page-local $N$), such as parabolic PDEs
after spatial discretization. The new methods are cheaper to implement than
the existing fourth- and fifth-order methods that satisfy the stiff order
conditions: Hochbruck–Ostermann's expRK4s5 and Luan–Ostermann's expRK5s8.
Cost is counted as the number of sequential evaluations of linear
combinations $\sum_k\phi_k(c_ihL)V_k$. One call of the Krylov routine
`phipm_simul_iom` evaluates several such combinations when they share the
vectors $V_k$ and differ only in the node $c_i$
([evaluating φ-functions](/methods/phi-function-evaluation.md)).

Earlier constructions weakened many of the
[stiff order conditions](/results/exponential-runge-kutta-stiff-order-conditions.md)
(replacing $b_i(hL)$ by $b_i(0)$). Their stages therefore depend on all
earlier stages and mix several nodes, so expRK4s5 needs 6 sequential
evaluations per step and expRK5s8 needs 11. Theorem 3.1 proves convergence
of order $p\le5$, uniform in the stiffness, when every condition of Table 3.1
holds in strong form except the quadrature condition $\psi_p$, which holds
only at $hL=0$. Parabolic smoothing absorbs the $O(hL)$ defect. Under this
theorem the paper derives two families. expRK4s6 has two independent stage
pairs and costs 4 evaluations. expRK5s10 has stage groups of sizes 2, 3 and
3 and costs 5 evaluations. Order 4 is not reachable with 5 stages along this
route, nor order 5 with 9 stages.

MATLAB tests cover a 1D semilinear parabolic problem, a 1D cubic nonlinear
Schrödinger equation and the 2D [Gray–Scott model](/benchmarks/gray-scott-model.md).
The new methods reach orders 4 and 5. At equal step counts they are as
accurate as the old ones or more so, and they are faster in CPU time. The
paper calls a method "stiffly accurate" when it satisfies the stiff order
conditions. This is not the Runge–Kutta [stiff accuracy](/properties/stiff-accuracy.md)
property.

# Key claims

Registry notation: $y$ the solution (source $u$), $L$ the stiff linear
operator (source $A$), $N$ the page-local nonlinear part (source $g$),
$f=Ly+N$ (source $F$), $Y_i$ the stages (source $U_{ni}$), $\phi_k$ the
φ-functions (source $\varphi_k$, same normalisation). $D_{ni}=N(t_n+c_ih,Y_i)-N(t_n,y_n)$
(written $D_i$ on the concept pages), $\psi_j$, $\psi_{j,i}$ are page-local.
$Z,X_1,X_2,X_3$ are the arbitrary matrices and $\mathcal B$ the arbitrary bilinear
map of the order conditions (source $Z,J,K,L$ and $B$; page-local).

1. Existing expRK methods with stiff order were derived by weakening many
   stiff order conditions: 4 of 9 for order 4 (expRK4s5), 9 of 16 for
   order 5 (expRK5s8) (§2, Remark 2.2; §4.2). **Stated**. The "9" for order 4
   does not match Table 3.1, which lists 7 conditions up to order 4 (see
   Printed slips).
2. expRK4s5 needs 6 sequential evaluations per step, because $Y_5$ contains
   two combinations with nodes $\tfrac12$ and 1. expRK5s8 needs 11 ($Y_7$
   contains 2 combinations and $Y_8$ contains 3), and none of its stages can
   be computed simultaneously (Remark 2.1; §5). **Derived**
3. `phipm_simul_iom` computes $r$ combinations $\sum_k\phi_k(\rho_iM)\rho_i^kv_k$,
   $1\le i\le r$, in one call when they share the vectors $v_k$ (§2.1,
   Eq. 2.2; §5, Eqs. 5.1–5.2). **Stated** (refers to Luan, Pudykiewicz &
   Reynolds 2019)
4. An $s$-stage expRK method whose every stage has the single-node form
   $Y_i=y_n+\sum_k\phi_k(c_ihL)V_{ki}$ (2.4) needs $s$ evaluations. Grouping
   stages that share their vectors, or that are mutually independent,
   reduces the count further (§2.1). **Derived**
5. **Theorem 3.1.** Let $L$ generate an analytic semigroup and let the
   solution and $N$ be smooth with bounded derivatives (Assumptions 1–2).
   An explicit expRK method that satisfies the conditions of Table 3.1 up to
   order $p$ ($2\le p\le5$) in strong form, except $\psi_p(Z)=0$, which
   holds only as $\psi_p(0)=0$, satisfies $\lVert y_n-y(t_n)\rVert\le Ch^p$ on
   $t_0\le t_n\le T$, with $C$ independent of $n$ and $h$ (§3.2, Eq. 3.7).
   **Proved** (short proof that defers details to Luan & Ostermann 2014)
6. Relaxing only one order condition is expected to give methods with better
   stability, and so better accuracy, on stiff problems (end of §3.2).
   **Stated**
7. For $p\le3$ no new method improves on expRK2s2 and expRK3s3 under this
   cost measure (§4). **Stated** ("We omit the details")
8. With $s=5$ the order-4 conditions cannot be met under the hypotheses of
   Theorem 3.1. Hochbruck–Ostermann showed that $s=5$ is minimal for the
   weakened set (conditions 1–3 strong, 4–7 weak) (§4.1). **Stated**
9. **expRK4s6** (Eqs. 4.10a–d): a family of fourth-order, 6-stage methods
   with independent pairs $\{Y_3,Y_4\}$ and $\{Y_5,Y_6\}$. The free nodes
   are $c_2,c_3,c_4,c_6$, and $c_5=(4c_6-3)/(6c_6-4)$ (Eq. 4.4) gives the
   weakened condition 4. Only $\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3$ appear (§4.1).
   **Derived**
10. The expRK4s6 representative has $c_2=c_3=\tfrac12$, $c_4=c_6=\tfrac13$,
    $c_5=\tfrac56$ (§4.1; §5). **Stated**
11. expRK4s6 needs 4 sequential evaluations per step, against 6 for
    expRK4s5 (Remark 4.1; §5). **Derived**
12. Order 5 under Theorem 3.1 requires $s\ge9$ (§4.2). With $s=9$ condition
    11 cannot hold in strong form. The requirements $a_{j2}=a_{j3}=0$ or
    $a_{j2}=\psi_{2,3}=0$ ($j=4,5,6$) conflict with $\psi_{2,j}=\psi_{3,j}=0$
    (§4.2 b, Eq. 4.29). **Derived** (along the author's construction path,
    not a general lower bound)
13. **expRK5s10** (display after Eq. 4.25; weights in Eq. 4.26): a family of
    fifth-order, 10-stage methods with independent groups $\{Y_3,Y_4\}$,
    $\{Y_5,Y_6,Y_7\}$, $\{Y_8,Y_9,Y_{10}\}$. Only condition 8 ($\psi_5$) is
    weakened. The nodes $c_8,c_9,c_{10}$ must satisfy
    $\frac{c_8+c_9+c_{10}}4-\frac{c_8c_9+c_8c_{10}+c_9c_{10}}3+\frac{c_8c_9c_{10}}2=\frac15$
    (Eq. 4.14) (§4.2 a). **Derived**
14. The expRK5s10 representative has $c_2=c_3=c_5=\tfrac12$,
    $c_4=c_6=\tfrac13$, $c_7=\tfrac14$, $c_8=\tfrac3{10}$, $c_9=\tfrac34$,
    $c_{10}=1$ (§4.2; §5). **Stated**. These nodes satisfy Eq. 4.14 *(compiler
    check, not in the sources)*.
15. expRK5s10 needs 5 sequential evaluations per step, against 11 for
    expRK5s8 (Remark 4.2; §5). **Derived**
16. With $s=11$ a scheme that satisfies all 16 conditions of Table 3.1 in
    strong form can be derived the same way and still costs 5 evaluations.
    It is not presented (Remark 4.2). **Stated**
17. Example 6.1 (1D semilinear parabolic, 200 grid points,
    $\lVert L\rVert_\infty\approx1.6\times10^5$, $N_{\mathrm{steps}}=4,\dots,64$,
    $T=1$): expRK4s6 and expRK5s10 reach orders 4 and 5. At equal step counts
    expRK4s6 is slightly more accurate than expRK4s5, and expRK5s10 gives
    almost the same errors as expRK5s8. Both new methods are "much faster".
    expRK4s6 is fastest for errors above $10^{-6}$, expRK5s10 below (§6,
    Fig. 6.1). **Shown**
18. Example 6.2 (cubic NLS, 128 Fourier modes, $T=3$,
    $N_{\mathrm{steps}}=64,\dots,1024$, ode15s reference at
    $\mathrm{atol}=\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-14}$): the new methods reach their
    orders. expRK4s6 is "much more accurate" than expRK4s5, and expRK5s10 a
    little more accurate than expRK5s8. expRK4s6 is most efficient near error
    $10^{-7}$, expRK5s10 at $10^{-8}$ or tighter (§6, Fig. 6.2). **Shown**
19. Example 6.3 (2D Gray–Scott, $150\times150$ grid, $T=2$, reference from
    expRK4s6 with a small step): expRK4s6 is much more accurate than
    expRK4s5, and expRK5s10 slightly more accurate than expRK5s8 (Fig. 6.3).
    expRK4s6 is the most efficient overall; expRK5s10 overtakes it below
    $10^{-11}$ (Fig. 6.4). **Shown**
20. Steps needed on Gray–Scott for error thresholds
    $10^{-5},\dots,10^{-11}$ (Table 6.1): expRK4s5 18, 36, 66, 121, 215,
    385, 685; expRK4s6 10, 19, 28, 46, 122, 230, 420; expRK5s8 7, 18, 33,
    57, 92, 149, 238; expRK5s10 8, 17, 30, 51, 82, 130, 208. **Shown**
21. The evaluation-count saving relies on computing grouped stages
    simultaneously with `phipm_simul_iom`. Any φ-action algorithm can still
    compute the groups in parallel, but algorithms that do not build Krylov
    subspaces may not support simultaneous computation (§1). **Stated**
22. All runs are serial MATLAB on one iMac (3.6 GHz Intel Core i7, 32 GB),
    with `phipm_simul_iom` at tol $=10^{-12}$, $m=1$, iom $=2$ (§5, §6).
    **Stated**

# Printed slips

- §5 step (vi), p. 17 (expRK4s5 implementation): "V = [0, v, −d4 + 5d5,
  4d4 − 8d5]", but Eq. (2.7) gives $\phi_2(hL)h(-D_{n4}+4D_{n5})$. "5d5"
  should read "4d5".
- §5 step (iii), p. 17 (expRK4s6 implementation): the fourth vector entry
  $\frac1{(c_3-c_4)c_3}d_3-\frac1{(c_3-c_4)c_4}d_4$ lacks the factor 2 of
  Eq. (4.10c), $\frac{2c_j^3}{c_3-c_4}$.
- p. 15 and §5 step (iv), p. 18 (expRK5s10, $\phi_3$ term of $Y_q$,
  $q=8,9,10$): printed "$\beta_5D_{n5}-\beta_6D_{n6}-\beta_7D_{n7}$". Eq. (4.20)
  gives $-(\beta_5D_{n5}+\beta_6D_{n6}+\beta_7D_{n7})$; the sign of the
  $\beta_5$ term is wrong. The $y_{n+1}$ line prints the sign correctly.
- §5 step (v), p. 18: "V = [0, v, α8d8+α9d9+α10d10, β8d8+β9d9+β10d10, …]"
  drops the minus sign of the displayed $\phi_3$ term of $y_{n+1}$.
- §5 step (iv), p. 18: "t = [c9, c10, c8]" $=[\tfrac34,1,\tfrac3{10}]$ is not
  increasing, against the routine's stated requirement
  $0<\rho_1<\dots<\rho_r\le1$.
- §5 steps (ii)–(v), p. 17 (expRK5s8 implementation): the prefactors
  $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac18$, $\tfrac12$, $\tfrac1{25}$, $\tfrac1{125}$ of Eq. (2.7) are
  missing (e.g. "$d_2/c_3^2$" for $\tfrac12d_2/c_3^2$). Steps (vi)–(xi) are
  consistent.
- Eq. (3.8): the leading term multiplies $G_{p-1,n}$ (a Fréchet derivative of
  $N$); by Eq. (3.1) it should multiply $f^{(p-1)}(t_n)$ in the source's
  notation, $N(t,y(t))$ differentiated in time.
- Eqs. (2.2)–(2.4): the last term "$\varphi_N(\rho M)V_q$" mixes the indices
  $N$ and $q$.
- §5: "imo = 2" for the parameter `iom`.
- Remark 2.2: "4 out of 9 order conditions" for order 4, where Table 3.1 has
  7 (and §4.1 says "weakening four conditions 4–7").
- §6, Example 6.3: "the new methods use smaller steps than the old ones …
  meaning that they can take larger step sizes"; Table 6.1 shows they use
  fewer steps. The text gives $N=32,\dots,1024$, but the Fig. 6.3 axis starts
  at 16.
- Ref. [22] (Pieper et al., J. Comput. Phys. 398) prints pages 817–837,
  identical to ref. [19] (J. Comput. Phys. 376:817–837).
