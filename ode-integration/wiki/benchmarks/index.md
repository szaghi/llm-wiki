# Benchmarks

Standard test problems.

* [Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equation](/benchmarks/benjamin-bona-mahony-equation.md) — The BBM equation u_t + u_x + u u_x − u_txx = 0 with periodic solitary-wave solutions and linear, quadratic and cubic invariants; its semidiscretizations test invariant preservation by Gauss–Legendre, relaxation and pseudo-energy-preserving methods.
* [Broadwell model](/benchmarks/broadwell-model.md) — Three-velocity discrete kinetic model whose stiff collision term relaxes to a local Maxwellian with relaxation time ε; used nonlinear and linearized to test positivity, asymptotic preservation and uniform accuracy of IMEX time integrators from the kinetic to the fluid regime.
* [Brusselator](/benchmarks/brusselator.md) — The one-dimensional advection–diffusion–reaction Brusselator, a three-species stiff reaction system with a fast relaxation term, is the shared test of IMEX, multirate and nonlinear-solver studies; the page gives the variants, parameters and splittings used and what each test showed.
* [Burgers equation](/benchmarks/burgers-equation.md) — Inviscid Burgers u_t + (u²/2)_x = 0, whose smooth data steepen into a shock, and viscous Burgers with manufactured or periodic solutions; the sources use it to test relaxation, superviscosity filtering, IMEX and multirate entropy behaviour, order reduction from boundary data, and TASE stability with inexact Jacobians.
* [Colliding acoustic pulses](/benchmarks/colliding-acoustic-pulses.md) — Klein's low-Mach test of two acoustic pulses that collide on a periodic domain; the sources run it with implicit and IMEX integrators at acoustic Courant numbers above one to check how well large steps resolve acoustics, and find the pulses captured at moderate Courant numbers but damped at very large ones.
* [Double Mach reflection](/benchmarks/double-mach-reflection.md) — Woodward and Colella's Mach 10 shock reflecting off a 30-degree wedge, run to t = 0.2; the sources use it to measure the cost of asynchronous local time stepping against a global step and the start-up transient that error-based step-size control handles while CFL-based control crashes.
* [FitzHugh–Nagumo model](/benchmarks/fitzhugh-nagumo-model.md) — The FitzHugh–Nagumo reaction–diffusion system of nerve conduction, a stiff method-of-lines test with a cubic excitable reaction; used to show unconditional stability and efficiency of TASE–Runge–Kutta methods with an inexact Jacobian and to compare second-order stabilized Chebyshev methods.
* [Flow past a cylinder (Schäfer–Turek)](/benchmarks/flow-past-cylinder.md) — The Schäfer–Turek two-dimensional time-dependent flow past a cylinder at Reynolds number 100, with John's reference maxima of drag 2.95 and lift 0.48: setup, the time integrators run on it (Radau IIA, Lobatto IIIC, Gauss, adaptive BDF2 with explicit or linearized convection) and what each run showed.
* [Gray–Scott model](/benchmarks/gray-scott-model.md) — The two-species Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion system on a periodic square, discretized by finite differences, is a stiff two-dimensional benchmark; the page gives the parameter sets of two sources and what they measured with exponential Runge–Kutta and linearly implicit multistep methods.
* [Gresho vortex](/benchmarks/gresho-vortex.md) — Stationary incompressible vortex with piecewise-linear velocity, run as a compressible flow at Mach numbers down to 10^-4 to test whether all-Mach IMEX and semi-implicit time integrators keep the vortex and its kinetic energy with a step independent of the Mach number.
* [Isentropic vortex](/benchmarks/isentropic-vortex.md) — Exact smooth solution of the compressible Euler equations, a vortex advected by a uniform stream or at rest, used to measure temporal convergence order, conservation and cost of time integrators: explicit pairs with PID control, fully implicit Runge–Kutta methods, paired and many-stage stabilized explicit methods, relaxation ADER-DG, and low-Mach IMEX and semi-implicit schemes.
* [Kelvin–Helmholtz instability](/benchmarks/kelvin-helmholtz-instability.md) — A periodic shear layer whose perturbation rolls up into vortices; the sources use an inviscid Euler version to test multirate P-ERK families and error-based step-size control under shock capturing and positivity limits, and a resistive-MHD version to compare exponential integrators, Leja and Krylov evaluation, step-size controllers and implicit BDF.
* [Kepler problem](/benchmarks/kepler-problem.md) — The planar two-body problem with three invariants (energy, angular momentum, Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector), and its perturbed variant with a precessing orbit, used to test invariant-preserving relaxation methods and the modified equation of Störmer–Verlet.
* [Korteweg–de Vries equation](/benchmarks/korteweg-de-vries-equation.md) — The dispersive KdV equation u_t + 6uu_x + u_xxx = 0 and its generalized and linear variants; its third-derivative term makes explicit steps scale like Δx³, so the sources use it to test explicit–implicit–null stabilization and energy-relaxed IMEX methods on soliton and travelling-wave data.
* [Linear hyperbolic relaxation system](/benchmarks/hyperbolic-relaxation-system.md) — Hyperbolic systems with a stiff relaxation source of strength 1/ε, linear or Jin–Xin type, whose ε → 0 limit is a scalar conservation law; the standard test of IMEX, asymptotic-preserving and uniformly accurate time integrators across the kinetic, intermediate and equilibrium regimes.
* [Prothero–Robinson problem](/benchmarks/prothero-robinson-problem.md) — The scalar stiff test problem $y'=\lambda(y-g(t))+g'(t)$, whose smooth exact solution $g$ is independent of the stiffness $\lambda$; it isolates the stiff-regime error of a one-step method, exposes order reduction through the stage-order residuals, and grounds weak stage order and Scholz's Rosenbrock conditions. Includes the multirate Kværnø–Prothero–Robinson variant.
* [Robertson problem](/benchmarks/robertson-problem.md) — Robertson's three-species chemical kinetics system, with rate constants spanning nine orders of magnitude and solutions integrated to t = 1e5 or 1e11; a stiff test with real negative Jacobian eigenvalues on which explicit methods fail and implicit deferred-correction and ADER variants are compared.
* [Sod shock tube](/benchmarks/sod-shock-tube.md) — The one-dimensional Euler Riemann problem of Sod, whose solution has a rarefaction, a contact and a shock; the sources use it to check that asynchronous, adaptive, semi-implicit and IMEX time integrators stay non-oscillatory at shocks, with qualitative comparisons against reference runs.
* [Taylor–Green vortex](/benchmarks/taylor-green-vortex.md) — Periodic vortex flow used to test time integrators on compressible-flow semidiscretizations: inviscid 3D at low Mach number as a stability-limited test for explicit pairs and step-size controllers, viscous 3D for optimized many-stage and semi-implicit methods, and 2D steady at Mach numbers down to 10^-4 for the Mach scaling of IMEX schemes.
* [Van der Pol oscillator](/benchmarks/van-der-pol-oscillator.md) — The nonlinear relaxation oscillator y1' = y2, y2' = mu (1 - y1^2) y2 - y1, whose parameter mu tunes it from non-stiff to very stiff; the sources use it to test step-size controllers and to show that weak stage order and deferred correction do not keep their design orders on a stiff nonlinear problem.
