# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Adaptive-weight (positivity-preserving) Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/adaptive-weight-runge-kutta.md) — After the stages of a Runge–Kutta step are computed, a linear program replaces the weights by the nearest weights that keep the update nonnegative and satisfy the order conditions up to a possibly reduced order; every step remains a Runge–Kutta step, so linear invariants are kept, and convex adaptation inherits A-stability.
* [ADER as a time integrator](/methods/ader-time-integration.md) — Modern ADER read as an ODE integrator: a Galerkin-in-time predictor on one step, a fixed-point problem with a time iteration matrix, solved by K Picard iterations of order min(M+1, K) or to convergence, when the Gauss–Legendre variant is a fully implicit Runge–Kutta method of nodal order 2M+1; it differs from historical Taylor–Cauchy–Kovalevskaya ADER.
* [Backward differentiation formulas (BDF)](/methods/backward-differentiation-formula.md) — The k-step BDF method differentiates the interpolant through the new and k past values: order k, A-stable for k = 1, 2 and A(alpha)-stable up to k = 6, one nonlinear solve per step; fixed- and variable-step forms, energy stability by G-norms and multipliers, step-ratio bounds, software and use in flow problems.
* [Backward error analysis, modified equations and modifying integrators](/methods/backward-error-analysis.md) — Backward error analysis represents a one-step method as the exact flow of a modified vector field, computed from B-series by the substitution law or from the BCH formula for splittings; it explains bounded energy errors of symplectic methods over exponentially long times, and its inverse, the modifying integrator, raises a method's order.
* [BGK penalization](/methods/bgk-penalization.md) — Asymptotic-preserving time stepping for stiff kinetic collision operators: subtract a BGK relaxation term β(M−F)/ε, treat it implicitly and the remainder explicitly, so that conservation makes the implicit step explicit; it is AP after an initial transient, and the successive-penalty variant reaches the limit in one step.
* [Bound-preserving flux limiting of Runge–Kutta stages and updates](/methods/bound-preserving-flux-limiting.md) — Writing a high-order Runge–Kutta update of a finite-volume scheme as a low-order forward Euler update plus antidiffusive fluxes, and limiting those fluxes against step-independent bounds, makes explicit Runge–Kutta methods of any order bound preserving under a forward-Euler-type step restriction; the page covers the semi-discrete maximum principle, the GMC limiter and its stage and final-update variants.
* [Boundary conditions for Runge–Kutta stages](/methods/runge-kutta-stage-boundary-treatment.md) — How time-dependent Dirichlet data are imposed on the internal stages of Runge–Kutta and IMEX methods in a method-of-lines setting: naive stage-time values create numerical boundary layers and order reduction, Irksome imposes the data's time derivative on stage derivatives, and a PDE-based boundary treatment recovers design order 3 and 4.
* [Composition methods and Runge–Kutta–Nyström splittings](/methods/composition-methods.md) — Composition methods raise the order of a basic one-step map by chaining it with weights $\gamma_j$ (triple and quintuple jump, Suzuki–Yoshida recursion, compositions with the adjoint); specialised splittings exploit structure (RKN, modified potentials, near-integrable, linear systems). The page records the recommended schemes of Blanes, Casas and Murua's catalogue.
* [Deferred correction (DeC)](/methods/deferred-correction.md) — Arbitrarily high-order integrators that correct a cheap low-order solution by high-order defect estimates: Abgrall's subnode form (bDeC, sDeC and their convex family, all Runge–Kutta methods) iterates a low-order operator against a collocation operator, can add subnodes between iterations, and has Patankar, two-derivative and spectral (SDC) variants; the implicit-midpoint hierarchy DC2j is A-stable at every order.
* [Dense output](/methods/dense-output.md) — Continuous extensions of a one-step method that give the solution between grid points: Hermite interpolants for nonstiff problems, Lagrange interpolation of stored steps for stiff ones, Rosenbrock weight polynomials (Rodas5, Rodas5P), full-order φ-method dense output, collocation and DG-in-time polynomials, and the time-continuous cell states of asynchronous time stepping.
* [Diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta methods (DIRK, SDIRK, ESDIRK)](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods with lower-triangular Butcher matrix, so each step solves $s$ sequential stage systems of the problem's size; covers the SDIRK, EDIRK, ESDIRK and GEDIRK subclasses, their order and stage-order limits, the tableaux used in the sources, and their cost against fully implicit methods.
* [DIRK schemes with high weak stage order](/methods/dirk-high-weak-stage-order.md) — Diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta schemes designed to have weak stage order above their stage order: the $(2,2,3)$ and $(3,3,3)$ families, the eigenvector-criterion schemes up to weak stage order 3, and the stiffly accurate L-stable DIRK-(7,4,4), DIRK-(12,5,4) and DIRK-(12,5,5) found by constrained polynomial optimization, which recover full order on linear stiff problems.
* [Discrete adjoints of Runge–Kutta and relaxation Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/discrete-adjoint-runge-kutta.md) — The discrete tangent and adjoint of a Runge–Kutta time stepping are the forward and backward substitutions of its global block-triangular system and its transpose; for relaxation methods the relaxation parameter must be differentiated implicitly, which adds rank-one terms, keeps the adjoint consistent and makes it exactly time-symmetric on skew-symmetric linear problems.
* [Embedded Runge–Kutta pairs and the FSAL property](/methods/embedded-runge-kutta-pair.md) — An embedded pair adds a second weight vector to a Runge–Kutta tableau so that the difference of two solutions of orders p and p-hat estimates the local error at no extra stage cost; FSAL and its interaction with relaxation, the affine family of all embeddings (null rules), design criteria, SSP, low-storage and paired pairs, Rosenbrock pairs.
* [Energy method for Runge–Kutta stability](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md) — The energy method writes $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2$ for a Runge–Kutta method on a linear seminegative problem as a diagonal sum plus a quadratic form in a dissipation semi-inner product, by discrete integration by parts, and reads strong, weak or unconditional stability from the signs; it works for non-normal operators, and a multiplier-matrix version gives stiffness-independent stability of IMEX pairs.
* [Evaluating φ-functions: Leja interpolation, Krylov and related methods](/methods/phi-function-evaluation.md) — An exponential integrator spends its time on actions of φ-functions on vectors; the page compares Krylov projection (phipm, KIOPS, EPIC), polynomial interpolation at Leja points, structure-exploiting evaluation (FFT, μ-mode, diagonal operators) and dense precomputation by auxiliary time integration, with the cost models, tolerances and measured speed-ups the sources report.
* [Explicit Runge–Kutta methods with high weak stage order](/methods/explicit-runge-kutta-high-weak-stage-order.md) — Explicit Runge–Kutta methods whose weights are orthogonal to the residual space up to weak stage order $q_{\mathrm w}\ge2$, which alleviates order reduction on hyperbolic problems with time-dependent boundary data; stage bound $p+q_{\mathrm w}\le s+1$, Sylvester-equation characterization, optimized schemes up to $(9,5,5)$, parallel-iterated $(p^2,p,p)$ schemes, and zero SSP coefficient.
* [Explicit–implicit–null (EIN) time marching](/methods/explicit-implicit-null-method.md) — The explicit–implicit–null method adds and subtracts a linear constant-coefficient surrogate of the stiffest term, treats the added copy implicitly and everything else explicitly, so stiff nonlinear diffusion or dispersion needs only constant-coefficient linear solves; stability needs the surrogate above a scheme-dependent fraction, about 0.54 for ARS(4,4,3).
* [Exponential integrators and φ-functions](/methods/exponential-integrators.md) — Exponential integrators treat a stiff linear part exactly through its exponential and the φ-functions; the page records the φ-function conventions, the exponential Runge–Kutta (Cox–Matthews, Krogstad, stiffly accurate), Rosenbrock, EPIRK and stiffness-resilient families, nondiagonal and partial linear operators, repartitioning for purely imaginary spectra and Parareal, multirate infinitesimal structure, stiff kinetic relaxation, structural dynamics and Magnus methods.
* [Extrapolation methods (Richardson extrapolation of a base integrator)](/methods/extrapolation-methods.md) — An extrapolation method combines several solutions of a low-order base method over one step, computed with different substep counts, so that their leading errors cancel; written as a Runge–Kutta method it has stages that are base-method steps and non-convex final weights, which the sources exploit for positivity and bound preservation.
* [Fully implicit collocation Runge–Kutta methods (Gauss, Radau IIA, Lobatto IIIC)](/methods/fully-implicit-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods with a full Butcher matrix built on Gauss, Radau or Lobatto nodes: Gauss of order $2s$, Radau IIA of order $2s-1$ and Lobatto IIIC of order $2s-2$, with high stage order, A-, L-, B-stability and symplecticity as the family allows; the coupled $sd$-dimensional stage system and its preconditioning decide their cost.
* [Generalized additive Runge–Kutta (GARK) methods and internal consistency](/methods/generalized-additive-runge-kutta.md) — GARK methods give each additive partition of the right-hand side its own stages and coupling matrices; the page states the scheme, internal consistency, the order-condition and stability machinery the sources use, and how IMEX, multirate and explicit methods embed in it.
* [IMEX and additive Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) — Additive Runge–Kutta methods integrate y' = fE + fI with an explicit tableau for the nonstiff part and a diagonally implicit tableau for the stiff part on shared stages; the page gives the scheme, the type I/II/ARS classification, named pairs, GARK and splitting views, SSP, asymptotic-preserving and uniformly accurate variants, solver costs and software practice.
* [IMEX linear multistep and IMEX-BDF methods](/methods/imex-linear-multistep-methods.md) — IMEX multistep methods treat a stiff part implicitly with BDF and the rest by explicit extrapolation of past values: IMEX-BDF coefficients, explicit versus linearly implicit treatment of the nonlinear term, uniform-in-epsilon accuracy for relaxation systems, robust Navier–Stokes bounds with their CFL-type conditions, and the linearly implicit multistep methods as IMEX schemes.
* [IMEX multirate infinitesimal GARK methods](/methods/imex-mri-gark.md) — IMEX-MRI-GARK methods split the slow scale of a three-way additive problem into implicit and explicit parts and treat the fast scale infinitesimally; the page states the scheme, its GARK form, internal-consistency and third- and fourth-order conditions, linear stability, the methods 3a, 3b and 4, and their measured behaviour.
* [IMEX multirate infinitesimal stage-restart (IMEX-MRI-SR) methods](/methods/imex-mri-sr.md) — IMEX-MRI-SR methods restart every fast sub-problem at the start of the macro step and follow it by a diagonally implicit slow solve; the page gives the scheme, its GARK form, internal-consistency and third- and fourth-order conditions, the n_Omega ≥ 2 barrier, linear stability, the embedded methods SR2(1), SR3(2), SR4(3), and MERK as a special case.
* [Lawson (integrating-factor) methods](/methods/lawson-methods.md) — Lawson methods apply an explicit Runge–Kutta method to the equation transformed by the integrating factor of the stiff linear part, so they need only matrix exponentials; the page gives the first- and second-order schemes, Caliari et al.'s stabilized variants and stability bounds for a partial linear operator, and the integrating-factor form of stiff kinetic relaxation.
* [Lax–Wendroff flux reconstruction (single-stage Taylor time stepping)](/methods/lax-wendroff-flux-reconstruction.md) — A single-step, single-stage Taylor (Cauchy–Kovalevskaya) time discretization of order N+1 for conservation laws that updates the solution with a Jacobian-free time-averaged flux inside flux reconstruction; its CFL limits come from von Neumann analysis, and a truncated flux series gives it an embedded error estimate for PID step-size control.
* [Linear multistep methods](/methods/linear-multistep-method.md) — A k-step linear multistep method combines k past values and right-hand sides into one new value per step; its generating polynomials, the root and stability-region conditions, variable-step coefficients, the Adams and BDF families, and the energy, linearly implicit and IMEX variants the sources build on it.
* [Linearly implicit multistep methods (Limm, Limm-W)](/methods/linearly-implicit-multistep-method.md) — Glandon, Narayanamurthi and Sandu's linearly implicit multistep methods add a Jacobian term to a linear multistep method so that each step costs one linear solve: definition and coefficient count, W- and exact-Jacobian order conditions via Taylor and B-series, linear stability, stiff convergence, optimized methods of orders 1 to 5 and a variable-step variable-order implementation.
* [Local time stepping](/methods/local-time-stepping.md) — Local time stepping lets regions, cells or subdomains of a locally refined semi-discretization advance with their own explicit steps instead of the global CFL step of the smallest cell; the page compares leapfrog LTS for waves, asynchronous cell-level LTS, overlapping-subdomain LTS, clustered ADER-DG LTS and multi-time-step partitioned coupling by interface treatment, conservation, order, stability and speedup.
* [Low-storage Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/low-storage-runge-kutta.md) — Explicit Runge–Kutta methods whose coefficients are restricted so that a step needs only two or three solution-sized registers; the 2N, 2R, 3S* and 3S*+ classes, the explicit Butcher-form characterization of Williamson 2N methods and its consequences, three registers as the minimum with an error estimate and step rejection, and SSP, optimized, many-stage and paired low-storage methods.
* [Micro–macro decomposition](/methods/micro-macro-decomposition.md) — Asymptotic-preserving reformulation of kinetic equations as an equilibrium part plus an ε-scaled microscopic correction, discretized with only a linear stiff operator implicit; its limit is a scheme for Euler or diffusion equations, with an explicit diffusive limit that keeps a parabolic step restriction uniform in ε.
* [Mixed-precision explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/mixed-precision-runge-kutta.md) — Mixed-precision explicit Runge–Kutta methods evaluate most right-hand sides in low precision and only one or two per step in high precision; written in delta form with low-precision Jacobian actions, Runge–Kutta–Chebyshev methods keep their first or second order instead of stagnating at the low-precision roundoff.
* [Modified Patankar methods (MPRK, MPDeC, MP linear multistep, Patankar predictor–corrector)](/methods/modified-patankar-methods.md) — Patankar-type schemes weight each production and destruction term by a ratio of new to old values, making every stage a linear M-matrix system; they are positive and conservative for every step size on production–destruction systems, up to arbitrary order, but can oscillate around steady states and lose order for vanishing constituents.
* [Multiderivative Runge–Kutta methods and the backward derivative condition](/methods/multiderivative-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods that also use higher time derivatives of the solution ($\dot f=f'f$ and beyond): implicit two-derivative ones with nonpositive $\dot f$ coefficients are SSP for every step at orders 2–4, explicit $r$-derivative ones are made Jacobian-free for conservation laws by approximate Taylor fluxes, and implicit Hermite–Birkhoff ones are discretized with an auxiliary variable.
* [Multiplier energy technique for multistep methods (Nevanlinna–Odeh multipliers)](/methods/multiplier-energy-technique.md) — Energy proofs for BDF and IMEX-BDF on parabolic and relaxation problems: test the scheme with y_{n+k} minus a multiplier combination of past values, telescope a G-norm, and bound the dissipative term per step (Nevanlinna–Odeh) or summed through Toeplitz positivity (Grenander–Szegő); known multipliers, the BDF6 gap and the variable-step BDF2 extension.
* [Multirate exponential Runge–Kutta and Rosenbrock methods (MERK, MERB)](/methods/multirate-exponential-methods.md) — MERK and MERB methods turn the φ-function weights of an exponential Runge–Kutta or Rosenbrock method into polynomial forcings of modified linear fast ODEs that any inner solver integrates; the page gives the construction lemma, the MERB schemes of orders 2–6, the convergence theorem with its inner-solver orders, MERK's order for nonlinear fast parts, and the measured cost.
* [Multirate GARK methods (MrGARK, compound-fast, multirate backward Euler)](/methods/multirate-gark.md) — Multirate GARK methods advance a slow partition with one macro step and a fast partition with m micro steps of the same base method; the page gives the standard and compound-fast forms, their order conditions, the implicit–implicit methods of orders one to four, their stability angles and their cost.
* [Multirate infinitesimal methods (MIS, MRI-GARK)](/methods/multirate-infinitesimal-methods.md) — Multirate infinitesimal methods advance the slow partition with a Runge–Kutta skeleton and reach each slow stage by solving a modified fast ODE forced by a polynomial in time built from slow tendencies; the page covers MIS, MRI-GARK and the stage-restart and exponential members, their order statements, embeddings and multirate step-size adaptivity, and their implementation and performance in ARKODE.
* [Newmark method](/methods/newmark-method.md) — The Newmark family integrates second-order structural dynamics with two parameters; its constant-average-acceleration member is second order, unconditionally stable and free of numerical damping, equals the first Padé scheme, and is the baseline that high-order Padé and Radau IIA integrators are measured against.
* [Nonlinearly partitioned Runge–Kutta (NPRK) methods](/methods/nonlinearly-partitioned-runge-kutta.md) — NPRK methods integrate y' = F(y,y) for any two-argument splitting F of the right-hand side, giving each argument its own implicitness through a rank-3 coefficient tensor; the page gives the scheme, its reductions to RK, ARK, PRK and SIRK, the order-condition route, IMEX and IMIM stability notions, the named IMEX-NPRK methods, and the multirate extension MR-NPRK with its L-stability theorem.
* [Operator splitting: Lie–Trotter, Strang and dimensional splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md) — Splitting methods advance $y'=f^{[1]}(y)+f^{[2]}(y)$ by composing the flows of the parts: Lie–Trotter is first order, Strang second order and symmetric; the page covers their error constants, conjugacy, linear stability, PDE (ADI, LOD, Peaceman–Rachford) forms, boundary order reduction, multiphysics comparisons with IMEX, and multirate and asymptotic-preserving uses.
* [Optimal stability polynomials for explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/optimal-stability-polynomial.md) — Choosing the free coefficients of an explicit method's stability polynomial to maximize the stable step for the spectrum of a given semi-discretization (Ketcheson–Ahmadia bisection over a convex problem, or a root parametrization for more than 100 stages), then realizing it by a tableau; gains grow with stage count and are measured per right-hand-side evaluation.
* [Padé-based high-order implicit time integration of structural dynamics](/methods/pade-time-integration.md) — Song and Eisenträger replace the matrix exponential of the exact one-step map of linear structural dynamics by its $(m,m)$ diagonal Padé approximant, giving one-step schemes of order $2m$ solved as $m$ shifted Newmark-like systems, one real or one complex system per root or conjugate pair.
* [Paired explicit Runge–Kutta (P-ERK) methods](/methods/paired-explicit-runge-kutta.md) — P-ERK families are explicit Runge–Kutta tableaux with a common stage count, abscissae and weights but different numbers of active stages, assigned per mesh region by local stiffness, also dynamically under AMR; one global step gives a multirate effect at second, third and fourth order, with optimized stability polynomials, an embedded stability-limit detector, and a loss of monotonicity at member interfaces.
* [ParaDiag: diagonalization-based parallel-in-time methods](/methods/paradiag.md) — ParaDiag writes all time steps of a linear implicit scheme as one space-time system and diagonalizes its time matrix, so that the steps decouple into independent shifted spatial solves. Gander and Palitta keep the circulant-plus-low-rank structure of backward Euler and BDF exactly via Sherman–Morrison–Woodbury and a Krylov capacitance solve.
* [Parallel-across-the-method spectral deferred correction (diagonal SDC)](/methods/parallel-sdc.md) — Diagonal SDC sweeps the collocation problem of one step with a diagonal preconditioner, so the node solves of each sweep run in parallel. The page gives the iteration matrices, the nilpotency-based coefficients MIN-SR-NS, MIN-SR-S and MIN-SR-FLEX, their order and stability, and measured and modelled efficiency, including adaptive steps.
* [Parallel-in-time integration](/methods/parallel-in-time-integration.md) — Parallel-in-time methods distribute the time direction over processes: across the steps (Parareal, pipelined SDC), through an all-at-once space-time solve (ParaDiag), or across the method (diagonal SDC). The page gives the taxonomy and how each source's speedup was measured, modelled or only predicted.
* [Parareal](/methods/parareal.md) — Parareal iterates a cheap serial coarse propagator and an accurate fine propagator run in parallel on all time slices; it converges to the serial fine solution, exactly after N iterations. The page gathers its convergence bounds and the variants with low-rank, optimized rational and exponential propagators.
* [Processing and effective order](/methods/processing-and-effective-order.md) — A kernel $\psi_h$ has effective order $p$ if a near-identity processor $\pi_h$ makes $\pi_h\circ\psi_h\circ\pi_h^{-1}$ of order $p$; since the processor acts only at output times, the kernel needs far fewer order conditions. The page covers Butcher's effective-order Runge–Kutta method, processed splittings and an explicit processor for highly oscillatory Strang splitting.
* [Pseudo-energy-preserving explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/pseudo-energy-preserving-runge-kutta.md) — A Runge–Kutta method of classical order p is pseudo-energy-preserving of order q ≥ p when its one-step energy error on every canonical Hamiltonian system is O(h^(q+1)); the conditions follow from energy-preserving B-series of the modified equation, and explicit methods up to q = 6 exist.
* [Quinpi: implicit high-order finite-volume time stepping with a-posteriori time limiting](/methods/quinpi.md) — Quinpi integrates stiff hyperbolic systems with a third-order SDIRK and CWENO finite volumes, using a first-order implicit predictor to freeze the nonlinear reconstruction weights so that each stage is nonlinear only through the flux, and a conservative entropy-triggered time limiter that falls back to predictor fluxes in troubled cells.
* [Relaxation Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/relaxation-runge-kutta.md) — Relaxation rescales each Runge–Kutta update by a scalar root γn of one entropy equation so that a convex entropy or invariant is conserved or dissipated at the fully discrete level; placed at tn+γnh it keeps order p, placed at tn+h (IDT) it loses one order. Variants cover IMEX, multirate, ADER, several invariants, FSAL pairs and adjoints.
* [Rodas5P](/methods/rodas5p.md) — Steinebach's 2023 coefficient set for the 8-stage stiffly accurate fifth-order Rosenbrock–Wanner structure of Rodas5: order 5(4) on index-1 DAEs, order reduction limited as in Rodas4P2, index-2 and inexact-Jacobian order 2, A-stable embedded method and an order-4 dense output.
* [Rosenbrock–Wanner (ROW) and W-methods](/methods/rosenbrock-wanner-method.md) — Linearly implicit one-step methods that replace the nonlinear stage solves of implicit Runge–Kutta methods by s linear solves with the single matrix M - h gamma J: the scheme, its ODE, index-1 and index-2 DAE, W-method and Prothero–Robinson order conditions, stiff accuracy, order reduction, and the Rodas family.
* [Semi-implicit and linearly implicit IMEX Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/semi-implicit-imex-runge-kutta.md) — Semi-implicit IMEX Runge–Kutta methods write y' = H(y, y) with an explicit and an implicit argument, duplicate the state and apply an IMEX tableau pair to the two copies, so that freezing coefficients at the explicit copy makes every stage solve linear; the page gives the scheme, its tableaux, its all-Mach, MHD and radiation-hydrodynamics uses and its limits.
* [Solving the stage equations of implicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md) — How the nonlinear and linear systems of implicit Runge–Kutta stages (and of implicit multistep, two-derivative and Patankar steps) are solved: Newton, Picard, simplified and matrix-free Newton, predictors, tolerances and Jacobian reuse, real-Schur, diagonalization-based and stage-parallel preconditioning, extended block-Jacobi, parallel-in-time solves, positivity-preserving linear solves and solver software.
* [Splitting and composition with complex coefficients](/methods/complex-coefficient-splitting.md) — Splitting and composition methods with complex coefficients of positive real part escape the negative-coefficient barrier and keep their classical order on parabolic problems generating analytic semigroups, up to order barriers of their own; symmetric-conjugate methods nearly preserve unitarity, and projection onto the real part raises odd orders by one.
* [Stabilized explicit Runge–Kutta methods: Chebyshev, Legendre and super-time-stepping](/methods/stabilized-explicit-runge-kutta.md) — Stabilized explicit Runge–Kutta methods fix a low order and use many stages whose stability polynomial, built from shifted Chebyshev or Legendre polynomials and realized by a three-term recurrence, has a real stability interval growing like s squared; the page covers RKC, RKL super-time-stepping, monotonic RKC, disk polynomials and their limits.
* [Step-size control: error-based controllers, PID filters and CFL control](/methods/step-size-control.md) — Adaptive time stepping: a scaled norm of the embedded local-error estimate decides acceptance and an I, PI, PID or Gustafsson controller sets the next step; the page covers controller stability and tuning, error- versus CFL-based control for stability-limited CFD, control with relaxation, stabilized, single-stage Taylor, positivity-adapted and SDC methods, multirate H-M controllers, cost-based control and variable-step multistep control.
* [Strong-stability-preserving (SSP) Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) — A Runge–Kutta method is SSP with coefficient $\mathcal C$ when it preserves every convex-functional bound that forward Euler preserves for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, under $h\le\mathcal C h_{\mathrm{FE}}$; the page gives the characterization, order and coefficient barriers, the optimal explicit methods, embedded pairs and the multiderivative and IMEX extensions.
* [Superviscosity-stabilized explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/superviscosity-stabilized-runge-kutta.md) — Sun and Shu enforce strong stability of an explicit Runge–Kutta method on linear seminegative problems by adding diffusive and dispersive superviscosity of order $h^{2\zeta-1}$ to the operator or as a post-step filter, with sharp critical coefficients and no loss of linear order, plus an adaptive filter for nonlinear problems.
* [TASE operators: time-accurate and highly stable explicit operators](/methods/tase-operators.md) — TASE operators precondition the stiff linear(ized) part of a semi-discrete system with a rational operator, a weighted sum of shifted implicit-Euler resolvents, so that an unchanged explicit Runge–Kutta method becomes A-stable or nearly so while keeping its order; with an inexact Jacobian its linear stability is decided by stability diagrams and generalized eigenvalues.
