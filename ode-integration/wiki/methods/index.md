# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [ADER as a time integrator](/methods/ader-time-integration.md) — Modern ADER read as an ODE integrator: a Galerkin-in-time predictor on one step, a fixed-point problem with a time iteration matrix, solved by K Picard iterations of order min(M+1, K); it differs from historical Taylor–Cauchy–Kovalevskaya ADER and has a Newton-type implicit variant for stiff sources.
* [Backward error analysis, modified equations and modifying integrators](/methods/backward-error-analysis.md) — Backward error analysis represents a one-step method as the exact flow of a modified vector field, computed from B-series by the substitution law or from the BCH formula for splittings; it explains bounded energy errors of symplectic methods over exponentially long times, and its inverse, the modifying integrator, raises a method's order.
* [BGK penalization](/methods/bgk-penalization.md) — Asymptotic-preserving time stepping for stiff kinetic collision operators: subtract a BGK relaxation term β(M−F)/ε, treat it implicitly and the remainder explicitly, so that conservation makes the implicit step explicit; it is AP after an initial transient, and the successive-penalty variant reaches the limit in one step.
* [Composition methods and Runge–Kutta–Nyström splittings](/methods/composition-methods.md) — Composition methods raise the order of a basic one-step map by chaining it with weights $\gamma_j$ (triple and quintuple jump, Suzuki–Yoshida recursion, compositions with the adjoint); specialised splittings exploit structure (RKN, modified potentials, near-integrable, linear systems). The page records the recommended schemes of Blanes, Casas and Murua's catalogue.
* [Deferred correction (DeC)](/methods/deferred-correction.md) — Arbitrarily high-order one-step integrator that iterates a cheap low-order operator against a high-order collocation operator on subnodes of the step; in Abgrall's form K explicit corrections give order min(M+1, K), and linearly implicit variants handle stiff sources.
* [Dense output](/methods/dense-output.md) — Continuous extensions of a one-step method that give the solution between grid points: Hermite interpolants for nonstiff problems, Lagrange interpolation of stored steps for stiff ones, and weight polynomials of Rosenbrock methods, with the order-4 dense output of Rodas5 and Rodas5P.
* [Diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta methods (DIRK, SDIRK, ESDIRK)](/methods/diagonally-implicit-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods with lower-triangular Butcher matrix, so each step solves $s$ sequential stage systems of the problem's size; covers the SDIRK, EDIRK, ESDIRK and GEDIRK subclasses, their order and stage-order limits, the tableaux used in the sources, and their cost against fully implicit methods.
* [DIRK schemes with high weak stage order](/methods/dirk-high-weak-stage-order.md) — Diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta schemes designed to have weak stage order above their stage order: the $(2,2,3)$ and $(3,3,3)$ families, the eigenvector-criterion schemes up to weak stage order 3, and the stiffly accurate L-stable DIRK-(7,4,4), DIRK-(12,5,4) and DIRK-(12,5,5) found by constrained polynomial optimization, which recover full order on linear stiff problems.
* [Embedded Runge–Kutta pairs and the FSAL property](/methods/embedded-runge-kutta-pair.md) — An embedded pair adds a second weight vector to a Runge–Kutta tableau so that the difference of two solutions of orders p and p-hat estimates the local error at no extra stage cost; FSAL, design criteria, SSP and low-storage pairs, Rosenbrock pairs.
* [Energy method for Runge–Kutta stability](/methods/runge-kutta-energy-method.md) — The energy method writes $\lVert y_{n+1}\rVert^2-\lVert y_n\rVert^2$ for a Runge–Kutta method on a linear seminegative problem as a diagonal sum plus a quadratic form in a dissipation semi-inner product, by discrete integration by parts, and reads strong, weak or unconditional stability from the signs; it works for non-normal operators.
* [Explicit Runge–Kutta methods with high weak stage order](/methods/explicit-runge-kutta-high-weak-stage-order.md) — Explicit Runge–Kutta methods whose weights are orthogonal to the residual space up to weak stage order $q_{\mathrm w}\ge2$, which alleviates order reduction on hyperbolic problems with time-dependent boundary data; stage bound $p+q_{\mathrm w}\le s+1$, Sylvester-equation characterization, optimized schemes up to $(9,5,5)$, parallel-iterated $(p^2,p,p)$ schemes, and zero SSP coefficient.
* [Exponential integrators and φ-functions](/methods/exponential-integrators.md) — Exponential integrators treat a stiff linear part exactly through its exponential and the φ-functions; the page records the φ-function conventions, the exponential structure of multirate infinitesimal methods with an exact fast solve, the exponential reformulation of stiff kinetic relaxation, and Magnus and commutator-free quasi-Magnus methods for linear non-autonomous problems.
* [Fully implicit collocation Runge–Kutta methods (Gauss, Radau IIA, Lobatto IIIC)](/methods/fully-implicit-runge-kutta.md) — Runge–Kutta methods with a full Butcher matrix built on Gauss, Radau or Lobatto nodes: Gauss of order $2s$, Radau IIA of order $2s-1$ and Lobatto IIIC of order $2s-2$, with high stage order, A-, L-, B-stability and symplecticity as the family allows; the coupled $sd$-dimensional stage system and its preconditioning decide their cost.
* [Generalized additive Runge–Kutta (GARK) methods and internal consistency](/methods/generalized-additive-runge-kutta.md) — GARK methods give each additive partition of the right-hand side its own stages and coupling matrices; the page states the scheme, internal consistency, the order-condition and stability machinery the sources use, and how IMEX, multirate and explicit methods embed in it.
* [IMEX and additive Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/imex-runge-kutta.md) — Additive Runge–Kutta methods integrate y' = fE + fI with an explicit tableau for the nonstiff part and a diagonally implicit tableau for the stiff part on shared stages; the page gives the scheme, its GARK and splitting views, SSP and asymptotic-preserving variants, solver costs and software practice.
* [IMEX multirate infinitesimal GARK methods](/methods/imex-mri-gark.md) — IMEX-MRI-GARK methods split the slow scale of a three-way additive problem into implicit and explicit parts and treat the fast scale infinitesimally; the page states the scheme, its GARK form, internal-consistency and third- and fourth-order conditions, linear stability, the methods 3a, 3b and 4, and their measured behaviour.
* [Low-storage Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/low-storage-runge-kutta.md) — Explicit Runge–Kutta methods whose coefficients are restricted so that a step needs only two or three solution-sized registers; the 2N, 2R, 3S* and 3S*+ classes, three registers as the minimum with an error estimate and step rejection, and SSP and optimized low-storage pairs.
* [Micro–macro decomposition](/methods/micro-macro-decomposition.md) — Asymptotic-preserving reformulation of kinetic equations as an equilibrium part plus an ε-scaled microscopic correction, discretized with only a linear stiff operator implicit; its limit is a scheme for Euler or diffusion equations, with an explicit diffusive limit that keeps a parabolic step restriction uniform in ε.
* [Multiderivative Runge–Kutta methods and the backward derivative condition](/methods/multiderivative-runge-kutta.md) — Two-derivative Runge–Kutta methods use $f$ and $\dot f=f'f$; under the forward Euler and backward derivative conditions, implicit ones with nonpositive $\dot f$ coefficients are SSP for every step size at orders 2–4, and their IMEX extension is SSP, positivity preserving and asymptotic preserving uniformly in a stiffness parameter.
* [Multirate GARK methods (MrGARK, compound-fast, multirate backward Euler)](/methods/multirate-gark.md) — Multirate GARK methods advance a slow partition with one macro step and a fast partition with m micro steps of the same base method; the page gives the standard and compound-fast forms, their order conditions, the implicit–implicit methods of orders one to four, their stability angles and their cost.
* [Multirate infinitesimal methods (MIS, MRI-GARK)](/methods/multirate-infinitesimal-methods.md) — Multirate infinitesimal methods advance the slow partition with a Runge–Kutta skeleton and reach each slow stage by solving a modified fast ODE forced by a polynomial in time built from slow tendencies; the page covers MIS and MRI-GARK, their stage structure, order statements and implementation in ARKODE.
* [Operator splitting: Lie–Trotter, Strang and dimensional splitting](/methods/operator-splitting.md) — Splitting methods advance $y'=f^{[1]}(y)+f^{[2]}(y)$ by composing the flows of the parts: Lie–Trotter is first order, Strang second order and symmetric; the page covers their error constants, conjugacy, linear stability, PDE (ADI, LOD, Peaceman–Rachford) forms, boundary order reduction and multirate and asymptotic-preserving uses.
* [Optimal stability polynomials for explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/optimal-stability-polynomial.md) — Choosing the free coefficients of an explicit method's stability polynomial to maximize the stable step for the spectrum of a given semi-discretization (Ketcheson–Ahmadia bisection over a convex problem), then realizing it by a tableau; gains grow with stage count and are measured per right-hand-side evaluation.
* [Processing and effective order](/methods/processing-and-effective-order.md) — A kernel $\psi_h$ has effective order $p$ if a near-identity processor $\pi_h$ makes $\pi_h\circ\psi_h\circ\pi_h^{-1}$ of order $p$; since the processor acts only at output times, the kernel needs far fewer order conditions. The page covers Butcher's effective-order Runge–Kutta method, processed splittings and an explicit processor for highly oscillatory Strang splitting.
* [Rodas5P](/methods/rodas5p.md) — Steinebach's 2023 coefficient set for the 8-stage stiffly accurate fifth-order Rosenbrock–Wanner structure of Rodas5: order 5(4) on index-1 DAEs, order reduction limited as in Rodas4P2, index-2 and inexact-Jacobian order 2, A-stable embedded method and an order-4 dense output.
* [Rosenbrock–Wanner (ROW) and W-methods](/methods/rosenbrock-wanner-method.md) — Linearly implicit one-step methods that replace the nonlinear stage solves of implicit Runge–Kutta methods by s linear solves with the single matrix M - h gamma J: the scheme, its ODE, index-1 and index-2 DAE, W-method and Prothero–Robinson order conditions, stiff accuracy, order reduction, and the Rodas family.
* [Solving the stage equations of implicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/implicit-stage-solvers.md) — How the nonlinear and linear systems of implicit Runge–Kutta stages are solved: Newton and simplified Newton iterations, predictors, tolerances and Jacobian reuse, real-Schur block preconditioning of fully implicit methods, block preconditioners in finite-element codes, multirate Newton structure, and solver software.
* [Splitting and composition with complex coefficients](/methods/complex-coefficient-splitting.md) — Splitting and composition methods with complex coefficients of positive real part escape the negative-coefficient barrier and keep their classical order on parabolic problems generating analytic semigroups, up to order barriers of their own; symmetric-conjugate methods nearly preserve unitarity, and projection onto the real part raises odd orders by one.
* [Step-size control: error-based controllers, PID filters and CFL control](/methods/step-size-control.md) — Adaptive time stepping: a scaled norm of the embedded local-error estimate decides acceptance, and an I, PI, PID or Gustafsson controller sets the next step; step-size-control stability, controller tuning per pair, and error-based versus CFL-based control for stability-limited CFD.
* [Strong-stability-preserving (SSP) Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/strong-stability-preserving-runge-kutta.md) — A Runge–Kutta method is SSP with coefficient $\mathcal C$ when it preserves every convex-functional bound that forward Euler preserves for $h\le h_{\mathrm{FE}}$, under $h\le\mathcal C h_{\mathrm{FE}}$; the page gives the characterization, order and coefficient barriers, the optimal explicit methods, embedded pairs and the multiderivative and IMEX extensions.
* [Superviscosity-stabilized explicit Runge–Kutta methods](/methods/superviscosity-stabilized-runge-kutta.md) — Sun and Shu enforce strong stability of an explicit Runge–Kutta method on linear seminegative problems by adding diffusive and dispersive superviscosity of order $h^{2\zeta-1}$ to the operator or as a post-step filter, with sharp critical coefficients and no loss of linear order, plus an adaptive filter for nonlinear problems.
