# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Convolution](/definitions/convolution.md) — The integral of f(x) g(z - x) over all x (Fourier form) or over 0 to t (Laplace form), commutative, associative and distributive; it smooths and broadens, models a measurement through a resolution function, and is inverted (deconvolution) by division of transforms.
* [Cross-correlation and energy spectrum](/definitions/cross-correlation.md) — The cross-correlation of f and g, the integral of conj(f(x)) g(x + z) over x, measures their similarity as one is displaced by z; it is associative and distributive but not commutative, and the auto-correlation of f is the inverse transform of sqrt(2 pi) |f-hat|^2, which defines the energy spectrum.
* [Dirac delta function](/definitions/dirac-delta-function.md) — The generalized function defined by the sifting property, with its scaling and composition rules, its derivative, the Heaviside step as its integral, its Fourier representation as a uniform superposition of all modes, and its limiting representation by the band-limited sinc kernel.
* [Dirichlet conditions](/definitions/dirichlet-conditions.md) — The four conditions (periodicity, single-valuedness with finitely many finite discontinuities, finitely many extrema per period, absolutely integrable over a period) under which a function may be expanded as a Fourier series; stated without proof as sufficient for convergence to the function at its points of continuity.
* [Discrete Fourier transform](/definitions/discrete-fourier-transform.md) — The transform between N equally spaced samples of one period and N discrete Fourier coefficients, built on the orthogonality of exp(2 pi i j m / N) over the points; it has shift and cyclic-convolution theorems like the continuous transform, a unitary matrix (in the symmetric normalization), Hermitian-symmetric coefficients for real samples, aliasing of modes m and m + N, and the fast Fourier transform as an O(N log N) evaluation.
* [Fourier series](/definitions/fourier-series.md) — Expansion of a periodic function of period L in the mutually orthogonal modes cos and sin of k_m x (real form) or exp(i k_m x) (complex form), with coefficients given by integrals over one period; symmetry of the function removes coefficient families, and a function on a finite range is expanded through a chosen periodic extension.
* [Fourier transform](/definitions/fourier-transform.md) — Integral representation of a non-periodic, absolutely integrable function as a superposition of modes exp(i k x), obtained as the infinite-period limit of the complex Fourier series; defined here with the symmetric 1/sqrt(2 pi) normalization, with sine and cosine transforms for odd and even functions, the multidimensional pair, and (in exercises) sampled and periodized forms.
* [Laplace transform](/definitions/laplace-transform.md) — The one-sided transform of f(t) on t >= 0 with kernel exp(-s t), defined for s above an abscissa of convergence (existing for functions of exponential order); linear, unique up to a null function (Lerch), inverted from a table of standard transforms or by the Bromwich contour integral, and the tool for initial-value problems because it turns derivatives into algebraic terms carrying the initial data.
