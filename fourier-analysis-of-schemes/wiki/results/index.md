# Results

Theorems, proved properties, impossibility results.

* [Convergence of Fourier series](/results/fourier-series-convergence.md) — Under the Dirichlet conditions the Fourier series converges to f at points of continuity and to the mean of the one-sided limits at a finite discontinuity (stated without proof); term-by-term integration converges to the integral, and term-by-term differentiation converges to f' when f is continuous and periodic and f' satisfies the Dirichlet conditions.
* [Convolution theorem](/results/convolution-theorem.md) — The Fourier transform of a convolution is sqrt(2 pi) times the product of the transforms, and the transform of a product is 1/sqrt(2 pi) times the convolution of the transforms (constants of the unitary normalization); the Laplace transform of the one-sided convolution is the plain product of the Laplace transforms.
* [Fourier transform properties](/results/fourier-transform-properties.md) — Operational rules of the unitary Fourier transform: differentiation multiplies the transform by i omega (so a derivative of order p by (i omega)^p), integration divides by i omega plus a delta for the constant, scaling, translation as a phase factor, exponential multiplication as a shift of the argument, and the transforms of t f(t) and of odd or periodic functions.
* [Gibbs phenomenon](/results/gibbs-phenomenon.md) — At a jump discontinuity the partial sums of a Fourier series overshoot the function; as more terms are added the overshoot moves arbitrarily close to the discontinuity but never disappears, and its size is proportional to the magnitude of the jump (stated without proof or numerical value).
* [Laplace transform properties](/results/laplace-transform-properties.md) — Operational rules of the Laplace transform: the transform of the p-th derivative is s^p times the transform minus a polynomial in s carrying the initial values, integration divides by s, multiplication by exp(a t) shifts s, multiplication of the transform by exp(-b s) delays f, plus scaling, multiplication by t^p, division by t, and (exercise) the limit s L[f] -> A as s -> 0.
* [Parseval's theorem](/results/parseval-theorem.md) — The mean of |f|^2 over a period equals the sum of |f-hat_m|^2 over the Fourier-series coefficients, and for the unitary Fourier transform the integral of |f|^2 equals the integral of |f-hat|^2; both follow from the general multiplication theorem, and the series form is a special case of the transform form.
* [Wiener–Khinchin theorem](/results/wiener-khinchin-theorem.md) — The Fourier transform of the cross-correlation of f and g is sqrt(2 pi) times conj(f-hat) times g-hat, so the auto-correlation of f is the inverse transform of the energy spectrum sqrt(2 pi) |f-hat|^2; the converse gives the transform of conj(f) g as the cross-correlation of the transforms.
