# Summary

The paper simulates neon-doped deuterium
[shattered pellet injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md) (SPI)
into an ASDEX Upgrade (AUG) H-mode (#40355, CLISTE reconstruction,
$B_\phi\approx1.8$ T, $I_p=0.8$ MA) with [JOREK](/software/jorek.md), using the
visco-resistive two-temperature [reduced MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md) model in
X-point geometry (70×110 flux-aligned elements, $n=0$–7, ideal wall). Impurity
charge states follow a
[collisional-radiative model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md)
on fluid-advected markers with OpenADAS data; ablation follows an improved
neutral-gas-shielding law for mixed Ne–D pellets
([pellet ablation models](/methods/pellet-ablation-models.md)); plumes of 53, 199
and 1105 fragments (shatter angles 12.5°, 20°, 25°) mimic the AUG shatter heads.
Three scans are run: neon fraction (0, 0.12, 1, 10%), fragment size (at 0.12%
and 10% Ne) and penetration speed (443 against 222 m/s). The
[thermal quench](/definitions/thermal-quench.md) comes in two stages: first,
about half of $W_{\mathrm{th}}$ is lost within roughly 0.1–0.15 ms of fragment
arrival by convective and conductive transport in a field stochastized by
pedestal breakup and then 3/1 and 2/1 modes, nearly independent of the neon
fraction; second, a radiation-dominated loss ends with a 1/1-driven core
collapse. Pure D shows no second stage. Lower neon fractions assimilate a larger
fraction of the pellet (≈20% at 0.12%) because full collapse comes later, while
the absolute neon assimilated still grows with neon fraction. Smaller and slower
fragments cool the edge and form a cold front, turning the cooling from
inside-out to outside-in; faster fragments shorten the TQ. Against AUG the runs
reproduce the $I_p$ spike and the trends but underestimate the pre-TQ duration
and the radiation fraction and overestimate the early toroidal
[radiation peaking](/definitions/radiation-asymmetry.md); the authors blame an
exaggerated first-stage loss, which they attribute to overestimated parallel heat
conduction. The size effect at low neon is opposite to experiment; the rocket
effect and [plasmoid drift](/definitions/plasmoid-drift.md) are named as
candidate causes.

# Key claims

Symbols (registry §11): $f_{\mathrm{rad}}=W_{\mathrm{rad}}/(W_{\mathrm{th}}(0)+W_{\mathrm{ohm}})$
radiation fraction, $W_{\mathrm{rad}}$, $W_{\mathrm{ohm}}$ cumulative radiated and
Ohmic energy; assimilation ratio = ablated fraction of the injected inventory
(words).

1. **Two-stage TQ** (Abstract; §3.2, Figs. 5a, 6, 10). An abrupt drop of ≈50% of
   $W_{\mathrm{th}}$ at fragment arrival (≈0.45 ms), "mainly due to the convective
   and conductive transport caused by the magnetic field line stochastization at
   the edge", largely independent of neon fraction; then a stage in which
   "radiation plays a dominant role". Shown.
2. **Radiation fraction by stage** (§3.2, Fig. 6). At $t=1$ ms the windowed
   radiation fraction $\Delta W_{\mathrm{rad}}/(\Delta W_{\mathrm{th}}+\Delta W_{\mathrm{ohm}})$
   is ≈0%, 6%, 27% for 0.12%, 1%, 10% Ne; in the second stage 80%, 93%, 97%.
   Shown.
3. **Pure deuterium** (§3.2, Figs. 5a, 12; §3.5, Fig. 25). With 0% Ne and no
   background impurity the plasma enters "a prolonged non-disruptive phase", as in
   many non-disruptive AUG shots. Shown (simulation); the experimental parallel is
   Stated.
4. **First-stage MHD sequence (0% Ne)** (§3.2, Figs. 7–10). Vanguard fragments at
   $q=3$ (0.479 ms) induce 3/1 islands and an $m=3$ density structure; the
   pedestal degrades and $T$ flattens near $q=3$ (≈0.5 ms); an outward heat flux
   ablates the following fragments suddenly; at $q=2$ (≈0.57 ms) a large 2/1
   island "triggers the global reconnection event, resulting in full
   stochastisation" (Poincaré plot with $n=0,1$ only). Most first-stage loss is
   between 0.5 and 0.6 ms. Shown.
5. **Second-stage MHD** (§3.2, Figs. 11–12). Neon cases show a second
   magnetic-energy peak, aligned with the second radiation peak: "a 1/1 mode
   activity, which leads to the core collapse"; core $T_e$ drops below 10 eV.
   Higher neon gives an earlier, stronger response; at low neon the collapse comes
   after the fragments have left. Shown.
6. **Double radiation peak** (§3.2, Fig. 5e). At 1% and 10% Ne the first peak is
   from the edge near the injection (initial ablation), the second from the core
   (1/1 mode at collapse). Shown.
7. **Assimilation self-regulation** (§3.2, Fig. 5c–d). 10% Ne gives the largest
   assimilated neon but the lowest ratio; lower neon cools more gradually, so
   ablation lasts longer ("consistent with the INDEX results", Patel et al. 2025).
   At 0.12% Ne the ablated fraction saturates at ≈20% when the fragments exit
   (≈3.5 ms), not at collapse; 10% and 1% stop at ≈2 and ≈3 ms, at the end of the
   TQ. Shown. Ratios read from Fig. 5d: ≈0.22, 0.13, 0.05 for 0.12%, 1%, 10%.
8. **Total atoms reverse the trend** (§3.5, Fig. 28). Total (Ne + D) assimilated
   atoms rise as neon falls, from ≈1.5×10²¹ at 10% to ≈6–7.5×10²¹ at 0–0.12%
   (figure reading), since D dominates the inventory. Shown.
9. **Fragment size at 0.12% Ne** (§3.3, Figs. 13, 15). More, smaller fragments
   give a shorter TQ (in agreement with the JOREK–INDEX comparison of Matsuyama et
   al. 2022), higher assimilation and earlier, stronger core collapse; end of TQ
   ≈2.9, 3.4, 5.4 ms for 1105, 199, 53 fragments (figure reading). Shown.
10. **Size effect opposite to experiment at low neon** (§3.3, §3.5, §4). "Our
    numerical results deviate from the experimental findings reported by Jachmich
    …, where larger fragments with low neon fraction exhibit better material
    assimilation." Rocket effect (not modelled) and plasmoid drift (under-resolved)
    are "promising candidates". Deviation Shown; explanation Stated.
11. **Fragment size at 10% Ne** (§3.3, Fig. 14; §4). Assimilation "not
    significantly different", ordered 199 > 1105 > 53, because large fragments
    ablate longer and ablation stops at the TQ before they reach the high-field
    side; consistent with experiment and attributed to suppressed plasmoid drift at
    high neon. Shown; attribution Stated.
12. **Cooling pattern and cold front** (§§3.2–3.4, Figs. 12, 16, 17, 19). With 53
    fragments the cooling is inside-out: a ≈100 eV edge layer survives while a cold
    region moves inward with the fragments. At 10% Ne, more and smaller fragments,
    or slower ones, turn it outside-in and remove the edge belt, because the plume
    keeps material at the edge. Shown.
13. **Penetration speed** (§3.4, Fig. 18; §3.5, Fig. 27). Faster fragments raise
    early assimilation, steepen the initial drop and shorten the TQ (they reach hot
    plasma sooner); the final assimilation differs very little. Shown.
14. **$I_p$ spike against AUG #40673** (§3.4, Fig. 20). The slow case LF_HV_Ne10
    gives "a comparable $I_p$ spike with experiment", more pronounced as the cold
    front forms; the simulated $I_p$ dip "occurs significantly earlier" (≈0.7 ms,
    figure reading), attributed to the "excessively sharp thermal energy drop" and
    "unrealistically short pre-TQ duration". Shown.
15. **Radiation fraction at $I_p=0.8I_p(0)$** (§3.5, Fig. 21). ≈40% (0.12% Ne,
    size-independent), ≈45% (1%), 56–74% (10%); faster and smaller fragments
    radiate more. Shown.
16. **Radiation fraction against AUG** (§3.5, Fig. 22). The simulated
    $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ is "lower than that observed in experiments" (simulation
    ≈0.40–0.75, AUG ≈0.45–0.82, figure reading); only 10% Ne with 1105 fragments
    is comparable. Shown.
17. **Toroidal peaking of radiation** (§3.5, Fig. 23). The first radiation peak
    has $\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ up to ≈2.4–2.6, from the outward heat flux and
    sharp ablation, "which is overestimated in simulations and not observed in
    experiments", stronger for smaller fragments; the TQ peak has small
    $\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}$, as measured. Opacity is offered as a reason.
    Shown; reason Stated.
18. **Sector distribution** (§3.5, Fig. 24). AUG radiation (#40671) is more
    toroidally uniform across the five foil-bolometer sectors (except S9) than the
    simulation; attributed to the missing equilibrium toroidal flow (and opacity,
    §4). Shown; cause Stated.
19. **Pre-TQ duration underestimated** (§3.5, Figs. 25–26). Pre-TQ+TQ is "3–5 ms"
    at 0.12% Ne, ≈1 ms at 10%, "effectively infinite" for pure D; AUG pre-TQ
    durations converge to 3–5 ms at ≥1% Ne and range from a few to tens of ms for
    pure D. "JOREK simulations … underestimate the pre-TQ duration, especially with
    a low neon fraction." Shown.
20. **Root cause** (§4). The excessive first-stage loss is "likely due to the
    overestimation of the parallel thermal conduction": stronger parallel cooling
    raises $\eta$ and boosts resistive MHD, and in stochastic regions it gives
    unrealistically high losses. Stated (not tested).
21. **One realization per plume** (§2.2). Different realizations "do not
    significantly alter the big picture" except for trace injections (DREAM,
    Halldestam et al. 2025). Attributed.
22. **Equilibrium drift** (§3.1). Without injection the equilibrium changes "only
    slightly" over 1.2 ms. Stated (no figure).
23. **Toroidal resolution** (§3.1). Eight harmonics ($n=0$–7) after "rigorous
    convergence tests"; no data shown. Stated.

# Models and numerics

**Model** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–7, rendered pp. 2–3 checked). JOREK two-temperature
reduced MHD in JOREK normalized units (no $\mu_0$ printed), with
$[f,g]=\mathbf e_\phi\cdot\nabla f\times\nabla g$, and, in registry symbols,

$$\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\psi&=\eta\Delta^*\psi-R[u,\psi]-F_0\,\partial_\phi u,\\
R\nabla\cdot\big[R^2(\rho\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}\partial_tu+\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u\,\partial_t\rho)\big]&=\tfrac12[R^2|\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u|^2,R^2\rho]+[R^4\rho\varpi,u]-R\nabla\cdot[R^2\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u\,\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)]\\
&\quad+[\psi,j]-\frac{F_0}{R}\partial_\phi j+[p,R^2]+R\nu\nabla^2_{\mathrm{pol}}\varpi,\\
B^2\partial_t(\rho\hat v_\parallel)&=-\tfrac12\rho\frac{F_0}{R^2}\partial_\phi(\hat v_\parallel B)^2-\frac{\rho}{2R}[B^2\hat v_\parallel^2,\psi]-\frac{F_0}{R^2}\partial_\phi p+\frac1R[\psi,p]\\
&\quad-B^2\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\hat v_\parallel+B^2\nu_\parallel\nabla^2_{\mathrm{pol}}\hat v_\parallel,
\end{aligned}$$

plus mass and impurity continuity with isotropic diffusivities and ablation
sources ($\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot[D\nabla(\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{imp}})]+\nabla\cdot(D_{\mathrm{imp}}\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{imp}})+S_{\rho,\mathrm D}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}}$),
and separate $p_i$, $p_e$ equations with advection, compression ($\Gamma$),
anisotropic conduction $\nabla\cdot(\kappa_\perp\nabla_\perp T_s+\kappa_{\parallel}\nabla_\parallel T_s)$,
kinetic-energy and parallel-viscous heating (ions), Ohmic heating
$(\Gamma-1)\eta j^2/R^2$, $-(\Gamma-1)(P_{\mathrm{rad}}+P_{\mathrm{ion}})$
(electrons) and collisional exchange. $j$, $\varpi$, $F_0$, $\Delta^*$, the
velocity ansatz and the total $p$ are not defined in print. Eq. 1 is on the
diffusive side of the open sign contradiction on
[reduced MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md); the viscous vorticity term carries the
factor $R$ (not $R^3$), the side of [Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md) and
[Hu et al. (2021b)](/sources/hu-2021-b.md) on the second open item
there.

**Resistivity** (Eqs. 8–9). $\eta=\eta_0T_e^{-3/2}\,Z_{\mathrm{eff}}(1+1.198Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.222Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2)/(1+2.966Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.753Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2)$,
$Z_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum n_iZ_i^2/\sum n_iZ_i$; the temperature dependence is cut at
1 eV and 1.35 keV (floor $2\times10^{-8}$ Ω m).

**Transport** (§3.1). $\nu=\nu_\parallel=4.85\times10^{-7}$ kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹ (dynamic,
constant; kinematic 1.6 m²/s on axis); $\chi_\perp=1$ m²/s; Spitzer–Härm
$\chi_\parallel=3.6\times10^{29}T_e[\mathrm{keV}]^{5/2}/n_e[\mathrm m^{-3}]$ m²/s
with "a floor value at 50 eV … to help deal with low temperature regions such as
temperature holes" (cf. [electron temperature hole](/results/electron-temperature-hole.md));
$D=D_{\mathrm{imp}}=1.6$ m²/s, transiently raised 4–6× after the TQ in the 10% Ne
runs "to overcome numerical difficulties". No background impurity, no initial
perturbation, no neutral D fluid, no D line radiation.

**Impurities** (§2.1). Collisional-radiative with OpenADAS coefficients; markers
created from the impurity source, convected with $\mathbf v$, carrying the
charge-state distribution (method of its ref. [33],
[Hu et al. (2021b)](/sources/hu-2021-b.md)); replaces the coronal equilibrium of
the earlier JOREK SPI work, its ref. [32], [Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md).

**Fragmentation and ablation** (§2.2, Eqs. 11–12). Spherical fragments; radii by
rejection sampling of Parks' model until the pellet volume is reached; speeds
Gaussian with mean $(v_{\mathrm{para}}+v_{\mathrm{pellet}})/2$ and standard
deviation 20% of the mean; directions uniform in a 20° cone; launch at the
shatter-tube midpoint; constant velocity, no rocket effect. Base pellet 443 m/s,
10 mm long, "radius of 8 mm" (see Recorded as printed). Ablation (improved NGS,
no magnetic reduction factor):
$G[\mathrm{g/s}]=[27.08+\tan(1.49X)]\,(T_e[\mathrm{eV}]/2000)^{5/3}(r_{\mathrm f}[\mathrm{cm}]/0.2)^{4/3}(n_e[10^{14}\mathrm{cm^{-3}}])^{1/3}$,
$X=N_{\mathrm D_2}/(N_{\mathrm D_2}+N_{\mathrm{Ne}})$. Deposition Gaussian in the
poloidal plane (width 0.03 m, about the grid spacing) and in $\phi$ (0.8 rad, set
by the toroidal resolution).

**Discretization** (§3.1, Fig. 4). Flux-aligned Bézier grid with X-point, 70
radial × 110 poloidal elements; $n=0$–7; ideal wall ("aimed for late TQ to early
CQ"). $\Delta t\approx6.1\times10^{-8}$ s $=0.1$ JOREK time units, raised to 1–2
units in the CQ (compiler's check: $\sqrt{\mu_0\rho_0}$ with
$\rho_0=3.0\times10^{-7}$ kg/m³ gives $6.2\times10^{-7}$ s). Time integrator and
solver not described (cited). HAWK (HLRS); no cost given. The MF_FV_Ne10 run
"could not proceed beyond the onset of the core collapse due to numerical
issues".

**Synthetic diagnostics.** Five-sector bolometer peaking factor
$\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}=P_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\max}/\sum_i[(P_{\mathrm{rad},i}+P_{\mathrm{rad},i+1})/2\cdot\Delta\phi_{i,i+1}/360°]$
(Fig. 23 caption); $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ as defined above.
