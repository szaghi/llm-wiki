# Summary

The paper adds a non-equilibrium, collisional-radiative treatment of
impurity charge states to [JOREK](/software/jorek.md), replacing the
[coronal-equilibrium](/methods/coronal-equilibrium-impurity-model.md)
(CE) assumption previously used for massive material injection (MMI)
simulations ([Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md), Nucl. Fusion). Impurities are represented by
equal-weight super-particles that are not pushed by an equation of motion
but advected with the local fluid velocity; each carries a full
charge-state density array evolved by a rate equation with open-ADAS
ionization and recombination coefficients at the local $(n_e,T_e)$. Five
moments are projected onto the finite-element fields: ionization power,
radiated power, $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (feeding a Spitzer-like resistivity),
mean charge (for quasi-neutral $n_e$) and impurity density. The model is
tested axisymmetrically on the DIII-D shot 137611 equilibrium of the Lyons
et al. (2019) M3D-C1/NIMROD benchmark, with central argon or neon gas
injection of about $4.37\times10^{23}$ atoms s⁻¹. Against CE, the
non-equilibrium model shows stronger early cooling by weakly ionized
states and an earlier balance of radiative and Ohmic power, with
comparable peak cooling powers. A two-temperature model cools the
electrons faster than a single-temperature one, markedly so for neon. The
benchmark with NIMROD (and, by the stated mutual agreement, M3D-C1) shows
general agreement in integrated cooling and Ohmic power and in the 2D
evolution of $T_e$ and $J_\phi$; the residual differences (argon peak
about 0.15 ms later; stronger neon recombination below 100 eV) are
attributed to the atomic data (ADAS vs KPRAD-fitted ADPAK), supported by an
appendix rerun with older ADAS argon line-radiation data. See the
[collisional-radiative impurity model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md).

# Key claims

1. **CE fails early in massive injection** (§1). Injection and transport
   time scales are comparable to ionization-equilibration times, and CE
   "tends to underestimate the line radiation contribution from the weakly
   ionized states". Stated (citing Fig. 2 of
   [Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md), a 0D test in which CE and
   non-equilibrium radiation agree once $T_e\lesssim100$ eV).
2. **Charge-state arrays per particle** (§2.2). Giving each super-particle
   a charge-state density array instead of a single charge yields a
   smoother ionization–recombination representation and allows MMI with a
   moderate particle count: $10^7$ super-particles for $10^{21}$–$10^{22}$
   atoms, weights $10^{14}$–$10^{15}$. Stated; no convergence study.
3. **Particle time step** (§2.1). $\Delta t<10^{-8}$ s suffices, after
   van Vugt (2019). Stated.
4. **Cost** (§2.2). Particle evolution adds from negligible to "tens of
   seconds per fluid time-step" late in a run; an axisymmetric fluid step
   takes about 30 s on 4 nodes × 32 FeiTeng FT-2000+ processors (Tianhe-3
   prototype). Stated.
5. **No double counting of recombination energy** (§2.3, Eq. 5). The
   ionization energy is returned to the electron thermal energy on
   recombination, because ADAS recombination radiation already contains
   it. Stated (modelling choice).
6. **Non-equilibrium vs CE, single temperature** (§4, Fig. 2; argon,
   Spitzer-like $\eta$). CE starts with larger ionization cooling (instant
   jump to the equilibrium distribution), but its combined cooling rises
   more slowly and later; the non-equilibrium run reaches
   radiation–Ohmic balance at $t\approx0.7$ ms, CE "only does so very
   late"; peak cooling is comparable. Shown.
7. **Two temperatures** (§4, Fig. 3). The same deviation, CE better
   behaved, balance slightly later for CE; peaks comparable. Shown;
   attributed ("probably") to faster electron cooling.
8. **Mean-charge lag** (§4, Fig. 4). Low at 0.26 ms (weakly ionized
   population), close to CE at 0.45 ms, higher than CE (slow
   recombination) and less hollow at 0.78 ms. Shown.
9. **Fluid vs particle density** (§4, Fig. 5). The fluid impurity density
   and the particle-projected density agree for most of the evolution; the
   deviation at termination (1.10 ms) is attributed to the missing
   diffusion in the pusher. Shown.
10. **CE-particle check** (§4, Fig. 6). Imposing CE on the particles
    reproduces the CE fluid run very well apart from a late delay,
    validating the particle representation against the fluid one. Shown.
11. **Single vs two temperature** (§5, Figs. 7–9). Two-temperature runs
    cool electrons faster, with an earlier turning point and comparable
    peaks; the delay is much more pronounced for neon. After
    $t\approx0.6$ ms the electron thermal energy exceeds the ion one
    because $n_e>n_i$. Shown.
12. **Energy budget at balance** (§5, Fig. 9). When cooling and Ohmic
    power balance, the thermal energy is mostly gone and Ohmic conversion
    of magnetic energy balances radiation. Shown.
13. **Benchmark, constant $\eta=10^{-5}$ Ω m** (§6.1, Figs. 10–12). The
    electron-number increase agrees early, with discrepancies near the
    100→10 eV turning points; argon cooling power has the same shape and a
    similar peak, JOREK's "delayed by 0.15ms", thermal energy depleted at
    the same time; neon agrees until 1.2 ms, then JOREK has a higher
    radiation peak and a rapid drop while NIMROD plateaus. Shown.
14. **Benchmark, Spitzer-like $\eta$** (§6.1, Figs. 13–14, 17). Better
    electron-number agreement, but JOREK neon still recombines strongly
    below 100 eV at 1.2 ms; argon cooling peak slightly later, Ohmic
    plateau at the same level, same final burst at the same time (NIMROD's
    Ohmic stronger); neon termination at about 1.7 ms in both codes.
    Shown.
15. **2D evolution** (§6.2, Figs. 15–16). Inside-out collapse, hollow
    $T_e$, thin current and temperature halo, final current
    re-concentration near a new axis and hot spot, as in Figs. 7–8 of
    Lyons et al. (2019); JOREK's current halo is "not as steep". Shown
    (visual). The final filament survives only because of axisymmetry; in
    3D it would end by a kink. Stated.
16. **Atomic data explain the differences** (App. A, Figs. A1–A2).
    Rerunning argon with the ADAS line-radiation data labelled "1942"
    instead of the 1989 data gives much better NIMROD agreement (Shown);
    for neon, ADAS recombination for charge states 4+–8+ exceeds the
    KPRAD-fitted ADPAK by up to an order of magnitude at $n_e=10^{21}$
    m⁻³, consistent with faster JOREK recombination (Shown as a data
    comparison, not a rerun).
17. **Production readiness** (§7). "The JOREK non-equilibrium impurity
    model is now ready for production"; the agreement "highlights the
    robustness of the radiative cooling dynamics". Stated.

# Models and numerics

* **Base model.** JOREK reduced MHD, single- and two-temperature variants
  of the CE model of [Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md), with particle-moment sources;
  axisymmetric nonlinear runs ($n=0$) only. JOREK normalized units with
  $\mu_0$ absent; parameters quoted in SI.
  $$\mathbf B=F_0\nabla\phi+\nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi,\qquad \mathbf v=v_\parallel\mathbf b-R^2\nabla u\times\nabla\phi,$$
  with $v_\parallel$ here the true parallel speed (the paper's coefficient
  $\hat v_\parallel$ times $B$).
* **Fluid equations** (Eqs. 8–17), with
  $[f,g]=R(\nabla f\times\nabla g)\cdot\nabla\phi=\mathbf e_\phi\cdot(\nabla f\times\nabla g)$:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \partial_t\psi&=\frac{\eta(T_e)}{\mu_0}\Delta^*\psi-R[u,\psi]-F_0\partial_\phi u,\qquad j=\Delta^*\psi,\quad J_\phi=-j/(\mu_0R),\\
  \partial_t\rho&=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot(D\nabla\rho)+S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}},\\
  \partial_t\rho_{\mathrm{imp}}&=-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{\mathrm{imp}}\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot(D\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{imp}})+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}},\\
  \partial_tp&=-\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p-\Gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\frac{\Gamma-1}{\mu_0^2R^2}\eta j^2+\nabla\cdot(\kappa_\perp\nabla_\perp T+\kappa_\parallel\nabla_\parallel T)\\
  &\quad+(\Gamma-1)\,(\text{parallel viscous heating})-(\Gamma-1)\big(P_{\mathrm{rad}}+\text{ionization power density}\big)+\tfrac{\Gamma-1}{2}|\mathbf v|^2(S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}}),
  \end{aligned}$$
  plus vorticity ($\varpi$ as defined in Eq. 13) and parallel-momentum
  equations; the two-temperature variant (Eqs. 16–17) splits $p$ into
  $p_i=(n_{\mathrm{bg}}+n_{\mathrm{imp}})T_i$ and
  $p_e=n_eT_e=(n_{\mathrm{bg}}+\langle z\rangle\,n_{\mathrm{imp}})T_e$ ($\langle z\rangle$ the
  projected mean charge) with collisional ion–electron exchange that
  depends on the projected $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$; Ohmic heating and radiation
  go to the electrons.
* **Super-particles** (§2.1–2.2). Advected with the local fluid velocity
  (all species share one velocity because of strong friction and charge
  exchange); no diffusion in the pusher. Several particle steps per fluid
  step; new neutral particles born each fluid step from the impurity
  source by rejection sampling, equal weights.
* **Charge-state rate equation** (Eq. 4, intended form):
  $$\frac{dn_{\mathrm{imp},z}}{dt}=n_e\Big[\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{ion},z-1}n_{\mathrm{imp},z-1}-\big(\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{ion},z}+\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{rec},z}\big)n_{\mathrm{imp},z}+\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{rec},z+1}n_{\mathrm{imp},z+1}\Big],$$
  with open-ADAS effective coefficients of $T_e$ (argon 1989, neon 1996
  data sets). The printed form differs (Recorded as printed).
* **Projected moments** (§2.3). Ionization energy per particle step
  (Eq. 5): $n_e\Delta t$ times the sum over charge states of (ionization
  rate minus recombination rate of the next state) times the ionization
  energy $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion},z}$; radiation (Eq. 6):
  $P_{\mathrm{rad}}=n_e\sum_zn_{\mathrm{imp},z}(L_{\mathrm{rad,line}}+L_{\mathrm{rad,rec}}+L_{\mathrm{rad,brems}})_z$;
  $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ summed over all species and charge states; mean
  charge; impurity density. Projection method of van Vugt (2019); see
  [particle-to-mesh projection](/methods/particle-to-mesh-projection.md).
* **Resistivity** (Eq. 7, after Hirshman):
  $$\eta=Z_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{Sp},0}}{\max(T_e,1\,\mathrm{eV})^{3/2}}\,\frac{1+1.198Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.222Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}{1+2.966Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.753Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2},\qquad \eta_{\mathrm{Sp},0}=1.83339\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\,\mathrm m\ (\mathrm{eV}^{3/2}),$$
  2.444 times the physical value, as in Lyons et al. (2019).
* **Source shape** (Eq. 18). Gaussian in the poloidal plane around
  $(R,Z)=(1.77037, 0.01447)$ m, width parameter 0.356, toroidally
  uniform; total about $4.37\times10^{23}$ s⁻¹; lacks the $R_0/R$ factor of
  Lyons et al.
* **Transport** (§3). $D=10$ m² s⁻¹; perpendicular heat diffusivity
  100 m² s⁻¹, parallel $10^8$ m² s⁻¹ (same for electrons and ions);
  viscosity (momentum diffusivity) 100 m² s⁻¹ initially, with the $T$
  dependence of $\eta$ (constant magnetic Prandtl number; Lyons et al.
  used constant momentum diffusivity). Initial pure D,
  $n_e=n_i=10^{20}$ m⁻³ uniform.
* **Discretization and solver.** Not described (inherits JOREK; see
  [Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md)); no mesh, fluid time
  step or solver details.

# Tests and results

* **Case.** DIII-D 137611 at 1950 ms (equilibrium of Lyons et al. 2019),
  axisymmetric, central Ar or Ne gas injection; the
  [DIII-D gas-injection impurity benchmark](/benchmarks/diii-d-mgi-impurity-benchmark.md).
* **§4 CE vs particles** (argon, Spitzer-like): power traces to about
  4000 MW over about 1.2 ms (Figs. 2–3); balance at about 0.7 ms (single
  temperature); mean-charge maps at 0.26, 0.45, 0.78 ms (Fig. 4); density
  consistency at 0.45, 0.78, 1.10 ms (Fig. 5); CE-particle vs CE-fluid in
  very good agreement (Fig. 6).
* **§5 single vs two temperature** (Figs. 7–9): Ar and Ne (about 2 ms);
  thermal energies up to about 0.5–0.6 MJ; the first cooling peak
  coincides with the plasma falling below 100 eV, the rise of Ohmic power
  and the peak of ion–electron transfer; then a plateau and a final spike
  before termination.
* **§6 vs NIMROD** (data digitized from Lyons et al.; only NIMROD shown
  because M3D-C1 and NIMROD "follow each other rather well"): constant
  $\eta$ (Figs. 10–12) and Spitzer-like (Figs. 13–17); electron-number
  increase up to about $4\times10^{21}$; Ar peak delayed 0.15 ms
  (constant $\eta$); Ne higher JOREK peak and rapid drop after 1.2–1.3 ms;
  Ne termination about 1.7 ms in both; 2D snapshots at 0.408, 0.585,
  0.890, 1.106 ms.
* **App. A**: Fig. A1, argon with the "1942" line-radiation data, much
  better agreement, slight deviation after 0.4 ms (newer recombination
  and continuum data kept); Fig. A2, Ne ADAS vs KPRAD-fitted ADPAK line
  radiation, ionization and recombination at $n_e=10^{21}$ m⁻³.
* No quantitative error metrics are reported; all comparisons are visual.
