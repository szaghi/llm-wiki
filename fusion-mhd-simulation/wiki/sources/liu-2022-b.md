# Summary

The paper makes all ions kinetic in [M3D-C1-K](/software/m3d-c1-k.md): thermal
and fast (beam) deuterium are both $\delta f$ guiding-centre PIC markers, the
electrons a fluid; the MHD advances the fields, the electron pressure (or
temperature) and the perpendicular velocity. With kinetic thermal ions the MHD
continuity and parallel-momentum equations become redundant with the particle
moments; because M3D-C1 solves the fluid and kinetic equations one after the
other within a step, the two estimates of $\delta n$ and $\delta v_\parallel$
drift apart, quasineutrality breaks and parasitic modes appear. The scheme
therefore **synchronizes**: $\delta n$ and $\delta v_\parallel$ are taken from
the particle deposition, and a parallel electric field
$-\nabla_\parallel p_e/(n_ee)$ (massless electrons) enters the ion equations.
The model is close to MEGA's (Sato & Todo 2019, 2020) but uses
[pressure coupling](/methods/pressure-and-current-coupling.md) instead of
current coupling. It is verified on 1D ion-acoustic waves (IAW), whose
frequency and damping follow Chen's (2013) Landau-damping fits for
$T_i/T_e=0.1$–0.3, with echoes at large $T_i/T_e$. Linear $n=1$ scans in
$q_{\min}$ for a DIII-D hybrid equilibrium (#125476, Bateman scaling) and an
NSTX equilibrium (#134020, current scan at fixed $\beta$, smoothed
anisotropic NUBEAM fast ions) show, with energetic particles only, a
transition from a [fishbone](/definitions/fishbone.md)-like branch to a
higher-frequency BAE-like branch. Kinetic thermal ions raise the fishbone
frequency, lower the growth rates and stabilize the BAE-like branch by
Landau damping ($\omega/k_\parallel$ comparable to the ion thermal speed);
without them, kinetic-MHD simulations of non-resonant fishbones can give
"false positive results". A nonlinear NSTX run ($q_{\min}=1.12$, 8 planes,
$1.6\times10^7$ markers, Perlmutter A100 GPUs) saturates at about 0.5 ms with
$\delta B/B\approx1.5\times10^{-2}$, forms (1,1) and (2,1) islands and lowers
the core fast-ion density by only about 5%.

# Key claims

Locations are sections of arXiv v1.

1. **Redundancy problem** (§1). Kinetic thermal ions under classical
   pressure or current coupling make the MHD density and momentum equations
   redundant; "the error between the two approaches can lead to numerical
   issues or even parasitic modes", and the parallel electric force between
   kinetic ions and fluid electrons must be supplied. Stated.
2. **Failure of the Park et al. (1999) scheme in M3D-C1** (§2, after
   Eq. 2.23). With thermal-ion pressure coupling and $n$, $v_\parallel$ from
   the MHD, "the difference between $\delta n$ and $\delta v_\parallel$ from
   the fluid and kinetic equations can increase with time, which violates the
   quasi neutrality condition and lead to parasitic modes that overwhelm the
   numerical result", attributed to the equations being "solved subsequently
   in one timestep". Stated (no figure of the failure).
3. **In-principle equivalence** (§2). "In principle, the pressure coupling
   scheme in Park et al. (1999) is equivalent to our coupling scheme"; the MHD
   parallel momentum equation follows from moments of the kinetic equations
   "if assuming small ion energy and ignoring the gradient and curvature
   drifts". Stated (derivation not shown).
4. **Synchronization** (§2, Eqs. 2.17–2.20; §7). The MHD density and parallel
   velocity are replaced by particle moments; the perpendicular momentum
   equation is kept and read as a decomposition of the perpendicular current
   ($\rho\,\partial_t\mathbf v_\perp$ the polarization current, the pressure
   terms the drift and magnetization currents). Stated.
5. **Coupling equivalence for $\mathbf v_\perp$** (§2). Pressure coupling
   (M3D-C1-K) and current coupling (MEGA) "are equivalent for calculating
   $v_\perp$". Attributed ([Liu et al. (2022)](/sources/liu-2022.md)).
6. **Parallel electron-pressure field** (§2, Eq. 2.11; §7). Ions feel
   $\mathbf E-\nabla_\parallel p_e/(n_ee)$, from dropping electron parallel
   inertia, "essential for the IAW simulation"; it is not in Ohm's law, and
   the $d_i/L$ two-fluid corrections are dropped (long wavelengths). Stated.
7. **Semi-implicit MHD** (§1, §7). The semi-implicit method of Jardin et al.
   (2012) "is helpful for stabilizing numerical instabilities after including
   ion kinetic terms" and allows large time steps. Stated (no evidence).
8. **IAW Landau damping** (§3, Figs. 1–2). MHD-only IAWs oscillate "with
   little damping"; with kinetic ions the damping matches the Landau rate
   ($T_i=0.2T_e$), and $\omega$, $\gamma$ match the Chen fits for
   $T_i/T_e=0.1$–0.3; above 0.3 the damping is comparable to the frequency.
   Shown (no error numbers).
9. **Echoes** (§3, Fig. 3). At $T_i=0.3T_e$ the damped IAW shows echoes, "a
   typical nonlinear behavior for the Landau damped mode, which is different
   from dissipative damping" (Kadomtsev 1968). Shown; interpretation Stated.
10. **DIII-D, MHD only** (§4, Fig. 5). The $n=1$ kink is unstable for
    $q_{\min}<1.06$. Shown.
11. **DIII-D, fast ions only** (§4, Fig. 5). Growth falls for
    $q_{\min}<1.04$ and the mode acquires a real frequency rising with
    $q_{\min}$; for $q_{\min}>1.1$ a weakly unstable, much higher-frequency
    mode remains: fishbone-like to BAE-like transition. Shown.
12. **Comparison with NIMROD** (§4). Frequencies and growth rates "are close
    to the NIMROD results in Brennan et al. (2012), except that the growth
    rate for $q_{\min}>1.1$ keeps dropping to zero as $q_{\min}$ increases,
    whereas in Brennan et al. (2012) the growth rate is almost a constant for
    large $q_{\min}$". M3D-C1-K side Shown; NIMROD side Stated (no NIMROD
    curve). A code disagreement on the BAE-like branch, no cause offered.
13. **DIII-D with kinetic thermal ions** (§4, Fig. 5). Fishbone frequencies
    rise, growth falls, the $n=1$ mode "is stable for $q_{\min}>1.06$" and
    the BAE-like branch is stabilized. Shown.
14. **Landau-damping estimate** (§4, Eq. 4.2). With
    $k_\parallel\approx(n-m/q_{\min})/R$, $m=1.5$:
    $\omega/k_\parallel=0.20\,v_{th,i}$ at $q_{\min}=1.04$ and
    $1.15\,v_{th,i}$ at 1.15 ($v_{th,i}=\sqrt{T_i/m_i}$), so
    both branches are Landau-damped; "it is necessary to include the thermal
    ion kinetic effects and Landau damping in those case to avoid false
    positive results". Derived (order of magnitude).
15. **Quasineutrality check** (§4, Figs. 7, 9). $\delta p_e$ and $\delta p_i$
    have similar structure; the synchronized $\delta v_\parallel$ is close to
    that of the fast-ion-only run with the MHD parallel equation, both
    dominated by $m=2$. Shown (qualitative).
16. **Non-adiabatic fast-ion response** $\delta p_{\perp h}-\delta p_{\parallel h}$
    sits on the low-field side, from resonant trapped particles (§4, Fig. 8).
    Shown; interpretation attributed (Fu 2006, Kim 2008, Liu 2022).
17. **$\beta$ scan at $q_{\min}=1.04$** (§4, Fig. 10). The MHD-only
    "non-resonant kink" growth depends strongly on $\beta$, "similar to
    pressure-driven modes"; with kinetic ions the dependence weakens and the
    frequency is almost $\beta$-independent. Shown.
18. **DIII-D experiment** (§4). Simulated 6.13 kHz at $q_{\min}=1.04$ against
    18 kHz (lab frame) with rotation ≈21 kHz, called "consistent with the
    measured frequency in the plasma frame"; the EFIT equilibrium
    ($q_{\min}=1.06$) is marginal, possibly through nonlinear current
    flattening. Stated (see Citations).
19. **NSTX** (§5, Fig. 13). The $q_{\min}$ threshold for $n=1$ is higher than
    in DIII-D "thanks to the larger β"; with fast ions only the mode becomes
    BAE-like above $q_{\min}\approx1.2$. Shown.
20. **NSTX with thermal ions** (§5, Figs. 12–13). Fishbone-like frequencies
    "increase significantly" and growth falls "because of Landau damping";
    the frequency rise is attributed to the isotropic thermal ions supplying
    a large trapped population that the co-passing-dominated NUBEAM fast ions
    lack; for $q_{\min}>1.22$ the BAE-like mode is stabilized. Shown;
    attribution Stated.
21. **NSTX experiment** (§5). $m=1$ is marginally unstable with thermal ions
    for $1.1<q_{\min}<1.2$; the observed plasma-frame frequency (<5 kHz) is
    close to the simulated one at $q_{\min}=1.12$; nonlinear down-chirping
    can lower it. Stated.
22. **Nonlinear NSTX fishbone** (§6, Figs. 15–16a). $n=1$ saturates at
    $t\approx0.5$ ms with peak $\delta B/B\approx1.5\times10^{-2}$; $n=0$ is
    excited by coupling and keeps growing; the axis shifts; (1,1) and (2,1)
    islands form, "consistent with the experimental observation". Shown.
23. **Weak fast-ion loss** (§6, Fig. 16b). The core fast-ion density drops by
    only ≈5%; the gradient survives because the Landau-damped mode "requires a
    finite radial gradient to excite". Shown; interpretation Stated.
24. **Call to revisit** (§7). Non-resonant fishbone simulations by kinetic-MHD
    codes without kinetic thermal ions (Brennan 2012; Wang F. 2013; Shen 2017,
    2020) should be revisited. Stated.

# Models and numerics

**Fluid part** (Eqs. 2.1–2.6), in registry notation with thermal-ion and
fast-ion pressure tensors $\mathsf P_s=p_{\parallel s}\mathbf b\mathbf b+p_{\perp s}(\mathsf I-\mathbf b\mathbf b)$,
$s=i,h$ (registry §§3, 10: $p_{\parallel h}$, $p_{\perp h}$ and $p_{\parallel i}$,
$p_{\perp i}$, $\mathsf P_i$):

$$\begin{aligned}
\rho\big[\partial_t\mathbf v_\perp+(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla\mathbf v_\perp\big] &= \mathbf J\times\mathbf B-\nabla_\perp p_e-\nabla_\perp\cdot\mathsf P_i-\nabla_\perp\cdot\mathsf P_h+\nu\nabla^2\mathbf v_\perp,\\
\partial_t\mathbf B=-\nabla\times\mathbf E,\qquad \mathbf E &= -\mathbf v_\perp\times\mathbf B+\eta\mathbf J,\qquad \mu_0\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B,\\
\partial_tT_e+(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla T_e &= -(\Gamma_e-1)T_e\nabla\cdot(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)+\nabla\cdot(\mathsf K\cdot\nabla T_e),
\end{aligned}$$

or the equivalent $p_e$ equation (2.5), with
$\mathsf K=\kappa_\parallel\mathbf b\mathbf b+\kappa_\perp(\mathsf I-\mathbf b\mathbf b)$.
Ohm's law has no Hall or $\nabla_\parallel p_e$ term; the ion parallel
velocity is used in the electron equations ("electrons and ions are moving
together").

**Particles** (Eqs. 2.7–2.11). Guiding-centre equations
$\dot{\mathbf X}=[v_{\parallel p}\mathbf B^*-\mathbf b\times(\mathbf E-\mu\nabla B/q_s)]/B^*_\parallel$,
$m_s\dot v_{\parallel p}=\mathbf B^*\cdot(q_s\mathbf E-\mu\nabla B)/B^*_\parallel$,
$\mathbf B^*=\mathbf B+(m_sv_{\parallel p}/q_s)\nabla\times\mathbf b$; the
weights use the effective field $\mathbf E^*=\mathbf E-\nabla_\parallel p_e/(n_ee)$
(Eqs. 2.14, 2.16; whether the push does is ambiguous, see Citations).
$\delta f$ weights (Eqs. 2.12–2.16) are evolved with $f_0$ written in guiding-centre
position, energy $\mathcal E$ and pitch $\xi=v_{\parallel p}/v$, with factor 1
(linear) or $1-w_p$ (nonlinear); alternatively the perturbed orbit terms are
obtained as differences of orbits in perturbed and equilibrium fields
([δf method](/methods/delta-f-method.md)).

**Synchronization** (Eqs. 2.17–2.22). For each ion species
$\delta n_s=\sum_p(w_p+\delta B_\parallel/B^*_\parallel)\,\mathcal S(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_p)$
with a shape function $\mathcal S$; $\delta\rho=m_i\delta n_i+m_h\delta n_h$,
$\delta n_e=Z_i\delta n_i+Z_h\delta n_h$; $\delta v_\parallel$ is the
charge-weighted sum of marker parallel velocities divided by $n_e$, minus the
equilibrium flows (Eq. 2.20); the parallel and perpendicular pressures are the
corresponding moments, the perpendicular one with an extra
$\delta B_\parallel/B$ term (Eq. 2.22). With FLR, field evaluation and
deposition are gyroaveraged.

**IAW test** (§3). 1D along $\mathbf k$, only the electron pressure equation
with $\mathbf v_\perp$ ignored, isothermal electrons ($\Gamma_e=1$),
$Z_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$, sinusoidal initial $v_\parallel$. References (Chen 2013):
$\omega=\omega_0\sqrt{1+3T_i/T_e}$ and
$\gamma/\omega=-1.1\,(T_i/T_e)^{7/4}\exp[-(T_i/T_e)^2]$ (damping), with
$\omega_0=k\sqrt{T_e/m_i}$, time unit $1/\omega_0$.

**Discretization and time integration.** M3D-C1 unstructured triangular
$C^1$ elements in $(R,Z)$; linear runs one toroidal harmonic (spectral),
nonlinear run 8 toroidal planes joined by Hermite elements. Semi-implicit MHD
(Jardin et al. 2012); fluid and kinetic equations solved sequentially in each
step; $\Delta t$ not given. DIII-D linear: 5495 triangles, $8\times10^6$
markers (half thermal, half fast). NSTX linear: 7199 elements (marker count not
stated). NSTX nonlinear: same mesh × 8 planes, $1.6\times10^7$ markers.
Boundary conditions not stated.

**Hardware.** Perlmutter (NERSC), 8 nodes of 64 AMD cores and 4 NVIDIA A100
GPUs: GPUs do the MHD matrix-element calculation and the particle push; CPUs
solve the matrix (PETSc) and compute the moments. Linear runs also on
Princeton's Traverse. No wall-clock costs.

**Equilibria.** DIII-D #125476 at 3425 ms (EFIT with MSE), $q_{\min}=1.06$,
weak core reversed shear, no rotation; $q_{\min}$ scanned 1.0–1.2 by Bateman
scaling (a constant added to $F^2$ at fixed $p$ and toroidal current);
thermal D Maxwellian, $T_i=T_e$, $n_i=n_e-n_h$; fast D isotropic slowing-down
$f_0\propto n_h(\psi)/(\mathcal E^{3/2}+\mathcal E_c^{3/2})$, $\mathcal E_c=10$
keV, cutoff 50 keV, fast-ion pressure fraction 16%; a $\beta$ scan at
$q_{\min}=1.04$ by re-reconstruction. NSTX #134020 at 700 ms: $q$ from
experiment, profiles from TRANSP, Grad–Shafranov re-solved on the mesh, all
ions D; core $n_e=1.04\times10^{20}$ m⁻³, $T_e\approx T_i\approx0.74$ keV,
$\beta_h/\beta=17.3\%$ and $\beta=50.8\%$ on axis (DIII-D 12.4%); Spitzer
$\eta$ from local $T_e$; fast-ion $f_0$ from NUBEAM in (radius, pitch, energy)
smoothed by a Gaussian operator so that its gradients exist; $q_{\min}$ scanned
by the plasma current at fixed toroidal field (β almost fixed).
