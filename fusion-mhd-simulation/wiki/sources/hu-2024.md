# Summary

The paper runs 3D nonlinear [reduced-MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md)
[JOREK](/software/jorek.md) simulations, with the
[collisional-radiative impurity model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md)
and two temperatures, of neon-doped dual
[shattered pellet injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md)
(SPI) from the ITER equatorial ports EQ-08 and EQ-17 into a 370 MJ
baseline H-mode and a 190 MJ "degraded" H-mode (an artificial H–L
back-transition by raised $\chi_\perp$ and $D_\perp$). A "full pellet"
carries 1% neon ($2.5\times10^{22}$ Ne + $1.8\times10^{24}$ H per injector,
300 fragments), a "quarter pellet" 5% neon (same neon, $4.5\times10^{23}$
H, 100 fragments); injector delays are 0 or 1 ms. In the baseline H-mode
with full pellets the vanguard fragments reaching the pedestal create a
strong local over-pressure that, through the $[p,R^2]$ ($\nabla B$) term of
the vorticity equation, polarizes the plasmoid, which drifts outward in
$\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ along the major radius. The drift expels density
beyond the LCFS, stochastizes the edge, collapses the pedestal and loses
more than 100 MJ before the fragments penetrate, with a radiated fraction
of 49.4% and peak conductive–convective wall fluxes of about $3\times10^{11}$
W m⁻². A higher neon fraction and the degraded H-mode suppress the drift
(radiated fraction 86–90%). The radiation heat flux on the real ITER first
wall is obtained by ray-tracing the JOREK emissivity (Raysect/CHERAB in
IMAS), and a time-integrated energy impact by a half-space heat-equation
convolution. Radiation peaks near the injecting port early and relaxes
later. In the baseline case the stainless-steel port armour (limit 13 MJ
s⁻¹ᐟ² m⁻²) melts while tungsten (38) does not; with quarter pellets in the
degraded H-mode the maximum is 14.9 for a 1 ms delay and 5.7 for perfect
synchronization.

# Key claims

1. **Outward plasmoid drift** (§3, Fig. 3, $t=0.41$–0.46 ms). In the
   baseline H-mode with low-neon full pellets, vanguard fragments at the
   pedestal create a local over-pressure that polarizes the plasmoid and
   drives a fast $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ drift toward the low-field
   side. Shown; mechanism attributed to Rozhansky et al. (1995) and read
   as acting through the $[p,R^2]$ term of Eq. 5 (Stated, qualitative).
2. **Material expulsion** (§3, Fig. 4). The $n=0$ density peak moves from
   near the LCFS into the SOL; no expulsion in DH-QP-dt0. Shown.
3. **Edge stochastization and pedestal collapse** (§3, Fig. 5). Pedestal
   field lines get short connection lengths; the pedestal has collapsed by
   0.46 ms. Shown.
4. **Wall heat flux** (§3, Fig. 6). A helical structure at EQ-08 with a
   peak $q_w\approx3\times10^{11}$ W m⁻² at 0.42 ms (colour bar); "the
   perpendicular heat flux dominates over the parallel one". Shown.
5. **Premature loss** (§3, Fig. 7a). "Over 100 MJ of thermal energy is
   prematurely lost before the fragments penetrate deep into the core", of
   370 MJ. Shown.
6. **Drift suppression** (§3, Fig. 7, Table 2). Higher neon fraction
   (local radiation lowers the over-pressure; attributed to Matsuyama et
   al. 2022) and the smaller thermal reservoir of the degraded H-mode
   mitigate the drift and raise the radiated fraction: 49.4% (BH-FP-dt0)
   against 89.8% (DH-QP-dt0). Shown; radiative mechanism Stated.
7. **Degraded H-mode with full pellets still drifts** (§3, Figs. 8–9). A
   weak drift at 0.74 ms, then a strong one at 1.17 ms near EQ-17 driven by
   heat leaving the X-point of the 1/1 island during core relaxation, with
   wall flux comparable to BH-FP-dt0; DH-QP-dt0 has "negligible plasmoid
   drift". Shown.
8. **Radiation–ablation–MHD loop** (§4, Fig. 10). Early radiation,
   ablation and MHD peaks correlate: local cooling and the pressure peak
   drive a helical current perturbation that destabilizes resonant modes,
   and the outgoing heat flux raises ablation. Shown (correlation);
   mechanism Stated.
9. **Wall radiation load relaxes** (§4, Fig. 12). Concentrated around the
   injecting port early, nearly uniform late; at 5.74 ms the peak wall
   radiation flux is an order of magnitude below the early phase despite
   high total $P_{\mathrm{rad}}$. Shown.
10. **Hot spot away from the fragments** (§4, Fig. 12b), from the
    interplay of impurity density and outgoing heat flux "reported
    previously" ([Hu et al. (2021)](/sources/hu-2021.md)). Shown; agreement.
11. **Energy impacts** (§4, Figs. 13–15, Table 2). Maximum 25.4 (BH-FP-dt0),
    5.7 (DH-QP-dt0) and 14.9 (DH-QP-dt1) MJ s⁻¹ᐟ² m⁻² against 13 for
    stainless steel and 38 for tungsten: the EQ-08 armour melts in
    BH-FP-dt0 already from the 0.43 ms radiation pulse; DH-QP-dt1
    approaches or slightly exceeds the steel limit; DH-QP-dt0 stays well
    below. Shown.
12. **Synchronization** (§4, §5). Good synchronization reduces the
    radiation asymmetry and the maximum impact (5.7 against 14.9). Shown.
13. **Armour geometry** (§4). The armour plate receives less flux than the
    neighbouring tiles "due to a geometric effect". Shown/Stated.
14. **Transient conductive load** (§5). Even "with expose time on the
    order of 1 µs" the conductive–convective energy impact of BH-FP-dt0
    would exceed the tungsten limit. Stated; the printed magnitude is
    suspect (Recorded as printed).
15. **Melt acceptability** (§5). 22 MJ s⁻¹ᐟ² m⁻² pulses roughen stainless
    steel by 1–2 µm per pulse without material loss (Klimov et al. 2013);
    the baseline maximum approaches the beryllium limit of 28, so tungsten
    tiles give more margin than beryllium. Stated.
16. **Caveats** (§5). The toroidally elongated source (0.3 rad) spreads
    impurities, so a realistic source would localize radiation more;
    neglected opacity overestimates peak radiation; the elongation's effect
    on the drift is undetermined. Stated.
17. **Viscosity** (§5). Per McClenaghan et al. (2023, NIMROD JET SPI),
    lowering viscosity from 2000 to 500 m² s⁻¹ shortens the TQ only from 2.9
    to 2.6 ms. Stated (attributed, cross-code).
18. **No neoclassical resistivity** (§2.1). Electrons do not complete a
    bounce before colliding after injection; the mean free path of
    few-hundred-eV electrons is comparable to the plasmoid size (after Hu
    et al. 2023). Stated.

# Models and numerics

* **Model.** JOREK reduced MHD, two temperatures, CR impurities; equations
  "the same as that described in Ref. [9] without modifications" (Hu et
  al. 2023), not repeated. Only the vorticity equation is printed (Eq. 5,
  JOREK units, $\mu_0=1$), with
  $\mathbf v=v_\parallel\mathbf b-R^2\nabla u\times\nabla\phi$ ($v_\parallel$
  the true parallel speed) and $\varpi$ the toroidal vorticity:
  $$R\nabla\cdot\Big[R^2\partial_t(\rho\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u)\Big]=\tfrac12[R^2|\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u|^2,R^2\rho]+[R^4\rho\varpi,u]-R\nabla\cdot\big[R^2\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}u\,\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\big]+[\psi,j]-\frac{F_0}{R}\partial_\phi j+[p,R^2]+R^3\nu_\perp(T_e)\nabla^2_{\mathrm{pol}}\varpi,$$
  the bracket undefined here, taken as
  $[f,g]=\mathbf e_\phi\cdot(\nabla f\times\nabla g)$ per Hu et al. (2023).
  $[p,R^2]$ is the $\nabla B$ drive of plasmoid polarization.
* **Transport.** Braginskii $\kappa_\parallel$ per species, capped at the
  electron value for $T_e\simeq1.6$ keV (mimicking flux limiting);
  $D_\perp=6$, $\chi_\perp=10$ m² s⁻¹ ("approach the gyro-Bohm scaling"),
  $\nu_\perp=30$, $\nu_\parallel=150$ m² s⁻¹ (the minimum for numerical
  stability); Spitzer resistivity with the $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ correction,
  no neoclassical correction; optically thin.
* **Source** (Eq. 1). Gaussian per fragment, poloidal width 0.08 m,
  toroidal width 0.3 rad (2.46 m at the edge to 1.86 m in the core),
  artificial and dictated by the toroidal resolution; amplitude from the
  neutral-gas-shielding ablation law of Hu et al. (2023).
* **SPI.** EQ-08 and EQ-17 ($R=8.4$ m, $Z=0.685$ m, toroidally opposite),
  radial injection, 20° cone; statistical fragmentation with
  characteristic size 1.43 mm (full) and 1.30 mm (quarter pellet); 500 m/s
  ±40%. In BH-FP-dt0 only the EQ-08 plume has a speed spread (all EQ-17
  fragments arrive at 0.54 ms), an asymmetry at zero delay; the EQ-08
  plume reaches the LCFS between 0.38 and 0.89 ms.
* **Discretization.** JOREK [Bézier elements](/methods/bezier-finite-elements.md)
  and Fourier harmonics with implicit stepping (by citation); 12
  harmonics, $n=0$–11; X-point grid bounded at the thin-wall equivalent of
  the blanket modules, ideally conducting; wall heat flux evaluated on a
  surface 10 cm inside the boundary as a first-wall proxy.
* **Radiation–wall coupling.** The JOREK radiation power density is
  ray-traced onto the ITER first-wall tiles and stainless-steel port
  armour with Raysect/CHERAB in IMAS; time slices coarser than the JOREK
  output, linearly interpolated.
* **Energy impact** (§2.2, Eqs. 2–4). For a semi-infinite slab under a
  constant surface flux $q_w$,
  $\Delta T_{\mathrm s}=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi\,e_{\mathrm m}}q_w\sqrt{\Delta t}$
  with effusivity $e_{\mathrm m}=\sqrt{k_{\mathrm m}\rho_{\mathrm m}c_{\mathrm m}}$
  of the wall material; for a time-varying flux, the half-line heat kernel
  at the surface gives
  $$\Delta Q(t)=\frac12\int_{t_0}^{t}\frac{q_w(t')}{\sqrt{t-t'}}\,dt',$$
  which recovers $q_w\sqrt{\Delta t}$ for constant flux
  (compiler's check). $\Delta T_{\mathrm s}$ and $e_{\mathrm m}$ are page-local.
* **Costs.** Not given; Tianhe-3 and the ITER cluster.

* Assimilated neon fractions (compiler's arithmetic): about 20%, 51%, 43%
  of $5\times10^{22}$; hydrogen 18% (of $3.6\times10^{24}$), 49% and 40%
  (of $9\times10^{23}$).
* Fig. 7 (read): BH-FP-dt0 loses about 110 MJ by 0.6 ms (step from 0.45
  ms) and 340 MJ by 3.2 ms, against about 170 MJ radiated; DH-QP-dt0
  loses about 170 MJ and radiates about 150 MJ by 6 ms.
* Fig. 6: peak conductive–convective flux about $3\times10^{11}$ W m⁻² at
  0.42 ms and $7\times10^{10}$ W m⁻² at 0.43 ms.
* Fig. 8: DH cases show two MHD peaks ($n=1$–6), fragment arrival with
  edge stochastization, then core relaxation.
* Energy-impact snapshots: DH-QP-dt1 (Fig. 13) approaches 13 at the armour
  at 0.56 and 0.94 ms, uniform at 5.74 ms; DH-QP-dt0 (Fig. 14) symmetric,
  well below 13; BH-FP-dt0 (Fig. 15) exceeds 13 at EQ-08 at 0.43 ms and
  at EQ-17 at 2.00 ms, below 38.
* Limits quoted: stainless steel 13, beryllium 28, tungsten 38 MJ s⁻¹ᐟ²
  m⁻².
* No resolution scan, no code or experimental benchmark.
* Relation to the bundle: the SPI page records, after
  [Hoelzl et al. (2024)](/sources/hoelzl-2024.md), that plasmoid drifts
  are essential for assimilation in JET deuterium SPI and that ITER
  neon–deuterium shards reach the core before the edge collapse; here, for
  low-field-side injection into the ITER baseline H-mode, the drift
  expels material and the pedestal collapses before penetration. The
  conditions differ (machine, species, target); the paper does not cite
  Hoelzl et al. (2024).
