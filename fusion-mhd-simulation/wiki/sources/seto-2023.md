# Summary

[BOUT++](/software/bout-plus-plus.md) (v3.1) stores its fields in
[field-aligned coordinates](/methods/field-aligned-coordinates.md) and takes
derivatives in "quasi-ballooning" coordinates (shifted radial derivative and
shifted metric). Its vorticity-to-potential solver, a Helmholtz equation, is
made one-dimensional in radius by the flute-ordering approximation
(derivative along the field-aligned coordinate set to zero). That
approximation is wrong for $n=0$ and degrades at low $n$, which in practice
forced BOUT++ runs to drop the $n=0$ component and to use $1/N$ toroidal
wedges with $N\sim5$. The paper adds a
[hybrid 2D/1D field solver](/methods/hybrid-2d-1d-field-solver.md): for each
toroidal harmonic $0\le n\le n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ the Helmholtz equation is solved in
the orthogonal flux-surface plane $(\psi,\theta)$ with fourth-order central
differences on a 9-point star stencil, by PETSc GMRES with a Hypre AMG
preconditioner; higher harmonics use the flute-ordered tridiagonal solver;
the two are combined in Fourier space. The perpendicular Laplacian and the
coefficient-gradient term in the evolution equations are split the same way.
On linear [ideal ballooning modes](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md) of the
cbm18_dens8 equilibrium ([cbm18 benchmark](/benchmarks/cbm18-ballooning-benchmark.md))
the 2D and 1D solvers give $\gamma\tau_A=0.244$ and 0.246 at $n=20$ (earlier
BOUT++ 0.245, ELITE 0.239); the gap grows to about 36% at $n=5$, read as the
$O(1/n)$ flute-ordering error. In four-field two-fluid drift-wave resistive
ballooning (DWRBM) pedestal-collapse runs on cbm18_dens4, $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}=4$
suffices; full and quarter tori reach a similar energy loss but by different
collapse paths, and the full torus develops a tearing-like (2,1) global
structure among high-$n$ filaments.

# Key claims

1. **Flute ordering fails at low $n$** (§1, §3.1). It "is not valid for n = 0
   mode and may not be valid for low-n modes"; earlier BOUT++ runs therefore
   zeroed the $n=0$ flow and field and used wedges. Stated.
2. **Coordinate coincidence** (§3.1, Eqs. 7, 18). Orthogonal flux and
   field-aligned coordinates coincide only for $n=0$, so the earlier $n=0$ 2D
   solver (Dudson & Leddy 2017) in $(x,y)$ does not extend to $n\ne0$.
   Derived.
3. **New 2D operator** (§3.2, Eq. 19, App. A.2). The Helmholtz equation for
   each $n$ in $(\psi,\theta)$ without flute ordering, reducing to Eq. 18 for
   $n=0$. Derived.
4. **Resolution limit** (§3.2). The 2D grid is not field-aligned, so its
   poloidal points must resolve $m\sim nq$; this restricts the 2D solver to low
   $n$, and the parallel and poloidal point counts must be equal for the
   FFT-plus-phase-shift transform (Eqs. 10–11). Stated/derived.
5. **AMG** (§3.1). Hypre AMG "is more effective" than the SOR preconditioner
   used before. Stated, no numbers.
6. **IBM verification** (§4, Fig. 3). cbm18_dens8, $n=20$:
   $\gamma\tau_A=0.244$ (2D), 0.246 (1D), against 0.245 (BOUT++, Dudson et al.
   2009) and 0.239 (ELITE). Shown.
7. **1D–2D gap** (§4, Fig. 3). $(\gamma_{\mathrm{1D}}-\gamma_{\mathrm{2D}})/\gamma_{\mathrm{2D}}$ is about 1% at
   $n=20$ and about 36% at $n=5$, 1D higher; "qualitatively consistent" with an
   $O(1/n)$ flute-ordering correction (Connor, Hastie & Taylor 1979). Shown;
   the $O(1/n)$ link Stated.
8. **Low-$n$ overestimate** (§4, §6). The 2D solver "can suppress" the low-$n$
   overestimate of BOUT++ growth rates relative to ELITE and GATO (Dudson et al.
   2011) "to some level". Stated; no ELITE/GATO low-$n$ values are re-plotted.
9. **Eigenfunction** (Fig. 4). $n=20$ IBM from the 2D solver: even in radius
   about the pressure-gradient peak and about the outboard midplane, poloidal
   harmonics $m=33$–51, resonant $q=41/20$. Shown.
10. **DWRBM linear spectrum** (§5.1, Fig. 5b). cbm18_dens4: most unstable
    $n=32$, $\gamma\tau_A=7.86\times10^{-2}$; $n\le10$ and $n\ge53$ stable; 1D and 2D
    differ by 2–8% for $n=11$–20. Shown.
11. **$n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ scan** (§5.2, Fig. 6). 4, 8, 12 give nearly the same energy
    loss, zonal and non-zonal kinetic energy and spectra, without a spectral
    discontinuity at $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$; "$n_{2D\max}=4$ is enough". Shown.
12. **Full versus quarter torus** (§5.3, Figs. 7–8). Similar final energy loss
    and saturated kinetic energies, different paths: in the quarter torus the
    initially unstable modes trigger collapse directly at $t\approx231\,\tau_A$; in
    the full torus they saturate at $\approx210\,\tau_A$, low-$n$ and zonal modes grow,
    the spectral peak shifts down, and the collapse comes at $\approx313\,\tau_A$,
    triggered by the down-shifted modes. Shown (one case).
13. **Domain recommendation** (§5.3). A wedge "may be enough to evaluate the
    resultant energy loss level", but the full annulus "is required to more
    self-consistently simulate energy loss process". Shown for one case.
14. **Phase dynamics** (§5.3). The later full-torus collapse is "qualitative
    consistent with" the phase-dynamics criterion (Xi, Xu & Diamond 2014).
    Stated.
15. **Global (2,1) structure** (§5.3, Figs. 9–10). At $330\,\tau_A$ in the full
    torus a $(m,n)=(2,1)$ structure coexists with high-$n$ filaments, "obtained in
    BOUT++ by the hybrid scheme at the first time"; tearing-like parity (jump in
    $\partial_\psi A_\parallel$, odd $p$ about $q=2$). Whether islands form is left open.
    Shown. See [tearing mode](/definitions/tearing-mode.md).
16. **Cost** (§5.3). Full-torus run $8.3\times10^5$ core-hours (4096 cores, 8.5 days,
    JFRS-1), about 40% in the field solver. Shown.
17. **Remaining flute ordering** (§1). Only the field-solver flute ordering is
    removed; the one built into the reduced model remains. BOUT++ v4.4.2 stores
    fields in flux coordinates but still differentiates in quasi-ballooning
    coordinates, so low $n$ "is therefore still an open issue" there (footnote
    1). Stated.

# Models and numerics

**Coordinates** (§2). Orthogonal flux coordinates $(\psi,\theta,\phi)$ with scale
factors $h_\psi=1/(RB_{\mathrm p})$, $h_\theta=\sqrt{(\partial_\theta R)^2+(\partial_\theta Z)^2}$, $h_\phi=R$,
$B_{\mathrm p}>0$ assumed. Field-aligned coordinates $x=\psi-\psi_o$, $y=\theta$,
$z=\phi-\int_{\theta_o}^{\theta}(\text{local pitch }B_\phi h_\theta/(B_{\mathrm p}R))\,\mathrm d\theta$, with
$2\pi q$ the pitch integrated over a poloidal turn; twist-shift periodicity
$f(x,y+2\pi,z-2\pi q)=f(x,y,z)$ and $f(x,y,z+2\pi/N)=f$ (Eq. 9). Field-aligned
and flux-coordinate harmonics are related by an FFT in the toroidal
direction and a phase factor $e^{\pm in\times(\text{shift angle})}$ (Eqs. 10–11).
The radial derivative at fixed field-aligned coordinates contains the
integrated shear, which deforms cells secularly; the quasi-ballooning
coordinates (Eqs. 12–13) remove it from metrics and derivatives.

**Field-solver equation** (Eq. 15): a Helmholtz problem
$\big(d\,\nabla_\perp^2+g^{-1}\nabla g\cdot\nabla_\perp+a\big)f=h$ with axisymmetric coefficients
$d$, $g$, $a$ (page-local names), $\nabla_\perp^2=\nabla^2-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf b\mathbf b\cdot\nabla)$.
Coefficient–field mode coupling is excluded (Boussinesq-type linearization),
so each harmonic $n$ is independent.

* 1D flute-ordered form (Eq. 17): drop derivatives along $y$; 2nd-order
  central differences, Thomas tridiagonal algorithm (or parallel variants).
* Earlier 2D $n=0$ form (Eq. 18): in $(x,y)$, 2nd order, 5-point stencil, PETSc
  GMRES with SOR.
* New 2D form (Eq. 19): in $(\psi,\theta)$ for each $n$, with geometric coefficients
  listed in App. A; 4th-order central differences, 9-point star stencil,
  PETSc GMRES with Hypre AMG; previous step's solution as initial guess.
* Hybrid assembly (Eq. 20): the potential harmonic is the 2D inverse for
  $n\le n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ and the 1D inverse for $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}<n\le n_{\mathrm{1D,max}}$, then
  transformed back. $\nabla_\perp^2$ and the coefficient-gradient term in the
  vorticity (and $j_\parallel$ in electromagnetic models) use flux coordinates up to
  $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ and quasi-ballooning coordinates above; all other operators stay
  quasi-ballooning.

**Model 1: linear three-field peeling–ballooning reduced MHD** (Eqs. 21–27),
perturbations (subscript 1) about equilibrium (subscript 0):

$$\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\varpi_1=-B\nabla_\parallel(j_{\parallel1}/B)+B[A_{\parallel1},j_{\parallel0}/B]+\mathcal K(p_1),\qquad \partial_tA_{\parallel1}=-\nabla_\parallel\Phi_1,\qquad \partial_tp_1=-[\Phi_1,p_0],\\
&\varpi_1=\nabla\cdot\Big(\frac{n_{i0}}{B^2}\nabla_\perp\Phi_1\Big),\qquad j_{\parallel1}=\nabla_\perp^2A_{\parallel1},
\end{aligned}$$

with $[f,g]=\mathbf b\times\nabla_\perp f\cdot\nabla_\perp g/B$ and curvature operator
$\mathcal K(f)=\mathbf b\times(\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla f/B$ (page-local), in normalized units
(below). With constant $n_{i0}$ the solver equation is
$[\nabla_\perp^2+B^2\nabla(B^{-2})\cdot\nabla_\perp]\Phi_1=B^2\varpi_1$.

**Model 2: four-field two-fluid DWRBM model** (Eqs. 28–35, from Seto et al.
2020): evolves the vorticity of the generalized potential
$\Phi+(\text{ion diamagnetic factor})\,p$, the pressure, $A_\parallel$ and $v_\parallel$, with
gyroviscous cancellation (Chang & Callen 1992), electron diamagnetic terms in
Ohm's law, resistivity and hyper-resistivity $\eta_h$, parallel and perpendicular
vorticity viscosities, heat diffusivities $\chi_\parallel$, $\chi_\perp$ and a parallel-flow
viscosity. Values (normalized): $\eta=10^{-8}$, $\eta_h=10^{-12}$, perpendicular
viscosities and $\chi_\perp=10^{-7}$, parallel ones and $\chi_\parallel=10^{-1}$. The
equilibrium E×B flow cancels the ion diamagnetic flow (no neoclassical
poloidal flow). Radial boundaries: Neumann at $\psi=0.4$, Dirichlet zero at
$\psi=1.2$; the domain lies inside the last closed surface (shifted circle, no
X-point).

**Normalization.** Poloidal Alfvén units. Model 1: $\bar R=3$ m, $\bar B=2$ T,
$\bar n_i=10^{19}$ m⁻³, deuterium, $\tau_A=3.08\times10^{-7}$ s. Model 2: $\bar R=3.5$ m,
$\bar B=2$ T, $\bar n_i=10^{19}$ m⁻³ ($\tau_A$ not printed). Diffusivities in
$\bar Rv_A$, $\eta$ in $\mu_0v_A\bar R$, $\eta_h$ in $\mu_0v_A\bar R^3$.

**Diagnostics.** Pedestal energy loss $-\int_{V_{\mathrm{ped}}}p_1\,\mathrm dV/\int_{V_{\mathrm{ped}}}p_0\,\mathrm dV$
over $0.40\le\psi\le0.85$ (Eq. 36); per-harmonic perpendicular kinetic energy
$W_{\mathrm{kin}}^{(n)}$ (Eq. 37); collapse time at the maximum of the logarithmic
rate of the energy loss.

**Time integration.** BDF through CVODE (SUNDIALS)
([implicit time integration](/methods/implicit-time-integration.md)).

**Resolution and cost.** IBM: 516 radial points (with guard cells) on
$0.6\le\psi\le1.2$, 32 binormal points on $[0,2\pi/N)$, 64 (1D) or 512 (2D)
parallel/poloidal points. $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ scan: $516\times256\times256$,
$n_{\mathrm{1D,max}}=80$, radial spacing about 1.2 mm against a hyper-resistive
current-sheet width of about 6.1 mm. Production: $N_\psi=1028$, 128 parallel
points, binormal spacing $2\pi/256$ (256 points full torus, 64 quarter torus),
$n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}=4$, $n_{\mathrm{1D,max}}=80$; MPI only, no GPU.

# Tests and results

| Test | Equilibrium | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Linear IBM, $n=20$ (Fig. 3) | cbm18_dens8 (TOQ, circular, $q\sim2$ at the gradient peak) | $\gamma\tau_A$: 2D 0.244, 1D 0.246, BOUT++ (2009) 0.245, ELITE 0.239 |
| 1D–2D gap (Fig. 3) | cbm18_dens8 | ≈1% at $n=20$, ≈36% at $n=5$ |
| DWRBM linear (Fig. 5b) | cbm18_dens4 | peak $n=32$, $\gamma\tau_A=0.0786$; unstable $11\le n\le52$; 1D–2D 2–8% for $n=11$–20 |
| $n_{\mathrm{2D,max}}$ = 4, 8, 12 (Fig. 6) | cbm18_dens4 | curves agree; energy loss saturates near 0.06–0.07 (plot reading) |
| Quarter vs full torus (Figs. 7–8) | cbm18_dens4 | collapse at ≈231 vs ≈313 $\tau_A$; saturated energy loss ≈0.065–0.07 both; final profiles at 450 $\tau_A$ nearly identical |
| Global structure (Figs. 9–10) | full torus, 330 $\tau_A$ | (2,1) tearing-like parity about $q=2$ (evaluated from the $n=0$ field) |

In the quarter torus, $n=0$ and $n=4$ are about an order of magnitude below the
$n=32$ peak at $210\,\tau_A$; in the full torus $n=2$ is the largest at $231\,\tau_A$ and
$n=0$ becomes comparable to the high-$n$ peak at the collapse. No quantitative
comparison with JOREK, NIMROD or M3D-C1. See also
[edge-localized mode](/definitions/edge-localized-mode.md).
