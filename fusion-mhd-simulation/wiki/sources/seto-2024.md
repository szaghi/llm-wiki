# Summary

With [BOUT++](/software/bout-plus-plus.md) and the
[hybrid 2D/1D field solver](/methods/hybrid-2d-1d-field-solver.md) of
[Seto et al. (2023)](/sources/seto-2023.md), the authors analyse a pedestal
collapse ([ELM](/definitions/edge-localized-mode.md) crash) driven by
resistive drift-[ballooning modes](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md) (RDBMs).
The model is a four-field reduced model (vorticity, $A_\parallel$, pressure,
ion parallel flow) on a shifted-circular TOQ equilibrium marginally unstable
to ideal ballooning, run in the full annular torus. The same run, grid,
coefficients and linear spectrum (peak $n=32$, $\gamma/\omega_A=7.86\times10^{-2}$)
identify it with the full-torus cbm18_dens4 case of Seto et al. (2023) §5,
although the equilibrium is not named here. The pedestal energy-loss rate has
two peaks. The first crash ($t=0$, when the $n=32$ kinetic energy saturates)
is triggered directly by the linear RDBMs; their nonlinear coupling drives
$n=0$ and low-$n$ magnetic energy at about twice the $n=32$ magnetic-energy
growth rate and, through nonlinear parity mixing, forms $m/n=2/1$ magnetic
islands at $q=2$, which sits at the pressure-gradient peak. Turbulence and
radial E×B heat flux stay near the island X-points, so the loss is small.
Between the crashes the spectral peak shifts down; the 4/2, 6/3 and 8/4
harmonics of the island develop, overlap and stochastize the $q=2$ region,
and turbulence spreads poloidally from their X-points. The second, main crash
($t=103\,\tau_A$) comes when the turbulence covers the whole flux surface; a
three-wave analysis attributes its trigger to the down-shifted middle-$n$
turbulence, not to the low-$n$ modes. Per-$n$ budgets of kinetic, magnetic and
internal energy with triad decompositions are derived. A wedge scan gives
crash intervals of 103, 64 and 3 $\tau_A$ for wedges of $2\pi/N$ with $N=1$,
2, 4, because the fundamental islands become $2N/N$, finer and quicker to
stochastize; the final loss stays at about 6% of the pedestal energy for every
$N$.

# Key claims

1. **Two crashes in the full torus** (§III, Fig. 3). The normalized loss rate
   of the pedestal energy peaks at $t=0$ and $t=103\,\tau_A$; cumulative loss
   about 0.005 at the first crash, 0.04 at the second and 0.063 at
   $200\,\tau_A$ (plot readings). Shown.
2. **First crash from the linear RDBMs** (§III, Figs. 4–5, 12a). At $t=0$ the
   $n=32$-peaked modes dominate the E×B drive of the $n=0$ pressure; a forward
   cascade to higher $n$ and an inverse cascade to lower $n$ follow, the latter
   shifting the spectral peak below 32. Shown.
3. **Nonlinear low-$n$ magnetic energy** (§III, Fig. 6). Before $t=0$ the
   $n=0$–4 magnetic energies grow "with the growth rate almost twice of that of
   the n = 32 magnetic energy", i.e. they are driven by RDBM pairs; after the
   first crash low $n$ saturates, while $n=0$ grows to the second crash and
   dominates afterwards. Shown.
4. **Tearing parity of $n=1$ from parity mixing** (§III, Fig. 8). The $n=1$
   magnetic energy is fed mainly by the linear electrostatic-plus-Hall
   transfer, which cannot itself mix parity; the $n=1$ pressure and potential
   are driven by the nonlinear E×B convection and Maxwell stress, which
   generate tearing-parity components (Poisson-bracket parity mixing, Sato &
   Ishizawa 2017, Ishizawa et al. 2019), and $A_\parallel$ inherits the
   [tearing](/definitions/tearing-mode.md) parity through the linear term.
   Budgets Shown, mechanism Stated.
5. **Triad channels into $n=1$** (§III, Eqs. 28–29, Fig. 9). E×B convection:
   RDBM pairs at the first crash, down-shifted middle-$n$ pairs at
   $50\,\tau_A$, a broad spectrum of both signs at the second crash. Maxwell
   stress: the pair $(1,0)$ is the dominant positive contribution at the first
   crash and at $50\,\tau_A$ (perpendicular kinetic energy of $n=1$ gained from
   the $n=0,1$ magnetic energy), strongly negative at the second crash. Shown.
6. **2/1 islands; heat flux only near X-points** (§III, Fig. 10). The $n=1$
   perturbation forms 2/1 islands at $t=0$; the radial E×B heat flux exists
   only in the stochastic layer around their X-points, whose width "seems to
   be determined by" the X-points of the 2/1 and 8/4 islands. Shown; width
   attribution hedged.
7. **Stochastization by island harmonics** (§III, Fig. 10g–k). The 4/2, 6/3
   and 8/4 harmonics overlap on $q=2$ and the heat flux leaves from their
   X-points (two 8/4 X-points at $\theta^*/2\pi\approx0.34$ and 0.84 at
   $50\,\tau_A$). Shown.
8. **Flow structure** (§III, Fig. 11). At the first crash radial E×B
   filaments are confined outside the islands, inside which the poloidal flow
   is laminar; at the second crash, with $q=2$ fully stochastic, pressure and
   radial-flow filaments spread over the whole surface. Shown; causality left
   open.
9. **Second crash from middle-$n$ turbulence** (§III, Fig. 12). At $50$ and
   $103\,\tau_A$ "a wide range of resonant modes with the down-shifted
   middle-n mode" drive the $n=0$ pressure. "The low-n modes have
   stabilization effects on the ELM crash" (§IV: the 2/1 islands). Trigger
   Shown; stabilization inferred from the delay, without a run lacking
   islands.
10. **Wedge scan** (§III, Fig. 13). Crash interval $103$, $64$ and
    $3\,\tau_A$ for $N=1$, 2, 4; for $N=4$ the RDBMs trigger the crash
    directly, "consistent with" the authors' earlier 1/5-wedge work (Seto et
    al. 2020). Shown (one run per $N$).
11. **Why the wedge matters** (§III, Figs. 14–15). For $N=2$ the 4/2 islands
    (at $t=-25\,\tau_A$) have more X-points and disappear faster by overlap;
    for $N=4$ the 8/4 islands are "already hidden in the sea of the
    stochasticity" at the first crash. Fundamental islands $2N/N$ are finer
    and stochastize sooner for larger $N$. Shown.
12. **Final ELM size insensitive to $N$** (§IV, Fig. 13a). About 0.063–0.065
    of the pedestal energy at $200\,\tau_A$ for all $N$ (plot reading). Shown.
13. **Fine-island overlap sets the large crash** (§IV). Large losses "arise
    from the intricate overlaps of fine magnetic islands, rather than the mere
    presence of low-n magnetic islands". Stated; no volume ratio computed.
14. **First-crash coupling postpones the loss** (§IV). The first-crash
    coupling converts perpendicular kinetic into magnetic energy, "effectively
    postponing the substantial loss of the internal energy". Stated
    (interpretation of Figs. 8–9).
15. **Domain size matters for the trigger** (abstract, §IV). "Nonlinear
    interactions of all channels in the full torus domain can significantly
    affect the trigger dynamics". Shown for one equilibrium and parameter set.
16. **Loss channels** (§II). With constant hyper-resistivity and a linearized
    parallel Laplacian (no magnetic flutter), stochastic electron heat
    transport (Rechester–Rosenbluth) and stochastic hyper-resistivity (Kaw et
    al.) are absent: "ELM energy loss in this work is driven by E × B
    convection and numerical diffusion". Stated (limitation).
17. **Wedge motivation** (§I). Too large a wedge number can give false
    convergence of full-$f$ gyrokinetic transport (Kim et al. 2017), and the
    number of mode couplings changes the nonlinear ELM criterion (Xi et al.
    2014). Attributed.

# Models and numerics

**Four-field model** (§II, Eqs. 1–4, checked on the rendered page; from Seto
et al. 2020, flat ion density $n_i$), in registry notation with $\delta(\cdot)$
the perturbation and unmarked fields total (equilibrium plus perturbation):

$$\begin{aligned}
\partial_t\delta\varpi &= -[\delta\Phi_{\mathrm g},\varpi]-[\Phi_{\mathrm g,0},\delta\varpi]+\mathcal G(\delta p,\Phi_{\mathrm g})+\mathcal G(p_0,\delta\Phi_{\mathrm g})+\mathcal K(\delta p)-B\nabla_\parallel\Big(\frac{\delta j_\parallel}{B}\Big)+B\Big[\delta A_\parallel,\frac{j_\parallel}{B}\Big]+\nu_\parallel\nabla_\parallel^2\delta\varpi+\nu_\perp\nabla_\perp^2\delta\varpi,\\
\partial_t\delta A_\parallel &= -[\Phi,\delta A_\parallel]-\nabla_\parallel\delta\Phi+\delta_{\mathrm d}\big(\nabla_\parallel\delta p-[\delta A_\parallel,p]\big)+\eta\,\delta j_\parallel-\eta_h\nabla_\perp^2\delta j_\parallel,\\
\partial_t\delta p &= -[\delta\Phi,p]-[\Phi_0,\delta p]-2c_\beta\mathcal K(\delta\Phi)-c_\beta B\nabla_\parallel\Big(\frac{\delta v_\parallel+d_i\delta j_\parallel}{B}\Big)+c_\beta B\Big[\delta A_\parallel,\frac{\delta v_\parallel+d_i\delta j_\parallel}{B}\Big]+\chi_\parallel\nabla_\parallel^2\delta p+\chi_\perp\nabla_\perp^2\delta p,\\
\partial_t\delta v_\parallel &= -[\Phi,\delta v_\parallel]-\tfrac12\nabla_\parallel\delta p+\tfrac12[\delta A_\parallel,p]+\nu_v\nabla_\perp^2\delta v_\parallel,
\end{aligned}$$

with page-local symbols: generalized flow potential
$\Phi_{\mathrm g}=\Phi+\delta_{\mathrm d}p$, diamagnetic factor
$\delta_{\mathrm d}=d_i/4$ ($p_e=p_i=p/2$, $d_i$ the ion skin depth),
vorticity $\varpi=\nabla\cdot(\nabla_\perp\Phi_{\mathrm g}/B^2)$,
$\delta j_\parallel=\nabla_\perp^2\delta A_\parallel$,
$\delta\mathbf B=\nabla\delta A_\parallel\times\mathbf b$,
$[f,g]=\mathbf b\times\nabla_\perp f\cdot\nabla_\perp g/B$,
$\mathcal K(f)=\mathbf b\times(\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla f/B$,
$\mathcal G$ the gyroviscous-cancellation operator (Chang–Callen form; printed
with a duplicated term), compression factor
$c_\beta=B^2/[0.5+B^2/(5p_0/3)]$, $\nu_v$ the perpendicular viscosity of the
parallel flow. $\nabla_\parallel=\mathbf b\cdot\nabla$ uses the equilibrium
$\mathbf b$ only (linearized, no magnetic flutter). $\Phi_0=-\delta_{\mathrm d}p_0$,
i.e. $\Phi_{\mathrm g,0}=0$: equilibrium E×B flow cancels the ion diamagnetic
flow, without neoclassical poloidal or return flow. Constant normalized
coefficients $\eta=10^{-8}$, $\eta_h=10^{-12}$,
$\nu_\perp=\chi_\perp=\nu_v=10^{-7}$, $\nu_\parallel=\chi_\parallel=10^{-1}$
(as in Seto et al. 2020 and 2023). Normalization: poloidal Alfvén units with
$\bar R=3.5$ m, $\bar B=2.0$ T, $\bar n_i=10^{19}$ m⁻³, deuterium, $Z=1$;
$\tau_A$ is not given in seconds.

* **Coordinates.** Orthogonal flux coordinates (poloidal-flux label 0.4–1.2,
  orthogonal poloidal angle, geometric toroidal angle) and
  [field-aligned coordinates](/methods/field-aligned-coordinates.md) $x$, $y$,
  $z$ with the shift angle integrated from $\theta=\pi$; the transform is done
  in Fourier space in $n$. Diagnostics use the straight-field-line angle
  $\theta^*$ (Eq. 30).
* **Equilibrium.** Shifted circular, no X-point, $q=2$ at the pressure-gradient
  peak (label $\approx0.85$); cf. the
  [cbm18 benchmark](/benchmarks/cbm18-ballooning-benchmark.md) (identification
  by the reader, not printed).
* **Linear spectrum** (Fig. 2): unstable for $11\le n\le52$, maximum
  $\gamma/\omega_A=7.86\times10^{-2}$ at $n=32$.
* **Resolution.** 1028 radial points on 0.4–1.2, 128 parallel points,
  $256/N$ binormal points on a $2\pi/N$ wedge (constant binormal spacing).
  2D solver for $0\le n\le4$, flute-ordered 1D solver for $4<n\le80$,
  $n\ge81$ filtered. Time integration not stated here (CVODE BDF in Seto et
  al. 2023).
* **Initial and boundary conditions.** Perturbations seeded on all $n\ne0$;
  Neumann at the inner and Dirichlet at the outer radial boundary for every
  field.
* **Computing.** JFRS-1 and SGI HPE8600 (QST/JAEA); no cost given (Seto et
  al. 2023: $8.3\times10^5$ core-h for the full-torus run).
* **Diagnostics** (Eqs. 5–34). Pedestal energy loss: minus the volume integral
  of $\delta p$ over that of $p_0$ inside the rational surface at the initial
  pressure-gradient peak. Per-$n$ perpendicular kinetic energy
  $W_{\mathrm{kin}}^{(n)}=\langle|\nabla_\perp\delta\Phi_{\mathrm g}^{(n)}|^2/2B^2\rangle_V$,
  magnetic energy $W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(n)}=\langle|\nabla_\perp\delta A_\parallel^{(n)}|^2/2\rangle_V$
  and internal energy $\langle|\delta p^{(n)}|^2/4c_\beta\rangle_V$
  ($\langle\cdot\rangle_V$ a volume average, page-local), with linear and
  nonlinear transfer terms (Reynolds stress, geodesic curvature, line bending,
  Maxwell stress, viscous loss; electrostatic-plus-Hall and resistive terms;
  E×B convection, compression, diffusion), triad transfers with
  $|n_1-n_2|=n_3$, radial and poloidal E×B speeds and the radial convective
  heat flux, and Poincaré plots.
