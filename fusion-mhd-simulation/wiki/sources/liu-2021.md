# Summary

The paper adds to [M3D-C1](/software/m3d-c1.md) a self-consistent fluid
runaway-electron (RE) model. Its RE continuity equation is not advanced with the
Galerkin $\theta$-implicit matrix used for the other fields. The convective part
is rewritten exactly as an advection equation for $n_{\mathrm{RE}}/B$, and
pseudo-particles placed at every quadrature point are integrated backwards along
$c_{\mathrm{RE}}\mathbf b+\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/B^2$; the values found at the foot
points are $L^2$-projected with the mass matrix (a semi-Lagrangian scheme). This
removes the CFL and matrix-conditioning limits that pushed earlier fluid-RE
codes to reduced convection speeds and artificial diffusion. The trajectories
use a "modified Boris algorithm", the massless limit of a Boris push with
$\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ subtraction, started by one RK4 step; it keeps the flux
error bounded where RK4 drifts secularly. On Summit the RE step costs 126 s
($\theta$-implicit, CPU), 39 s (characteristics, CPU) and 3.8 s
(characteristics, GPU). The model is applied to the final RE loss of DIII-D
shot 177040: the linear (2,1) resistive kink has growth rate and real frequency
following $\eta^{3/5}$ (as stated), the RE current gives it a real frequency,
and the mode structure agrees with MARS-F. Nonlinearly, the (2,1) mode with
nonlinearly excited $n>1$ harmonics stochastizes the outer plasma, more than
95% of the REs are lost within about 0.06 ms, the current converts to Ohmic
current with a small spike, $q_0$ drops to 1 and a (1,1) kink flattens the
residual RE core. Losses land on the lower high-field-side wall with $n=1$
structure. The loss fraction converges only with ≥16 toroidal planes, and the
saturation level depends on $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$.

# Key claims

Page-local symbol (not in the registry): $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$, the parallel
convection speed of the RE fluid, a numerical parameter $\le c$ quoted as
$c_{\mathrm{RE}}/v_A$.

1. **Coupled fluid RE model** (§2, Eqs. 1–9). The RE density enters the
   momentum equation, Ohm's law and Ohmic heating through $\mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}}$.
   Stated (model description).
2. **Ill-conditioning of the implicit RE equation** (§3, pp. 6–7). With GMRES
   and block-Jacobi in 3D and $c_{\mathrm{RE}}\gg v_A$, the toroidal derivative
   of $n_{\mathrm{RE}}$ becomes an off-diagonal block that "can dominate the
   matrix" at Alfvénic steps; subcycling, reduced $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$ and
   artificial diffusion are costly or "can possibly reduce the MHD mode growth
   or even suppress unstable modes". Stated; the diffusion claim is not
   demonstrated in the paper.
3. **Advective rewrite** (§3, Eq. 11, App. A). The convective part of the RE
   continuity equation equals an advection equation for $n_{\mathrm{RE}}/B$ with
   source $(n_{\mathrm{RE}}/B^2)\,\mathbf E\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf b)$. Derived
   (reader checked A.1–A.11). The source acts on the resistive time scale and is
   added as a separate source (Stated).
4. **No CFL limit, no artificial diffusion** (§3, p. 8). Stated; particle
   subcycling $\Delta t_p<\Delta t$ is still used for orbit accuracy.
5. **Timing** (§3, Fig. 1). 126 s / 39 s / 3.8 s for one RE step
   ($\Delta t=0.65$ µs, $c_{\mathrm{RE}}=8v_A$, 16 planes × 6454 elements,
   printed 1 290 800 pseudo-particles, $\Delta t_p=\tau_A/32$), 32 POWER9 CPUs
   against 96 V100 GPUs. Shown; the hardware is not like-for-like (CPU-to-CPU
   algorithmic gain ≈3.2).
6. **Modified Boris conserves current** (§4, Fig. 2a). In a static-field test
   at $c_{\mathrm{RE}}=32v_A$ RK4 drifts from ≈0.75 to ≈0.90 MA in 0.2 ms while
   the modified Boris stays at ≈0.745 MA, at equal cost (one Boris step = one
   quarter of an RK4 step). Shown (plot readings).
7. **Bounded flux error** (§4, Fig. 2b). For one pseudo-particle the RK4
   $\psi$ error grows secularly to ≈$3\times10^{-4}$ of the reference flux in
   0.65 µs; the Boris error oscillates within ≈$\pm1.5\times10^{-4}$. Shown;
   attributed to the structure-preserving property of Boris.
8. **Linear resistive scaling** (§5.2, Fig. 5). The (2,1) growth rate and real
   frequency both "follow the 3/5 power law" of the inverse Lundquist number;
   only with RE current does the mode have a real frequency. Shown, no fitted
   exponent printed; the reader's end-point slope of Fig. 5 is ≈0.65.
9. **DIII-D value** (§5.2). At $S^{-1}=3\times10^{-4}$ (≈DIII-D at
   $T_e\approx2$ eV), $\gamma\tau_A\approx0.053$ with REs. Shown.
10. **$q_a$ scan** (§5.2, Fig. 6). The ideal kink is unstable for $q_a\le2$, with
    growth rising as $q_a$ drops, "consistent with the theory"; resistive cases
    stay unstable above 2; with REs the real frequency peaks at $q_a=2.0$
    ($\omega\tau_A\approx0.085$) and drops sharply below. Shown.
11. **Mode structure** (§5.2, Figs. 7–8). $m=2$ dominated; $\delta n_{\mathrm{RE}}$
    and the normal displacement localize near $q=2$, "in agreement with" MARS-F.
    Shown; no MARS-F curve is overlaid.
12. **$c_{\mathrm{RE}}$ insensitivity, linear** (§5.2, Fig. 9). $\gamma$ and
    $\omega$ "not sensitive" for $c_{\mathrm{RE}}/v_A>4$. Shown, but $\gamma\tau_A$
    still falls ≈11% from 4 to 16.
13. **Prompt loss** (§5.3, Figs. 10, 12a). Islands reach the last closed flux
    surface, the outer region stochastizes by overlap, and "within 0.06ms,
    almost all the RE are lost to the wall" (>95%). Shown.
14. **Ohmic conversion and current spike** (§5.3, Fig. 12). The lost RE current
    is replaced by Ohmic current; flattening in the stochastic region lowers
    $l_i$ and gives a small spike, "also seen in JOREK simulation" and observed
    in DIII-D. Shown (spike) / Stated (experiment).
15. **Core kink** (§5.3, Fig. 12b). $q_0$ drops from ≈1.10 to 1.0, $E_\parallel$ on
    axis rises to ≈30 V/m, and a (1,1) kink flattens $n_{\mathrm{RE}}$ in the core.
    Shown.
16. **Deposition** (§5.3, Fig. 13). Lower high-field-side wall, single toroidal
    peak ($n=1$), similar to JOREK JET results. Shown.
17. **Toroidal convergence** (§5.3, Fig. 14a). The loss fraction rises with the
    number of planes, with a "significant difference between 4 planes and 16
    planes": higher-$n$ harmonics matter for stochasticity. Shown.
18. **$c_{\mathrm{RE}}$ sets nonlinear saturation** (§5.3, Fig. 14b).
    $c_{\mathrm{RE}}/v_A=2$ saturates $W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(1)}$ at ≈3000 J against
    ≈1400–1600 J for 8 and 32, "confirmed the necessity of using a large value of
    $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$"; the loss fraction barely changes. Shown.
19. **Post-loss phase** (§5.4, Figs. 15–16). With density raised tenfold by hand
    at 0.3 ms and a Rosenbluth–Putvinski avalanche source ($E/E_c\approx10$):
    $T_e\approx20$ eV, $\eta\approx15$ µΩ m, current decay time ≈3 ms
    "consistent with experimental observation"; RE current regrows but stays
    small; persistent (2,1)+(1,1) activity keeps the outer region stochastic and
    $q_0\approx1$. Shown / Stated.
20. **Discussion** (§6). $S\approx10^3$ post-disruption, so resistive and
    Alfvénic scales are close; the simulated spike is smaller than measured,
    suggested evidence of fast reconnection not captured; no Dreicer or hot-tail
    source is needed below 20 eV; linear growth weakly dependent on
    $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$ "consistent with the JOREK result". Stated.

# Models and numerics

* **Equations** (§2, Eqs. 1–9, translated; the paper writes $\mu_0=1$ in
  Ampère's law), with $\rho=n_im_i$, single temperature $T_e=T_i$:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \rho\big(\partial_t\mathbf v+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v\big)&=en_{\mathrm{RE}}\mathbf E+(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}})\times\mathbf B-\nabla p,\qquad \partial_tn_i+\nabla\cdot(n_i\mathbf v)=0,\\
  \partial_tn_{\mathrm{RE}}+\nabla\cdot\Big[n_{\mathrm{RE}}\Big(c_{\mathrm{RE}}\mathbf b+\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}\Big)\Big]&=S_{\mathrm{av}},\qquad \mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}}=-en_{\mathrm{RE}}\Big(c\,\mathbf b+\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}\Big),\\
  \mathbf E&=-\mathbf v\times\mathbf B+\eta(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}}),\qquad \partial_t\mathbf B=-\nabla\times\mathbf E,\qquad \mu_0\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B,\\
  \frac{n_i}{\Gamma-1}\Big[\partial_tT+\nabla\cdot(T\mathbf v)\Big]&=-n_iT\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\nabla\cdot\big(n_i\mathsf K\cdot\nabla T\big)+\eta(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}})^2,
  \end{aligned}$$
  with $T=T_e+T_i$, $p=n_iT$ and $\mathsf K$ the anisotropic conduction tensor
  (diffusive sign; the printed sign is anti-diffusive, see Citations). The
  current uses $c$, the convection $c_{\mathrm{RE}}$. The parallel residual
  $en_{\mathrm{RE}}E_\parallel$ of the momentum equation "is not included"; no RE
  friction in Ohm's law; no RE gradient or curvature drifts.
* **Resistivity.** Uniform inside the last closed flux surface for linear runs,
  $10^3$ times larger outside (Fig. 5) or $S^{-1}=1$ outside (Fig. 6) to mimic
  vacuum; nonlinear runs Spitzer with $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$ (Eq. 20), ≈300 µΩ m at
  the 2 eV floor. Large $\kappa_\parallel$, $\kappa_\perp$ (values not given).
* **RE source.** None in §5.2–5.3; Rosenbluth–Putvinski avalanche in §5.4. No
  Dreicer, hot-tail, tritium or Compton source.
* **Method of characteristics** (§3, Eqs. 10–14).
  $$\partial_t\Big(\frac{n_{\mathrm{RE}}}{B}\Big)+\Big(c_{\mathrm{RE}}\mathbf b+\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}\Big)\cdot\nabla\Big(\frac{n_{\mathrm{RE}}}{B}\Big)=\frac{n_{\mathrm{RE}}}{B^2}\,\mathbf E\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf b),\qquad \frac{d\mathbf x_p}{dt}=c_{\mathrm{RE}}\mathbf b+\frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}.$$
  Steps: pseudo-particles at every quadrature point (125 per 3D element); push
  backwards over $\Delta t$; pull back $n_{\mathrm{RE}}/B$ from the foot point;
  solve the mass-matrix system $\sum_i(\Lambda_k,C_i\Lambda_i)=\sum_m\Lambda_k(\mathbf x_m)(n_{\mathrm{RE}}/B)(\mathbf x_m)\mathcal J(\mathbf x_m)$
  for the coefficients; add the source. Fields frozen across subcycles. Unlike
  PIC, $n_{\mathrm{RE}}$ stays a field: no re-seeding, no particle noise. A
  characteristic leaving the mesh sets $n_{\mathrm{RE}}=0$ at its origin (absorbing
  boundary).
* **Modified Boris** (§4, Eqs. 15–19), for marker $p$ at step $l$:
  $\mathbf x^{l+1}=\mathbf x^l+\mathbf v^{l+1/2}\Delta t_p$; subtract
  $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/B^2$; rotate; add it back. In the massless limit the
  rotation becomes $(\mathbf v'^{\,l+3/2}+\mathbf v'^{\,l+1/2})\times\mathbf B^{l+1}=0$ with
  $|\mathbf v'^{\,l+3/2}|=|\mathbf v'^{\,l+1/2}|$ ("a reflection mirror"), whose
  intended root keeps the parallel and reverses the perpendicular part. Started
  by one RK4 step; inspired by the slow-manifold Boris of Xiao & Qin used in
  M3D-C1-K ([Liu et al. (2022)](/sources/liu-2022.md)); valid for
  $\Delta t_p$ above the electron gyro-period. See
  [particle pushers](/methods/particle-pushers.md).
* **Time advance and solvers** (§2). Split $\theta$-implicit advance: momentum
  (semi-implicit, parabolic term), then plasma and RE densities, then field and
  pressure. GMRES with block-Jacobi. Earlier 2D linear work solved the RE
  equation in the same Galerkin matrix with a direct solver.
* **Discretization.** High-order $C^1$ elements, unstructured in $(R,Z)$,
  Hermite cubic between toroidal planes. Linear runs: 2D mesh, $n=1$. Nonlinear:
  16 planes (4, 16, 24 for convergence), mesh refined near $q=2$; 6454 elements
  per plane in the timing test.
* **Parallelism.** Pseudo-particle push on GPUs (Summit V100); programming
  model not named. $\Delta t=0.65$ µs, $\Delta t_p=\tau_A/32$; $\tau_A$ not
  defined (implied $R_0/v_A$, with $S^{-1}=\eta R_0/(\mu_0v_Aa^2)$).

# Tests and results

* **Equilibrium** (§5.1). EFIT of DIII-D 177040 at 1025 ms: pressure ≈0,
  $\mathbf J\approx\mathbf J_{\mathrm{RE}}$, $B_\phi=2$ T on axis,
  $n_i=1.5\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ D, $v_A\approx0.026c$, $q_a\approx2.1$,
  $q_0\approx1.1$, monotonic $q$ and $n_{\mathrm{RE}}$; initial RE current
  ≈0.75 MA.
* **Linear scans** (plot readings).

  | Scan | Values |
  |---|---|
  | $S^{-1}=10^{-6}$–$3\times10^{-4}$ (Fig. 5) | $\gamma\tau_A$ ≈$1.2\times10^{-3}$–$5\times10^{-2}$ (no RE), ≈$1.3\times10^{-3}$–$5.3\times10^{-2}$ (RE) |
  | $q_a=1.8$–2.3 (Fig. 6) | ideal $\gamma\tau_A$ ≈0.093 at 1.8, 0 at 2.1; resistive 0.11→0.03; with RE 0.15→0.046 |
  | $c_{\mathrm{RE}}/v_A=2,4,8,16$ (Fig. 9) | $\gamma\tau_A$ ≈0.073, 0.058, 0.0527, 0.0515; $\omega\tau_A$ ≈0.050–0.0535 |

* **Nonlinear** (Figs. 10–14). Islands at 0.18 ms; stochastic outer region
  0.20–0.22 ms; only core REs at 0.24 ms. $W_{\mathrm{kin}}^{(1)}$ peak ≈190 J,
  $W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(1)}$ peak ≈1450 J decaying to ≈570 J at 0.5 ms;
  $W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(2,3,4)}$ ≈750/550/250 J. RE current 0.75 → ≈0.05 MA between
  0.17 and 0.24 ms. Deposition toroidal peak near $\phi\approx\pi/2$.
* **Convergence** (Fig. 14). 4 planes keep ≈0.35 MA of RE at 0.26 ms against
  ≈0.05 MA for 16 and 24 planes; 24 planes ≈0.02 ms earlier than 16.
* **Post-loss** (Figs. 15–16, to 1.5 ms). Total current 0.75 → ≈0.5 MA; RE
  current regrows to ≈0.12 MA with avalanche (≈0.03 MA without); plasma drifts
  to the high-field side without horizontal control.
* No code-to-code benchmark of the nonlinear phase; MARS-F agreement is
  qualitative.
