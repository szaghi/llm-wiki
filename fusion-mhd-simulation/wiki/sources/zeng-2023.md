# Summary

[NIMROD](/software/nimrod.md) resistive-MHD simulations coupled to the KPRAD
impurity-radiation module show that a localized neon deposition on the $q=2$
surface of an EAST-like L-mode equilibrium, which the authors call stable, can
trigger a 2/1 [tearing mode](/definitions/tearing-mode.md). Radiative cooling
digs a narrow hollow pressure layer around $q=2$; the local pressure gradient
changes sign, so the Glasser–Greene–Johnson resistive-interchange parameter
$D_R$ turns from negative (stabilizing) to positive (destabilizing). A threshold
peak impurity density of $2\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ separates decaying
perturbations from island growth to saturation. The classical thermo-resistive
onset criterion (local radiation above Ohmic heating) cannot explain the
threshold, because radiation exceeds Ohmic heating on $q=2$ in every run. The
Lütjens et al. (2001) modified Rutherford equation with a $D_R$ term reproduces
the onset for $t<0.5$ ms; afterwards $D_R$ relaxes to about zero and the growth
is fitted by an ad hoc three-constant equation, with the drive attributed mainly
to the 2/1 resistivity perturbation from cooling, opposed by a perturbed
Pfirsch–Schlüter current. A $\kappa_\parallel$ scan at threshold impurity level
shows growth for $\kappa_\parallel<10^9$ m²/s and a small saturated island
above, explained through $D_R$: fast parallel equilibration restores the
negative pressure gradient sooner. A short, mostly qualitative mechanism paper
with toroidal harmonics $n=0,1$ and constant transport coefficients.

# Key claims

Registry symbols (§10): $D_R$ the resistive-interchange
(Glasser–Greene–Johnson) parameter; $w$ the island width, $w_{\mathrm{sat}}$ its
saturation width; $L_p=p/p'$, $L_q=q/q'$ signed gradient lengths;
$\epsilon_s=r_s/R_0$; $j_{\mathrm{PS}}$ Pfirsch–Schlüter current; $r_s$ the radius
of $q=2$. Page-local: $w_d$ the small-island width of the Lütjens equation;
$\Delta'_0$ the jump in logarithmic derivative of the 2/1 radial field measured
in the simulation.

1. **Impurity threshold** (§3, Fig. 1a). Scanning the peak neon density
   ($1,2,2.5,3,4,5,10\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$), below $2\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$
   "the magnetic perturbation initially induced by the impurity decays away
   gradually and no magnetic island forms"; above it the island grows to
   saturation. Shown.
2. **Radiation above Ohmic heating is not sufficient** (§3, Fig. 1b). For every
   level the local radiation on $q=2$ "largely exceeds" $\eta J^2$ throughout, so
   the thermo-resistive criterion "may not be able to explain" the threshold.
   Shown (two diagnostics); the inference is the authors'. Note: the cited
   criterion applies within an island, here compared on $q=2$ before an island
   exists.
3. **Hollow layer and force balance** (§3, Fig. 2a–b). A narrow hollow-pressure
   layer forms at $q=2$; $j_\parallel$ dips inside with spikes at the layer edges;
   $\nabla p=\mathbf J\times\mathbf B$ "remains well satisfied" since
   $|\delta\mathbf v|\sim10$–$10^2$ m/s. Shown.
4. **Perturbed Pfirsch–Schlüter current** (§3, Fig. 2b). The $(0,0)$ current
   perturbation outside the layer matches
   $\delta j_{\mathrm{PS}}=-2\,(r/R)\,B_{\mathrm{pol}}^{-1}\,\partial_r\delta p\,\cos\theta$,
   $\delta p=p(t)-p(0)$. Shown (model against profile).
5. **$D_R$ reversal** (§3 p. 6, Fig. 3a). The hollow pressure reverses
   $D_R\approx(\epsilon_s^2\beta_p/\hat s)(L_q/L_p)(1-1/q^2)$ from negative to
   positive. Shown (diagnostic); formula Stated (GGJ cited in §1).
6. **Lütjens equation captures onset only** (§3, Fig. 3b).
   $dw/dt=(1.22/\tau_R)\big[\Delta'(1-w/w_{\mathrm{sat}})+6.35D_R/\sqrt{w^2+0.65w_d^2}\big]$
   with $\Delta'=120$, $w_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.1$ m, $w_d=0.02$ m "gives the right trend of the
   island growth onset" for $t<0.5$ ms; after that $|D_R|\approx0$ while the
   island grows again, so $D_R$ dominates only the small-island phase. Shown
   (fit).
7. **Ad hoc equation** (§3, Eq. 8, Fig. 4a).
   $\tau_R\,dw/dt=\lambda(\Delta'_0+\alpha_2D_R-\alpha_1j_{\mathrm{PS}}^{(2,1)}/j_{\mathrm{PS},0})$
   with measured terms and fitted $\lambda=35$, $\alpha_1=0.5$, $\alpha_2=10$; for
   $t>0.5$ ms growth is driven by $\Delta'_0$ against the stabilizing nonlinear
   2/1 PS current. Shown (three-parameter fit, no independent validation); the
   authors call it "crude".
8. **Helical-current decomposition** (§3, Eq. 9, Fig. 4b–c). $\Delta'_0$ tracks
   the 2/1 helical current, "schematically" decomposed (after Wesson eq. 4.4.9)
   into a PS term from $\partial_rp^{(2,1)}$, a resistivity-perturbation term and
   an inductive term. Stated (schematic, coefficients undefined).
9. **Two phases** (§3, Fig. 4c–d). The first narrow current peak (0–0.2 ms)
   coincides with a positive $\partial_rp^{(2,1)}$; the later dip and second peak
   are "mainly caused by the variation of the plasma resistivity", which leads to
   the nonlinear growth. Shown (time-trace correlation).
10. **Parallel conduction suppresses growth** (§4, Fig. 5a). At
    $2\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ the island grows for $\kappa_\parallel<10^9$ m²/s
    and saturates "at a much smaller size" above, agreeing with Xu et al. 2019.
    Shown ($10^8$, $10^9$, $10^{10}$ m²/s and a $T_e$-dependent model).
11. **Mechanism of the $\kappa_\parallel$ effect** (§4, Fig. 5b). At $10^8$ m²/s
    $D_R$ is larger and positive longer than at $10^{10}$ m²/s; strong parallel
    equilibration weakens the radial gradient of the $T_e$ perturbation so the
    pressure recovers its negative gradient sooner and $D_R$ turns stabilizing.
    Agrees with "smaller $\kappa_\parallel/\kappa_\perp$ ratio leads to more unstable
    tearing mode" (Lütjens 2001 PPCF). Shown ($D_R$ traces) / Stated
    (interpretation, with Fitzpatrick 1995).
12. **Temperature-dependent transport** (§2, §4, Fig. 5a). $\kappa_\perp\propto T^{-1/2}B^{-2}$,
    $\kappa_\parallel\propto T^{5/2}$ give the same main conclusion. Shown (one
    run).
13. **Toroidal convergence** (§2). Runs with $n=0$–6 show higher-$n$ effects
    "negligible on the main conclusion". Stated (no data).
14. **Outlook** (§1). $D_R$ reversal may seed neoclassical tearing modes at high
    $\beta$ and is analogous to destabilizing resistive interchange in
    negative-central-shear plasmas (Chu 1996). Stated.

# Models and numerics

* **Equations** (§2, Eqs. 1–6, translated; checked on rendered p. 3), single
  temperature $T_e$ for all species:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \rho\frac{d\mathbf v}{dt}&=-\nabla p+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B+\nabla\cdot(\rho\nu\nabla\mathbf v),\\
  \frac{dn_i}{dt}+n_i\nabla\cdot\mathbf v&=\nabla\cdot(D_\perp\nabla n_i)+\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ion}}-\mathcal R_{\mathrm{rec}},\\
  \frac{dn_{\mathrm{imp},z}}{dt}+n_{\mathrm{imp},z}\nabla\cdot\mathbf v&=\nabla\cdot(D_\perp\nabla n_{\mathrm{imp},z})+\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ion}}-\mathcal R_{\mathrm{rec}}\quad(z=0,\dots,10),\\
  n_e\frac{dT_e}{dt}&=(\Gamma-1)\big[-n_eT_e\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\nabla\cdot(n_e\mathsf K\cdot\nabla T_e)-P_{\mathrm{rad}}\big],\\
  \partial_t\mathbf B&=\nabla\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)-\nabla\times(\eta\mathbf J),
  \end{aligned}$$
  with $\mathsf K=\kappa_\parallel\mathbf b\mathbf b+\kappa_\perp(\mathsf I-\mathbf b\mathbf b)$
  in the diffusivity sense, isotropic particle diffusivity, Spitzer
  $\eta\propto T_e^{-3/2}$. The energy equation is given in the standard sign; the
  printed one differs (see Citations). Rates from KPRAD, updated every step.
* **Radiation.** $P_{\mathrm{rad}}$ = background, line, bremsstrahlung, 3-body
  recombination, ionization and recombination terms, line radiation
  ($n_en_{\mathrm{imp}}L_{\mathrm{rad}}$) dominant. App. 7, Eqs. 10–21 print the
  KPRAD fits: polynomial fits in $\log_{10}T_e$ for background (coronal fit,
  2–20 keV), line and ionization rates; bremsstrahlung
  $1.69\times10^{-32}\,n_e^2\sqrt{T_e}\,Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (cgs, eV); recombination
  rate $5.2\times10^{-14}n_e(z+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ion},z}/T_e}\,f_{\mathrm{rec}}$;
  units mixed as printed. Atomic data said to originate from ADAS. See the
  [collisional-radiative impurity model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md).
* **Initial impurity.**
  $n_{\mathrm{imp}}\exp[-((r-r_s)/L_1)^2]\exp(-\phi^2)(1+\cos\theta)$, $L_1=1$ cm, a
  crude pellet-deposition model; peak close to the local main-ion density,
  line-averaged level 1–10% of the background ("slightly higher than the typical
  experimental value"); no initial flow.
* **Equilibrium** (Table 1, "stable EAST L-mode like"). $a=0.45$ m, $R_0=1.85$ m,
  $I_p=0.38$ MA, $B_\phi=2.267$ T, $q_0=1.484$, $q_{95}=9.768$,
  $n_e=2.3\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$ and $T_e=2.959$ keV in the core, $T_e=3.875$ eV
  at the edge, $S=6.413\times10^9$, core $\eta=6.334\times10^{-11}$ Ω m,
  $\kappa_\perp=1$ m²/s, $\kappa_\parallel=10^{10}$ m²/s, $D_\perp=0.1$ m²/s.
* **Discretization.** 70 × 64 finite elements with third-order Lagrange basis in
  the poloidal plane; toroidal harmonics $n=0,1$ (check runs to $n=6$). Perfectly
  conducting wall at the plasma boundary, no vacuum region. Time integration,
  solvers, parallelism and cost not described (USTC Supercomputing Center).
* **Diagnostics.** $\Delta'_0$ from the 2/1 radial field across the equilibrium
  $q=2$ surface (which barely moves); local $\tau_R=\mu_0r_s^2/\eta_s$.
