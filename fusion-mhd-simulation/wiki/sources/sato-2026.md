# Summary

A review of the authors' [MIPS](/software/mips.md) (MHD) and
[MEGA](/software/mega.md) (kinetic-MHD hybrid) studies of pressure-driven
instabilities in high-$\beta$, inward-shifted LHD plasmas, with new results
that add kinetic energetic ions. The question is why LHD sustains
$\langle\beta\rangle\approx5\%$ without significant MHD activity while linear
MHD predicts strong instability. HINT equilibria with central $\beta=7.5\%$ and
9.2% have a Mercier-unstable outer region (magnetic hill) and a stochastic
layer outside the last closed flux surface. In MHD (Sato et al. 2017),
resistive [ballooning modes](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md) are most
unstable for $10^4\le S\le10^6$; they are "2D-type" (weak toroidal coupling
within the [mode families](/definitions/toroidal-mode-family.md) of
$N_{\mathrm{fp}}=10$), start in the periphery and nonlinearly produce an $n=1$
mode whose self-coupling lowers the axisymmetric pressure and collapses the
core. For $S>10^6$ a resonant $(m,n)=(3,2)$ ideal
[interchange mode](/definitions/interchange-mode.md) at $\iota=2/3$ dominates
and still collapses the core at $S=10^7$. With drift-kinetic
[$\delta f$](/methods/delta-f-method.md)
[kinetic thermal ions](/methods/kinetic-thermal-ions.md) (MEGA, Sato & Todo
2019–2021) the growth rates equal the MHD ones at $S\sim10^4$ and fall below
them as $S$ rises. The cause given is the poloidal precession of
helically trapped ions: once the precession frequency $\omega_{\mathrm d}$ relative to the mode
phase exceeds $\gamma$, trapped ions average out the perturbation, and the
perpendicular ion-pressure perturbation drops to about 5% (parallel about 15%)
of the electron one. The ideal (3,2) interchange is stabilized; at $S=10^7$
the nonlinear activity stays peripheral and the central pressure survives, in
line with experiment. New: Maxwellian 100 keV energetic ions with
$\beta_h\le0.8\%$ lower the linear $n=2$ interchange growth rate, attributed to
the reduced bulk pressure; their own pressure response is negligible because
they precess fast, and saturation and final profiles are unchanged.

# Key claims

1. **The discrepancy** (Introduction, refs. [1–10]). LHD keeps
   $\langle\beta\rangle\approx5\%$ in inward-shifted configurations without
   significant MHD activity, whereas fixed-boundary linear MHD predicts strong
   instability; free-boundary ideal MHD (stabilizing outward shift), nonlinear
   MHD, two-fluid and equilibrium-flow models have not fully reconciled them.
   Attributed.
2. **KTI-MEGA does not reduce to MHD** (p. 2). Switching off the kinetic ions
   "does not strictly reduce the system to the conventional single-fluid MHD
   equations", since density and parallel velocity are ion moments; hence
   MIPS for MHD and MEGA for kinetic runs. "The MIPS code was developed by
   extracting this pure MHD part of the original MEGA framework". Stated.
3. **No ion diamagnetic term, no FLR** (p. 2). The ion response to equilibrium
   pressure gradients enters only through the kinetic ions; FLR is available
   but off "in order to isolate the dominant stabilization mechanism", and is
   "not expected to alter the primary stabilization mechanism". Stated.
4. **$S$ against experiment** (p. 3). The experimental magnetic Reynolds
   number $3$–$8\times10^6$ of Sakakibara et al. (2008) corresponds to
   $2\times10^7\lesssim S\lesssim5\times10^7$ in this normalization; $S=10^7$
   is "of the same order of magnitude". Stated (conversion not shown).
5. **Equilibria** (p. 3, Fig. 1). HINT, no nested-surface assumption,
   $p=p_0(1-\psi_{t,N})$; the stochastic region outside the last closed
   surface grows with $\beta$; VMEC re-solved inside it agrees with HINT; core
   Mercier-stable (well from the Shafranov shift), outer region
   Mercier-unstable (hill). Shown.
6. **Mode families** (p. 4). For $N_{\mathrm{fp}}=10$, harmonic $n$ couples to
   $n\pm kN_{\mathrm{fp}}$; there are $1+\lfloor N_{\mathrm{fp}}/2\rfloor=6$
   families. Stated.
7. **2D-type ballooning** (p. 4). For $10^4\le S\le10^6$ the resistive
   ballooning mode is the most unstable, with weak toroidal coupling; no
   3D-type ballooning mode was seen, so $n$ identifies an eigenmode. Shown in
   Sato et al. (2017), stated here.
8. **MHD core collapse at $S=10^5$** (pp. 4–5, Figs. 2–3). Peripheral
   resistive ballooning modes (dominant $n=12,13$ at 7.5%, $n=11,10$ at 9.2%)
   nonlinearly generate low-$n$ modes such as $n=1$ that reach the core;
   quadratic coupling of the $n=1$ velocity and pressure produces the
   $(0,0)$ pressure drop; collapse by $t\approx147\tau_A$. Shown.
9. **High-$S$ collapse by a resonant (3,2) interchange** (p. 5, Fig. 7). At
   high $S$ ideal interchange modes replace resistive ballooning; the
   collapse at $S=10^7$ (by $3326\tau_A$) "is associated with a resonant
   $(m,n)=(3,2)$ ideal interchange mode" at $\iota=2/3$, contrasted with the
   non-resonant $m=n>1$ interchange of Jardin et al. (2020) in tokamaks. Shown.
10. **Linear KTI effect versus $S$** (pp. 6–7, Fig. 4). Equal growth rates at
    $S\sim10^4$, lower KTI rates at higher $S$; trend lines
    $\gamma\propto S^{-1/3}$ (MHD resistive ballooning) and $\propto S^{-1}$
    (KTI). Shown.
11. **Mechanism: poloidal precession of helically trapped ions** (pp. 6–7,
    Fig. 5). Ions trapped in helical ripples precess toroidally and
    poloidally (only toroidally in tokamaks); with short poloidal wavelengths
    they cross positive and negative fluctuations and "effectively average out
    the perturbations". Stated with supporting evidence (Figs. 4, 6).
12. **Selective suppression of the perpendicular ion pressure** (p. 7, Fig.
    6). For the $n=6$ ballooning mode, $\delta p_{\perp i}\approx5\%$ and
    $\delta p_{\parallel i}\approx15\%$ of $\delta p_e$ (normalized to its
    $m=8$ harmonic); trapped ions, which dominate $\delta p_{\perp i}$, couple
    weakly. Shown (from Sato & Todo 2020).
13. **Growth rate against precession** (p. 7, Fig. 4). Without diamagnetic
    effects the mode is nearly purely growing; the effect is weak while the
    precession frequency is below $\gamma$ ($S\sim10^4$) and strong once
    $\gamma$ falls below it; the ideal (3,2) interchange has $\gamma$ below the
    precession frequency and is stabilized, leaving "the resistive interchange
    mode" dominant. Shown and interpreted.
14. **Nonlinear: only MHD collapses at $S=10^7$** (pp. 7–8, Figs. 7–8). At
    $S=10^5$ both models degrade the central pressure; at $S=10^7$ the MHD
    core collapses while with KTIs the activity stays peripheral and the
    central pressure is kept, "better agreement with experimental
    observations". Shown (from Sato & Todo 2021).
15. **Saturated fluctuations** (p. 8, Fig. 9). With KTIs the $(0,0)$
    perturbation of $p_{\perp i}$ is "significantly suppressed" relative to
    $p_e$ and $p_{\parallel i}$ ($t=84811\tau_A$), and the axisymmetric
    profile stays close to the initial one, whereas the MHD profile at
    $3326\tau_A$ is flattened inside $s\approx0.6$. Shown.
16. **Core density collapse caveat** (p. 8). CDC in LHD has been tied to fast
    ideal ballooning-type modes, possibly with $\gamma$ above the precession
    frequency, where kinetic stabilization is weaker; the absence of collapse
    "does not rule out CDC-related mechanisms". Stated.
17. **New: energetic ions lower the growth rate** (p. 8, Fig. 10a). At
    $S=10^7$, central $\beta=7.5\%$, 100 keV Maxwellian ions: the $n=2$
    interchange growth rate falls from about $4.9\times10^{-4}$
    ($\beta_h=0$) to $3.6\times10^{-4}$ ($\beta_h=0.8\%$) (units unlabelled,
    presumably $\tau_A^{-1}$), attributed to the reduced bulk pressure drive,
    "as also pointed out" by Pinon et al. (2018). Shown.
18. **New: negligible energetic-ion response** (p. 8, Fig. 10b). The
    perpendicular energetic-ion pressure perturbation is negligible, "likely
    due to" precession much faster than $\gamma$. Shown; interpretation
    Stated.
19. **New: saturation unchanged** (pp. 8–9, Figs. 11–12). With
    $\beta_h=0.8\%$ the $n=2$ mode grows slower but saturates at nearly the
    same level; the weakly unstable $(1,1)$ interchange then dominates in both
    runs; the saturated axisymmetric pressure profiles do not differ
    significantly. Shown.
20. **Possible re-destabilization** (p. 9). Pinon et al. (2018) find the
    instability enhanced again beyond a threshold $\beta_h$; not explored.
    Attributed.
21. **Generality** (Summary, p. 10). The origin of the stabilization is the
    deviation of orbits from field lines (finite orbit width), which "may
    become particularly important in plasmas near the beta limit"; for
    $\beta$-limit assessment "the conventional MHD model may be
    insufficient". Stated.

# Models and numerics

No governing equations are printed; the paper refers to its predecessors for
the MIPS and MEGA equations (the same pair of models is written out in
[Sato et al. (2024)](/sources/sato-2024.md)).

* **MIPS**: single-fluid visco-resistive MHD; fourth-order central
  differences on a cylindrical $(R,\phi,Z)$ grid; explicit fourth-order
  Runge–Kutta (p. 2).
* **MEGA**: the fluid part (fields and electron pressure) with MIPS numerics;
  bulk ions as drift-kinetic $\delta f$ markers whose density, parallel flow
  and pressures enter the fluid equations
  ([hybrid kinetic-MHD](/methods/hybrid-kinetic-mhd.md)); no explicit ion
  diamagnetic drift in the momentum equation; FLR off. Energetic ions are a
  second kinetic species (Maxwellian, 100 keV) in the new runs.
* **Dimensionless numbers.** $S=\mu_0R_0^2/(\eta\tau_A)$, $\tau_A=R_0/v_A$
  (p. 3; "magnetic Reynolds number", stated equivalent to the Lundquist
  number).
* **Dissipation** (p. 3). $\nu=\kappa=10^{-7}v_AR_0$ in the earlier works;
  anisotropic $\kappa_\perp=10^{-7}v_AR_0$, $\kappa_\parallel=10^{-3}v_AR_0$ in
  Sato & Todo (2021); a realistic parallel conductivity would impose a severe
  explicit time-step limit. Dissipation and sources preserve the initial
  equilibrium.
* **Boundary conditions** (pp. 2–3). Plasma plus surrounding vacuum;
  velocity, pressure and density perturbations vanish in the vacuum region;
  $\delta\mathbf B=0$ at the domain edge; thermal-ion markers loaded in
  $R\in[2.55,4.75]$ m, $Z\in[-1.1,1.1]$ m and removed on entering the vacuum
  region.
* **Equilibrium.** HINT (Suzuki et al. 2006), $p=p_0(1-\psi_{t,N})$, central
  $\beta=7.5\%$ and 9.2%. Diagnostics in Boozer coordinates, built by
  re-solving inside the HINT boundary with [VMEC](/software/vmec.md) and
  transforming with NEWBOZ; Mercier evaluation with VMEC.
* **Initial kinetic loading.** $p_e=p_{\perp i}=p_{\parallel i}=p_{\mathrm{HINT}}/2$,
  Maxwellian ions, uniform density; with energetic ions
  $p_e=p_{\perp i}=p_{\parallel i}=(p_{\mathrm{HINT}}-p_h)/2$.
* **Resolution.** MIPS $256\times640\times256$ $(R,\phi,Z)$; MEGA
  $128\times640\times128$ with about $6.7\times10^8$ thermal-ion markers;
  full torus implied by 640 toroidal points.
* **Cost.** The nonlinear KTI run at $S=10^7$ used 2048 SX-Aurora vector
  engines for about 164.4 h; new runs on the NIFS Plasma Simulator and Fugaku.
  No GPU. No convergence study in this paper.
