# Summary

The paper presents [SCELT](/software/scelt.md), a linear eigenvalue code
for single-fluid, compressible, resistive ("full", non-reduced) MHD in
axisymmetric toroidal geometry. Its distinguishing feature is the
construction method, not the physics model. Two C++ modules are built: a
symbolic vector-analysis module (linked-list polynomial classes and
covariant/contravariant grad, div, curl, dot and cross written directly in
magnetic flux coordinates) that linearizes the vector MHD equations and
expands them into scalar form (up to about 500 terms per equation), and an
automatic discretization module that turns each generated term into matrix
coefficients by a poloidal Fourier expansion and radial cubic finite
elements (Galerkin). The resulting generalized complex eigenproblem for
three velocity components, the pressure perturbation and two field
components is solved with the dense MATLAB `eig`, and spurious modes are
removed by an eigenvalue window and an oscillation filter. The modules are
demonstrated on a 2D Poisson problem and on the inverse
Grad–Shafranov problem (Solov'ev
equilibrium), both converging at about fourth order. SCELT is verified on
circular equilibria with $R_0/a=5$: the resistive $m=n=1$
[internal kink](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md) scales as
$\eta^{0.3766}$ (theory $\eta^{1/3}$), the ideal internal kink has a
poloidal-beta threshold of about 0.375 (theory 0.3, Bussac et al.), and the
$m/n=2/1$ [tearing mode](/definitions/tearing-mode.md) scales as
$\eta^{0.5590}$ (theory $\eta^{3/5}$). The eigen-solution strategy works
only for unstable modes (stable global Alfvén eigenmodes were not
obtained), and no comparison with another eigenvalue code is shown.

# Key claims

1. **First symbolic-computation-built fusion code** (§1, §5). SCELT is,
   "as far as we know", the first code built "by directly using the
   symbolic computation technique" in plasma physics. Stated.
2. **Term count** (§3, §3.1.3). In flux coordinates the non-orthogonal
   metric gives "up to 500 terms" in the expanded full MHD equations.
   Attributed (Qin et al. 1999).
3. **Automatic linearization** (§3.1.1, §4). The symbolic module linearizes
   the nonlinear equations by flagging first-order quantities. Stated (the
   generated equations are not listed).
4. **Generic term** (§3.1.3, Eq. 9). Every generated term is a coefficient
   function of $(s,\theta)$ times $\partial_s^a\partial_\phi^b\partial_\theta^c$
   of an unknown, with $a+c<3$ for this model, so only the value, the two
   first derivatives and the three second derivatives in $(s,\theta)$ are
   needed. Stated.
5. **Axis regularity** (§3.1.4, App. B, Eqs. B.1–B.6). A proper axis
   condition "may have a significant effect on the numerical stability";
   the regularity conditions $\hat v_s^{(m)}=-im\,\hat v_\theta^{(m)}$ (and
   the same for $\mathbf B$) are derived. Derived (stability effect
   Stated).
6. **Poisson convergence** (§3.2, Fig. 4). The L1 error converges at order
   3.98, consistent with the radial discretization. Shown.
7. **Grad–Shafranov convergence** (§3.2, Fig. 5). The L1 error of the
   inverse Solov'ev solution converges at order 3.81. Shown.
8. **Resistive internal kink** (§4.1, Fig. 8). $m=n=1$ at axis pressure
   $5\times10^{-5}$: $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.3766}$ against $\eta^{1/3}$ (Ara
   et al. 1978), "match[ing] well" with theory and earlier simulations.
   Shown.
9. **Ideal-kink threshold** (§4.1, Fig. 10). Poloidal-beta threshold
   ≈0.375 against "the theoretical prediction of 0.3" (Bussac et al.
   1975), called "consistent". Shown, but Fig. 10 shows $\gamma$ against
   the axis pressure only, with no $\beta_p$ axis and no zero crossing.
10. **Tearing mode** (§4.2, Figs. 11–12). $m/n=2/1$: $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.5590}$
    against $\eta^{3/5}$ (Furth et al. 1973), "good agreement"; the
    velocity perturbation is localized at $q=2$. Shown.
11. **Solver limitation** (§3.1.5). The dense `eig` plus filtering "is not
    the best one in terms of computational efficiency or rate of success.
    It is only suitable for solving unstable problems"; stable global
    Alfvén eigenmodes have not been obtained. Stated.
12. **Local refinement** (§3.1.3, §4). The radial mesh can be refined
    locally anywhere, as needed for kink and tearing eigenfunctions.
    Stated.

# Models and numerics

**Model** (Eqs. 1–7, written here in SI; the paper uses Alfvén units with
$\mu_0=1$):

$$\begin{aligned}
&\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0,\qquad
\rho\,\partial_t\mathbf v+\rho\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v=-\nabla p+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B+\nu\nabla^2\mathbf v,\\
&\partial_t p=-\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p-\Gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+(\Gamma-1)\eta|\mathbf J|^2,\qquad
\partial_t\mathbf B=-\nabla\times\mathbf E,\\
&\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B=\eta\mathbf J,\qquad \mu_0\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B,\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,
\end{aligned}$$

with $\Gamma=5/3$. Normalization: density and field by their axis values,
pressure by $B^2/\mu_0$, velocity by $v_A$, time by $\tau_A=a/v_A$,
resistivity by $\mu_0a^2/\tau_A$ (several printed forms drop $a$; see
Citations).

**Linearization** (§2). Each field is an equilibrium function of the
radial label plus a perturbation $\propto e^{i\omega t}$ with complex
$\omega$; the equilibrium is static ($\mathbf v=0$, uniform density, zero
viscosity in §4); toroidal dependence $\partial_\phi\to-in$. The
continuity equation decouples, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ eliminates the
toroidal field component, and the system becomes a generalized complex
eigenproblem ($\omega$ times one operator on the state equals a second
operator on the state, Eq. 8) for $(v_s,v_\phi,v_\theta,\delta p,B_s,B_\theta)$
(derivation in Guo & Ma 2020).

**Coordinates** (§3.1.2). Magnetic flux coordinates $(s,\phi,\theta)$
following Jardin's textbook: covariant metric elements of the poloidal
plane and their 2D Jacobian; in §4 $s=\sqrt{1-\psi/\psi_{\max}}$ (flux
measured from the axis); the grid is uniform in poloidal flux and angle.

**Symbolic module** (§3.1.1–3.1.2). Polynomial algebra on linked lists
(symbol, monomial, polynomial and vector classes) with substitution, data
binding by tag, covariant/contravariant basis conversion and the vector
operators; worked outputs for the Laplacian (Fig. 2) and the
Grad–Shafranov operator (Fig. 3).

**Discretization** (§3.1.3, App. A). Each term is discretized
independently and coefficients of the same unknown are summed. Fourier
series in $\theta$ with $2m_0+1$ harmonics ($m\in[-m_0,m_0]$), cubic finite
elements in $s$ ("cubic B-spline" in §3.1.3, "cubic Hermitian element" in
App. A), Galerkin projection on test functions (radial element times
$e^{-im\theta}$); equilibrium coefficients are Fourier-expanded and enter
as convolutions. First radial derivatives are integrated by parts with a
boundary term (Eq. A.1). Finite differences are mentioned as an
alternative.

**Boundary conditions** (§3.1.4, App. B). Ideal conducting wall at $s=1$:
$v_s=B_s=0$. At the axis, $v_\phi$ and $\delta p$ keep only $m=0$; the
flux-coordinate components satisfy
$\hat v_s^{(m)}=-im\,\hat v_\theta^{(m)}$, likewise for $B_s$, $B_\theta$
(Eq. B.6; follows from Eqs. B.4–B.5, checked by the reader).

**Eigensolver and filtering** (§3.1.5). SCELT only assembles the
matrices; test problems use the EIGEN library, the MHD eigenproblem the
dense MATLAB `eig` (all eigenpairs). Spurious modes are removed by an
eigenvalue window, then by an oscillation parameter (the maximum over
poloidal harmonics of the summed absolute jumps between adjacent radial
coefficients, divided by the coefficient range; Eq. 10; threshold about
15), then by inspection of the mode structure and a grid-change check.

**Equilibrium** (§3.2, §4). SCELT's own inverse Grad–Shafranov solver,
$\Delta^*\psi=-\mu_0R^2\,dp/d\psi-F\,dF/d\psi$ (Eq. 13), given $q$ and $p$
profiles, with $F$ obtained from $q$ and the flux-surface geometry
(Eq. 14).

**Implementation and cost.** C++ and MATLAB, GPL-3.0, CPC Library /
Mendeley Data doi:10.17632/35h3xmc28k.1. No parallelism, run times or
resolutions are given ("sufficient numbers of radial grids and Fourier
modes").

# Tests and results

* **Poisson** (§3.2, Eq. 12, Figs. 1, 4). $\nabla^2$ of an unknown equals a
  source on a shaped D-like domain given by discrete $R(s,\theta)$,
  $Z(s,\theta)$, manufactured Gaussian solution, Dirichlet boundary;
  $N\times N$ grids, $N=20$–160; L1 error from about $10^{-4}$ to
  $10^{-10}$, order 3.98.
* **Solov'ev** (§3.2, Eq. 13, Fig. 5). Linear $p(\psi)$ and $FF'(\psi)$,
  $R_0=5$ (constants in the Notation map); order 3.81. The reader verified
  that the printed Solov'ev solution satisfies Eq. 13 in the paper's sign
  convention.
* **Equilibria for MHD tests** (§4, Figs. 6–7). Circular, $R_0=5$, $a=1$,
  $p=p_0(1-s^2)$ with axis pressure $p_0$, uniform density, no flow, no
  viscosity. Kink case: $q$ from 0.9 (text; ≈0.7 in Fig. 7a) to 1.98, $q=1$
  at $s=0.48$. Tearing case: $q$ from 1.75 to 2.5, $q=2$ at $s=0.74$.
* **Resistive internal kink** (§4.1, Figs. 8–9). $p_0=5\times10^{-5}$;
  $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.3766}$ for $\log_{10}\eta\in[-8,-4.6]$
  ($\log_{10}\gamma$ from about −3.3 to −2.05); rigid $m=1$ displacement
  inside $q=1$ at $\eta=10^{-7}$.
* **Ideal internal kink** (§4.1, Fig. 10). $\gamma$ rises from about
  $0.5\times10^{-3}$ at $p_0=0.025$ to about $4\times10^{-3}$ at
  $p_0=0.12$; stated threshold $\beta_p\approx0.375$ against 0.3.
* **Tearing** (§4.2, Figs. 11–12). $p_0=5\times10^{-5}$;
  $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.5590}$ for $\log_{10}\eta\in[-9,-6.2]$;
  eigenfunction at $\eta=10^{-7}$ peaked at $s\approx0.74$.
* No table, no cross-code comparison of growth rates.
