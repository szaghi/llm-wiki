# Summary

The paper uses the stellarator capability of [M3D-C1](/software/m3d-c1.md)
to simulate the sawtooth-like core electron-temperature crashes observed in
W7-X under electron cyclotron current drive (ECCD), the first nonlinear,
transport-timescale MHD simulations of these crashes in full W7-X geometry.
The model is single-fluid visco-resistive MHD in SI units with constant
density (the pressure equation acts as a temperature equation). The ECCD
enters Ohm's law as a fixed toroidal source current from TRAVIS ray
tracing, peaked at $r/a\approx0.15$; the rotational-transform profile comes
from a 1D current-diffusion model, and its near-axis hump
($\iota_{\max}\approx1.2$) has two $\iota=1$ resonances. The equilibrium is
made self-consistent by VMEC, a one-field-period M3D-C1 run to saturation,
and a second VMEC solve on the saturated profiles. A full-torus run then
shows two successive (1,1) [internal kinks](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md):
the inner one reconnects first and lowers the core $T_e$ only slightly
(5 → 4.8 keV), and the authors suggest it corresponds to the experimental
precursors; the outer one flattens the core, $T_e$ falling from 5 to below
2.5 keV (about 50 %) with inversion radius $r_{\mathrm{inv}}/a\approx0.4$,
both consistent with experimental "type-A" crashes. The crash takes
$\gtrsim1$ ms against $\lesssim100\,\mu$s measured, which the authors
attribute to purely resistive reconnection with enhanced viscosity. After
the crash a saturated (1,1) helical core forms, related to flux pumping, and
the cycle does not repeat. The paper presents itself as a partial
validation of M3D-C1 for stellarators.

# Key claims

1. **Two $\iota=1$ resonances from near-axis ECCD** (Abstract, §I, §III,
   Figs. 1–2a). The current drive raises $\iota$ above 1 in a hump near the
   axis. Shown for the model profile; its 1D current-diffusion origin is
   attributed to Aleynikova et al. 2021 (ref. [16]); no $\iota$ measurement
   exists on W7-X.
2. **Two consecutive (1,1) crashes, inner first** (§IV, Figs. 3–5). The
   inner kink moves the core "upward", reconnects at the inner resonance and
   affects only $r/a\lesssim0.1$ ($T_e$ on axis 5 → about 4.8 keV between
   $t\approx1.2$ and 2 ms); the outer kink moves the new core "downward" and
   grows a (1,1) island at the outer resonance that eliminates the core
   (about 4.8 → <2.5 keV between $t\approx2$ and 3 ms). Shown.
3. **Kadomtsev signatures** (§IV, Fig. 4). The island "expels and eventually
   overtakes the original core". Shown qualitatively (Poincaré plots); the
   mapping to Kadomtsev's model is interpretation.
4. **Inner crash as precursor** (§IV). "We speculate that the inner crash may
   correspond to the sawtooth precursors" of type-A crashes. Stated
   (speculation).
5. **Semi-quantitative agreement** (§IV, Fig. 6). Outer-crash amplitude
   about 50 % against "≳ 50%" for medium type-A crashes; $r_{\mathrm{inv}}/a\approx0.4$
   "consistent with typical experimental measurements". Shown for the
   simulation; experimental values attributed to Zanini et al. 2020 (ref.
   [3]); no data overplotted.
6. **Crash about 10× too slow** (§IV). $\gtrsim1$ ms against
   $\lesssim100\,\mu$s; attributed to purely resistive reconnection (refs.
   [9–12]) and "relatively large viscosity"; two-fluid or plasmoid
   reconnection "may be needed". Discrepancy Shown; explanation Stated.
7. **Saturated (1,1) core, no cycle** (§IV, Figs. 7–8). "The plasma does not
   tend to restore the initial equilibrium"; related to "magnetic flux
   pumping"; robust to changed settings including a temperature-dependent
   $\eta$. Shown; flux-pumping link Stated.
8. **ECCD needed for the saturated core** (§IV). It "does not form if the
   ECCD is turned off during the crash (not shown)"; an ECCD model that
   follows the field could recover cyclic behaviour. Stated.
9. **Temperature-dependent resistivity** (§IV, Fig. 8). $\eta\propto T_e^{-3/2}$
   from $7\times10^{-7}$ Ω m (centre) to $7.5\times10^{-5}$ Ω m (edge),
   $\iota_{\max}\approx1.18$, more peaked $T_e$: the crash is slower and
   smaller, $r_{\mathrm{inv}}/a\approx0.45$. Shown (reader's figure reading:
   about 4.7 → 2.8 keV, about 40 %, over $t\approx6.7$–8.3 ms).
10. **$\iota=5/6$ inert; type-B crashes not reproduced** (§IV). A near-axis
    $\iota=5/6$ resonance has "no noticeable effect"; with two $\iota=1$
    surfaces "the inner crash is always immediately followed by an outer
    crash", unlike the small observed type-B crashes, which ref. [16] links
    to $\iota=5/6$. Shown (own runs) and Attributed.
11. **Seed immaterial** (§IV, not shown). Runs without the (1,1) velocity
    seed give "essentially the same crash features". Stated.
12. **Parameter insensitivity** (§III). "Qualitatively similar results" for
    varied parameters. Stated.
13. **Self-consistent equilibrium needed** (§III, Fig. 2). An ideal VMEC
    equilibrium must be "approximately sustained" by the dissipative,
    sourced M3D-C1 model; with the two-pass procedure $\iota$ and $T_e$
    "barely evolve" and only small island chains form, notably (5,5) at the
    outer $\iota=1$. Shown.
14. **Limitations** (§II, §III, §V). Two-fluid and other effects "are not yet
    functional in stellarator geometry"; the VMEC coordinate map is fixed in
    time; better preconditioners are needed for lower dissipation. Stated.
15. **Positioning** (§V). With JOREK and NIMROD (NIMSTELL), this capability
    "enables studies of nonlinear, transport-timescale MHD physics in complex
    stellarators"; earlier work had shorter time scales or simpler
    geometries. Stated.
16. **Mechanism of type-A crashes** (§IV, §V). They "are likely due to
    reconnection driven by (1, 1) internal kink modes", consistent with ref.
    [16]. Shown only in the sense of Claim 5; the $\iota$ profile itself
    comes from ref. [16]'s model, so the agreement is not independent in its
    input.

# Models and numerics

* **Equations** (§II, Eqs. 1–3, SI, checked on the rendered page 2), with
  $\mathbf J_x$ the ECCD source current and $S_p$ the pressure source:
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \rho(\partial_t\mathbf v+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v)&=\mathbf J\times\mathbf B-\nabla p-\nabla\cdot\mathsf\Pi,\\
  \partial_tp+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p+\Gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v&=(\Gamma-1)\big(\eta J^2-\nabla\cdot\mathbf q-\mathsf\Pi:\nabla\mathbf v\big)+S_p,\\
  \partial_t\mathbf B&=\nabla\times\big[\mathbf v\times\mathbf B-\eta(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_x)\big],\qquad\mu_0\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B,
  \end{aligned}$$
  $$\mathsf\Pi=-\nu(\nabla\mathbf v+\nabla\mathbf v^{\mathrm T})-2(\nu_c-\nu)(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)\mathsf I,\qquad
  \mathbf q=-\kappa_\perp\nabla T-\kappa_\parallel\mathbf b\mathbf b\cdot\nabla T,\qquad T=m_ip/\rho,$$
  with $\nu$, $\nu_c$ isotropic and compressional dynamic viscosities and
  $\Gamma=5/3$. This is a variant of the
  [single-fluid visco-resistive MHD model](/methods/visco-resistive-mhd-model.md)
  (the paper calls it "single-fluid extended MHD"). The $\kappa$ are in
  (m s)⁻¹, i.e. number density times diffusivity. Continuity is not solved:
  $\rho$ is constant, justified by the flat experimental core density.
* **Sources.** $S_p=(\Gamma-1)\,\frac{w_Q}{2\pi\sigma_Q^2}\exp\!\big(-\psi_{t,N}/(2\sigma_Q^2)\big)$
  (Gaussian in $r/a=\sqrt{\psi_{t,N}}$), $w_Q=9.73\times10^6$ Pa s⁻¹,
  $\sigma_Q=0.15$. $\mathbf J_x=J_{\mathrm{CD}}\mathbf e_\phi$ along the
  cylindrical toroidal direction, fixed in space, from TRAVIS, peaked at
  $r/a\approx0.15$ with peak about 400 kA m⁻² (Fig. 1). Bootstrap current
  neglected (small in the crash region, per ref. [3]).
* **Parameters** (§III). $\nu=\nu_c=3.65\times10^{-5}$ kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹; uniform
  $\eta=2.74\times10^{-6}$ Ω m, both "enhanced from realistic values";
  $\kappa_\parallel=2.18\times10^{26}$ (m s)⁻¹;
  $\kappa_\perp\propto T_e^{-1/2}$ from $4.03\times10^{19}$ (centre) to
  $1.12\times10^{20}$ (m s)⁻¹ (edge), anisotropy about $10^6$–$10^7$
  (reader's arithmetic). Hydrogen, $n_e=2\times10^{19}$ m⁻³, $T_e/T=5/6$.
  $S_p$, $\kappa_\perp$, $\kappa_\parallel$ found by trial and error to hold
  the initial pressure profile.
* **Geometry and equilibrium.** Fixed boundary; [VMEC](/software/vmec.md)
  supplies $R(\psi_{t,N},\theta,\phi)$, $Z(\psi_{t,N},\theta,\phi)$ ($\theta$
  the VMEC angle), defining a non-axisymmetric domain bounded by the last
  closed surface; the map does not evolve. VMEC $\mathbf B$ and $p$ are the
  initial condition. $R_0=5.51$ m, $a=0.506$ m, toroidal flux 2.133 Wb;
  experimental $p$; $\iota$ from the 1D model of ref. [16]. Procedure: VMEC
  → one-period M3D-C1 to saturation → VMEC on the saturated $\iota$, $p$ →
  M3D-C1 (see [inverse equilibrium solvers](/methods/inverse-equilibrium-solvers.md)).
* **BCs.** Ideal on $\mathbf B$, no-slip on $\mathbf v$, fixed $p$.
* **Discretization.** $C^1$ finite elements in all three dimensions extended
  to non-axisymmetric geometry (Zhou et al. 2021): 3807 reduced quintic
  elements in $(R,Z)$; 16 Hermite cubic elements toroidally per field
  period ($2\pi/5$), 80 for the full torus. The "relatively high toroidal
  resolution" is needed for the strongly anisotropic heat transport.
* **Time integration.** Split-implicit scheme with steps above the Alfvén
  time (Jardin 2012); $\Delta t=0.573\,\mu$s (Fig. 2 snapshots at 0.573 and
  1.145 ms are steps 1000 and 2000). Full-torus start: the one-period state
  at 1.145 ms plus a small (1,1) velocity perturbation. Linear solvers,
  preconditioners, parallelism and cost are not given.
* **Diagnostics.** Field-line tracing for $\iota$ and Poincaré plots at
  $\phi=\pi$; $T_e$ profiles at $\theta=0,\phi=0$ (Fig. 2b) and at
  $Z=0,\phi=0.1$ (Figs. 6, 8b); core $T_e$ at $(\psi_{t,N}=0,\phi=0)$.

# Tests and results

* **Equilibrium sustainment** (Fig. 2; readings): over 0–1.145 ms $\iota$
  barely changes (axis about 0.83, maximum about 1.21 at $r/a\approx0.2$,
  minimum about 0.93 near $r/a\approx0.6$, edge about 0.98), placing the
  resonances at $r/a\approx0.08$ and 0.33; $T_e$ 5 keV on axis, about
  0.25 keV at the edge.
* **Full-torus baseline** (Figs. 3–7): core $T_e$ flat to about 1.2 ms;
  about 4.8 keV by 2 ms (inner crash); about 2.5 keV at 3.1 ms (outer
  crash), slow reheating to about 2.8 keV at 4 ms. Poincaré snapshots at
  1.122, 1.443, 1.764 ms (inner), 1.947, 2.52, 3.092 ms (outer), 3.985 ms
  (saturated (1,1)). Profile peak 5 → 4.85 → 3.4 → about 2.4 keV, flat inside
  $r_{\mathrm{inv}}/a\approx0.4$.
* **Against W7-X** (ref. [3]): amplitude about 50 % vs $\gtrsim50$ %;
  $r_{\mathrm{inv}}/a\approx0.4$ consistent; duration $\gtrsim1$ ms vs
  $\lesssim100\,\mu$s (disagreement). Shot-terminating crashes not modelled.
* **$\eta(T_e)$ variant** (Fig. 8): Claim 9; a saturated (1,1) forms again.
* No tables, no growth rates, no convergence or resolution study.
