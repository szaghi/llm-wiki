# Summary

The paper compares the MHD code MIPS with the kinetic-MHD hybrid code
[MEGA](/software/mega.md), run with the **bulk thermal ions** as drift-kinetic
$\delta f$ markers ([kinetic thermal ions](/methods/kinetic-thermal-ions.md),
KTIs) rather than an energetic minority, on the $n=3$ infernal mode of two
circular, weak-central-shear tokamak equilibria ($R_0=2$ m, $B=2$ T,
hydrogen, $n_i=8\times10^{19}$ m⁻³). At central $\beta=5.7\%$ (the JT-60U-like
equilibrium of Ozeki et al. 1993) the mode is ideal; MIPS is first
benchmarked against the ideal eigenvalue code MINERVA (growth rate and mode
structure agree at high $S$). At $\beta=4.1\%$ the ideal mode is stable
(MINERVA) but a resistive infernal mode remains, with $\gamma$ falling with
$S$. Linearly, KTIs leave $\gamma$ unchanged at low $S$, but at high $S$
(ideal case $S=10^8$, resistive case $S=10^7$), where $\gamma$ is small, the
mode rotates in the ion diamagnetic direction with $\omega_r\sim\gamma$ and
$\gamma$ drops. The drop is traced to a phase shift: the perpendicular
ion-pressure perturbation (trapped-ion dominated, shifted by precession) stays
in phase with the radial flow, while the adiabatic electron pressure lags, so
the electron-pressure work on $\mathbf v_\perp$ turns negative. Nonlinearly,
the ideal mode flattens the pressure ("beta collapse") with and without KTIs;
for the resistive mode MHD again collapses the profile, but with KTIs the
$n=3$ saturation level halves and the axisymmetric pressure stays close to its
initial profile, because the dominant (5,3) velocity and pressure
perturbations are out of phase. The authors bracket the infernal beta limit
between 4.1% and 5.7% with KTIs, above the MHD estimate (<4.1%), and conclude
that MHD alone is inadequate to predict it.

# Key claims

1. **MIPS–MINERVA benchmark** (§3.1.1, Figs. 2–3). With $\nu=\kappa=0$ MIPS
   reproduces the MINERVA ideal growth rate of the $n=3$ mode at high $S$ and
   its structure (dominant $m=4$, with $m=3,5,6$); meshes $(256,64,256)$ and
   $(512,64,512)$ give "almost identical" results, "indicating a good
   convergence". Shown. From Fig. 2, MIPS at $S=10^8$ is about 5% above
   MINERVA on the finer meshes (compiler's reading).
2. **Ideal character at 5.7%** (§3.1.1, Fig. 2). $\gamma$ becomes independent
   of $S$ at high $S$. Shown.
3. **Resistive infernal mode at 4.1%** (§2, §3.1.3, Fig. 7). The ideal mode is
   stable (MINERVA, "not shown here"); a resistive infernal mode is unstable
   with $\gamma$ decreasing as $S$ increases. Shown; the MINERVA part Stated.
4. **When KTIs matter** (§3.1.2–3.1.3, Figs. 4, 7). KTIs leave $\gamma$
   unchanged when $\omega_r\ll\gamma$ ($S=10^5$ for both equilibria, also
   $10^6$ at 4.1%) and reduce it when $\omega_r\approx\gamma$ ($S=10^8$ at
   5.7%, $10^7$ at 4.1%); the mode rotates in the ion diamagnetic direction.
   Shown.
5. **Ion-pressure phase shift** (§3.1.3, Figs. 9–10). The perpendicular
   ion-pressure perturbation is shifted in the ion diamagnetic direction
   relative to the electron pressure and to the parallel ion pressure in the
   outer torus; passing ions dominate the parallel, trapped ions the
   perpendicular pressure; "it is suggested that the precession motion of
   trapped ions … is the cause". Shift Shown; mechanism Stated.
6. **Energy budget** at $(\beta,S)=(4.1\%,10^7)$ (§3.1.3, p. 8). Work of the
   electron pressure on $\mathbf v_\perp$ over the perpendicular kinetic
   energy $-0.164$, of the perpendicular ion pressure $0.585$; MHD $1.38$. The
   loss of the electron-pressure drive, from the delayed adiabatic response
   of $p_e$ to a rotating mode, is "one of the factors" reducing $\gamma$.
   Numbers Shown, interpretation Stated; "the physical mechanism determining
   the phase relationship … remains unclear".
7. **Ideal beta collapse** $(5.7\%,10^8)$ (§3.2.1, Figs. 11–13). The same
   $n=3$ saturation level with and without KTIs; pressure strongly flattened
   for $s>0.5$ in both, slightly less with KTIs for $s<0.5$; this "can be
   interpreted as the beta collapse due to ideal infernal modes observed in
   tokamak experiments". Shown; experimental link Stated.
8. **Resistive case** $(4.1\%,10^7)$ (§3.2.2, Figs. 14–16). MHD flattens the
   axisymmetric pressure; with KTIs the peak perpendicular-pressure
   perturbation is "approximately half" of the MHD one and the core profile
   stays "almost close to the initial equilibrium value". Shown.
9. **Phase, not amplitude** (§3.2.2, Fig. 17). The halved amplitude alone
   cannot explain the much smaller axisymmetric change; the $(0,0)$ pressure is
   generated by self-coupling of the dominant (5,3) harmonic, and with KTIs its
   radial-velocity and pressure structures are out of phase. Phase plots
   Shown; causal link Stated.
10. **$n=18$ ballooning** (§3.2.2, Fig. 14b). In the KTI resistive run an
    $n=18$ [ballooning mode](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md) reaches a linear
    growth rate comparable to $n=3$ (KTIs lower the $n=3$ and raise the $n=18$
    rate) but saturates lower; its influence on $n=3$ "appears to be small".
    Shown (qualitative).
11. **Ballooning unstable in MHD too** (§3.2.2). The equilibria are also
    unstable to $n>3$ ballooning modes; in MHD the $n=3$ infernal mode
    saturates before they appear. Shown.
12. **Infernal beta limit** (§4). MHD: below 4.1%; with KTIs: between 4.1%
    and 5.7%; "the MHD model without KTIs is inadequate" to predict it.
    Derived from two equilibria (a bracket, not a scan), at two different $S$.
13. **Different mechanism than in helical plasmas** (§4). In the authors'
    earlier helical work (Sato & Todo 2019–2021) KTIs reduce the ion-pressure
    fluctuation amplitude; here electron and ion pressure perturbations have
    comparable amplitudes and the difference is phase. Stated.
14. **No electron diamagnetic effects** (§2, §4). The $\nabla_\parallel p_e$
    term of the Ohm's law is neglected in all results; extended effects are
    future work. Stated.

# Models and numerics

**MIPS** (Eqs. 1–5), in registry notation:

$$\begin{aligned}
\rho\partial_t\mathbf v &= -\rho(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v-\nabla p+\mathbf J\times\mathbf B-\nabla\times(\nu\rho\nabla\times\mathbf v)+\tfrac43\nabla(\nu\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf v),\\
\partial_tp &= -\nabla\cdot(p\mathbf v)-(\Gamma-1)p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+(\Gamma-1)\nabla\cdot[\kappa\nabla(p-p_{\mathrm{eq}})],\\
\partial_t\mathbf B &= -\nabla\times\mathbf E,\qquad \mathbf E=-\mathbf v\times\mathbf B+\eta(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{eq}}),\qquad \mu_0\mathbf J=\nabla\times\mathbf B,
\end{aligned}$$

with $\kappa$ an isotropic diffusivity; dissipation acts on perturbations
only. No continuity equation is printed (uniform initial density) and no
Ohmic heating.

**MEGA with KTIs** (Eqs. 6–25). Drift-kinetic guiding centres:
$\dot{\mathbf X}=v_{\parallel p}\mathbf B/B^*_\parallel+\mathbf v_C+\mathbf v_E+\mathbf v_{\nabla B}$,
with curvature drift $\mathbf v_C=(\rho_\parallel v_{\parallel p}B/B^*_\parallel)\nabla\times\mathbf b$,
$\rho_\parallel=m_iv_{\parallel p}/(q_iB)$,
$B^*_\parallel=B(1+\rho_\parallel\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf b)$,
$\mathbf v_E=\mathbf E^*\times\mathbf B/B^*_\parallel$ with
$\mathbf E^*=-\mathbf v_\perp\times\mathbf B-(m_i/(q_i\rho))\nabla_\parallel p_e$,
$\mathbf v_{\nabla B}=-\mu\nabla B\times\mathbf b/(q_iB^*_\parallel)$, and
$m_iv_{\parallel p}\dot v_{\parallel p}=(v_{\parallel p}\mathbf B/B^*_\parallel+\mathbf v_C)\cdot(q_i\mathbf E^*-\mu\nabla B)$.
[δf](/methods/delta-f-method.md) weights in particle-number form,
$\dot w_p=-V_p[(\mathbf v_E+v_{\parallel p}\delta\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla+\dot{\mathcal E}\,\partial_{\mathcal E}]f_0$,
with $V_p$ the phase-space volume of the marker and $f_0(\psi,\mathcal E)$
Maxwellian. Moments: $\rho$, $v_\parallel$, $p_{\parallel i}$ and
$p_{\perp i}$ (perturbations $\sum_pw_p\{m_i,\;v_{\parallel p},\;m_iv_{\parallel p}^2,\;B\mu_p\}\mathcal S(\mathbf x-\mathbf X_p)$
added to the equilibrium values), $\mathcal S$ the shape factor.

The fluid evolves only $\mathbf v_\perp$ and $p_e$, with $v_\parallel$ from the
ions ([current coupling](/methods/pressure-and-current-coupling.md)):

$$\begin{aligned}
\rho\partial_t\mathbf v_\perp &= -\rho[(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)\cdot\nabla]\mathbf v_\perp-\nabla_\perp p_e+(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_i)\times\mathbf B-\nabla\times(\nu\rho\nabla\times\mathbf v_\perp)+\tfrac43\nabla(\nu\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf v_\perp),\\
\partial_tp_e &= -\nabla\cdot[p_e(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)]-(\Gamma-1)p_e\nabla\cdot(\mathbf v_\perp+v_\parallel\mathbf b)+(\Gamma-1)\nabla\cdot[\kappa\nabla(p_e-p_{e,\mathrm{eq}})],\\
\mathbf E &= -\mathbf v_\perp\times\mathbf B-\frac{m_i}{q_i\rho}\nabla_\parallel p_e+\eta(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{eq}}),\\
\mathbf J_i &= \frac1B\big(p_{\parallel i}\nabla\times\mathbf b-p_{\perp i}\nabla\ln B\times\mathbf b\big)-\nabla\times\Big(\frac{p_{\perp i}}{B}\mathbf b\Big),
\end{aligned}$$

$\mathbf J_i$ being the kinetic-ion perpendicular current (curvature, $\nabla B$
and magnetization parts) built from the kinetic pressures. The
$\nabla_\parallel p_e$ terms are neglected in all results.

**Discretization (both codes).** Finite differences on a cylindrical
$(R,\phi,Z)$ grid; convection by the Kawamura–Kuwahara third-order upwind
scheme, other terms fourth-order central; explicit fourth-order Runge–Kutta for
fluid and particles. Toroidal wedge $\phi\in[0,2\pi/3]$, periodic, so only
$n=3,6,9,\dots$ are kept. Grid $256\times48\times256$ $(R,\phi,Z)$ by default;
benchmark meshes $(128,64,128)$, $(256,64,256)$, $(512,64,512)$; 32 markers per
cell, about $10^8$ markers. $\nu=\kappa=10^{-6}R_0^2/\tau_A$ (zero in the
benchmark), $\tau_A=R_0/v_A$, $S=\mu_0R_0^2/(\eta\tau_A)$ scanned
$10^5$–$10^8$. Wall boundary conditions not stated. Hardware: NIFS Plasma
Simulator (NEC SX-Aurora TSUBASA) and Fugaku; no GPU; no run times.

**Equilibria.** Grad–Shafranov, circular; uniform density;
$p_{e,\mathrm{eq}}=p_{\perp i,\mathrm{eq}}=p_{\parallel i,\mathrm{eq}}=p_{\mathrm{MHD}}/2$;
central $T_i=1.9$ keV (5.7%) and 1.4 keV (4.1%). From Fig. 1: weakly reversed
core $q$ with $q_{\min}\approx1.4$–1.6 near $s\approx0.5$, $q_0\approx1.9$
(5.7%) or 2.1 (4.1%), $q$ at the edge ≈7 (approximate readings). Analysis in
flux coordinates $(s,\theta^*,\zeta)$, $\zeta=-\phi$, $s=\sqrt{\psi/\psi_b}$,
Fourier phases $m\theta^*-n\zeta$. Linear reference: MINERVA (Aiba et al.
2009; see [linear MHD eigenvalue codes](/methods/linear-mhd-eigenvalue-codes.md)).

* Coarse mesh $(128,64,128)$ about 20% high at $S\ge10^7$; mode structure
  $m=4$ dominant peaking at $s\approx0.5$ (Fig. 3); resistive case dominant
  $m=5$ with $m=4,6,7$ (Fig. 8). The MHD resistive $\gamma$ falls roughly as
  $S^{-0.8}$ (compiler's reading of Fig. 7, not stated).
* Energy-transfer ratios at $(4.1\%,10^7)$: $-0.164$ (electron pressure),
  $0.585$ (perpendicular ion pressure), MHD $1.38$; work
  $-\int\mathbf v_\perp\cdot\nabla\delta p\,dV$ over
  $\int\tfrac12\rho|\mathbf v_\perp|^2dV$.
* Nonlinear ideal $(5.7\%,10^8)$: $n=3$ saturates at a pressure perturbation
  ≈$4\times10^{-3}$ ($B^2/\mu_0$ units) at $t\approx2300\tau_A$ (MHD) and
  ≈$2900\tau_A$ (KTI); central axisymmetric pressure ≈0.029, flattened to
  ≈0.009 over $0.3\lesssim s\lesssim0.8$ in MHD.
* Nonlinear resistive $(4.1\%,10^7)$: MHD $n=3$ saturates at ≈$2\times10^{-3}$
  near $21000\tau_A$; KTI peak ≈$1.2\times10^{-3}$ near $27000\tau_A$, run to
  $40000\tau_A$; $n=18$ saturates near $10^{-4}$ around $20000\tau_A$. MHD core
  pressure at $s=0.3$ falls from 0.02 to ≈0.01; the KTI profile stays on the
  initial curve.
