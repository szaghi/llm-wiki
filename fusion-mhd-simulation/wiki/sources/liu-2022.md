# Summary

The paper introduces M3D-C1-K, a hybrid kinetic-MHD code that adds an
energetic-particle (EP) marker module to the high-order 3D finite-element
code [M3D-C1](/software/m3d-c1.md). Markers are pushed either by RK4
integration of the drift-kinetic guiding-centre equations (the M3D-K
formulation) or by the "slow manifold Boris" algorithm of Xiao & Qin (2021),
which applies the standard Boris full-orbit pusher to the Pauli particle by
folding the mirror force into an effective electric field. This allows time
steps longer than the gyro-period, takes the curvature drift from the Boris
orbit itself, and needs only first derivatives of the finite-element field
polynomials. Weights evolve by a [δf scheme](/methods/delta-f-method.md) with
optional four-point gyro-averaging for finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) effects.
Parallel and perpendicular EP pressures are deposited by a Galerkin
projection with a δ-function shape and enter the MHD momentum equation by
pressure coupling (M3D-K style) or current coupling (MEGA style); the paper
derives that the two are nearly equivalent in the drift-kinetic limit and
finds them numerically indistinguishable in its runs. The particle push uses
particle-based parallelization with OpenACC on GPUs (OpenMP on CPUs), each
GPU holding the whole-mesh field; the MHD stays implicit or semi-implicit on
CPUs, with particle subcycling. The GPU push is about 11× faster than the CPU
push on Summit. Linear benchmarks reproduce the M3D-K/NIMROD $n=1$ fishbone,
the ITPA $n=6$ TAE benchmark (Könies et al. 2018) and the DIII-D #159243 RSAE
benchmark (Taimourzadeh et al. 2019).

# Key claims

1. **Long-time conservation** (§2, Fig. 1). RK4 guiding-centre integration
   accumulates error in $P_\phi$ and $\mathcal E$, while the slow-manifold
   Boris error "is always bounded"; the benefit is "only significant for long
   time simulations ($t>500$ms)", and the RK4 error is smaller at short
   times. Shown.
2. **Super-gyro-period steps** (§2, Eqs. 5–6, footnote 1). With the mirror
   force folded into the effective electric field, Boris pushes markers with
   $\Delta t>2\pi/\Omega_h$ "as long as particles stay close to the slow
   manifold"; one RK4 guiding-centre step must initialize the marker to
   avoid oscillation across the slow manifold. Stated, attributed to Xiao &
   Qin (2021).
3. **Derivative order** (§2). Boris needs only first derivatives of the
   finite-element polynomials ($\nabla B$ from $B$ treated as a scalar
   field); RK4 guiding-centre needs second derivatives (curvature), which
   dominate its cost by profiling. Stated; no profiling numbers.
4. **GPU speed-up** (§5, Fig. 2). Porting the push to GPU without algorithm
   changes gives about 11×: 134.22 s CPU RK4, 12.65 s GPU RK4, 10.32 s GPU
   Boris with the Boris $\Delta t$ one quarter of the RK4 one. Shown. The
   particle count is printed inconsistently (Citations).
5. **No further GPU gain needed** (§5). Coalescing or single precision would
   not improve overall performance much, because the push time is already
   close to the CPU MHD time. Stated.
6. **Coupling equivalence** (§4, Eqs. 23–25). In the drift-kinetic limit the
   current-coupling force $\mathbf J_h\times\mathbf B$ (EP current: parallel,
   drift and magnetization parts) equals the perpendicular part of the EP
   pressure-tensor force, so the same $p_{\parallel h}$, $p_{\perp h}$
   moments serve both schemes. Derived.
7. **Identification with earlier schemes** (§4). The pressure coupling of
   Park et al. (1999), which keeps only the perpendicular
   $\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h$, "is exactly the same as" Eq. 25 and equivalent
   to MEGA's simplified current coupling. Derived/Stated.
8. **Dropped $-q_hn_h\mathbf E$** (§4). Omitting this term from the bulk
   momentum equation in current coupling is justified because it cancels the
   $\mathbf J_h\times\mathbf B$ contribution of the EP $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$
   current. Stated, attributed to Zhao et al. (2020); an explicit divergence
   from Park et al. (1992, 1999).
9. **Coupling results** (§4, Figs. 3, 5). M3D-C1-K pressure coupling keeps
   all components of $\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h$ and drops the time derivative
   of the EP momentum density in all directions, like M3D-K; "for all the
   simulations we have conducted, the two coupling schemes give almost the
   same results." Shown. The paper also says the parallel EP-momentum term
   "cannot be safely ignored", yet ignores it.
10. **Perpendicular compressibility** (§3). With the compression-corrected
    weight and the $B_{1\parallel}/B_0$ term, the EPs "behave like plasma
    with heat capacity ratio [$\Gamma$] $=2$ in the perpendicular direction".
    Stated.
11. **Fishbone** (§6.1, Fig. 3). Linear $n=1$: $\gamma$ and $\omega$ agree
    with M3D-K and NIMROD "except for the mode real frequency at large
    $\beta_h/\beta_{\mathrm{total}}$"; the mode changes from ideal kink to
    fishbone as $\beta_h/\beta_{\mathrm{total}}$ goes from 0 to 0.75, $\gamma$
    has a minimum near 0.25 and $\omega$ rises roughly linearly. Shown.
12. **Trapped-particle drive** (§6.1, Fig. 4). The non-adiabatic response
    $\delta p_{\perp h}-\delta p_{\parallel h}$ is localized on the low-field
    side, read as trapped-particle resonance; mode structure consistent with
    NIMROD (Kim et al. 2008). Shown.
13. **TAE** (§6.2, Fig. 5). $n=6$: "close to the results from gyrokinetic,
    hybrid-MHD and eigenvalue codes"; FLR lowers growth rates markedly at
    high EP temperature and the frequency slightly; pressure and current
    coupling give "equal" results. Shown.
14. **RSAE** (§6.3, Figs. 7–8). DIII-D #159243, $n=3$–6 with FLR: results
    "agree well" with other initial-value MHD and gyrokinetic codes; growth
    largest for $n=4,5$; the $n=4$ mode is localized at $q_{\min}$ and
    dominated by $m=12$. Shown; the spread against NOVA/FAR3D is not
    discussed (Tests and results).
15. **Outlook** (§7). Suited to long-time nonlinear EP physics (chirping,
    kink/tearing with EPs); for short-timescale problems (GAE/CAE) the CPU
    MHD dominates the cost, hence planned MHD GPU porting; slow-manifold
    Boris is a candidate for runaway electrons. Stated; the massless "modified
    Boris" of [Liu et al. (2021)](/sources/liu-2021.md) (this paper's ref.
    [27]) applies the idea to fluid-RE pseudo-particles.

# Models and numerics

**Guiding-centre option** (Eqs. 1–4, M3D-K form, Littlejohn Lagrangian, 5D
phase space):

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\mathbf X}{dt}&=\frac{1}{B^*_\parallel}\Big[v_{\parallel p}\mathbf B^*-\mathbf b\times\Big(\mathbf E-\frac{\mu}{q_h}\nabla B\Big)\Big],\qquad
m_h\frac{dv_{\parallel p}}{dt}=\frac{1}{B^*_\parallel}\mathbf B^*\cdot(q_h\mathbf E-\mu\nabla B),\\
\mathbf B^*&=\mathbf B+\frac{m_hv_{\parallel p}}{q_h}\nabla\times\mathbf b,\qquad B^*_\parallel=\mathbf B^*\cdot\mathbf b .
\end{aligned}$$

**Slow-manifold Boris** (Eqs. 5–6), leapfrog-staggered, volume-preserving,
solved explicitly:

$$\frac{\mathbf x_p^{l}-\mathbf x_p^{l-1}}{\Delta t}=\mathbf v_p^{l-1/2},\qquad
\frac{m_h}{q_h}\frac{\mathbf v_p^{l+1/2}-\mathbf v_p^{l-1/2}}{\Delta t}=\mathbf E^\dagger(\mathbf x_p^l)+\frac{\mathbf v_p^{l+1/2}+\mathbf v_p^{l-1/2}}{2}\times\mathbf B(\mathbf x_p^l),\qquad
\mathbf E^\dagger=\mathbf E-\frac{\mu}{q_h}\nabla B,$$

with the $1/q_h$ restored for dimensional consistency (printed without it;
Citations). Bootstrapped by one RK4 guiding-centre step,
$\mathbf v_p^{1/2}=(\mathbf x_p^1-\mathbf x_p^0)/\Delta t$; the weight equation
uses the average of the two half-step velocities.

**δf weights** (Eqs. 7–13), $w_p=\delta f/f$, $\mu$ invariant,
$f_0=f_0(P_\phi,\mathcal E,\mu)$, no equilibrium electric field:

$$\frac{dw_p}{dt}=-(1-w_p)\frac{1}{f_0}\frac{df_0}{dt},\qquad
\frac{df_0}{dt}=\frac{dP_\phi}{dt}\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial P_\phi}+\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}\frac{\partial f_0}{\partial\mathcal E},\qquad
\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=q_h\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf E_1+\mu\frac{\partial B_{1\parallel}}{\partial t},$$

with $dP_\phi/dt$ from the perturbed drift
$\mathbf E_1\times\mathbf B_0/B_0^2+v_{\parallel p}\mathbf B_1/B_0$ and the
perturbed parallel acceleration. Linear runs use equilibrium orbits and
$(1-w_p)\to1$; nonlinear runs take the perturbed terms as differences between
full-field Boris and equilibrium-field guiding-centre orbits. Markers are
loaded by Monte Carlo from $f_0$. Compression correction (Belova et al. 1997):
$w_p$ is replaced by $w_p+(1-w_p)B^*_{1\parallel}/B^*_\parallel$.

**FLR** (Eq. 14): four-point gyro-average of $\mathbf B_1$ over points at
$\mathbf X+\boldsymbol\rho_L$, with
$\boldsymbol\rho_L=\mathbf v_\perp\times\mathbf b/\Omega_h$; deposition is
gyro-averaged the same way.

**Moments and deposition** (Eqs. 15–18): $\delta p_{\parallel h}$ and
$\delta p_{\perp h}$ are marker sums of $m_hv_{\parallel p}^2w_p$ and
$\mu B(w_p+B_{1\parallel}/B_0)$, projected by Galerkin onto the finite-element
test functions and followed by a mass-matrix solve. The shape function is a
δ-function in all runs, which gives spiky pressures with high-order
polynomials; a finite-width shape would also require finite-width field
interpolation.

**Coupling** (Eqs. 19–27), $n_h\ll n_i$ throughout. Pressure coupling, with
the time derivative of the EP momentum density dropped (as in M3D-K):

$$\rho\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf v}{\partial t}+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v\Big)=\mathbf J\times\mathbf B-\nabla p-\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h,\qquad
\mathsf P_h=p_{\parallel h}\mathbf b\mathbf b+p_{\perp h}(\mathsf I-\mathbf b\mathbf b),$$

linearized about an isotropic EP equilibrium
$\mathbf J_0\times\mathbf B_0=\nabla p_0+\nabla p_{h0}$. Current coupling:

$$\rho\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf v}{\partial t}+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v\Big)=(\mathbf J-\mathbf J_h)\times\mathbf B-\nabla p,\qquad
\mathbf J_h\times\mathbf B=\Big[\nabla p_{\perp h}+\nabla\cdot\big((p_{\parallel h}-p_{\perp h})\mathbf b\mathbf b\big)\Big]\times\mathbf b\times\mathbf b,$$

the right-hand side being $-(\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h)_\perp$ (compiler's
rewriting). The EP current is the sum of parallel, curvature-plus-$\nabla B$
drift and magnetization ($\nabla\times(p_{\perp h}\mathbf b/B)$) currents.
With $\mathbf J_{h0}\times\mathbf B_0=\nabla p_{h0}$ the equilibrium term
reduces to $\mathbf b_0(\mathbf B_1/B_0)\cdot\nabla p_{h0}$, MEGA's simplified
current coupling (Eq. 27). See
[pressure and current coupling](/methods/pressure-and-current-coupling.md).

**MHD side.** M3D-C1, "high order 3D finite elements" (element type not
stated in this paper), fields represented by potentials $(\Phi,\mathbf A)$,
implicit or semi-implicit time stepping not CFL-limited, matrices built and
solved on CPUs with MPI domain decomposition. Particles are subcycled between
two MHD steps with frozen fields, transferred CPU→GPU once per MHD step.
Boundary conditions are not discussed.

**Parallelism.** Particle-based: one thread per particle for several steps,
no inter-subdomain migration (as in GTC). OpenACC (GPU) or OpenMP (CPU) at
compile time. Every GPU holds the full 3D-mesh field, gathered with MPI-3
shared memory within a node and MPI between nodes; deposition of
$p_{\parallel h}$, $p_{\perp h}$ is on CPUs.
