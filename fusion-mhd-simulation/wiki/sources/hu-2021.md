# Summary

The paper uses 3D nonlinear [reduced-MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md)
[JOREK](/software/jorek.md) simulations to study MHD destabilization and
radiation asymmetry during the [thermal quench](/definitions/thermal-quench.md)
(TQ) that follows impurity
[shattered pellet injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md)
(SPI). Impurities use the
[coronal-equilibrium](/methods/coronal-equilibrium-impurity-model.md) (CE)
model with the ionization energy carried as potential energy, in one- or
two-temperature runs. In a JET-like L-mode (pulse 85943 template) with
$1.5\times10^{22}$ argon atoms, the strong-cooling limit, the $n=0$
current contraction follows the cooling front and forms a current sheet;
the 3/1 and then 2/1 modes grow as the front crosses $q=3$ and $q=2$, the
2/1 couples nonlinearly to the 3/2 core mode and the TQ starts when the
fragments reach $q=2$ ($t\approx2.00$ ms). The core $T_e$ collapses more
than 100 µs before impurities mix into the core, because at TQ onset
edge field lines still need more than 50 toroidal turns to reach the core.
In an ITER 7.5 MA L-mode with a broad, weakly reversed $q\approx1$ core,
the TQ is triggered when fragments enter $q=1$, through the
[1/1 kink](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md), which convects
material deposited at or inside $q=1$ into the core, so deeper-penetrating
mixed H/Ne pellets give much better core neon penetration than pure neon.
Two-temperature runs reach $|T_e-T_i|\approx2$ keV in the core. Mono-SPI
gives strongly asymmetric radiation, toroidally and poloidally, before and
during the TQ (toroidal peaking factor above 2), helical with the
helicity of the triggering surface (1/1 in ITER, 2/1 in JET), relaxing by
the end of the TQ. Dual-SPI from opposite ports mitigates the peaking even
with a 1 ms delay and, when synchronized, makes even $n$ dominate and
removes the 1/1 kink motion.

# Key claims

1. **Two destabilization mechanisms** (§3, p. 12, Fig. 5; §6). The $n=0$
   axisymmetric current contraction (time scale $\propto a^2/\eta$) and
   helical cooling at each rational surface (time scale $\propto\delta
   l^2/\eta$, $\delta l$ the width of the helical current redistribution);
   the first matters when shorter than the fragment flight time. For
   full-argon SPI the contraction "follows the propagation of the cooling
   front closely until the onset of field line stochasticity"; in pure-D
   SPI (Hu et al. 2018) helical cooling dominates. Shown (JET case);
   time-scale argument Stated, attributed.
2. **Hyper-resistive arrest** (§3, pp. 13–14, Fig. 5d). Stochasticity
   stops the contraction at 1.91 ms because the nonlinear
   $\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$ term "tends to act like a hyper-resistivity
   upon the n = 0 component of the current density"; the core current is
   also flattened after the TQ. Shown; mechanism Stated (cf. the current
   spike on [disruption](/definitions/disruption.md)).
3. **JET argon: TQ triggered at $q=2$** (§3, Figs. 6–7). 3/1 unstable when
   the front reaches $q=3$ (its islands at 1.01 ms "probably mainly a
   consequence of the axisymmetric current contraction"); 2/1 from 1.72
   ms; 3/2 nonlinearly destabilized at 1.90 ms; global stochasticity and
   TQ at about 2.00 ms. "Remarkable agreement" with JET SPI observations on
   the trigger location and raised MHD activity. Shown (comparison
   qualitative).
4. **Current spike and radiation burst** (§3, Fig. 4). $I_p$ rises from
   about 1.93 to 2.12 MA during the TQ; $W_{\mathrm{th}}$ falls from about
   0.43 MJ; radiated energy and ablation rate jump at TQ onset. Shown
   (values read from the figure).
5. **Temperature collapse precedes core mixing** (§3, Figs. 8, 10; §6).
   "More than 100 µs delay between the core temperature collapse and the
   eventual core impurity mixing by MHD modes": at 2.00 ms edge-started
   field lines stay outside the core after 50 toroidal turns, at 2.13 ms
   they reach it within 5. Fast parallel conduction collapses $T_e$, slower
   parallel convection carries the material. The hollow density is
   unfavourable for [runaway-electron](/definitions/runaway-electrons.md)
   suppression. Shown; RE consequence Stated.
6. **Start position decides core access** (§3, Fig. 10d–f). Field lines
   started slightly further in reach the core much better, related to the
   mode structure through Rechester–Rosenbluth diffusion
   $\propto\sum(\delta B/B)^2$. Shown (tracing); interpretation Stated.
7. **Helical radiation in JET** (§3, Fig. 9). At the TQ the radiation
   density is helical with 2/1 helicity, an order of magnitude larger at
   the injection plane than at $\pi$ from it; the argon density shows a
   "3D stream". Qualitative agreement with JET. Shown.
8. **Trigger surface depends on the $q$ profile** (§4.1, Figs. 13–14; §6).
   In ITER L-mode (large $q\approx1$ region, two $q=1$ surfaces, strong
   edge shear) the 2/1 and 3/1 islands are too narrow to couple to core
   modes and the TQ is triggered "by the vanguard fragments entering the
   q = 1 surface", the 1/1 O-point coinciding with the fragments; in JET
   (almost no $q=1$ surface, lower shear near $q=2$) the trigger is $q=2$.
   Shown for the two cases; general explanation Stated.
9. **No 1/1 kink without injection** (§2.4). The 1/1 kink "remains
   negligibly small within our timescale of interest" (low β, ideal kink
   stable; resistive kink slow). Shown (observation); reason Stated.
10. **Core penetration by kink convection** (§4.1, Fig. 14; §6). ITER shot
    1 (H/Ne) deposits neon near or within $q=1$ and gets "remarkably
    better" core transport than shot 2 (pure Ne, same neon content), whose
    fragments "barely reach the q = 1 surface before being entirely
    ablated"; $m>1$ modes give a weaker version of the benefit. Shown for
    1/1; Stated for $m>1$.
11. **Milder radiation rise for mixed SPI** (§4.1, Fig. 11). No radiation
    jump at the TQ in ITER shot 1, consistent with NIMROD (Kim et al.
    2019) and DIII-D (Shiraki et al. 2016); the edge temperature barely
    changes, partly because the ideal wall suppresses edge stochasticity.
    Shown; comparisons qualitative.
12. **$T_e$–$T_i$ decoupling** (§4.2, Fig. 15). $|T_e-T_i|$ reaches about
    2 keV in the core at 4.56 ms (shot 3); electrons are flattened by
    parallel conduction while $T_i$ still shows the kink. A one-temperature
    model lets ions heat electrons artificially and would overestimate
    electron-driven ablation. Shown (decoupling); ablation consequence
    Stated.
13. **Mono-SPI radiation asymmetry** (§4.3, Fig. 16; §5.1, Fig. 18a,c).
    The toroidal radiation peaking factor $\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ (poloidal-plane-integrated
    radiation over its toroidal mean) is "larger than 2 during the TQ when
    the radiation power is strongest", peaking near the injection angle:
    about 5.7 at 2.32 ms, 4.5 at 3.80 ms, 2.4 at 4.24 ms (early TQ),
    relaxing to about 1.2 at 4.67 ms (read); peak about 580 MW rad⁻¹. The
    structure is poloidal as well as toroidal, helical 1/1, and its peak
    need not coincide with the fragments or the impurity-density peak.
    Shown.
14. **Synchronized dual-SPI** (§5.1, Fig. 18b,d). Peaks at $\phi=0,\pi$
    before the TQ (factor about 2.4 at 2.52 ms), falling towards TQ onset
    ($t\sim4.1$ ms) and "mostly mitigated" at 4.16 ms (factor ≈1); more
    total radiation than mono-SPI (≈1370 against ≈580 MW rad⁻¹, read),
    qualitatively as in KSTAR. Shown.
15. **Dual-SPI with 1 ms delay** (§5.2, Fig. 21). Similar pre-TQ peaking,
    earlier injector stronger, "mostly flattened" shortly after the TQ;
    "good mitigation of toroidal peaking can be maintained with O(1 ms)
    time delay"; for delays comparable to the mitigation time the result
    tends to mono-SPI. Shown for one delay; trend Stated.
16. **MHD parity follows injection symmetry** (§5.1–5.2, Figs. 17, 19,
    20). Synchronized dual-SPI gives a 4/2 island at $q=2$, $n=2,4,6$
    dominant until the TQ and an $m=2$ core deformation with no 1/1 kink;
    with a delay $n=1$ dominates first and a 1/1 kink appears at the TQ,
    its O-point not at the fragments. Mono-SPI "can be seen as an
    asymmetric dual-SPI with extremely long delay". Shown.
17. **SPI radiation peaks near the injector, unlike MGI** (§6, p. 31).
    Here and in NIMROD SPI the toroidal radiation peak is near the
    injector; in Izzo's MGI simulation (2013) it is at $\pi$ from it,
    explained by toroidally uniform impurities in MGI against localized
    ones in SPI. Stated; no MGI run here.
18. **CE acceptable** (§2.3, Fig. 2). In a 0D test ($T_e$ decaying from 5
    keV to 10 eV on 20 µs, constant impurity density), CE and
    non-equilibrium radiation agree acceptably once $T_e\lesssim100$ eV
    (about 80 µs); with slow cooling, "the CE assumption produces a milder
    radiation collapse than that is actually the case". Shown (0D only).
19. **Ionization energy as potential energy** (§2.3). Under CE a rapid
    $T_e$ change shifts the charge-state distribution artificially fast,
    producing spurious recombination radiation; carrying the ionization
    energy as potential energy "ensures the energy conservation and
    prevents artificially large recombination radiation". Stated.
20. **CQ less critical** (§1). The CQ radiates more energy but "much more
    relaxed", so only the TQ is studied. Stated, partly supported.

# Models and numerics

* **Model.** JOREK nonlinear reduced MHD ("energy conserving", "benchmarked
  intensely against full MHD"), ideal wall, one velocity for all species
  and charge states, one- ($T_e=T_i$) or two-temperature model; JOREK
  normalized units with $\mu_0=1$ (stated for Eq. 4 only), restored below.
  $$\mathbf B=F_0\nabla\phi+\nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi,\qquad\mathbf v=v_\parallel\mathbf b-R^2\nabla u\times\nabla\phi,$$
  $v_\parallel$ here the true parallel speed (the paper's coefficient
  $\hat v_\parallel$ times $B$); $[f,g]=\mathbf e_\phi\cdot(\nabla f\times\nabla g)$.
* **Induction** (Eqs. 3–4):
  $$\partial_t\psi=\frac{\eta(T_e)}{\mu_0}\Delta^*\psi-R[u,\psi]-F_0\partial_\phi u,\qquad j=\Delta^*\psi,\quad J_\phi=-j/(\mu_0R).$$
* **Resistivity** (p. 4): Spitzer with the Hirshman $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$
  factor divided by a passing fraction of about 0.5,
  $$\eta=\frac{1}{f_{\mathrm{pass}}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2m_e}\,Z_{\mathrm{eff}}e^2\ln\Lambda}{12\pi^{3/2}\varepsilon_0^2\max(T_e,T_{\mathrm{thr}})^{3/2}}\,\frac{1+1.198Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.222Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}{1+2.966Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.753Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2},$$
  $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$ summed over all charge states; initial core
  $\eta\simeq5.6\times10^{-8}T_{e0}[\mathrm{keV}]^{-3/2}$ Ω m; floor
  $T_{\mathrm{thr}}=2$ keV in ITER "to ensure resolution of the resistive
  skin current". $f_{\mathrm{pass}}$, $T_{\mathrm{thr}}$ page-local.
* **Continuity** (Eqs. 5–6):
  $\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot(\mathsf D\nabla\rho)+S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}}$
  and the same for $\rho_{\mathrm{imp}}$ with source $S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}}$;
  $D_\parallel$ small.
* **Vorticity and parallel momentum** (Eqs. 7–9), JOREK units: the
  toroidal projection of the curl of the momentum equation for $u$ with
  $\varpi=R^{-1}\partial_R(R\partial_Ru)+\partial_Z^2u$, containing the
  inertial brackets, $[\psi,j]$, $-(F_0/R)\partial_\phi j$, the pressure
  term $[p,R^2]$ and perpendicular viscosity $R\,\nu_\perp(T_e)\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}^2\varpi$
  (factor $R$ as printed in this paper); and the $\mathbf B$-projected
  parallel momentum equation with parallel viscosity $\nu_\parallel$.
  $\nu_\perp\propto T_e^{-3/2}$ (fixed magnetic Prandtl number),
  $\nu_\parallel$ constant.
* **Pressure, one temperature** (Eq. 10, intended form; see Recorded as
  printed), $p=n_eT_e+(n_{\mathrm{bg}}+n_{\mathrm{imp}})T_i$:
  $$\begin{aligned}\partial_tp=&-\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p-\Gamma p\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\frac{\Gamma-1}{\mu_0^2R^2}\eta j^2+\nabla\cdot(\kappa_\perp\nabla_\perp T+\kappa_\parallel\nabla_\parallel T)+(\Gamma-1)\nu_\parallel[\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}v_\parallel]^2\\&-(\Gamma-1)P_{\mathrm{rad}}+\tfrac{\Gamma-1}{2}|\mathbf v|^2(S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}})-(\text{ionization potential-energy terms}),\end{aligned}$$
  $P_{\mathrm{rad}}=n_en_{\mathrm{imp}}L_{\mathrm{rad}}(T_e)$ from open-ADAS
  CE data; Braginskii $\kappa_\parallel\propto T_e^{5/2}$; perpendicular
  viscous heating neglected. Two-temperature version (Eqs. 11–12): ion
  equation with frictional and viscous heating, electron equation with
  Ohmic heating, radiation and ionization, and collisional exchange
  $\propto(\text{e–imp}+\text{e–bg collision rates})(T_i-T_e)$; one $T_i$
  for all ion species.
* **Ablation** (§2.2, Eqs. 13–17). Strongly-shielded neutral-gas-shielding
  law, Sergeev's model for full-impurity pellets (JET), Parks' model for
  mixed pellets (ITER):
  $$G[\mathrm{g\,s^{-1}}]=\lambda(X)\Big(\frac{T_e[\mathrm{eV}]}{2000}\Big)^{5/3}\Big(\frac{r_p[\mathrm{cm}]}{0.2}\Big)^{4/3}\big(n_e[10^{14}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]\big)^{1/3},\qquad\lambda(X)=27.08+\tan(1.49X),$$
  $X$ the D₂ molar fraction, split into Ne and D₂ rates by molar masses
  (20.18 and 4.028 g mol⁻¹); Ar/D₂ pellets assumed to separate into pure
  fragments ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Ar}}=36.63$, $\lambda_{\mathrm D_2}=39.00$).
  All page-local symbols.
* **Fragments** (Eq. 18). Statistical fragmentation:
  $P(r_p)\propto r_pK_0(r_p/\ell_p)$, normalized by $\ell_p^2$, $\ell_p$ a
  characteristic size; uniform velocities in the stated range; 20° cone.
* **Deposition** (Eq. 19): Gaussian in $(R,Z)$ around each fragment with
  width 2 cm and in $\phi$ with width 1 rad, "an already somewhat
  toroidally relaxed plasma cloud", forced by the toroidal resolution and
  "may result in artificially mitigated toroidal peaking".
* **Radiation** optically thin (questionable near the fragments).
* **Equilibria** (§2.4, Fig. 3). JET-like (pulse 85943 at 62.4 s): $q_0=0.98$,
  $B\simeq3$ T, $I_p\simeq2$ MA, $T_e(0)\simeq3.28$ keV,
  $n_e(0)\simeq2.1\times10^{19}$ m⁻³. ITER hydrogen L-mode:
  $B\simeq2.65$ T, $I_p\simeq7.5$ MA, $T_e(0)\simeq6.05$ keV,
  $n_e(0)\simeq4.5\times10^{19}$ m⁻³, $W_{\mathrm{th}}\approx33.3$ MJ,
  $q_0$ slightly above 1 with two $q=1$ surfaces; outer-midplane
  injectors.
* **Discretization, time step, solver, harmonics, cost: not given**
  (Bézier FEM by citation; spectra up to $n=6$ shown). Runs on MARCONI and
  the Tianhe-3 prototype.

# Tests and results

| Case | Impurity | H isotope | Fragments | Speed [m/s] | Symmetry | Temperatures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JET shot 1 | $1.5\times10^{22}$ Ar | – | 100 | 200±50 | mono | 1 |
| ITER shot 1 | $4\times10^{21}$ Ne | $3.6\times10^{22}$ H | 400 | 150±50 | mono | 1 |

* **0D CE test** (Fig. 2): after about 0.1 ms CE and non-equilibrium
  radiation agree (Ne ≈$3\times10^8$, Ar ≈$4\times10^9$ W m⁻³, read).
* **JET shot 1**: TQ at about 2.00 ms; $I_p$ spike to about 2.12 MA; about
  $0.3\times10^{21}$ Ar assimilated by 2.3 ms (read); core argon mixing at
  about 2.29 ms.
* **ITER shot 1**: fragments arrive at about 0.8 ms; $l_i$ rises from
  about 1.0 to 1.05 until the TQ at 3.50 ms. **Shot 2**: TQ at about 1.69
  ms. **Shot 3**: $|T_e-T_i|$ up to 2 keV at 4.56 ms; peaking values in
  claim 13. **Shot 4**: TQ at about 4.1 ms, 4/2 island, even $n$ dominant.
  **Shot 5**: TQ at about 4.2 ms, peaking flattened by 4.24–4.36 ms.
* No convergence study, no code-to-code benchmark; comparisons with JET,
  DIII-D, KSTAR and NIMROD are qualitative.
* The review statement of [Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md)
  (§6.2.4) that the radiation peaking factor exceeds 2 in the pre-TQ and
  early TQ and that multiple injectors reduce it summarizes this paper.
  Its §6.2.1 statement that impurity SPI usually triggers the TQ at
  $q=2$ is qualified here by the ITER $q=1$ trigger, which the paper ties
  to the $q$ profile.
