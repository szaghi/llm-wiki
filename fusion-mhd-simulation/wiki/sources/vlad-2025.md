# Summary

Despite the title and its "brief review" label, the paper is mainly an
original code comparison: the first nonlinear benchmark of four codes for
the self-consistent interaction of energetic particles (EPs) with shear
Alfvén waves. HYMAGYC couples linear resistive MHD to gyrokinetic PIC EPs by
pressure coupling; MEGA couples nonlinear MHD to drift-kinetic EPs (optional
ad-hoc gyro-average) by current coupling; ORB5 is a global electromagnetic
gyrokinetic PIC code for all species; XTOR-K couples two-fluid MHD to full-f,
full-orbit PIC ions by pressure coupling (see
[hybrid kinetic-MHD](/methods/hybrid-kinetic-mhd.md) and
[pressure and current coupling](/methods/pressure-and-current-coupling.md)).
The case is the NLED-AUG reference (AUG #31213, $t=0.84$ s, fully shaped,
CHEASE-rescaled to $q_0\simeq2.39897$) with the off-axis-peaked EP density,
a single $|n|=1$, isotropic Maxwellian deuterium EPs at $T_h=93$ keV, nominal
parameters only. Fluid nonlinearities are stated to be omitted, so the
comparison isolates wave–particle nonlinearity; it continues the linear
benchmark of Vlad et al. (2021). In the linear phase all codes find the same
mode, an energetic particle mode (EPM) dominated by $m=2$ near $s\approx0.2$
where the EP density gradient is positive; ORB5's growth rate is about 1.6–1.7
times MEGA's and its frequency lower. Nonlinearly all four agree
qualitatively: the EPM saturates and fills the hollow EP core, and later a
subdominant external TAE ($m=2,3$, $s\approx0.4$) of opposite frequency sign
appears, driven where the gradient is negative. They differ quantitatively:
the EPM chirps down in HYMAGYC and MEGA but up in ORB5; MEGA's saturated
energy is ≈15 times below HYMAGYC's and its EP redistribution much weaker;
the TAE onset delay differs (≈0.1 vs >0.2 ms); edge transport is strong in
ORB5 and XTOR-K and barely visible in HYMAGYC and MEGA. Candidate causes
(coupling scheme, fast-ion inertia, particle boundary conditions) are listed
but not quantified.

# Key claims

1. **Qualitative agreement** (Abstract, §7; Figs. 2, 8, 10–13). The four
   codes agree qualitatively in the nonlinear phase "despite the different
   physical models considered". Shown.
2. **Same linear mode** (§3, §5, §6; footnote 1). All codes find an EPM near
   the axis, $m=2$ dominant, peaking at $s\approx0.2$ (ORB5), driven by the
   positive EP density gradient. Shown.
3. **TAE renamed EPM** (footnote 1, p. 5). The mode called a TAE in Vlad et
   al. (2021) is an EPM, because a single $m=2$ harmonic dominates instead of
   the $m,m+1$ pair of a TAE, although its frequency is "almost within the
   shear Alfvén toroidal gap". Stated (reinterpretation).
4. **Linear spread** (§5, Table 2). ORB5 has higher $\gamma$ and lower
   $\omega$ than HYMAGYC and MEGA, with and without FLR. Shown. FOW-only
   $\gamma$ spans 174,606–348,489 s⁻¹ (factor 2.0, HYMAGYC $(s,\theta)$ to
   ORB5); with FLR 170,247–286,400 s⁻¹ (factor 1.68).
5. **FLR lowers $\gamma$** (§4, §7). In MEGA, HYMAGYC $(R,Z)$ and ORB5; §7
   generalizes ("typically, retaining FLR effects results in smaller growth
   rates and smaller saturation amplitudes"). Shown, but in HYMAGYC
   $(s,\theta)$ $\gamma$ rises with FLR (174,606 → 199,841 s⁻¹), so the
   general claim fails for one of four rows.
6. **FLR effect on frequency inconsistent** (§4). FLR lowers the frequency in
   MEGA (171.43 → 157.00 kHz), leaves HYMAGYC $(R,Z)$ almost unchanged
   (162.42 → 163.00) and raises ORB5 (127.09 → 145.24). Shown.
7. **Saturation and redistribution** (§3, §7, Fig. 13). The EPM saturates at
   $t\omega_{A}\simeq700$ (0.14 ms) in HYMAGYC, filling the hollow core and
   lowering the mid-radius peak; "MEGA looks to have much lower transport
   than the other three codes". Shown.
8. **Chirping direction** (§3 Fig. 3, §4, §5). The EPM chirps down in HYMAGYC
   and MEGA, following the lower continuum of the toroidal gap as its peak
   moves slightly outward; in ORB5 it chirps up into the TAE gap, "opposite
   to the one observed by both HYMAGYC and MEGA". XTOR-K's chirping is not
   reported. Shown (a disagreement).
9. **Late external TAE** (§3–§7). All codes see a subdominant TAE in the
   outer region ($s\approx0.4$, $m=2,3$), counter-rotating relative to the
   EPM, driven by the negative EP gradient, non-chirping (HYMAGYC), visible
   as a second growth stage of the energy. Shown.
10. **TAE onset delay** (§4). ≈0.1 ms after the first saturation in MEGA,
    >0.2 ms in HYMAGYC; MEGA's earlier onset is attributed to the steeper
    outer gradient that survives its weak redistribution. Shown (delay) /
    Stated (explanation).
11. **Saturation amplitude** (§4, Figs. 5, 9). MEGA's volume-integrated energy
    is "considerably lower" than HYMAGYC's, "a factor ≈ 15 for the first
    overshooting". A HYMAGYC run with reduced on-axis EP density still
    redistributes much more than MEGA, so amplitude alone does not explain
    the transport gap. Shown (that run not plotted).
12. **Losses and relaxation** (§3, §6, Fig. 4). HYMAGYC loses ≈15% of EPs as
    first-orbit losses (0–0.040 ms) and ≈20% more by the end; EPs crossing
    the last closed flux surface are not re-injected. MEGA shows no initial
    relaxation. XTOR-K relaxes within ≈0.05 ms toward an ORB5-like profile,
    because a full-f Maxwellian is not a kinetic equilibrium. Shown.
13. **Edge transport** (§5, §7, Fig. 13). Strong in ORB5 and XTOR-K, "only
    barely observable" in HYMAGYC and MEGA; ORB5 and XTOR-K are
    "qualitatively similar", XTOR-K flattening somewhat more. Shown.
14. **FOW vs FLR transport** (§7, Fig. 14). FOW-only runs transport more,
    inward and outward: clear in HYMAGYC ($s\gtrsim0.8$), barely visible in
    MEGA, most pronounced in ORB5 (a "knee" for $s\gtrsim0.8$). Shown.
15. **HYMAGYC stress-tensor numerics** (§3, Fig. 7). Computing
    $\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h$ on the $(s,\theta)$ grid in the nonlinear phase is
    prone to short-scale perpendicular noise and instability, especially
    FOW-only; a Cartesian $(R,Z)$ mesh acts as an intrinsic filter, coarser
    meshes ($121^2$, $181^2$, $361^2$ compared) cutting the poloidal-spectrum
    tails. The FOW $(s,\theta)$ case is unstable in the long saturated phase.
    Shown.
16. **XTOR-K frequencies** (§6). 166 kHz (near-axis mode, early saturation)
    and −196 kHz (late mode), "in good agreement" with the other codes; growth
    rates are buried in full-f noise. Shown, but no nonlinear-phase
    frequencies of the other codes are tabulated to support "good
    agreement".
17. **Candidate causes** (§5, §7). Current coupling (MEGA) vs pressure
    coupling (HYMAGYC, XTOR-K); fast-ion inertia, present in fully
    gyrokinetic but not in hybrid models and relevant at this EP fraction;
    different particle boundary conditions. Stated; none quantified.
18. **Not a validation** (§3, §7). Inward EP transport is seen in AUG
    off-axis-NBI discharges (Lauber 2020), but the setups differ (isotropic
    Maxwellian at 93 keV vs anisotropic slowing-down with 93 keV beams), so
    this is explicitly not a validation. Stated.
19. **Theory and outlook** (§7). Results "consistent with the state of the
    art theoretical understanding" (Chen & Zonca 2016); future work:
    quantitative saturation and phase-space comparisons, a common IMAS
    database, theory checks, AUG validation. Stated.
20. **Convergence** (§3, §5). All numerical parameters "have been checked for
    convergence"; for ORB5 by reference to Vannini (2022). Stated; no data
    shown.

# Models and numerics

**Common setup** (§2, Table 1). NLED-AUG equilibrium (Lauber 2016) from a
standard EQDSK, rescaled with CHEASE to keep $q_0\simeq2.39897$ (toroidal gap
fully open), transformed so that $B_\phi>0$ and $I_p>0$ in COCOS = 2.
$s=\sqrt{|\psi-\psi_0|/|\psi_{\mathrm{edge}}-\psi_0|}$ (the registry $s$;
any $2\pi$ cancels). $Z_in_i=n_e-Z_hn_h$ with $Z_i=Z_h=1$, both species
deuterium. Isotropic Maxwellian EPs, $T_h=0.093$ MeV, flat in radius; since
this is not an equilibrium distribution, HYMAGYC, MEGA and ORB5 solve a
[δf](/methods/delta-f-method.md) equation with the term (unperturbed
phase-space velocity)·∇$f_0$ suppressed, which prevents relaxation; full-f
XTOR-K relaxes. Single $|n|=1$; fluid wave–wave nonlinearity omitted, EP
nonlinearity retained.

**HYMAGYC** (§3, Appendix). Linear full resistive MHD from MARS
(eigenvalue solver converted to initial value), fully implicit backward
Euler; Fourier in poloidal and toroidal angle, generalized finite elements
with the Tunable Integration Method in $s$; equal-arc-length poloidal angle
(Jacobian $\propto R|\nabla\psi|^{-1}$). $S^{-1}=5\times10^{-7}$ with
$S=\mu_0R_0v_A/\eta$, $\Gamma=5/3$, no viscosity. 180 radial points,
$m\in[-3,13]$ for $n=-1$. EPs: nonlinear gyrokinetic Vlasov by PIC to
$O(\epsilon^2)$ and $O(\epsilon\epsilon_B)$ ($\epsilon\sim\rho_L/L_n$,
$\epsilon_B\sim\rho_L/L_B$), δf, pressure coupling (Park et al. 1992) with
$\nabla\cdot\mathsf P_h$ on the $(s,\theta)$ grid or on a new Cartesian
$(R,Z)$ grid ($121^2$ default). PIC grid $180\times120$, gridless
finite-size in $\phi$, 8 toroidal points, 64 markers per cell,
11,059,200 markers; FLR by 32-point gyro-average, FOW-only by gyrocentre
evaluation (drift-kinetic). Gyrocentre equations (Brizard 1990) in
$(\mathbf X,\mu,v_{\parallel p})$, Eqs. A1–A4, in Gaussian-like form with $c$;
the effective potential combines the gyro-averaged $\delta\Phi$, the parallel
and the gyro-tangential $\delta\mathbf A$.

**MEGA** (§4). Nonlinear full MHD with drift-kinetic EPs (Todo 2006),
ad-hoc $N$-point gyro-average for FLR, current coupling, δf;
$S^{-1}=5\times10^{-7}$, $\nu/(R_0v_A)=5\times10^{-7}$, $\Gamma=5/3$. Grid
$(R,\phi,Z)=128\times16\times256$, 8,388,608 markers (16 per cell); output in
flux coordinates with $n\in\{-1,0,1\}$, $m\in[0,64]$. Time scheme not stated.

**ORB5** (§5). Global nonlinear electromagnetic gyrokinetic PIC for bulk
ions, EPs and electrons (collisions and sources off); δf markers pushed by
RK4 along gyrocentre characteristics $(\mathbf X,v_{\parallel p},\mu)$;
straight-field-line poloidal angle; quasi-neutrality and parallel Ampère's law
with finite elements (typically cubic B-splines); field-aligned Fourier
filter $m\in[-nq(s)\pm\Delta m]$; mixed-variable pullback for the
cancellation problem. Grid $2000\times144\times48$; $3\times10^7$ ion and EP
markers, $1.2\times10^8$ electron markers.

**XTOR-K** (§6). Two-fluid equations with ion and electron temperatures,
coupled to full-f PIC kinetic ions through a kinetic pressure term in the
perpendicular momentum equation. Fluid: fully implicit preconditioned
Newton–Krylov; ions: Boris–Buneman full orbit, 24 steps per gyration, 16
particle steps per fluid step; both nested in a Picard iteration at every
fluid step. Quasi-neutrality enforced each step. Fluid mesh $(s,\theta,\phi)$
with FFT in both angles and linear finite differences in $s$, $204\times64\times8$;
kinetic part toroidal FFT and Cartesian $(R,Z)$, $400\times400\times8$;
$3.072\times10^8$ markers. Radial decomposition (fluid); domain cloning or
decomposition (kinetic). For this benchmark the fluid model is restricted to
ideal MHD with very small viscosity and only the $n=1$ part of each field
increment is kept.

**Compute.** Marconi, Piz Daint, GENCI; no costs; no GPU use reported.
