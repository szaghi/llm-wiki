# Summary

The paper reports nonlinear 3D resistive-MHD simulations with
[NIMROD](/software/nimrod.md) of the two disruption-mitigation systems planned
for SPARC (compact high-field D–T tokamak, ≈8.7 MA): massive gas injection (MGI)
of a 90/10 D₂/Ne mixture from several valves (see
[shattered pellet and massive material injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md)),
and the passive $n=1$ runaway-electron mitigation coil (REMC). NIMROD's
charge-state-resolved neon model (one continuity equation per charge state,
non-equilibrium rates) is fed by a zero-temperature neutral source between the
separatrix and the boundary (Gaussian in poloidal angle, von Mises in toroidal
angle, 4 eV floor temperature outside the separatrix). Four valve layouts (6- and
4-valve, "resonant" and "non-resonant") compared at low resolution ($n\le10$,
toroidally broad source) all give radiated fractions above 95% and a TQ
[radiation peaking factor](/definitions/radiation-asymmetry.md) of at most 1.33;
the 6-valve non-resonant layout, lowest in every phase, was selected for the
SPARC system. Higher-resolution runs ($n\le21$, a source four times narrower,
20% of the D₂ restored to the source) give a later
[thermal quench](/definitions/thermal-quench.md) with a larger, narrower
radiation flash (≈65–70 GW), TQ peaking below 1.5 and peak emissivity in a single
helical flux tube at mid-radius, where 1/1 flows begin to pull neon in from the
edge. Ray tracing with Cherab gives a first-wall flux above 1 GW/m² for ≈100 µs
and an energy impact of ≈20 MJ m⁻² s$^{-1/2}$, below the melt limit of tungsten heavy
alloy. With the REMC added, a closed-circuit coil carrying current from $t=0$
triggers the TQ earlier and raises the radiated fraction from 95% to 99%; the
coil keeps the field stochastic only while $q_0<2$, and when $q_0$ crosses 2
depends strongly on how the region between the limiter and the vessel is
parametrized, a model sensitivity the authors flag for better treatment.

# Key claims

Symbols: $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ (registry symbol with this paper's definition) the
cumulative (radiated + Ne ionization) energy over the cumulative lost (thermal +
magnetic) energy; page-local $t_{\mathrm{TQ}}$,
$t_{\mathrm{CQ}}$ onset times of TQ and CQ; "upper TPF" the peaking factor of the
radiation integrated over the upper half of the volume.

1. **All layouts mitigate** (Abstract; §II C, Fig. 4, Table II). The four MGI
   layouts give $f_{\mathrm{rad}}>95\%$ and TQ $\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}<1.5$.
   Shown.
2. **Low-resolution time history** (§II C, Fig. 4). A 2 ms pre-TQ with
   $P_{\mathrm{rad}}$ (volume-integrated) rising from 5 to 15 GW, then a TQ flash of
   40–55 GW; in the CQ Ohmic heating rises to match radiation. Non-resonant layouts
   have slightly shorter TQ times than resonant ones, 6-valve shorter than 4-valve;
   peak radiation is "unsystematic". Shown.
3. **Even symmetry suppresses the $n=1$** (§II C, Figs. 4c–e). In the 4-valve
   resonant layout the large $n=1$ at the end of the TQ is absent ($n=1$ magnetic
   energy more than ten times smaller, "predominantly even symmetry is preserved
   until the CQ phase"), there is no $I_p$ spike, connection lengths at 3 ms are
   several times larger and $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ shows no dip; in the other three
   $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ drops by "a few %" when $n=1$ peaks, as heat is conducted to
   the boundary during the [current spike](/definitions/disruption.md). Shown.
4. **Peaking factors** (§II C, Table II). See Tests and results; 6-valve
   non-resonant is lowest in all phases. The broad toroidal source underpredicts the
   peaking, especially pre-TQ, so the values serve relative comparison only. Shown.
5. **Layout choice** (§II C, §IV). The 6-valve non-resonant layout (three upper,
   three lower valves, rows clocked by 60°) was chosen on these runs, complementary
   M3D-C1 runs (Kleiner et al. 2025), port allocation and engineering constraints.
   Stated.
6. **Localized, D-richer source** (§II D, Fig. 5). A later TQ and a narrower,
   larger radiation peak, "increasing from 50 to 55 GW to 70 GW", with higher
   radiated fractions. Shown.
7. **Peaking of neon reflects the source** (§II D, Fig. 6). Most Ne stays neutral
   outside the separatrix, so the total-Ne peaking factor mirrors the source: the
   non-resonant case (six toroidal locations) has about half that of the resonant
   case (three). This does not mean lower radiation near each valve: the upper TPF
   is nearly identical up to 0.25 ms. Ionized Ne has a peaking factor ≈1 within
   0.5 ms. Shown.
8. **Radiation patterns** (§II D, Fig. 6). The resonant run shows an $n=3$
   radiation pattern in every phase, with an $n=1$ overlay at the TQ from the large
   1/1 mode; the non-resonant run shows a slight $n=6$ variation early and $n=1$
   dominance at the TQ; in both the TQ peaking upticks but stays ≤1.5. Shown.
9. **Peak emissivity in a helical flux tube** (§II D, Fig. 7). At the
   radiated-power peak the dominant emissivity is at mid-radius and poloidally
   localized, neither near a valve nor on axis, in a single helical flux tube at
   $q\approx1.05$ (no $q=1$ surface exists), coinciding with neon blobs pulled in by
   1/1 flows: "The peak radiation occurs at the very start of the inward radial
   mixing of Ne and not when the Ne is well mixed or has reached the axis." Shown.
   (Cf. the non-resonant kink of [resistive internal kink](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md).)
10. **First-wall load** (§II D, Fig. 8). Cherab ray tracing: peak radiant flux
    $q_w>1$ GW/m² for ≈100 µs; energy impact $\Delta Q\approx20$ MJ m⁻² s$^{-1/2}$
    (continuous form "from [Hu 2024]"), "near-to but below the critical value" for
    melting and vaporization of tungsten heavy alloy (the SPARC limiter material);
    melting of pure W, critical value 50 MJ m⁻² s$^{-1/2}$, is "unlikely in any SPARC
    mitigated disruption". Numbers Shown; limits Stated (no WHA value printed).
11. **Radiation spectrum** (Appendix, Table III, Fig. 14). Over 75% of the TQ-peak
    radiation lies between 16 and 35 eV (35–77 nm), close to the coronal Ne
    radiation peak near 30 eV (Post et al. 1977); 96 of 399 OPEN-ADAS lines are
    non-negligible (0.40 MW–11.7 GW), summing to 70.1 GW against NIMROD's 65 GW.
    Shown.
12. **Closed-circuit REMC** (§III B, Figs. 10–11). Small $n=1$ perturbations in the
    pre-TQ, smaller than the MGI-driven $n=3$, cause "earlier growth and saturation
    of the large unstable $n=1$ mode", hence an earlier TQ with similar peak
    amplitude, a more pronounced brief peaking at the flash, and $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$
    (ionization included) at the end of the TQ of 99% against 95%. Shown.
13. **Switch-activated REMC** (§III B, Fig. 10). With the coil switched on at the
    $I_p$ spike, the large $n=1$ and higher-$n$ modes first decay and then regrow
    from ≈4 ms under the applied field. Shown.
14. **Ex-limiter parameters change the radiation peak** (§III C, Fig. 9b–c). The
    plasma-like case has the largest peak; enhancing resistivity and mass density
    together gives two peaks: the heavier ex-limiter plasma forestalls the 1/1, so
    higher-$n$ modes collapse the mid-radius temperature first and the $I_p$ spike
    comes later. Shown.
15. **Ex-limiter parameters change the core $q$** (§III C, Fig. 12), "perhaps
    surprisingly, close to the core": plasma-like, $q=2$ moves in and pauses at
    $\psi_N=0.25$ and $q_0>2$ only ≈1.5 ms after the spike; enhanced resistivity,
    $q_0>2$ almost at the spike; enhanced resistivity and mass density, as
    plasma-like; closed-circuit coil, as plasma-like, shifted. The mechanism is "not
    immediately clear". Shown; mechanism not explained.
16. **REMC requires $q_0<2$** (§III C, Fig. 13). Fields are fully stochastic during
    the TQ in every case; once $q_0>2$ two islands appear and coalesce into a core
    of good surfaces. Up to 4.6 ms the REMC prevents a good-surface core that forms
    without it, supporting the earlier finding (Izzo et al. 2022, 2024) that the
    $n=1$ coil needs $q_0<2$. Shown.
17. **Avalanche-gain estimate** (§IV). At a CQ rate of 1.75 MA/ms, even 1.5 ms of
    coil-driven [runaway](/definitions/runaway-electrons.md) deconfinement "could
    result in a $10^3$ reduction in the overall avalanche gain". Stated (no
    derivation).
18. **Main approximations** (§IV). High viscosity and constant $\kappa_\parallel$,
    both needed for convergence and stability. Lower viscosity would likely give
    faster impurity mixing and less peaked, more core-localized radiation
    (favourable); constant $\kappa_\parallel$ overestimates transport in cold
    regions against $T^{5/2}$, which tends to underpredict peaking (unfavourable)
    but overestimate conducted wall energy. Stated.
19. **Agreement with M3D-C1** (§II C, §IV). For 6-valve cases the codes show "very
    similar levels of peak radiated power in the TQ and comparably high radiated
    energy fractions" (different runs and approximations). Stated (no M3D-C1 numbers
    printed).
20. **Vertical stability** (§II C, §III A). SPARC is expected to be passively
    vertically stable with a ≈50 ms wall time, so little vertical motion is
    expected in the TQ; if the plasma moves in the CQ, its REMC coupling first
    increases, peaking at 35 cm off the midplane (Battey et al.). Attributed.

# Models and numerics

**Code and model** (§II A). NIMROD (Sovinec et al. 2004): 2D high-order finite
elements in the poloidal plane × finite Fourier series in $\phi$; run here as
single-fluid resistive MHD with impurities. **No equations are printed**; the
model is described in words with reference to Izzo (2013). One continuity
equation per Ne charge state with the centre-of-mass velocity; radiation and
ionization energy sinks; local $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$-dependent resistivity. Neutrals
(Ne and D) are deposited at zero temperature, "do nothing except diffuse and
ionize" with a diffusivity of 10 m²/s, and on ionization equilibrate instantly with
the background (dilution cooling). The radiation model is called "coronal
non-equilibrium" (charge states not assumed in equilibrium); a newer unpublished
version uses ADAS collisional-radiative rates
([collisional-radiative model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md)).

**Source** (§II A, Fig. 1). Radially flat between the boundary and the
separatrix with a tanh drop at the separatrix; Gaussian poloidally (half-width
6.4°); von Mises toroidally with concentration 40.5 (≈9° half-width) at high
resolution, four times broader at low resolution. Total $10^{23}$ D₂ molecules
and $1.11\times10^{22}$ Ne atoms, shared evenly among valves over 2 ms; the run
starts when the gas reaches the separatrix (valve transit 1.3 ms). Low
resolution: 10% of the D₂ ($2\times10^{22}$ D) is taken as assimilated uniformly
at $t=0$ and the rest discarded (target density ≈3.7× flat-top). High resolution
and REMC: 20% of the D ($4\times10^{22}$) is restored to the localized source and
10% of the rest assimilated at $t=0$. Floor temperature 4 eV (no energy for
ionization or radiation at the floor; recombination below it neglected).

**Coefficients** (§II C). $\eta=3.39\times10^{-4}Z_{\mathrm{eff}}\min\{\max\{T_e[\mathrm{eV}],4\},2000\}^{-3/2}$ Ω m;
isotropic and parallel kinematic viscosity $\nu=500$ m²/s; constant
$\kappa_\parallel=10^8$ and $\kappa_\perp=10$ m²/s (diffusivities);
hyper-resistivity $\eta_h=10^{-6}$ m⁴/s; density diffusivity of ionized species
$D=2.0$ m²/s (Sec. II) or 5.0 m²/s (Sec. III).

**Resolution and cost** (Table I, §II D).

| Section | radial × poloidal elements | degree | $n_{\max}$ | $D$ [m²/s] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| II C (low) | 56 × 72 | 4 | 10 | 2.0 |
| II D (high) | 56 × 72 | 4 | 21 | 2.0 |
| III (REMC) | 96 × 128 | 3 | 21 | 5.0 |

High resolution: 1408 CPUs, ≈48 h wall clock; low: 704 CPUs, ≈24 h. High-resolution
runs stop just after the TQ.

**Wall and coil** ([resistive-wall coupling](/methods/resistive-wall-coupling.md)).
Sec. II: resistive wall on $n=1$–6 with $\mu_0\eta_w/d_w=40$ m/s; ideal on $n=0$
(no vertical motion) and on $n=7$–10 for stability. Sec. III: smoothed SPARC
vessel boundary, resistive for $n>0$, ideal for $n=0$. The REMC enters as
time-dependent normal-field boundary conditions reproducing the coil perturbation
at the last closed surface (cf. [RMP](/definitions/resonant-magnetic-perturbation.md));
the wall is transparent to it but resistive to the plasma response. The coil
amplitude grows with the $I_p$ decay through a ThinCurr coil-current versus
plasma-current mapping; closed-circuit (from $t=0$) or switch-activated (at the
$I_p$ spike).

**Ex-limiter region** (§III A). Between limiter and vessel the same equations
are solved with parameters changed through a tanh transition: plasma-like,
resistivity ×1000, or resistivity and mass density ×1000 (the latter inhibits
flows; momentum conservation can break if charged particles cross the interface).
In the divertor regions plasma-like parameters extend to the boundary, so
[halo currents](/definitions/halo-current.md) can reach it. This contrasts with
the separate plasma and vacuum regions of Sovinec & Bunkers (2019).

**Equilibrium** (Fig. 3). SPARC target with very flat core $q$,
$q_{\min}=1.05$; on-axis pressure ≈1.5 MPa and $I_p\approx8.7$–8.8 MA (figure
readings; the text says "9MA"). Post-processing: Cherab ray tracing, OPEN-ADAS
PECs (nearest-neighbour interpolation), OMFIT.

# Tests and results

| $\mathrm{TPF}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ / time | 6 non-res. | 6 res. | 4 non-res. | 4 res. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-TQ | 1.07 | 1.09 | 1.39 | 1.28 |
| $t_{\mathrm{TQ}}$ [ms] | 2.16 | 2.43 | 2.10 | 2.52 |
| TQ | 1.21 | 1.26 | 1.33 | 1.33 |
| $t_{\mathrm{CQ}}$ [ms] | 2.58 | 2.70 | 2.76 | 2.82 |
| CQ | 1.11 | 1.41 | 1.51 | 1.47 |

* Low resolution: $W_{\mathrm{th}}\approx22$–23 MJ initially; $n=1$ magnetic
  energy up to ≈10⁶ J; $f_{\mathrm{rad}}$ 0.95–0.99, ionization a few %.
* High resolution: ≈26 MJ lost by ≈2.7 ms, almost all radiated; total-Ne peaking
  ≈4.5 (resonant) against ≈2.3 (non-resonant); radiation peaking from ≈3–4
  initially to ≈1–1.5. Near the upper injector at $\phi=60°$: $q_w\approx0.2$–0.4
  GW/m² pre-TQ, ≈1.2 GW/m² near 2.6 ms, $\Delta Q\to\approx20$ MJ m⁻² s$^{-1/2}$.
* Spectrum: the strongest lines are 25.36 eV (48.95 nm, 11.7 GW) and 22.87 eV
  (54.28 nm, 11.4 GW); 13 lines above 1 GW between 16.05 and 71.93 eV.
* REMC runs: $\delta B/B=\sqrt{W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(n)}/W_{\mathrm{mag}}^{(0)}}$ for
  $n=1$–3 shows $n=3$ (MGI-driven), then $n=1$, then $n=2$; Poincaré plots at
  4.60 ms with connection lengths up to 2×10⁴ transits.
* No convergence study: the low/high comparison changes source width, D content
  and $n_{\max}$ together.
