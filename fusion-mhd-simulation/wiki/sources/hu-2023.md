# Summary

The paper simulates neon–deuterium
[shattered pellet injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md)
(SPI) into an ITER 15 MA / 5.3 T L-mode with 3D nonlinear
[JOREK](/software/jorek.md) in its two-temperature
[reduced-MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md) form with the
[collisional-radiative impurity model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md).
The equilibrium has a large $q<1$ region (to $\psi_N\simeq0.3$). The
injection is $5\times10^{22}$ Ne plus $1.8\times10^{24}$ D in 300
fragments at 500 or 200 m/s (±40%) from the outboard midplane, ablating by
a neutral-gas-shielding law with a magnetic reduction factor. The paper
confirms in 3D the "size effect" predicted by 1.5D/2D work (Matsuyama et
al. 2022): fragments reach the core before the edge radiative collapse,
the post-injection region stays at $O(100\,\mathrm{eV})$ and the
axisymmetric current contraction is negligible. The
[thermal quench](/definitions/thermal-quench.md) onset (core relaxation)
is triggered when vanguard fragments cross $q=1$ and destabilize the
[1/1 resistive kink](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md), with
convective density mixing, a "cold bubble" and core temperature
relaxation. A transport barrier in the stochastic field just outside
$q=1$ blocks full relaxation and breaks only after about 6 ms in the
200 m/s case, when edge modes grow. Slower injection gives stronger
radiative collapse and larger MHD amplitude. Transient 3D "electron
temperature holes" form around the fragment plume because the Braginskii
$\kappa_\parallel$ collapses in the cold, radiating region; they raise the
resistivity on the field lines through the plume, amplify the MHD response
and motivate a "non-local" ablation scheme with flux-surface-averaged
$T_e$, $n_e$. Sensitivity studies (ablation scheme, magnetic factor,
toroidal deposition width, background Be/W, constant against Braginskii
$\kappa_\parallel$) show self-regulated assimilation and more variable
radiated power; radiation from toroidally elongated sources is a lower
bound.

# Key claims

1. **Core before edge collapse** (§3.2, Fig. 8a; §5). Fragments can reach
   the core before the edge radiative collapse and before strong MHD and
   stochastic transport, "especially for the 500m/s fast injection case";
   the post-injection region stays at $O(100\,\mathrm{eV})$, consistent
   with 1.5D/2D results. Shown.
2. **Size effects** (§2.4). The fragment number is roughly independent of
   pellet size (characteristic fragment size $\propto$ pellet length,
   Eq. 19), hence total ablation rate $\propto N_0^{4/9}B^{-0.842}$ and
   ablation time $\propto N_0^{5/9}B^{0.842}$, $N_0$ the atoms injected;
   at fixed $N_0/V$ with $V\propto R_0^3$ the ablation time scales as
   $R_0^{5/3}B^{0.842}$. Current redistribution scales with the square of
   the redistribution length, fragment flight with the length, so large
   devices respond more slowly. Derived (scaling argument).
3. **Negligible $n=0$ contraction** (§3.1, Fig. 10). It plays a "minimal
   role" in the pre-TQ and early TQ; only "jagged" current perturbations
   at rational surfaces appear before core relaxation. Shown.
4. **1/1-kink trigger** (§3.1, Figs. 3–4). Core relaxation starts when
   vanguard fragments enter $q=1$; the 1/1 resistive kink, flux
   displacement and a "cold bubble" relax the temperature inside $q=1$;
   $n=1$ dominates. Shown.
5. **Convective mixing** (§3.1, Figs. 6–7). Kink convection moves density
   cells $O(1\,\mathrm m)$ in 0.2 ms while fragments travel 0.1 m; a 2D
   axisymmetric twin keeps a hollow $n_e$. Shown.
6. **Slow injection** (§3.1–3.2, Figs. 4, 8, 9). 200 m/s gives stronger
   radiative cooling at equal penetration and larger MHD amplitude at core
   relaxation than 500 m/s, with comparable neon assimilation. Shown.
7. **Multi-stage TQ and energy budget** (§3.1, Fig. 5; 200 m/s,
   non-local, toroidal width 0.3 rad). At $t\simeq4.85$ ms the thermal
   loss exceeds the radiated energy by 6 MJ; parallel kinetic and
   ionization energies are each $O(1\,\mathrm{MJ})$; by about 6.4 ms 25 of
   36 MJ are lost with a radiated fraction "about 80%". Shown.
8. **Stochastic transport barrier outside $q=1$** (§3.3, Fig. 13a,d).
   Stochastic field lines inside $q=1$ stay confined for 2000 toroidal
   turns, attributed to the stiff 1/1 eigenfunction vanishing outside
   $q=1$ and weak 2/1, 3/1 edge modes, so that "some good KAM surfaces …
   might survive". Shown; interpretation Stated.
9. **Barrier breakdown** (§3.3, Figs. 13c,f, 14, 15; §5). By $t\approx6$
   ms (200 m/s) the edge $n=1,2$ modes grow, mixing reaches the whole
   volume and connection lengths to the wall drop to $\sim10^4$ m; by then
   2/3 of the initial thermal energy is radiated; the 500 m/s case "may
   take on the order of 10ms". Shown for 200 m/s; Stated for 500 m/s.
10. **Electron temperature holes** (§3.2, Figs. 11–12). Transient,
    localized $T_e$ minima along field lines through the plume, sustained
    by strong local radiation and a low Braginskii $\kappa_\parallel$ in
    the cold dense region; absent with constant $\kappa_\parallel$ (the
    500 eV Braginskii value). Shown.
11. **Collisionality check** (§3.2, Eq. 20). The line density needed for
    collisional transport into the hole, $v_{th,e}n_e/\nu_{ee}\simeq
    2\times10^{21}$ m⁻², against $\int n_e\,dl\simeq2.4\times10^{21}$ m⁻²
    across the half-width: "barely collisional". Derived (estimate).
12. **Holes amplify MHD** (§3.2, §4.4, Fig. 21). Higher resistivity along
    the hole field lines enhances current expulsion, so Braginskii runs
    have stronger MHD than constant-$\kappa_\parallel$ runs. Shown.
13. **Self-regulated assimilation** (§4.1, Figs. 16, 18). Non-local
    ablation and removing the magnetic factor change neon assimilation
    mildly but raise radiated power strongly; radiation is not
    self-limiting before the collapse. 3D non-local assimilation converges
    to the 2D result; 3D local ablation assimilates less (the hole cuts
    ablation), its density localization and reduced ablation
    "approximately cancel" in radiation before 2.5 ms. Shown.
14. **Toroidal localization** (§4.2, Fig. 19; §5). Narrowing the toroidal
    deposition width from 0.5 to 0.3 rad raises radiation at equal
    assimilation, but "less than proportional", attributed to parallel
    relaxation and a "plasmoid conglomerate"; radiation from toroidally
    elongated sources "should be considered as a lower estimation". Shown
    (trend); explanation Stated. [Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md)
    (§2.8) state an inverse scaling of radiated power with the toroidal
    width: same direction, weaker dependence here.
15. **Background impurities negligible** (§4.3, Fig. 20). Uniform Be
    ($3.5\times10^{17}$ m⁻³) and W ($3.5\times10^{11}$ m⁻³) in coronal
    equilibrium radiate about $10^3$ times less than the injected neon;
    W gives 0.1% of the background radiation. Shown.
16. **Constant $\kappa_\parallel$** (§4.4, Figs. 21–22). Suppresses the
    holes, weakens the MHD response, keeps good surfaces outside $q=1$ at
    2.39 ms and delays the rise of radiated power. Shown.
17. **CR cools more than CE** (§3.1). Stronger radiative cooling than
    coronal equilibrium through the larger fraction of low neon charge
    states. Stated (against earlier CE runs; no side-by-side run).
18. **Transient $T_e$ rise at kink onset** (§3.2), "possibly a result of
    asymmetric flux tube compression"; needs full MHD and possibly
    kinetic effects. Stated.
19. **Reduced MHD and the 1/1 mode** (§2.2). Reduced MHD "tends to
    underestimate" the 1/1 resistive-kink instability (Hoelzl et al.
    2021; Graves et al. 2019); without SPI no significant 1/1 growth on
    the time scale of interest. Stated, plus a numerical observation.
20. **H-mode outlook** (§5). Stronger assimilation and local radiation
    peaks expected; at low neon fraction plasmoid drift can reduce
    assimilation; vanguard fragments destabilize the pedestal; a
    post-H-mode target is more realistic. Stated.

# Models and numerics

* **Model** (§2.1, Eqs. 1–11, checked on rendered pages). JOREK reduced
  MHD, two temperatures, CR impurities (collisional ionization and
  excitation, radiative recombination and line radiation), one velocity
  for all species and charge states; JOREK normalized units ($\mu_0$
  absent, never stated), coefficients quoted in SI. With $\mu_0$ restored
  and $v_\parallel$ the true parallel speed:
  $$\mathbf B=F_0\nabla\phi+\nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi,\qquad\mathbf v=v_\parallel\mathbf b-R^2\nabla u\times\nabla\phi,\qquad j=\Delta^*\psi,\quad J_\phi=-j/(\mu_0R),$$
  $$\partial_t\psi=\frac{\eta(T_e)}{\mu_0}\Delta^*\psi-R[u,\psi]-F_0\partial_\phi u,$$
  $$\partial_t\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot(D_\perp\nabla\rho)+S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}},\qquad\partial_t\rho_{\mathrm{imp}}=-\nabla\cdot(\rho_{\mathrm{imp}}\mathbf v)+\nabla\cdot(D_\perp\nabla\rho_{\mathrm{imp}})+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}},$$
  $$\begin{aligned}\partial_tp_i=&-\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p_i-\Gamma p_i\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\nabla\cdot(\kappa_\perp\nabla_\perp T_i+\kappa_{\parallel,i}\nabla_\parallel T_i)+\tfrac{\Gamma-1}{2}|\mathbf v|^2(S_{\rho,\mathrm{bg}}+S_{\rho,\mathrm{imp}})\\&+(\Gamma-1)\nu_\parallel[\nabla_{\mathrm{pol}}v_\parallel]^2+(\text{e–i exchange}),\\
  \partial_tp_e=&-\mathbf v\cdot\nabla p_e-\Gamma p_e\nabla\cdot\mathbf v+\nabla\cdot(\kappa_\perp\nabla_\perp T_e+\kappa_{\parallel,e}\nabla_\parallel T_e)+\frac{\Gamma-1}{\mu_0^2R^2}\eta j^2\\&-(\Gamma-1)(P_{\mathrm{rad}}+\text{ionization power density})-(\text{e–i exchange}),\end{aligned}$$
  with $p_i=(n_{\mathrm{bg}}+n_{\mathrm{imp}})T_i$, $p_e=n_eT_e$ and
  exchange proportional to the sum of electron–impurity and
  electron–background collision rates times $(T_i-T_e)$. The vorticity
  equation (Eq. 8, $\varpi=R^{-1}\partial_R(R\partial_Ru)+\partial_Z^2u$)
  and the $\mathbf B$-projected parallel momentum equation (Eq. 9) have
  the JOREK form with the pressure term $[p,R^2]$; the perpendicular
  viscous term is printed $R^3\nu_\perp(T_e)\nabla^2_{\mathrm{pol}}\varpi$.
* **Resistivity** (Eq. 12): $\eta=Z_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{Sp},0}\max(T_e,1\,\mathrm{eV})^{-3/2}\,\frac{1+1.198Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.222Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}{1+2.966Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+0.753Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}$.
* **Charge states** (Eq. 16, intended form):
  $$\frac{dn_{\mathrm{imp},z}}{dt}=n_e\big[\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{ion},z-1}n_{\mathrm{imp},z-1}-(\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{ion},z}+\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{rec},z})n_{\mathrm{imp},z}+\langle\sigma v\rangle_{\mathrm{rec},z+1}n_{\mathrm{imp},z+1}\big],$$
  written in the paper for fractional abundances, open-ADAS rates;
  background neutrals ionized instantly. Radiation (Eq. 17):
  $P_{\mathrm{rad}}=n_e\sum_zn_{\mathrm{imp},z}(L_{\mathrm{rad,line}}+L_{\mathrm{rad,rec}}+L_{\mathrm{rad,brems}})_z$;
  $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$, ionization and radiation power are projected marker
  moments ([particle-to-mesh projection](/methods/particle-to-mesh-projection.md)).
* **Coefficients.** Braginskii $\kappa_\parallel$ per species (variant:
  constant, the 500 eV value); $D_\perp=6$, $\kappa_\perp=10$,
  $\nu_\perp=30$, $\nu_\parallel=150$ m² s⁻¹, "with the initial density".
* **Ablation** (Eqs. 14–15). Neutral-gas shielding, mixed pellets, magnetic
  reduction:
  $$G[\mathrm{g\,s^{-1}}]=\lambda(X)\,\zeta_B\Big(\frac{T_e[\mathrm{eV}]}{2000}\Big)^{5/3}\Big(\frac{r_p[\mathrm{cm}]}{0.2}\Big)^{4/3}\big(n_e[10^{14}\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]\big)^{1/3},\quad\lambda(X)=27.08+\tan(1.49X),\quad\zeta_B\simeq\Big(\frac{2}{\max(B[\mathrm T],2)}\Big)^{0.843},$$
  $X$ the D₂ molar fraction (page-local symbols). "Local" ablation uses
  local $T_e$, $n_e$; "non-local" uses flux-surface averages (long
  mean-free-path hot electrons).
* **Deposition** (Eq. 13): Gaussian per fragment, poloidal width 0.08 m,
  toroidal width 0.3 or 0.5 rad (an artificial elongation forced by the
  toroidal resolution; plume envelope ≈0.5 m resolved; insensitive to the
  poloidal width below the plume width).
* **Fragments** (Eqs. 18–19). Statistical fragmentation, size PDF
  $\propto r_pK_0(r_p/\ell_p)$, with $N_{\mathrm{frag}}\,n_{\mathrm{solid}}\,6\pi^2\ell_p^3=N_0$;
  $\ell_p\simeq1.43$ mm; 300 solid fragments, no gas or droplets; flat
  speed distribution ±40% around 200 or 500 m/s in a 20° cone from the
  outer equatorial port; no rocket effect, drag or size–velocity
  correlation.
* **Discretization.** Toroidal Fourier series with 12 harmonics,
  $n=0$–11 (keeping the source spectrum "up to the first order in strength"
  for 0.3 rad, "third order" for 0.5); flux-aligned X-point grid (Fig. 2);
  [Bézier elements](/methods/bezier-finite-elements.md) not named; time
  integration not described. Ideal wall at the thin-wall-equivalent
  position of the blanket modules.
* **Diagnostics.** Poincaré plots and connection lengths over 2000 turns;
  300 tracers per launch point over 500 turns, mean and variance of
  $\sqrt{\psi_N}$.
* **Runs.** 3D at 500 m/s (local and non-local, 0.5 rad; non-local 0.3;
  non-local 0.3 with constant $\kappa_\parallel$), 3D at 200 m/s
  (non-local 0.3 with and without magnetic factor; non-local 0.5), 2D
  axisymmetric twins at 500 m/s. Costs not given; Tianhe-3F.

# Tests and results

* **Equilibrium** (§2.2, Fig. 1): ITER L-mode, $B=5.3$ T, $I_p=15$ MA,
  central pressure $8.654\times10^4$ Pa, $W_{\mathrm{th}}=36$ MJ,
  $T_{e0}\approx5$ keV, $n_{e0}\approx5.4\times10^{19}$ m⁻³ (read);
  slightly reversed shear, $q_0\approx1.05$, $q_{\min}$ slightly below 1
  near $\psi_N\approx0.1$, $q=1$ at $\psi_N\simeq0.3$.
* **Reference case** (500 m/s, local, 0.5 rad): TQ onset at 2.09 ms
  (Fig. 3), MHD peak about 2.1 ms. **200 m/s non-local**: MHD peaks at
  about 4.8 ms (core relaxation) and 6.2 ms (radiative collapse).
* **Temperature** (Fig. 8): $O(100\,\mathrm{eV})$ plateau at 500 m/s, none
  at 200 m/s; barriers at $R=5$ and 7.5 m (≈$q=1$).
* **Current** (Fig. 10): no $n=0$ contraction before relaxation; then flat
  current inside $q=1$ and a negative sheet outside; late edge-current
  "scraping-off" at 200 m/s.
* **Temperature hole** (Figs. 11–12; 500 m/s non-local, 1.26 ms): at
  $R\simeq7.75$ m, $Z\simeq0.65$ m; $T_e$ from about 1 keV to very low
  values along the hole, $n_e\sim10^{21}$ m⁻³.
* **Assimilation** (Fig. 16b): about $6$–$8\times10^{21}$ Ne by about 3 ms
  at 500 m/s (read) of $5\times10^{22}$ injected; radiated power peaks at
  a few GW (axis units not printed).
* **Background impurities**: radiation about $10^3$ below the total.
* No code-to-code benchmark and no experimental comparison.
* [Hoelzl et al. (2024)](/sources/hoelzl-2024.md) (§3.2, Fig. 8) summarize
  these ITER results (core before edge collapse, barrier outside $q=1$).
  The trigger at $q=1$ qualifies the statement of
  [Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md) (§6.2.1) that impurity
  SPI usually triggers the TQ at $q=2$; the paper itself says that without
  a large $q=1$ surface the 2/1 or 3/2 modes "would dominate instead".
