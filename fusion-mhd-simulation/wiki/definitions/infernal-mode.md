# Infernal mode

An infernal mode is a low-$n$, pressure-driven MHD instability of a tokamak
in which a large pressure gradient lies in a region of **weak magnetic
shear**. Such a region typically forms when a broad, off-axis
[bootstrap current](/definitions/bootstrap-current.md) flattens or reverses
the $q$ profile in the core. Because the shear is weak, many poloidal
harmonics at the same $n$ couple, and the mode peaks on the outer torus like a
[ballooning mode](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md), yet it can be unstable
below the high-$n$ ballooning limit. It has an ideal branch and a resistive
branch; the resistive branch survives where the ideal one is stable. These
statements are the introduction of
[Sato et al. (2024)](/sources/sato-2024.md) (§1), which summarizes the
earlier literature (Manickam 1987, Charlton 1989–1991, Brunetti 2014–2020
and others, as cited) and links the mode experimentally to the poloidal
beta collapse of JT-60U (Ozeki et al. 1993, 1995, as cited). The source
gives no defining equation; the operational signature used below is the
$S$-dependence of the growth rate.

## The $n=3$ cases of Sato et al. (2024)

Two circular-cross-section, weak-central-shear equilibria with
$R_0=2$ m, $B=2$ T on axis, hydrogen at uniform
$n_i=8\times10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$; $q_{\min}\approx1.4$–1.6 near
$s=\sqrt{\psi_N}\approx0.5$ and $q_0\approx2$ (figure reading)
([Sato et al. (2024)](/sources/sato-2024.md), §2, Fig. 1). The $\beta_0=5.7\%$
equilibrium is the one of Ozeki et al. (1993), as cited. Growth rates are
in units of $\tau_A^{-1}$ with $\tau_A=R_0/v_A$, and $S=\mu_0R_0^2/(\eta\tau_A)$
(called "magnetic Reynolds number" in the paper).

- **Ideal infernal mode, $\beta_0=5.7\%$.** Dominant $m=4$ with $m=3,5,6$,
  peaked at $s\approx0.5$. $\gamma$ becomes independent of $S$ at high $S$:
  about 0.028 at $S=10^5$ falling to about 0.006 at $S=10^8$ without
  dissipation, against the ideal eigenvalue $\approx0.0057$ of MINERVA
  (§3.1.1, Figs. 2–3; figure reading). This is the benchmark recorded on
  [MIPS](/software/mips.md).
- **Resistive infernal mode, $\beta_0=4.1\%$.** MINERVA finds the ideal mode
  stable ("not shown here"), but a resistive mode, dominant $m=5$ with
  $m=4,6,7$, is unstable with $\gamma$ falling as $S$ rises: about 0.018 at
  $S=10^5$ to about $5\times10^{-4}$ at $S=10^7$ (§3.1.3, Figs. 7–8).
- **Neighbouring instabilities.** The same equilibria are unstable to $n>3$
  ballooning modes; in MHD the $n=3$ infernal mode saturates before they
  appear (§3.2.2).

## Nonlinear evolution: beta collapse

In MHD both branches flatten the axisymmetric pressure profile. For the
ideal mode at $(\beta_0,S)=(5.7\%,10^8)$ the profile becomes flat for
$s>0.5$, which the authors read as "the beta collapse due to ideal infernal
modes observed in tokamak experiments" (§3.2.1, Figs. 11–13). For the
resistive mode at $(4.1\%,10^7)$ MHD also collapses the core pressure
(§3.2.2, Fig. 16). The $(m,n)=(0,0)$ pressure change is generated by
self-coupling of the dominant $(5,3)$ harmonic (§3.2.2, Fig. 17).

## Effect of kinetic thermal ions

With the bulk ions treated kinetically in MEGA (see
[kinetic thermal ions](/methods/kinetic-thermal-ions.md)):

- **Linear.** When the mode grows fast ($\omega_r\ll\gamma$, $S=10^5$, and
  $10^6$ for the 4.1% case) the growth rate equals the MHD value. When it
  grows slowly ($S=10^8$ at 5.7%, $S=10^7$ at 4.1%) the mode rotates in the
  ion diamagnetic direction with $\omega_r\approx\gamma$, and $\gamma$ drops,
  e.g. from about 0.0045 to 0.0031 at $(5.7\%,10^8)$ with
  $\nu=\kappa=10^{-6}R_0^2/\tau_A$ (§3.1.2–3.1.3, Figs. 4, 7).
- **Mechanism.** The perpendicular ion pressure perturbation, dominated by
  trapped ions, is shifted in the ion diamagnetic direction relative to the
  electron and parallel ion pressures and stays in phase with the radial
  flow; the adiabatic electron pressure lags. At $(4.1\%,10^7)$ the
  pressure-work ratios to the perpendicular kinetic energy are $-0.164$ for
  the electron pressure and $0.585$ for the perpendicular ion pressure,
  against $1.38$ for the MHD pressure (§3.1.3, Figs. 9–10). The loss of
  electron-pressure drive is "one of the factors" reducing $\gamma$;
  trapped-ion precession is suggested as the cause of the shift, and "the
  physical mechanism determining the phase relationship … remains
  unclear".
- **Nonlinear, ideal mode.** Same $n=3$ saturation level with and without
  KTIs, and comparable flattening; KTIs flatten slightly less for $s<0.5$
  (§3.2.1).
- **Nonlinear, resistive mode.** With KTIs the saturated pressure
  perturbation is about half the MHD value and the core $(0,0)$ profile
  stays "almost close to the initial equilibrium value". The halved
  amplitude alone does not explain this: with KTIs the $(5,3)$ radial
  velocity and pressure perturbations are out of phase, which suppresses
  the self-coupled $(0,0)$ drive (§3.2.2, Figs. 14–17). An $n=18$ ballooning
  mode grows faster with KTIs and becomes comparable to $n=3$ linearly, but
  saturates lower; its influence on $n=3$ "appears to be small" (§3.2.2).

## Beta limit

From the two equilibria, MHD gives an infernal beta limit below 4.1%,
while with KTIs it lies between 4.1% and 5.7%; "the MHD model without KTIs
is inadequate" for predicting it
([Sato et al. (2024)](/sources/sato-2024.md), §4). This is a bracket from
two equilibria, not a scan, and the two collapse cases are at different
$S$ ($10^8$ and $10^7$), which the paper does not discuss (recorded on the
Source page).

## Contradictions

None within the bundle: no other ingested source computes infernal modes or
an MHD infernal beta limit. Sato et al.'s claim that MHD underestimates the
limit is directed at MHD-only predictions in the literature they cite
(Ozeki et al. 1993, 1995), which are not ingested.
