# Definitions

Precise domain terms.

* [Ballooning mode](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md) — Pressure-driven, high-n MHD instability localized on the outboard (bad-curvature) side of flux surfaces, whose ideal growth rate rises with n without bound in the absence of stabilizing flows, with its resistive and peeling-ballooning variants in tokamak pedestals and resistive ballooning in high-beta LHD plasmas; the standard linear verification case of edge and stellarator MHD codes.
* [Bootstrap current and its MHD implementation](/definitions/bootstrap-current.md) — The neoclassical parallel current driven by density and temperature gradients in toroidal geometry, and its inclusion in MHD codes as a non-inductive current J_x in Ohm's law toward which the plasma current relaxes resistively; M3D-C1 uses Sauter or Redl fits (mapped to quasi-symmetric stellarators), JOREK a Sauter-evolved toroidal source current, and NIMROD a heuristic neoclassical electron stress that produces the drive without an imposed current.
* [Disruption: phases, current quench and current spike](/definitions/disruption.md) — A disruption is the rapid loss of thermal energy and then of plasma current of a tokamak discharge; it proceeds through a trigger phase, a thermal quench, a transient plasma-current spike and a current quench, and its consequences (heat loads, electromagnetic wall forces, runaway-electron beams) are what nonlinear MHD codes are asked to predict and mitigate.
* [Double tearing mode](/definitions/double-tearing-mode.md) — A tearing mode with two resonant surfaces of the same helicity in a non-monotonic q profile, whose coupled islands can relax the current profile violently; in 3D JOREK simulations of a 4/1 mode in a JET runaway-beam-like plasma the violent, helicity-conserving global relaxation with a ~30% current spike occurs only at intermediate resistivity, set by how fast secondary tearing modes grow relative to the primary.
* [Edge harmonic oscillation and QH-mode](/definitions/edge-harmonic-oscillation.md) — The quiescent H-mode is an ELM-free H-mode in which a saturated low-n kink-peeling mode, seen as the edge harmonic oscillation with frequencies at integer multiples of a fundamental, provides the edge transport that holds the pedestal below the ELM limit; JOREK reproduces it for DIII-D, ASDEX Upgrade and, predictively, HL-3, with access in windows of q95 and without torque input.
* [Edge-localized mode (ELM) and ELM-free regimes](/definitions/edge-localized-mode.md) — Repetitive edge MHD instability of the H-mode pedestal, set off by coupled peeling-ballooning modes, that expels pedestal energy and particles in filaments; with the naturally ELM-free or small-ELM regimes (QH-mode, EDA H-mode, QCE) and the active control methods (RMPs, pellets, vertical kicks) as reproduced by nonlinear MHD codes, including ITER RMP suppression, current-sheet reconnection in peeling-ballooning ELMs and the two-stage crash of resistive drift-ballooning ELMs.
* [Electromagnetic wall forces (vertical and sideways)](/definitions/wall-forces.md) — The electromagnetic wall force is the integral of the Lorentz force over the conducting structures, computed as a Maxwell-stress surface integral; its vertical component is set mainly by the axisymmetric VDE and current quench and, when the current quench is much faster than the wall time, peaks on the wall time after the plasma current has vanished; its horizontal (sideways) component is set by low-n kinks and 3D halo currents, and its toroidal rotation matters for resonance with the vessel.
* [Energetic particle mode (EPM) and frequency chirping](/definitions/energetic-particle-mode.md) — Alfvénic mode whose existence and frequency are set by the energetic particles themselves rather than by a gap eigenmode of the bulk plasma, typically sitting in the shear-Alfvén continuum and dominated by a single poloidal harmonic; nonlinearly EPMs and TAEs sweep their frequency (chirp) through phase-space holes and clumps, the codes of the NLED-AUG benchmark disagree on the sweep direction, and the chirp rate grows with background dissipation as Berk–Breizman theory predicts.
* [Fishbone instability](/definitions/fishbone.md) — Energetic-particle-modified n = 1 internal kink that acquires a finite real frequency through resonance with trapped energetic particles; benchmarked linearly by M3D-C1-K against M3D-K and NIMROD, and, in its non-resonant form at q_min slightly above 1, shown to be raised in frequency, reduced in growth and partly stabilized by thermal-ion Landau damping.
* [Grad–Shafranov equation](/definitions/grad-shafranov-equation.md) — The axisymmetric ideal-MHD force balance J×B = ∇p written as an elliptic equation for the poloidal flux per radian, Δ*ψ = −μ0R²p′(ψ) − FF′(ψ); the nonlinear MHD codes solve it on their own finite-element basis to obtain a discretely balanced initial state.
* [Halo current](/definitions/halo-current.md) — The halo current is the current that flows partly in the scrape-off layer and closes through conducting structures in contact with it; its poloidal magnitude (halo fraction), toroidal peaking factor and 3D filamentary structure set the largest wall forces of VDEs, and its modelling depends on sheath and ion-saturation-current boundary conditions and on how the MHD code lets current cross into the wall.
* [Infernal mode](/definitions/infernal-mode.md) — Low-n pressure-driven MHD instability of a tokamak region with weak magnetic shear and a large pressure gradient, made of many coupled poloidal harmonics at one n, with ideal and resistive branches; nonlinearly it flattens the pressure profile (beta collapse), and kinetic thermal ions reduce its slow growth and suppress the resistive collapse.
* [Interchange modes and the Mercier criterion](/definitions/interchange-mode.md) — Pressure-driven instabilities localized at rational surfaces where unfavourable average curvature beats magnetic shear; their ideal local limit is the Mercier criterion (shear, current, well and geodesic terms), their resistive counterpart the Glasser–Greene–Johnson parameter; in low-shear stellarators they are benchmarked between reduced and full MHD and need parallel flow to be captured at finite compressibility, a resonant ideal interchange collapses the high-beta LHD core in MHD but is stabilized by kinetic thermal ions, and the 1/1 quasi-interchange drives flux pumping in tokamaks.
* [Magnetic flux pumping](/definitions/magnetic-flux-pumping.md) — A saturated (1,1) helical core mode whose dynamo redistributes current so as to clamp the central safety factor at about 1, replacing the sawtooth cycle; found in JOREK for sufficiently peaked pressure (1/1 quasi-interchange), quantified for the ASDEX Upgrade hybrid scenario at a slow negative dynamo of a few mV/m, and seen in M3D-C1 simulations of W7-X after an ECCD-driven crash.
* [Massive gas injection](/definitions/massive-gas-injection.md) — Massive gas injection (MGI) is the disruption-mitigation scheme that puffs a large quantity of deuterium and noble gas through fast valves into the plasma edge; MHD codes represent it by a localized neutral or density source outside or near the separatrix, and its simulated thermal quench proceeds by a tearing-mode cascade from q = 2 inward or by a 1/1 mode that pulls the gas into the core, with toroidal radiation peaking set by the number and arrangement of the valves.
* [MHD dynamo effect](/definitions/mhd-dynamo.md) — The axisymmetric electromotive force produced by correlated non-axisymmetric velocity and field fluctuations, which redistributes the mean current; in nonlinear tokamak simulations a slow negative dynamo of a few mV/m clamps q0 in flux pumping and a fluctuation-induced emf builds counter-current sheets in peeling-ballooning ELMs.
* [Modified Rutherford equation](/definitions/modified-rutherford-equation.md) — The evolution equation for the width of a thin, single-helicity magnetic island in the nonlinear (Rutherford) regime, in which the island grows on the resistive time at a rate set by the tearing index plus bootstrap, curvature (D_R) and other corrections; used in the sources as a fit to simulated island growth, as an NTM growth check and through its time-rescaling invariance.
* [Neoclassical tearing mode](/definitions/neoclassical-tearing-mode.md) — A tearing mode that is classically stable but metastable: once a seed island exceeds a critical width, the loss of the bootstrap current inside the island drives its growth; simulated in NIMROD with heuristic neoclassical closures and a transient boundary perturbation that seeds a 2/1 NTM in a DIII-D ITER-baseline plasma through a time-ordered sequence of three-wave couplings.
* [Plasmoid drift of ablation clouds](/definitions/plasmoid-drift.md) — Plasmoid drift is the major-radius-outward E×B motion of a dense, over-pressured pellet-ablation cloud, polarized vertically by the ∇B drift current and braked by shear Alfvén waves and parallel return currents; in MHD simulations of shattered pellet injection it helps assimilation in some cases and expels material and heats the wall in others, and artificially elongated toroidal sources under-predict it.
* [Plasmoid instability and plasmoid-mediated reconnection](/definitions/plasmoid-instability.md) — Break-up of a thin current sheet at high Lundquist number into chains of plasmoids that make resistive reconnection fast; in nonlinear NIMROD simulations of peeling-ballooning ELMs, axisymmetric counter-current sheets and finger-like edge current sheets form and the latter break into current blobs read as 3D plasmoid-mediated reconnection.
* [Quasi-symmetry](/definitions/quasi-symmetry.md) — Property of a stellarator field whose strength, in Boozer coordinates, depends on the flux label and on a single helical angle combination only, B = B(ψ, Mθ − Nζ); it gives guiding-centre conservation analogous to axisymmetry and lets tokamak neoclassical results, such as bootstrap-current fits, be mapped to the stellarator, but a quasi-axisymmetric reactor configuration optimized without a stability target is violently MHD-unstable at its edge.
* [Resistive internal kink (m/n = 1/1)](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md) — The m/n = 1/1 mode resonant at the q = 1 surface: a rigid helical shift of the core inside q = 1 that resistivity turns into a reconnecting mode with gamma ~ eta^(1/3); its ideal, pressure-driven limit has a poloidal-beta threshold, reduced MHD models fail to reproduce that limit at finite beta, and the mode triggers the thermal quench when SPI fragments reach q = 1.
* [Resistive tearing mode](/definitions/tearing-mode.md) — Current-driven resistive instability that reconnects the magnetic field at a rational surface q = m/n and forms magnetic islands; its linear growth rate follows gamma ~ eta^(3/5) at low resistivity, the standard verification target of nonlinear MHD codes in tokamak and stellarator geometry.
* [Resistive-wall tearing mode](/definitions/resistive-wall-tearing-mode.md) — A resistive-wall tearing mode is a tearing mode that is stable with an ideally conducting wall but unstable because its flux diffuses through a resistive wall; its growth time scales with the wall time, and it is proposed as the cause of the thermal quench in locked-mode disruptions, with a thermal quench lasting about half a wall time.
* [Resonant magnetic perturbations and plasma response](/definitions/resonant-magnetic-perturbation.md) — Small non-axisymmetric fields applied by external or in-vessel coils whose harmonics resonate with edge rational surfaces q = m/n; the plasma response (screening, kink-peeling amplification, island penetration) decides whether they mitigate or suppress ELMs, and nonlinear MHD codes model them through a ramped vacuum boundary field or self-consistent coils.
* [Reversed-shear Alfvén eigenmode (RSAE)](/definitions/reversed-shear-alfven-eigenmode.md) — Shear-Alfvén eigenmode localized at the minimum of a non-monotonic safety-factor profile, dominated by the single harmonic m ≈ n q_min, whose frequency rises with toroidal mode number and follows q_min in time; benchmarked in this bundle on DIII-D #159243.
* [Runaway electrons and their generation mechanisms](/definitions/runaway-electrons.md) — Electrons accelerated to relativistic energies when the parallel electric field exceeds the critical field set by collisional drag; in disruptions they form after the thermal quench by primary (Dreicer, hot-tail, tritium decay, Compton) and secondary (avalanche) generation, can carry most of the plasma current as a beam, and are lost when MHD stochastizes the field, apart from a trapped remnant or a confined core remnant that can reseed the beam.
* [Runaway-electron mitigation coil](/definitions/runaway-electron-mitigation-coil.md) — A passive non-axisymmetric (n = 1) coil driven by the loop voltage of the current quench, whose resonant field keeps the post-thermal-quench field stochastic so that runaway electrons are deconfined before the avalanche grows; NIMROD simulations for SPARC model it by a time-dependent normal-field boundary condition and find it effective only while q0 < 2, a condition sensitive to the treatment of the region between limiter and vessel.
* [Runaway-electron wall loads and sacrificial limiters](/definitions/runaway-electron-wall-loads.md) — The energy that a terminating runaway-electron beam deposits on plasma-facing components, estimated in MHD workflows by seeding guiding-centre test markers from the fluid runaway density, giving them the magnetic energy converted to the beam and tracing them through the stored 3D fields of the termination; sacrificial limiters shrink the wetted first-wall area but concentrate the load on themselves.
* [Sawtooth crash and Kadomtsev reconnection](/definitions/sawtooth-crash.md) — The periodic collapse of the core temperature inside an inversion radius, explained in the Kadomtsev model by complete reconnection driven by the (1,1) internal kink once a q = 1 (ι = 1) surface exists; simulated in tokamaks and, with M3D-C1, in full W7-X geometry, where purely resistive reconnection gives crashes about ten times slower than measured.
* [Shattered pellet injection and massive material injection](/definitions/shattered-pellet-injection.md) — Massive material injection (MMI) is the deliberate injection of large amounts of deuterium and/or impurities to trigger a controlled disruption; shattered pellet injection (SPI), the basis of the ITER disruption mitigation system, breaks a cryogenic pellet into fragments whose ablation is modelled in MHD codes by per-fragment ablation laws and localized density sources.
* [Soft β limit of stellarators](/definitions/soft-beta-limit.md) — A β limit set not by disruptive MHD but by enhanced transport from mild, saturated pressure-driven activity, typical of net-current-free stellarators; JOREK3D simulations of W7-AS attribute it to visco-resistive activity that leaves field lines ergodically confined in radially separated sub-volumes, but reach no β flat top, whereas in high-beta LHD conventional MHD collapses the core and only kinetic thermal ions keep the activity peripheral.
* [Thermal quench](/definitions/thermal-quench.md) — The thermal quench is the disruption phase in which the plasma loses most of its thermal energy, by parallel transport along stochastic field lines opened by MHD modes (external kinks, resistive-wall tearing modes, the 1/1 kink or tearing cascades after injection), by impurity radiation, or both; its duration (0.1 ms in NSTX, about 20 ms predicted for unmitigated ITER VDEs, 75–125 ms proposed for ITER locked-mode disruptions) is sensitive to parallel heat conduction, impurity atomic model, boundary conditions and numerical stabilization.
* [Toroidal Alfvén eigenmode (TAE)](/definitions/toroidal-alfven-eigenmode.md) — Discrete shear-Alfvén eigenmode in the toroidicity-induced gap between the continua of two neighbouring poloidal harmonics m and m+1, located near q = (m+1/2)/n and destabilized by resonant energetic particles; the standard verification target of hybrid kinetic-MHD codes.
* [Toroidal mode families in stellarators](/definitions/toroidal-mode-family.md) — In an equilibrium with N_fp field periods, a perturbation of toroidal mode number n couples linearly only to n ± k N_fp, so the toroidal spectrum splits into independent families; single-field-period runs keep only the family of the equilibrium harmonics, low-n physics needs the full torus, a two-period quasi-axisymmetric configuration destabilizes only its even family, and 3D walls with their own periodicity couple harmonics in the same way.
* [Toroidal radiation asymmetry and first-wall energy impact](/definitions/radiation-asymmetry.md) — The toroidal asymmetry of the power radiated during a mitigated thermal quench, measured by the radiation toroidal peaking factor and converted into a first-wall energy impact; in JOREK simulations single-injector SPI gives peaking factors above 2 with a helical structure set by the triggering rational surface and dual injection removes most of it even with a 1 ms delay, while NIMROD multi-valve gas injection in SPARC stays below 1.5 in the thermal quench.
* [Vertical displacement event (hot and cold VDE)](/definitions/vertical-displacement-event.md) — A vertical displacement event is the axisymmetric vertical instability of an elongated plasma after loss of position control, slowed to the wall time by eddy currents; in a hot VDE the thermal quench is caused by the displacement itself and low-n external kinks ($0<nq_a<m$) make the late phase 3D, in a cold VDE the displacement follows the thermal quench.
