# Linear MHD eigenvalue codes

An eigenvalue code writes every perturbed quantity as
$\mathbf X=\mathbf X_0+\hat{\mathbf X}(\mathbf x)e^{i\omega t}$ about a static or
stationary equilibrium, discretizes the linearized operator, and solves the
generalized eigenproblem
$$\omega\,\mathsf A\,\hat{\mathbf X}=\mathsf B\,\hat{\mathbf X}$$
for complex $\omega$ ([Ma & Guo (2022)](/sources/ma-2022.md), §2, Eq. 8).
Ma and Guo list the classical codes (ERATO, FAR, NOVA, KINX, CASTOR, MARS,
MINERVA) and call their discretization, Fourier harmonics in the poloidal
angle times radial finite elements, "a very classical scheme" (§1, §3.1.3).
Initial-value codes recover only the fastest-growing mode of each toroidal
harmonic; eigenvalue codes return the spectrum and so serve as their linear
references.

## SCELT

[SCELT](/software/scelt.md) ([Ma & Guo (2022)](/sources/ma-2022.md)) solves
compressible resistive single-fluid MHD in axisymmetric flux coordinates
$(s,\phi,\theta)$ for
$\hat{\mathbf X}=(v_s,v_\phi,v_\theta,\delta p,B_s,B_\theta)$, eliminating
$B_\phi$ through $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$; equilibria are static with uniform
density and zero viscosity (§2, §4). The operators are generated by
[symbolic derivation](/methods/symbolic-code-generation.md) and discretized
with poloidal Fourier harmonics ($e^{im\theta}$, $|m|\le m_0$) times radial
cubic elements, with $\partial_\phi\to-in$ (§3.1.3, App. A).

- **Boundary conditions.** A perfectly conducting wall gives $v_s=B_s=0$ at
  $s=1$. At the axis $v_\phi$ and $\delta p$ are single-valued, so their
  $m\ne0$ components vanish; for the poloidal components regularity of the
  Cartesian velocity gives
  $$\hat v_{s,m}=-im\,\hat v_{\theta,m},$$
  and the same for $B_s,B_\theta$ (§3.1.4, App. B, Eqs. B.1–B.6; the reader
  verified B.6 from B.4–B.5). A proper axis condition "may have a significant
  effect on the numerical stability" (§3.1.4).
- **Solver and spurious modes.** The dense problem is solved for all
  eigenpairs with MATLAB `eig`; spurious modes are removed by an eigenvalue
  window and an oscillation parameter
  $$C_{\mathrm{osc}}=\max_m\frac{\sum_k|\hat H_{m,k+1}-\hat H_{m,k}|}{\max_k\hat H_{m,k}-\min_k\hat H_{m,k}}$$
  with threshold about 15, followed by inspection of the mode structure and a
  grid-change check (§3.1.5, Eq. 10). The approach "is only suitable for
  solving unstable problems"; an attempt to compute stable global Alfvén
  eigenmodes "has not succeeded" (§3.1.5).
- **Verification** on circular equilibria with $R_0/a=5$: resistive internal
  kink $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.3766}$ (theory $\eta^{1/3}$), ideal internal-kink
  poloidal-beta threshold $\approx0.375$ (Bussac theory 0.3), and $2/1$ tearing
  $\gamma\propto\eta^{0.5590}$ (theory $\eta^{3/5}$) (§4, Figs. 8, 10, 11; see
  [resistive internal kink](/definitions/resistive-internal-kink.md) and
  [tearing mode](/definitions/tearing-mode.md)). No other eigenvalue code is
  compared.

## Eigenvalue references for initial-value codes

- **MAS against GMEC.** MAS is a Landau-fluid eigenvalue code in general
  geometry with multi-level physics models, in Boozer coordinates with FFTs in
  both angles. Against the field-aligned finite-difference initial-value code
  [GMEC](/software/gmec.md) the ideal ballooning growth rates agree within 4%
  over $n=4$–20 and over a pressure scan through second stability; with
  diamagnetic drift the frequencies agree within 1% and the growth rates within
  20%; both codes follow $\gamma\propto\eta^{3/5}$ for $2/1$ tearing below
  $\eta=10^{-6}\ \Omega$ m ([Jiang et al. (2024)](/sources/jiang-2024.md),
  §IX, Figs. 18, 23, 28). The residual differences are attributed to MAS
  neglecting some equilibrium derivatives and to the different
  discretizations (§IX A).
- **CASTOR3D against JOREK3D.** [CASTOR3D](/software/castor3d.md), a
  linear full-MHD code for 3D equilibria, is the reference for the stellarator benchmark of
  [JOREK3D](/software/jorek3d.md): tearing growth rates agree within 13.2%
  ($\beta$ scan) and 26.2% ($\eta$ scan), ballooning within 6.4%, JOREK3D
  systematically higher ([Nikulsin et al. (2022)](/sources/nikulsin-2022.md),
  §VI–VII, Figs. 8, 12). Adding the harmonic $n=15$ lowers the JOREK3D $n=10$
  growth rate by 5–6% but the CASTOR3D one by less than 0.003%: spectral
  blocking in the non-linear code "accounts for most of" the ballooning
  discrepancy (§VII). The linearized ideal operator of the stellarator reduced
  model is self-adjoint, so ideal perturbations have real $\omega^2$
  (App. B, Eqs. B1–B5). On W7-AS the $(2,1)$ tearing growth rates agree
  within about 3 %, and interchange modes in a low-shear $l=2$
  stellarator within 10 % once JOREK3D retains the parallel flow; CASTOR3D
  also identifies an $n=5$ free-boundary external mode that the
  fixed-boundary JOREK3D cannot benchmark
  ([Ramasamy et al. (2024)](/sources/ramasamy-2024.md), §3.1–3.2, Figs. 2,
  5, 7).
- **MINERVA against MIPS.** The ideal eigenvalue code MINERVA (Aiba et al.
  2009) is the reference for the $n=3$ ideal
  [infernal mode](/definitions/infernal-mode.md) of a circular JT-60U-like
  equilibrium at central β 5.7 %: the initial-value growth rate of
  [MIPS](/software/mips.md) at $\nu=\kappa=0$ falls with Lundquist number
  and approaches MINERVA's (about $0.0057\tau_A^{-1}$, $\tau_A$ with
  length $R_0$) at $S=10^8$, about 5 % above it on the finer meshes
  (figure reading), with matching mode structure (dominant $m=4$); at 4.1 %
  MINERVA finds the ideal mode stable ("not shown")
  ([Sato et al. (2024)](/sources/sato-2024.md), §3.1, Figs. 2–3).
- **CASTOR3D against JOREK (tokamak).** JOREK full MHD agrees with CASTOR3D on
  $1/1$ kink and $2/1$ tearing growth rates; JOREK reduced MHD deviates at low
  $\eta$ for the kink and "clearly fails" at finite $\beta$
  ([Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md), §4.4, Figs. 18–19; see
  [reduced MHD](/methods/reduced-mhd.md)). Passive-coil VDE growth rates agree
  with CASTOR3D within about 20% (§4.8, Fig. 30).
- **ELITE against BOUT++.** For the $n=20$ ideal ballooning mode of cbm18 the
  eigenvalue code ELITE gives $\gamma/\omega_A=0.239$, the initial-value code
  BOUT++ 0.244–0.246 ([Seto et al. (2023)](/sources/seto-2023.md), §4,
  Fig. 3; [cbm18 benchmark](/benchmarks/cbm18-ballooning-benchmark.md)).
- **Eigenvalue solvers turned initial-value.** The MHD module of the hybrid
  code [HYMAGYC](/software/hymagyc.md) derives from MARS, converted from an
  eigenvalue to an initial-value solver, with Fourier harmonics in both angles
  and finite elements in radius ([Vlad et al. (2025)](/sources/vlad-2025.md),
  §3). HELENA and CASTOR compute the Alfvén continua against which JOREK's
  energetic-particle modes are located
  ([Bogaarts et al. (2022)](/sources/bogaarts-2022.md), equilibrium set-up).

## Qualifications

- **"Consistent" at 25%.** SCELT's poloidal-beta threshold 0.375 is called
  consistent with Bussac's 0.3; the comparison sets large-aspect-ratio theory
  against $R_0/a=5$ numerics, and Fig. 10 plots $\gamma$ against central
  pressure with no $\beta_p$ axis or zero crossing, the conversion not being
  given ([Ma & Guo (2022)](/sources/ma-2022.md), §4.1; reader's note).
- **Printed axis condition.** §3.1.4 of Ma and Guo prints
  $\hat v_{s,m}=-\Im\,\hat v_{\theta,m}$, without the factor $m$ and with the
  imaginary-part symbol for the imaginary unit; App. B gives the correct
  $-im$ (reader's note).
- The JOREK3D–CASTOR3D gaps are partly attributed to the neglect of the
  parallel velocity in the stellarator reduced model, which the authors
  compare to the quasi-interchange overestimate by $\sqrt{1+2q_s^2}$
  ([Nikulsin et al. (2022)](/sources/nikulsin-2022.md), §VI); a
  Publisher's Note to that paper exists with unknown content.
  [Ramasamy et al. (2024)](/sources/ramasamy-2024.md) (§3.1) attribute the
  tearing gap instead to insufficient poloidal resolution in CASTOR3D, an
  eigenvalue-code-side cause, without showing the re-run; the conflict is
  open on [JOREK3D](/software/jorek3d.md) and
  [CASTOR3D](/software/castor3d.md). If it holds, the reference code, not
  only the initial-value code, needs a resolution study in such
  benchmarks.
