# Methods

Algorithms and schemes.

* [Bézier–Hermite finite elements with toroidal Fourier harmonics](/methods/bezier-finite-elements.md) — JOREK's spatial discretization: isoparametric bicubic Bézier patches in the poloidal plane, written in nodal cubic-Hermite form with four degrees of freedom per node and element-dependent scale factors, combined with a real toroidal Fourier series; flux-aligned grids, convergence orders, the toroidally varying JOREK3D geometry, and the open dispute over whether the elements are G1 or C1.
* [Collisional-radiative (non-equilibrium) impurity model](/methods/collisional-radiative-impurity-model.md) — Impurity model that evolves the charge-state distribution in time with collisional-radiative ionization and recombination rates instead of assuming coronal equilibrium; in JOREK it is carried by fluid-advected super-particles whose radiation, ionization power, effective charge, mean charge and density are projected onto the finite-element fields.
* [Coronal-equilibrium impurity model](/methods/coronal-equilibrium-impurity-model.md) — Impurity model for MHD codes in which one advected impurity density carries all charge states, whose distribution is set instantaneously to the local ionization–recombination balance at the electron temperature; it supplies radiation, ionization energy, effective charge and electron density to the fluid equations.
* [Dommaschk potentials for the vacuum field](/methods/dommaschk-potentials.md) — Analytic toroidal-harmonic solutions of Laplace's equation in cylindrical coordinates used to represent the stellarator coil field as ∇χ; the coefficients are fitted to a computed vacuum field by Fourier and Legendre projections on a cylinder, giving triangular linear systems, so that ∇·B = 0 holds to machine precision in JOREK3D.
* [Field-aligned coordinates, twist-shift condition and shifted metric](/methods/field-aligned-coordinates.md) — Coordinates in which one coordinate follows the equilibrium field line, so that few points resolve the parallel direction; their twist-shift periodicity, the phase-shift Fourier transform to flux coordinates, the integrated-shear boundary instability, and its cures by numerical diffusion, Scott's shifted metric, quasi-ballooning coordinates and a simple shifted metric aligned with the toroidal angle.
* [Fluid neutral models and plasma–neutral atomic sources](/methods/fluid-neutral-models.md) — Fluid treatments of atomic neutrals in MHD codes, from a single diffusive neutral density to a full neutral continuity–momentum–energy system, and the ionization, recombination, charge-exchange and radiation source terms that couple them to the plasma conservatively.
* [Free-boundary and resistive-wall coupling](/methods/resistive-wall-coupling.md) — Methods that close a nonlinear MHD model with the vacuum field and the eddy currents of resistive conductors: a meshed vacuum and wall inside the computational domain (M3D-C1, NIMROD), or an un-meshed exterior handled by Green's functions and a natural boundary condition on the tangential field, with thin walls (JOREK-STARWALL) or 3D volumetric conductors through a virtual-casing equivalent current (JOREK-CARIDDI).
* [Hybrid 2D/1D field solver and the flute-ordering error](/methods/hybrid-2d-1d-field-solver.md) — BOUT++ extension that inverts the vorticity–potential Helmholtz equation of each toroidal harmonic with a 2D solver in orthogonal flux coordinates for n = 0 and low n, and with the flute-ordered 1D radial solver for higher n; it removes the O(1/n) error of the flute ordering at low n and allows full-annulus edge simulations with zonal components.
* [Hybrid kinetic-MHD models](/methods/hybrid-kinetic-mhd.md) — Models that advance the bulk plasma as an MHD fluid and a minority energetic-particle population as kinetic markers, closing the fluid momentum equation with a moment of the marker distribution; the codes of this bundle differ in the MHD model, the kinetic ordering, the representation of the distribution and the time coupling.
* [Implicit time integration and preconditioned Krylov solvers for nonlinear MHD](/methods/implicit-time-integration.md) — The linearized three-level theta/xi scheme of JOREK (Crank–Nicolson, BDF2, implicit Euler) solved each step by restarted GMRES with a block-Jacobi preconditioner over toroidal harmonics whose blocks are LU-factorized and reused, set against the split-implicit, leapfrog, backward-Euler, Newton–Krylov, BDF (CVODE) and explicit Runge–Kutta advances of the other codes; includes the variable-step order condition and the open BDF2-versus-BDF5 slip.
* [Kinetic (Monte Carlo) neutral model coupled to MHD](/methods/kinetic-neutral-model.md) — JOREK model in which atomic neutrals are Monte Carlo superparticles that undergo ionization, charge exchange, line radiation and recombination, recycle and sputter at the wall, and return their moments to the implicit fluid as explicit sources once per fluid step through a regularized finite-element projection.
* [Linear MHD eigenvalue codes](/methods/linear-mhd-eigenvalue-codes.md) — Codes that linearize the MHD equations about an equilibrium and solve a generalized eigenproblem for complex frequencies and mode structures, typically with poloidal (and toroidal) Fourier harmonics times radial finite elements; their role as references for initial-value codes, the spurious-mode problem, axis regularity, and the benchmark agreements of SCELT, MAS and CASTOR3D.
* [Operator splitting and Newton–Krylov treatment of atomic-physics sources](/methods/operator-splitting-of-atomic-sources.md) — Ways of adding stiff, temperature-dependent ionization, recombination and charge-exchange sources to a staggered semi-implicit MHD advance: Crank–Nicolson linearization, Jacobian-free Newton–Krylov on the non-linear remainder, per-field Strang splitting, and Strang splitting interleaved in the leapfrog with a Douglas–Rachford-inspired coupling source; with the error taxonomy that ranks them and the explicit-source practice of the implicit codes.
* [Particle pushers: Boris, slow-manifold Boris, guiding-centre and gyrokinetic](/methods/particle-pushers.md) — Orbit integrators that advance the markers of the kinetic modules attached to MHD codes: the full-orbit Boris scheme, its slow-manifold variant for Pauli particles with time steps above the gyro-period, RK4 and variational guiding-centre integrators, and gyrokinetic gyrocentre equations with N-point gyroaverages, with their conservation properties as measured in this bundle's sources.
* [Particle-to-finite-element moment projection](/methods/particle-to-mesh-projection.md) — Weak-form (L2) projection of marker-particle moments onto the finite-element basis of an MHD code: the mass matrix is factorized once, the right-hand side is a weighted sum of basis functions at the marker positions, and optional Laplacian or biharmonic regularization and time accumulation control Monte Carlo noise and conservation.
* [Pressure coupling and current coupling](/methods/pressure-and-current-coupling.md) — The two ways a hybrid kinetic-MHD code closes the bulk momentum equation with energetic particles: by the divergence of the energetic-particle pressure tensor or by the energetic-particle current; analytically equivalent in the perpendicular direction in the drift-kinetic limit, yet reported to differ in noise and suspected of driving nonlinear benchmark discrepancies.
* [Reduced MHD for stellarators](/methods/stellarator-reduced-mhd.md) — Generalization of tokamak reduced MHD in which the fixed toroidal field is replaced by the curl-free coil field ∇χ: the field is ∇χ plus field-line bending ∇ψχ×∇χ and the flow is ∇u×∇χ/|∇χ|²; a full-MHD stellarator equilibrium leaves a residual force of fourth order in the ordering parameter; implemented in JOREK3D with v∥ = 0 and verified on Wendelstein 7-A.
* [Reduced MHD for tokamaks](/methods/reduced-mhd.md) — MHD models that restrict the magnetic field to a poloidal flux function plus a fixed toroidal field and the perpendicular flow to an E×B stream function, removing the fast magnetosonic wave; derived either by an aspect-ratio or flute ordering or, as in JOREK, by a Galerkin ansatz without geometric approximation; energy-conserving, not locally momentum-conserving, and inaccurate for pressure-driven internal kinks at finite β.
* [Runaway-electron fluid model coupled to MHD](/methods/runaway-electron-fluid-model.md) — JOREK model in which runaway electrons are a separate species described only by their number density, streaming along the field at the speed of light, drifting with the E×B flow and carrying a current that is removed from the resistive Ohm's law; sources are avalanche with partial screening plus Compton and tritium seeds, treated explicitly.
* [Semi-implicit leapfrog](/methods/semi-implicit-leapfrog.md) — NIMROD's time advance: velocity staggered half a step from density, temperature and magnetic field, the fields solved one after another in dependency order, a semi-implicit operator built on the linearized ideal-MHD force stabilizing the waves of the velocity advance, and time-centred implicit advection in the other equations.
* [Sheath (Bohm) boundary conditions](/methods/sheath-boundary-conditions.md) — Boundary conditions for MHD codes where field lines intersect a material wall: the plasma leaves at the sound speed (Bohm criterion), the energy flux is a sheath transmission factor times the convected thermal energy, density has no diffusive flux, and the electric potential and current are prescribed, optionally limited by the ion saturation current.
* [Shock-capturing artificial viscosity for MHD finite elements](/methods/shock-capturing-artificial-viscosity.md) — Solution-dependent, field-aligned artificial diffusion added to the hydrodynamic equations of JOREK full MHD, with a coefficient built from a pressure-gradient sensor and a pressure-rate shock strength; it lets 3D shattered-pellet simulations run with larger time steps at preserved thermal-quench dynamics, but over-stabilization suppresses the quench.
* [Single-fluid visco-resistive MHD model](/methods/visco-resistive-mhd-model.md) — The full (non-reduced) single-fluid, single-temperature resistive MHD system with viscosity, anisotropic heat conduction and particle diffusion that the nonlinear tokamak codes solve; its variants differ in the evolved field (B or A), the viscous and heat-flux closures, the source current in Ohm's law and the normalization.
* [Symbolic derivation and automatic discretization of MHD operators](/methods/symbolic-code-generation.md) — Construction of MHD solvers by symbolic computation: the vector equations are expanded, linearized and written in curvilinear (flux or field-aligned) coordinates by a symbolic engine, and every generated scalar term is discretized automatically; realized at run-time-independent level in SCELT (C++ polynomial algebra, Fourier times cubic elements) and at compile time in GMEC's C++20 metaprogramming solver (finite differences, merged coefficients).
* [The δf particle method](/methods/delta-f-method.md) — Marker method that evolves only the deviation of the energetic-particle distribution from a known equilibrium part, carrying it as a weight advanced along the characteristics; it lowers noise relative to full-f, and the hybrid codes of this bundle use it with or without suppressing the relaxation of a non-equilibrium initial distribution.
* [Three-dimensional inverse equilibrium solvers](/methods/inverse-equilibrium-solvers.md) — Codes that compute 3D ideal-MHD equilibria with nested flux surfaces by solving for the positions R, Z of the surfaces as functions of flux coordinates: VMEC (Fourier in angle, radial finite differences, energy descent), DESC (global Fourier–Zernike pseudospectral, Newton-type solve of the force residual) and GVEC (B-spline radial basis, energy minimization); they supply initial states and grids to nonlinear MHD and hybrid codes.
* [Variational multiscale (VMS) stabilization for MHD finite elements](/methods/variational-multiscale-stabilization.md) — Residual-based stabilization of the Galerkin weak form in which the unresolved scales are modelled as minus a stabilization matrix times the residual; in JOREK it uses a diagonal matrix scaled by element size over the fast magnetosonic speed and a residual truncated to its convective part, and it contains streamline diffusion and Taylor–Galerkin stabilization as special cases.
