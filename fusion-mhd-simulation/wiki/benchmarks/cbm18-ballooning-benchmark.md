# cbm18 ideal-ballooning benchmark

The cbm18 equilibria are a series of shifted-circular, H-mode-like pedestal
equilibria with no X-point, generated by the TOQ code. The series goes back to
Snyder et al. (2002) and Wilson et al. (2002). It is a cross-code test set for
the linear [ballooning mode](/definitions/ballooning-mode.md), on which BOUT++,
ELITE, GATO and M3D-C1 have been compared.
[Seto et al. (2023)](/sources/seto-2023.md) (§4) use two members of the
series:

- **cbm18_dens8** is the most unstable member. $q\approx2$ at the peak of the
  pressure gradient. It is used for ideal-ballooning (IBM) verification.
- **cbm18_dens4** is marginally IBM-unstable. It is used for the nonlinear
  pedestal-collapse study (see
  [edge-localized modes](/definitions/edge-localized-mode.md)). The
  full-annulus run analysed by [Seto et al. (2024)](/sources/seto-2024.md)
  is this case by the compiler's identification only (identical model,
  coefficients, grid and linear spectrum; the 2024 paper does not name the
  equilibrium); see
  [two-stage crash of resistive drift-ballooning ELMs](/results/two-stage-elm-crash.md).

## Setup in Seto et al. (2023)

- **Model** (§4, Eqs. 21–27). Linear three-field peeling–ballooning reduced
  MHD for vorticity, the parallel vector potential and pressure, with
  constant ion density.
- **Field-solver equation.** The vorticity-to-potential inversion is a
  Helmholtz equation with coefficients $d=1$, $c=B^{-2}$ and $a=0$ in the
  paper's notation.
- **Normalization.** Poloidal Alfvén units: reference length 3 m, field 2 T,
  density $10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$, deuterium. This gives
  $\tau_A=3.08\times10^{-7}$ s. Growth rates are quoted as $\gamma\tau_A$.
- **Grid.**
  - Radial: 516 points, including guard cells, on $0.6\le\psi_N\le1.2$.
  - Binormal: 32 points on one toroidal wedge $[0,2\pi/N)$. The toroidal
    mode $n=N$ is computed as the wedge fundamental.
  - Parallel/poloidal: 64 points for the flute-ordered 1D field solver and
    512 for the 2D field solver.
- **Code.** [BOUT++](/software/bout-plus-plus.md), with fields in
  [field-aligned coordinates](/methods/field-aligned-coordinates.md) and the
  [hybrid 2D/1D field solver](/methods/hybrid-2d-1d-field-solver.md).

## Reference values and results

At $n=20$ on cbm18_dens8 (Seto et al. 2023, §4, Fig. 3):

| Code / solver | $\gamma\tau_A$ | vs ELITE |
|---|---|---|
| ELITE, as reported by Dudson et al. (2009) (ref. [14] of Seto et al.) | 0.239 | — |
| BOUT++, earlier result, same reference | 0.245 | +2.5% |
| BOUT++ with the new 2D field solver | 0.244 | +2.1% |
| BOUT++ with the flute-ordered 1D field solver | 0.246 | +2.9% |

The percentages are computed from the printed values; the paper prints none.

**Flute-ordering error.**

- The 1D and 2D BOUT++ solvers differ by about 1% at $n=20$ and by about 36%
  at $n=5$; the 1D solver is higher.
- The paper reads this as qualitatively consistent with the $O(1/n)$
  correction to the flute ordering of Connor, Hastie and Taylor (1979).
- **Eigenfunction** (2D solver, Fig. 4): even in radius about the
  pressure-gradient peak, even about the outboard midplane, with poloidal
  harmonics $m=33$–51 around $q=41/20$.

**DWRBM spectrum on cbm18_dens4** (§5.1, Fig. 5b). With the two-fluid
four-field model, whose poloidal Alfvén units use a 3.5 m reference length
(not the 3 m of the ideal-ballooning runs; no $\tau_A$ value is printed for
it, Seto et al. 2023, Table 2; Seto et al. 2024, §II, use the same
normalization):

- the most unstable mode is $n=32$, at $\gamma\tau_A=7.86\times10^{-2}$;
- the unstable range is $11\le n\le52$;
- the 1D and 2D solvers differ by 2–8% for $n=11$–20.

## Use as a finite-element convergence case

[Hoelzl et al. (2021)](/sources/hoelzl-2021.md) (§4.1, Fig. 14) use the CBM18
$n=20$ ballooning mode to measure the spatial convergence of
[JOREK](/software/jorek.md).

- **Grids.** $(n_{\mathrm{flux}},n_{\mathrm{tht}})$ from (27,180) to
  (90,600).
- **Reduced MHD.** The growth-rate error converges at 5th order in
  $\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{flux}}n_{\mathrm{tht}}}$, with the converged value
  extrapolated. A full-MHD tearing mode shows the same order.
- **Rationale given.** Fourth-order local accuracy of the bi-cubic elements,
  plus one order from integrating the energy (see
  [Bézier finite elements](/methods/bezier-finite-elements.md)).
- **Full MHD.** On the same ballooning case, full MHD converges at only 3rd
  order. The paper says the reason is "under investigation".

## Qualifications

- **Inner boundary.** The Fig. 2 caption of Seto et al. (2023) puts the inner
  radial boundary at $\psi_N=0.4$. The text gives 516 points on
  $0.6\le\psi_N\le1.2$, and Fig. 4b starts at 0.6. The IBM domain is
  therefore ambiguous as printed.
- **Normalization of $\psi$.** The radial label is a normalized poloidal flux
  that exceeds 1 outside the last closed surface (domain up to 1.2). The
  $2\pi$ convention is not stated.
- **Flux-surface geometry only.** The test has no X-point and no
  scrape-off layer. It verifies linear ideal ballooning physics, not
  divertor-geometry effects.
- **Unexplained full-MHD convergence order.** JOREK's 3rd-order convergence
  in full MHD is open, so this case does not verify the full-MHD
  discretization to the design order.

## Contradictions

**Low-$n$ overestimate of flute-ordered BOUT++ — Open.**

- *Earlier result.* The cbm18 benchmark of Dudson et al. (2011), as
  reported by Seto et al. (2023, §4, their ref. [36], Fig. 2 there), found
  that flute-ordered BOUT++ "overestimates the growth rate in the low-n
  regime" relative to ELITE and GATO.
- *Seto et al. (2023)* (§4, §6) state that their 2D field solver "can
  suppress this overestimation to some level". The supporting evidence is
  indirect: the 1D–2D gap grows to about 36% at $n=5$, with the
  flute-ordered result higher. ELITE and GATO low-$n$ values are not
  re-plotted, and at $n=20$ both BOUT++ solvers still sit above ELITE.
- *What would settle it.* A direct overlay of the 2D-solver growth rates for
  $n\approx5$–15 on cbm18_dens8 with the ELITE and GATO values.
