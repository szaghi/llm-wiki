# Results

Theorems, proved properties and impossibility results.

* [Edge instabilities of an optimized quasi-axisymmetric reactor stellarator](/results/stellarator-edge-instability-qa.md) — First nonlinear M3D-C1 simulations of a reactor-scale two-field-period quasi-axisymmetric stellarator optimized at β = 2.5 % without a stability target: edge pressure-driven high-n modes destroy the flux surfaces within about 1500 Alfvén times for every resistivity model, only even toroidal harmonics are destabilized, temperature gradients survive in the chaotic field, and Spitzer resistivity gives a secondary nonlinear growth acceleration; the authors conclude that MHD stability must enter stellarator optimization.
* [Electron temperature hole and the stochastic barrier at q = 1 after SPI](/results/electron-temperature-hole.md) — In JOREK simulations of neon-deuterium SPI into ITER L-mode, transient electron temperature holes form along field lines through the fragment plume because Braginskii parallel conduction collapses in the cold radiating cloud, raising local resistivity and the MHD response; and a transport barrier of the stochastic field just outside q = 1 confines the core during the early thermal quench until edge modes grow.
* [High-field-side high-density region](/results/high-field-side-high-density-region.md) — In JOREK simulations of the ITER PFPO-1 H-mode with kinetic neutrals, poloidal E×B drifts trap a dense, cold, low-pressure region in a dominant high-field-side E×B vortex near the X-point; it forms dynamically over 10–20 ms, only when drifts are included, with a phase-like transition of the upstream density and full inner-target detachment.
* [Kinetic thermal ions confine LHD pressure-driven activity to the periphery](/results/kinetic-ions-prevent-core-collapse-lhd.md) — In high-beta inward-shifted LHD equilibria, conventional MHD (MIPS) predicts a core collapse by peripheral resistive ballooning modes at moderate Lundquist number and by a resonant (3,2) ideal interchange at high Lundquist number, whereas MEGA with drift-kinetic thermal ions lowers the growth rates through the poloidal precession of helically trapped ions and keeps the nonlinear activity peripheral with the central pressure maintained at S = 10^7, as observed; energetic ions up to 0.8 % beta lower the linear growth rate without changing saturation.
* [Reduced-MHD boundary condition suppresses the toroidal asymmetry of the plasma current](/results/reduced-mhd-current-asymmetry.md) — In ansatz-based reduced MHD the poloidal electric field is a pure potential gradient and the boundary condition on the normal velocity makes the potential constant on the boundary, so the boundary acts as a poloidal ideal conductor, no current crosses it and the plasma current is the same in every poloidal plane; JOREK therefore shows below 0.01% toroidal asymmetry of $I_p$ in a 3D VDE where full-MHD codes find 1-4% and experiments 10-20%.
* [Runaway current reconstitution after an MHD flush](/results/runaway-reconstitution-after-mhd-flush.md) — After a low-Z purge triggers large-scale MHD that stochastizes the field and expels a runaway beam (benign termination), magnetically trapped relativistic electrons survive as a seed of 0.1–1% of the beam and avalanche back once the flux surfaces reheal, so an ITER-like beam loses only about 2–3 MA before it reforms; 3D MHD simulations of the flush itself find more than 95% prompt loss and conversion to Ohmic current in DIII-D, but in EU-DEMO a 77% loss followed by re-avalanche of a confined core remnant.
* [Tearing triggered by impurity radiation through resistive-interchange reversal](/results/radiation-triggered-tearing.md) — NIMROD/KPRAD simulations show that neon deposited on the q = 2 surface of a nominally tearing-stable tokamak plasma triggers a 2/1 tearing mode above a threshold impurity density, because radiative cooling makes the pressure locally hollow and reverses the sign of the Glasser–Greene–Johnson resistive-interchange parameter D_R; the classical radiation-exceeds-Ohmic-heating criterion does not set the threshold, and fast parallel conduction suppresses the mode.
* [Two-stage crash of resistive drift-ballooning ELMs](/results/two-stage-elm-crash.md) — In BOUT++ full-annulus simulations a pedestal collapse driven by resistive drift-ballooning modes proceeds in two crashes: the saturating linear modes first nonlinearly create 2/1 islands with turbulence confined to their X-points, and the main loss follows when the island harmonics have stochastized q = 2 and down-shifted middle-n turbulence covers the surface; the interval shrinks from 103 to 3 Alfvén times as the toroidal wedge number grows from 1 to 4, while the final loss stays about 6% of the pedestal energy.
* [Vertical-force mitigation by massive material injection](/results/vertical-force-mitigation.md) — Massive impurity injection during a VDE reduces the vertical wall force because the cooled plasma carries a large, predominantly toroidal current in a broad scrape-off layer, which decouples the current centroid from the magnetic-axis motion and reduces its vertical excursion; the poloidal halo current stays moderate, and the mechanism fails when a runaway beam keeps the current inside the last closed surface.
